Futures: the market's dashboard.

Long before the opening bell, futures on stock indexes, Treasury bonds, oil, gold and currencies are already trading, and their prices are the first read on the day. Here is which contracts to watch, what each one is telling you, how to read the US dollar, and how the pieces fit together on a calm day and a scared one.

At the table

Reading futures before the open is watching the room before you take a seat: who's stacking chips, who's leaving, how big the pots are getting. You haven't played a hand, but you already know whether it's a loose night or a tight one.

04Treasury futures: the price of safety

Futures on US Treasury notes and bonds, the 10-year note above all, trade around the clock too. Like any bond, their prices move opposite to yields, as the bonds lesson shows: Treasury futures rising means yields falling. On a scared morning, money tends to flow into Treasuries, pushing their futures up. On a morning when inflation news is hot, or the market expects the Fed to lean hawkish, they fall.

05Oil and gold: growth and fear

Crude oil futures are a read on the world economy's appetite, and on supply shocks: oil rises when growth looks strong, or when war or production cuts threaten supply, and falls when the economy looks weak. The contango lesson has what the shape of the oil curve says.

Gold pays nothing, so it tends to do better when interest rates, after inflation, are low or falling, and when investors want something outside the financial system. It often rises on scared days, but not always: in a sharp sell-off, people sometimes sell gold too, to raise cash.

07Reading the board together

No single price tells you much. Together they tell a story. Two common ones:

Market"Risk-on": confident"Risk-off": scared
Stock index futuresUp, small companies mostDown, Nasdaq-100 and Russell 2000 most
Treasury futuresDown (yields up)Up (yields down)
US dollarOften softerOften stronger, as money looks for safety
OilUpDown
GoldMixedOften up, unless investors are selling everything for cash

These are patterns, not laws. In 2022, stocks and Treasuries fell together, because the threat was inflation and rising rates, which hurt both; the usual cushion of bonds rising when stocks fall didn't show up. When the board doesn't match either column, that is information too: the market is worried about something other than growth.

09Common misreads

  • "Futures are up, so stocks will close up." They tell you where trading is now, not where it ends. A lot can change in six and a half hours of trading.
  • "The dollar index is the dollar." It is the dollar against six currencies, mostly the euro. Against the Chinese yuan or the Mexican peso it says nothing.
  • "A strong dollar means a strong US economy." Sometimes. It can also mean the rest of the world is scared, and buying dollars for safety.
  • "Gold always rises when markets panic." Often, not always: in a rush for cash, gold gets sold with everything else.
