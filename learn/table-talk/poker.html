Poker & Gambling Glossary

The words you’ll hear at the poker table and read on The Tables, from pot odds to the house edge. Learn the language before you learn the game.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVW#

A

Advantage play

AKA AP

Covers Advantage player · Backed off · Back-off · Flat-bet

Methods that turn a game to a player edge by exploiting how it is run: card counting, hole-carding, full-pay video poker with cash-back, progressives near their ceiling. Most casinos can refuse such players: back them off the game, cap their bets or bar them.

In the marketAn arbitrage: real, small, and closed as soon as the other side notices.

TagsGeneral

Related Card counting, Hole-carding, Progressive jackpot, Edge sorting

All-in

AKA Shove · Jam

Covers No-limit · Pot-limit

Betting every chip you have on the table, which no-limit games allow at any time. A player who is all-in can't be forced out of the hand and can win from each opponent only as much as they put in themselves.

TagsPoker

Related Side pot, Fold equity

Angle shooting

AKA Angle shoot · Angling

Covers Angle shooter

Using tricks that skirt the rules to mislead opponents, such as acting as if to fold or hiding large chips behind small ones. Card rooms penalize it, and players remember it.

TagsPoker

Related String bet, Betting actions

Ante

A forced bet posted before the deal on top of the blinds, by every player or, in many tournaments, by the big blind for the whole table; common in later tournament stages. In casino carnival games the ante is your main bet against the dealer.

At the casinoIn casino carnival games, your main bet against the dealer, posted before the deal. Three Card Poker: post a $10 Ante, see your three cards, then fold or add a $10 Play bet.

TagsPoker

Related Blinds, Ante (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Ante (Three Card Poker)

Ante (Three Card Poker)

The bet that opens a hand: post it, see three cards, then fold, losing it, or add an equal Play bet. If the dealer qualifies, the better hand wins both bets. With three cards a straight is rarer than a flush, so it ranks higher.

ExampleHouse edge 3.37% of the ante, or 2.01% of all money bet, with the queen-six-four rule.

TagsCarnival games

Related Play bet (Three Card Poker), Queen-six-four (Q-6-4), Ante Bonus, Pair Plus, Dealer qualifies

Ante (Ultimate Texas Hold'em)

One of two equal bets every player posts before the deal (as opposed to the Blind, which pays a bonus only on a winning straight or better). The Ante pays even money if you beat the dealer, but pushes if the dealer doesn't qualify.

TagsCarnival games

Related Blind (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Play bet (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Dealer qualifies, Trips

Ante Bonus

Covers Ante bonus paytable · 5-4-1

An extra payout on the Three Card Poker Ante for a straight or better, paid whether or not you beat the dealer. Its paytable moves the game's edge: 5-4-1 gives 3.37% of the ante, 3-2-1 gives 4.28%.

TagsCarnival games

Related Ante (Three Card Poker), Paytable

B

Bad beat

Covers Suckout · Sucked out

Losing a hand you were a strong favorite to win when an opponent catches a long-shot card. Seen from the winner's side, it is a suckout.

TagsPoker

Related Cooler, Tilt, Equity

Banker

AKA Banker bet

Covers Bank

The baccarat bet on the hand that draws last. It wins slightly more often than Player, about 45.9% of hands against 44.6%, so it pays even money less a 5% commission. House edge 1.06%.

TagsBaccarat

Related Player, Tie, Vig

Bankroll

Covers Unit · Betting unit · Base bet

Money set aside for gambling, apart from money needed for anything else. Measured in units, a player's standard bet, it decides how long the swings can be absorbed. The simulators count results in units and convert at the unit you enter.

In the marketRisk capital. Sizing each bet against it is position sizing; the Kelly criterion is the formal version.

TagsGeneral

Related Risk of ruin, Drawdown, Standard deviation

Basic strategy

AKA The book

Covers The chart · Strategy card · Basic strategy chart

The mathematically best play for every player hand against every dealer upcard, given the table's rules. Played perfectly it leaves a house edge of roughly half a percent. Printed strategy cards are allowed at the table.

TagsBlackjack

Related Index plays, House edge, Upcard

Bet

Covers Open · Opening bet · Lead out · Bet out

Put chips in first in a betting round; before the flop the big blind counts as that bet. Everyone after you must then call, raise or fold.

TagsPoker

Related Check, Raise, Value bet, Bluff

Betting actions

AKA The five actions

The five things a player can do when it is their turn: check, bet, call, raise or fold. Which ones are open depends on whether anyone has bet yet in the round.

TagsPoker

Related Check, Bet, Call, Raise, Fold

Betting system

Covers Martingale · Progression · Table maximum · Table limits

A rule for changing bet size based on past results, such as Martingale: double after every loss. No sequence of bets on a negative-edge game produces a positive edge; the table maximum makes sure the rare catastrophe arrives.

ExampleMartingale on a 5.26% wheel is still a 5.26% game, now with a small chance of a very large loss.

In the marketDoubling down on a losing position: the average doesn't move, the tail gets fatter.

TagsGeneral

Related Gambler's fallacy, Risk of ruin, House edge

Big 6 and Big 8

Covers Big 6 · Big 8

A bet that 6 (or 8) rolls before a 7, paid even money. It is the same event as a place bet on 6 or 8, which pays 7 to 6, so it costs 9.09% against 1.52%.

TagsCraps

Related Place bet, True odds

Blackjack (natural)

AKA Blackjack · Natural · Two-card 21

An ace and a ten-value card as your first two cards: a two-card 21. It beats any other 21 and traditionally pays 3 to 2.

TagsBlackjack

Related 3:2 vs 6:5, Even money, Basic strategy

Blackjack side bets

Covers 21+3 · Perfect Pairs · Lucky Ladies

Optional bets on the first cards dealt. 21+3 pays when your two cards and the dealer's upcard make a flush, straight, three of a kind or straight flush; Perfect Pairs pays on a first-two-card pair; Lucky Ladies on a two-card 20.

ExampleTypical paytables: 21+3 about 3–7%, Perfect Pairs 6–11%, Lucky Ladies 17–25%, against about 0.5% for the main game.

TagsBlackjack

Related Side bet, Paytable

Blackjack Switch

Covers Switch

Two hands of equal size, with the right to swap their second cards. A blackjack pays even money, one made by switching counts as 21, and a dealer 22 pushes. House edge 0.58% with the full switching strategy, about 0.75% with the simple rule.

TagsBlackjack

Related Push 22, Even money

Blind (Ultimate Texas Hold'em)

AKA Blind bet

The second of two equal bets every player posts before the deal (as opposed to the Ante, which pays even money on a win over a qualifying dealer). The Blind pays a bonus only on a winning straight or better and pushes on lesser wins.

TagsCarnival games

Related Ante (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Play bet (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Dealer qualifies

Blinds

Covers Small blind · Big blind · SB · BB

Forced bets posted before the deal by the two players to the left of the button: the small blind and the big blind, usually twice as large. They seed the pot so every hand has something to play for.

TagsPoker

Related Ante, Button, Position

Bluff

A bet or raise with a weak hand that wins only if opponents fold. A bluff that succeeds often enough to cover the times it is called is profitable.

TagsPoker

Related Semi-bluff, Fold equity, Value bet

Bust

AKA Busting · Break

Going over 21, which loses at once. A player who busts loses even if the dealer busts afterward: that asymmetry is worth about 8% to the house before the player's options claw most of it back.

TagsBlackjack

Related Hit and stand, Soft hand, Push 22

Button

AKA Dealer button · BTN

Covers On the button

A disc marking the player in the dealer's position for the hand; it moves one seat to the left after every hand. The button acts last on every betting round after the flop, which makes it the best seat at the table.

TagsPoker

Related Position, Blinds

Buy bet

AKA Buy

Covers Buying the 4 · Buying the 10

A place bet paid at true odds, less a 5% commission charged either when it wins or when the bet goes down. Buying the 4 or 10 with the commission taken only on a win costs 1.67%.

TagsCraps

Related Place bet, Vig, Lay bet

C

Call

AKA Calling

Covers Flat call

Match the current bet to stay in the hand.

TagsPoker

Related Pot odds, Raise, Fold

Card counting

AKA Counting cards

Covers Counter · Hi-Lo · Bet spread · Betting ramp

Tracking dealt cards to know when the rest of the shoe is rich in tens and aces, which favors the player, and betting more then. Hi-Lo counts 2–6 as +1, tens and aces as −1. Legal without a device; most casinos can still bar counters.

ExampleAgainst a good game, a skilled counter's edge is about 0.5–1.5%, with large swings.

TagsBlackjack

Related True count, Index plays, Penetration, Advantage play

Carnival game

AKA Carnival games

Covers Table poker games

A table game, often poker-flavored, played against the dealer with fixed rules and paytables, such as Ultimate Texas Hold'em and Three Card Poker. There is no opponent to outplay or bluff; the house edge is built into the rules.

TagsCarnival games

Related Ante (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Ante (Three Card Poker), Element of risk

Cash game

AKA Ring game

Covers Live game

Poker played for the chips on the table, which stand for real money. Players can buy in, top up and leave whenever they like, and the blinds stay fixed.

TagsPoker

Related Tournament, Rake

Check

AKA Checking

Pass without betting and keep your cards, allowed only while no one has bet in the round. Before the flop the blinds count as bets, so only the big blind can check, and only if no one raised.

TagsPoker

Related Blinds, Bet, Call, Betting actions

Come bet

A pass line bet made after the point is set (as opposed to don't come, which bets against the shooter). The next roll is its own come-out, and any point number moves it to that number. House edge 1.41%, the same as pass.

TagsCraps

Related Don't come bet, Pass line, Free odds

Come-out roll

AKA Come-out

Covers Natural · Craps · Crap out

The first roll of a new round. For pass-line bets, a 7 or 11 (a natural) wins, a 2, 3 or 12 (craps) loses, and any other number becomes the point.

TagsCraps

Related Point, Pass line, Don't pass

Commission-free baccarat

AKA No-commission baccarat

Covers EZ Baccarat · Dragon 7 · Panda 8

Versions that drop the 5% Banker commission and instead make a winning Banker three-card 7 a push. The Banker edge stays about the same, roughly 1.02%; the side bets that come with it, Dragon 7 and Panda 8, cost 7.61% and 10.19%.

TagsBaccarat

Related Banker, Side bet

Community cards

AKA Board · The board · Shared cards

The face-up cards in the middle of the table that every player can use, five in hold'em, together called the board. Ultimate Texas Hold'em uses the same board, played against the dealer.

TagsPoker

Related Preflop, flop, turn and river, Hole cards, Play bet (Ultimate Texas Hold'em)

Comps

AKA Complimentaries

Covers Player's club · Cash-back · Points · Mailers

Rewards a casino gives players based on how much they bet: points, cash-back, meals, rooms. They are a partial rebate on expected losses, not winnings; on video poker they are often worth more than the house edge itself.

TagsGeneral

Related Advantage play, Whale, Video poker

Continuation bet

AKA C-bet · Cbet

A bet on the flop by the player who made the last raise before it, continuing to represent a strong hand whether or not the flop helped.

TagsPoker

Related Bet, Bluff, Fold equity

Cooler

A hand in which two very strong hands meet and the loser couldn't reasonably have folded, such as a full house against four of a kind. Unlike a bad beat, no one played badly.

TagsPoker

Related Bad beat, The nuts

Cost per hour

AKA Hourly cost · Expected loss per hour

Covers Hands per hour · Pace · Bleed

What a game costs in expectation for an hour of play: the house edge times the bet times the decisions per hour. Speed is a hidden multiplier, so pace matters as much as edge.

FormulaCost per hour = house edge × bet × decisions per hour

ExampleSlots: $2.50 a spin × 600 spins × 10% = $150 an hour. Blackjack: $25 a hand × 70 hands × 0.5% ≈ $9.

In the marketA low fee charged on high turnover can cost more than a high fee charged rarely.

TagsGeneral

Related House edge, Mini-baccarat, Penetration

D

Dealer qualifies

AKA Qualify

Covers Dealer doesn't qualify

The minimum hand the dealer needs: a pair in Ultimate Texas Hold'em, queen-high in Three Card Poker. If it misses, in the first the Ante pushes and the raise still wins or loses; in the second the Ante pays even money and the Play pushes.

TagsCarnival games

Related Ante (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Ante (Three Card Poker), Push

Degen

AKA Degenerate · Degenerate gambler

Short for degenerate gambler: someone who will bet on anything, at any odds, usually more than is wise. Among players it is often said with affection, and often about oneself.

TagsGeneral

Related Tilt, Whale

Deuces Wild

Covers Wild card

A video poker game in which every 2 is wild and stands for any card. Full-pay Deuces Wild returns 100.76% with perfect play, a player edge; full-pay versions are rare. Other video poker games, such as Double Bonus, pay more for four of a kind.

TagsVideo poker & slots

Related Full pay, Optimal strategy

Dice control

AKA Controlled shooting

Covers Dice setting

The claim that a practiced grip and soft throw can make 7s rarer than 1 roll in 6. No controlled study has shown a repeatable effect in a real casino; the dice must hit the textured back wall, which randomizes the bounce.

TagsCraps

Related Advantage play, Shooter

Don't come bet

AKA Don't come

A don't pass bet made after the point is set (as opposed to a come bet, which bets with the shooter). The next roll is its own come-out, and any point number moves it to that number. House edge 1.36%, the same as don't pass.

TagsCraps

Related Come bet, Don't pass, Free odds

Don't pass

AKA Don't pass bar

Covers Bar 12 · Wrong bettor · Dark side

The bet against the shooter: on the come-out, 2 or 3 wins, 12 pushes (the bar 12), and 7 or 11 loses; after that it wins if a 7 comes before the point. House edge 1.36%, slightly better than pass and unpopular at the table.

TagsCraps

Related Pass line, Come bet, Free odds

Double after split

AKA DAS

Covers NDAS

A blackjack rule that allows a double on a hand created by a split; NDAS means no doubling after a split. Without DAS, the house edge rises by about 0.14%.

TagsBlackjack

Related Resplit aces, Split, Double down, Spanish 21

Double down

AKA Double · Doubling

Doubling your bet in exchange for exactly one more card. The chart doubles where the player is the favorite, such as 11 against a dealer 6.

TagsBlackjack

Related Split, Double after split, Basic strategy

Double Exposure

Both dealer cards are dealt face up. In return the dealer wins ties except blackjack against blackjack, blackjack pays even money, and doubling and splitting are usually restricted. The edge runs from 0.26% to 1.47% depending on the posted rules.

TagsBlackjack

Related Upcard, Even money

Double-zero wheel

AKA Double zero

Covers American roulette · 00 · Triple-zero wheel · Triple zero · 000

A wheel with 38 pockets: 1 to 36, a zero and a double zero. The payouts are the same, so the edge nearly doubles, to 5.26% on every bet but the five-number bet. A triple-zero wheel, 39 pockets, costs 7.69%.

TagsRoulette

Related Single-zero wheel, Five-number bet

Drawdown

Covers Biggest drawdown · Peak to trough

The largest drop from a high point to a later low within a session: how deep a bankroll sinks before it recovers or play ends. Our simulators report it for a typical session and the size 1 session in 20 reaches or exceeds.

In the marketThe same measure as a portfolio's peak-to-trough decline.

TagsGeneral

Related Bankroll, Variance, Risk of ruin

E

Edge sorting

Reading tiny asymmetries in the printed backs of cards, after getting the dealer to turn the high cards around, to tell high from low before betting. Courts in the UK (2017) and New Jersey (2016) ruled it cheating or a breach of the gaming contract.

TagsBaccarat

Related Advantage play, Hole-carding

Element of risk

The house edge measured against all the money a player puts at risk in a round, not just the first bet. Games where you add money mid-hand quote both figures, and both are true.

ExampleUltimate Texas Hold'em: 2.19% of the ante, but 0.53% of all money bet, since an average hand ends with about 4.15 antes on the table.

TagsGeneral

Related House edge, Play bet (Ultimate Texas Hold'em), Ante (Three Card Poker)

En prison

A single-zero rule for even-money bets: on a zero, the bet is held for the next spin and returned without winnings if it wins (as opposed to la partage, which returns half at once). The edge drops to 1.35%-1.39%, depending on the rules.

TagsRoulette

Related La partage, Single-zero wheel, Outside bets

Equity

AKA Pot equity · Hand equity

Your share of the pot on average: the chance your hand wins, counting split pots as part-wins, if all the remaining cards were dealt with no more betting.

ExampleA flush draw with nine clean outs and one card to come wins 9 times in 46: about 20% equity against a made hand.

TagsPoker

Related Expected value (EV), Fold equity, Outs

Even money

AKA 1 to 1 · Even-money bet

Any 1-to-1 payout. In blackjack, the offer to take 1 to 1 on your blackjack when the dealer shows an ace, instead of 3 to 2 with the risk of a push: the same bet as insurance, at the same cost.

TagsBlackjack

Related Insurance, Blackjack (natural), Outside bets

Expected value (EV)

AKA EV · Expectation

Covers +EV · −EV · Positive expectation

The average result of a decision if it could be repeated many times: each outcome times its probability, added up. +EV means a profit on average, −EV a loss; any single result can go either way.

FormulaEV = Σ (probability of outcome × payoff)

ExampleA coin flip that wins $11 on heads and loses $10 on tails: 0.5 × $11 − 0.5 × $10 = +$0.50 a flip.

In the marketExpected return. Over one year or one hand, most of what you see is luck; over many, the expectation is what's left.

TagsGeneralPoker

Related Equity, House edge, Variance

Exploitative play

AKA Exploitative strategy

Covers Exploit

Departing from balanced play to profit from one opponent's habits: bluffing a player who folds too often, value betting one who calls too much. It earns more against weak players and leaves you open to being exploited back.

In the marketTrading a known mispricing: it pays until the other side adjusts.

TagsPoker

Related Game theory optimal (GTO), Fish

F

Field

AKA Field bet

A one-roll bet that wins on 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11 or 12 and loses on 5, 6, 7 or 8. Seven numbers win, but the four losers come up 20 times in 36.

ExampleEdge 5.56% when 2 and 12 pay double; 2.78% when 12 pays triple.

TagsCraps

Related Proposition bets, House edge

First base and third base

Covers First base · Third base · Anchor

The first seat to the dealer's left, which acts first, and the last seat, which acts just before the dealer. Where you sit doesn't change your odds, and neither does how anyone else at the table plays.

TagsBlackjack

Related Basic strategy, Hole-carding

Fish

AKA Donkey · Donk

Covers Recreational player

A weak player who loses money over time, often without knowing it. Donkey, or donk, is the ruder version.

TagsPoker

Related Shark, Whale, Exploitative play

Five-number bet

AKA Top line · Basket · 0-00-1-2-3

A bet on 0, 00, 1, 2 and 3 on a double-zero wheel, paying 6 to 1. At 7.89% it is the one bet on the layout that costs more than the wheel's 5.26%.

TagsRoulette

Related Double-zero wheel, Inside bets

Fold

AKA Folding

Covers Muck

Give up the hand and any chips already in the pot; the cards go into the muck, the pile of discards.

TagsPoker

Related Fold equity, Call, Betting actions

Fold equity

The part of a bet's value that comes from the chance opponents fold. A semi-bluff earns from both: its fold equity now and its equity when called.

FormulaEV of a bet = (chance they fold × pot) + (chance they call × EV when called)

TagsPoker

Related Semi-bluff, Equity, Bluff

Free Bet Blackjack

AKA Free Bet

Covers Lammer

The house funds free doubles on hard 9, 10 or 11 and free splits of any pair but tens, marked with a lammer: a win is paid on it, a loss costs only your own bet. The price is push 22. House edge 1.04%.

TagsBlackjack

Related Push 22, Double down, Split

Free odds

AKA Odds bet

Covers Taking odds · Laying odds · Lay odds · 3-4-5x odds

An extra bet behind a pass, come, don't pass or don't come bet once it has a number, paid at true odds: the only bet in the casino with no house edge. Tables cap it at a multiple of the line bet, such as 3-4-5x.

ExampleThe pass line alone costs 1.41%; with full 3-4-5x odds behind it, the edge on all the money bet falls to 0.37%.

In the marketA fee-free add-on sold only with a product that carries a fee.

TagsCraps

Related True odds, Pass line, Point

Full pay

AKA Full-pay

Covers Short pay · Short-pay

A machine carrying the best common version of its game's paytable, such as 9/6 Jacks or Better; a short-pay machine has one or more lines cut. Two side-by-side machines can differ by 3–5% in return.

TagsVideo poker & slots

Related 9/6, Paytable, Deuces Wild

G

Gambler's fallacy

Covers Due · Due number · Hot numbers · Hot table · Hot hand · Scorecards · Roads · Big road · Bead plate

The belief that past independent results change the next one: that red is due after a run of black, that a hot shooter will keep making points, or that a baccarat scorecard shows a pattern. Dice, wheels and RNGs have no memory.

TagsGeneral

Related Law of large numbers, Betting system, Random number generator (RNG)

Game theory optimal (GTO)

AKA GTO · Equilibrium strategy

Covers Nash equilibrium · Solver · Balanced play

A strategy no opponent can profit against by changing their own play, approximated in practice by solver software. It doesn't try to exploit anyone; it makes sure no one can exploit you.

In the marketAn efficient market: you earn from other people's mistakes, not from beating a fair price.

TagsPoker

Related Exploitative play, Range

Grinder

Covers Grind · Grinding

A player who earns a modest, steady edge through long hours and volume, closer to an office job than an adventure.

TagsPoker

Related Shark, Rounder, Sample size

H

Hand rankings

Covers Poker hands · Royal flush · Straight flush · Four of a kind · Quads · Full house · Flush · Straight · Three of a kind · Trips · Set · Two pair · One pair · High card

The order of five-card poker hands, highest first: royal flush, straight flush, four of a kind, full house, flush, straight, three of a kind, two pair, one pair, high card. The rarer the hand, the higher it ranks.

TagsPoker

Related Kicker, The nuts, Ante (Three Card Poker)

Hard hand

Covers Hard 16 · Stiff

A blackjack hand with no ace counted as 11, such as 10-6, a hard 16 (as opposed to a soft hand, where an ace counts as 11). A hard 12 to 16 is a stiff: one more card can bust it.

TagsBlackjack

Related Soft hand, Bust, Basic strategy

Hardways

Covers Hard 4 · Hard 6 · Hard 8 · Hard 10 · Easy way

A bet that 4, 6, 8 or 10 rolls as a pair, such as 3 + 3 for hard 6, before it rolls any other way (the easy way) or a 7 shows.

ExampleEdge 9.09% on hard 6 or 8, 11.11% on hard 4 or 10.

TagsCraps

Related Proposition bets, Working and off

Heater

AKA On a heater · Rush · Hot streak

A winning streak better than skill alone would explain. It ends; the edge, or the lack of one, stays.

TagsGeneral

Related Run good and run bad, Gambler's fallacy

Hit and stand

Covers Hit · Stand · Stay · Draw

The two basic choices: hit takes another card, stand keeps your total and ends your turn. The dealer has no choices; they follow the rule printed on the felt.

TagsBlackjack

Related Bust, S17 and H17, Basic strategy

Hit frequency

The share of plays that return anything at all. It says how often a game pays, not how much; on a multi-line slot many hits pay back less than the bet.

In the marketWin rate versus payoff: a strategy can be right most of the time and still lose money.

TagsGeneralVideo poker & slots

Related Losses disguised as wins, Volatility, Return to player (RTP)

Hold

AKA Slot hold

Covers Loose · Tight · Loose slots

The share of money played that a machine keeps over time, the slot version of house edge; a loose machine holds less, a tight one more. The casino sets it and doesn't display it.

ExamplePublished average holds run from about 2–4% in high-limit rooms to 10–12% on penny machines.

TagsVideo poker & slots

Related Return to player (RTP), House edge, Payline

Hole cards

AKA Pocket cards

Covers Pocket pair · Suited · Offsuit

Your private, face-down cards: two in Texas hold'em. A pair dealt as hole cards is a pocket pair; two cards of the same suit are suited, two of different suits offsuit.

TagsPoker

Related Community cards, Texas hold'em

Hole-carding

AKA Hole carding

Seeing a dealer's face-down card that a careless dealer exposes. One known card turns Ultimate Texas Hold'em or Three Card Poker into a player edge. In Nevada, seeing it with your own eyes and no device isn't cheating; casinos still remove such players.

TagsCarnival games

Related Advantage play, Upcard, Edge sorting

House edge

AKA House advantage

Covers Edge · Player edge

The share of each bet the casino keeps on average over the long run, built in by paying winners less than the true odds. It is the price of the game, quoted on the amount first bet unless stated otherwise.

FormulaHouse edge = −(expected value of the bet ÷ amount bet)

ExampleOn a double-zero roulette wheel the edge is 5.26%: $100 a spin costs $5.26 a spin on average, though each spin wins or loses outright.

In the marketAn expense ratio: small, certain and charged on every bet, whatever the result.

TagsGeneral

Related True odds, Return to player (RTP), Element of risk, Cost per hour

I

ICM (Independent Chip Model)

AKA ICM · Independent Chip Model

A model that converts tournament chip stacks into shares of the prize pool. Because prizes are paid in steps, the chips you could lose are worth more than the chips you could win, so near the prizes players should risk fewer of them.

In the marketDiminishing marginal utility of money, made into a formula.

TagsPoker

Related Tournament, Expected value (EV)

Implied odds

Covers Reverse implied odds

Pot odds plus the money you expect to win on later streets if you hit. A call that is too expensive on today's pot can still pay if a made hand will get paid off later.

TagsPoker

Related Pot odds, Outs

Index plays

AKA Deviations

Covers Illustrious 18 · Fab 4 · Index numbers

Departures from basic strategy at set true counts, such as standing on 16 against a 10 at a true count of 0 or higher. The Illustrious 18 and the Fab 4 surrenders capture most of the count's playing value; insurance is the most valuable one.

TagsBlackjack

Related True count, Basic strategy, Insurance

Inside bets

Covers Straight up · Single number · Split · Corner

Bets on the numbered grid: a single number pays 35 to 1, a split (two adjacent numbers) 17 to 1, a corner (four numbers) 8 to 1. Bigger payouts, the same edge as every other bet on that wheel.

TagsRoulette

Related Outside bets, True odds

Insurance

A side bet, offered when the dealer shows an ace, that the hidden card is a ten. It pays 2 to 1 on up to half your bet, on roughly a 9-to-4 shot: about a 7.4% house edge in a six-deck game.

TagsBlackjack

Related Even money, Index plays, Upcard

J

Jacks or Better

AKA JoB

FormulaBets until the edge is undeniable ≈ (1.96 × SD per bet ÷ house edge)²
