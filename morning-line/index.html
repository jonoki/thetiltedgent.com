The Morning Line

The odds, posted before the bell.

At the racetrack, the morning line is the first set of odds, posted before a dollar is bet. This is ours for the stock market: what's on the calendar, what the news says and the prices traders have circled, every weekday before the 9:30 a.m. ET open. A read on the field, not a tip on the winner.

What matters today

The morning's news in three lines: what traders are talking about before the open, and why it could move prices. The lines are written by AI from the headlines further down; each one links to the stories it draws on.

The calendar

Scheduled economic reports, times in Eastern. Before each one, economists publish their average forecast, the consensus: it's the line. Prices already reflect it, so markets move on the gap between the actual number and the consensus, not on the number itself.

Earnings

Big companies report their quarterly results either before the 9:30 a.m. open or after the 4 p.m. close. One giant's numbers can move its whole industry, and the index futures with it.

The levels

Futures are contracts to buy or sell a stock index at a set price on a later date. They trade almost around the clock, from 6 p.m. ET to 5 p.m. the next day, so they show where the market is leaning before the stock exchanges open. The prices below are the ones traders mark on their charts: the last regular session's high, low and close, and the overnight range. They are reference points, not predictions or signals.
