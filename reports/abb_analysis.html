01 Company Overview

ABB is a Zurich-based maker of the equipment that moves and controls electricity inside buildings, factories, ships, grids and — increasingly — data centers: switchgear, circuit breakers and uninterruptible power supplies (Electrification), electric motors, drives and power converters (Motion), and the control systems that run process plants, ports and vessels (Automation). It was formed in 1988 when Sweden's ASEA merged with Switzerland's Brown, Boveri & Cie, and it reports in US dollars. Over the twelve months to September 30, 2026, continuing operations booked revenue of $36.96B, up 14.9% on the year before (ABB's rolling twelve-month figures), and its return on capital employed reached 29.9%. In the first nine months of 2026 its Operational EBITA was $6.00B, a 21.4% margin.

The ABB of 2026 is a narrower company than the one most readers remember. The power-grid business went to Hitachi (80.1% in July 2020, the rest in 2022), the turbocharger business was spun off as Accelleron in October 2022, and in October 2025 ABB agreed to sell its robot-arm division to SoftBank Group for an enterprise value of $5.375B instead of listing it separately. Robotics is reported as a discontinued operation; at the Q3 results ABB expected the sale to close in the fourth quarter of 2026 and to book a gain of about $2.4B on it. What remains is a pure electrification-and-automation business. The market paid up for it into the summer and has since marked it down: the shares closed at CHF 68.92 on October 22, 2026, 21.3% below the June 2026 month-end peak of CHF 87.58 but still 16.4% above CHF 59.22 at the end of 2025 and about 2.4 times the October 2021 month-end, at 30.4 times trailing earnings.

The latest quarter (Q3 2026, reported October 20 before the Swiss open) shows the gap between the business and the share price. Orders reached a record $12.06B, up 40% (40% comparable), edging up from Q2 where the season usually brings a dip, with data-center orders again growing at a triple-digit rate and Electrification orders up 70%. Revenue rose 14% to $9.70B and Operational EBITA rose 24% to $2.03B, a 20.8% margin, up 160 bps. But basic earnings per share fell 2% to $0.65, below the $0.79 consensus reported by Investing.com, mostly because of a roughly $115M non-cash loss on the currency hedge ABB holds for its Rotork purchase. ABB nudged its full-year growth guidance up to “low teens”. The stock fell 5.0% on the day, from CHF 74.70 to CHF 70.96, and Reuters quoted analysts who put the drop down to nervousness about AI spending rather than the numbers. The debate is not whether ABB is executing — it plainly is — but how long the data-center build-out lasts and what investors will pay for it.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base across grids, plants & ships Medium-voltage technology (UPS, switchgear) Global manufacturing & service footprint $32.3B order backlog Pricing power (just above 2% in Q3) Low leverage (net debt/EBITDA 0.2x)

ABB's advantage is less any single product than being specified in. Electrical equipment that sits inside a hospital, a ship, a mine or a data hall is chosen early, certified, and then maintained for decades, which is why ABB can push price (net price increases of just above 2% in Q3) without losing volume, and why a 29.9% return on capital employed is possible in a business that makes physical hardware. Management's account of Q3 is that it is adding capacity step by step rather than in big leaps: capital spending runs at about $1B a year, which ABB does not expect to rise further in the medium term, alongside automation of its own plants and some outsourced assembly. The vulnerability is the mirror image — the strongest growth is concentrated in one customer group, data centers, whose spending cycle and power architecture can change faster than an equipment maker's factories; on results day, analysts quoted by Reuters said investors were worried about the debts AI cloud providers are taking on to build them.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The price still assumes a lot, and the mood has turned. At 30.4x trailing and 23.3x 2027 consensus earnings (both in CHF at the October 22 close) the stock is cheaper than at the previous edition's 35.6x, but still priced for the order surge to keep turning into revenue. On results day an analyst at Zürcher Kantonalbank told Reuters: “There is a feeling of insecurity and uncertainty in the market that even good numbers may not be good enough.”
02
Data-center concentration and the switch to direct current. Data-center orders grew at a triple-digit rate again in Q3 and drove Electrification's 70% order growth. Next-generation AI facilities are moving toward 800-volt DC power; ABB's Infinitus portfolio is its entry, with the solid-state transformer at its core commercially available only in the second half of 2027, and ABB expects the first fully DC-native facilities using the whole portfolio within two to three years of the September launch. Winning that transition is not guaranteed.
03
Margin leakage below the headline. Group gross margin slipped 60bps to 40.6% in Q3 (130bps to 40.0% over nine months), with unrealised currency and commodity hedges the largest drag; Motion's fell 260bps and Automation's 180bps. Below operating profit, net income fell 2% while Operational EBITA rose 24%: a roughly $115M non-cash loss on the Rotork currency hedge pushed net finance into a $127M expense, and discontinued operations lost $43M, including a tax charge from setting up the Robotics business's legal structure ahead of the sale.
04
Portfolio reshaping in flight. The Robotics sale (now expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026), the Rotork purchase (first half of 2027) and the new Mitsubishi Electric joint venture (second half of 2027) overlap. Motion is still absorbing a weak acquisition — Gamesa Electric cost about 40bps of its Q3 margin — and each deal brings integration work while ABB is also adding factory capacity.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the 2026 figures. First, real-estate gains: Q1 2026 Operational EBITA included a $377M net gain on a property sale booked in Corporate and Other, which lifts the nine-month margin to 21.4% and the trailing earnings behind the P/E; a second sale of Swiss commercial properties, completed October 13, 2026, brings a pre-tax gain of about CHF 240M in Q4. Second, Robotics sits in discontinued operations: its results are in net income and EPS but not in revenue or Operational EBITA. In Q3 discontinued operations lost $43M, including a tax charge from setting up the business's legal structure ahead of the sale; the divestment also leaves stranded corporate costs ($25M in Q3, about $100M guided for the year), and ABB expects a gain of about $2.4B when the sale closes, which its guidance excludes. Third, the Rotork currency hedge: Q3 net finance expense of $127M included about $115M of non-cash revaluation on the contract that fixes the currency cost of the sterling purchase — the main reason basic EPS fell 2% while Operational EBITA rose 24%. Fourth, nine-month income from operations carried about $100M of provisions for legacy claims and remediations. Separately, ABB's issued share count fell from 1,844 million at the end of 2025 to 1,823 million at September 30, 2026 as it cancelled repurchased shares, and under the programme of up to $2B launched February 9, 2026 it bought 5,726,505 shares for about $565M in Q3.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Orders are running well ahead of revenue — a 1.24 book-to-bill, a $32.3B backlog up 37% comparable, and Electrification orders up 70% — so the next several quarters of revenue are largely already booked. ABB delivered what it guided for Q3 (14% comparable growth, a higher margin than Q2) and nudged full-year guidance up again; the CEO says data-center demand is still accelerating. Returns are high (29.9% ROCE), leverage is minimal (net debt 0.2x EBITDA), and the Robotics sale, now due in Q4, frees cash for Rotork and the Mitsubishi Electric venture. The share price has fallen 21% from its June month-end peak while orders and margins rose, so at 30.4x trailing and 23.3x 2027 earnings less of the growth is in the price than in the summer. If the 800 VDC transition arrives on the timeline ABB describes, Infinitus gives it a seat in the next generation of AI data centers too.

Bear Case

Even after the fall the market is paying a premium for the surge, which is why ten of thirteen firms in MarketBeat's panel say Hold and why the stock has now fallen on both of its last two record quarters (−5.9% in July, −5.0% in October). Growth leans on one customer group, data centers, whose spending depends on AI cloud providers that investors now worry are over-borrowed, and whose shift to direct current is a technology race in which ABB's key product arrives only in late 2027. Earnings per share fell as hedging losses hit, gross margin is drifting down, Motion's margin fell again, Automation orders were flat, and the company is closing one large disposal while buying Rotork and a stake in a Japanese joint venture. At a premium price, any one of these could lower the multiple of earnings investors are willing to pay.

Top 3 Risks

01
A data-center slowdown at a premium multiple. Triple-digit data-center order growth drove Q2 and Q3. A pause in hyperscale or AI-cloud spending would hit Electrification's order rate first, and at 30.4x trailing earnings the share price still has limited cushion; the October sell-off showed how quickly worries about how AI projects are financed reach ABB's shares.
02
Price/cost and execution. Group gross margin fell 60bps in Q3 and 130bps over nine months. Motion's Operational EBITA margin fell 100bps in Q3 after 130bps in Q2, with Gamesa Electric still weighing. ABB guides the Q4 margin to soften by less than usual; if it softens more, the full-year margin target of at least +270bps comes under pressure.
03
Deal timing and integration. The Robotics sale (expected Q4 2026), Rotork (H1 2027) and the Mitsubishi Electric venture (H2 2027) overlap. A delay in one leaves ABB carrying more at once, and until Rotork closes the currency hedge on its price can move reported earnings, as it did by about $115M in Q3.
Industry Commentary
Power demand from AI data centers
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Every AI data center is first an electrical project: medium-voltage switchgear to take power from the grid, UPS systems to keep it clean, distribution down to the rack, and cooling. ABB's Q3 shows how fast that market is still growing — data-center orders up at a triple-digit rate for another quarter, Americas Electrification orders up 117% — and the CEO told reporters the value of its Q3 data-center orders was the highest yet (Reuters). The next step is architectural: ABB's Infinitus launch is built for 800 VDC distribution and solid-state transformers, which it presents as the way to power next-generation AI chips efficiently; its solid-state transformer becomes commercially available in the second half of 2027, so the business of the next two years is still largely the AC equipment ABB already sells. The new question is money rather than demand: on results day analysts told Reuters that investors were worried about the debts AI cloud providers are taking on, and Reuters cited Gartner's forecast that 80% of such “neocloud” providers will cease independent operations by 2030.

Input costs, tariffs and pricing
ONGOING HEADWIND

Electrical equipment is metal-intensive. In Q3 ABB says positive pricing and efficiency gains more than offset inflation in commodities and freight at group level, with no material net impact from tariffs; net pricing was just above 2%. Gross margin still fell 60bps, mostly on unrealised hedging losses, and in Motion and Automation higher input costs and mix ate the benefit of price increases. ABB's answer is volume leverage and cost discipline: selling, general and administrative costs fell to 17.8% of revenue from 19.1%. The structural point is that in a supply-constrained market the industry can raise prices; the open question for each quarter is whether the increases keep up with metal prices.

Portfolio focus and the valuation that comes with it
VALUATION

ABB has spent six years narrowing to electrification and automation: Power Grids to Hitachi, Accelleron spun off, Robotics sold to SoftBank, and now Rotork, the Mitsubishi Electric low-voltage venture and smaller deals added. The bull framing: a focused company with 29.9% ROCE and a record backlog deserves the multiple peers such as Eaton and Schneider Electric command, and the share price since 2021 says the market agrees. The bear framing: focus concentrates the bet; a company priced at 30.4x trailing earnings needs data-center demand to keep compounding and three deals to go cleanly at the same time. October gave the bears the price move and the bulls the numbers; the Q4 print on January 28, 2027 is the next data point on both.
