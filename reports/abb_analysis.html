01 Company Overview

ABB is a Zurich-based maker of the equipment that moves and controls electricity inside buildings, factories, ships, grids and — increasingly — data centers: switchgear, circuit breakers and uninterruptible power supplies (Electrification), electric motors, drives and power converters (Motion), and the control systems that run process plants, ports and vessels (Automation). It was formed in 1988 when Sweden's ASEA merged with Switzerland's Brown, Boveri & Cie, and it reports in US dollars. Over the twelve months to June 30, 2026, continuing operations booked revenue of $35.75B, up 14.4% on the year before, with an Operational EBITA of $7.20B and a return on capital employed of 28.4%.

The ABB of 2026 is a narrower company than the one most readers remember. The power-grid business went to Hitachi (80.1% in July 2020, the rest in 2022), the turbocharger business was spun off as Accelleron in October 2022, and in October 2025 ABB agreed to sell its robot-arm division to SoftBank Group for an enterprise value of $5.375B instead of listing it separately. Robotics is now reported as a discontinued operation, and at the July 16 results ABB expected the sale to close in the second half of 2026, bringing in about $4.8B of net cash. What remains is a pure electrification-and-automation business, and the market has paid up for it: the shares closed at CHF 81.90 on October 2, 2026, up 38.3% from CHF 59.22 at the end of 2025 and almost three times the October 2021 month-end, at 35.6 times trailing earnings.

The latest quarter (Q2 2026, reported July 16) explains both the rally and the hesitation. Orders hit a record $12.04B, up 28% on a comparable basis, driven by data centers, where orders grew at a triple-digit rate; revenue rose 12% comparable to $9.48B; the Operational EBITA margin widened 90 bps to 20.2%; and management raised its full-year growth guidance. The same morning ABB agreed to buy UK valve-actuator maker Rotork for about $5.5B in cash. The stock fell 5.9% that day, from CHF 83.18 to CHF 78.26. The debate is not whether ABB is executing — it plainly is — but how much of a data-center build-out the price already assumes.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base across grids, plants & ships Medium-voltage technology (UPS, switchgear) Global manufacturing & service footprint $30.0B order backlog Pricing power (~2% in Q2) Low leverage (net debt/EBITDA 0.3x)

ABB's advantage is less any single product than being specified in. Electrical equipment that sits inside a hospital, a ship, a mine or a data hall is chosen early, certified, and then maintained for decades, which is why ABB can push price (close to 2% in Q2) without losing volume, and why a 28.4% return on capital employed is possible in a business that makes physical hardware. The CEO's own account of the quarter is that capacity, not demand, is the constraint: lead times are unchanged and the order surge reflects "more capacity online". The vulnerability is the mirror image — the strongest growth is concentrated in one customer group, data centers, whose spending cycle and power architecture can change faster than an equipment maker's factories.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The price already assumes a lot. At 35.6x trailing and 28.1x 2027 consensus earnings (both in CHF at the October 2 close), the stock is priced for the order surge to keep converting. Deutsche Bank kept a Hold on September 22 even while raising its target, on valuation grounds — about 20x estimated 2027 EV/EBITA — and warned that ABB would likely struggle to beat already elevated buy-side order expectations in Q3.
02
Data-center concentration and the switch to direct current. Data-center orders grew at a triple-digit rate in Q2 and were the main driver of Electrification's sequential gain. Next-generation AI facilities are moving toward 800-volt DC power, where ABB had no orders at the Q2 call; its Infinitus portfolio, launched September 21, expects the first fully DC-native facilities using it within two to three years. Winning that transition is not guaranteed.
03
Margin leakage below the headline. Group gross margin slipped 50bps to 40.0% in Q2, mainly on unrealised FX and commodity hedges; Electrification's fell 140bps, with raw-material costs also running ahead of prices, and Motion's 120bps, mainly on the weak Gamesa acquisition and the same hedges. Income from operations grew only 8% against a 20% rise in Operational EBITA, after about $100M of provisions for legacy claims and remediation, about $60M from hedge mark-to-market and about $30M on an equity investment.
04
Portfolio reshaping in flight. The Robotics sale (expected to close in the second half of 2026) and the Rotork purchase (expected in the first half of 2027) run in parallel, with ABB funding the latter from the former. Motion is already absorbing a weak acquisition — Gamesa Electric cost about 70bps of its Q2 margin — and 40% of Rotork's business is oil and gas — though the CEO put the addition to ABB's overall oil-and-gas exposure at about 0.5 percentage points.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the 2026 figures. First, the real-estate gain: Q1 2026 Operational EBITA included a $377M net gain on a property sale booked in Corporate and Other, which lifts the first-half margin to 21.8% and the trailing earnings behind the P/E. Second, Robotics sits in discontinued operations: its profits are in net income and EPS but not in revenue or Operational EBITA, and the divestment leaves stranded corporate costs ($25M in Q2, about $100M guided for the year). Third, Q2 income from operations carried about $100M of provisions for unasserted legacy claims and remediations, about $60M from marking FX and commodity hedges to market and about $30M on an equity investment — the reason operating income grew 8% while Operational EBITA grew 20%. Separately, ABB cancelled 20,744,831 shares bought under its 2025 buyback in April 2026 and launched a new programme of up to $2B on February 9, 2026, under which it had bought 9,865,326 shares by September 30.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Orders are running well ahead of revenue — a 1.27 book-to-bill, a $30.0B backlog up 28% comparable, and record orders in Electrification and Motion — so the next several quarters of revenue are largely already booked. Management says this is capacity coming online, not customers ordering early, and has raised guidance; Q3 is guided to low- to mid-teens comparable growth with a higher margin. Returns are high (28.4% ROCE), leverage is minimal (net debt 0.3x EBITDA), and the Robotics proceeds of about $4.8B fund Rotork without stretching the balance sheet. If the 800 VDC transition arrives on the timeline ABB describes, Infinitus gives it a seat in the next generation of AI data centers too.

Bear Case

At 35.6x trailing and 28.1x 2027 earnings the market is already paying for the surge, which is why ten of thirteen firms in MarketBeat's panel say Hold and why the stock fell 5.9% on a record quarter. Growth leans on one customer group, data centers, whose spending is lumpy and whose shift to direct current is a technology race ABB had no orders in at the Q2 call. Gross margin is drifting down as costs outrun prices, Motion's margin fell 130bps on a weak acquisition, Automation orders fell 14% comparable, and the company is closing one large disposal while buying a $5.5B business, 40% of it oil and gas. Any one of these at a premium multiple is a de-rating risk.

Top 3 Risks

01
A data-center slowdown at a peak multiple. Triple-digit data-center order growth drove Q2. A pause in hyperscale or co-location spending would hit Electrification's order rate first, and at 35.6x trailing earnings the share price has little cushion. Deutsche Bank already flagged the risk that Q3 orders fall short of buy-side expectations.
02
Price/cost and execution. Group gross margin fell 50bps in Q2 and 170bps in the first half, and the CFO said a gap between prices and input costs remains. Motion's margin fell 130bps on Gamesa Electric and Traction delays. If the second-half improvement management expects does not arrive, the margin guidance comes under pressure.
03
Deal timing and integration. The Robotics sale (expected H2 2026) funds Rotork (expected H1 2027). A delay in one leaves ABB carrying both, and Rotork's oil-and-gas weighting adds cyclical exposure to an Automation division whose process customers are already soft.
Industry Commentary
Power demand from AI data centers
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Every AI data center is first an electrical project: medium-voltage switchgear to take power from the grid, UPS systems to keep it clean, distribution down to the rack, and cooling. ABB's Q2 shows how fast that market is growing — data-center orders up at a triple-digit rate, Americas Electrification orders up 114% — and the CEO said on the call that ABB has gained market share in medium-voltage UPS in particular. The next step is architectural: ABB's Infinitus launch is built for 800 VDC distribution and solid-state transformers, which it presents as the way to power next-generation AI chips efficiently. ABB had no 800 VDC orders at the Q2 call and dates commercial impact to late 2027 or 2028 onward, so the business of the next two years is still largely the AC equipment it already sells.

Input costs, tariffs and pricing
ONGOING HEADWIND

Electrical equipment is metal-intensive, and ABB's Q2 release lists commodities and tariffs among the costs it had to offset. Pricing added close to 2% to revenue, but gross margin still fell 50bps, with the gap largest in Electrification. ABB's answer is volume leverage and SG&A discipline: SG&A fell to 18.2% of revenue from 20.1%. The structural point is that in a supply-constrained market the industry can raise prices; the open question for each quarter is whether the increases keep up with metal prices.

Portfolio focus and the valuation that comes with it
RE-RATING

ABB has spent six years narrowing to electrification and automation: Power Grids to Hitachi, Accelleron spun off, Robotics sold to SoftBank, and now Rotork and smaller deals added (three announced acquisitions together worth about 3.5% of 2025 revenue, including Rotork). The bull framing: a focused company with 28.4% ROCE and a record backlog deserves the multiple peers such as Eaton and Schneider Electric command, and the share price since 2021 says the market agrees. The bear framing: focus concentrates the bet; a company priced at 35.6x trailing earnings needs data-center demand to keep compounding and the Rotork integration to go cleanly at the same time. The Q3 print on October 20 is the next data point on both.
