01 Company Overview
Abbott Laboratories is a diversified healthcare company operating four reporting segments — Medical Devices, Diagnostics, Nutrition and Established Pharmaceutical Products — selling in roughly 160 countries. On October 21, 2026 it announced plans to separate Nutrition into its own publicly traded company by the end of 2027. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $44.33B, up 5.7% reported and 5.5% organic (6.7% organic excluding COVID-19 testing), with GAAP diluted EPS of $3.72 and adjusted diluted EPS of $5.15. On a TTM basis through Q3 2026, revenue has reached $48.23B (+10.0% YoY), boosted by the March 2026 acquisition of Exact Sciences.
The company ranks #66 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization at $181.48B. That capitalization is down roughly 17.5% over the past year (the October 27, 2025 close was $127.18; the October 26, 2026 close is $104.88) — an unusual outcome for a company compounding organic sales in the mid-single digits, and the central tension in the story. Growth is concentrated in Medical Devices (48% of FY2025 revenue, +11.9% organic), led by the FreeStyle Libre continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) franchise and cardiovascular devices. Diagnostics shrank through 2025 as COVID-19 testing normalized and China's volume-based procurement (VBP) programs, in which the government pools hospital purchases to push prices down, compressed reagent pricing; it grew 6.4% comparable in Q3 2026. Overhanging all of it are the necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) preterm infant-formula lawsuits: Abbott agreed on August 20, 2026 to settlements totalling about $670M, but roughly 1,700 lawsuits remain.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
CGM Scale (~53%) Sensor Manufacturing Cost Curve Physician & Hospital Relationships Regulatory Approval Barriers Installed Instrument Base Consumer Brand Equity 54-Year Dividend Record
Abbott's moat is unusually well-diversified for a medtech company, and its strongest single pillar is manufacturing scale in continuous glucose monitoring. With roughly 52.8% of a $15.77B global CGM market versus Dexcom at 33.9% and Medtronic at 10.1%, Abbott produces sensors at a unit cost that competitors have struggled to match, which is precisely why Libre has been able to attack the over-the-counter and non-intensive-insulin segments on price while preserving segment margin. Sensors are ~89% of CGM revenue and are consumed on a recurring 14-day cycle, so share won today keeps paying with every replacement sensor. In diagnostics, the same razor/razorblade logic applies through the installed base of Alinity analyzers, where switching costs are measured in lab workflow revalidation rather than list price. In Established Pharmaceuticals, the moat is brand trust in markets with variable generic quality — a genuinely durable asset that is hard for a US-centric competitor to replicate. And the corporate-level moat is the balance sheet and the 54 consecutive years of dividend increases, which keeps Abbott in the dividend-growth and quality-focused funds that favor companies with records like this. What the moat does not do is protect near-term earnings from litigation, currency, or Chinese procurement policy — three forces that have behind the stock's roughly 17.5% decline over the past twelve months ($127.18 on October 27, 2025 to $104.88) despite mid-single-digit organic growth.
Key Vulnerabilities

NEC infant-formula litigation — partly settled, still open. NEC is necrotizing enterocolitis, a serious intestinal disease in premature babies; plaintiffs allege Abbott's preterm formula causes it, and Abbott denies that. Abbott absorbed a $495M Missouri verdict in July 2024 (Gill v. Abbott: $95M compensatory plus $400M punitive) and a $70M Illinois verdict in April 2026 across four consolidated cases ($53M compensatory, $17M punitive). On August 20, 2026 — two days after the previous edition of this report — Abbott announced agreements resolving the Gill case and NEC claims for approximately 2,000 additional infants for about $670M in aggregate. Abbott said the agreements are "a compromise of disputed claims and not in any way an admission of liability" and that roughly 1,700 lawsuits involving approximately 12,700 infants remain pending in federal and state courts. The Q3 2026 results carry about $670M of legal settlements among the items Abbott excludes from adjusted earnings. The one other favorable development is the June 2026 reversal on appeal of a $60M verdict against co-defendant Mead Johnson, where the Illinois Appellate Court's Fifth District held that the learned intermediary doctrine applied and remanded for a new trial. On the Q3 call, management said both Abbott and the new nutrition company would be well capitalized to keep defending the remaining cases, and that resolving them is not a condition of completing the separation. Abbott has not given an estimate of what the remaining ~12,700 claims could cost; any single figure you see for that is an assumption, not a disclosure.

CGM growth deceleration and competitive intensity. Diabetes Care organic growth fell from +16.3% in FY2025 to +9.0% in Q2 2026 and +6.7% comparable in Q3; CGM specifically grew 7.7% comparable in Q3. Management now expects low-to-mid single-digit CGM growth for the next few quarters. As of the previous edition, Dexcom was growing total revenue at 15.5% off a $4.97B base and spending aggressively on direct-to-consumer marketing; Medtronic held 10.1% share and is integrated into pump systems. The bull case requires the OTC (no-prescription) and dual glucose-ketone products — Libre Duo won FDA authorization in August 2026 — to open non-diabetic and wellness demand faster than the core insulin-user market saturates. If they do not, Abbott is left with a decelerating franchise carrying a premium multiple.

Diagnostics: policy and pricing, not demand. Most of the Diagnostics reset was arithmetic: COVID testing sales fell $450M from 2024 to 2025, more than the segment's whole sales decline of about $400M, and that drag has annualized away. China is the part that has not: China's volume-based procurement programs structurally reprice reagents, and management has called it out explicitly. Rapid/Molecular fell 8.0% comparable in Q2 2026 and grew 0.9% in Q3. The Exact Sciences acquisition reframes the segment around oncology screening, but it also means Diagnostics' reported growth embeds acquisition accounting — Q3 reported growth of 46.7% against comparable growth of 6.4% is a 40-point gap that will flatter comparisons until March 2027.

Balance sheet and earnings quality post-Exact Sciences. At June 30, 2026 (the latest balance sheet; the Q3 10-Q is not yet filed) debt-to-equity was 0.63 against 0.27 at FY2025, current ratio 1.38 against 1.58 and quick ratio 0.80 against 1.02. ROIC (trailing, per stockanalysis.com) dropped from 12.14% to 7.25% and ROE from 12.96% to 10.58%, mechanically, because the acquired goodwill and intangibles enter the capital base immediately while the earnings contribution phases in. The gap between Q3 GAAP EPS of $0.53 and adjusted EPS of $1.42 — specified items of $0.89 a share after tax, including $841M of intangible amortization and about $670M of legal settlements — and between 50.3% reported and 57.2% adjusted gross margin is now largely acquisition amortization plus the NEC settlements. Investors have to decide how much of that adjustment to accept.

Notable One-Off Items

Abbott's reported earnings are currently distorted by an unusually large number of non-recurring items, and taking GAAP figures at face value will mislead you in both directions.

1. FY2024's outsized net income. FY2024 net income of $13.40B produced a 31.94% net margin and $7.64 diluted EPS on only $41.95B of revenue — roughly double the underlying earnings power. This reflected a large non-recurring benefit, not operating performance. Every "net income down 61.2% YoY" headline you see on ABT is measured against that inflated base and is close to meaningless.

2. Exact Sciences acquisition accounting. The deal closed March 23, 2026. It adds purchase-accounting intangible amortization ($841M in Q3 2026) that drove the gap between Q3 GAAP EPS of $0.53 and adjusted EPS of $1.42, and between 50.3% reported and 57.2% adjusted gross margin. It also inflates reported Diagnostics growth (+46.7% reported vs +6.4% comparable in Q3) and mechanically depressed ROIC, ROE, current ratio and quick ratio. None of this is operational deterioration; all of it flows through the headline numbers.

3. NEC litigation charges. A $495M Missouri verdict (July 2024) and a $70M Illinois verdict (April 2026) are known adverse judgments, and on August 20, 2026 Abbott agreed to about $670M of settlements covering the Gill case and claims for roughly 2,000 more infants; that amount sits in the Q3 2026 specified items. Abbott has not published a reserve for the roughly 1,700 lawsuits (about 12,700 infants) that remain, and legal defense costs on them are an ongoing, non-trivial operating expense.

4. COVID-19 testing normalization. COVID test sales of $297M in FY2025 versus $747M in FY2024 — a $450M year-over-year revenue decline concentrated in Rapid Diagnostics. FY2025 organic growth was 5.5% including COVID and 6.7% excluding it. This is a receding, quantifiable drag rather than a permanent impairment, and it substantially annualizes out during 2026.

5. Currency. Established Pharmaceuticals is entirely ex-US and Nutrition and Diagnostics are heavily international, so reported-versus-comparable gaps are dollar-driven. In Q3 2026 the dollar cut EPD growth (+6.8% reported vs +8.4% comparable) and Medical Devices growth (+6.9% vs +7.8%), while Nutrition was roughly neutral (+5.1% vs +4.9%). Abbott's finance chief said in the Q3 call that exchange rates should reduce fourth-quarter sales by about 1%. Treat reported growth as a currency-contaminated number and comparable growth as the operating signal.

Key near-term event
Next 12 months
Libre Duo Launch — Dual Glucose-Ketone Sensor
The FDA authorized Libre Duo in August 2026 (it had earlier received CE Mark in Europe). Abbott describes it as the world's first dual glucose-ketone biowearable sensor. Ketone sensing addresses diabetic ketoacidosis risk — a clinically differentiated claim rather than a price-driven one — and extends the sensor platform's addressable use cases beyond glucose. Combined with over-the-counter CGM availability, which removes prescription friction, this is the mechanism by which the CGM market could expand beyond people who use insulin. Management, however, expects low-to-mid single-digit CGM growth for the next few quarters.

Both target categories where Boston Scientific currently leads — LAA occlusion (Watchman) and coronary calcium modification — would add incremental growth to the cardiovascular franchise if approved.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Abbott is a 54-year dividend grower with a 0.58 beta trading at 18.0x forward earnings against $5.50–$5.55 of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance, for a business now guiding to about 6.0% comparable sales growth. It owns 52.8% of a CGM market growing at a 15.1% CAGR toward $31.83B by 2031 (third-party estimates), with Libre Duo, now FDA authorized, and OTC availability opening non-diabetic demand. Medical Devices, 48% of revenue, grew 7.8% comparable in Q3 2026 with Electrophysiology at 15.5%. Free cash flow of $7.82B ($4.52/share, trailing twelve months through Q2) covers the $2.52 dividend nearly twice over. The August settlements removed the largest NEC verdict, and the planned Nutrition separation by the end of 2027 would leave a company of medical devices, diagnostics and established pharmaceuticals. If the remaining NEC cases are contained and CGM growth stabilizes, the gap between the trailing and forward multiples narrows and the stock can be valued higher without needing any acceleration in the underlying business.

Bear Case

The growth engine is decelerating: Diabetes Care organic growth fell from +16.3% in FY2025 to +6.7% comparable in Q3 2026 and CGM specifically to 7.7%, and management expects low-to-mid single digits for the next few quarters, while Dexcom (as of the previous edition) grew 15.5% and markets aggressively direct-to-consumer. Abbott cut its full-year sales growth outlook to about 6.0% from 6.5%–7.5%. Diagnostics growth is carried by an acquisition (+46.7% reported vs +6.4% comparable in Q3). The Exact Sciences deal more than doubled leverage (D/E 0.27 → 0.63) and cut ROIC by more than a third (12.14% → 7.25%) to buy growth in the weakest segment. And roughly 1,700 NEC lawsuits (about 12,700 infants) remain after the $670M August settlements. A five-year price decline of roughly 16.6% is not an anomaly to be corrected; it may be the market correctly pricing a low-growth conglomerate with an open-ended legal liability.

  Industry Commentary
CGM Adoption Beyond Diabetes
EMERGING TAILWIND
The global continuous glucose monitoring market is estimated at $15.77B in 2026 and is projected to hit $31.83B by 2031 at a 15.1% CAGR. Concentration is extreme — Abbott 52.8%, Dexcom 33.9%, Medtronic 10.1%, or 96.8% combined — so category growth accrues almost entirely to three companies. Sensors are 89.4% of revenue with a 15.4% CAGR (transmitters grow at just 6.0%), which favors Abbott's disposable-sensor architecture. Home and personal use is 73.6% of revenue, and hospital use is the fastest-growing channel at 18.1% CAGR. Crucially, the GLP-1 thesis that crushed medtech in 2023 has inverted: providers increasingly pair CGM data with GLP-1 therapy for precision dosing, making the two complementary rather than substitutes. Over-the-counter availability removes prescription friction and is the mechanism for non-diabetic wellness adoption in North America and Europe. North America is 50.6% of global revenue; Asia-Pacific grows fastest at 15.8%.
Litigation & Policy Overhangs in Medtech
ONGOING HEADWIND
Two policy forces are compressing healthcare multiples independent of operating performance. The first is US product-liability litigation: after Abbott's August 20, 2026 settlements (about $670M), roughly 1,700 preterm-infant-nutrition lawsuits involving about 12,700 infants remain against Abbott (its own count), with verdicts of $495M and $70M against Abbott and a $60M verdict against co-defendant Mead Johnson reversed on appeal in June 2026 on learned-intermediary grounds. Markets price open-ended liabilities badly — the discount applied is usually a function of uncertainty, not expected value. The second is international pricing policy: China's volume-based procurement (VBP) programs, which management explicitly cited as constraining Core Laboratory diagnostics, structurally reprice reagents and devices in the world's second-largest healthcare market. Neither force is cyclical; both require either legal resolution or geographic mix shift to fade. Tariffs and dollar direction add further unquantified variance for a company generating the majority of its revenue outside the United States.
Procedure Volumes vs. Diversification: The Core Debate
BULL vs BEAR FRAMING
Abbott's cardiovascular growth has cooled: Structural Heart grew +11.5% organic in FY2025 and +6.9% comparable in Q3 2026, and management pointed to a slowdown in the LAA closure market for its Electrophysiology outlook. The bull reading: Abbott's four-segment diversification means no single failure is fatal, EPD compounds at 8.4% comparable with zero US pricing exposure, the balance sheet supports the 54-year dividend record, a 0.58 beta genuinely dampens portfolio volatility, and at 18.0x forward earnings you are paying less than the index for a business guiding about 6.0% comparable growth. The bear reading: diversification is precisely why the stock is down about 16.6% in five years — the growth engine (CGM) is decelerating, the full-year sales outlook was just cut, and management levered up to buy assets inside the weakest segment. The resolution comes down to whether CGM growth stabilizes, whether Diagnostics keeps growing on a comparable basis, and what the January 2027 guidance says; the Nutrition separation, due by the end of 2027, then changes what Abbott is.
