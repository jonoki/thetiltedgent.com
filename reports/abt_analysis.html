01 Company Overview
Abbott Laboratories is a diversified healthcare company operating four reporting segments — Medical Devices, Diagnostics, Nutrition and Established Pharmaceutical Products — selling in roughly 160 countries. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $44.33B, up 5.7% reported and 5.5% organic (6.7% organic excluding COVID-19 testing), with GAAP diluted EPS of $3.72 and adjusted diluted EPS of $5.15. On a TTM basis through Q2 2026, revenue has reached $46.59B (+8.1% YoY), boosted by the March 2026 acquisition of Exact Sciences.
The company ranks #107 globally and #61 in the United States by market capitalization at $194.98B. That capitalization is down roughly 14% over the past year — an unusual outcome for a company compounding organic sales in the mid-to-high single digits, and the central tension in the story. Growth is concentrated in Medical Devices (48% of FY2025 revenue, +11.9% organic), led by the FreeStyle Libre continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) franchise and cardiovascular devices, while Diagnostics has been shrinking as COVID-19 testing normalizes and China's volume-based procurement (VBP) programs compress reagent pricing. Overhanging all of it is the necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) preterm infant-formula litigation.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
CGM Scale (~53%) Sensor Manufacturing Cost Curve Physician & Hospital Relationships Regulatory Approval Barriers Installed Instrument Base Consumer Brand Equity 54-Year Dividend Record
Abbott's moat is unusually well-diversified for a medtech company, and its strongest single pillar is manufacturing scale in continuous glucose monitoring. With roughly 52.8% of a $15.77B global CGM market versus Dexcom at 33.9% and Medtronic at 10.1%, Abbott produces sensors at a unit cost that competitors have struggled to match, which is precisely why Libre has been able to attack the over-the-counter and non-intensive-insulin segments on price while preserving segment margin. Sensors are ~89% of CGM revenue and are consumed on a recurring 14-day cycle, so share won today annuitizes. In diagnostics, the same razor/razorblade logic applies through the installed base of Alinity analyzers, where switching costs are measured in lab workflow revalidation rather than list price. In Established Pharmaceuticals, the moat is brand trust in markets with variable generic quality — a genuinely durable asset that is hard for a US-centric competitor to replicate. And the corporate-level moat is the balance sheet and the 54 consecutive years of dividend increases, which fixes Abbott in dividend-growth and quality-factor index mandates and provides a persistent structural bid. What the moat does not do is protect near-term earnings from litigation, currency, or Chinese procurement policy — three forces that have driven the stock's 14% twelve-month decline despite mid-single-digit organic growth.
Key Vulnerabilities

NEC infant-formula litigation — the material overhang. MDL 3026 (Abbott Laboratories et al., Preterm Infant Nutrition) held approximately 779 pending cases as of the most recent update. Abbott has already absorbed a $495M Missouri verdict in July 2024 (Gill v. Abbott: $95M compensatory plus $400M punitive) and a $70M Illinois verdict in April 2026 across four consolidated cases ($53M compensatory, $17M punitive). No global settlement has been announced as of August 2026, and the litigation remains in active bellwether trial phase. The one genuinely favorable development is the June 2026 reversal on appeal of a $60M verdict against co-defendant Mead Johnson, where the Fifth District Court of Appeal held that the learned intermediary doctrine applied and remanded for a new trial — a ruling that, if followed, materially weakens the plaintiff theory for hospital-administered products. Honest framing of the exposure: with ~779 cases pending and per-case verdicts ranging from roughly $17.5M (the Illinois average) to $495M, arithmetic extrapolation would produce a range spanning tens of billions — but bellwether verdicts are systematically unrepresentative, punitive awards are routinely reduced on appeal, and most MDLs resolve at a small fraction of headline verdicts. Any single-point liability estimate you see for Abbott's NEC exposure is an assumption, not a disclosure. Abbott has not disclosed a quantified reserve that was verifiable in this build.

CGM growth deceleration and competitive intensity. Diabetes Care organic growth fell from +16.3% in FY2025 to +9.0% in Q2 2026, and CGM specifically grew 9.5%. Dexcom is growing total revenue at 15.5% off a $4.97B base and is spending aggressively on direct-to-consumer marketing; Medtronic holds 10.1% share and is integrated into pump systems. The bull case requires the OTC and dual glucose-ketone products (Libre Duo received CE Mark in Q2 2026) to open non-diabetic and wellness demand faster than the core insulin-user market saturates. If the OTC category grows more slowly than expected, Abbott is left with a decelerating franchise carrying a premium multiple.

Diagnostics: policy and pricing, not demand. Two-thirds of the Diagnostics reset is arithmetic (COVID testing down $450M from 2024 to 2025) and will annualize away. The other third is not: China's volume-based procurement programs structurally reprice reagents, and management has called it out explicitly. Rapid/Molecular was still down 8.0% comparable in Q2 2026. The Exact Sciences acquisition reframes the segment around oncology screening, but it also means Diagnostics' reported growth now embeds acquisition accounting — Q2 reported growth of 42.3% against organic growth of 2.9% is a 39-point gap that will flatter comparisons until March 2027.

Balance sheet and earnings quality post-Exact Sciences. Debt-to-equity moved from 0.27 at FY2025 to 0.63 TTM; current ratio fell from 1.58 to 1.38 and quick ratio from 1.02 to 0.80. ROIC dropped from 12.14% to 7.51% and ROE from 12.96% to 10.58%, mechanically, because the acquired goodwill and intangibles enter the capital base immediately while the earnings contribution phases in. The gap between Q2 GAAP EPS of $0.53 and adjusted EPS of $1.31 — and between 52.5% reported and 58.0% adjusted gross margin — is now largely acquisition-related amortization. Investors have to decide how much of that adjustment to accept.

Notable One-Off Items

Abbott's reported earnings are currently distorted by an unusually large number of non-recurring items, and taking GAAP figures at face value will mislead you in both directions.

1. FY2024's outsized net income. FY2024 net income of $13.40B produced a 31.94% net margin and $7.64 diluted EPS on only $41.95B of revenue — roughly double the underlying earnings power. This reflected a large non-recurring benefit, not operating performance. Every "net income down 61.2% YoY" headline you see on ABT is measured against that inflated base and is close to meaningless.

2. Exact Sciences acquisition accounting. The deal closed March 23, 2026. It adds purchase-accounting intangible amortization that drove the Q2 2026 gap between GAAP EPS of $0.53 and adjusted EPS of $1.31, and between 52.5% reported and 58.0% adjusted gross margin. It also inflates reported Diagnostics growth (+42.3% reported vs +2.9% comparable) and mechanically depressed ROIC, ROE, current ratio and quick ratio. None of this is operational deterioration; all of it flows through the headline numbers.

3. NEC litigation charges. A $495M Missouri verdict (July 2024) and a $70M Illinois verdict (April 2026) are known adverse judgments. Abbott has not published a quantified aggregate reserve that could be verified in this build. Legal defense costs across ~779 pending MDL cases are an ongoing, non-trivial operating expense.

4. COVID-19 testing normalization. COVID test sales of $297M in FY2025 versus $747M in FY2024 — a $450M year-over-year revenue decline concentrated in Rapid Diagnostics. FY2025 organic growth was 5.5% including COVID and 6.7% excluding it. This is a receding, quantifiable drag rather than a permanent impairment, and it substantially annualizes out during 2026.

5. Currency. Established Pharmaceuticals is entirely ex-US and Nutrition and Diagnostics are heavily international, so reported-versus-organic gaps are dollar-driven. In Q2 2026 the direction was favorable at the segment level in EPD (+8.4% reported vs +8.7% organic is roughly neutral), but FY2025 showed reported growth running slightly below organic in Nutrition and EPD. Treat reported growth as a currency-contaminated number and organic as the operating signal.

Five years, essentially zero return. The entire medtech complex derated on the thesis that GLP-1 weight-loss drugs would shrink demand for diabetes devices, cardiac procedures and bariatric surgery. Abbott, with the largest CGM franchise in the world, was the most direct casualty. The thesis has since been substantially rejected — providers increasingly pair CGM data with GLP-1 therapy for dosing precision, making the products complementary rather than substitutes.

Jun–Aug 2026
Recovery — $90.10 → $112.68 (+25%)
Three drivers in sequence: the June appellate reversal of Mead Johnson's $60M verdict on learned-intermediary grounds (reframing the litigation risk), the July 16 Q2 beat-and-raise, and the August 13 Wolfe Research upgrade to Outperform with a $130 target. The stock has recovered roughly two-thirds of its 2026 decline.
Overhang partially lifting

Relative performance. Against the S&P 500, ABT has underperformed massively over five years — roughly +5% total versus a double-digit annualized index return, one of the widest gaps in large-cap healthcare. Against its own sector the picture is more nuanced: Boston Scientific has grown revenue 13.5% TTM with net income up 46.7% and trades at 20.84x trailing earnings, while Abbott grows 8.06% and trades at 35.71x trailing (19.03x forward). Edwards trades at 54.18x on 14.6% revenue growth, and Dexcom at 35.00x on 15.5%. On forward earnings ABT is the cheapest large-cap medtech name in that group — the market is charging Abbott a diversification discount plus a litigation discount, and paying up for the pure-play growth stories. Whether that gap closes depends almost entirely on NEC resolution and on CGM growth stabilizing above high single digits.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Abbott is a 54-year dividend grower with a 0.58 beta trading at 19.0x forward earnings against $5.45–$5.60 of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance — a discount to both medtech peers and the S&P 500, for a business guiding 6.5%–7.5% organic growth. It owns 52.8% of a CGM market growing at a 15.1% CAGR toward $31.83B by 2031, with Libre Duo and OTC availability opening non-diabetic demand. Medical Devices, 48% of revenue, grew 11.9% organically in FY2025. Free cash flow of $7.82B ($4.52/share) covers the $2.52 dividend nearly twice over. If the NEC litigation resolves at a manageable number and CGM growth stabilizes, the trailing-to-forward multiple gap closes and the stock re-rates without needing any acceleration in the underlying business.

Bear Case

The growth engine is decelerating: Diabetes Care organic growth fell from +16.3% in FY2025 to +9.0% in Q2 2026, and CGM specifically to 9.5%, while Dexcom grows 15.5% and markets aggressively direct-to-consumer. Two of four segments are shrinking organically — Nutrition at −3.6% and Diagnostics carried by an acquisition (+42.3% reported vs +2.9% organic). The Exact Sciences deal doubled leverage (D/E 0.27 → 0.63) and halved ROIC (12.14% → 7.51%) to buy growth in the weakest segment. And ~779 NEC cases remain pending with no settlement, after verdicts of $495M and $70M. Five years of roughly zero total return is not an anomaly to be corrected; it may be the market correctly pricing a low-growth conglomerate with an open-ended legal liability.

Top 3 Risks
01
NEC litigation without a settlement ceiling. Approximately 779 cases pending in MDL 3026, no global settlement as of August 2026, and adverse verdicts of $495M (Missouri, July 2024) and $70M (Illinois, April 2026) already on the board. Abbott has not disclosed a verifiable aggregate reserve, which means the market is discounting an unbounded number — and unbounded liabilities do not get valued efficiently.
02
CGM deceleration into a competitive market. The entire growth thesis rests on Diabetes Care, which decelerated from 16.3% to 9.0% organic in two quarters. If OTC and wellness adoption does not offset saturation in the intensive-insulin base, Abbott loses its only high-growth franchise while still carrying two shrinking segments — and the 19x forward multiple stops being cheap.
03
Capital-allocation and integration risk on Exact Sciences. ROIC fell from 12.14% to 7.51% and D/E from 0.27 to 0.63 to acquire a business inside Abbott's weakest-performing segment. If Cologuard growth (+13.3% comparable in Q2) fades or reimbursement tightens, the deal becomes a permanent impairment to returns on capital in a company whose valuation case rests on quality.
  Industry Commentary
CGM Adoption Beyond Diabetes
EMERGING TAILWIND
The global continuous glucose monitoring market reached $15.77B in 2026 and is projected to hit $31.83B by 2031 at a 15.1% CAGR. Concentration is extreme — Abbott 52.8%, Dexcom 33.9%, Medtronic 10.1%, or 96.8% combined — so category growth accrues almost entirely to three companies. Sensors are 89.4% of revenue with a 15.4% CAGR (transmitters grow at just 6.0%), which favors Abbott's disposable-sensor architecture. Home and personal use is 73.6% of revenue, and hospital use is the fastest-growing channel at 18.1% CAGR. Crucially, the GLP-1 thesis that crushed medtech in 2023 has inverted: providers increasingly pair CGM data with GLP-1 therapy for precision dosing, making the two complementary rather than substitutes. Over-the-counter availability removes prescription friction and is the mechanism for non-diabetic wellness adoption in North America and Europe. North America is 50.6% of global revenue; Asia-Pacific grows fastest at 15.8%.
Litigation & Policy Overhangs in Medtech
ONGOING HEADWIND
Two policy forces are compressing healthcare multiples independent of operating performance. The first is US product-liability litigation: MDL 3026 holds ~779 pending preterm-infant-nutrition cases against Abbott and Reckitt/Mead Johnson, with verdicts of $495M and $70M against Abbott and a $60M verdict against Mead Johnson reversed on appeal in June 2026 on learned-intermediary grounds. Markets price open-ended liabilities badly — the discount applied is usually a function of uncertainty, not expected value. The second is international pricing policy: China's volume-based procurement (VBP) programs, which management explicitly cited as constraining Core Laboratory diagnostics, structurally reprice reagents and devices in the world's second-largest healthcare market. Neither force is cyclical; both require either legal resolution or geographic mix shift to fade. Tariffs and dollar direction add further unquantified variance for a company generating the majority of its revenue outside the United States.
Procedure Volumes vs. Diversification: The Core Debate
BULL vs BEAR FRAMING
Medtech procedure volumes have been robust across the industry — Boston Scientific grew 13.5% TTM, Edwards 14.6%, Medtronic 8.4% — so Abbott's cardiovascular deceleration (Structural Heart +11.5% organic in FY2025 falling to +5.7% in Q2 2026) is company-specific, not category-wide. The bull reading: Abbott's four-segment diversification means no single failure is fatal, EPD compounds at 8.7% with zero US pricing exposure, the balance sheet supports the 54-year dividend record, a 0.58 beta genuinely dampens portfolio volatility, and at 19.0x forward earnings you are paying less than the index for a business guiding 6.5%–7.5% organic growth. The bear reading: diversification is precisely why the stock has returned roughly 5% in five years — two of four segments are shrinking organically, the growth engine is decelerating, and management responded by levering up to buy assets inside the weakest segment. The resolution comes down to whether the next two prints show CGM stabilizing above high-single-digit growth and Diagnostics returning to positive organic growth. Neither is knowable today, and both are testable in October.
