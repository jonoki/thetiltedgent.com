01 Company Overview

Accenture plc is the world's largest publicly traded professional-services firm, selling strategy and consulting, technology and systems integration, managed services, industry-specific software ("Industry X"), creative and marketing services ("Song"), and — increasingly — security. It reported fiscal 2025 revenue of $69.67B, up 7.4% YoY, and trailing-twelve-month revenue through May 2026 of $73.10B, up 6.7%. Accenture is currently the world's 200th most valuable listed company at a $114.15B market capitalization — a rank that has fallen sharply, with market cap down roughly 33% over the past year and ~57% from its 2021 peak of ~$262B.

Its fiscal year ends August 31, which means FY2026 is ending right now. The most recent reported quarter is Q3 FY2026 (three months ended May 31, 2026), released June 18, 2026 — a print that triggered an ~18% single-day decline, the worst in the company's history as a public company. The Q4/full-year FY2026 report and first FY2027 guidance land October 1, 2026 and are by far the most important near-term event in this file.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
GLOBAL DELIVERY SCALE C-SUITE RELATIONSHIPS 799K TRAINED WORKFORCE HYPERSCALER PARTNERSHIPS INDUSTRY-DEPTH IP SWITCHING COSTS IN MANAGED SVCS ASSET-LIGHT BALANCE SHEET

Accenture's moat has never been technology — it is distribution and trust at the top of the enterprise. A Fortune 500 CIO buying a multi-year, multi-country transformation is buying execution certainty and indemnity, and there are perhaps four or five firms on earth that can staff a 3,000-person programme across 40 countries. That capability shows up in the numbers: 104 client bookings of $100M or more year-to-date in FY2026, up 13% — the large-deal engine is still working even as total bookings fell 2%. The model is genuinely asset-light: ROIC of 27.09% and ROE of 24.41% on a balance sheet carrying $10.17B of cash against debt-to-equity of 0.25, which funds a $6.52 dividend (3.50% yield) and $5.19B of buybacks in the first nine months of FY2026 without stress.

The vulnerability is that this moat protects the right to sell the work, not the price of the work. Accenture's revenue is, at root, headcount × utilisation × bill rate. Anything that structurally reduces the hours required per unit of client outcome attacks the numerator directly, and the moat does not stop it.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. The billable-hour model versus AI-assisted delivery. This is the existential item and the reason the stock lost roughly half its value in eight months. If AI coding and testing tools cut the labour required for systems integration by even 20–30%, Accenture must either shrink revenue or convince clients to pay outcome-based prices for a smaller input base. Management's answer is that AI transformation itself is the new demand — and there is evidence for it (advanced AI revenue reportedly grew from ~$0.9B in FY2024 to ~$2.7B in FY2025, with ~$2.2B of advanced AI bookings in Q1 FY2026 alone). But $2.7B of AI revenue against a $73B base is under 4%: it is not yet large enough to offset deflation in the core.

2. US federal government concentration. Contract reviews and slower procurement in the US federal business cost an estimated 1 percentage point of FY2026 revenue growth — guidance was explicitly framed as 3–4% local currency versus 4–5% excluding the federal effect. Health & Public Service printed 0% local-currency growth as a direct result. Management expects a return to growth in FY2027; that is an assumption, and a second round of federal budget action would break it.

3. Managed Services bookings decay. Managed Services bookings fell 15% YoY to $9.06B, putting that book's book-to-bill at 0.96×. Managed Services is the annuity half of the business — the part that smooths revenue through downturns. A sub-1.0 book-to-bill there is a slow-acting but compounding problem, and it is exactly what you would expect if clients believe automation will let them run those operations with fewer outsourced people.

4. Pricing and competitive convergence. Indian-heritage competitors — Infosys ($20.30B TTM revenue, +4.1%), Cognizant ($21.64B, +5.6%), TCS — compete on cost per delivered outcome, and AI tooling narrows Accenture's quality-of-delivery premium. Meanwhile IBM ($69.10B, +7.9% growth) is growing faster than Accenture at similar scale. Accenture's gross margin of 32.01% leaves little room to buy share on price.

Notable One-Off Items. Several non-recurring items materially distort the GAAP figures above and are worth isolating:

Business optimization program — $923M total. Accenture recorded $615M in Q4 FY2025 and a further $307.5M in Q1 FY2026 (roughly 0.6% of revenue) in restructuring and severance costs. This is the reconciling item between nine-month FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $10.27 and adjusted EPS of $10.67 — a $0.40 per-share gap. It also explains why nine-month adjusted operating margin was 15.9% while the Q3 GAAP figure was 17.0%. FY2026 guidance is given both ways: GAAP EPS $13.38–$13.50 (+10–11%) versus adjusted EPS $13.78–$13.90 (+7–8%). Note the inversion — GAAP growth looks better than adjusted because the prior-year base was itself charged.

Currency. Q3 FY2026 revenue grew 6% in USD but only 3% in local currency — a ~3-point tailwind from a weaker dollar. Anyone modelling forward growth off the reported USD line is overstating the underlying business by roughly half.

Middle East disruption. Regional conflict cost approximately $100M of Q3 revenue and ~$400M of bookings. Roughly $100M of the consulting revenue shortfall traces to this. It is genuinely one-off but it means the reported −2% bookings decline slightly overstates underlying demand weakness.

Acquisitions. Announced simultaneously with Q3 results: a majority stake in Dragos plus full acquisitions of runZero and NetRise, combined enterprise value approximately $4.2B — a large operational-technology security push. Expect purchase-price allocation and intangible amortisation to weigh on FY2027 GAAP earnings, and note the optics of committing $4.2B of capital in the same breath as a guidance cut.

Not disclosed. Accenture did not quantify utilisation, attrition, or generative-AI bookings in the Q3 FY2026 release. Any figure you see for those in this quarter is inference, not disclosure. The advanced-AI revenue path (~$0.9B FY2024 → ~$2.7B FY2025) and Q1 FY2026 advanced-AI bookings (~$2.2B) come from secondary reporting of company commentary and should be treated as approximate.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward. Above 70 the stock has risen a lot in a short time and may be stretched; below 30 it has fallen hard and may be washed out. It describes momentum, not value.

The five-year trajectory. On a dividend-adjusted basis, ACN closed September 2021 at $294.39 and August 2026 at $186.50 — a total return of approximately −36.6% over five years. That is a catastrophic outcome for what was, through the 2010s, one of the most reliable large-cap compounders in the market. The peak-to-trough numbers are worse: from the December 2021 adjusted high of $382.61 to the June 2026 monthly close of $124.44 the stock lost 67%, and from the January 2025 cycle peak of $373.32 it is still down 50%.

Critically, the decline is a multiple compression story, not an earnings collapse. Revenue rose from $50.5B in FY2021 to $73.1B TTM (+45%) and EPS from $9.16 to $12.50 (+36%) over the same window. The entire drawdown is the market re-rating Accenture from a ~35× premium compounder to a ~15× value stock. Whether that is right depends entirely on whether AI ends the growth algorithm or extends it.

Relative performance. ACN has materially underperformed the S&P 500 over one, three and five years — the index was broadly higher over a period in which ACN lost roughly a third of its value, so the relative shortfall is on the order of 80–100 percentage points cumulatively. Precise S&P 500 return figures were not verified from a live source in this build; treat the magnitude as directional. Against IT-services peers the picture is less lonely: Infosys's market cap is down 32.4% and Cognizant's 20.7% over the past year versus ACN's roughly 33% — this is a sector de-rating, not a company-specific failure. IBM, by contrast, is down only 3.5%, which the market appears to read as evidence that a software/infrastructure mix is more defensible than a labour-based one.

08 Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

You are paying 14.9× trailing / 13.0× forward earnings for the world's largest professional-services franchise generating $12.58B of free cash flow (an 11% FCF yield), a 27.1% ROIC, net cash, a 3.50% dividend and a further 1.5% of buyback. The growth problem is substantially identifiable and arguably temporary: roughly 1 point of the FY2026 shortfall is US federal, which management expects to reverse in FY2027, and ~$100M of revenue plus ~$400M of bookings was Middle East disruption. Meanwhile the leading indicators the bears ignore are positive — consulting bookings +13%, 104 client bookings over $100M YTD, up 13%, CMT growing +9% local currency, and advanced-AI revenue reportedly tripling to ~$2.7B. If AI is a demand driver rather than a substitute, a return to mid-single-digit growth at a 20× multiple implies a materially higher share price.

BEAR CASE

Accenture's revenue is headcount × utilisation × bill rate, and AI attacks all three. Managed Services bookings fell 15% to $9.06B — a 0.96× book-to-bill on the annuity half of the business. Consulting grew just 1% in local currency. Total company local-currency growth is 3%, and even excluding federal it is only 4–5% — barely above inflation for a business that compounded at double digits. A ~$2.7B advanced-AI revenue line cannot offset deflation across a $73B base. The company is spending $4.2B on acquisitions and $923M on restructuring in the same year it cut guidance — that is a business managing decline, not funding growth. At 14.9× a structurally shrinking earnings stream, the stock is not cheap; Morgan Stanley's $130 target is the honest version of this view.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
AI deflation of the billable-hour model. If AI-assisted delivery cuts the labour hours required per client outcome faster than Accenture wins new AI-transformation work, revenue declines regardless of demand. The 15% fall in Managed Services bookings is the first hard evidence this is already happening at the margin rather than being a future risk. Watch Q4 Managed Services bookings above all else.
02
US federal exposure does not recover. Management's 3–4% FY2026 guide becomes 4–5% "excluding federal," and the FY2027 story depends on that gap closing. If federal procurement stays constrained, Health & Public Service — 20.6% of revenue and already at 0% growth — drags consolidated growth toward 2–3% indefinitely, which would not support even the current multiple.
03
Consensus targets already sit below the price. The average target of $181.31 is 2.8% under the $186.53 close after a 50% ten-week rally. There is no sell-side upside cushion, short interest is elevated at 5.38% of shares, and the October 1 print delivers both Q4 results and the first FY2027 guidance simultaneously. A disappointing FY2027 guide into a stock that has already re-rated upward is the highest-probability path to a sharp reversal.
  Industry Commentary
Generative AI: Demand Driver and Solvent, Simultaneously
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the whole argument, and it is genuinely unresolved. The demand side is real and measurable: advanced-AI revenue reportedly went from ~$0.9B in FY2024 to ~$2.7B in FY2025, with ~$2.2B of advanced-AI bookings in Q1 FY2026 alone; Communications, Media & Technology grew +9% in local currency; and large-deal activity is accelerating with 104 bookings of $100M+ year-to-date, up 13%. Enterprises cannot deploy AI at scale without integration partners, and Accenture is the largest one.

The deflation side is equally real. Roughly half of Accenture's work is application development, testing, systems integration and managed operations — precisely the categories where AI tooling reduces hours required. Total local-currency growth of 3%, consulting growth of 1%, and a 15% decline in Managed Services bookings are all consistent with clients needing fewer delivered hours. The arithmetic problem is scale mismatch: ~$2.7B of AI revenue is under 4% of the $73B base. Even growing 50% a year it takes several years to become the dominant driver, and the core has to hold in the meantime. The market's June 18 verdict — an ~18% single-day decline, reportedly the worst on record — was a vote that the deflation arrives faster than the substitution.

Accenture has not disclosed generative-AI bookings or revenue as a formal reporting line in its earnings release. The figures above come from secondary reporting of company commentary and should be treated as approximate.
Government Consulting Budgets and Discretionary IT Spend
ONGOING HEADWIND
Two separate spending constraints are compounding. US federal: contract reviews, cancellations and slower procurement cycles cost an estimated 1 percentage point of FY2026 total-company revenue growth — management explicitly guided 3–4% local currency, or 4–5% excluding the federal impact — and pushed Health & Public Service (20.6% of revenue) to 0% local-currency growth. This is policy risk, not demand risk, which makes it both more binary and less forecastable. Management expects a return to growth in FY2027.

Broader discretionary IT spend: outside the technology sector, growth is running low single digits — Products +3%, Financial Services +3%, Resources +1%, Americas +1%. Clients are funding AI pilots by cutting other transformation programmes rather than expanding budgets. That zero-sum dynamic is why Accenture can report strong AI bookings and weak total bookings in the same quarter. It also explains why the ~3-point currency tailwind (6% USD vs 3% local currency growth) matters so much to the reported headline — and why it should be stripped out of any forward model.
Framing the Debate: What Would Settle It
BULL VS BEAR — THE DECIDING VARIABLES
The bull needs three things by October 1: (1) Managed Services bookings returning to at least flat — a 0.96× book-to-bill on the annuity book is the single most damaging number in the Q3 release; (2) FY2027 local-currency guidance of 5%+, confirming the federal drag reverses as management expects; (3) formal disclosure of AI revenue as a reporting line, which would signal management believes it is large and durable enough to be judged on.

The bear needs only one: a second consecutive quarter of declining bookings. Bookings lead revenue by roughly four to six quarters, so a second decline effectively pre-commits FY2027 to low growth regardless of what guidance says.

The valuation frames the asymmetry. At 14.9× trailing / 13.0× forward earnings with an 11% FCF yield, a 3.50% dividend and 27.1% ROIC, a great deal of bad news is priced in — the stock fell to roughly 11× at the June low and has since rallied ~50%. But the PEG of 2.05 is the discipline check: the multiple is low because growth is low, and a low multiple on a shrinking business is not value. Sector context matters too — Infosys trades at 14.4× and Cognizant at 13.4×, so ACN's discount is a sector de-rating, while IBM at 20.5× and +7.9% growth shows what the market will pay for a services business with a software and infrastructure mix. The strategic question for Accenture is whether the ~$4.2B cybersecurity acquisitions are the start of that same transition.
