01 Company Overview

Analog Devices designs and manufactures high-performance analog, mixed-signal and power management semiconductors — the components that convert real-world signals (temperature, pressure, motion, current, light, radio waves) into digital data and manage the power that computing systems consume. It is the second-largest analog semiconductor supplier globally behind Texas Instruments, and its ~75,000-part catalogue is sold into roughly 100,000 customers with unusually long product lifecycles measured in decades rather than quarters.

Fiscal 2025 (ended November 1, 2025) revenue was $11.02B, up 16.9% YoY as the company climbed out of a severe inventory-correction trough. The recovery has since accelerated sharply: TTM revenue through the August 1, 2026 quarter reached $13.88B, fiscal Q3 2026 was a record $4.02B (+40% YoY, after +37% in Q2), and the first nine months of FY2026 were up 36%. Management guides Q4 to another record, $4.3B ±$100M. At the September 21, 2026 close ADI's market capitalization is $185.59B (484.57M shares × $383.00, our calculation); the August 18 rankings of roughly #115 globally and #63 in the US have not been re-checked.

Fiscal calendar note: ADI's fiscal year ends in early November (FY2025 ended Nov 1, 2025). Fiscal Q3 2026 ran to August 1, 2026 and was reported on August 19, 2026; fiscal Q4 ends October 31, 2026. All "FY" references on this page use ADI's fiscal calendar, not the calendar year.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
~75,000 SKU catalogue
10–20 yr design cycles
Analog design talent scarcity
Hybrid internal-fab model
Aerospace/defense qualification
~100,000 customer diversification
Linear + Maxim IP consolidation

Analog is the least Moore's-Law-driven corner of semiconductors, and that is the whole moat. High-performance analog parts are not shrunk onto leading-edge nodes; they are designed by scarce engineers with intuition for noise, drift and thermal behaviour on mature 180nm–65nm processes, and they stay in production for fifteen or twenty years. Once an ADI data converter or amplifier is designed into an industrial motion controller, an MRI front end or a defense radar, the switching cost is a full board redesign plus requalification — often years and millions of dollars for a component that may be a few dollars of BOM. That produces very sticky revenue with pricing power, which is why ADI's gross margin was 67.3% GAAP / 72.5% adjusted in Q3 FY2026 (67.3% / 73.0% in Q2) versus a peer-median well below that. The catalogue breadth compounds the effect: a customer that has standardised on ADI's toolchain and reference designs picks ADI for the next socket by default.

ADI also runs a hybrid manufacturing model — it owns and operates internal fabs and test facilities while sourcing the balance of wafers from external foundries. That gives it two things competitors relying purely on foundries lack: proprietary specialty processes that are hard to copy, and a "swing" capability that lets it flex utilisation between internal and outsourced capacity through the cycle, cushioning gross margin in downturns and shortening lead times in upturns. (ADI has historically targeted roughly half of production internally — verify the current split against the latest 10-K.) Layered on top is a decade of consolidation: Linear Technology (2017) brought best-in-class power management, and Maxim Integrated (2021) added automotive and broad-market power plus a large consumer/wearables franchise. Together they removed two of ADI's most credible high-performance analog competitors from the field and delivered cost synergies that are visible in the operating-margin structure — while also creating the amortization charge that depresses GAAP earnings to this day.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Cyclicality that the moat does not prevent. The moat protects share and price, not volume. FY2024 revenue fell -23.4% to $9.43B and GAAP gross margin compressed to 57.1% as customers burned inventory — a reminder that a decade of design wins offers no protection when the entire channel destocks at once. The current growth (+40% in Q3 FY2026, +36% for the first nine months) is partly a genuine recovery and partly the mirror image of that collapse. Anyone underwriting today's numbers as a new baseline is extrapolating the up-leg of a cycle.

2. Chinese domestic analog competition. China is both ADI's largest growth opportunity and the source of a structural pricing threat. A large cohort of domestic Chinese analog suppliers, subsidised and explicitly directed to substitute Western parts, has been taking the commodity and mid-performance end of the market on price. This starts at the low end of the catalogue but works upward over time, and it caps ADI's ability to grow unit volume in the world's largest electronics manufacturing base. Export-control policy cuts both ways: it restricts some ADI shipments while also accelerating Chinese customers' incentive to design out Western analog entirely.

3. Texas Instruments' capacity and price posture. TI is the direct comparison and roughly 1.4x ADI's revenue at $19.45B TTM (+16.7%, as of the Aug 18 edition). TI has spent heavily on 300mm internal capacity with an explicit strategy of using structurally lower cost per die to compete on price and win share in the broad market. If TI runs that capacity aggressively into a soft demand environment, industry pricing deteriorates and ADI's premium-margin positioning gets tested at the edges of its catalogue.

4. Automotive: one good quarter after a stall. Automotive is 25% of revenue. It went from +8% YoY in Q1 FY2026 to +2% in Q2 and then +16% in Q3 — a turn, but a single data point. ADI's auto content story depends on EV battery management and ADAS deployment volumes, both hostage to EV adoption rates, OEM platform delays and Chinese ADAS timing. The previous edition's worry (a quarter of the business contributing nothing) is gone for now; the new question is whether Q3 was demand or a restock.

Notable One-Off Items & Reporting Distortions

Acquisition amortization is the single largest distortion, and it is not small. ADI's purchases of Linear Technology (2017) and Maxim Integrated (2021) created a very large intangible asset base that is amortized through cost of goods sold and operating expense every quarter. The effect is directly visible in the reported reconciliation for fiscal Q3 2026: GAAP gross margin 67.3% vs adjusted 72.5% (a 520bp gap), GAAP operating margin 40.1% vs adjusted 50.0% (a 990bp gap), and GAAP diluted EPS $2.74 vs adjusted $3.45 (a $0.71 gap), of which acquisition-related expenses were $0.81 per share. Q2 showed the same pattern ($2.40 GAAP vs $3.09 adjusted), as did Q1 ($1.69 vs $2.46). Acquisition-related expenses ran at about $400M in Q3 (pre-tax) and the Q4 outlook includes $405M — roughly $1.6B a year. This is why the trailing P/E of 45.49 looks so much worse than the forward 23.43, and why ROE of 12.23% understates the economic return on the operating business. It is a real accounting charge but a non-cash one — TTM free cash flow of $4.94B exceeds GAAP net income of $4.14B, which is the tell. Q3 also carried $23M of Empower transaction and integration costs and a $24M net credit in special charges.

Cycle-trough charges are still in the comparison base. FY2024 (revenue -23.4%, gross margin 57.1%, operating margin 21.9%) absorbed the cost of running fabs at low utilisation plus inventory reserves and restructuring taken during the destocking correction. Those charges depressed the base against which the current +40% growth and 65.8% TTM gross margin are measured, which mechanically flatters every year-over-year comparison on this page. ADI has also disclosed restructuring actions during the downturn; the specific amounts were not verified from a live source for this snapshot — check the FY2025 10-K and the Q3 FY2026 10-Q.

Effective tax rate of 12.1% TTM (13.3% GAAP in Q3; Q4 guided at 12–14%) is below the US statutory rate, reflecting ADI's Irish and international manufacturing footprint. Changes to global minimum tax rules or US international tax policy would be a genuine, if unglamorous, earnings variable.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 suggest the stock has risen far and fast and may be stretched; below 30 suggests the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a forecast.

Five-year trajectory. From the September 2021 close of $167.48 to the September 21, 2026 close of $383.00, ADI returned +128.7% on price alone — roughly an 18.1% annualised price CAGR. Adding the $18.11 per share of dividends with ex-dates from December 2021 to September 2026 (not reinvested) lifts the total return to roughly +139% (our calculation). But the path was anything but linear, and the compounding was overwhelmingly back-loaded: the stock spent the first three of those five years essentially range-bound between about $140 and $235 at month-end, and about 69% of the five-year price gain came after the October 2025 close of $234.13.

The shape of the chart is the analog cycle. The 2022 derate took ADI from $180.25 (Nov 2021) to $139.34 (Sep 2022), a -22.7% month-end drawdown driven by rate compression rather than fundamentals — FY2022 revenue actually grew 64.2%. The far more damaging move came later: as FY2024 revenue collapsed -23.4% and gross margin fell to 57.1%, the stock chopped sideways from $198.56 (Dec 2023) to $212.46 (Dec 2024), going nowhere for a year while earnings halved. The April 2025 month-end low of $194.92 marked the trough of sentiment. From there the recovery was violent: +112.3% from April 2025 to the May 2026 close of $413.85, as bookings inflected, Communications began compounding on data-center power, and the market re-rated ADI from a cyclical industrial supplier to an AI-adjacent one. The stock then gave some of that back into the Q3 print ($376.63 on August 18), shrugged off a beat-and-raise (−0.89% on August 19), closed August at $362.14 and closed as low as $354.52 on September 1 before recovering to $383.00 on September 21 — 7.5% below the May close and 14.1% below the $445.91 52-week high. Relative performance. ADI's market capitalisation rose from $92.33B at the end of 2021 to $185.59B at the September 21, 2026 close (+101.0%; the 2026 figure is our calculation). Its growth is well ahead of the analog peer group — the peer-median TTM revenue growth across TXN, Infineon, STM, NXP, Microchip and onsemi was +9.7% as of the Aug 18 edition, against ADI's +40% in Q3 — so the re-rating since 2025 has been earned rather than purely multiple-driven. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

ADI is in a multi-year analog up-cycle with the best margin structure in the industry: 72.5% adjusted gross margin and a record 50.0% adjusted operating margin in Q3 FY2026, a Q4 guide of $4.3B and 52.0% that topped consensus, and FY2026 consensus of $15.13B revenue (+37.3%) and $12.89 adjusted EPS (+65.5%). The recovery broadened in Q3: Automotive, the laggard, grew 16%. Layered on top is a structural driver — Communications grew 84% YoY on data-center demand, Empower (closed in July) targets the AI-server power bottleneck directly, and Alif adds edge-AI processing. The stock barely moved while earnings rose, so at a 0.93 PEG and 23.4x FY2027 consensus EPS, with 22 straight years of dividend increases and $5.17B of TTM capital returned, an investor is paying less for the same story than before the print.

Bear Case

Every headline growth number is measured against a collapsed FY2024 base — revenue -23.4%, gross margin 57.1% — so +40% Q3 growth substantially reflects channel refill rather than end-demand. Industrial at +53% YoY cannot be a sustainable run rate; when it normalises, the comparison inverts violently, and one strong automotive quarter after a +2% quarter is not yet a trend. The stock trades at 45x trailing GAAP earnings with a 12.2% ROE weighed down by $28B of goodwill, and two acquisitions in one year ($1.5B Empower, $1.35B Alif pending) add integration risk while commercial paper and current debt pushed the current ratio down to 1.25. Meanwhile Chinese domestic analog suppliers are taking the low end on price in a region representing 45.7% of global analog revenue, and Texas Instruments' 300mm capacity build gives the largest competitor both scale and an incentive to compete on price. The low sell-side target of $365 still sits below the current price, and the market's flat reaction to a beat-and-raise says a lot of good news was already in it.

Top 3 Risks
01
Cycle timing — the recovery is priced, the duration is not. ADI has already re-rated on the assumption that FY2026–FY2027 delivers a sustained up-cycle. If Industrial's +53% is substantially inventory replenishment and Automotive's +16% proves a one-quarter restock, the FY2027 consensus of $16.35 EPS (+26.8%) becomes unreachable and a 23.4x forward multiple on a cyclical semiconductor compresses fast. Q3 passed the first test; the Q4 print and first FY2027 outlook are the next.
02
China: demand exposure and structural substitution. Asia-Pacific is 45.7% of global analog revenue and China is central to ADI's automotive and industrial volume. Subsidised domestic Chinese analog suppliers are taking the commodity tier on price and working upward through the performance stack, while US export controls and Chinese countermeasures create a two-sided policy risk that neither ADI nor its investors can hedge.
03
Data-center concentration is a narrower base than it appears. The Communications segment carrying the AI narrative is $655M a quarter — 16% of revenue. If AI capital spending decelerates or hyperscalers shift power architectures toward vertically integrated or competing suppliers, the segment that justifies the multiple expansion is small enough to disappoint without a large revenue hole, but large enough in narrative terms to take the multiple down with it.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
The Analog Cycle Is Recovering and Broadening — But From a Very Low Base
ONGOING HEADWIND
Analog semiconductors run on inventory cycles, not technology cycles, and the 2023–2024 destocking was one of the most severe on record. ADI's FY2024 revenue fell -23.4% to $9.43B with gross margin compressing to 57.1% and operating margin to 21.9%. FY2025 recovered to $11.02B (+16.9%) and the TTM figure is now $13.88B — only about 13% above the FY2023 level of $12.31B. Three years of growth were erased and are only now being recovered. In Q3 FY2026 the recovery broadened: Industrial +53%, Communications +84%, Automotive +16% (after +2% in Q2), with Consumer slowing to +6%. The broader analog market is forecast to grow at just a 2.67% value CAGR to 2031 (6.98% by unit volume) — meaning ADI's current growth rate is a cycle artifact, and long-run share gain, content growth and pricing will have to do the work once the refill is complete.
Chinese Domestic Analog Competition
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the slow-moving structural threat that the cycle narrative tends to obscure. Asia-Pacific accounts for 45.72% of global analog semiconductor revenue, and China's stated industrial policy is to substitute domestic analog for imported parts. Chinese suppliers began at the commodity end — general-purpose amplifiers, regulators, interface — where the design moat is thinnest, and are working upward. The mechanism of damage is not a sudden share loss; it is a persistent cap on ADI's unit growth in the largest electronics manufacturing base on earth, plus downward pressure on catalogue pricing at the low end that gradually shifts ADI's mix toward a narrower, higher-performance core. Export controls compound rather than solve this: restricting what ADI can ship to China simultaneously accelerates Chinese customers' motivation to design ADI out permanently. ADI's counter is the top of the performance stack — precision converters, RF, aerospace/defense-qualified parts — where the design and qualification barriers remain genuinely high.
Data-Center Power: A Genuinely New Analog Growth Vector
EMERGING TAILWIND
For twenty years analog was a GDP-plus business. AI infrastructure has changed that for one specific slice of it. As accelerator power draw climbs, the conversion chain from grid to core has to move to higher voltages and put the final conversion stage physically closer to the die — which raises the analog content per rack sharply and puts a premium on power density and efficiency. ADI's Communications segment, more than 75% data-center-driven as of Q2, grew 84% YoY to $655M in Q3 FY2026, and the company's own framing is that power delivery and efficiency are the gating factors for scaling AI compute. Empower Semiconductor, acquired in July, targets integrated voltage regulators for exactly this bottleneck.

Bull vs bear framing: The bull view is that this converts ADI from a cyclical industrial supplier into a structural AI-infrastructure beneficiary, justifying a permanently higher multiple and making the 23.4x forward P/E look reasonable against a 0.93 PEG. The bear view is arithmetic: at $655M a quarter this is 16% of revenue, and 84% of ADI is an analog business lapping an artificially depressed base. The market is paying an AI-adjacent multiple for a company where the AI-adjacent piece is roughly one dollar in six of revenue. Both readings are defensible from the same data. The August 19 print strengthened the numbers without settling the argument — the stock's flat reaction shows how much was already priced — so the first FY2027 outlook at the Q4 print is the next place to look.
