01 Company Overview

Analog Devices designs and manufactures high-performance analog, mixed-signal and power management semiconductors — the components that convert real-world signals (temperature, pressure, motion, current, light, radio waves) into digital data and manage the power that computing systems consume. It is the second-largest analog semiconductor supplier globally behind Texas Instruments, and its ~75,000-part catalogue is sold into roughly 100,000 customers with unusually long product lifecycles measured in decades rather than quarters.

Fiscal 2025 (ended November 1, 2025) revenue was $11.02B, up 16.9% YoY as the company climbed out of a severe inventory-correction trough. The recovery has since accelerated sharply: TTM revenue through the May 2, 2026 quarter reached $12.74B, +29.8% YoY, and fiscal Q2 2026 was a record $3.62B (+37% YoY). ADI ranks roughly #115 globally and #63 in the US by market capitalization at $183.45B.

Fiscal calendar note: ADI's fiscal year ends in early November (FY2025 ended Nov 1, 2025). Fiscal Q3 2026 covers roughly May–July 2026 and is reported on August 19, 2026. All "FY" references on this page use ADI's fiscal calendar, not the calendar year.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
~75,000 SKU catalogue
10–20 yr design cycles
Analog design talent scarcity
Hybrid internal-fab model
Aerospace/defense qualification
~100,000 customer diversification
Linear + Maxim IP consolidation

Analog is the least Moore's-Law-driven corner of semiconductors, and that is the whole moat. High-performance analog parts are not shrunk onto leading-edge nodes; they are designed by scarce engineers with intuition for noise, drift and thermal behaviour on mature 180nm–65nm processes, and they stay in production for fifteen or twenty years. Once an ADI data converter or amplifier is designed into an industrial motion controller, an MRI front end or a defense radar, the switching cost is a full board redesign plus requalification — often years and millions of dollars for a component that may be a few dollars of BOM. That produces very sticky revenue with pricing power, which is why ADI's gross margin was 67.3% GAAP / 73.0% adjusted in Q2 FY2026 versus a peer-median well below that. The catalogue breadth compounds the effect: a customer that has standardised on ADI's toolchain and reference designs picks ADI for the next socket by default.

ADI also runs a hybrid manufacturing model — it owns and operates internal fabs and test facilities while sourcing the balance of wafers from external foundries. That gives it two things competitors relying purely on foundries lack: proprietary specialty processes that are hard to copy, and a "swing" capability that lets it flex utilisation between internal and outsourced capacity through the cycle, cushioning gross margin in downturns and shortening lead times in upturns. (ADI has historically targeted roughly half of production internally — verify the current split against the latest 10-K.) Layered on top is a decade of consolidation: Linear Technology (2017) brought best-in-class power management, and Maxim Integrated (2021) added automotive and broad-market power plus a large consumer/wearables franchise. Together they removed two of ADI's most credible high-performance analog competitors from the field and delivered cost synergies that are visible in the operating-margin structure — while also creating the amortization charge that depresses GAAP earnings to this day.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Cyclicality that the moat does not prevent. The moat protects share and price, not volume. FY2024 revenue fell -23.4% to $9.43B and GAAP gross margin compressed to 57.1% as customers burned inventory — a reminder that a decade of design wins offers no protection when the entire channel destocks at once. The current +29.8% TTM growth is partly a genuine recovery and partly the mirror image of that collapse. Anyone underwriting today's numbers as a new baseline is extrapolating the up-leg of a cycle.

2. Chinese domestic analog competition. China is both ADI's largest growth opportunity and the source of a structural pricing threat. A large cohort of domestic Chinese analog suppliers, subsidised and explicitly directed to substitute Western parts, has been taking the commodity and mid-performance end of the market on price. This starts at the low end of the catalogue but works upward over time, and it caps ADI's ability to grow unit volume in the world's largest electronics manufacturing base. Export-control policy cuts both ways: it restricts some ADI shipments while also accelerating Chinese customers' incentive to design out Western analog entirely.

3. Texas Instruments' capacity and price posture. TI is the direct comparison and roughly 1.5x ADI's revenue at $19.45B TTM (+16.7%). TI has spent heavily on 300mm internal capacity with an explicit strategy of using structurally lower cost per die to compete on price and win share in the broad market. If TI runs that capacity aggressively into a soft demand environment, industry pricing deteriorates and ADI's premium-margin positioning gets tested at the edges of its catalogue.

4. Automotive concentration risk and a stalled recovery. At 24% of revenue and only +2% YoY in Q2 FY2026 (down from +8% in Q1), automotive is not participating in the recovery. ADI's auto content story depends on EV battery management and ADAS deployment volumes — both of which are hostage to EV adoption rates, OEM platform delays, and Chinese ADAS timing. A quarter of the business is currently contributing roughly nothing to growth.

Notable One-Off Items & Reporting Distortions

Acquisition amortization is the single largest distortion, and it is not small. ADI's purchases of Linear Technology (2017) and Maxim Integrated (2021) created a very large intangible asset base that is amortized through cost of goods sold and operating expense every quarter. The effect is directly visible in the reported reconciliation for fiscal Q2 2026: GAAP gross margin 67.3% vs adjusted 73.0% (a 570bp gap), GAAP operating margin 38.1% vs adjusted 49.0% (a 1,090bp gap), and GAAP diluted EPS $2.40 vs adjusted $3.09 (a $0.69 gap). Fiscal Q1 2026 showed the same pattern: $1.69 GAAP vs $2.46 adjusted. Annualised, the reconciling items are worth roughly $2.80 per share, or ~$1.4B. This is why the trailing P/E of 56.07 looks so much worse than the forward 26.84, and why ROE of 9.64% understates the economic return on the operating business. It is a real accounting charge but a non-cash one — free cash flow of $4.57B exceeds GAAP net income of $3.31B, which is the tell.

Cycle-trough charges are still in the comparison base. FY2024 (revenue -23.4%, gross margin 57.1%, operating margin 21.9%) absorbed the cost of running fabs at low utilisation plus inventory reserves and restructuring taken during the destocking correction. Those charges depressed the base against which the current +29.8% growth and 64.5% gross margin are measured, which mechanically flatters every year-over-year comparison on this page. ADI has also disclosed restructuring actions during the downturn; the specific amounts were not verified from a live source for this snapshot — check the FY2025 10-K and the Q3 FY2026 10-Q.

Effective tax rate of 15.5% is below the US statutory rate, reflecting ADI's Irish and international manufacturing footprint. Changes to global minimum tax rules or US international tax policy would be a genuine, if unglamorous, earnings variable.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 suggest the stock has risen far and fast and may be stretched; below 30 suggests the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a forecast.

Five-year trajectory. From the September 2021 close of $154.48 to the August 18, 2026 close of $376.63, ADI returned +143.8% on price alone — roughly a 19.5% annualised price CAGR. Adding the approximately $18.50 per share of dividends paid across FY2022–FY2026 lifts the total return to roughly +156% (estimated). But the path was anything but linear, and the compounding was overwhelmingly back-loaded: the stock spent the first three of those five years essentially range-bound between $130 and $230, and produced the entire second half of its return in the eighteen months from November 2025 onward.

The shape of the chart is the analog cycle. The 2022 derate took ADI from $166.26 (Nov 2021) to $130.87 (Sep 2022), a -21% drawdown driven by rate compression rather than fundamentals — FY2022 revenue actually grew 64.2%. The far more damaging move came later: as FY2024 revenue collapsed -23.4% and gross margin fell to 57.1%, the stock chopped sideways from $190.89 (Dec 2023) to $207.78 (Dec 2024), going nowhere for a year while earnings halved. The April 2025 low of $191.46 marked the trough of sentiment. From there the recovery was violent: +96.7% from April 2025 to the May 2026 close of $412.72, as bookings inflected, Communications began compounding on data-center power, and the market re-rated ADI from a cyclical industrial supplier to an AI-adjacent one. The subsequent pullback to $376.63 — 8.7% below the May close and 15.5% below the $445.91 52-week high — is the market taking risk off ahead of a print it has already paid for.

Relative performance. ADI's market capitalisation rose from $92.33B at the end of 2021 to $183.45B today (+98.7%), with the largest single-year jump coming in the trailing period (+35.3%). Its 52-week return of +62.6% is well ahead of the analog peer group's fundamental growth rates — the peer-median revenue growth across TXN, Infineon, STM, NXP, Microchip and onsemi is just +9.7% versus ADI's +29.8%, so the outperformance is earned rather than purely multiple-driven. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

ADI is early in a multi-year analog up-cycle with the best margin structure in the industry: 73.0% adjusted gross margin and 49.0% adjusted operating margin in Q2 FY2026, record B2B bookings, and consensus FY2026 revenue of $14.81B (+34.4%) on adjusted EPS of $12.43 (+59.6%). Layered on top of the cyclical recovery is a genuinely new structural driver — Communications is now over 75% data-center, grew 79% YoY, and the Empower Semiconductor acquisition targets the AI-server power bottleneck directly. Automotive at just +2% means a quarter of the business is still contributing nothing, which is upside optionality rather than a problem. At a 1.11 PEG and 26.8x forward earnings, with 22 straight years of dividend increases and $5.04B of TTM capital returned, an investor is not paying an AI premium for AI-linked growth.

Bear Case

Every headline growth number is measured against a collapsed FY2024 base — revenue -23.4%, gross margin 57.1% — so +29.8% TTM growth substantially reflects channel refill rather than end-demand. Industrial at +56% YoY cannot be a sustainable run rate; when it normalises, the comparison inverts violently, and automotive at +2% shows the recovery is narrow, not broad. The stock has already risen 62.6% in 52 weeks and trades at 56x trailing GAAP earnings with a 9.6% ROE weighed down by $30B+ of acquisition-inflated equity. Meanwhile Chinese domestic analog suppliers are taking the low end on price in a region representing 45.7% of global analog revenue, and Texas Instruments' 300mm capacity build gives the largest competitor both scale and an incentive to compete on price. The low sell-side target of $365 already sits below the current price.

Top 3 Risks
01
Cycle timing — the recovery is priced, the duration is not. ADI has already re-rated on the assumption that FY2026–FY2027 delivers a sustained up-cycle. If Industrial's +56% is substantially inventory replenishment and decelerates to single digits before Automotive inflects from +2%, the FY2027 consensus becomes unreachable and a 26.8x forward multiple on a cyclical semiconductor compresses fast. The Q3 FY2026 print on August 19 is the first real test.
02
China: demand exposure and structural substitution. Asia-Pacific is 45.7% of global analog revenue and China is central to ADI's automotive and industrial volume. Subsidised domestic Chinese analog suppliers are taking the commodity tier on price and working upward through the performance stack, while US export controls and Chinese countermeasures create a two-sided policy risk that neither ADI nor its investors can hedge.
03
Data-center concentration is a narrower base than it appears. The Communications segment carrying the AI narrative is $555M a quarter — 15% of revenue. If AI capital spending decelerates or hyperscalers shift power architectures toward vertically integrated or competing suppliers, the segment that justifies the multiple expansion is small enough to disappoint without a large revenue hole, but large enough in narrative terms to take the multiple down with it.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
The Analog Cycle Is Recovering — But From a Very Low Base
ONGOING HEADWIND
Analog semiconductors run on inventory cycles, not technology cycles, and the 2023–2024 destocking was one of the most severe on record. ADI's FY2024 revenue fell -23.4% to $9.43B with gross margin compressing to 57.1% and operating margin to 21.9%. FY2025 recovered to $11.02B (+16.9%) and the TTM figure is now $12.74B (+29.8%) — but note that TTM revenue has only just exceeded the FY2023 level of $12.31B. Three years of growth were erased and are only now being recovered. The recovery is also uneven in a way that matters: Industrial +56%, Communications +79%, Consumer +23%, but Automotive only +2%, and decelerating from +8% in Q1 FY2026. The broader analog market is forecast to grow at just a 2.67% value CAGR to 2031 (6.98% by unit volume) — meaning ADI's current growth rate is a cycle artifact, and long-run share gain, content growth and pricing will have to do the work once the refill is complete.
Chinese Domestic Analog Competition
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the slow-moving structural threat that the cycle narrative tends to obscure. Asia-Pacific accounts for 45.72% of global analog semiconductor revenue, and China's stated industrial policy is to substitute domestic analog for imported parts. Chinese suppliers began at the commodity end — general-purpose amplifiers, regulators, interface — where the design moat is thinnest, and are working upward. The mechanism of damage is not a sudden share loss; it is a persistent cap on ADI's unit growth in the largest electronics manufacturing base on earth, plus downward pressure on catalogue pricing at the low end that gradually shifts ADI's mix toward a narrower, higher-performance core. Export controls compound rather than solve this: restricting what ADI can ship to China simultaneously accelerates Chinese customers' motivation to design ADI out permanently. ADI's counter is the top of the performance stack — precision converters, RF, aerospace/defense-qualified parts — where the design and qualification barriers remain genuinely high.
Data-Center Power: A Genuinely New Analog Growth Vector
EMERGING TAILWIND
For twenty years analog was a GDP-plus business. AI infrastructure has changed that for one specific slice of it. As accelerator power draw climbs, the conversion chain from grid to core has to move to higher voltages and put the final conversion stage physically closer to the die — which raises the analog content per rack sharply and puts a premium on power density and efficiency. ADI's Communications segment, now more than 75% data-center-driven, grew 79% YoY to $555M in Q2 FY2026, and the company's own framing is that power delivery and efficiency are the gating factors for scaling AI compute. The pending Empower Semiconductor acquisition targets integrated voltage regulators for exactly this bottleneck.

Bull vs bear framing: The bull view is that this converts ADI from a cyclical industrial supplier into a structural AI-infrastructure beneficiary, justifying a permanently higher multiple and making the 26.8x forward P/E look reasonable against a 1.11 PEG. The bear view is arithmetic: at $555M a quarter this is 15% of revenue, and 85% of ADI is still a mid-single-digit-growth analog business lapping an artificially depressed base. The market is currently paying an AI-adjacent multiple for a company where the AI-adjacent piece is one segment in seven dollars of revenue. Both readings are defensible from the same data — which is precisely why the August 19 print and the FY2027 framing on that call matter more than usual.
