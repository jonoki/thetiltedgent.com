01 Company Overview

Archer-Daniels-Midland originates, transports, crushes, refines and ferments agricultural commodities — principally oilseeds and corn — and sells the outputs as vegetable oils, protein meals, sweeteners, starches, ethanol, and specialty human and animal nutrition ingredients. It is one of the four historic global grain merchants, operating through three reported segments: Ag Services & Oilseeds, Carbohydrate Solutions and Nutrition. FY2025 revenue was $80.27B, down 6.2% from $85.53B in FY2024 — the third consecutive annual decline from the $101.56B commodity-price peak of FY2022 — and revenue over the twelve months to September 2026 has turned back up to $84.06B. At a $33.71B market capitalisation (475.92M shares at the $70.84 close), the company is valued at about 40% of one year's revenue, which is the first clue that something unusual is going on.

Read the operating profit line, not the revenue line. This is the single most important framing on the page, so it is stated up front and repeated: over the last twelve months ADM's net profit margin is 2.95% and its gross margin is 7.44%. In almost any other business those numbers would be a screaming red flag. Here they are simply what the business is. ADM buys a commodity, adds a processing step, and sells a commodity; the raw material passes straight through the income statement at close to cost, so revenue measures the value of the grain flowing through the plants, not the size of the economic activity ADM actually owns. A 2–3% net margin is normal and healthy for an agricultural processor and is not, on its own, evidence of a problem. The number that matters is segment operating profit — the processing spread ADM keeps — and that figure went from $4.2B in FY2024 to $3.2B in FY2025 (−23%) and has since turned up hard: $1.38B in Q3 2026, up 64% from $845M a year earlier, and $3.60B for the first nine months, up 48%. On November 3 ADM raised its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance for the third time this year, to $5.50–$5.80. The central debate of this report is whether that turn is a lasting margin recovery or a policy-driven peak in the cycle that the market has already paid for.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
ORIGINATION FOOTPRINT RIVER & RAIL LOGISTICS CRUSH CAPACITY SCALE REPLACEMENT COST BARRIER FARMER RELATIONSHIPS 53-YEAR DIVIDEND RECORD WILMAR EQUITY STAKE

ADM's moat is physical and logistical, not commercial. It owns elevators where the grain is, barges and rail cars to move it, crush plants sited against that flow, and export terminals at the end of it. That network took a century to assemble and could not be rebuilt at anything near book value — which is why a business earning a ROIC of just 6.33% is nonetheless extremely hard to displace. But notice what the moat does and does not do. It reliably delivers volume: global crush volumes were 1.6% higher in Q3 2026, after roughly 5% more oilseed volume in Q2. It does not deliver price. ADM takes the market price on both sides of every transaction — it does not set the price of soybeans, of soybean meal, of soybean oil, of corn, or of ethanol. Its entire economic return is the spread between two prices it does not control, magnified by a fixed asset base it must run near capacity to cover its costs. That is why FY2025 segment operating profit fell 23% and Q3 2026 rose 64% with no meaningful change in the asset base or the strategy. The moat protects the franchise; it does not protect the earnings.

The second, softer moat is the dividend record: the board raised the quarterly dividend to 52 cents in February 2026, the 53rd consecutive year of increases. A 53-year streak is not a business advantage in any operational sense, but it is a genuine constraint on management behaviour and a real anchor for the shareholder base — it may help explain why this stock has a beta of 0.63 despite segment profit that rose 64% year over year in the latest quarter.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Policy is the margin. The company itself has put the 2026 recovery down to a constructive biofuels environment: the finalised 2026–2027 RVO, "policy incentives" supporting North American ethanol margins, and higher energy prices. That is an honest disclosure and a serious dependency: a meaningful share of the current earnings power is a regulatory decision, renewable on a policy cycle rather than a business cycle. The FY2025 release said the mirror image — that "ongoing uncertainty around U.S. biofuel policy created a challenging operating environment." The same lever works abroad: in Q3 2026 the delay to Brazil's biofuel policy changes held back ADM's South American crush margins. Same company, same assets, opposite results, driven by governments.
▸
Crush margin swings cannot be hedged away across a cycle. Crushing operating profit went from $844M (FY2024) to $159M (FY2025) — an 81% collapse — then earned $311M in Q3 2026 alone, against $13M a year earlier. ADM hedges individual positions, but it cannot hedge the spread itself across a full cycle. Industry capacity additions, Chinese soybean demand, South American harvest size and biodiesel feedstock demand all move that spread, and none of them are within management's control.
▸
Nutrition has not yet earned the higher valuation it was bought for. A decade of acquisitions produced a segment contributing $417M of FY2025 operating profit — about 13% of the company total — and $441M in the first nine months of 2026, about 12%. Q3 2026 Nutrition profit rose just 3%, with Human Nutrition down 3%. The strategic promise was a steadier business that investors would value more highly; the delivered result so far is a modest, improving, still-small business. Until Nutrition is materially larger, ADM trades as what it is: a commodity processor.
▸
Low returns on a large capital base. ROE of 10.64% and ROIC of 6.33% are still at or below most plausible estimates of the cost of capital, even after the earnings recovery. With $1.2–1.3B of capital expenditure guided for 2026, $8.74B of borrowings against $0.71B of cash at September 30, and trailing free cash flow of just $0.35B, the business consumes a great deal of capital to earn a spread it does not set. The $500–750M multi-year cost-reduction programme set out with the FY2025 results is management's answer, and it is the only lever here that does not depend on a commodity price or an EPA ruling.

Notable One-Off Items

Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.70 came in below adjusted EPS of $1.77. The gap is mostly $47M of impairment, exit and restructuring charges, partly offset by a $10M tax benefit from reversing a first-quarter provision after new U.S. Treasury guidance. Net earnings were $821M against adjusted net earnings of $858M. For the nine months: $4.18 GAAP against $4.33 adjusted, with $82M of impairment and restructuring charges, $55M of Wilmar-related charges and $66M of gains on asset sales among the adjustments. Neither gap is large enough to change any conclusion on this page — the adjusted-versus-GAAP gap that dominates the analysis of many companies is not a material issue at ADM.

Mark-to-market timing is the recurring distortion here, not restructuring charges. The company said Ag Services & Oilseeds included about $35M of net positive mark-to-market and timing effects in Q3 2026, after a Q2 in which Refined Products & Other fell 3% on mark-to-market effects. ADM carries large derivative and inventory positions that are revalued each period; gains and losses land in a quarter in which the physical transaction has not yet occurred. Quarter-to-quarter segment profit therefore contains genuine timing noise in both directions, and single-quarter comparisons should be treated accordingly.

The FY2025 base was genuinely depressed, which inflates every 2026 growth rate on this page. Crushing operating profit fell 81% in FY2025 to $159M. Q3 2026 Crushing profit of $311M against $13M a year earlier is about 24 times the prior-year figure, which says more about the base than the quarter — though the absolute dollars ($778M of AS&O operating profit in one quarter, against $1.6B for all of FY2025) are real.

Buybacks have restarted. ADM bought back 4 million shares for $360M in the third quarter (10-Q); the first nine months of 2025 had none. Dividends paid in the first nine months of 2026 were $764M. Two smaller Q3 items: Other Business benefited from a one-time release of insurance reserves, and Corporate costs fell because prior-year impairment losses did not recur. The $500–750M cost-reduction programme, set out with the FY2025 results as running three to five years from 2025, is a genuine structural item rather than a one-off: it is the only earnings lever on this page that is independent of commodity spreads and biofuel policy.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start where the money is actually made. ADM's income statement invites a category error, so it is worth walking the chain deliberately. FY2025 revenue: $80.27B. FY2025 gross profit: $5.03B — 6.3% of it. FY2025 total segment operating profit: $3.2B. FY2025 net income: $1.08B, or 1.3% of revenue. Each step down that ladder discards cost that was never ADM's economics in the first place: the farmer's grain, the freight, the energy. The ~2% net margin is a feature of the model, not a defect in it, and should never be used to argue the business is weak. A drugstore chain earns 2% net margins; so does a grain merchant; so does an airline in a good year. The question in every such business is the same — how many dollars of spread, on how much capital.

On the first half of that question, 2026 has been excellent. Total segment operating profit was $1.38B in Q3, up 64% year over year, and $3.60B for the first nine months, up 48%, with all three segments up over the nine months. The composition is instructive: in Q2, two of the three segments' revenue fell while profit rose (Carbohydrate Solutions revenue −1.3% with operating profit +22%; Nutrition revenue −4.6% with operating profit +51%), and in Q3 Nutrition revenue fell 4.1% while its profit edged up 3%. If you had read ADM's revenue line alone for the past four quarters you would have concluded the company was stagnating. Read the operating-profit line and you see the sharpest inflection in a decade. This is precisely why the segment operating profit disclosure, not the top line, is the number that matters.

The crush margin environment — what actually changed. ADM's core economics are the crush margin (the spread between the cost of a bushel of soybeans and the combined sale value of the meal and oil it yields) and the ethanol crush (the equivalent spread between corn cost and ethanol plus distillers' grains value). Both were severely compressed through 2025: Crushing segment operating profit fell from $844M in FY2024 to $159M in FY2025, −81%, which is as close to a zero as a business of that size gets. In Q2 2026 Crushing added $330M of operating profit year over year; in Q3 it earned $311M against $13M a year earlier, on what the company described as higher U.S. demand after the RVO was finalised in March, strong soybean meal demand and 1.6% higher global crush volumes. On the corn side, Vantage Corn Processors — the dry-mill ethanol operation — added $103M year over year in Q3, on top of an FY2025 in which it had already risen from $33M to $152M. Two separate spreads, both inflecting, both for the same underlying reason.

The biofuel and renewable-diesel policy backdrop is that reason, and it is the load-bearing risk. ADM said so directly: its Q2 release credited Crushing to "a constructive environment for biofuels, which was underpinned by the RVO," the finalised 2026–2027 Renewable Volume Obligations (the EPA's mandated biofuel blending volumes), and its Q3 release named RVO-driven demand alongside higher global energy prices. Carbohydrate Solutions' ethanol strength was attributed to "robust North American ethanol margins, including policy incentives." The mechanism is straightforward: mandated demand for biomass-based diesel pulls soybean oil out of the food market and into the fuel market, widening the crush margin; mandated ethanol blending plus high energy prices widens the corn crush. Neither spread is a function of ADM's operating skill. The company's own FY2025 language is the control experiment — Luciano then blamed "ongoing uncertainty around U.S. biofuel policy" for a challenging environment and guided 2026 to just $3.60–4.25. Six months later the same management guided $5.15–5.60, and on November 3, $5.50–5.80, again crediting a constructive biofuels environment. The guidance midpoint has risen about $1.73 in nine months, with the biofuels backdrop named as the main driver in the Q2 and Q3 releases. That is the bull case and the bear case in one sentence, depending on which direction you think policy travels next. On the November 3 call Luciano said he sees a 2027 market structure much like 2026's.

The dividend record is the other half of the story. The February 2026 raise to 52 cents quarterly was the 53rd consecutive annual increase — a streak that has survived the 2008 crisis, the 2014–2016 grain deflation, the pandemic and the 2025 crush collapse. The current $2.08 annualised payout yields 2.94% and is 40.6% of trailing GAAP earnings; against guided 2026 adjusted EPS of $5.50–5.80 the payout ratio is 36–38%. Cash is the weak spot: trailing free cash flow is $0.35B against roughly $1.0B of annual dividends, operating cash flow for the first nine months fell to $1.79B from $5.77B a year earlier, and 2026 capital expenditure is now guided at $1.2–1.3B. ADM also bought back $360M of stock in the third quarter.

Valuation — and why the Street is unimpressed. Be explicit about the earnings base. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $5.12, ADM trades at 13.8× — down from 23.8× at the previous edition, as the price fell and a weak 2025 quarter dropped out of the trailing year. (ii) On the midpoint of company-guided 2026 adjusted EPS ($5.65), it trades at 12.5×. (iii) On the 2027 consensus of $5.67 (stockanalysis.com, adjusted basis) it is also 12.5× — and that consensus implies almost no growth after 2026. (iv) Our enterprise value of about $41.7B is the $33.71B market cap plus $8.74B of borrowings less $0.71B of cash. Now put that against ROIC of 6.33%: on trailing numbers this business still earns below its cost of capital, and its price is being set on earnings driven by a spread it does not control and a policy it does not set. The sell-side's average target is $80.30, 13.4% above the price, yet 10 of 11 analysts rated the stock Hold or Sell in the October count. The stock sits 46% of the way up its 52-week range. The disagreement is no longer about whether 2026 earnings will be good — the company has raised its guide three times. It is about whether 2027 holds at that level, and what you pay for earnings that may be at a cyclical peak.

Bull Case

The FY2025 trough was policy-driven, not structural, and it has broken decisively: total segment operating profit rose 64% in Q3 2026 and 48% over nine months, management has raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance three times, from $3.60–4.25 to $5.50–5.80, and on the November 3 call Luciano said he sees a 2027 market structure much like 2026's. The asset network — origination, barge and rail logistics, crush capacity — is irreplaceable at book value and is running harder (global crush volumes +1.6% in Q3). A $500–750M cost programme adds self-controlled earnings on top. Leverage is conservative (D/E 0.37), the dividend has risen for 53 straight years and is covered about 2.5 times by trailing GAAP earnings, buybacks have restarted, and at 12.5× the midpoint of guided 2026 earnings with a 0.63 beta, an investor is paid 2.9% to wait — with the stock now 18.5% cheaper than in September.

Bear Case

The entire 2026 recovery traces to a spread ADM does not set and a policy it does not control — the company named the RVO and "policy incentives" as drivers itself, and the delay to Brazil's biofuel rules already held back South American margins in Q3. Trailing ROIC of 6.33% and ROE of 10.64% say the asset base does not earn its cost of capital across a cycle, and the FY2022 peak (EPS $7.71) was followed by three straight years of decline, so peak-cycle earnings have a demonstrated history of not lasting. Trailing free cash flow of $0.35B does not cover a dividend of about $1.0B a year. Nutrition, the steadier-earnings bet, grew profit just 3% in Q3 and is about 12% of nine-month segment profit after a decade of acquisitions. And the market sold the stock 6.8% on a quarter that beat and raised.

  Industry Commentary
Washington Sets the Crush Margin
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Vegetable oil is now a fuel feedstock as much as a food ingredient, and that single fact has made U.S. biofuel policy the dominant input into the profitability of every oilseed crusher on earth. ADM's own filings are the cleanest evidence: FY2025 Crushing operating profit fell 81% to $159M while management cited "ongoing uncertainty around U.S. biofuel policy"; in 2026, with the 2026–2027 Renewable Volume Obligations finalised, Crushing added $330M year over year in Q2 and earned $311M in Q3 against $13M a year earlier, and the company raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance from $3.60–4.25 to $5.50–5.80. Nothing about the plants changed. The Q3 release showed the same lever abroad: the delay to Brazil's biofuel policy changes held back South American margins. For the whole sector — ADM, Bunge, the private merchants — the mandated volume of biomass-based diesel is now a more important variable than the weather.
Deflating Commodity Prices, Widening Spreads
ONGOING HEADWIND
Agricultural commodity prices have deflated steadily from the 2022 shock — ADM's revenue fell from $101.56B (FY2022) to $80.27B (FY2025), three consecutive annual declines totalling 21%. For a processor this is genuinely ambiguous rather than simply bad: lower input costs can widen the processing spread even as the revenue line shrinks, which is exactly what Carbohydrate Solutions showed in Q2 2026 (revenue −1.3%, operating profit +22%). The headwind is real in two places, though: lower prices reduce the absolute dollar spread available on a given volume, and they squeeze farmer incomes, which over time reduces planted acreage and the origination volumes the whole network is sized for. Any analyst who reads a falling revenue line at an agricultural processor as deterioration has misread the business; any analyst who ignores multi-year commodity deflation entirely has misread the cycle.
Specialty Ingredients: The Escape Route Nobody Has Finished Building
EMERGING TAILWIND
Every large agricultural processor has spent the last decade trying to buy its way into flavours, proteins and specialty nutrition, on the thesis that formulated ingredients earn a higher and steadier margin than crushing. ADM's Nutrition segment earned $134M of Q3 2026 operating profit at a 7.3% operating margin, against 4.5% for Ag Services & Oilseeds — the margin thesis holds — but its profit rose only 3%, after +51% in Q2. Bull framing: if Nutrition gets back to Q2's pace while the cost programme delivers $500–750M, ADM's earnings become progressively less dependent on policy and investors may come to value it more like an ingredients company than a grain merchant. Bear framing: after ten years and many billions of acquisitions, Nutrition is 8.5% of nine-month revenue and about 12% of nine-month segment operating profit, and Q3 Human Nutrition profit fell 3%. Until that mix shifts materially, the RVO matters more to this stock than the flavour portfolio does, and the market is right to price it that way.
