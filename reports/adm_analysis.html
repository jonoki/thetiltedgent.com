01 Company Overview

Archer-Daniels-Midland originates, transports, crushes, refines and ferments agricultural commodities — principally oilseeds and corn — and sells the outputs as vegetable oils, protein meals, sweeteners, starches, ethanol, and specialty human and animal nutrition ingredients. It is one of the four historic global grain merchants, operating through three reported segments: Ag Services & Oilseeds, Carbohydrate Solutions and Nutrition. FY2025 revenue was $80.27B, down 6.2% from $85.53B in FY2024 — the third consecutive annual decline from the $101.56B commodity-price peak of FY2022. At a $41.89B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #623 globally, which is the first clue that something unusual is going on: it books more than $80B of revenue and is valued at half that.

Read the operating profit line, not the revenue line. This is the single most important framing on the page, so it is stated up front and repeated: ADM's net profit margin is 2.16% and its gross margin is 6.87%. In almost any other business those numbers would be a screaming red flag. Here they are simply what the business is. ADM buys a commodity, adds a processing step, and sells a commodity; the raw material passes straight through the income statement at close to cost, so revenue measures the value of the grain flowing through the plants, not the size of the economic activity ADM actually owns. A ~2% net margin is normal and healthy for an agricultural processor and is not, on its own, evidence of a problem. The number that matters is segment operating profit — the processing spread ADM keeps — and that figure went from $4.2B in FY2024 to $3.2B in FY2025 (−23%) and is now inflecting hard upward: $1.45B in Q2 2026, up 75% year over year. The central debate of this report is whether that inflection is a durable margin recovery or a policy-driven cyclical spike that the market has already paid for.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
ORIGINATION FOOTPRINT RIVER & RAIL LOGISTICS CRUSH CAPACITY SCALE REPLACEMENT COST BARRIER FARMER RELATIONSHIPS 53-YEAR DIVIDEND RECORD WILMAR EQUITY STAKE

ADM's moat is physical and logistical, not commercial. It owns elevators where the grain is, barges and rail cars to move it, crush plants sited against that flow, and export terminals at the end of it. That network took a century to assemble and could not be rebuilt at anything near book value — which is why a business earning a ROIC of just 4.96% is nonetheless extremely hard to displace. But notice what the moat does and does not do. It reliably delivers volume: the company moved roughly 5% more global oilseed volume in Q2 2026 through better asset utilisation. It does not deliver price. ADM is a price-taker on both sides of every transaction — it does not set the price of soybeans, of soybean meal, of soybean oil, of corn, or of ethanol. Its entire economic return is the spread between two prices it does not control, levered by a fixed asset base it must run near capacity. That is why FY2025 segment operating profit fell 23% and Q2 2026 rose 75% with no meaningful change in the asset base or the strategy. The moat protects the franchise; it does not protect the earnings.

The second, softer moat is the dividend record: the board raised the quarterly dividend to 52 cents in February 2026, the 53rd consecutive year of increases. A 53-year streak is not a business advantage in any operational sense, but it is a genuine constraint on management behaviour and a real anchor for the shareholder base — it is why this stock has a beta of 0.61 despite earnings that swing 75% in a quarter.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Policy is the margin. The Q2 2026 surge was explicitly attributed by the company to the finalised 2026–2027 RVO and to "policy incentives" supporting North American ethanol margins. That is an honest disclosure and a serious dependency: a meaningful share of the current earnings power is a regulatory decision, renewable on a policy cycle rather than a business cycle. The FY2025 release said the mirror image — that "ongoing uncertainty around U.S. biofuel policy created a challenging operating environment." Same company, same assets, opposite result, driven by Washington.
▸
Crush margin volatility is unhedgeable in aggregate. Crushing operating profit went from $844M (FY2024) to $159M (FY2025) — an 81% collapse — then added $330M in a single quarter in 2026. ADM hedges individual positions, but it cannot hedge the structural spread across a full cycle. Capacity additions across the industry, Chinese soybean demand, South American harvest size and biodiesel feedstock demand all move that spread, and none of them are within management's control.
▸
Nutrition has not yet earned the re-rating it was bought for. A decade of acquisitions produced a segment contributing $417M of FY2025 operating profit — about 13% of the company total — with FY2025 Human Nutrition profit actually down 2%. The strategic promise was a structurally higher multiple; the delivered result so far is a modest, improving, still-small business. Until Nutrition is materially larger, ADM trades as what it is: a commodity processor.
▸
Low returns on a large capital base. ROE of 7.64% and ROIC of 4.96% are at or below most plausible estimates of the cost of capital, even after the earnings recovery began. With $1.3–1.5B of annual capital expenditure guided for 2026 and $9.34B of total debt against $1.09B of cash, the business consumes a great deal of capital to generate a spread it does not set. The $500–750M multi-year cost-reduction programme is management's answer, and it is the right one — it is also the only lever here that does not depend on a commodity price or an EPA ruling.

Notable One-Off Items

Q2 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $1.87 came in above adjusted EPS of $1.84 — an unusual and, on its face, reassuring configuration: the adjustments made reported earnings slightly worse, not better. Net earnings were $908M against adjusted net earnings of $895M. Year-to-date the relationship reverses modestly: $2.49 GAAP versus $2.56 adjusted. Neither gap is large enough to change any conclusion on this page, which is itself worth saying — the adjusted-versus-GAAP gap that dominates the analysis of many companies is not a material issue at ADM.

Mark-to-market timing is the recurring distortion here, not restructuring charges. The company flagged that Refined Products & Other operating profit declined 3% in Q2 2026 specifically on mark-to-market effects. ADM carries large derivative and inventory positions that are revalued each period; gains and losses land in a quarter in which the physical transaction has not yet occurred. Quarter-to-quarter segment profit therefore contains genuine timing noise in both directions, and single-quarter comparisons should be treated accordingly.

The FY2025 base was genuinely depressed, which inflates every 2026 growth rate on this page. Crushing operating profit fell 81% in FY2025 to $159M. A +129% Q2 segment result measured against that base is arithmetically less impressive than it sounds — though the absolute dollars ($867M of AS&O operating profit in one quarter, against $1.6B for all of FY2025) are real.

No share repurchases occurred in 2025. Capital return was entirely by dividend — $510M paid in the first half of 2026. The $500–750M cost-reduction programme, running three to five years from 2025, is a genuine structural item rather than a one-off: it is the only earnings lever on this page that is independent of commodity spreads and biofuel policy.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $64.24 to the September 10, 2026 close of $86.91, ADM returned +35.3% in price over five years — a compound annual price return of about 6.2%. Dividends add meaningfully on top: the payout ran from $1.48 per share in FY2021 to $2.08 annualised now, so total return is materially higher than the price line implies, roughly two to two and a half percentage points a year. That is the defensive, dividend-growth profile the 0.61 beta advertises. It is also, unmistakably, a full round trip: the stock made its five-year monthly-close high of $97.50 in November 2022 and its low of $47.20 in February 2025 — a −51.6% drawdown over two and a quarter years — before rallying +84.1% off that low to today. Anyone who bought the 2022 commodity-shock peak is still 10.9% underwater on price nearly four years later.

Relative performance. A like-for-like total-return comparison against the S&P 500 (the benchmark index for this NYSE listing) and against the NASDAQ-100 over the identical October 2021 – September 2026 window could not be verified from the sources fetched and is therefore not stated as a number. What can be said from verified data on this page: ADM's 52-week price change is +41.12%, achieved almost entirely in the nine months since December 2025 ($57.49 month-end close) as the biofuel-policy and crush-margin backdrop turned; and the 0.61 beta means that over any full cycle this stock should be expected to lag a rising broad index and to fall less in a declining one. The five-year path is consistent with that: a deep, slow, commodity-cycle drawdown that had nothing to do with the equity market, followed by an equally commodity-driven recovery.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start where the money is actually made. ADM's income statement invites a category error, so it is worth walking the chain deliberately. FY2025 revenue: $80.27B. FY2025 gross profit: $5.03B — 6.3% of it. FY2025 total segment operating profit: $3.2B. FY2025 net income: $1.08B, or 1.3% of revenue. Each step down that ladder discards cost that was never ADM's economics in the first place: the farmer's grain, the freight, the energy. The ~2% net margin is a feature of the model, not a defect in it, and should never be used to argue the business is weak. A drugstore chain earns 2% net margins; so does a grain merchant; so does an airline in a good year. The question in every such business is the same — how many dollars of spread, on how much capital.

On the first half of that question, 2026 has been excellent. Total segment operating profit was $1.45B in Q2 alone, up 75% year over year, and $2.21B for the first half, up 40%. All three segments contributed. And the composition is instructive, because in two of the three segments revenue fell while profit rose: Carbohydrate Solutions revenue −1.3% with operating profit +22%; Nutrition revenue −4.6% with operating profit +51%. Only Ag Services & Oilseeds grew both lines (+10.1% revenue, +129% profit). If you had read ADM's revenue line alone for the past four quarters you would have concluded the company was stagnating. Read the operating-profit line and you see the sharpest inflection in a decade. This is precisely why the segment operating profit disclosure, not the top line, is the number that matters.

The crush margin environment — what actually changed. ADM's core economics are the crush margin (the spread between the cost of a bushel of soybeans and the combined sale value of the meal and oil it yields) and the ethanol crush (the equivalent spread between corn cost and ethanol plus distillers' grains value). Both were severely compressed through 2025: Crushing segment operating profit fell from $844M in FY2024 to $159M in FY2025, −81%, which is as close to a zero as a business of that size gets. In Q2 2026 Crushing added $330M of operating profit year over year on what the company described as improved margins plus roughly 5% higher global oilseed volumes through better asset utilisation. On the corn side, Vantage Corn Processors — the dry-mill ethanol operation — added $52M, on top of an FY2025 in which it had already risen from $33M to $152M. Two separate spreads, both inflecting, both for the same underlying reason.

The biofuel and renewable-diesel policy backdrop is that reason, and it is the load-bearing risk. ADM said so directly: the Q2 improvement reflected a "constructive biofuels environment supported by RVO finalisation," with the finalised 2026–2027 Renewable Volume Obligations (the EPA's mandated biofuel blending volumes) named alongside elevated global energy prices. Carbohydrate Solutions' ethanol strength was attributed to "robust North American ethanol margins, including policy incentives." The mechanism is straightforward: mandated demand for biomass-based diesel pulls soybean oil out of the food market and into the fuel market, widening the crush margin; mandated ethanol blending plus high energy prices widens the corn crush. Neither spread is a function of ADM's operating skill. The company's own FY2025 language is the control experiment — Luciano then blamed "ongoing uncertainty around U.S. biofuel policy" for a challenging environment and guided 2026 to just $3.60–4.25. Eight months later the same management guided $5.15–5.60. Roughly $1.40 of guided EPS moved on a policy outcome. That is the bull case and the bear case in one sentence, depending on which direction you think policy travels next.

The dividend record is the other half of the story. The February 2026 raise to 52 cents quarterly was the 53rd consecutive annual increase — a streak that has survived the 2008 crisis, the 2014–2016 grain deflation, the pandemic and the 2025 crush collapse. The current $2.08 annualised payout yields 2.39% and consumes 56.99% of trailing GAAP earnings. Against guided 2026 adjusted EPS of $5.15–5.60 that payout ratio falls toward 37–40%, the most comfortable coverage in three years. Free cash flow of $1.68B TTM covers roughly $1.0B of annual dividends with room, though not lavishly, and 2026 capital expenditure is guided at $1.3–1.5B. No shares were repurchased in 2025; the dividend is the capital-return policy.

Valuation — and why the Street is unimpressed. Be explicit about the earnings base. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $3.65, ADM trades at 23.8× — expensive for a commodity processor, but the denominator includes two quarters of the 2025 trough. (ii) On the mid-point of company-guided 2026 adjusted EPS ($5.375), it trades at 16.2×. (iii) On the reported forward P/E of 14.55×, the market is discounting an earnings base of roughly $5.97 — above the top of the company's own guidance range, i.e. some part of the Street is already modelling 2027 upside. (iv) Against enterprise value of $49.91B, the reported EV/EBITDA is 16.74× on trailing figures. Now put that against ROIC of 4.96% and the tension resolves: this is a business earning below its cost of capital on trailing numbers, being asked to support a mid-teens forward multiple on earnings that are driven by a spread it does not control and a policy it does not set. The sell-side's answer is an average target of $78.70, 9.4% below the current price, with 10 of 11 analysts at Hold or Sell. The stock sits 94% of the way up its 52-week range. The disagreement is not about whether 2026 earnings will be good — everyone now agrees they will be. It is about what multiple you pay for a cyclical peak.

Bull Case

The FY2025 trough was policy-driven, not structural, and it has broken decisively: total segment operating profit rose 75% in Q2 2026 with all three segments up, and management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance twice in six months, from $3.60–4.25 to $5.15–5.60. The asset network — origination, barge and rail logistics, crush capacity — is irreplaceable at book value and is now running at higher utilisation (oilseed volumes +5%). A $500–750M cost programme adds self-controlled earnings on top. Leverage is conservative (D/E 0.39), the dividend has risen for 53 straight years and is now covered far more comfortably, and at 16.2× the mid-point of guided 2026 earnings with a 0.61 beta, an investor is paid 2.4% to wait for Nutrition to keep compounding.

Bear Case

The entire 2026 recovery traces to a spread ADM does not set and a policy it does not control — the company named RVO finalisation and "policy incentives" as the drivers itself. Trailing ROIC of 4.96% and ROE of 7.64% say the asset base does not earn its cost of capital across a cycle, and the FY2022 peak (EPS $7.71) was followed by three straight years of decline, so peak-cycle earnings have a demonstrated history of not lasting. The stock has already risen 41% in twelve months to within 2.1% of its 52-week high; 10 of 11 analysts rate it Hold or Sell, the average target is 9.4% below the price, and the single most bullish target on the Street is only 9.3% above it. Nutrition, the de-cyclicalisation bet, is still only 14% of segment profit after a decade of acquisitions.

Top 3 Risks

01
Biofuel policy reversal or dilution. The finalised 2026–2027 RVO and associated incentives are, on the company's own account, the principal driver of both the crush and ethanol margin recovery. Any change to mandated volumes, to the treatment of feedstocks, or to the value of the credits feeds directly into two of three segments within a quarter or two. The FY2025 result is a fully worked example of what the downside looks like: Crushing profit −81%.
02
Crush margin mean-reversion at a cyclical peak multiple. Segment operating profit of $1.45B in a single quarter compares with $3.2B for all of FY2025. If H2 2026 does not sustain the Q2 run rate, the raised $5.15–5.60 guide is at risk, and the current 16× forward multiple is being paid on earnings that reverse. Industry crush capacity additions, South American harvest size and Chinese demand all influence this spread and none are under management's control.
03
Structurally low returns on capital. ROIC of 4.96% and ROE of 7.64% on trailing figures, against $1.3–1.5B of annual guided capital expenditure and $9.34B of debt, mean that even a good year may not clearly create economic value. The $500–750M cost programme and the Nutrition mix shift are the two answers on offer; both are multi-year, and neither has yet moved the return metrics enough to argue the case on its own.
  Industry Commentary
Washington Sets the Crush Margin
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Vegetable oil is now a fuel feedstock as much as a food ingredient, and that single fact has made U.S. biofuel policy the dominant input into the profitability of every oilseed crusher on earth. ADM's own filings are the cleanest evidence: FY2025 Crushing operating profit fell 81% to $159M while management cited "ongoing uncertainty around U.S. biofuel policy"; two quarters later, with the 2026–2027 Renewable Volume Obligations finalised, Crushing added $330M year over year in a single quarter and the company raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance from $3.60–4.25 to $5.15–5.60. Nothing about the plants changed. For the whole sector — ADM, Bunge, the private merchants — the mandated volume of biomass-based diesel is now a more important variable than the weather.
Deflating Commodity Prices, Widening Spreads
ONGOING HEADWIND
Agricultural commodity prices have deflated steadily from the 2022 shock — ADM's revenue fell from $101.56B (FY2022) to $80.27B (FY2025), three consecutive annual declines totalling 21%. For a processor this is genuinely ambiguous rather than simply bad: lower input costs can widen the processing spread even as the revenue line shrinks, which is exactly what Carbohydrate Solutions showed in Q2 2026 (revenue −1.3%, operating profit +22%). The headwind is real in two places, though: lower prices reduce the absolute dollar spread available on a given volume, and they squeeze farmer incomes, which over time reduces planted acreage and the origination volumes the whole network is sized for. Any analyst who reads a falling revenue line at an agricultural processor as deterioration has misread the business; any analyst who ignores multi-year commodity deflation entirely has misread the cycle.
Specialty Ingredients: The Escape Route Nobody Has Finished Building
EMERGING TAILWIND
Every large agricultural processor has spent the last decade trying to buy its way into flavours, proteins and specialty nutrition, on the thesis that formulated ingredients earn a higher and steadier margin than crushing. ADM's Nutrition segment posted $172M of Q2 2026 operating profit, up 51%, at a 9.0% operating margin versus 4.8% for Ag Services & Oilseeds — the margin thesis is visibly correct. Bull framing: if Nutrition keeps compounding at these rates while the cost programme delivers $500–750M, ADM's earnings become progressively less policy-dependent and the multiple should widen toward an ingredients peer rather than a merchant. Bear framing: after ten years and many billions of acquisitions, Nutrition is 8.6% of revenue and about 14% of H1 2026 segment operating profit, and FY2025 Human Nutrition profit actually fell 2%. Until that mix shifts materially, the RVO matters more to this stock than the flavour portfolio does, and the market is right to price it that way.
