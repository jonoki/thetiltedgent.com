01 Company Overview

Automatic Data Processing is the world's largest payroll and human capital management (HCM — the software and services that handle payroll, benefits, time and talent for an employer's workforce) provider, running pay for tens of millions of workers through platforms including RUN Powered by ADP (small business), ADP Workforce Now (mid-market), ADP Vantage and the newer ADP Lyric HCM (enterprise), plus a large PEO (professional employer organization — an arrangement where ADP becomes the co-employer of record for a client's staff, taking on payroll, benefits procurement and workers' compensation administration) business under the ADP TotalSource brand.

The latest quarter is the first of fiscal 2027 (July–September 2026), reported October 28, 2026: revenue of $5.49 billion, up 6% YoY (6% organic constant currency, meaning before acquisitions and currency swings), GAAP diluted EPS of $2.83 (+14%) and adjusted diluted EPS of $2.82 (+13%), and a raised full-year outlook. Fiscal 2026, which ended June 30, 2026, brought revenue of $21.95 billion (+6.7%), GAAP diluted EPS of $10.94 and adjusted diluted EPS of $11.12. ADP ranks #106 in the S&P 500 and #42 in the Nasdaq-100 by market value, and its 2026 proxy statement counts 51 consecutive years of dividend increases. Its own payroll data also produces a widely watched jobs indicator: the ADP National Employment Report.

Revenue Mix
Payroll, tax filing, time & attendance, benefits administration, talent and HR outsourcing sold across every client size band — from single-employee shops on RUN, through Workforce Now in the mid-market, to Lyric HCM and Vantage at enterprise scale, plus a multinational business spanning 140+ countries. FY2026 revenue rose 7% reported / 5% organic constant currency, with segment margin up 60 bps to 36.7% (Q4 margin 34.4%, up 90 bps). The three operating tells investors watch: new business bookings +6% to $2.2 billion for the year, client revenue retention flat at 92.1% — near record levels — and US pays per control +1%. Pays per control is ADP's same-store employment metric: the change in the number of employees on payrolls at ADP clients that have been on the books for at least a year. It strips out client wins and losses, so it is effectively a read on the US labour market itself, and at +1% for the year it signalled a cool one.

The first quarter of fiscal 2027 (to September 30, 2026) was a little warmer: Employer Services revenue rose 6% to $3.69 billion (6% organic constant currency), segment margin widened to 36.5% from 35.2%, and US pays per control grew 2%, against 0% a year earlier. That led ADP to lift its full-year pays-per-control outlook to about 1% (from 0–1%) and its Employer Services revenue outlook to about 6% (from 5–6%). It kept the bookings outlook at 4–7% growth and expects client revenue retention to slip 0.1–0.3 points from 92.1%; the release gives no first-quarter figure for either.

Critically, this segment also books interest on funds held for clients. ADP holds payroll and tax money in transit between employers and employees/tax authorities and invests it in a laddered (bonds bought to mature in a steady sequence over several years), high-grade fixed-income portfolio. In FY2026 average client funds balances were $40.4 billion (+7%) at an average yield of 3.4%, producing $1.355 billion of interest revenue, up 14% — roughly 6% of total company revenue and a far larger share of profit, since the associated cost is near zero. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 it rose 15% to $329.3 million on average balances of $36.9 billion at a 3.6% yield, and ADP raised the full-year outlook to $1.590–$1.610 billion (about 18% above fiscal 2026, from $1.540–$1.560 billion before) on 4–5% balance growth and a yield of about 3.8%. Because the portfolio is laddered out several years, lower-coupon bonds bought in the 2020–2021 zero-rate era are still rolling into higher-yielding paper, which is why the yield is guided up even as short-term rates ease. That mechanic also runs in reverse: a sustained fall in reinvestment rates would compress this line with a multi-year lag, and it is the single most rate-sensitive item in ADP's income statement.

Revenue Mix
Under a PEO arrangement ADP becomes the co-employer of record for a client's workforce: it runs payroll, files employment taxes, sponsors and procures the health and retirement benefit plans, and carries workers' compensation administration. The pitch to a 40-person business is Fortune-500 benefit pricing and compliance cover it could never buy alone. FY2026 revenue grew 7% to $7.13 billion on average paid worksite employees up 2% to roughly 762,000 (Q4 average ~775,000).

The margin optics need care. A large slice of PEO revenue is zero-margin pass-through — the health insurance premiums and statutory costs ADP collects and remits on the client's behalf. When pass-throughs grow faster than the service fee, reported segment margin falls even if underlying economics are unchanged, which was part of the story in FY2026: segment margin fell 1.1 percentage points to 13.1% (Q4 down 1.0 point to 12.2%). The FY2026 10-K lists four causes: higher selling and marketing expenses, the zero-margin benefit pass-through costs, a larger pre-tax loss at ADP Indemnity (ADP's workers' compensation insurer) and state unemployment insurance costs. Only the pass-throughs are an accounting effect; the other three are real costs. FY2027 guidance of 5–7% PEO revenue growth is the widest band in the outlook, reflecting how sensitive worksite-employee counts are to small-business hiring and to benefit-plan pricing. The first quarter landed in the middle: revenue +7% to $1.81 billion (+4% excluding pass-throughs), segment margin 13.0%, unchanged on the year, and 771,000 average worksite employees, up 2%.

ADP's moat is boring and durable. Payroll is a system of record that touches every employee, every tax authority and every benefits carrier a company deals with; ripping it out mid-year risks misfiled returns, penalties and unpaid staff. That fear shows up as 92.1% client revenue retention — meaning ADP keeps roughly $0.92 of every revenue dollar it had a year ago before any new sales. Layer on compliance depth across US federal, 50 states, thousands of local jurisdictions and 140+ countries, and the barrier is regulatory as much as technical: a startup can build elegant payroll UX in a year; it cannot build two decades of tax-agency filing relationships and audit history in a year. The float portfolio compounds this — $40.4 billion of client money in transit threw off $1.36 billion in fiscal 2026 of near-zero-cost interest income that a subscale competitor simply cannot replicate, because float scales with pays processed. Finally, the dataset. ADP's anonymized weekly payroll records cover more than 26 million US private-sector employees, and the ADP National Employment Report — produced with the Stanford Digital Economy Lab and released monthly ahead of the government's payroll print — turns that by-product into a national jobs indicator carried by every financial wire. It is the rarest kind of marketing asset: a free, credibility-building distribution channel that competitors have no way to buy.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Growth is capped by the labour market. Pays per control grew just 1% in FY2026. The first quarter of FY2027 was better at +2%, and ADP lifted its full-year outlook to about 1% (from 0–1%), but that still leaves little room. ADP's own National Employment Report showed private payrolls up 90,000 in September 2026 after a revised 36,000 in August, and its weekly NER Pulse had hiring slowing again to about 16,750 jobs a week in the four weeks to October 10. Same-store employment is the volume line under a large share of Employer Services revenue; when it goes to zero, growth has to come entirely from pricing, bookings and cross-sell, and a negative print would turn it into an outright drag.

2. Float income is a rate bet dressed as an operating line. $1.36 billion of FY2026 interest revenue carries almost no associated cost, so it drops disproportionately to profit. It is now guided up about 18% in FY2027, to $1.59–1.61 billion, as older low-coupon bonds mature, but that tailwind is mechanical and finite. If reinvestment rates fall materially, the portfolio yield stops climbing and eventually reverses — and because investors value this income at ADP's full ~25× earnings multiple, the risk to the share price is larger than the risk to earnings.

3. AI and the seat-based pricing question. HCM software has historically been priced per employee per month. If agentic AI (software that carries out tasks on its own) genuinely shrinks headcount in administrative and support functions, the billable unit shrinks. The market's verdict over the past year is split: Workday −15.5% and Paychex −6.8%, but Paycom +39.8% and Paylocity +19.1%, with ADP at +9.5% and the NASDAQ-100 (QQQ) at +12.5%. Prices have recovered from the spring lows; the question itself has not been settled.

4. Well-funded private challengers at the entry point. Rippling (~$1B ARR, $16.8B valuation), Deel (~$1B ARR, $17.3B valuation) and Gusto ($1B of actual trailing revenue, cash-flow positive, $9.3B valuation) are all going after small and mid-sized employers with modern, all-in-one software — precisely where RUN and Workforce Now sit (TechCrunch, May 7, 2026). None is yet material to a $22B revenue base, but three private challengers each at about $1 billion of revenue or ARR (annual recurring revenue) is a lot of new competition in ADP's core market.

Notable One-Off Items

The first quarter of fiscal 2027 had almost none: GAAP diluted EPS of $2.83 was a cent above adjusted EPS of $2.82. Fiscal 2026 carried a modest but identifiable set of non-recurring items, which together explain essentially all of the $0.18 gap between GAAP diluted EPS of $10.94 and adjusted diluted EPS of $11.12:

• Business alignment program — $91 million pre-tax charge. Severance and strategic project costs, the largest single adjustment of the year. This is a restructuring line, and investors should watch whether it recurs in FY2027 — a "one-off" that appears three years running is an operating expense.
• Legal settlement — $18 million net pre-tax charge.
• Workforce optimization reversals — $5 million pre-tax gain (a partial write-back of a prior-period charge).
• ADP Ventures investment gains — $8 million pre-tax gain on the corporate venture portfolio; inherently lumpy and not part of recurring operating earnings.

Two things are notable by their absence. There were no material realised losses on the client funds portfolio disclosed for FY2026 — unrealised marks on the available-for-sale portfolio flow through other comprehensive income rather than earnings, so a rate move can move book equity without touching EPS. And there were no significant divestitures; ADP's portfolio has been stable since the 2014 CDK Global spinoff. That spinoff is worth remembering for a different reason: ADP held AAA credit ratings until April 2014, when S&P and Moody's cut them over the planned separation of the auto-dealer services business (Bloomberg, April 10, 2014). Any description of ADP as "AAA-rated" is more than a decade out of date.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A 63% ROIC business with 92.1% revenue retention, a 51-year dividend growth streak and top short-term credit ratings is trading at 25.4× trailing earnings against 31.8× for the NASDAQ-100 (our weighted aggregate of the members' reports). FY2027 guidance, raised in October to 10–12% adjusted EPS growth, rests on a highly visible ~18% step-up in client funds interest revenue to $1.59–1.61 billion, 0.7–0.9 points of guided margin expansion, and productivity gains, partly from AI, that are already showing up in reported margin (+1.4 points in Q4 FY2026, +1.3 points in Q1 FY2027). Add a 2.38% dividend yield and a ~1.7% buyback yield and the shareholder return is ~4.1% before a dollar of growth. Pays per control already ran at +2% in the first quarter; if it merely holds near the ~1% outlook and investors pay a little more for each dollar of earnings again, the arithmetic works without heroics.

Bear Case

Employer Services grew 5% organically in fiscal 2026, and same-store employment is guided to only about 1% this year. A good part of FY2027's profit growth comes from the bond portfolio: client funds interest is guided up roughly $245 million (from $1.355 billion to $1.59–1.61 billion), income the market values at about 25× earnings and that shrinks if rates fall further. In October the outlook lines that went up were client funds interest, pays per control and Employer Services revenue; the bookings, retention, PEO and margin outlooks did not move, and the stock fell 2.6% on the day. Eleven of 18 analysts sit at Hold with an average target 3.5% above the price; after this print most listed calls raised their targets, Morgan Stanley's stayed in the high $280s (the two ratings tables disagree on its direction), and none changed its rating. Meanwhile three private challengers have each reached ~$1 billion of revenue attacking the small-business entry point, and a forward P/E of 23.2 versus a peer median of 18.7 means ADP is still the expensive way to own the industry.

01
US employment stalls or turns negative. Pays per control ran at +2% in Q1 FY2027, but the full-year outlook of about 1% leaves little cushion. ADP's own National Employment Report showed +90,000 private jobs in September 2026 after a revised +36,000 in August, and its weekly NER Pulse had hiring slowing again into mid-October. A negative same-store employment print would remove the volume contribution from Employer Services entirely and simultaneously pressure PEO worksite employee counts — the two exposures are correlated, not diversifying.
02
Float income reverses. $1.355 billion of FY2026 interest on funds held for clients is near-costless profit now guided to $1.59–1.61 billion in FY2027 on a portfolio yield rising to ~3.8%. That climb depends on maturing low-coupon bonds being reinvested at higher rates. A sustained decline in reinvestment yields flips this from a ~18% growth contributor to a multi-year drag, and because investors value it at the same ~25× earnings as the rest of ADP, the share-price damage would exceed the earnings damage.
03
Agentic AI compresses the billable unit. HCM pricing is fundamentally per-employee-per-month. If AI reduces headcount in the administrative and support roles that HR software bills for — or if AI-native entrants undercut on price because their cost to serve collapses — the pricing model erodes from both ends. The market has not made up its mind: over the year to October 29, 2026 the four listed HR-software peers range from Workday −15.5% to Paycom +39.8%. ADP's counter-argument is that its proprietary data on 26+ million employees is exactly the asset AI needs, and a 94% rise in Lyric HCM live clients in FY2026 suggests adoption is real. Which force wins is genuinely unresolved, and it is the dominant swing factor in what investors will pay for ADP's earnings.
  Industry Commentary
A Cooling US Labour Market Reads Straight Through to Revenue
ONGOING HEADWIND
Most companies have to infer macro conditions. ADP measures them. Its anonymized weekly payroll data covers more than 26 million US private-sector employees, and the National Employment Report it publishes with the Stanford Digital Economy Lab showed private payrolls rising 90,000 in September 2026, the first pickup in hiring since May, after a revised 36,000 in August; base pay rose 3.2% year over year. Its weekly NER Pulse then had hiring slowing to about 16,750 jobs a week in the four weeks to October 10. The internal equivalent, pays per control, grew 1% in FY2026 and 2% in Q1 FY2027, and the full-year outlook is now about 1%. That metric strips out client wins and losses, so it isolates whether ADP's existing clients are hiring — and they are adding staff, slowly. It also hits PEO from the same direction: average worksite employees grew 2% to 771,000 in the quarter. Same-store employment is the one growth input ADP cannot manufacture with product, pricing or sales execution. The October report is due November 4 and the next NER Pulse November 10, 2026.
Float Income: A Mechanical Tailwind With a Rate-Path Expiry
EMERGING TAILWIND
ADP holds payroll and tax dollars in transit — $40.4 billion of average client funds balances in FY2026, up 7% — and invests them in a laddered high-grade bond portfolio. That produced $1.355 billion of interest revenue, up 14%, at an average yield of 3.4%, on essentially zero associated cost. Q1 FY2027 added $329.3 million, up 15%, and in October ADP raised the FY2027 outlook to $1.590–$1.610 billion (from $1.540–$1.560 billion) on 4–5% balance growth and a yield of ~3.8%. The counterintuitive part: the yield is guided up even as short-term rates ease, because bonds bought in the 2020–21 zero-rate era are still maturing and being reinvested at far higher coupons. ADP's broader measure, the net impact of its client funds extended investment strategy, is guided to the same $1.590–$1.610 billion (from $1.545–$1.565 billion). But the mechanism is arithmetic, not skill, and it has a finite life. Once the legacy low-coupon paper has fully rolled, portfolio yield converges on prevailing reinvestment rates — and a sustained fall in those rates turns this line from ADP's most reliable earnings tailwind into a multi-year headwind. Balances themselves also shrink if employment and wages soften, which correlates the two risks in this section.
AI in HR Software: Does It Kill the Seat or Feed the Moat?
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the question the whole sector's valuation now hinges on, and the market has already priced an answer. Over the year to October 29, 2026 the answer has split the group: Workday −15.5% and Paychex −6.8%, but Paycom +39.8% and Paylocity +19.1%, against the NASDAQ-100 (QQQ) at +12.5% and ADP at +9.5%. HCM software is sold per employee per month; if agentic AI genuinely shrinks administrative and support headcount, the billable unit shrinks with it — and AI-native entrants with radically lower cost-to-serve can price beneath incumbents (Gusto says AI now writes half its new code and handles an equal share of support cases).

Bull framing: ADP is the data layer, not the seat. Payroll records for 26+ million US workers, 140+ countries of tax logic and 77 years of compliance history are precisely the proprietary substrate that general-purpose models cannot synthesise. Adoption is measurable rather than aspirational — Lyric HCM live clients up 94% in FY2026 and ADP Assist agents available to nearly all clients — and management says AI tools embedded across product, service and sales teams are improving quality and productivity, while adjusted EBIT margin rose 1.4 points in Q4 FY2026, 0.8 points for fiscal 2026 and 1.3 points in Q1 FY2027. If AI reshapes jobs rather than eliminating them at scale, the seat count survives and ADP keeps the productivity gain.

Bear framing: incumbents always sound like this early. If AI's cost savings are competed away in price rather than retained as margin, ADP ends up with the same seats, lower revenue per seat and a permanently lower valuation. And the productivity gains ADP describes are available to every competitor, and to entrants with no legacy service organisation to shrink. The honest position is that this is unresolved, that it hung over the whole group during the 37.6% fall from the May 2025 peak, and that no quarter of results in the next year will settle it.
