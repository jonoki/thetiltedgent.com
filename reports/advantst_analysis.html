01 Company Overview

Every chip is tested before it ships, often more than once: while it is still on the silicon wafer, and again after it has been cut out and packaged. Advantest builds the machines that do it — automatic test equipment (ATE) — and the handlers and interface boards that feed chips into them. It is the market leader: on its own estimate it held about 65% of the whole tester market in calendar 2025, including about 66% of the market for testers of logic chips (processors, AI accelerators, custom chips — Advantest calls these SoC testers) and about 60% of the memory-tester market. In the year to March 2026 (Advantest calls it FY2025) it booked ¥1,128.6B of sales, up 44.7%, operating income of ¥499.1B (+118.8%) and net income of ¥375.4B — all records. Almost none of it is sold at home: 99.4% of sales in the six months to September 2026 went to customers outside Japan, and more than half of the July–September quarter's sales were shipped to Taiwan, where, Advantest says, most of the logic business of US fabless (design-only) chip companies lands.

The story of the last three years is AI. An AI accelerator is a stack of chips and memory in one package, and each new generation needs longer tests and more test steps than the one before. Advantest's own framing, in its prepared remarks for the July 29, 2026 briefing: "While semiconductor unit volumes often attract the most attention, we believe the more important driver of future tester demand is not just unit volumes alone, but the continued growth in test content and test insertions with each new device generation." That is why sales have nearly tripled from ¥416.9B in the year to March 2022 to FY2025, and why the company now guides to ¥1,863.0B for FY2026 (April 2026 – March 2027), up 65.1%. It has raised that forecast twice: by ¥294.0B in July and by another ¥149.0B on October 28, after a second record quarter. July–September sales were ¥477.7B (+81.7%), the operating margin 55.9% and basic EPS ¥267.75, all above the company's own July guidance. In its prepared remarks Advantest described the AI chip market as being in "a period of high growth that could be described as a 'megacycle,'" with demand spreading from GPUs to custom ASICs, CPUs and high-performance memory. The stock had priced in a great deal already: it set a record close of ¥41,850 on October 6, then fell with other AI chip stocks, and closed at ¥34,860 on October 30, 2026 — up 56.5% in a year and about 14.9-fold since the October 2021 month-end, valuing the company at ¥25.2 trillion ($159.5B), 44 times trailing earnings and 34 times the company's own forecast for this year.

Where the testers go

By ship-to region in Q2 FY2026 (company presentation, chart data): Taiwan ¥261.3B (54.7%), China ¥90.1B (18.9%), South Korea ¥64.6B (13.5%), the Americas, Europe and other regions ¥59.9B together (12.5%) and Japan ¥1.8B (0.4%). Taiwan is the largest because, in Advantest's words in the July Q&A, most US fabless (design-only) logic business "is landing there" — the chips are made and packaged by Taiwanese foundries and packaging houses; in Q2 the company said sales for high-end SoC applications there rose from Q1. China rose 72.9% from ¥52.1B a year earlier, on both logic and memory testers. In July Advantest said China had stayed around 20% of sales and expected it to remain at that level or lower until new Chinese fabs open around 2028–2029.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

~66% SoC tester share (CY2025) V93000 platform standard TechInsights #1 supplier, 7 years running Installed base → support revenue Capacity: 5,000+ → 10,000 systems/yr 18-month customer forecasts Net cash, R&I AA- rating

The moat is the platform. In July Advantest said its V93000 covers x86 and Arm processors, fabless designers and integrated chipmakers alike, so market-share shifts among CPU makers "are not really relevant" to it, and customers can buy capacity and reconfigure it for different chips. Scale now reinforces that: in October Advantest said it already has capacity for more than 5,000 SoC testers a year and aims to reach 10,000 well ahead of the original plan (the end of FY2028), and that a further doubling to 20,000 a year is under active internal discussion. It runs a hybrid model — contract manufacturers plus its own plants — to add capacity without becoming the bottleneck. It also says close customers now share 18-month forecasts, against roughly six months historically, which it attributes to the industry's scale and to constraints in advanced packaging and wafer starts.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Memory chips are an input cost. Testers use a lot of DRAM, and the Q2 release says demand for key components such as memory chips "continues to exceed supply as AI investments expand across industries." In the October Q&A Advantest said the first half was helped by DRAM bought before prices rose and by a one-time reversal of inventory write-downs, that higher DRAM and other component costs are the main reason the operating margin falls in the second half, and that they will remain a headwind. It plans to offset much of it through pricing on higher-specification tester configurations, licences and new instruments rather than direct price rises, and guides a second-half operating margin in the low 50s (50.1% on the forecast, against 54.0% in the first half).
02
Concentration — of customers and of geography. More than half of Q2 sales shipped to Taiwan, and computing and communications chips are forecast at about 90% of SoC tester sales this year. A pause in AI chip production or in advanced-packaging capacity (which Advantest itself names as an industry uncertainty) would hit it directly.
03
Execution of a fast capacity ramp. Production leans on contract manufacturers plus long-term component agreements, and the forecast needs second-half sales about 20% above the first half (¥1,017.8B against ¥845.2B), with SoC tester sales rising again in Q3 and further in Q4. Advantest says memory supply is tight across the industry and "we are not immune"; customers understand components must be found for testers to ship. Missing deliveries in a tight market is how share is lost.
04
A cyclical stock at a high multiple. In the five-year chart the shares fell 26.5% in April 2024, 20.3% in March 2025 and 24.3% in March 2026 — single months — and in October 2026 they fell 25.8% in 13 sessions, from the ¥41,850 record close on October 6 to ¥31,070 on October 26. At 44 times trailing earnings, the price assumes the AI test boom runs for years.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things flatter the recent numbers. First, investment gains below operating income: first-half financial income of ¥57.0B includes ¥54.2B of valuation gains on financial instruments tied to strategic investments — ¥41.1B in Q1 and about ¥13.1B in Q2 (our arithmetic) — and Q4 FY2025 included ¥17.3B from valuing share call options. All of it sits in trailing EPS. Strip out the Q2 gain at Q2's effective tax rate of about 30.0% (¥82.9B of tax on ¥276.2B) and Q2 EPS would have been about ¥255, still about 23% above the ¥207.79 consensus read on October 5 (our estimate). Second, the gross margin: Advantest says the first half was helped by DRAM bought before prices rose and by a one-time reversal of inventory write-downs; it guides about 68% for the year against 70.4% in the first half. Third, the yen: the dollar averaged ¥161 in Q2 against ¥146 a year earlier. The forecast assumes ¥150 for the second half, and Advantest now says each yen of weakness against the dollar adds ¥5.8B to FY2026 operating income. At the October 30 rate of ¥157.80 that would be roughly ¥45B of upside if it held (our arithmetic on the company's full-year sensitivity).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Test demand is growing faster than chip volumes, because every AI generation adds test time and test steps, and inference AI is spreading demand from GPUs to custom ASICs, server CPUs and DRAM. Advantest holds about two-thirds of the logic-tester market and says it can reach 70% or more; it already has capacity for more than 5,000 systems a year, is bringing 10,000 forward and is discussing 20,000. It says the tester market has grown about 50% a year on average over the past few years and it does not see that slowing. FY2026 guidance (¥1,863.0B sales, 51.9% operating margin, EPS ¥1,027.08) assumes ¥150 to the dollar for the second half, below the October 30 rate of ¥157.80, and Q2 beat the company's own guidance by about 10% on sales and 19% on operating income. With ¥294.5B of net cash, AA- credit and a ¥280B buyback under way, a 34x forward multiple falls quickly if earnings keep growing the way consensus (about ¥1,330 EPS for FY2027, S&P Global via StockAnalysis, read Oct 31) expects.

Bear Case

At ¥34,860 the stock trades at 44 times trailing earnings and 34 times the company's forecast, with a monthly RSI of 76.5. October showed how fast sentiment turns: a 25.8% fall from the record close in 13 sessions, and a 5.9% drop on October 30 as worries about AI spending hit chip stocks, the day after a strong print. Management guides the operating margin down to about 50% in the second half as DRAM costs rise, the first half had a one-time inventory benefit, and trailing EPS includes ¥71.5B of investment and option gains over three quarters. The business depends on one end market — about 90% of forecast SoC tester sales go into computing and communications chips — and on Taiwan for more than half of Q2 shipments. In July Advantest said memory-tester share may fall this year. The chart shows falls of 26.5%, 20.3% and 24.3% in single months within the past two and a half years.

Top 3 Risks

01
An AI capex pause. SoC testers are 73.8% of forecast FY2026 sales and about 90% of that goes into computing and communications chips. Advantest names advanced-packaging capacity and memory supply as industry uncertainties; any slowdown in AI chip output reaches its orders quickly, and the stock's beta of 2.37 to the Nikkei 225 says it would move more than the market.
02
Memory costs squeeze margins. The forecast assumes a full-year gross margin of about 68% against 71.1% in Q2, and an operating margin of 50.1% in the second half against 54.0% in the first. Advantest calls higher DRAM and component costs a headwind that will remain, and plans to offset much of it through pricing on higher-specification configurations rather than direct price rises; if memory prices rise further, that plan is tested.
03
Valuation leaves no room for a miss. 44x trailing and 34x guided earnings; even after the October sell-off the price is 1.9 times its 52-week low. The ¥100B convertible bond due 2031 can also add shares if it is converted.
Industry Commentary
Test intensity: why a tester company grows faster than chip units
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Advantest estimates the tester market at about $9.0B in 2025 ($6.9B logic, $2.1B memory) and $13.0–14.5B in 2026, a range it kept in October with logic near the top and memory near the midpoint. In the October Q&A it said the market has grown at an annual average of about 50% or slightly more over the past few years, that it does not see that slowing, and that it will give a CY2027 figure in January. Its explanation is test content. In the July Q&A it said 3D packaging is making accelerators more complex, adding test time and test insertions (separate test steps). It also said accelerators need more test time at final test than CPUs, while wafer-level testing is similar or slightly higher for CPUs — so growth in CPUs adds volume, and growth in accelerators adds intensity.

The memory squeeze reaches equipment makers
COST HEADWIND

The same AI boom that fills Advantest's order book has made DRAM scarce and expensive, and a tester is full of it. Advantest's results release says demand for key components such as memory chips "continues to exceed supply". The company has long-term agreements with component suppliers, but its guidance has the gross margin at about 68% for the year against 71.1% in Q2. Memory makers are also customers, and Advantest expects the memory market itself to "expand significantly over the next several years as new fabs come online", in the July Q&A summary's words.

A capacity race in a cyclical industry — bull and bear framing
SWING FACTOR

The bull framing: Advantest called this an "elongated and durable cycle" in July and a "megacycle" in October, backs it with 18-month customer forecasts, and is building capacity ahead of demand with buffers so it is never the bottleneck — the Q&A summary says it never wants to be the critical path for customers. The bear framing: semiconductor equipment has always been cyclical, and capacity built for a boom becomes a cost in a downturn. The chart's own history — a 38.5% fall in 2022, three months of 20%+ declines since 2024 and a 25.8% slide in October 2026 that came with no change in the company's outlook — shows how fast the market reprices when sentiment about AI spending wobbles.
