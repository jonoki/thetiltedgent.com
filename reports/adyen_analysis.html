01 Company Overview

Adyen is the Amsterdam company that large merchants use to take payments online, in stores and inside apps through one system it built itself. It is also a licensed bank, supervised by De Nederlandsche Bank, which lets it plug straight into the card networks instead of renting that access from another bank. Its customers include Google, Microsoft, Spotify and Uber, and in the first half of 2026 it added OpenAI, Aritzia, Xiaomi and the UK government's GOV.UK Pay. FY2025 net revenue was €2,364.2M, up 18% reported and 21% in constant currency, with EBITDA of €1,245.7M (a 53% margin) and net income of €1,062.5M. The first half of 2026 brought €1,302.9M of net revenue (+19%, +21% in constant currency), €803.8B of payments processed (+24%) and diluted EPS of €17.18, up 12.9% from €15.22. The Q3 2026 business update on October 28 showed growth speeding up: net revenue of €738.1M, up 23% (22% in constant currency), on €451.1B of volume (+30%). A quarterly update reports revenue and volume, not profit, so the earnings figures on this page still run to June 30.

The business kept growing; the share price did not. The October 30, 2026 close of €826.90 is 39.9% below the €1,375.00 year-end 2025 close and 68.3% below the October 2021 month-end where this report's chart starts. The turn came on February 12, 2026. At its November 2025 Investor Day Adyen had said: "We expect annual net revenue to grow between the low- and mid-twenties in 2026." The February letter guided to 20 to 22% in constant currency, and the shares fell 21.9% that session, their worst day of the past year. The CFO then announced his exit (May 27), the stock hit its 52-week closing low of €785.20 on June 11, and the H1 results on August 13 sent it up 16.4% in a day. That gain had gone by early October. Then on October 28 the Q3 update beat revenue forecasts but cut the 2026 EBITDA margin outlook to 50% and dropped the medium-term margin target, citing faster investment in people, its platform and products. The shares fell 13.35% that session (€944.00 to €818.00), their second-worst day of the past year. At about 23 times trailing earnings, the market now asks two questions: can Adyen keep growing near 20% a year as its largest customers move to lower volume-tier prices, and when will the extra spending show up as higher profit?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

One global platform, built in-house Own banking licences Payment data across channels Share of wallet compounds over a decade Blue-chip enterprise base Net cash, A- credit rating

Adyen's advantage is architectural. It runs one codebase and holds its own licences, so a merchant can switch on a new country or a store network without a new integration. The H1 2026 letter names three layers: a single global platform, global banking licences, and "Dynamic Identification", the data layer that recognises a shopper across online and in-store payments. The test of a moat is whether customers deepen over time, and Adyen's numbers say they do: Google, Microsoft, Spotify and Uber are still expanding usage after a decade, and share of wallet typically doubles from under 20% to over 40%. The 2026 strategy is to widen the moat beyond the payment itself. In the letter's words, "we are no longer just a payments company", with loyalty (Talon.One), usage-based billing (Orb), agent-driven shopping (Adyen Agentic) and treasury (Intelligent Money Movement) added around the transaction.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Scale lowers the price. Take rate fell to 16.2 bps from 16.8 bps a year earlier. The more a big merchant processes, the lower its per-transaction fee, so Adyen's success with its largest customers turns into slower revenue than volume. Volume +24% against net revenue +19% in H1 2026 is the size of that gap today.
02
Big customers move the numbers. In FY2025 one large-volume customer pulled group volume growth from 21% to 8%. Growth is broadening (300 merchants now drive about 60% of it, down from over 70%), but a handful of global enterprises still decide a large part of each year's result.
03
Short visibility, high bar. In February the company wrote that its "visibility into customer plans, and therefore into our own growth outlook, is most meaningful over a 6 to 12 month horizon." The shares fell 21.9% on the day the 20–22% guide replaced the November 2025 "low- and mid-twenties" aim, and 13.35% on October 28, when the margin outlook was cut. A company valued on steady 20% growth that can see only a year ahead is exposed to every guidance update.
04
Margin and capex move the wrong way in 2026. In October Adyen cut its 2026 EBITDA margin outlook to 50% (Reuters put the previous expectation at about 52%) to spend more on people, the platform and products, and dropped its medium-term EBITDA objective, an EBITDA margin above 55% in 2028, "to retain flexibility" (Q3 2026 business update). 2026 capex is guided to about 7% of net revenue (5% in FY2025 and in H1 2026) as Adyen pulls data-centre spending forward from 2027. Adyen still expects margins to widen over time but now gives no target; the 2027 outlook comes in February. Meanwhile, finance is led by an interim CFO until February 2027.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items shape the 2026 figures. First, the acquisitions: Talon.One (preliminary consideration €750M, of which €69.8M paid in Adyen shares) and Orb (US$335M, US$33.2M in shares) closed on July 1, 2026. H1 opex carries €6.0M of transaction costs, and together the deals are expected to add about one point to 2026 net revenue growth. In October Adyen lowered its 2026 EBITDA margin outlook to 50%, pointing to faster investment in people, platform and products. Second, capex: Adyen is "proactively pulling investment from 2027 into H2 2026" to secure data-centre capacity and pricing, lifting 2026 capex to about 7% of net revenue from a usual 5%; free cash flow in H2 will carry that. Third, interest income: H1 finance income of €143.2M, mostly from balances at central and commercial banks, equals 19.9% of pre-tax income (€720.0M). A lower-rate world trims net income without touching the payments business; the FY2025 letter noted that net income growth "was hindered by the interest rate environment." Adyen reports no adjusted EPS, so the P/E above uses IFRS diluted earnings.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 30, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end of €2,610.50 to the October 30, 2026 close of €826.90, Adyen lost 68.3% of its price; it pays no dividend, so that is also the total return. Over the same window the AEX rose 39.9% on price. The path has been violent in both directions. The chart starts at its peak: 2022 took the stock down to €1,288.40 by December. On August 17, 2023, the day Adyen published H1 2023 results with net revenue up 21% but EBITDA down 10%, the shares fell 39.0% in one session, and August closed down 54.3%, the worst month in the window. October 2023's €633.90 is the five-year low. November 2023, the month of the Investor Day, was the best month (+69.0%, including +37.8% on November 9, the session after it). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At €826.90 you pay 23.2 times trailing and 21.8 times this year's consensus earnings for a company whose net revenue grew 22% in constant currency in Q3, with a TTM EBITDA margin of 52%, no borrowings, €4.6B of its own cash and an A- credit rating. The customer data supports durability: share of wallet compounds from under 20% to over 40% over a decade, growth is spreading beyond the top 300 merchants, and Platforms (+37%) and Unified Commerce (+25%) are growing faster than the mature Digital pillar. The 2026 margin and capex dents are spending choices, not lost business: Q3 growth sped up from the first half, and the update reports early enterprise wins from the Talon.One and Orb integrations. Every firm that stated a rating in an action since early October rates it Buy, with an average target 54% above the price. If the February 2027 outlook pairs about 20% growth with a margin near 50%, the gap between the numbers and the price is hard to justify.

Bear Case

The bear case says the decline in the growth rate is structural. Each upgrade in scale with the largest merchants comes at a lower take rate (16.2 bps from 16.8 bps), so volume growth of 24% delivers 19% revenue growth, and the next billion euros of volume is priced lower again. The company says it can see only six to twelve months ahead, it guided below its own November 2025 growth aim three months later, and in October it cut the 2026 margin outlook to 50% and dropped its 2028 margin target, five weeks after saying its financial objectives were unchanged. Finance is led by an interim CFO until February 2027. About a fifth of pre-tax profit is interest on cash, which falls with rates. Two acquisitions show that Adyen is now buying growth beyond payments rather than only building it, and they cost margin first. A price of 23 times trailing earnings is not cheap for a company whose growth may settle in the mid-teens and whose margin no longer has a target.

Top 3 Risks

01
Growth falls short of the 20% story. The investment case rests on about 20% annual net revenue growth for years after 2026. February 2026 showed what a two-point gap between aim and guidance does to the price (−21.9% in a day), and October 28 showed the same for margins (−13.35%). The February 11, 2027 results, with the 2027 objectives, are the next test.
02
Take-rate erosion at the top of the customer base. Volume tiering is the cost of winning more of each large merchant's payments. If big customers keep shifting volume to Adyen faster than smaller, higher-priced merchants grow, net revenue keeps lagging volume, as it did in H1 2026 (19% against 24%).
03
Execution through a leadership and integration year. An interim CFO until February 2027, a new executive leadership model from January 31, 2027, two acquisitions to integrate, faster hiring and capex pulled forward from 2027. Each is manageable; together they add risk to the guidance in the year the market trusts it least, and October's margin cut has already tested that trust.
Industry Commentary
Payments stocks in 2026: the sector, not just Adyen
SECTOR SELL-OFF

Over the year to October 30, 2026, Worldline fell 57.8%, Adyen 45.5%, Fiserv 23.3%, PayPal 16.1% and Nexi 3.5%, while Block rose 3.1% and the AEX 15.6%. Four of the five listed peers in this report's set fell; each has its own story, and together they show investors paying less for payment companies' earnings. Adyen's own figures show where scale costs it: in H1 2026 processed volume rose 24% but net revenue 19%, because the largest merchants pay less per transaction as they grow. The H2 2025 letter also expected "market volume growth to remain broadly in line with 2025 levels, reflecting continued macroeconomic uncertainty."

Beyond the transaction: loyalty, billing, agents
STRATEGY SHIFT

Adyen's answer to a maturing payments market is to sell more around each payment. In 2026 it bought Talon.One (loyalty and promotions) and Orb (usage-based billing for software and AI companies), launched Adyen Agentic so merchants can accept payments started by AI shopping agents, and launched Intelligent Money Movement for treasury and payouts. Management's own framing in the H1 release: "we built the complete financial operating system for modern commerce to position us for sustained, durable growth." The deals add about one point of revenue growth in 2026. The first cost is already visible: the October update cut the 2026 EBITDA margin outlook to 50%, citing faster investment in people, platform and products. Whether the new products add margin later, or turn Adyen into a broader software company with a lower margin, will take several halves to show.

The valuation gap: price vs. the Street
WIDE DISAGREEMENT

The average analyst target of €1,276.29 is 54% above the October 30 close, and the lowest target (€863) is still above it. On our numbers Adyen trades at 23.2 times trailing earnings, about 16.1 times EBITDA after cash and a 4.4% free-cash-flow yield. The bull framing: a 20%-growth, net-cash, high-margin platform has been marked down with a sector whose weaker members are shrinking. The bear framing: after the update analysts cut their targets (JP Morgan to €1,160, Jefferies to €1,077, DZ Bank to €1,250) but kept them far above the price, and the price says the market trusts the margin story less than they do. The 2027 objectives on February 11 will show which side was right.
