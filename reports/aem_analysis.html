01 Company Overview

Agnico Eagle is a senior gold producer: ten operating mines in Quebec, Ontario, Nunavut, Australia, Finland and Mexico, about 85% of its gold coming out of Canada (85.2% of Q3 2026 ounces). Its market value is $83.6B at the October 30, 2026 close (our calculation: 505.55M shares × $165.38). The common shares trade one-for-one in Toronto and New York — there is no ADR ratio to convert — and the company reports in US dollars, so every per-share figure on this page is already USD per NYSE share. The 2026 guide is 3.3–3.5 million ounces of gold, which management said on October 28 it expects near the low end.

The whole report comes down to one subtraction. In Q3 2026 (July–September) Agnico sold gold at an average realised $4,264/oz and spent an AISC of $1,648/oz. That leaves a margin of $2,616 per ounce, up from $2,145 a year earlier ($3,476 realised − $1,331 AISC, restated, in Q3 2025) but down from $3,024 in Q2 2026. The realised price rose 22.7% year over year; the margin rose 22.0%. Because most mining costs do not move with the gold price, a change in the price swings profit by a much larger percentage. Over five years the stock is up 219% on price. Management puts a number on it: "Every $100 move in the gold price impacts our after-tax free cash flow … by about a quarter of a million dollars" — the transcript's wording (Investing.com, September 9, 2026); the same Investing.com write-up gives the figure as about $250 million, a quarter of a billion dollars, or about $0.49 per share. The arithmetic of what that means for the stock is in Section 02.

Today's company is the product of the Kirkland Lake Gold merger of February 2022, which brought Detour Lake (now the largest single mine in the portfolio), Macassa and Fosterville into the group; FY2022 revenue grew 48.4%, consistent with that step-change. The home market is the Toronto Stock Exchange (S&P/TSX Composite); the benchmark on this page is the S&P 500, which AEM is not a member of.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Tier-one jurisdictions (85% Canada) AISC $1,648/oz (Q3) Detour Lake scale Net cash $3.42B Organic pipeline (Hope Bay, Odyssey, Upper Beaver) District-scale Abitibi infrastructure

A gold miner cannot differentiate its product, so its moat is its place on the cost curve and the politics of where its rock sits. Agnico has both: most of its ounces come from Canada, a jurisdiction where the risk of a mine being taxed away or nationalised is low, and its nine-month AISC of $1,529/oz sits inside the $1,400–$1,550 guidance band while the Q3 realised price was $4,264. The Q3 figure alone, $1,648, was above the band, and management expects the year near its upper end. The less obvious advantage is clustering: owning several mines in the same Abitibi belt lets mills, people and haul roads be shared. Management's own hurdle-rate claim — internal projects returning 30–60% IRRs at current prices — is the reason it says it "can afford to be very patient and very selective" on acquisitions.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The gold price is the whole P&L. At ~$250M of FCF per $100/oz (Jefferies transcript), a fall from the Q3 realised $4,264 to $4,000 would take roughly $0.66B a year out of free cash flow (2.64 × $250M — linear estimate). Front-month COMEX gold futures closed October 30, 2026 at $3,899 (Yahoo Finance), below that level. The leverage that made the last two years works just as hard in reverse.
02
Geotechnical and execution risk. Barnat showed how quickly one pit can dent a year: 60,000–80,000 oz lost from H2 2026 output and up to 150,000 oz in each of 2027 and 2028 on a single rock-mass movement. Detour Lake lost a week of milling to a tailings line breach in August. Hope Bay, Detour underground and Odyssey are large, multi-year builds in cold or deep settings, where cost overruns are the historical norm.
03
Cost creep. AISC rose 23.8% year on year to $1,648/oz. The release names the drivers: diesel (about 11% of operating costs, with Nunavut sealift diesel purchases about 45% above budget net of hedges), consumables inflation of about 6% (drill bits about 50%), and Canadian retaliatory tariffs of about $5/oz that are already in guidance. Most operating costs are in Canadian dollars, so a strong loonie raises USD costs.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value.

From the September 2021 close of $51.85 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $165.38, AEM gained 219.0% on price, before dividends. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 13.5x trailing and ~14.3x forward earnings (FY2026 consensus) for a mine base that is 85% in Canada, a $2,616/oz AISC margin, $4.17B of trailing free cash flow, $3.42B of net cash and about $1.5B returned to shareholders in nine months (dividends $678M plus buybacks of about $800M). The sensitivity cuts both ways but, on management's number, every $100/oz on gold adds ~$0.49/share of FCF. On top of the price option there is a funded growth pipeline — Detour underground, Hope Bay, Odyssey, Upper Beaver — that management says earns 30–60% IRRs at current prices and that the company says could lift annual production 20–30% over the next decade without a large acquisition.

Bear Case

This is a gold price with a mine attached. Earnings per share are up 79.4% in a year because the metal rose, not because output did — ounces were down 6.1% YoY in Q3 and 2026 production is guided to the low end. At 13.5x trailing earnings the market is still capitalising something like today's gold price; on the estimate in Section 02 a 10% fall in gold is roughly a 17–26% fall in earnings or free cash flow. Costs are rising (AISC +23.8% YoY to $1,648 in Q3, above the top of the full-year band), the big projects are in the Arctic and deep underground where overruns are normal, and Barnat showed that a single pit can move output for years: up to 150,000 oz a year in 2027 and 2028.

Industry Commentary
Strong margins across the gold sector
TAILWIND

Front-month COMEX gold futures went from about $4,000 in mid-July to above $4,500 in late August, then closed October 30 at $3,899 (Yahoo Finance). For a producer whose costs are already fixed, most of any higher price falls through to margin. Agnico's Q3 AISC margin of $2,616/oz, 22% above a year earlier, is the clearest example on this page of what that does to a mining P&L.

Cost inflation follows the price
ONGOING HEADWIND

Royalties are levied on revenue, so they rise with gold by design; labour, contractors and consumables tend to follow with a lag. In Q3 Agnico's AISC rose 23.8% year on year while the realised gold price rose 22.7%: the margin still grew in dollars (by $471/oz) but costs rose faster than the price in percentage terms, and the quarter's AISC was above the full-year band. The release puts diesel at about 11% of operating costs, consumables inflation at about 6%, and Canadian retaliatory tariffs at about $5/oz. The risk is the gap closing if the gold price stalls while costs keep rising.

Capital allocation: buy back, build, or buy?
STRATEGIC CHOICE

Record cash flow puts every senior miner in front of the same choice. Agnico's answer so far is to return and build, with small purchases: about $1.5B returned in the first nine months of 2026, a buyback limit of up to $2.0B through May 5, 2027, a $2,605–$2,825M capex budget plus $290–$330M of capitalised exploration, and a stated intention to return more than half of 2026 free cash flow at current spot gold prices. The one deal of size was in Finland (the $325.0M FinGold stake plus Aurion and Rupert), and on larger M&A management says it "can afford to be very patient and very selective". The industry's record is that acquisitions made at high gold prices are where value gets destroyed, and Agnico's 2022 merger was struck when gold was far below today's level. Whether it keeps that discipline as the cash piles up is a fair thing to watch.
