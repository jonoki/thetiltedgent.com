01 Company Overview

Agnico Eagle is a senior gold producer: ten-odd operating mines in Quebec, Ontario, Nunavut, Australia, Finland and Mexico, about 85% of its gold coming out of Canada. It is the #8 company in Canada by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap country ranking) and roughly #237 globally at a $100.0B market value. The common shares trade one-for-one in Toronto and New York — there is no ADR ratio to convert — and the company reports in US dollars, so every per-share figure on this page is already USD per NYSE share. The 2026 guide is 3.3–3.5 million ounces of gold, now expected near the low end.

The whole report comes down to one subtraction. In Q2 2026 (April–June) Agnico sold gold at an average realised $4,483/oz and spent an AISC of $1,459/oz. That leaves a margin of $3,024 per ounce, up from $2,007 a year earlier ($3,288 realised − $1,281 AISC in Q2 2025). Gold rose 36% year over year; the margin rose 51%. That is operating leverage, and it is the reason the stock is up 281% over five years. Management puts a number on it: "Every $100 move in the gold price impacts our after-tax free cash flow … by about a quarter of a million dollars" — the transcript's wording; the only reading consistent with the company's scale is a quarter of a billion dollars, about $0.49 per share. The arithmetic of what that means for the stock is in Section 02.

Today's company is the product of the Kirkland Lake Gold merger of February 2022, which brought Detour Lake (now the largest single mine in the portfolio), Macassa and Fosterville into the group; FY2022 revenue grew 48.4%, consistent with that step-change. The home market is the Toronto Stock Exchange (S&P/TSX Composite); the benchmark on this page is the S&P 500, which AEM is not a member of.

The number that drives everything: $100/oz of gold

Management's own figure (Jefferies, Sep 9, 2026): each $100/oz move in gold changes after-tax free cash flow by ~$250M a year (the transcript literally reads "a quarter of a million dollars"; ~$250M is this page's reading, not confirmed by a second source), which they call "a decent proxy for earnings." Leverage to the metal (est.)
~1.9x – 2.5x
A $100 move is 2.2% of the Q2 realised price ($100 ÷ $4,483). Earnings move 4.2% (4.2 ÷ 2.2 ≈ 1.9x); FCF moves 5.5% (5.5 ÷ 2.2 ≈ 2.5x). Estimate: linear, ignores cost inflation that tends to follow the gold price.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Tier-one jurisdictions (85% Canada) AISC $1,459/oz Detour Lake scale Net cash $3.27B Organic pipeline (Hope Bay, Odyssey, Upper Beaver) District-scale Abitibi infrastructure

A gold miner cannot differentiate its product, so its moat is its place on the cost curve and the politics of where its rock sits. Agnico has both: most of its ounces come from Canada, a jurisdiction where the risk of a mine being taxed away or nationalised is low, and its AISC sits within a $1,400–$1,550 guidance band while the realised price is above $4,400. The less obvious advantage is clustering: owning several mines in the same Abitibi belt lets mills, people and haul roads be shared. Management's own hurdle-rate claim — internal projects returning 30–60% IRRs at current prices — is the reason it says it "can afford to be very patient and very selective" on acquisitions.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The gold price is the whole P&L. At ~$250M of FCF per $100/oz (this page's reading of the Jefferies transcript, unconfirmed), a fall from the Q2 realised $4,483 back to July's ~$4,000 region would take roughly $1.2B a year out of free cash flow (4.83 × $250M — linear estimate). The operating leverage that made the last two years works just as hard in reverse.
02
Geotechnical and execution risk. Barnat showed how quickly one pit can dent a year: 60,000–80,000 oz lost from H2 2026 guidance on a single rock-mass movement. Hope Bay, Detour underground and Odyssey are large, multi-year builds in cold or deep settings, where cost overruns are the historical norm.
03
Cost creep. AISC rose 13.9% year on year to $1,459/oz. Some of that is price-linked royalties (which rise with gold by design), some is labour, energy and the Canadian dollar. The company hedges about 60% of remaining C$ exposure at an average floor of 1.38 C$/US$ against a 1.36 guidance assumption; a strong loonie raises USD costs.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 16.9x trailing and ~16.3x forward earnings for the lowest-risk jurisdictional footprint among the large gold producers, a $3,024/oz AISC margin, $4.56B of trailing free cash flow, $3.27B of net cash and ~$2B a year returned to shareholders. The sensitivity cuts both ways but, on management's number, every $100/oz on gold adds ~$0.49/share of FCF. On top of the price option there is a funded growth pipeline — Detour underground, Hope Bay, Odyssey, Upper Beaver — that management says earns 30–60% IRRs at current prices and could add 20–30% to production without an acquisition.

Bear Case

This is a gold price with a mine attached. Earnings nearly doubled in a year because the metal rose, not because output did — ounces were down 1.2% YoY in Q2 and 2026 guidance was trimmed to the low end. At 16.9x trailing earnings the market is already capitalising something like today's gold price; ~2x operating leverage means a 10% fall in gold is roughly a 20% fall in earnings. Costs are rising (AISC +13.9% YoY), the big projects are in the Arctic and deep underground where overruns are normal, and Barnat showed that a single pit can move guidance.

Top 3 Risks

01
Gold price reversal. ~$250M of FCF per $100/oz (management remark as read from a transcript; unconfirmed). A return to July's ~$4,000 from the Q2 realised $4,483 is roughly −$1.2B of annual FCF (estimate, linear).
02
Operational slippage at Canadian Malartic and on the major projects. Barnat has already cost 60,000–80,000 oz of H2 2026 output; Hope Bay and Detour underground are multi-year builds.
03
Cost inflation and the Canadian dollar. AISC +13.9% YoY; 85% of ounces are Canadian with C$ costs, only ~60% hedged for the rest of 2026.
Industry Commentary
Record margins across the gold sector
TAILWIND

The August 2026 move was macro: softer US data scaled back expectations of more Federal Reserve tightening, the US Treasury expanded buybacks of longer-dated debt, and central-bank gold buying is described as "a structural floor throughout 2026" (Canadian Mining Report, Aug 20, 2026). For a producer whose costs are already fixed, most of any higher price falls through to margin. Agnico's Q2 AISC margin of $3,024/oz is the clearest example on this page of what that does to a mining P&L.

Cost inflation follows the price
ONGOING HEADWIND

Royalties are levied on revenue, so they rise with gold by design; labour, contractors and consumables tend to follow with a lag. Agnico's AISC rose 13.9% YoY while the gold price rose 36%, so margins still widened. The company's own disclosures put diesel at about 7% of total costs, consumption near 100 litres per ounce against an industry average of 150 (Jefferies, Sep 9), and a 10% diesel move at ~$4/oz of H2 2026 cash cost. The risk is the gap closing if the gold price stalls while costs keep rising.

Capital allocation: buy back, build, or buy?
STRATEGIC CHOICE

Record cash flow puts every senior miner in front of the same choice. Agnico's answer so far is to return and build, not buy: ~$2B returned in 2026, a $2B buyback limit, a $2,605–$2,825M capex budget for the year, and on M&A management says it "can afford to be very patient and very selective". The industry's record is that acquisitions made at high gold prices are where value gets destroyed, and Agnico's 2022 merger was struck when gold was far below today's level. Whether it keeps that discipline as the cash piles up is a fair thing to watch.
