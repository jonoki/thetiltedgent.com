01 Company Overview

Aflac Incorporated underwrites supplemental health and life insurance — cash-benefit policies that pay the policyholder directly when a covered event occurs (cancer diagnosis, hospitalisation, accident, disability), sitting alongside rather than replacing major medical cover. It is not a property-and-casualty insurer, and it is not a major-medical carrier. It reports in two operating segments, Aflac Japan and Aflac U.S., plus a Corporate and Other line that now houses a fast-growing reinsurance business.

Full-year 2025 revenue was $17.16 billion, down 9.31% from $18.93 billion in 2024, with diluted EPS of $6.82 versus $9.63; both lines are heavily distorted by investment and currency marks rather than by underwriting (see Notable One-Off Items). On a TTM basis through September 2026 revenue is $16.60 billion and net income $3.53 billion. Aflac ranks #197 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation and is the third-largest US-listed name in StockAnalysis's Insurance — Life industry group, behind Manulife Financial and MetLife.

The fact most casual readers get wrong: Japan, not the United States, is the larger and far more profitable half of this company. In the first nine months of 2026, Aflac Japan produced $6.5 billion of total adjusted revenue against Aflac U.S.'s $5.3 billion, and $2.5 billion of pretax adjusted earnings against the U.S. segment's $1.2 billion — roughly two-thirds of segment pretax profit from Japan. The investment case, the currency exposure and the structural risk all follow from that single split.

The third quarter needs a careful read. Adjusted EPS of $2.43 beat the $1.80 Nasdaq consensus, but $0.71 of it came from the annual review of actuarial assumptions, the yearly update of the estimates of future claims and policy lapses behind its reserves. Without that review the figure is $1.72. GAAP EPS was $0.61, after $1.1 billion of net investment losses driven by selling and replacing part of the portfolio.

Aflac Japan sells cancer, medical, nursing-care and whole-life policies and is described in the FY2025 10-K as the largest insurer in Japan for cancer and medical policies, with over 22 million individual policies in force including roughly 14 million cancer policies and ¥1.18 trillion of annualised premium in force at end-2025. In Q3 2026 net earned premium (NEP) was ¥240.3 billion, down 2.0% in yen and down 9.3% in dollars. Aflac attributes the decline mainly to an external reinsurance deal, an internal reinsurance deal on cancer policies and limited-pay policies becoming fully paid up. Underlying earned premiums, which exclude reinsurance, paid-up policies and the deferred profit liability (an accounting adjustment), fell 1.2%, which the CFO said was roughly where the company expects to be for the year. Pretax adjusted earnings were ¥152.3 billion, down 15.2% in yen ($961 million, down 21.0%), because last year's third quarter carried a larger gain from the annual actuarial review. The benefit ratio was 49.2% against 39.3% a year earlier; management estimates it at 61.7% without the review. The expense ratio rose 1.4 percentage points to 21.2%, with cybersecurity costs adding about 1.8 points. The pretax margin was 44.4% against 52.2%. Premium persistency, the share of premium that renews, was 92.9% against 93.3%. The dollar comparison shows why Japan matters: Q3 NEP of $1.51 billion was slightly below Aflac U.S.'s $1.54 billion, yet Japan's pretax earnings of $961 million were almost double the U.S. segment's $493 million. Both figures include the actuarial review; management's 2026 outlook, which is the cleaner guide, is a Japan pretax margin of 35% to 38% (raised this quarter) against a U.S. margin above a 17% to 20% range. Japan's population is shrinking and ageing, and management says savings-type cover helps attract young and middle-aged customers: Tsumitasu (savings) ran at about 20% of total Japan sales, by management's Q3 account, and on the Q2 call cancer or medical cover sold alongside it was "largely exceeding" the 25% share first planned; Miraito (cancer) launched in 2025; Anshin Palette (medical) launched December 2025. Q3 Japan sales fell 4.0% to ¥18.8 billion against high prior-year Miraito sales after its March 2025 launch; several sales initiatives were also suspended after June's cyber incident, until September. Nine-month sales were up 3.0% to ¥56.1 billion, and management expects the full year to exceed 2025.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
#1 JAPAN CANCER & MEDICAL 22M+ JAPAN POLICIES IN FORCE 92.9% JAPAN PERSISTENCY ¥1.18T PREMIUM IN FORCE 44-YEAR DIVIDEND STREAK WORKSITE AGENCY FORCE RBC >575% · ESR 225%

Aflac's moat is distribution plus incumbency in a product category almost nobody else built. In Japan it has spent decades assembling the country's largest cancer and medical business — 22 million-plus individual policies, roughly 14 million of them cancer policies, ¥1.18 trillion of annualised premium in force — sold through bank, post-office and agency channels that took years to license and populate. Persistency of 92.9% means the policies renew almost automatically, and management's 2026 outlook of a benefit ratio slightly below 60% to 63% and an expense ratio at the low end of 20% to 23% leaves a 35% to 38% pretax margin, high for an insurance operation and built on a long claims history and a cost base spread over an enormous number of policies already sold. In the US the equivalent asset is the worksite agency force and the employer relationships it sits inside: once Aflac is on the payroll-deduction menu at an employer, adding dental, vision or group disability is incremental, which is exactly what the $1.07 of voluntary-benefit sales per $1.00 of dental/vision sales statistic from Q2 measures. Capital strength reinforces it — RBC slightly above 575%, Japanese ESR of 225% (238% with the undertaking-specific parameter, a company-specific risk calibration), adjusted leverage 22.6%, $2.7 billion of unencumbered liquidity against a $1 billion minimum — and it funds a 44-year run of dividend increases.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Japan's population is shrinking and ageing. Two-thirds of segment pretax profit comes from that one country. Japan net earned premium fell 2.0% in yen in Q3 2026, which Aflac attributes mainly to reinsurance deals and to limited-pay policies becoming fully paid up; underlying earned premiums, which exclude those effects, still fell 1.2%, and that is a local-currency decline, not translation. Persistency slipped to 92.9% from 93.3%. Management says Tsumitasu, its savings product and about 20% of sales, helps attract young and middle-aged customers, though saving is a different business from the cancer cover that built the franchise.

2. Currency translation swamps operating performance. Earnings are generated in yen and reported in dollars. The Q3 average rate of ¥158.49 was 6.8% weaker than the prior year's ¥147.68, which turned a 2.0% yen NEP decline into a 9.3% dollar decline and took $0.03 off adjusted EPS. Adjusted EPS fell 2.4% as reported and 1.2% excluding currency. Reported dollar results can move materially with nothing operational changing in either direction.

3. Headline earnings lean on an annual actuarial review. The Q3 assumption update added $0.71 to adjusted EPS of $2.43, against a $1.80 Nasdaq consensus. Without it the figure is $1.72, and the U.S. segment's 31.5% pretax gain rests on a benefit ratio of 40.7% that management puts at 50.2% without the review. Readers comparing quarters need the review taken out.

4. Investment portfolio composition and reinvestment risk. Aflac's liabilities are very long-dated and in yen, and its portfolio holds US private credit, middle-market loans and commercial real estate alongside Japanese government bonds. Net investment income fell 6.0% to $1.00 billion in Q3, and the quarter took $1.1 billion of net investment losses, driven by realized losses from portfolio repositioning. Management sold and replaced $2.3 billion of Japanese government bonds in the quarter and says active management has added over $80 million of annual net investment income this year. US statutory results carried $6 million of impairments and a $6 million mortgage-loan allowance; on the Japan FSA basis, a ¥5.2 billion allowance on real-estate loans, with net realized gains of ¥15.2 billion from reversed impairments.

5. Cybersecurity. An unauthorized party accessed Aflac Japan systems between 15 and 25 June 2026; the files included policy, personal and bank account details, and SecurityWeek, citing the subsidiary, reported about 4.38 million customers and agents likely affected. The incident cost $40 million, or $0.06 per diluted share, in Q3, and Aflac Japan suspended several sales initiatives, including direct mail and outbound calls to customers, until September.

Notable One-Off Items

Aflac's headline GAAP numbers are unusually noisy and several of them should not be read at face value.

Reported and adjusted earnings moved apart again in FY2025. FY2025 revenue fell 9.31% and diluted EPS fell 29.18% to $6.82, yet FY2025 adjusted earnings were $4.0 billion ($7.49 per share) versus $4.1 billion ($7.21) in 2024. The reported swing is investment and currency remeasurement, not underwriting.

Q3 2026 GAAP vs adjusted. Net earnings were $303 million ($0.61 per diluted share, against $3.08 a year earlier) while adjusted earnings were $1.2 billion ($2.43, down 2.4%). The gap is $1.1 billion of net investment losses, driven by realized losses from portfolio repositioning.

Annual actuarial review. In the CFO's words, "The total net impact from the Q3 assumption update increased earnings per share by $0.71." That figure is inside the $2.43; last year's third quarter carried a larger gain.

Currency. A negative $0.03 per share of FX impact in Q3. Excluding it, adjusted EPS was $2.46, down 1.2%.

Cybersecurity. $40 million, or $0.06 per diluted share, of expenses and enhancements in Japan after the June incident.

Portfolio repositioning. $2.3 billion of Japanese government bonds were sold and replaced in Q3. Variable investment income ran $22 million ($0.03 per share) below management's long-term expectations.

Reinsurance. About ¥500 billion of cancer-policy reserves moved to Aflac Re Bermuda in Q3. The Japan Post Insurance coinsurance deal effective 31 March 2026 remains the first external transaction; no reserve amount was disclosed in that release.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a Japanese franchise guided to a 35% to 38% pretax margin this year — the country's largest cancer and medical insurer, 22 million-plus policies, 92.9% persistency — at 16.03× FY2026 consensus earnings and 2.29× adjusted book excluding AOCI (unrealised investment and currency swings held in equity). Management raised the Japan margin outlook and expects the U.S. margin above its 17% to 20% range. The share count shrank 5.7% in a year on top of a 1.97% yield and a 44-year dividend-increase record. Moving about ¥500 billion of reserves to Aflac Re Bermuda should release about $1 billion of capital, with internal reinsurance able to reach 30% of Japan's FSA reserves, and management says that is explicitly aimed at lifting ROE.

Bear Case

Two-thirds of segment pretax profit comes from a shrinking, ageing country, and Japan's underlying earned premiums fell 1.2% in yen in Q3, so currency is not the reason. The $2.43 adjusted EPS that beat the $1.80 Nasdaq consensus by 35% contains $0.71 from the annual actuarial review; without it the figure is $1.72. GAAP EPS of $0.61 carried $1.1 billion of investment losses, the trailing P/E is up to 18.03×, above the life-insurance group's 11.61×, and net investment income fell 6.0%. Buybacks and dividends of $3.69 billion in nine months ran ahead of operating cash flow of $2.28 billion. The average target of $117.20 is 5.5% below the close, and four of sixteen analysts rate the stock a sell.

Top 3 Risks
01Japanese demographic decline. Japan's underlying earned premiums fell 1.2% in yen in Q3 and persistency slipped from 93.3% to 92.9%. Management says savings-type products like Tsumitasu, about 20% of Japan sales, help attract young and middle-aged customers, but saving is a different business from the 14-million-policy cancer business. If sales keep shifting toward it, the 35% to 38% Japan margin outlook would be at risk.
02Yen translation and the limits of the hedge. The Q3 average of ¥158.49 was 6.8% weaker year over year and cost $0.03 of adjusted EPS, turning a −2.0% yen premium decline into −9.3% in dollars. Management hedges economically — about 64% of group debt is in yen — which protects the dollar value of the Japanese business but does not protect the reported quarterly earnings line. Reported results can miss on currency alone.
03Investment portfolio and net investment income. Alongside Japanese government bonds, the portfolio backing long-dated yen liabilities holds US private credit, middle-market loans and commercial real estate. Net investment income fell 6.0% to $1.00bn in Q3, the quarter took $1.1bn of net investment losses from repositioning, and management increased loan reserves because of commercial real estate pressure. Credit deterioration in those higher-yielding loans would hit both earnings and regulatory capital.
  Industry Commentary
Japan's Demographics Are the Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Aflac's profit centre sits inside a shrinking, ageing customer base. Japan net earned premium fell 2.0% in yen in Q3 2026, which Aflac attributes mainly to reinsurance deals and to limited-pay policies becoming fully paid up; underlying earned premiums still fell 1.2%, and persistency fell from 93.3% to 92.9%. The in-force book remains vast (22m+ policies, ~14m cancer policies, ¥1.18tn of annualised premium), which is precisely why margins are so high and why the decline is slow. The company's counter is to change what it sells: Tsumitasu, a savings product now running at about 20% of Japan sales, which management says helps attract young and middle-aged customers; Miraito (cancer, 2025) and Anshin Palette (medical, December 2025) refresh the core. Nine-month Japan sales were up 3.0% even though Q3 fell 4.0% against high prior-year Miraito sales after its March 2025 launch.
The Yen Moves Reported Profit Without Anything Happening
ONGOING HEADWIND
Aflac earns in yen and reports in dollars, so the exchange rate is an earnings input in its own right. In Q3 2026 the yen averaged ¥158.49 against ¥147.68 a year earlier — 6.8% weaker — which is the difference between Japan NEP falling 2.0% (yen) and 9.3% (dollars), and it cost $0.03 of adjusted EPS. Adjusted EPS was $2.43, down 2.4%; excluding currency it was $2.46, down 1.2%. The hedge sits in the balance sheet: about 64% of group debt is in yen, which protects the dollar value of Aflac Japan. Note what that does and does not do — it defends the balance sheet, not the quarterly earnings line. It also shows up in book value: adjusted book value per share excluding AOCI is $54.21, but excluding FX remeasurement as well it is $40.73, so price-to-book is 2.10×, 2.29× or 3.05× depending which currency effects you strip out.
Reinsurance and the Portfolio — Where Bull and Bear Actually Divide
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two things are happening on the balance sheet at once, and how you weigh them decides the stock. On the constructive side, internal reinsurance capacity was expanded from 10% of US GAAP assets to 30% of FSA reserves on a ¥10.8 trillion base, about ¥500 billion of cancer reserves moved to Aflac Re Bermuda in Q3 for roughly $1 billion of capital release by the CFO's estimate, and Aflac Re Bermuda closed its first external deal with Japan Post Insurance. On the cautious side, the portfolio behind very long-dated yen liabilities holds US private credit, middle-market loans and commercial real estate alongside Japanese government bonds; net investment income fell 6.0% to $1.00bn, and Q3's repositioning came with $1.1bn of net investment losses. Bull: a well-capitalised insurer (RBC slightly above 575%, ESR 225%) converting spare capital into buybacks and a higher return on equity. Bear: a shrinking core franchise using reinsurance and riskier investments to hold EPS flat, with an average price target 5.5% below the stock.
