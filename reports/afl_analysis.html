01 Company Overview

Aflac Incorporated underwrites supplemental health and life insurance — cash-benefit policies that pay the policyholder directly when a covered event occurs (cancer diagnosis, hospitalisation, accident, disability), sitting alongside rather than replacing major medical cover. It is not a property-and-casualty insurer, and it is not a major-medical carrier. It reports in two operating segments, Aflac Japan and Aflac U.S., plus a Corporate and Other line that now houses a fast-growing reinsurance business.

Full-year 2025 revenue was $17.16 billion, down 9.31% from $18.93 billion in 2024, with diluted EPS of $6.82 versus $9.63; both lines are heavily distorted by investment and currency marks rather than by underwriting (see Notable One-Off Items). On a TTM basis through June 2026 revenue is $18.07 billion and net income $4.86 billion. Aflac ranks #437 among all listed companies worldwide by market capitalisation and is the second-largest US-listed name in StockAnalysis's Insurance — Life industry group behind Manulife Financial.

The fact most casual readers get wrong: Japan, not the United States, is the larger and far more profitable half of this company. In the first six months of 2026, Aflac Japan produced $4.3 billion of total adjusted revenue against Aflac U.S.'s $3.6 billion, and $1.5 billion of pretax adjusted earnings against the U.S. segment's $733 million — roughly two-thirds of segment pretax profit from Japan. The investment case, the currency exposure and the structural risk all follow from that single split.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
#1 JAPAN CANCER & MEDICAL 22M+ JAPAN POLICIES IN FORCE 92.7% JAPAN PERSISTENCY ¥1.18T PREMIUM IN FORCE 43-YEAR DIVIDEND STREAK WORKSITE AGENCY FORCE RBC >600% · ESR 226%

Aflac's moat is distribution plus incumbency in a product category almost nobody else built. In Japan it has spent decades assembling the country's largest cancer and medical book — 22 million-plus individual policies, roughly 14 million of them cancer policies, ¥1.18 trillion of annualised premium in force — sold through bank, post-office and agency channels that took years to license and populate. Persistency of 92.7% means the block renews almost automatically; a benefit ratio of 64.0% against an expense ratio of 20.2% leaves a 34.3% pretax margin, which is extraordinary for any insurance operation and reflects both underwriting experience on a claims history no competitor possesses and a cost base amortised over an enormous in-force book. In the US the equivalent asset is the worksite agency force and the employer relationships it sits inside: once Aflac is on the payroll-deduction menu at an employer, adding dental, vision or group disability is incremental, which is exactly what the $1.07 of voluntary-benefit sales per $1.00 of dental/vision sales statistic measures. Capital strength reinforces it — RBC above 600%, Japanese ESR of 226% (240% with the undertaking-specific parameter), adjusted leverage 21.8%, $3.3 billion of unencumbered liquidity against a $1 billion minimum — and it funds a 43-year run of dividend increases that anchors a large income shareholder base.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Japanese demographics are a one-way structural headwind. Two-thirds of pretax profit comes from a country whose population is shrinking and ageing. The evidence is in the numbers already: Japan net earned premium fell 3.7% in yen in Q2 2026 — that is the local-currency, currency-neutral decline, so it is real volume and mix, not translation. Persistency also slipped, from 93.7% to 92.7%. Management's response is to sell savings-type first-sector products (Tsumitasu, ~20% of sales) to reach younger buyers, which is a different, lower-margin business than the cancer franchise it is meant to backfill.

2. Currency translation swamps operating performance. Earnings are generated in yen and reported in dollars. The Q2 average rate of ¥159.45 was 9.3% weaker than the prior year's ¥144.60, which turned a 3.7% yen NEP decline into a 12.7% dollar decline and knocked $0.05 off adjusted EPS. Adjusted EPS fell 1.7% as reported but rose 1.1% excluding FX. Reported dollar results can therefore move materially with nothing operational changing in either direction.

3. US margin compression. The US benefit ratio deteriorated 220 basis points to 49.5% in Q2, pushing pretax earnings down 4.6% and the pretax margin from 22.5% to 20.9% despite premium growth of 2.3%. Growth guidance was also trimmed to "just below" the 3–6% CAGR range. The growth half of the company is currently the margin-losing half.

4. Investment portfolio composition and reinvestment risk. A supplemental insurer with very long-dated yen liabilities cannot fund them with plain yen government bonds at acceptable yields, so the portfolio reaches into US private credit, middle-market loans and commercial real estate. Adjusted net investment income fell 9.6% to $937 million in Q2. Management repositioned $4.8 billion (about 5% of the portfolio) via switch trades in the quarter for roughly $50 million of annualised NII benefit, and booked $11 million of US statutory impairments plus ¥15.8 billion of securities impairments on a Japan FSA basis. That is small in context but it is a live line item, not a theoretical one.

Notable One-Off Items

Aflac's headline GAAP numbers are unusually noisy and several of them should not be read at face value.

The FY2025 collapse and the TTM rebound are the same item seen twice. FY2025 revenue fell 9.31% and diluted EPS fell 29.18% to $6.82, yet FY2025 adjusted earnings were $4.0 billion ($7.49 per share) versus $4.1 billion ($7.21) in 2024 — essentially flat per share. The reported swing is investment and currency remeasurement, not underwriting. The TTM EPS growth figure of +111.56% is the mirror image of that base effect and is meaningless as a growth rate.

Q2 2026 GAAP vs adjusted. Net earnings were $825 million ($1.63 per diluted share, against $1.11 a year earlier) while adjusted earnings were $883 million ($1.75, down 1.7%). GAAP rose while adjusted fell — the gap is again FX remeasurement and investment marks.

Currency. A negative $0.05 per share of FX impact in Q2. Excluding it, adjusted EPS was $1.80, up 1.1%.

Portfolio repositioning. $4.8 billion of switch trades (~5% of the portfolio) executed in the quarter for ~$50 million of annualised net investment income and "minimal capital impact." Impairments: $11 million US statutory, $1 million of mortgage loan valuation allowance, and ¥15.8 billion on a Japan FSA basis — described by management as "well within our expectations."

Reinsurance. The Aflac Re Bermuda / Japan Post Insurance coinsurance deal effective 31 March 2026 is the first external transaction; no reserve amount was disclosed in the press release, so its earnings contribution cannot be quantified here.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a 34.3%-pretax-margin Japanese franchise — the country's largest cancer and medical insurer, 22 million-plus policies, 92.7% persistency — at 12.44× trailing earnings and 2.12× adjusted book excluding AOCI, while the index trades at 29.47×. The Japan benefit ratio improved 250bp year over year, adjusted EPS rose 1.1% excluding currency, and management is shrinking the share count 5.75% a year on top of a 2.10% yield and a 43-year dividend-increase record. Bear Case

Two-thirds of pretax profit comes from a shrinking, ageing country, and Japan's net earned premium is already falling 3.7% in yen — that decline is real volume, not translation. The growth half of the business is losing margin: the US benefit ratio deteriorated 220bp, pretax earnings fell 4.6%, and guidance was cut to "just below" the 3–6% range. Net investment income fell 9.6%. Consensus 2026 EPS of $7.02 is 25% below TTM EPS of $9.37, which is why the forward P/E (15.96) is higher than the trailing (12.44) — the cheapness is partly an accounting artefact. Four of fifteen analysts are outright negative and Wolfe Research initiated at $103.

  Industry Commentary
Japan's Demographics Are the Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Aflac's profit centre sits inside a shrinking, ageing customer base, and the effect is already visible in reported numbers rather than in projections. Japan net earned premium fell 3.7% in yen in Q2 2026 — currency-neutral, so genuinely fewer or smaller policies — and premium persistency fell from 93.7% to 92.7%. The in-force book remains vast (22m+ policies, ~14m cancer policies, ¥1.18tn of annualised premium), which is precisely why margins are so high and why the decline is slow. But the direction is set. The company's counter is to change what it sells: Tsumitasu, a first-sector savings product now running at about 20% of Japan sales, explicitly targets younger buyers, with cancer and medical attachment "largely exceeding" the 25% target; Miraito (cancer, 2025) and Anshin Palette (medical, December 2025) refresh the core. First-half Japan sales were up 7% even though Q2 fell 5.6% against the Miraito launch comparison.
The Yen Moves the P&L Without Anything Happening
ONGOING HEADWIND
Aflac earns in yen and reports in dollars, so the exchange rate is an earnings input in its own right. In Q2 2026 the yen averaged ¥159.45 against ¥144.60 a year earlier — 9.3% weaker — which is the entire difference between Japan NEP falling 3.7% (yen) and 12.7% (dollars), and it cost $0.05 of adjusted EPS. Adjusted EPS was $1.75, down 1.7%; excluding FX it was $1.80, up 1.1%. The hedge is structural rather than transactional: roughly 63% of group debt is yen-denominated, an "intentional" enterprise hedge designed to protect the dollar economic value of Aflac Japan, and CFO Brodén described the hedges as taking "a very long-term view" that makes 4% near-term swings "quite minor." Note what that does and does not do — it defends the balance sheet, not the quarterly earnings line. It also shows up in book value: adjusted book value per share excluding AOCI is $55.01, but excluding FX remeasurement as well it is $41.22, so price-to-book is 1.93×, 2.12× or 2.83× depending which currency effects you strip out.
Reinsurance and the Portfolio — Where Bull and Bear Actually Divide
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two things are happening on the balance sheet at once, and how you weigh them decides the stock. On the constructive side, internal reinsurance capacity has been expanded from 10% of US GAAP assets to 30% of FSA reserves on a ¥10.8 trillion base, which management says will "reduce risk, improve balance sheet efficiency and ultimately generate a higher ROE," and Aflac Re Bermuda closed its first external deal — Japan Post Insurance whole-life annuities, effective 31 March 2026 — opening a third-party line management expects to become "a very, very good supplemental business." On the cautious side, funding very long-dated yen liabilities has pushed the investment portfolio into US private credit, middle-market loans and commercial real estate; adjusted net investment income still fell 9.6% to $937m, and the quarter's $4.8bn (≈5% of portfolio) of switch trades bought only about $50m of annualised income. Bull: a cheap, over-capitalised (RBC >600%, ESR 226%) insurer converting balance-sheet slack into ROE and buybacks. Bear: a shrinking core franchise using financial engineering and a reach-for-yield portfolio to hold EPS flat, with consensus 2026 EPS of $7.02 telling you the market does not believe the trailing $9.37.
