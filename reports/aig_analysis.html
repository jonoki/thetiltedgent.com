01 Company Overview

American International Group underwrites commercial, institutional and personal property-casualty insurance globally — property, liability, workers' compensation and cyber, sold largely through brokers in North America and international markets. The restructuring is finished; the open question is how fast what remains can grow. AIG separated its life and retirement business into Corebridge Financial and, on May 7, 2026, sold its remaining Corebridge interest for approximately $710 million in proceeds. What is left is a property-casualty (P&C) underwriter organised into three premium segments — North America Commercial, International Commercial and Global Personal — plus a corporate "Other Operations" line that ran an adjusted pre-tax loss of $141 million in Q3 2026. On the November 4 call, CEO Eric Andersen said AIG was choosing to "step back from the low to mid teens net premiums written growth expectation we shared in February." This is a much smaller company than the pre-2020 conglomerate: revenue was $51.8B in FY2021 and $26.6B in FY2025 (−1.54% year over year), with trailing-twelve-month revenue of $27.79B on StockAnalysis's series, up 2.47%.

AIG's market value is roughly $40.3B — among the largest in StockAnalysis's "Insurance — Diversified" industry group, far behind Berkshire Hathaway, and under a third the size of pure P&C peer Chubb ($139.5B). Three threads dominate the investment case: whether the underwriting improvement lasts now that reserve releases have shrunk and storms have returned, how much premium growth is left after management dropped its target, and how much of the improvement in per-share figures is simply a shrinking share count rather than a growing business.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
GLOBAL LICENSING FOOTPRINT BROKER DISTRIBUTION SCALE REINSURANCE PURCHASING POWER LONG-TAIL CLAIMS DATA INSURANCE FLOAT CAPITAL & RATINGS STRENGTH COMPLEX-RISK UNDERWRITING

AIG's durable advantages are structural rather than technological. A licensed presence across a large number of jurisdictions is expensive and slow to replicate, and it is what lets AIG write multinational commercial programmes that a domestic carrier cannot. Scale in the broker channel matters because commercial insurance is sold through brokers: AIG competes for a place on Aon's, Marsh's and WTW's panels, not for retail attention. Scale also buys reinsurance more cheaply and lets management reshape the risk profile by ceding rather than by shrinking. Finally, an insurer holds float — premium collected now against claims paid later — which it invests for its own account. AIG's float produced $1,100M of net investment income in Q3 2026 ($1,075M on the adjusted pre-tax income basis management reports, against $1,024M a year earlier). That income arrives whether or not underwriting is good, and it is the reason a combined ratio slightly under 100% can still support a double-digit return on equity.

What the moat does not obviously include is pricing power. On the Q3 call Andersen said AIG "will not chase growth for growth's sake" and that conditions vary widely by line and country, while North America property prices remain under pressure. AIG's ROE on a trailing GAAP basis is 8.22%, below the 11.1% core operating ROE management reported for Q3 2026, and the shares change hands at 1.04× book value ($77.75 against book value per share of $74.96 at September 30, 2026). A market that believed the underwriting franchise was clearly superior would pay more than book for it.

Key Vulnerabilities
A.
Reserve releases are shrinking. Favourable prior-year development fell to $91M in Q3 2026 from $180M a year earlier, worth 1.5 points of combined ratio against 3.1. In North America Commercial it fell to 1.4 points from 5.9. Across the first nine months AIG booked $368M ($132M + $145M + $91M, from its three quarterly releases). These releases cannot go on forever: once the cushion in reserves set aside for old years has been released, it is gone.
B.
Storm exposure in the largest US book. Q3 catastrophe charges rose to $283M (4.4 loss-ratio points) from $100M (1.6 points), driven by severe convective storms in North America, and they added 7.1 points to North America Commercial's own combined ratio. Walsh called the charge "a modest figure as compared to our historical norms" — which is the point: a normal or heavy year costs more than this one did.
C.
A smaller earnings base beneath the headlines. Revenue fell every year from FY2021 to FY2025 — $51.8B, $29.7B, $27.8B, $27.0B, $26.6B — and only the trailing year has turned up. Q3 net income rose to $844M from $519M, but AIG said the increase was primarily due to changes in the fair value of its investments. Adjusted after-tax income, which excludes those swings, fell 11% to $1,086M, and only 6% per share to $2.06 because the share count is smaller. A company cannot repurchase its way to growth indefinitely.
D.
Leadership turnover at the moment of proof. Eric Andersen, a broker executive rather than a career underwriter, became CEO on June 1, 2026. Peter Zaffino, the architect of the turnaround, left the Executive Chair and the board on September 15, General Insurance CEO Jon Hancock retires on December 31, and Andersen says segment, underwriting and distribution leaders will report directly to him from 2027. The new team has also just abandoned the growth target set in February. The market reaction to the January 2026 succession news was unkind: the stock fell 7.5% on January 6, from $84.38 to $78.07, and ended the month at $74.88.

Notable One-Off Items

Three distortions matter when reading AIG's history, and all three are large.

1. The FY2022 revenue cliff. Revenue fell from $51,808M in FY2021 to $29,721M in FY2022, −42.63%. This coincides with the separation of the life and retirement business into Corebridge Financial and its deconsolidation from AIG's accounts. Comparisons that span 2021 and 2022 are comparing two different companies.

2. The FY2024 GAAP loss. AIG reported a net loss of $1,426M (−$2.17 per diluted share) in FY2024 despite operating income of $4,228M. The main driver was a $4.7B loss on the June 2024 deconsolidation of Corebridge, mostly $7.2B of accumulated other comprehensive loss (unrealised losses previously held in equity) moved into earnings (AIG Q2 2024 release, July 31, 2024). FY2025 net income returned to $3,096M ($5.43 per share).

3. The final Corebridge exit. On May 7, 2026 AIG sold its remaining Corebridge interest for approximately $710M. Q3 2025 still included $20M of Corebridge dividends in Other Operations; Q3 2026 has none, so comparisons are nearly clean from here.

Prior-year reserve development is not a one-off in the accounting sense — it recurs every quarter — but at $368M in the first nine months of 2026, a little under a fifth of underwriting income, it is worth stripping out of any estimate of what the book earns in a normal year. Q3's GAAP net income also carries large swings from changes in the fair value of investments, which is why management's adjusted figures fell while net income rose.

Trajectory. AIG closed November 2021 at $52.60 and November 6, 2026 at $77.75 — a price gain of 47.8% on this chart, excluding dividends, against +61.4% for SPY and +66.6% for QQQ from the same month-end. Most of that gain was earned between late 2022 and early 2025: the stock ran from $47.48 at end-September 2022 to $86.94 at end-March 2025, +83% in thirty months, and has fallen back since. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The restructuring is finished — the final Corebridge stake went in May 2026 — and premium is still growing: net premiums written rose 12.1% over the first nine months and 7% in Q3. Over nine months the combined ratio improved 1.4 points to 89.2%, the accident-year ex-cat ratio improved 0.5 point to 87.7%, and underwriting income topped $2 billion. Management expects the expense ratio to drop below 30% for 2027. The share count is down 4.7% in a year to 518.7M, fourth-quarter buybacks are expected at a similar level to the third, and the 32.65% payout ratio leaves plenty of room for the $0.50 quarterly dividend. All of that is available at 1.04× book value and 8.87× FY2027 consensus adjusted earnings, with an average analyst target of $88.40 (+13.7%) and the lowest published target ($80) still above the current price.

Bear Case

The growth story just got smaller. Management stepped back from its low-to-mid-teens growth expectation only nine months after setting it, and third-quarter premium rose 7%. The combined ratio went to 91.3% from 86.8%, underwriting income fell 29%, and adjusted after-tax income fell 11% to $1,086M, cushioned to −6% per share by a smaller share count. Reserve releases halved to $91M, and North America Commercial's accident-year ex-cat ratio worsened by 1.8 points. Net income rose 63%, but AIG attributes that primarily to changes in the fair value of its investments. Book value per share slipped 1% to $74.96. Revenue fell four straight fiscal years. Trailing ROE is 8.22%. At 1.04× book the market is paying little more than net assets for the franchise, and it may be right.

Top 3 Risks
01
Reserve development normalises or reverses. Favourable development fell to $91M in Q3 2026 from $180M a year earlier, and in North America Commercial to 1.4 points from 5.9. If the cushion in reserves for earlier years runs out — or if US liability claims, which can take years to settle, come in above what was reserved — the calendar combined ratio moves toward the 87.7% nine-month accident-year ratio and then above it, with no operational change whatsoever.
02
A hard catastrophe year. Charges were $283M in Q3 2026 (4.4 loss-ratio points), $210M in Q2 and $180M in Q1, against $525M and 9.1 points in Q1 2025; the nine-month ratio is 3.6 points against 4.5 a year earlier. Walsh called the Q3 charge modest against historical norms. A single severe season would erase the half-point of nine-month underlying improvement several times over.
03
Buyback arithmetic runs out of road. Shares outstanding fell 4.7% over the year to 518.7M, and management expects about $2.2 billion of buybacks in 2026. That is genuine value creation below book value, but at 1.04× book a repurchase trims book value per share rather than lifting it, and the effect shrinks as the base shrinks. Underneath it, revenue fell every fiscal year from FY2021 to FY2025 and trailing ROIC is 8.34%.
  Industry Commentary
Commercial pricing after years of rising premiums
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Net premiums written rose 7% in Q3 (10% in constant dollars excluding North America Property) against 12.1% for the nine months. North America Commercial went from +37% in Q1 to +2% in Q3, which shows how uneven that growth is. On the call Andersen said conditions vary significantly by line of business and country, and Hancock said North America property pricing is still under pressure; excluding property, Walsh put North America commercial renewal pricing up 5%. The unavoidable tension: an underwriter that has deliberately tightened limits and ceded more to reinsurers has fewer ways to grow when pricing softens. Andersen's reason for dropping the February target was that parts of the market had deteriorated since then and AIG chose profitable growth over chasing a percentage.
Catastrophe load and reinsurance economics
ONGOING HEADWIND
AIG's catastrophe load has been moderate: 4.4 loss-ratio points in Q3 2026, 3.4 in Q2 and 3.0 in Q1, against 9.1 points in Q1 2025. Asked about reinsurance on the call, Andersen said "We like the program where it is" and that it was built to manage volatility. Reinsurance is bought at a price, and a book protected by heavy cession keeps less of the upside in a quiet year. Q3's storms landed mostly on North America Commercial, at 7.1 points, so that is where the retained exposure showed up this time. Nothing in the data separates "better structure" from "kinder weather" — only a genuinely severe season will.
Higher-for-longer rates and the value of float
EMERGING TAILWIND
Net investment income of $1,100M in Q3 2026, and $1,075M on the adjusted basis against $1,024M a year earlier, is the quiet engine. Float income scales with yields, not with underwriting skill, and it arrives every quarter. Bull framing: at 1.04× book with a 2.57% dividend, a 32.65% payout and a 4.7% annual reduction in share count, an investor is buying a growing stream of investment income and does not need underwriting heroics. Bear framing: if the earnings are coming from the bond portfolio rather than the underwriting book, then AIG is a bond fund with an 8.22% ROE and a catastrophe tail — and book value, not earnings, is the right ceiling on what to pay.
