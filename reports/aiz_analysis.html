01 Company Overview

Every time a phone is traded in at a carrier store, a car is sold with a service contract, or a mortgage servicer discovers a borrower's homeowners policy has lapsed, there is a decent chance Assurant is the company standing behind the promise. It does not sell much to you directly. It sells through the brands you already deal with — wireless carriers, retailers, auto dealers, lenders — and it gets paid for insuring, servicing, repairing and replacing the things those customers own. Assurant reports two operating segments, Global Lifestyle (connected devices and vehicles) and Global Housing (homeowners, lender-placed and renters cover). TTM revenue was $13.46B, up 9.36%, on TTM EPS of $20.88.

The company's roots go back to 1892 as the La Crosse Mutual Aid Association in Wisconsin, a disability insurer. It spent decades inside the Dutch-Belgian group that became Fortis, was spun out through a February 5, 2004 NYSE IPO, and joined the S&P 500 on April 10, 2007. The Assurant of 2026 is the product of subtraction: the health business went in 2015 and Employee Benefits was sold to Sun Life for $940M in 2016, leaving a specialty protection company rather than a conventional life-and-health insurer. That history matters for the chart below — this is the same ticker and the same legal company throughout, but per-share earnings before FY2023 include businesses and one-offs that no longer exist.

The stock has had a very good two years: from $119.99 at the May 2023 month-end to $277.38 at the September 21, 2026 close, and +15.2% year-to-date from the $240.85 December 2025 close. The question for a buyer at 13.3x trailing earnings is simple to state and hard to answer: is Housing's record profitability the new floor, or a quiet hurricane season flattering the numbers?

Revenue Mix

The engine room is lender-placed homeowners cover. Assurant tracks more than 34 million loans (+9%) for mortgage servicers; when a borrower's own policy lapses, the servicer places Assurant's cover. Only a sliver of tracked loans ever need it — the placement rate was 2.02%, down sequentially after a client portfolio transfer — but the tracking relationship is the moat. A new partnership with Freedom Mortgage, described on the call as a top-10 US servicer with 2.6 million loans, adds to the base. Q2 Adjusted EBITDA was $274.8M (+28%), or $287M and +18% excluding $12.2M of reportable catastrophes, helped by a non-cat loss ratio of about 35% from lower-than-typical claims frequency. This is a property book in hurricane country: the earnings are excellent when the weather is quiet and lumpy when it is not.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Embedded B2B2C distribution Loan-tracking scale (34M+ loans) Device repair & logistics network Carrier & OEM program integration Regulatory licensing footprint Holding-co liquidity ($911M)

The moat is plumbing. A carrier that launches a device-protection program with Assurant is integrating claims, repair, logistics and trade-in flows into its own stores and apps; a servicer that uses Assurant's loan tracking has handed it the monitoring of millions of insurance records. Neither relationship is glamorous and neither is easy to rip out. The flip side is concentration: when your customers are a handful of very large carriers and servicers, one contract loss or one portfolio transfer — as the Q2 placement-rate dip showed — moves the numbers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Catastrophe exposure in Housing. Q2 2026 reportable catastrophes were only $12.2M. A single landfalling hurricane in a year with a normal loss ratio would look very different, and Housing is more than half of segment profit.
02
Flattering loss experience. The ~35% non-cat loss ratio reflects lower-than-typical claims frequency, and 2026 already faces a $71M smaller tailwind from favourable prior-year reserve development than 2025. Management's own "mid single digits" headline growth guide versus "~10% underlying" is that gap in words.
03
Partner concentration. Lifestyle's growth rides on a small number of mobile and retail programs, and Housing's on servicer portfolios that can be transferred. Q2 already showed a placement-rate dip to 2.02% from one client portfolio transfer.
04
Non-run-rate help in Lifestyle. $10.2M of Connected Living's Q2 gain was favourable non-run-rate items; ex those, growth was 22% rather than 29%.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items distort the headline numbers. First, prior-year reserve development: releases of reserves booked in earlier years flattered 2025, and management guides a $71M smaller benefit in 2026 — which is why the outlook reads "mid single digits" growth in Adjusted EBITDA and EPS ex-cats, but "~10%" on an underlying basis. Second, catastrophes: the company reports earnings including and excluding "reportable catastrophes", and the two can diverge sharply in a bad storm year. Q2 2026 cats were $12.2M. Longer-run per-share history also needs care — FY2021 EPS of $22.65 and FY2022's $5.05 bracket the swings; the components of those years were not itemised in the sources fetched.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 13.3x trailing and about 12.5x forward earnings for a business that has grown EPS every year since 2022, earns an 18.3% ROE, runs Housing at a 72% combined ratio, and hands back $300–$350M a year in buybacks from $911M of holding-company liquidity. Lifestyle is not an insurance story at all — it is a device-and-vehicle services platform embedded in the checkout flows of large partners, growing Adjusted EBITDA 21% in Q2. Tracked loans are up 9% with a new top-10 servicer onboarding. Every one of seven covering analysts rates it a buy, with the lowest target 15% above the price.

Bear Case

Half of segment profit comes from a property book in hurricane country that just posted a ~35% non-cat loss ratio on lower-than-typical claims frequency — the definition of a good year, not a run rate. Management itself guides headline growth to only mid single digits for 2026 because $71M less reserve-release help is coming. Lifestyle's Q2 included $10.2M of non-run-rate benefits, and the whole company depends on a small set of very large partners. The stock has more than doubled off its 2023 low and the monthly RSI is overbought at 74.9; unanimous sell-side bullishness leaves no one left to upgrade.

Top 3 Risks

01
Catastrophe losses. Q2's $12.2M of reportable cats is small. Housing is 53% of segment Adjusted EBITDA; a major landfall would hit the most profitable part of the company directly.
02
Loss-ratio normalisation. A ~35% non-cat loss ratio from low claims frequency, plus a $71M smaller reserve-release tailwind, means Housing earnings can fall without anything going "wrong".
03
Partner concentration. Program-level economics depend on a handful of carriers, retailers and servicers; a lost contract or portfolio transfer (as in Q2's placement-rate dip to 2.02%) moves segment results.
Industry Commentary
Weather is the house edge — in both directions
SWING FACTOR

Property insurance is a game where the premium is set before the dice are rolled. A 72% combined ratio in H1 2026 means Housing kept 28 cents of every premium dollar before investment income — an outstanding result, and one that depends in part on a quieter-than-typical claims environment. That is why Assurant reports earnings both including and excluding "reportable catastrophes": the underlying business and the weather draw are two separate bets, and investors price the stock on the first while living with the second.

The phone upgrade cycle as an insurance line
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Connected Living makes money from the life cycle of a device, not just from insuring it: protection plans, repairs, trade-ins, refurbishment and resale. More than 7 million devices serviced in a quarter show the scale, and Assurant's own industry trends report counts $1.43B returned to US consumers through mobile trade-ins in Q2 2026. The more expensive phones get and the more carriers lean on trade-in offers to drive upgrades, the more that plumbing is worth — and the less this segment looks like insurance at all.
