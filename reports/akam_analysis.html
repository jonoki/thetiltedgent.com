01 Company Overview

Akamai made its name in CDNs: when a streaming service, a bank or a software vendor needs its bytes to arrive fast everywhere at once, Akamai's servers do the carrying. Nearly three decades on, the company reports three businesses — Security, Delivery and Compute — and the one it was founded on is now the one shrinking. FY2025 revenue was $4.208B, up 5%, of which Security was $2.243B (+10%), Delivery $1.257B (−5%) and Compute $708M (+12%). GAAP EPS was $3.07; the company's non-GAAP EPS was $7.12 — a gap wide enough that which one you use decides whether the stock looks expensive or cheap.

2026 is the year the market stopped valuing Akamai as a slow CDN with a security business attached and started pricing it as a possible AI-infrastructure landlord — and then started counting what that costs. On May 8, 2026 a seven-year, $1.8B commitment for its Cloud Infrastructure Services sent the stock up 26.6% in a day, on to a 52-week high of $165.45 on May 13. On September 24 Akamai announced a far larger one: $11.6B over seven years from Anthropic, with about $5.5B of capital spending needed to deliver it and a warrant letting Anthropic buy up to 7.74M shares at $111.33. By the Q3 2026 release on November 5, multi-year CIS commitments signed this year had passed $14B. The stock went the other way: from $117.41 on September 21 to $80.29 on November 9, down 31.6%, as the bill came into view — full-year 2026 capital spending guided to $3.46–$3.66B, a fourth quarter below expectations because of supply-chain and data-centre delays, and a proposed $2.2B sale of convertible notes on November 9. The price is the market's running estimate of one question: can a debt-funded build-out of AI inference capacity earn a return before the legacy delivery line erodes further?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Globally distributed edge footprint Traffic position → security upsell Enterprise security relationships Multi-year CIS commitments ($14B+) Adjusted EBITDA $1.8B (FY25)

The moat is position. Akamai's servers already sit inside networks all over the world, between users and the companies they are trying to reach. That position made the CDN, made the security business (you filter traffic where you already see it), and is now the pitch for inference: put the model close to the user and the answer comes back faster. The weakness is that position is not the same as pricing power. Delivery shows what happens when a service becomes a commodity — revenue falls even as traffic grows — and the compute business has to win against far larger clouds for the same customers. The $14B+ of 2026 commitments is hard evidence that customers will pay for Akamai's version; the open question is now the return on the capital it takes to serve them.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The build-out comes before the revenue. Non-GAAP operating margin fell 6 points to 25% in Q3 2026 and FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance is now $6.47–$6.68 — below FY2025's $7.12. Consensus has FY2026 non-GAAP EPS at $6.54, down 8.08% (StockAnalysis). Capital spending is guided to $3.46–$3.66B for 2026, most of it in Q4. Shareholders are paying today for capacity that customers pay for over four to seven years.
02
One customer carries the story. Most of the $14B+ of 2026 CIS commitments comes from Anthropic, which ties a large slice of the growth plan to one AI model company's own funding and priorities over seven years. The warrant issued with the September contract lets Anthropic buy up to 7.74M shares, about 5.4% of the 144.2M outstanding, at $111.33; 40% vests on its first payment and the rest only as it expands its commitment.
03
The balance sheet changed in two quarters. Total debt went from $5.87B at March 31 to $9.45B at September 30, 2026 (StockAnalysis, including $1.88B of operating leases), and debt-to-equity is 1.84. Convertible notes alone are $7.64B of principal across five series due 2027, 2029, 2030, 2032 and 2033 (10-Q), and $2.2B more was proposed on November 9. The May notes pay no coupon, so their cost shows up as potential dilution above ~$191–$201 rather than as interest.
04
Delivery keeps shrinking. −5% in FY2025, −7% in Q1 2026, −6% in Q2, −3% in Q3. Security and CIS have to outgrow a ~$1.2B+ declining base every year before the group grows at all.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the GAAP/non-GAAP gap is unusually wide: FY2025 GAAP EPS of $3.07 against non-GAAP $7.12, and Q3 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.77 against non-GAAP $1.77. The reconciling items are in the company's releases; the practical point is that the trailing P/E (31.2) and the forward P/E (12.3) are not measuring the same thing. Second, financing: the May 2026 convertible issue moved $3.5B onto the balance sheet in one quarter and funded a $350M buyback at $141.34 alongside it; the September Anthropic warrant added $149.1M to paid-in capital with a matching asset for its first 40% (10-Q); and $2.2B more of convertible notes was proposed on November 9. Third, capex timing: the new CIS contracts are dedicated capacity, so spending comes first — $3.46–$3.66B is guided for 2026 — and revenue is recognised over four to seven years; near-term free cash flow will understate the economics if the contracts perform, and overstate them if they don't.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $112.70 to the November 9, 2026 settled close of $80.29, AKAM lost 28.8% on price; over the same dates SPY gained 65.2% and QQQ 72.5% (price only, Yahoo Finance closes). It pays no dividend, so that is also its total return. For four of those five years the stock went nowhere: it spent 2022 through 2025 between a $72.60 month-end low (February 2023) and a $123.23 high (January 2024), and was back at $75.10 in October 2025. 2026 then ran the whole range in one year: up to a $149.54 May month-end on the first AI contract, and back to $80.29 by November 9 as the cost of building for the contracts came into view.

It hit 82.8 in March 2026 and 82.6 in May 2026, the two most overbought readings in the five-year series, after bottoming at 22.5 in May 2025. It now reads 32.8, just above the oversold line of 30. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Akamai has spent years building a distributed network that nobody paid a premium for. In 2026 customers started to: more than $14B of multi-year CIS commitments against $314M of FY2025 CIS revenue, CIS growing 34–40% year over year each quarter, and management guiding 2027 revenue growth of 17–22% in constant currency and 2028 revenue above $7.0B. Security — over half the company — keeps growing at about 10%. On the company's own earnings measure the stock is 12.3x FY2026 consensus, with $211M of trailing free cash flow and $564.8M of buyback authorisation left. Management, as Scotiabank’s note was reported by Investing.com, puts fully ramped CIS deals at roughly a 15% return on invested capital. If the new contracts convert on schedule, 2027 is the first year of double-digit growth in the five-year window, and the build-out spend of 2026 starts to look like an investment rather than a cost.

Bear Case

On GAAP earnings the stock is 31.2x, margins are falling (GAAP operating margin 10.49% TTM versus 23.32% in FY2021; non-GAAP 25% in Q3, down 6 points), FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance of $6.47–$6.68 is below FY2025's $7.12, and 2026 capital spending is guided to $3.46–$3.66B, more than 16 times trailing free cash flow. Debt is $9.45B with $2.2B more proposed, and the Anthropic warrant can add up to 7.74M shares. Delivery keeps shrinking. The contracts are real, but they pay out over seven years, two deals have already slipped on supply-chain delays, and ROIC is 3.53% today. A 13.29% short interest and a $73 Goldman target say a good part of the market thinks the compute pivot is being paid for twice — once in capex, once in the share price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Execution on dedicated capacity. The $11.6B, $1.8B and $600M+ contracts require Akamai to buy, deploy and run compute on schedule. Supply-chain and third-party data-centre delays on two deals have already moved about $30M of revenue from Q4 2026 into 2027; any further slip pushes the 2027 growth case back and leaves the capex without matching revenue.
02
Customer concentration in CIS. Anthropic accounts for most of the $14B+ signed in 2026. If its plans or funding change, a large share of the growth story goes with it.
03
Margin and leverage. Non-GAAP operating margin guidance has come down from 26–28% to 25% for FY2026, with 24% guided for Q4, and debt-to-equity is 1.84 with $2.2B more convertible debt proposed. If the stock rises above the ~$191–$201 conversion prices of the May notes they turn into dilution (partly offset by hedges Akamai bought when it sold them); if it doesn't, $3.5B has to be repaid or refinanced in 2030 and 2032, and the $1.15B due 2027 comes first.
Industry Commentary
Inference moves to the edge
EMERGING TAILWIND

Training an AI model happens in a few huge data centres; answering millions of user requests with it (inference) benefits from being close to the user. That is Akamai's argument, and in 2026 customers backed it with more than $14B of multi-year commitments. The open question is not whether demand exists but how much of it goes to a smaller specialist instead of the largest clouds, and at what margin once the servers are depreciated.

Content delivery is a price-deflation business
ONGOING HEADWIND

Delivery revenue fell 5% in FY2025, 6% in Q2 2026 and 3% in Q3 even as internet traffic keeps growing: the price per unit falls faster than volume rises. Akamai has leaned into that rather than fought it — the network is valuable as a platform for security and compute even if delivery itself shrinks. Gross margin shows the cost: 63.34% in FY2021, 56.39% TTM.

Funding AI capacity with convertibles
KEY CONSTRAINT

The $3.5B of 0% convertible notes issued in May 2026 was sold at a moment of strength — conversion prices of about $201.41 (2030) and $190.81 (2032) against a $141.34 reference close — and the company used $350M of it to buy back stock at that price. At $80.29 the stock is 43% below that buyback price, the balance sheet carries $9.45B of debt, a further $2.2B of convertibles was proposed on November 9, and Anthropic holds a warrant struck at $111.33. Each new contract needs capital before it pays; the return on all of it depends on contracts that pay out over the rest of the decade.
