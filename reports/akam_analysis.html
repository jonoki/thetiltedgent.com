01 Company Overview

Akamai made its name in CDNs: when a streaming service, a bank or a software vendor needs its bytes to arrive fast everywhere at once, Akamai's servers do the carrying. Nearly three decades on, the company reports three businesses — Security, Delivery and Compute — and the one it was founded on is now the one shrinking. FY2025 revenue was $4.208B, up 5%, of which Security was $2.243B (+10%), Delivery $1.257B (−5%) and Compute $708M (+12%). GAAP EPS was $3.07; the company's non-GAAP EPS was $7.12 — a gap wide enough that which one you use decides whether the stock looks expensive or cheap.

2026 is the year the market stopped valuing Akamai as a slow CDN with a security business attached and started pricing it as a possible AI-infrastructure landlord. The hinge was May 8, 2026: a seven-year, $1.8B commitment for its Cloud Infrastructure Services from a customer Akamai did not name — Bloomberg, citing people familiar, identified it as Anthropic; neither company confirmed — sent the stock up 26% in a day. It kept going to a 52-week high of $165.45 on May 13, and then gave back most of it: $149.54 at the end of May, $108.62 at the end of August. Monday September 21 added +12.33% in a single session to $117.41; no company release explains it, and the only attribution found was a vague one (ad-hoc-news: "AI security cooperation headlines"). The price is the market's running estimate of one question: can a convertible-funded build-out of distributed inference capacity earn a return before the legacy delivery line erodes further?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Globally distributed edge footprint Traffic position → security upsell Enterprise security relationships Multi-year CIS contracts ($2.8B+) Adjusted EBITDA $1.8B (FY25)

The moat is position. Akamai's servers already sit inside networks all over the world, between users and the companies they are trying to reach. That position made the CDN, made the security business (you filter traffic where you already see it), and is now the pitch for inference: put the model close to the user and the answer comes back faster. The weakness is that position is not the same as pricing power. Delivery shows what happens when a service becomes a commodity — revenue falls even as traffic grows — and the compute business has to win against far larger clouds for the same customers. The $2.8B of 2026 contracts is the first hard evidence that customers will pay for Akamai's version.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The build-out comes before the revenue. Non-GAAP operating margin fell 5 points to 25% in Q2 2026 and the FY2026 EPS guide was narrowed to $6.40–$7.05 — below FY2025's non-GAAP $7.12. Consensus has FY2026 non-GAAP EPS at $6.69, down 5.98%. Shareholders are paying today for capacity that customers pay for over four to seven years.
02
Two customers carry the story. Most of the $2.8B of 2026 CIS contracts comes from two deals. A dedicated-capacity contract with one AI model company ties a large slice of the growth plan to that customer's own funding and priorities over seven years.
03
The balance sheet changed in one quarter. Total debt went from $5.87B at March 31 to $9.34B at June 30, 2026 (StockAnalysis, including $1.41B of long-term leases), and debt-to-equity is now 1.97. $1.706B is classed as current. The new notes pay no coupon, so the cost shows up as potential dilution above ~$191–$201 rather than as interest.
04
Delivery keeps shrinking. −5% in FY2025, −7% in Q1 2026, −6% in Q2 2026. Security and CIS have to outgrow a ~$1.2B+ declining base every year before the group grows at all.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the GAAP/non-GAAP gap is unusually wide: FY2025 GAAP EPS of $3.07 against non-GAAP $7.12, and Q2 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.52 against non-GAAP $1.59. The reconciling items are in the company's releases and are not itemised here; the practical point is that the trailing P/E (42.7) and the forward P/E (17.6) are not measuring the same thing. Second, the May 2026 convertible issue moved $3.5B onto the balance sheet in one quarter and funded a $350M buyback at $141.34 alongside it, so Q2 2026 cash, debt and share count are all shaped by one transaction. Third, capex timing: the new CIS contracts are dedicated capacity, so spending comes first and revenue is recognised over four to seven years; near-term free cash flow will understate the economics if the contracts perform, and overstate them if they don't.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Akamai has spent years building a distributed network that nobody paid a premium for. In 2026 customers started to: more than $2.8B of multi-year CIS contracts against $314M of FY2025 CIS revenue, CIS growing 39–40% year over year, and consensus FY2027 revenue growth of 13.03% versus about 6% this year. Security — half the company — keeps growing at 10%. On the company's own earnings measure the stock is 17.6x FY2026 consensus, with $956.1M of trailing free cash flow and a management team buying back stock. If the new contracts convert on schedule, 2027 is the first year of double-digit growth in the five-year window, and the build-out spend of 2026 starts to look like an investment rather than a cost.

Bear Case

On GAAP earnings the stock is 42.7x, margins are falling (GAAP operating margin 11.92% versus 23.32% in FY2021; non-GAAP 25%, down 5 points), consensus FY2026 EPS is below FY2025, and debt jumped to $9.34B in one quarter to fund capacity for two large customers. Delivery keeps shrinking by 5–7% a year. The contracts are real, but they pay out over four to seven years while the servers must be bought now, and ROIC is 4.12% today. A 13.54% short interest and a $93 Goldman target say a good part of the market thinks the compute pivot is being paid for twice — once in capex, once in the share price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Execution on dedicated capacity. The $1.8B and $600M+ contracts require Akamai to buy, deploy and run compute on schedule. The first expected revenue is only $20–25M in Q4 2026; any delay pushes the 2027 growth case back and leaves the capex without matching revenue.
02
Customer concentration in CIS. Two contracts account for most of the $2.8B signed in 2026. If either customer's plans change, a large share of the growth story goes with it.
03
Margin and leverage. Non-GAAP operating margin guidance has already come down from 26–28% to 25–26% for FY2026, and debt-to-equity is 1.97 after the convertible issue. If the stock rises above the ~$191–$201 conversion prices the notes turn into dilution (partly offset by the note hedges); if it doesn't, $3.5B has to be repaid or refinanced in 2030 and 2032.
Industry Commentary
Inference moves to the edge
EMERGING TAILWIND

Training an AI model happens in a few huge data centres; answering millions of user requests with it (inference) benefits from being close to the user. That is Akamai's argument, and in 2026 two customers backed it with $2.4B of multi-year commitments. Guggenheim's John DiFucci said the company's background positions it well for "providing inference at the edge." The open question is not whether demand exists but how much of it goes to a smaller specialist instead of the largest clouds, and at what margin once the servers are depreciated.

Content delivery is a price-deflation business
ONGOING HEADWIND

Delivery revenue fell 5% in FY2025 and 6% in Q2 2026 even as internet traffic keeps growing: the price per unit falls faster than volume rises. Akamai has leaned into that rather than fought it — the network is valuable as a platform for security and compute even if delivery itself shrinks. Gross margin shows the cost: 63.34% in FY2021, 57.45% TTM.

Funding AI capacity with convertibles
KEY CONSTRAINT

The $3.5B of 0% convertible notes issued in May 2026 was sold at a moment of strength — conversion prices of about $201.41 (2030) and $190.81 (2032) against a $141.34 reference close — and the company used $350M of it to buy back stock at that price. That is well-timed financing. It also means that at $117.41 the stock is 17% below the price at which the company bought back those shares, the balance sheet carries $9.34B of debt, and the return on all of it depends on contracts that pay out over the rest of the decade.
