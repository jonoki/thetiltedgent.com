01 Company Overview

Astera Labs designs the connectivity silicon and software that sit between the expensive parts of an AI server — the GPUs, CPUs, memory and network cards — so that data moves between them at full speed without errors. Its product families are Aries (retimers for PCIe and CXL, the standard links between chips in a server; a retimer re-times and cleans up a high-speed signal so it survives a longer copper trace), Taurus (active electrical cables for Ethernet), Leo (CXL memory controllers) and, increasingly, Scorpio fabric switches, all managed by its COSMOS software suite. FY2025 revenue was $852.5M, up 115% from $396.3M in FY2024; trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 is $1.56B, up 115.2%. At a $33.33B market capitalisation (174.78M shares at the Nov 6 close) it is a member of the Nasdaq-100.

The debate on this page is not whether AI capital spending is real — Broadcom reported $16.7B of AI semiconductor revenue in its fiscal third quarter. It is whether Astera can graduate from a component supplier (retimers that Nvidia, Broadcom and Marvell can each design around) into a platform supplier whose Scorpio X-Series switches become the fabric of the scale-up rack via UALink (Ultra Accelerator Link — the open standard competing with Nvidia's proprietary NVLink for connecting accelerators inside a rack). The third quarter moved that argument forward: revenue of $584.9M beat the top of the $540–560M guide, Scorpio became the largest product family as management had said it would, and the Q4 guide of $730–750M implies another 27% sequential step at the midpoint. The stock still fell 38.6% between this edition and the last, from $310.40 to $190.71. Its worst day was Nov 5 (−19.6%), when the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell 11.2%. At 75.4× trailing GAAP earnings and a beta of 3.86 it is still priced for the transition to keep working, and the 10-Q shows how narrow the base is: one unnamed customer was 53% of Q3 revenue, up from 23% a year earlier.

Scorpio is a PCIe switch — a chip that lets many GPUs, NICs and storage devices share bandwidth inside a server (P-Series, "scale-out") or that links accelerators to each other across a rack (X-Series, "scale-up"). The Scorpio X-Series 320-lane fabric switch reached volume production in 2026 and led the step from $392.4M of revenue in Q2 to $584.9M in Q3. Management said X-Series shipped to two customers across multiple platforms in Q3, and President and COO Sanjay Gajendra said there is Q4 backlog from multiple additional customers. The CFO also said the first X-Series platforms have completed much of their initial ramp, so 2027 growth has to come from new platforms and customers. A Scorpio P-Series 320L variant was shown working with AMD Instinct platforms at AMD's Advancing AI 2026 event (Q2 2026 release). Margin profile: switches are larger, more complex chips than retimers. GAAP gross margin was 72.3% in Q3, against 73.3% in Q2 and 76.2% a year earlier, and is guided to about 72% for Q4. Management has tied margin moves to the share of hardware in sales, and said a customer warrant would take about 2 points off Q2's non-GAAP margin (MarketBeat on the Q1 2026 call). The bull reading is that a switch socket is worth many retimer sockets per rack; the bear reading is that a switch is also exactly what Broadcom and Nvidia sell.

The product that built the company. A retimer is a signal-conditioning chip placed on a board or inside a cable so that a PCIe Gen5/Gen6 link between a GPU and a CPU or network card still runs without errors over a longer distance. Every accelerator in a modern AI server tends to need one or more, which is why the stock tends to rise and fall with spending on Nvidia and Amazon Trainium systems, only harder. CFO Desmond Lynch said Aries growth in Q3 was led by PCIe Gen 6 adoption. Aries is a high-margin, high-volume, comparatively low-ASP (average selling price) part — the 75–77% gross margins of FY2024–FY2025 are largely an Aries number. The structural risk is that retimers are a well-understood function with other credible suppliers (Broadcom, Marvell, Parade, Montage) and that Nvidia's own NVLink-based designs need fewer PCIe retimers per GPU than PCIe-centric racks do.

Leo lets a server attach extra DRAM over the CXL (Compute Express Link) bus — "smart memory tiering" — and customers can evaluate it in Microsoft Azure M-series virtual machines, per the FY2025 release. CXL adoption has been slower than the 2022–2023 hype implied, so Leo is a possible future earner rather than a revenue driver today; management expects Leo to start contributing in 2027. COSMOS is the software layer across every product: telemetry, diagnostics and rack-level validation. It is not sold separately in any disclosed way, but it is the closest thing Astera has to switching costs — once a hyperscaler's fleet-management tooling is built around COSMOS telemetry, swapping in a Broadcom retimer is no longer a drop-in change.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Time-to-market on each PCIe generation Hyperscaler design-in & qualification cycles COSMOS fleet telemetry software Cloud-Scale Interop Lab UALink consortium seat Fabless, asset-light — 67% ROIC $1.33B net cash

Astera's real advantage is not a patent wall; it is cadence. It shipped PCIe 5.0 retimers into hyperscaler AI servers before the larger incumbents prioritised the category, and it repeated the approach on Gen6 and on CXL; on UALink, where its products are still in development, it is trying to do it again. Once a part is qualified into a hyperscaler platform — Amazon's Trainium racks, Nvidia-based systems, AMD Instinct — the qualification cost and the COSMOS telemetry integration make it sticky for that platform's life. The ROIC of 67.11% and a current ratio of 8.03 are the financial signature of a fabless designer that spends on engineers, not fabs. The Cloud-Scale Interop Lab (expanded in Taiwan in 2026) lets customers validate whole racks, which is a service the component vendors do not typically offer.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Customer concentration. The 10-Q puts numbers on it, without names: Customer A was 53% of Q3 2026 revenue (23% a year earlier), Customer B 14% and Customer C 10%; Customer A was also 58% of receivables at Sep 30. Some of these are manufacturing partners buying for end customers. Amazon's February 2026 warrant ties vesting to up to $6.5B of purchases, so one of the largest buyers is also tied to the equity. A single platform decision at Amazon (Trainium roadmap), Nvidia (fewer PCIe retimers per GPU under NVLink Fusion) or Microsoft can move a quarter's revenue sharply.
▸
Nvidia's captive fabric. NVLink and NVSwitch are the incumbent scale-up fabric, and Nvidia sells them as part of the system, not as a socket Astera can win. If NVLink Fusion (Nvidia's programme to let third-party CPUs and accelerators attach to NVLink) succeeds, the UALink scale-up market Scorpio X-Series is built for shrinks to non-Nvidia racks — i.e., to the same concentrated set of hyperscaler custom-silicon programmes Astera already depends on.
▸
Broadcom and Marvell can bundle. Broadcom sells the Ethernet switch, the PCIe switch, the retimer, the high-speed signal circuits (SerDes) and, for Google and Meta, the accelerator itself. Marvell sells custom silicon to Amazon and Microsoft. Both can price connectivity as an accessory to a larger socket in a way a pure-play cannot match, and both have retimer and switch products in the same generations Astera is shipping.
▸
Gross margin mix drift. Gross margin has stepped from 77.7% (Q3 2024) to 72.3% (Q3 2026) and is guided to about 72% for Q4 2026 as switches and cable modules grow and customer warrants reduce reported revenue. At 21× trailing sales there is not much room for a further slide.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The stack, from the top. Revenue of $584.9M in Q3 2026, up 154% year over year and 49% sequentially, with a Q4 guide of $730–750M. GAAP gross margin 72.3%, down about 4 percentage points in a year as switches and cable modules take share from bare retimers and customer warrants reduce reported revenue. GAAP operating margin 30.6%; non-GAAP operating margin 44.7%. The 14.1-point gap between those two operating margins is SBC: $82.6M in the quarter, 14.1% of revenue, split $49.4M R&D, $19.7M sales and marketing, $12.6M G&A, $0.9M cost of revenue. For the full year 2025 SBC was $160.0M on $852.5M of revenue — 18.8% — the largest item between GAAP EPS of $1.22 and non-GAAP EPS of $1.84, partly offset by a $49.1M income tax effect (Q4 2025 release). It is the price of hiring engineers in competition with Nvidia and Broadcom.

GAAP and non-GAAP are back in the normal order. In Q2 2026 GAAP net income ($153.1M, $0.83 a share) was higher than non-GAAP ($145.8M, $0.80), the reverse of the usual pattern, because a $70.0M income tax effect outweighed $64.0M of added-back stock compensation. In Q3 GAAP was $182.9M, $0.99 a share and non-GAAP $243.4M, $1.32. The GAAP figure is still helped by low tax: Q3 net income was above operating income ($179.1M), and the nine months carry a $48.1M tax benefit. Investors who want the operating picture should look at the Q4 guide, where GAAP EPS of $1.13–1.17 assumes a tax rate of about 20% and non-GAAP EPS of $1.62–1.66 assumes 12%.

Dilution, and the Amazon warrants. Shares outstanding are up 3.5% year over year, from 168.90M to 174.78M (10-Q covers, Oct 31 of each year); the Q4 diluted count is guided to about 187M, against 184.1M in Q3. At a $33B market cap, 3.5% a year is roughly $1.2B of value passing to employees and warrant holders annually at current prices. Layered on top are the Amazon warrants: under the February 2026 agreement Amazon can buy up to 3,262,299 shares at $142.82 as it buys up to $6.5B of Astera's switch, signal-conditioning and optical products, on top of earlier warrants for up to 2,316,175 shares. It aligns a very large customer's interest with the equity and it is also, mechanically, a discount on what Amazon spends: warrants reduced Q3 revenue by $18.1M. The 10-Q does not name its customers, so it does not say whether Customer A, at 53% of Q3 revenue, is Amazon, and some of the customers it lists are manufacturing partners buying for end customers.

Valuation — three denominators. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $2.53, 75.4× — the fully loaded number, though flattered by low tax. (ii) On FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $4.38, 43.5×, and on FY2027's $7.11, 26.8×; the StockAnalysis forward P/E works out at 27.7× at this close. (iii) On sales: 21.4× trailing revenue and about 16.4× FY2026 consensus revenue of $2.03B. Broadcom, growing AI semiconductor revenue at 221%, trades at 37.5× trailing GAAP earnings and 16.9× forward; Credo, growing revenue at 165%, trades at 42.6× trailing and 16.5× forward. The lower share price and higher trailing earnings and revenue have roughly halved the multiples in the last edition (152.9× trailing earnings, 45× sales), but Astera still costs more than both. The way that premium is earned is if Scorpio X-Series makes Astera the default scale-up fabric — a platform position — rather than one of several retimer vendors. That is the whole bet.

Bull Case

Revenue more than doubled again and the Q4 guide accelerates it: $730–750M is +174% YoY at the midpoint. Scorpio is already running above $1B annualized, management cites Q4 backlog from additional X-Series customers, and UALink gives every non-Nvidia accelerator programme — Trainium, Maia, MTIA, Instinct — an open scale-up fabric. Non-GAAP operating margin is 44.7%, ROIC is 67%, net cash is $1.33B and there are no borrowings. Consensus FY2027 revenue of $3.35B would put the stock near 10× sales at this price. An S&P 500 addition would force index funds to buy.

Bear Case

At 75× trailing GAAP earnings and 21× sales, with SBC at 14–19% of revenue and the share count growing about 3.5% a year, the stock still needs the platform thesis to be right. One unnamed customer was 53% of Q3 revenue, against 23% a year earlier, and management said the first X-Series platforms have completed much of their initial ramp. Gross margin is 5.4 points below its Q3 2024 level and guided flat to lower. Nvidia's NVLink Fusion aims to make UALink irrelevant inside Nvidia-anchored racks, Broadcom can bundle switches with the accelerators it builds for Google and Meta, and Marvell is embedded in Amazon's and Microsoft's custom silicon. A 3.86 beta means the falls are violent when they come: the stock is already 60% below June's month-end close.

Top 3 Risks

01
Scorpio X-Series fails to become the standard scale-up fabric. Q3 2026 was the first quarter in which the switch was the largest product line. If UALink deployments stay confined to one or two custom-silicon programmes while Nvidia keeps NVLink captive and Broadcom wins the Ethernet-based scale-up alternatives, Astera stays a retimer company with a switch side-business — and investors would pay no more for its earnings than they pay for Credo's or Marvell's.

Customer concentration and the Amazon relationship. Three customers were 77% of Q3 2026 revenue, one of them 53% (10-Q). Amazon's Trainium cadence, and the terms of warrants that vest with Amazon's spend, make one customer both a growth engine and a large single point of failure.
03
The price can fall a long way without a miss. On month-end closes the stock fell 55% (Dec 2024–Mar 2025), 44% (Sep 2025–Mar 2026) and 60.5% (Jun–Nov 2026) in periods when revenue grew every quarter. At 43.5× FY2026 EPS, any slowdown in growth — say, a quarter growing "only" 15% sequentially after 49% and a guided 27% — could set off another such fall. A beta of 3.86 is the market's own measure of that fragility.
  Industry Commentary
The Scale-Up Fabric War: NVLink vs UALink vs Ethernet
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Inside a rack of 72 or more accelerators, something has to connect every chip to every other chip at terabit speeds. Nvidia's answer is NVLink and NVSwitch, sold only with Nvidia GPUs; the open answer is UALink, backed by AMD, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Google, Intel and Astera; Broadcom's answer is scale-up Ethernet. Astera's entire premium over peers rests on UALink winning meaningful share of non-Nvidia racks and Scorpio X-Series being the switch inside them. Nvidia's NVLink Fusion programme — letting third-party CPUs and accelerators join NVLink — is a direct attempt to pre-empt that. This is a standards fight, and standards fights are decided by the volume buyer, not the best silicon.
Hyperscaler Capex Is Concentrated in Five Buyers
ONGOING HEADWIND
Every AI-connectivity vendor — Astera, Credo, Marvell, and the AI half of Broadcom — ultimately sells to Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta and Oracle plus the smaller AI cloud companies they fund. Broadcom's fiscal Q3 AI semiconductor revenue of $16.7B (+221%) shows the spend is enormous; it also shows how few cheques it comes from. Astera's own 10-Q shows the same thing from the other side: three customers were 77% of Q3 revenue. A pure-play like Astera, with 756 employees and one segment, has no diversification against a capex pause, a change of platform architecture, or a customer deciding to bring connectivity in-house — as several hyperscalers have already done with the accelerator itself. The Amazon warrants are the industry's concentration problem written into the company's equity.
Connectivity Content per Rack Is Rising Faster than GPU Units
EMERGING TAILWIND
Each generation of accelerator rack — from 8-GPU servers to 72-GPU NVL-class racks to the multi-hundred-accelerator scale-up domains now being specified — multiplies the number of high-speed links, and each PCIe or Ethernet generation shortens the distance copper can carry a clean signal, which is precisely the problem retimers, AECs and fabric switches exist to solve. Bull framing: content per rack grows on top of unit growth, and Scorpio adds a new, larger socket to the same racks Aries already ships into. Bear framing: more content per rack also makes connectivity a bigger line item that hyperscalers and Nvidia have every incentive to integrate, commoditise or dual-source — and at 21× sales, Astera is priced as if few of them will.
