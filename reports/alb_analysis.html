01 Company Overview

Albemarle is a lithium producer first: brine from Chile's Salar de Atacama and Silver Peak, Nevada, hard rock from its Australian interests, and conversion plants that turn both into battery-grade LCE for cathode makers. Behind the lithium sits a steadier bromine-and-specialties business. The company was spun out of Ethyl Corporation in February 1994, bought Rockwood Holdings in January 2015 in a cash-and-stock deal valued at about $6.2B, and joined the S&P 500 on July 1, 2016.

This is a commodity story, and the income statement shows it. Revenue went from $3.33B in FY2021 to $9.62B in FY2023 as lithium prices spiked, then fell to $5.14B in FY2025 when they collapsed. Diluted EPS went from $22.84 in FY2022 to −$11.20 in FY2024. In Q2 2026 it realised $19.53/kg of LCE, up 60.5% on a year earlier, and its 2026 guidance treats ~$10/kg as the low case. That price move is the whole of the 2026 turnaround: Q2 2026 net sales $1.743B (+31%), adjusted EBITDA $858.1M (+155%), diluted EPS $3.52 against −$0.16 a year earlier. The stock ran with it, from a $55.76 month-end low in May 2025 to $196.70 in April 2026, a 3.5x move. Then Chinese lithium carbonate prices turned down again and the stock gave back 43% to $112.92.

So you are not really buying a chemical company. You are buying a bet on the lithium price with a balance sheet attached, and that balance sheet is in much better shape than it was a year ago. Management's own 2026 guidance shows how much hangs on that one price: at about $10/kg it guides to $0.9–1.0B of adjusted EBITDA, and at about $30/kg to $4.2–4.4B. That is a 4x range on a single input.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Atacama brine (Chile) Australian hard-rock interests (Talison, Wodgina) Silver Peak, Nevada (US lithium brine) Conversion know-how Bromine franchise Long-term customer agreements (~40% of salts)

In a commodity business, the moat is position on the cost curve, not pricing power: when prices collapse, the higher-cost producers idle first. Albemarle's exact position on that curve was not quantified in the sources fetched. The recent losses show the limits of whatever advantage it has: at 2025-level prices even Albemarle idled capacity (Kemerton Train 1) and reported negative GAAP EPS in FY2024 and FY2025. The Specialties bromine business is the steadier half. It grew both price and volume in Q2 2026, but it is a quarter of sales.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Price-taker on a volatile commodity. Management's own 2026 guide spans $0.9B to $4.4B of adjusted EBITDA depending on the lithium price. No operational decision the company makes moves the result as much as that one input.
02
Chinese supply can come back fast. TipRanks, June 26, 2026: the stock slid as Chinese lithium carbonate prices hit a ten-week low, with traders bracing for CATL's Jianxiawo mine to restart and "unleash more supply into an already shaky market." Supply decisions in China, not demand in the US, set the marginal price.
03
Chile. Albemarle's Atacama brine is in Chile, where in April 2023 the government announced plans to take controlling stakes in the lithium industry through state-owned Codelco (as summarised on Wikipedia). Sovereign terms on the best asset are a permanent overhang.
04
Dilution from the mandatory convertible. The Q2 release puts the diluted share count at about 136M, which assumes conversion of the mandatory convertible preferred. That compares with 118.01M common shares outstanding per StockAnalysis, about 15% more shares by our arithmetic. Preferred dividends were $41.7M in Q2 alone.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $112.92 you pay under 10x consensus 2026 EPS of $11.56 and about 5.5x the midpoint of the ~$20/kg adjusted EBITDA guide. That is for a company that has already cut debt by $1.3B, sold its non-core catalyst businesses, is running a $100–150M cost programme and generated $638M of free cash flow in one quarter. Leverage is 0.5x. If lithium holds near Q2's $19.53/kg, the market is pricing in a relapse that has not happened in the numbers. If it moves toward the ~$30/kg 2021–25 average, guided EBITDA rises by roughly 70% from the ~$20/kg midpoint. Thirteen of 22 analysts rate it a Buy, and the average target is $172.56.

Bear Case

Every good number in 2026 is a price number, and the price is set in China. The stock fell 23.5% in June on a supply scare alone. At ~$10/kg, management's low case, management's own guide is $0.9–1.0B of EBITDA, and 9.8x forward earnings becomes something far higher. Trailing GAAP EPS is $0.49. There is roughly 15% dilution waiting in the mandatory convertible, the best asset sits under Chilean sovereign terms, and the CEO is changing in February. Consensus FY2027 EPS already falls 2.3%, and three of the five most recent named targets are $190 or higher.

Top 3 Risks

01
The lithium price. A 4x spread in the company's own 2026 EBITDA scenarios ($0.9B to $4.4B) on one input. Nothing else on this list comes close.
02
Chinese supply response. Mine restarts such as CATL's Jianxiawo can add supply faster than Western producers can respond, and the June 2026 sell-off shows the market prices that risk in quickly.
03
Dilution and transition. About 136M diluted shares versus 118.01M common outstanding once the mandatory convertible converts, plus a CEO handover on February 1, 2027 with the lithium cycle still unresolved.

CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Albemarle's 2026 guidance spells out the problem: three scenarios at roughly $10, $20 and $30 per kilogram of LCE, producing net sales from $4.1B to $7.8B. The company does not forecast the price. It shows you what each price is worth. In late June 2026, TipRanks reported the stock sliding as Chinese lithium carbonate prices hit a ten-week low and traders braced for CATL's Jianxiawo mine to restart, with "fears of a global lithium glut and sharp swings in futures prices" pressuring the stock. Long-term agreements covering about 40% of salts volume dampen the swings but do not remove them.

Self-help while the price does the heavy lifting
IN PROGRESS

Management has done what a price-taker can do. It sold control of Ketjen and exited Eurecat, retired $1.3B of debt, idled Kemerton Train 1, held capex to about $500M, and is tracking toward the high end of $100–150M in cost savings. These steps lower the price at which Albemarle breaks even. They do not change the price it receives. The board's choice of a BHP commercial chief as the next CEO fits that direction.

Valuation depends on the lithium price you assume
OPEN QUESTION

On trailing numbers ALB looks absurdly expensive (230x P/E). On forward numbers it looks cheap (9.8x FY2026 consensus, ~5.5x the ~$20/kg EBITDA midpoint, our calc.). Both are correct. They describe two different lithium prices. The bull framing: the balance sheet and cost base are now built to survive $10/kg and profit heavily at $20. The bear framing: $20 is the anomaly, not the norm, and Chinese supply will make sure of it. A reader should form a view on the lithium price first. The stock valuation follows from it.
