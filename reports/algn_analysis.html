01 Company Overview

Align Technology makes Invisalign — the clear plastic aligners that replaced wires and brackets for a generation of adults — plus the iTero intraoral scanner that captures the 3D image of a patient's teeth and exocad CAD/CAM software for dental labs and restorative dentists. Two Stanford classmates, Zia Chishti and Kelsey Wirth, founded it in 1997; the company listed on NASDAQ on January 26, 2001. By its own count Align has helped doctors treat approximately 23.5 million patients and serves about 302.0 thousand doctor customers. FY2025 revenue was $4.03B, up just 0.90%, with GAAP EPS of $5.65; trailing-twelve-month revenue to June 30, 2026 is $4.14B (+4.41%).

Here is the hand the market was dealt. In September 2021 this stock closed the month at $665.43. It closed September 21, 2026 at $146.88 — −77.9% in five years, with no stock split in between to muddy the arithmetic. Revenue over the same stretch went sideways: $3.95B in FY2021, $4.03B in FY2025. The business did not shrink much. What collapsed was the multiple the market would pay once the 2021 boom-year growth rate (+59.9%) did not repeat. Today the company is a slow grower with a gross margin above 70%, $981.75M of net cash, an activist shareholder (Elliott Investment Management) at the table, and a strategic and operating-model review underway. The debate now: is this a franchise that lost its growth and needs to be run for cash — or a platform that has already bottomed on volume (Q2 2026 case shipments were a record 691.8 thousand, +7.4%) and is priced as though it hasn't?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Invisalign brand (~23.5M patients) ~302K doctor customers iTero scan-to-case workflow Patent enforcement record 70.7% gross margin Net cash $981.75M

The moat is a workflow, not just a brand. Hogan: "Every digital workflow begins with a scan." A doctor with an iTero in the chair, trained on Invisalign's ClinCheck treatment-planning software, and with a patient base that asks for Invisalign by name, finds it easy to keep sending cases to Align and awkward to switch. Align also enforces its patents: on August 10, 2026 a Chinese court (Jinan Intermediate People's Court) found that competitor Angelalign infringed an Align extraction-gap-closure patent and awarded RMB 10 million in damages (first instance, open to appeal). This follows a February 2026 Unified Patent Court preliminary injunction in Europe. The weakness is that the same workflow control is what the European Commission is now looking at.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth is price-taking, not price-making. Case volume grew 7.4% in Q2, but 2026 guidance has aligner prices flat to slightly down and total revenue up only 3–4%. More cases at a flat price is a volume story, not a pricing-power story.
02
The scanner is under regulatory review. The European Commission's June 30, 2026 investigation, opened on a competitor's complaint, concerns what Align describes as its "scanner and scan acceptance policies." Align says iTero scans "can be freely exported to order aligners other than Invisalign aligners" and that the step "is purely procedural." The outcome is unknown. A finding against Align would weaken the scan-to-case link that makes the platform sticky.
03
Elective, cash-pay demand. Most aligner cases are optional purchases paid for by patients. The July 31, 2025 session showed what happens when volumes miss: the stock fell 36.6% in one day (our calculation from Yahoo daily closes, $203.57 → $129.01) after Q2 2025 revenue of $1.01B fell short of the $1.06B consensus, as reported by GuruFocus via Yahoo Finance.
04
Tax and legal leakage. On July 7, 2026 the UK Upper Tribunal ruled that clear aligners are not VAT-exempt dental prostheses. Align booked an estimated $37.5M liability (inclusive of interest), started charging 20% UK VAT from September 7, 2026 with list prices unchanged, and intends to appeal. Q1 2026 also carried $30.6M of legal settlements. One-offs keep turning up.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings is large and has been there for years. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $5.65 against non-GAAP EPS of about $10.51 (implied by StockAnalysis's estimates table). The non-GAAP figures exclude stock-based compensation, amortization of acquired intangibles, restructuring, legal settlements and contingencies, and discrete tax items. In 2026 alone: $30.6M of legal settlements in Q1, the $37.5M UK VAT contingency in Q2 (plus $1.2M of legal settlements), and guided one-time charges of $90–110M for the full year, "primarily related to restructuring and other charges" per the release. This follows a 2025 restructuring for which the company guided $150–170M of charges, as reported by GuruFocus via Yahoo Finance. The practical question for anyone valuing ALGN on the 13x forward multiple is whether these "one-offs" really are one-off. Two straight years of them suggests treating part of the gap as a recurring cost.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $146.88 you pay about 13x consensus 2026 adjusted earnings and 10.2x trailing EV/EBITDA for the category leader in clear aligners. The business has a 70%+ gross margin, $981.75M of net cash (about 9% of the market cap), a 6.07% free-cash-flow yield and a $400–500M buyback running this year. Volumes are at records (691.8K cases, +7.4%), doctor utilization is rising, and revenue per case edged up. Management has committed to ~100bps of operating-margin expansion in both 2026 and 2027, and an activist with board influence is pushing in the same direction. If the strategic review cuts costs and the cheap-scanner strategy feeds more cases, earnings can grow faster than revenue for several years — and even the lowest analyst target ($170) is above the current price.

Bear Case

Five years, −77.9%, and revenue that has not grown since 2021. On GAAP earnings the stock is 25.5x, not 13x: the gap is restructuring, legal settlements, tax contingencies and stock compensation that recur year after year. Aligner prices are flat to down, scanner revenue is shrinking by design, Angelalign competes hard in China, and a formal European Commission investigation now targets the scan-to-case link that holds the platform together. Demand is elective and cash-pay, and July 31, 2025 showed how a single volume miss gets priced: −36.6% in a day. The buyback bought stock at $169.45 that now trades at $146.88. The one Sell rating on the board (Bank of America) was maintained the day after the Q2 print.

Top 3 Risks

01
Price erosion outrunning volume. 2026 guidance pairs ~6% volume growth with flat-to-lower aligner prices and a 6–8% decline in Systems & Services, which nets to 3–4% revenue growth. If competitors (Angelalign, Spark, SureSmile and others) push prices down faster than volumes grow, the margin-expansion plan has to come entirely from cost cuts.
02
Regulatory and legal outcomes. The EC investigation into scanner and scan-acceptance policies, the UK VAT ruling under appeal ($37.5M already reserved; 20% VAT charged from September 7, 2026), and recurring legal settlements. Any one of them can move a quarter's GAAP result by tens of millions of dollars.
03
Consumer-sensitive demand with a high-beta stock. A 1.64 beta and three separate months of falls greater than 30% in five years (April 2022, October 2023, July 2025). The next miss on case volume, starting with the Q3 print (estimated for October 28), is likely to be priced just as fast.
Industry Commentary
Who controls the scan controls the case
REGULATORY OVERHANG

Digital orthodontics now starts with an intraoral scan, and Align owns both ends of that workflow: the iTero scanner that takes the scan and the Invisalign system that turns it into aligners. That integration is the strategy. Hogan: "Every digital workflow begins with a scan, making scanner adoption important driver to increasing patient access to orthodontic care and long-term platform growth." It is also exactly what a competitor has asked the European Commission to examine. Align's position is that iTero scans are exportable to other aligner makers and that its acceptance policy exists for "clinical quality, patient safety, and system reliability." The Commission has said nothing on the merits. The outcome will decide whether cheap and leased scanners keep feeding Invisalign cases, or become a neutral tool that feeds competitors too.

Competition is fought in courtrooms as well as clinics
ONGOING

Align's original Invisalign patents began expiring in 2017 (per Wikipedia), and the category has filled with rivals since: Angelalign in China, Envista's Spark and Dentsply Sirona's SureSmile among the listed ones. Align's response has been to litigate on newer technology. So far: a February 2026 Unified Patent Court preliminary injunction against Angelalign's use of Align's ClinCheck Live Update technology (upheld on appeal), the August 10, 2026 Chinese first-instance win, and a US ITC case on five patents with an initial determination expected in November 2026. The wins protect specific features, not the category. RMB 10 million in damages is immaterial next to $4.14B of revenue. What these cases can do is slow a competitor's entry into specific markets.

From growth stock to margin story
EMERGING TAILWIND

The market has stopped paying for Align's growth and is waiting to see if it will pay for margins. The pieces are in place: a non-GAAP operating margin guided to ~23.7% in 2026 and at least ~100bps higher in 2027, a strategic review backed by a global consulting firm, three new independent directors following Elliott's involvement, and $400–500M of buybacks against a $10.43B market cap. The bull framing: a 70%-gross-margin category leader with net cash at 13x adjusted earnings only needs operating leverage to re-rate. The bear framing: adjusted earnings have been flattered by excluding charges that keep recurring, and a business whose prices are flat to falling may be cutting costs just to stand still. The deciding numbers are all reported quarterly: case volume, revenue per case, GAAP operating margin, and whether the one-time charges line finally goes quiet in 2027.
