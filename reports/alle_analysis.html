01 Company Overview

Every building has doors, and every door that matters has a lock, a closer, an exit bar or a card reader on it. Allegion makes those — door controls, locks, electronic security and access-control technology — under brands that include Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN, CISA, Interflex and Kryptonite. It is a 2013 spin-off: Ingersoll Rand separated its security business on December 1, 2013, and the new company, domiciled in Ireland, entered the S&P 500 the next day. FY2025 revenue was $4.07B, up 7.82%, with diluted EPS of $7.44; trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 is $4.39B at a 21.5% operating margin.

The story here is not disruption; it is installed base. A commercial door hardware specification gets written into an architect's drawings, installed by a locksmith or integrator, and then replaced on the same standard for decades. That is how a hardware company earns a 45.1% gross margin and a 33.6% return on equity. In 2026 the two regions have pulled apart, and the third quarter kept the pattern: the Americas grew 9.1% organically at a 30.1% adjusted operating margin, led by low-double-digit growth in non-residential (commercial and institutional buildings), while International slipped 0.2% organically as lower volume outweighed higher prices. Management raised its full-year guide on October 22, 2026, after raising it in July. The stock fell from a $177.35 close in September 2025 to $130.07 in May 2026 — a 26.7% slide in eight months — and has since climbed 37.6% to $178.98, the highest point on its five-year monthly chart. The question for a buyer is whether you are paying for a steady compounder at 22.3x trailing earnings, or for a non-residential construction cycle that is closer to its top than its bottom.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Specification into building plans Installed base & replacement cycle Brand (Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN) Pricing power (30.1% Americas margin) Bolt-on acquisition engine Electronic access & credentials (ELATEC)

The moat is the spec sheet. Commercial door hardware is chosen early, by architects and hardware consultants, and it has to meet fire and life-safety codes; once a building standardises on a brand, repairs, rekeys and expansions follow that brand. The customer is rarely the person who pays, and the part is a small fraction of the building's cost — the classic conditions for pricing power. The numbers bear it out: a 45.1% gross margin and a 33.6% return on equity on a hardware business. The vulnerability is that the moat protects share, not volume: when non-residential construction slows, Allegion sells fewer doors' worth of hardware no matter how entrenched it is — and Europe is showing what that looks like.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One segment carries the growth. Americas grew 9.1% organically in Q3 2026; International shrank 0.2%. With Americas at roughly 80% of revenue and far higher margins, the group result is effectively a bet on the US non-residential and residential cycle.
02
Europe is soft and changing hands. International's adjusted margin fell 40bp to 13.9% in Q3 on lower volume, after a first-quarter ERP disruption hit production. Its leader since January 2021 retires on December 31, 2026. A new head, an ERP recovery and a weak end market in the same twelve months is a lot to land at once.
03
Growth by acquisition carries integration risk. Acquisitions added 2.6 points to Q3 2026 reported growth. ELATEC (€327.9M, closed July 2025), DCI (March 2026) and Overly (about $88.7M, October 2026) have to earn their keep; bolt-ons that do not are a slow drag on the returns that justify the multiple.
04
Leverage is real, if manageable. Borrowings of $2.03B against $388.1M of cash leave about $1.64B of net debt, roughly 1.5x trailing EBITDA of $1.10B, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.94 (balance sheet at September 30, 2026). Buybacks ($280.3M in the first nine months) and dividends are funded comfortably today; they compete with deals for the same cash.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 22.3x trailing and about 18.3x forward earnings for a business with a 45.1% gross margin, a 30.1% margin in its main segment, 19.4% ROIC and a 4.9% free-cash-flow yield. The Americas is growing high-single digits organically, with non-residential up low-double digits on volume and price; management raised its revenue, organic-growth and adjusted EPS guidance in July and again in October; and cash flow has caught up with earnings (year-to-date available cash flow up $54.2M). Europe is the drag, but it is 20% of revenue at a 14% margin — a recovery there is upside, not a requirement. Earnings are up 51% in five years and the stock 39.5%; if the multiple simply holds, holders compound with the earnings.

Bear Case

The bear case is a cycle argument, now with a fuller price attached. Allegion sells into non-residential and residential construction, and its growth is concentrated in one region while the other shrinks organically. Reported growth still leans on acquisitions — 2.6 points in Q3, with Overly added in October. After a 12.6% two-day jump the stock sits at the analysts' $178 average target, five of eleven rate it Hold, and the monthly RSI is above 70. Leverage of about 1.5x net debt to EBITDA leaves less room than the "quality compounder" label implies, and 22.3x trailing earnings is a full price for a hardware maker if US construction rolls over.

Top 3 Risks

01
US construction cycle. Americas is roughly 80% of revenue and nearly all of the organic growth. A slowdown in US non-residential building would hit the one segment that is carrying the group.
02
International execution. Organic decline of 0.2% in Q3, a 40bp margin squeeze, an ERP recovery still in progress, and a change of segment leader on January 1, 2027.
03
Acquisition dependence. Deals supplied 2.6 points of Q3 growth. If bolt-ons such as ELATEC, DCI and Overly do not reach group returns, the 19.4% ROIC drifts down and the premium multiple with it.
Industry Commentary
Two regions, two cycles
ONGOING HEADWIND

Q3 2026 showed the split plainly: in the Americas, non-residential grew low-double digits organically and residential mid-single digits; in International, lower volume produced a 0.2% organic decline despite higher prices. For a company whose product goes on doors, demand follows building activity, so Allegion's results are partly a readout of US versus European construction. Management's raised full-year guide — 5.0–6.0% organic growth, with one quarter left — rests on the Americas strength holding through year-end.

Mechanical to electronic access
EMERGING TAILWIND

The strategic direction is visible in the acquisitions: ELATEC brings RFID readers and credentials, while Schlage's Sense Pro smart deadbolt carries the consumer brand into connected locks. The logic is to attach electronic, credential-based products to an installed base of mechanical hardware. Whether electronics lift margins or just add revenue is not yet visible in the segment numbers — Americas margins were diluted 40bp by acquisitions in Q2 2026.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
DEBATE

At 15.2x EV/EBITDA and 22.3x trailing earnings, ALLE is priced as a quality industrial, not a bargain. The bull framing: a replacement-heavy, specification-driven franchise with a 30% core margin deserves a premium, and two beat-and-raise quarters in a row show earnings growth can carry the stock. The bear framing: the premium assumes US construction keeps growing while Europe stabilises; if either assumption breaks, the multiple and the earnings fall together.
