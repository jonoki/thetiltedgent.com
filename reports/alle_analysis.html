01 Company Overview

Every building has doors, and every door that matters has a lock, a closer, an exit bar or a card reader on it. Allegion makes those — door controls, locks, electronic security and access-control technology — under brands that include Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN, CISA, Interflex and Kryptonite. It is a 2013 spin-off: Ingersoll Rand separated its security business on December 1, 2013, and the new company, domiciled in Ireland, entered the S&P 500 the next day. FY2025 revenue was $4.07B, up 7.82%, with diluted EPS of $7.44; trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 2026 is $4.29B at a 21.1% operating margin.

The story here is not disruption; it is installed base. A commercial door hardware specification gets written into an architect's drawings, installed by a locksmith or integrator, and then replaced on the same standard for decades. That is how a hardware company earns a 44.8% gross margin and a 33.7% return on equity. The debate in 2026 is narrower than the price chart suggests: the Americas business grew 8.9% organically in Q2 2026 at a 30.1% adjusted operating margin, while International shrank 1.2% organically on weaker European demand after a first-quarter ERP disruption. Management raised its full-year guide on July 23, 2026. The stock fell from a $177.35 close in September 2025 to $130.07 in May 2026 — a 26.7% slide in eight months — and has since recovered to $151.36. The question for a buyer is whether you are paying for a steady compounder at 19.9x trailing earnings, or for a non-residential construction cycle that is closer to its top than its bottom.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Specification into building plans Installed base & replacement cycle Brand (Schlage, Von Duprin, LCN) Pricing power (30.1% Americas margin) Bolt-on acquisition engine Electronic access & credentials (ELATEC)

The moat is the spec sheet. Commercial door hardware is chosen early, by architects and hardware consultants, and it has to meet fire and life-safety codes; once a building standardises on a brand, repairs, rekeys and expansions follow that brand. The customer is rarely the person who pays, and the part is a small fraction of the building's cost — the classic conditions for pricing power. The numbers bear it out: a 44.8% gross margin and a 33.7% return on equity on a hardware business. The vulnerability is that the moat protects share, not volume: when non-residential construction slows, Allegion sells fewer doors' worth of hardware no matter how entrenched it is — and Europe is showing what that looks like.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One segment carries the growth. Americas grew 8.9% organically in Q2 2026; International shrank 1.2%. With Americas at roughly 80% of revenue and far higher margins, the group result is effectively a bet on the US non-residential and residential cycle.
02
Europe is soft and changing hands. International's margin fell 70bp to 12.4% on weaker core European demand, after a first-quarter ERP disruption hit production. Its leader since January 2021 retires on December 31, 2026. A new head, an ERP recovery and a weak end market in the same twelve months is a lot to land at once.
03
Growth by acquisition carries integration risk. Acquisitions added 5.1 points to Q2 2026 reported growth and diluted Americas margin by 40bp. ELATEC (€330M, closed July 2025) and DCI (March 2026) have to earn their keep; bolt-ons that do not are a slow drag on the returns that justify the multiple.
04
Leverage is real, if manageable. Debt of $2.22B against $320.6M of cash leaves about $1.90B of net debt, roughly 1.8x trailing EBITDA of $1.05B, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.05. Buybacks and dividends are funded comfortably today; they compete with deals for the same cash.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 19.9x trailing and about 16.9x forward earnings for a business with a 44.8% gross margin, a 30.1% margin in its main segment, 18.8% ROIC and a 5.2% free-cash-flow yield. The Americas is growing high-single digits organically on volume and price, management just raised its revenue, organic-growth and adjusted EPS guidance, and every one of the 11 analysts has a target above the current price. Europe is the drag, but it is 20% of revenue at a 12% margin — a recovery there is upside, not a requirement. Earnings are up 43% in five years and the stock only 14.5%; if the multiple simply stops shrinking, holders compound with the earnings.

Bear Case

The bear case is a cycle argument. Allegion sells into non-residential and residential construction, and its growth is now concentrated in one region while the other shrinks organically. Reported growth leans on acquisitions — 5.1 points in Q2 — and year-to-date available cash flow fell even as EPS rose 17.6%. Seven of eleven analysts rate it Hold. Leverage of about 1.8x net debt to EBITDA leaves less room than the "quality compounder" label implies, and a 19.9x trailing multiple is not a distressed price for a hardware maker if US construction rolls over.

Top 3 Risks

01
US construction cycle. Americas is roughly 80% of revenue and nearly all of the organic growth. A slowdown in US non-residential building would hit the one segment that is carrying the group.
02
International execution. Organic decline of 1.2%, a 70bp margin squeeze, an ERP recovery still in progress, and a change of segment leader on January 1, 2027.
03
Acquisition dependence. Deals supplied 5.1 points of Q2 growth. If bolt-ons such as ELATEC and DCI do not reach group returns, the 18.8% ROIC drifts down and the premium multiple with it.
Industry Commentary
Two regions, two cycles
ONGOING HEADWIND

Q2 2026 showed the split plainly: in the Americas, both non-residential and residential grew high-single digits organically; in International, weaker demand in core European markets produced a 1.2% organic decline. For a company whose product goes on doors, demand follows building activity, so Allegion's results are partly a readout of US versus European construction. Management's raised full-year guide — 3.5–4.5% organic growth — assumes the Americas strength persists and Europe does not worsen much.

Mechanical to electronic access
EMERGING TAILWIND

The strategic direction is visible in the acquisitions: ELATEC brings RFID readers and credentials, while Schlage's Sense Pro smart deadbolt carries the consumer brand into connected locks. The logic is to attach electronic, credential-based products to an installed base of mechanical hardware. Whether electronics lift margins or just add revenue is not yet visible in the segment numbers — Americas margins were diluted 40bp by acquisitions in Q2 2026.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
DEBATE

At 14.1x EV/EBITDA and 19.9x trailing earnings, ALLE is priced as a quality industrial, not a bargain. The bull framing: a replacement-heavy, specification-driven franchise with a 30% core margin deserves a premium, and the 2026 pullback took the multiple back to where earnings growth alone can carry the stock. The bear framing: the premium assumes US construction keeps growing while Europe stabilises; if either assumption breaks, the multiple and the earnings fall together. The Q3 print on the estimated October 22 date — Americas organic growth and International's recovery — is the next data point for both sides.
