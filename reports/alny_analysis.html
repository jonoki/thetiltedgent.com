01 Company Overview

Alnylam discovers, develops and sells medicines built on RNA interference (RNAi). In one plain-English sentence: RNAi drugs intercept and destroy the cell's messenger-RNA instructions for building a specific protein, so instead of blocking a harmful protein after it exists, the drug stops the body from making it at all. The company's four wholly-owned marketed products — Amvuttra (vutrisiran), Onpattro (patisiran), Givlaari (givosiran) and Oxlumo (lumasiran) — plus collaboration and royalty income produced FY2025 revenue of $3.71B, up 65.2% from $2.25B in FY2024; trailing-twelve-month (TTM) revenue through September 2026 is $5.01B, up 56.2% on the twelve months before (our calculation from the company's releases). Alnylam is a Nasdaq-100 member worth $22.10B at the October 30 close (133.94M shares). It is a GAAP-profitable commercial biotech, which it was not two years ago: FY2024 was a $278M net loss and FY2025 a $314M net profit. TTM net income is $2.65B, but $1.87B of that is a one-time, non-cash tax benefit booked in Q3 2026, when Alnylam judged it would earn enough to use its past losses against future tax (a "valuation allowance release").

The central debate is not whether Alnylam can make money — it does — but how big the TTR franchise gets, and how fast. Amvuttra's March 2025 label expansion into ATTR amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is what flipped the P&L. It also set expectations the company has twice had to lower. On July 30, 2026 Alnylam cut FY2026 product-revenue guidance from $4.9–5.3B to $4.7–5.1B and the stock fell 28% in one session. On October 29, 2026 it kept that range but said TTR sales would land at its low end, that new-patient demand is building more slowly than planned, and that a tighter UnitedHealth Medicare Advantage policy and a proposed change to Medicare's 340B drug-payment program could slow orders; the stock fell 25.1% that day and 5.2% the next. Between the two, on July 9, AstraZeneca and Ionis reported that eplontersen (Wainua) missed its primary endpoint in the Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform ATTR-CM trial. The stock closed October 30 at $165.00: down 63.3% over 52 weeks and 63.8% below its October 2025 monthly peak of $456.04, while TTM revenue grew 56%. Sections 02 through 05 take apart how much of that fall is the business and how much is lost confidence in the forecast.

This is the company. ATTR amyloidosis is a disease in which the transthyretin (TTR) protein misfolds and deposits in nerves (polyneuropathy, ATTR-PN) or heart muscle (cardiomyopathy, ATTR-CM). Amvuttra silences TTR production at the source and is injected once every three months by a healthcare provider; Onpattro is the older infused predecessor and is being replaced by Amvuttra on purpose — its 50% fall to $19.6M is patients switching within Alnylam's own portfolio. Amvuttra was approved for ATTR-CM in March 2025; in Q3 2026 it sold $1,130M, up 65%, driven by more patients with ATTR-CM in the US and a modest rise in US net price (Q3 2026 release). Two items flattered the quarter: a $17M US inventory build by distributors and about $10M of one-time pricing adjustments, which management said do not measure demand. At quarter end Amvuttra held about 24% of all ATTR prescriptions and over 30% of new-to-brand prescriptions; more than 2,000 doctors have prescribed it, but only about a third of the doctors who treat the disease (Q3 2026 call and release).

What changed in October. In July, Alnylam cut FY2026 TTR guidance from $4.4–4.7B to $4.2–4.5B, saying the early surge of already-diagnosed patients switching to Amvuttra had run its course. In October it kept the range, but CFO Jeffrey Poulton said on the call: "we expect to land at the low end of our $4.2 billion to $4.5 billion range." CEO Yvonne Greenstreet added that she does not expect "acceleration of growth in Q4 2026." Management gave three reasons: a bigger sales force is reaching more doctors but, in Poulton's words, efforts "are gaining traction but are not yet translating into demand at the pace that we anticipated"; UnitedHealth made its Medicare Advantage policy for first-line Amvuttra stricter from October 1, which management expects to delay new patients (existing ones are unaffected); and a proposed Medicare rule would cut what hospitals in the 340B program (a federal scheme that lets qualifying hospitals buy drugs at a discount) are paid for drugs like Amvuttra from January 1, 2027, which could make Q4 orders uneven. Alnylam will give 2027 revenue guidance with Q4 results in mid-February rather than in January.

The pipeline is the other half of the market cap. Four programmes carry the long-dated value, and none of them brings in product revenue today:

Nucresiran — next-gen TTR
ATTR-CM launch by 2030
The key long-term asset: a more potent TTR silencer that Alnylam hopes to dose less often than Amvuttra, and its plan for defending the franchise before Amvuttra's patents run out. On October 29 Alnylam enlarged the TRITON-CM outcomes trial from 1,750 to about 2,250 patients, focused on earlier-stage disease. R&D chief Pushkal Garg said the change is "to address the potential risk of lower-than-expected event rates" — patients on today's drugs have fewer heart events, so a trial needs more of them to show a difference. Alnylam still expects an ATTR-CM launch by 2030, if the data are positive and regulators approve. A bigger trial buys statistical power and costs more.
Zilebesiran — hypertension
Phase 3 (Roche)
An RNAi drug for high blood pressure, partnered with Roche and in the ZENITH Phase 3 outcomes trial (the first patient dosed earned Alnylam the $300M milestone in Q3 2025). This is the platform's shot at a mass-market disease rather than a rare one: an injection given a few times a year instead of daily pills. The Q3 2026 release names ZENITH as one reason R&D spending rose.
Mivelsiran — CAA / Alzheimer's
Phase 2
Targets amyloid precursor protein. Enrolment is complete in the cAPPricorn-1 Phase 2 trial in cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), and a Phase 2 trial in Alzheimer's disease linked to Down syndrome has started (Q3 2026 release). The brain is the hardest place to deliver an RNAi drug and the most valuable place to succeed; read this as a long shot with a large payoff, not a revenue line.
Brezosiran — Huntington's disease
Pivotal study planned 2027
Partnered with Regeneron. Initial Phase 1b data on October 23, 2026: a single dose cut the disease-causing huntingtin protein in spinal fluid by 60–64% on average at month 3 in the highest-dose group. A placebo-controlled pivotal study is planned for 2027, pending agreement with regulators. Also on the list: ALN-6400 (bleeding disorders), with Phase 1 data at ASH in December.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
RNAi PLATFORM IP GalNAc DELIVERY RARE-DISEASE PRESCRIBER NETWORK ORPHAN EXCLUSIVITY CLINICAL DATA IN BOTH ATTR-PN & ATTR-CM ROYALTY STREAM FROM PARTNERED ASSETS SELF-FUNDED R&D

The durable advantage is not any single drug — it is that Alnylam has turned RNA interference into a repeatable commercial engine. Four approved wholly-owned products, two partnered products earning royalties (Leqvio, Qfitlia) and a Phase 2/3 pipeline spanning cardiology, hepatology, haematology and CNS all come from the same delivery chemistry and the same manufacturing base. That is why the TTM gross margin is 77.3% and why a company of about 2,500 employees can pay for a 2,250-patient outcomes trial out of its own cash flow. The commercial moat is narrower and more conventional: specialist prescribers in amyloidosis centres, orphan-drug exclusivity (extra years of market protection for rare-disease drugs), and the reluctance to switch a patient off a chronic therapy that is working. More than 2,000 doctors have now prescribed Amvuttra; that only about a third of the doctors who treat the disease have tried it is the moat's current limit, and in October management said winning the rest is taking longer than it hoped.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One drug is 77.5% of revenue. Amvuttra is $1.13B of a $1.46B quarter. Every material risk to the company — competition, regulation, reimbursement, manufacturing, patents — runs through one molecule. Onpattro is shrinking by design, and Givlaari and Oxlumo together grew 12% and are too small to absorb a shock. October showed the reimbursement side of that risk: one insurer's policy (UnitedHealth's Medicare Advantage plans) and one proposed Medicare rule were enough to cloud the next quarter.
02
Doubt spilling over from the CARDIO-TTRansform failure. On July 9, 2026 eplontersen — a TTR-lowering antisense drug, a close mechanistic cousin — missed its primary endpoint on cardiovascular mortality and events in ATTR-CM. Ionis closed 23.9% lower that day and AstraZeneca's US-listed shares 5.7% lower. The uncomfortable question it raised is whether knocking down TTR production is sufficient in cardiomyopathy, or whether the stabiliser mechanism (tafamidis, acoramidis) is doing more of the work than the silencer camp assumed. Alnylam has its own positive ATTR-CM data and its own approval; but investors now put some odds on the whole TTR-silencing approach falling short in heart disease, and sales figures cannot argue those odds away.
03
Growth now depends on new diagnoses and new prescribers. Management's July explanation was that the pool of already-diagnosed patients switching to Amvuttra had been used up; in October it added that a larger sales force has not yet turned into faster demand. Growth now depends on how fast new ATTR-CM patients are diagnosed and how fast the two-thirds of treating doctors who have never prescribed Amvuttra start to — things Alnylam influences but does not control. That makes revenue harder to forecast and means guidance can disappoint again for reasons that have nothing to do with how well the drug works. Management has already pushed 2027 guidance from January to mid-February.
04
A larger incumbent with a pill. Pfizer's tafamidis family sold $1.76B in Q2 2026, up 9%, as a pill against Amvuttra's injection every three months, and BridgeBio's Attruby (acoramidis) is a second pill in the same class. Alnylam is asking cardiologists to start elderly heart patients on an injection ahead of an established oral standard of care — a harder sell than any of its rare-disease launches.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A company whose TTM revenue grew 56% to $5.01B, whose flagship product grew 65% to $1.13B in a quarter, which generated $806M of free cash flow over twelve months and holds $3.59B of cash and securities, is trading 63.8% below its peak and at a market value of $22.10B — about 4.4× its trailing revenue. The FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $11.93 puts it at about 14× earnings. The royalty line doubled, the rare-disease franchise grows at 12% with little competition, and the pipeline — nucresiran, zilebesiran with Roche, mivelsiran, brezosiran with Regeneron — gets little value at this price. Twenty-two of thirty analysts rate it Buy or better, none rates it Sell, and the lowest target, $199, is above the close.

Bear Case

One drug is 77.5% of revenue, and management has now twice said its demand is growing more slowly than planned: in July it cut guidance, in October it pointed to the low end and pushed 2027 guidance to February. A large insurer tightened access from October 1, a proposed Medicare rule threatens how hospitals are paid for the drug from 2027, and Pfizer's pill franchise still sells more. A similar competitor drug failed its ATTR-CM outcomes trial in July, and Alnylam's next-generation answer, nucresiran, has had its outcomes trial enlarged to about 2,250 patients with a launch not expected before 2030. The headline P/E of 8.6× is an accounting effect of a one-time tax benefit; on earnings without it the stock trades near 29× trailing.

Top 3 Risks

01
The 2027 guide in February. July cut the range; October kept it but pointed to its low end and moved 2027 guidance from January to mid-February. If the 2027 number shows little growth over 2026, or Q4 misses the low end because of 340B-related ordering swings, investors would have a third reason to doubt the forecast. Without the tax benefit, the TTM net margin is about 15.5%, which leaves little cushion for a revenue shortfall.
02
The mechanism question, unanswerable until 2030. CARDIO-TTRansform put a legitimate scientific doubt on the table about TTR-lowering in cardiomyopathy. Alnylam cannot resolve it with commercial data — only with the TRITON-CM outcomes readout, which is now a trial of about 2,250 patients targeting a 2030 launch. Until then, every quarter of Amvuttra sales gets discounted by a probability the market controls and the company cannot move.
03
Reimbursement and concentration. With 77.5% of revenue in one drug and the next-generation drug years away, there is no buffer. A payer policy (like UnitedHealth's October change), the final 340B rule, a manufacturing interruption, a label change or an unfavourable comparison study would hit almost the entire P&L at once — and with a 0.27 beta, a rising market is unlikely to cushion it.
  Industry Commentary
Silencers vs Stabilisers: An Unsettled Scientific Argument
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
ATTR-CM has two competing drug philosophies. Stabilisers — Pfizer's tafamidis ($1.76B of sales in Q2 2026) and BridgeBio's acoramidis — hold the existing TTR protein in shape so it stops misfolding. Silencers — Alnylam's Amvuttra and nucresiran, and Ionis/AstraZeneca's eplontersen — stop the liver making TTR at all. On July 9, 2026, eplontersen missed its primary endpoint in the CARDIO-TTRansform outcomes trial (up to 140 weeks of follow-up); Ionis closed 23.9% lower that day and AstraZeneca's US-listed shares 5.7% lower. 57% of the trial's patients were already on a stabiliser drug when they joined (ESC 2026 presentation, August 2026), which makes the result hard to interpret and is the strongest argument that the failure does not carry over cleanly to Amvuttra. But the science takes years to settle, and share prices move in an afternoon. Until TRITON-CM reads out toward 2030, this argument sits unresolved underneath Alnylam's entire valuation.
Diagnosis Rate Is Now the Growth Constraint
ONGOING HEADWIND
An estimated 300,000–500,000 people worldwide have ATTR-CM and the large majority are undiagnosed — the disease presents as heart failure in elderly patients and gets attributed to age. Early in the launches, growth came from patients already diagnosed and waiting. Alnylam's July guidance cut was its acknowledgement that this phase is over, and October added that winning new prescribers is slower than planned. That makes the category's growth rate a function of how often cardiologists test for the disease and refer patients, and of what insurers will pay, rather than of drug launches. It is a slower curve — and it is why Amvuttra's ~24% prescription share, its 2,000+ prescribers and the fact that only about a third of treating doctors have tried it now matter more than quarterly revenue.
RNAi Is Graduating From Rare Disease to Chronic Disease
EMERGING TAILWIND
The strategic shift underneath all of this is that RNA interference is moving out of ultra-orphan indications and into populations measured in tens of millions. Leqvio (Novartis) already does this in cholesterol; zilebesiran, partnered with Roche, attempts it in hypertension with the promise of infrequent dosing replacing daily pills; mivelsiran attempts the hardest version of it in CAA and Alzheimer's. Alnylam's royalty and collaboration line — guided up to $575–625M for FY2026 in July even as product guidance came down, with Q3 royalties up 105% — is early, checkable evidence that this is real revenue and not a slide. Bull framing: at $22.10B the market is paying about 14× FY2027 consensus earnings for the TTR franchise and getting the platform's chronic-disease programmes nearly free, which is what a 96% average analyst upside is saying. Bear framing: zilebesiran and mivelsiran are years from revenue and partly partnered away, nucresiran's outcomes trial now reads out toward 2030, and in the meantime the company is one cardiology drug whose growth outlook has been lowered twice in three months. The pipeline's value is real; it also cannot be proved or disproved for several years, and investors tend to stop paying for that kind of value after a company disappoints on guidance.
