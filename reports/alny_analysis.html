01 Company Overview

Alnylam discovers, develops and sells medicines built on RNA interference (RNAi). In one plain-English sentence: RNAi drugs intercept and destroy the cell's messenger-RNA instructions for building a specific protein, so instead of blocking a harmful protein after it exists, the drug stops the body from making it at all. The company's four wholly-owned marketed products — Amvuttra (vutrisiran), Onpattro (patisiran), Givlaari (givosiran) and Oxlumo (lumasiran) — generated FY2025 revenue of $3.71B, up 65.2% from $2.25B in FY2024, and trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 2026 is $4.80B, up 95.1%. Alnylam ranks roughly #737 globally by market capitalisation at $35.61B. It is a GAAP-profitable commercial biotech, which it was not two years ago: FY2024 was a $278M net loss, FY2025 a $314M net profit, and the TTM figure is $775M.

The central debate is not whether Alnylam can make money — it demonstrably does — but how big the TTR franchise actually gets, and how fast. Amvuttra's March 2025 label expansion into ATTR amyloidosis with cardiomyopathy (ATTR-CM) is what flipped the P&L. It also set expectations that the company has now had to walk back: on July 30, 2026 Alnylam cut FY2026 product-revenue guidance from $4.9–5.3B to $4.7–5.1B and the stock fell 28% in one session. Three weeks earlier, on July 9, 2026, AstraZeneca and Ionis reported that eplontersen (Wainua) missed its primary endpoint in the Phase 3 CARDIO-TTRansform ATTR-CM trial — a competitor's failure that the market read as a warning about the whole TTR-silencing mechanism rather than as good news for Alnylam. The stock is down 40.5% over 52 weeks and 41.6% below its October 2025 monthly peak of $456.04 while revenue nearly doubled. Sections 02 through 05 take apart which of those two facts is the mispriced one.

This is the company. ATTR amyloidosis is a disease in which the transthyretin (TTR) protein misfolds and deposits in nerves (polyneuropathy, ATTR-PN) or heart muscle (cardiomyopathy, ATTR-CM). Amvuttra silences TTR production at the source and is dosed quarterly by a healthcare provider; Onpattro is the older infused predecessor and is being actively cannibalised — its −65% YoY decline to $18.5M is by design, not by competitive loss. Amvuttra was approved for ATTR-CM in March 2025, and the ramp since has been the fastest in Alnylam's history: +106% YoY to $1,010M in Q2 2026, with US TTR revenue up 114% YoY and international up 31%. The commercial argument management makes is a first-line positioning one — quarterly dosing, approval in both ATTR-CM and hATTR-PN, and a consistent treatment effect with or without a background stabiliser. Roughly 80% of ATTR-CM category growth is now coming from first-line starts, over 1,700 new prescribers have been added since the CM launch, and about one-third of active TTR treaters have prescribed Amvuttra — the last number is the bull and bear case in a single statistic, depending on whether you read it as headroom or as resistance.

What broke in July. Q2 Amvuttra revenue of $1.01B missed a $1.05B consensus, and FY2026 TTR guidance was cut from $4.4–4.7B to $4.2–4.5B. The stated reason was narrow and specific: "second-line patient flow, which was elevated early in the launch due to pent-up demand, has normalised to more sustainable levels." In plain terms, the backlog of already-diagnosed patients switching off a stabiliser has been worked through, and growth from here depends on newly diagnosed first-line patients — a slower, more durable, and less predictable source. That is a change in the shape of the curve, not in its direction.

This line is Alnylam's platform being monetised by other people's sales forces: royalties on Leqvio (inclisiran, an RNAi cholesterol-lowering therapy commercialised by Novartis), Qfitlia (fitusiran, in haemophilia, partnered with Sanofi), and collaboration payments from the Roche partnership on zilebesiran. The pipeline is the other half of the market cap. Three programmes carry the long-duration value, and none of them contributes a dollar of revenue today:

Nucresiran — next-gen TTR
2028 / 2030
The key long-term asset. Designed for >95% TTR knockdown with potential twice-yearly dosing — a step change over Amvuttra's quarterly HCP-administered schedule, and the mechanism by which Alnylam intends to defend the franchise against its own patent cliff. TRITON-CM was expanded from ~1,250 to 1,750 participants (targeting a 2030 launch); TRITON-PN runs ~125 participants targeting 2028. The trial expansion cuts both ways: more statistical power, but also more cost and a later readout.
Zilebesiran — hypertension
Roche partnered
An RNAi therapy targeting angiotensinogen for hypertension, partnered with Roche. This is the platform's shot at a genuinely mass-market indication rather than a rare disease — the economics of a twice-yearly blood-pressure injection are enormous if it works, and the partnership structure means Alnylam shares both the cost and the upside. Confirmed as "advancing" in the Q2 2026 materials; specific trial timelines were not verified from the sources fetched.
Mivelsiran — CAA / Alzheimer's
Ph2 enrolled
Targets amyloid precursor protein. Phase 2 enrolment complete in cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), and Alzheimer's disease studies have been initiated. CNS is the hardest place to deliver an RNAi drug and the highest-value place to succeed; treat this as a genuine option with a low probability and a very large payoff, not as a base-case revenue contributor. A fourth programme, ALN-6400, entered two Phase 2 trials in bleeding disorders.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
RNAi PLATFORM IP GalNAc DELIVERY RARE-DISEASE PRESCRIBER NETWORK ORPHAN EXCLUSIVITY CLINICAL DATA IN BOTH ATTR-PN & ATTR-CM ROYALTY STREAM FROM PARTNERED ASSETS SELF-FUNDED R&D

The durable advantage is not any single drug — it is that Alnylam is the only company that has industrialised RNA interference into a repeatable commercial engine. Four approved wholly-owned products, two partnered products earning royalties (Leqvio, Qfitlia) and a Phase 2/3 pipeline spanning cardiology, hepatology, haematology and CNS all come from the same delivery chemistry and the same manufacturing base. That is why the gross margin is 79.7% and why a rare-disease company with 2,500 employees can fund a 1,750-patient outcomes trial out of operating cash flow. The commercial moat is narrower and more conventional: a specialist prescriber base in amyloidosis centres, orphan-drug exclusivity, and the switching friction of a chronic therapy. Roughly 1,700 new prescribers added since the ATTR-CM launch is the moat being widened in real time; the fact that only one-third of active TTR treaters have written a single Amvuttra script is the moat's current limit.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Single-product concentration at 78% of revenue. Amvuttra is $1.01B of a $1.29B quarter. Every material risk to the company — competitive, regulatory, reimbursement, manufacturing, patent — routes through one molecule. Onpattro is already in managed decline, and Givlaari and Oxlumo together grow at 11% and cannot absorb a shock. There is no diversification here for at least four years, and arguably not until nucresiran launches.
02
Mechanism read-through from the CARDIO-TTRansform failure. On July 9, 2026 eplontersen — a TTR-lowering antisense drug, a close mechanistic cousin — missed its primary endpoint on cardiovascular mortality and events in ATTR-CM. Ionis fell ~19% and AstraZeneca ~13% on the day. The uncomfortable question it raised is whether knocking down TTR production is sufficient in cardiomyopathy, or whether the stabiliser mechanism (tafamidis, acoramidis) is doing more of the work than the silencer camp assumed. Alnylam has its own positive ATTR-CM data and its own approval; but the market has now priced a non-zero probability that the class thesis is wrong, and that probability is very hard to argue away with commercial metrics.
03
The second-line backlog is gone. Management's own explanation for the guidance cut is that the pool of already-diagnosed patients switching onto Amvuttra has been consumed. Growth now depends on the rate at which new ATTR-CM patients are diagnosed — a function of cardiology referral patterns and imaging uptake that Alnylam influences but does not control. This makes revenue less predictable quarter to quarter and, critically, means a guidance range can be missed again for reasons that have nothing to do with the drug's efficacy.
04
An incumbent with 15× the sales force and a stabiliser with a decade of data. Pfizer's tafamidis franchise, cited at $6.4B, is an oral pill against a quarterly injection. BridgeBio's Attruby is growing over 200% and competing on structural-reversal claims. Alnylam is arguing for first-line displacement of an oral standard of care in an elderly cardiology population — a commercially harder sell than any of its rare-disease launches, and one where physician inertia is a real cost line.

A note on how to read these. In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a stock can stay oversold for a long time if the fundamentals are genuinely deteriorating.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 monthly close of $159.56 to the September 4, 2026 close of $266.11, ALNY returned +66.8% over five years — a compound annual rate of about 10.8%, with no dividend. That respectable headline hides two completely different companies. The first three years were a pre-commercial slog: the stock spent from October 2021 to May 2024 range-bound between roughly $126 and $238, bottoming at $125.80 in May 2022 as a loss-making platform biotech was repriced by rates. Then, over twenty-nine months from May 2024 to October 2025, it rose +262% to a monthly peak of $456.04 as ATTR-CM went from possibility to approval to a billion-dollar quarter. And then it gave back 41.6% in eleven months, more than half of that in a single session.

Relative performance. A precisely matched NASDAQ-100 and S&P 500 total-return comparison over this identical window could not be verified from the sources fetched and is therefore not quoted. What can be stated from verified data: ALNY's 52-week price change is −40.52%, a period over which both the NASDAQ-100 and the S&P 500 rose, and over which Alnylam's own TTM revenue grew 95%. Separately, coverage of the sell-off noted the stock was down roughly 30% in 2026 "despite a booming biotech market" — so this is idiosyncratic underperformance, not sector beta. The reported beta of 0.30 is consistent with that framing and is exactly why it should not be mistaken for low risk: ALNY does not move with the index, it moves with its own guidance.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A company whose TTM revenue grew 95% to $4.80B, whose flagship product grew 106% to a $1B quarter fifteen months after approval, which generates $819.9M of free cash flow and holds $3.31B of cash, is trading 41.6% below its peak because it trimmed a guidance range by $200M for a reason management explained in a single sentence. The 2027 consensus EPS of $12.59 puts the stock at 21× two-year earnings. Meanwhile the collaboration and royalty line was raised $150M, the rare-disease franchise compounds at 11% with no competition, and the pipeline — nucresiran, zilebesiran with Roche, mivelsiran in CAA and Alzheimer's — is essentially unpriced at this level. Twenty-three of twenty-nine analysts rate it Buy or better and not one rates it Sell.

Bear Case

One drug is 78% of revenue, and the source of its growth just changed from a working-down of pent-up demand to the far slower rate at which new patients are diagnosed. Management missed its own guidance within five months of setting it. A mechanistically adjacent competitor failed its ATTR-CM outcomes trial in July, which means the market now assigns real probability to the possibility that TTR silencing is not the winning approach in cardiomyopathy — and Alnylam's answer to that, nucresiran, just had its pivotal trial enlarged to 1,750 patients with a 2030 launch target. The stock still trades at 47× trailing earnings built on four profitable quarters, one of which looks distorted by a non-recurring item, against an incumbent with a $6.4B oral franchise and a challenger growing over 200%.

Top 3 Risks

01
A second consecutive guidance revision. The July cut was survivable because management gave a clean, mechanical explanation. A second cut would convert "the backlog normalised" into "we do not have visibility on demand," and the multiple would compress against a still-thin 16.1% net margin. The Q3 print in late October is where this is decided.
02
The mechanism question, unanswerable until 2030. CARDIO-TTRansform put a legitimate scientific doubt on the table about TTR-lowering in cardiomyopathy. Alnylam cannot resolve it with commercial data — only with the TRITON-CM outcomes readout, which is now a 1,750-patient trial targeting a 2030 launch. Until then, every quarter of Amvuttra sales gets discounted by a probability the market controls and the company cannot move.
03
Concentration compounding into the patent horizon. With 78% of revenue in one molecule, no dividend, negligible book equity and the franchise-defence asset four years out, there is no buffer. A manufacturing interruption, a reimbursement decision, a label change or an unfavourable comparative study in Amvuttra would hit almost the entire P&L at once — and the 0.30 beta guarantees no index move will cushion it.
  Industry Commentary
Silencers vs Stabilisers: An Unsettled Scientific Argument
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
ATTR-CM has two competing drug philosophies. Stabilisers — Pfizer's tafamidis (cited at $6.4B of sales) and BridgeBio's acoramidis — hold the existing TTR protein in shape so it stops misfolding. Silencers — Alnylam's Amvuttra and nucresiran, and Ionis/AstraZeneca's eplontersen — stop the liver making TTR at all. On July 9, 2026, eplontersen missed its primary endpoint in the 140-week CARDIO-TTRansform outcomes trial; Ionis fell ~19%, AstraZeneca ~13%, and Wainua's peak-sales estimate was cut from ~$6.5B to ~$4B. Notably, 57% of patients in that trial were on tafamidis at baseline, rising to ~80% by study end — which makes the result genuinely hard to interpret, and is the strongest argument that the failure does not read across cleanly to Amvuttra. But interpretation takes years and repricing takes an afternoon. Until TRITON-CM reads out toward 2030, this argument sits unresolved underneath Alnylam's entire valuation.
Diagnosis Rate Is Now the Growth Constraint
ONGOING HEADWIND
An estimated 300,000–500,000 people worldwide have ATTR-CM and the large majority are undiagnosed — the disease presents as heart failure in elderly patients and gets attributed to age. For three years the industry grew by treating the identified backlog. Alnylam's July guidance cut is the first public admission that this phase is over: second-line flow "has normalised," and ~80% of category growth now comes from first-line starts. That reframes the entire sector's growth rate as a function of cardiology imaging and referral behaviour rather than of drug launches. It is a slower, more durable curve — and it is why 1,700 new prescribers and the fact that only one-third of TTR treaters have written Amvuttra are now the metrics that matter more than quarterly revenue.
RNAi Is Graduating From Rare Disease to Chronic Disease
EMERGING TAILWIND
The strategic shift underneath all of this is that RNA interference is moving out of ultra-orphan indications and into populations measured in tens of millions. Leqvio (Novartis) already does this in cholesterol; zilebesiran, partnered with Roche, attempts it in hypertension with the promise of infrequent dosing replacing daily pills; mivelsiran attempts the hardest version of it in CAA and Alzheimer's. Alnylam's royalty and collaboration line — guided up to $575–625M for FY2026 even as product guidance came down — is the early, verifiable evidence that this is real revenue and not a slide. Bull framing: at $35.6B the market is paying roughly 21× 2027 consensus earnings for the TTR franchise and getting the platform's chronic-disease optionality free, which is what a 39% average analyst upside is expressing. Bear framing: zilebesiran and mivelsiran are years from revenue and partnered away in part, nucresiran's pivotal now reads out toward 2030, and in the meantime the investable company is one cardiology drug whose growth rate just got revised down. The optionality is real; it is also unfalsifiable for the next several years, which is precisely the kind of value the market stops paying for when a guidance range gets missed.
