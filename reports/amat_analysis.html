01 Company Overview
Applied Materials is the world's largest supplier of materials engineering equipment for semiconductor manufacturing — the deposition, etch, chemical mechanical planarization (CMP, a polishing step that flattens wafer surfaces between layers), ion implantation, thermal processing, and process-control tools that physically build a transistor layer by layer. It is the only vendor with meaningful share across essentially every non-lithography step, which gives it roughly 19–20% of global wafer fab equipment (WFE) spending — second overall only to ASML, whose extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography monopoly makes it #1 by revenue.
Fiscal 2026 (ended October 25, 2026) revenue was a record $34.45B, up 21% YoY, with GAAP EPS of $12.62 (+46%) and non-GAAP EPS of $12.89 (+37%) on GAAP net income of $10.09B. The year sped up into its close: fiscal Q4, reported November 12, 2026, brought record revenue of $10.41B, up 53% YoY, non-GAAP EPS of $4.16 (+92%), non-GAAP gross margin of 50.6% and record quarterly free cash flow of $2.6B. Management guided fiscal Q1 2027 to $11.1B ±$500M and $4.35 ±$0.20 of non-GAAP EPS (CFO Brice Hill put the revenue midpoint 58% above a year earlier), said Applied is on track to grow its Semiconductor Systems business more than 40% in calendar 2026, and on the call Dickerson said "wafer fab equipment demand will be up more than 30% year-over-year" in 2027. Analysts' consensus (StockAnalysis, read November 17, 2026) models fiscal 2027 revenue of $47.95B (+39%) and non-GAAP EPS of $19.29.
And the stock has fallen anyway. AMAT closed at $350.25 on November 16, 2026 — down 20.7% from the $441.85 September 1 close at which this report was last published, and 52.6% below the June 30 intraday all-time high of $739.67. The path was not a straight line: the shares climbed to $542.28 by October 5, lost 24% in nine sessions starting with the October 13 investor day (Applied set 2030 margin and market-share targets there but declined to forecast industry spending), fell 13.6% on November 5, and slid 8.1% from the November 12 close through November 16 despite the record quarter. Earnings estimates rose while the price fell, so the valuation has come down hard: about 18.2x fiscal 2027 consensus EPS, against 25.1x next-twelve-months earnings in the last edition. Applied is still up 36% in 2026 (from the $256.99 close on December 31, 2025), at a ~$278.0B market capitalization.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Broadest process portfolio Large global installed base $6.9B recurring services Ion implant ~70% share PVD & CMP leadership Co-development with foundries Integrated multi-step platforms 23,500+ patents
Applied's moat is a breadth-plus-integration moat rather than a single-product monopoly. No customer can build a leading-edge fab without Applied's deposition, CMP and implant tools, and once a tool is qualified into a process flow the switching cost is enormous: requalifying a deposition step at a new vendor means months of yield learning on a node where a single point of yield can be worth hundreds of millions of dollars. That qualification lock-in then feeds the recurring AGS business, where roughly two-thirds of $6.9B in revenue recurs regardless of new-tool orders. Breadth also lets Applied sell integrated solutions that competitors structurally cannot — moving a wafer between deposition, treatment and metrology under a single vacuum, which becomes more valuable as gate-all-around and backside power delivery add process steps. Management estimates Applied captures more than half the equipment value in the 15 semiconductor-process steps required to build an HBM stack. Finally, scale matters: Applied spent $4.18B on research, development and engineering in fiscal 2026, more than the total revenue of most single-product equipment vendors, and by October 2026 it had announced 15 partnerships with chipmakers and others at its EPIC research center in Silicon Valley, so it is designing tools for chips that are years from production. At the investor day Applied targeted a gain of more than 2 percentage points of the equipment market between 2025 and 2030.
Key Vulnerabilities

1. China concentration and export controls. China was 25% of fiscal Q4 2026 revenue ($2.64B) and 27% of the full year ($9.33B), down from 30% in fiscal 2025 and 37% in fiscal 2024, and on the Q4 call Hill said China "will probably be less than 25% of the business" in 2027. In an 8-K filed October 2, 2025, Applied said the new US "affiliates rule", which extends export controls to companies at least 50% owned by blacklisted ones, would cut about $110M from fiscal Q4 2025 revenue and about $600M from fiscal 2026. The rule was then suspended from November 10, 2025, and on November 5, 2026 the US Commerce Department extended the suspension to January 10, 2027, so it is a pending risk rather than a running cost. Equipment for logic below 14nm and DRAM below 18nm effectively cannot be licensed to Chinese customers. Worse, restrictions are simultaneously subsidising domestic Chinese competitors: Naura and AMEC are taking mature-node deposition and etch share inside China, permanently shrinking Applied's serviceable market there.

2. Value migration toward lithography. As chip features shrink, a growing share of each extra equipment dollar flows to ASML's High-NA EUV lithography rather than to deposition and etch. ASML, the largest equipment maker by revenue, holds about 30% of WFE spending, while Applied's share has drifted from the low 20s toward ~19%; Applied's 2030 plan to win back more than 2 points is a target, not a result. High-NA also enables single-patterning where multi-patterning previously required extra deposition/etch cycles — a direct substitution risk for Applied's core tools at the leading edge.

3. Customer concentration and cycle timing. A handful of buyers — TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, Micron and SMIC — account for the large majority of Semiconductor Systems revenue, and each can defer a fab by two quarters with a single board decision. Long order books cut both ways: a large part of the fiscal 2027–28 consensus rests on orders that customers can cancel or push out if AI computing demand cools. Cash flow has so far backed the story. After FCF fell to $210M in Q2 FY26 as inventory and capital spending built ahead of the ramp, Applied generated $2.33B in Q3 and a record $2.62B in Q4, for $6.20B in fiscal 2026 (up from $5.70B), though inventories still rose to $6.89B from $5.92B a year earlier. The October investor day added a different kind of uncertainty: Applied declined to put a number on industry spending, and the shares fell 6.7% that day.

4. Regulatory and compliance overhang. Applied paid a $252.5M penalty to the Bureau of Industry and Security in February 2026 (booked as a $253M legal-settlement charge in fiscal 2026) to settle the agency's charges that 56 shipments of chipmaking equipment to China's SMIC between 2020 and 2022, routed through Applied's Korean subsidiaries, broke US export rules. The settlement carries a three-year suspended denial order, which BIS can activate to cut off Applied's own export privileges if the company breaks the settlement's terms, and requires two internal audits of its export compliance. The DOJ and SEC closed their parallel subpoena-driven probes without enforcement action, but the suspended order is a low-probability, very-high-severity tail risk for a company that ships from US soil into Asia.

Notable One-Off Items
Applied's GAAP results swing with the value of its stakes in other companies, and the gap between GAAP and non-GAAP runs in both directions. Over fiscal 2026 the swings nearly cancelled (GAAP EPS $12.62 against non-GAAP $12.89), but single quarters moved by up to 76 cents.

Q1 FY26 (Jan 2026): a $253M charge for the legal settlement with the US Department of Commerce over export-controls compliance, offset by a $484M unrealized gain on strategic investments. The net effect flattered GAAP EPS ($2.54, +75% YoY) while non-GAAP EPS was flat at $2.38 — a 16-cent gap in the "good" direction.

Q2 FY26 (Apr 2026): GAAP EPS of $3.51 versus non-GAAP $2.86, a 65-cent gap, again driven mainly by investment gains rather than operations.

Q3 FY26 (Jul 2026): the polarity flipped. GAAP EPS of $3.17 came in below non-GAAP $3.50 — the normal, healthier relationship — with GAAP gross margin of 50.3% versus non-GAAP 50.4% and GAAP operating margin of 33.7% versus 34.0%. The gap was 33 cents.

Q4 FY26 (Oct 2026): GAAP EPS of $3.40 against non-GAAP $4.16, a 76-cent gap driven mainly by a $653M unrealized loss on strategic investments. For the full year those holdings produced a net unrealized gain of $296M. On October 26 Applied agreed to raise its stake in BE Semiconductor Industries (Besi), a Dutch maker of chip-packaging and hybrid-bonding tools, to about 20% through an accelerated share purchase, pending US antitrust clearance; it does not intend to go beyond that or seek a Besi board seat.

FY2025: a $181M restructuring charge announced in Q4 (roughly 4% of the workforce), a $659M tax benefit from remeasuring deferred tax assets under Singapore incentives, and a $403M valuation allowance against corporate alternative minimum tax credits. These three tax and restructuring items largely offset but individually distort quarterly comparisons.

Ongoing, not one-off: Applied estimated the affiliates rule would cut about $110M from fiscal Q4 2025 revenue and about $600M from fiscal 2026; the rule was suspended on November 10, 2025, and the suspension now runs to January 10, 2027. The suspended BIS denial order carries no accounting charge.

As Investing.com reported, Applied stopped short of a specific forecast for industry equipment spending; according to the same report, Dickerson named factory space, supply chains and the financing of energy infrastructure as the limits on how fast demand for computing turns into equipment orders. The shares fell 6.7% that day and 24% over nine sessions, from $497.53 on October 12 to $377.24 on October 23.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Applied sells the widest range of tools into an equipment market its CEO expects to grow more than 30% in 2027, after Applied's own Semiconductor Systems business grows more than 40% in calendar 2026. The growth is in exactly the places Applied is strongest: DRAM rose to 33% of systems revenue in fiscal Q4, against 26% for fiscal 2025, and advanced packaging revenue is set to rise around 90% this year. Fiscal 2026 brought record revenue of $34.45B, a record Q4 up 53%, non-GAAP EPS up 37%, $6.20B of free cash flow, a 32.5% return on invested capital and net cash, and Q1 FY27 is guided to $11.1B, up 58%. Management has set 2030 targets of about 54% gross margin and 40% operating margin, and services alone above $12B. Yet the stock is 21% cheaper than at the last edition. The valuation objection has largely been paid off by the market itself: forward P/E has fallen from 25.1x next-twelve-months earnings at the last edition to about 18.2x fiscal 2027 estimates, PEG to about 0.47, and even after the post-print cuts the average analyst target sits 60% above the price.

Bear Case

The bear case has changed shape, not disappeared. The valuation leg is weak now — 18.2x fiscal 2027 estimates is not demanding. But the drawdown is itself the argument: the market has marked this business down 53% from its high, through two record quarters and a raised outlook, because it doubts the AI spending funding the equipment cycle will last. Applied's order book is only as good as the cloud-company and memory-maker board decisions behind it, and at the investor day Applied would not put a number on industry spending. China, at 25% of Q4 revenue and guided below 25% for 2027, is shrinking by regulatory design while Naura and AMEC take domestic share. The extra equipment dollar is still drifting toward ASML's High-NA EUV, which replaces some of the repeated deposition and etch steps Applied sells. And estimates are high: consensus has fiscal 2027 revenue up 39% and EPS up about 50%, so a pause in 2027 spending would leave a long way for estimates to fall before they meet the price.

Top 3 Risks
01
Cycle-timing risk. Chipmaking equipment is a capacity business, and capacity cycles end. Applied trades at 18.2x fiscal 2027 estimates that assume revenue grows 39%; the long order book management presents as visibility is equally a stack of cancellable orders. With a beta of 1.63, the drawdown on a single disappointing spending datapoint can be severe — and the last five months are the proof, not the hypothetical: the stock has fallen 53% from its June high while reporting record results, including 24% in nine sessions around the investor day and 8% after the November print. The lower price has cut the multiple, but it has not removed the cycle risk.
02
China: a quarter of revenue under active political control. China was 25% of Q4 FY26 sales and 27% of the year, down from 37% in fiscal 2024, and Hill expects it below 25% in 2027. Rules can tighten unilaterally and without notice — Applied estimated the affiliates rule would cut about $110M from fiscal Q4 2025 revenue and about $600M from fiscal 2026 before it was suspended, and the suspension now runs only to January 10, 2027. The suspended BIS denial order from the $252.5M February 2026 settlement is a low-probability but company-altering tail: breaking the settlement's terms within three years could restrict Applied's own export privileges.
03
Value migration to lithography and share erosion. Applied's WFE share has drifted from the low-20s to roughly 19% as ASML's EUV franchise absorbs a larger slice of leading-edge budgets; Applied's 2030 target of regaining more than 2 points is still only a target. High-NA EUV also structurally reduces multi-patterning steps, the source of much of Applied's deposition and etch content. Separately, Tokyo Electron and ASM International continue to press in ALD, and Chinese domestic vendors are taking mature-node share that Applied is barred from defending.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
The Memory Supercycle Is the Whole Story
EMERGING TAILWIND
For three years AI drove semiconductor content without driving semiconductor capacity — which is why WFE was roughly flat through 2024–25 even as NVIDIA's revenue exploded. That broke in late 2025, when memory chips ran short and memory makers started building again. In June 2026 South Korea's government unveiled plans, with Samsung and SK Hynix, for about $518B of spending on four new memory fabs, plus about $52B for an HBM packaging hub (TechCrunch). Applied's own numbers show the shift: DRAM rose from 26% of fiscal 2025 systems revenue to 33% in fiscal Q4 2026, Semiconductor Systems is on track to grow more than 40% in calendar 2026, and Dickerson expects advanced packaging revenue to rise around 90% this year. On the Q4 call he said demand for advanced chips "remains much higher than supply" and that Applied expects equipment demand to rise more than 30% in 2027. The constraint Hill named is space: he said clean-room capacity "remains a gating factor" for how much equipment the industry can take.
China Export Controls: A Permanent, Not Cyclical, Haircut
ONGOING HEADWIND
China has fallen from 37% of Applied's revenue in fiscal 2024 to 25% in fiscal Q4 2026 (27% for the year, $9.33B), and Hill expects it below 25% in 2027. China revenue still grew in dollars in fiscal 2026, from $8.53B to $9.33B; the share falls because the rest of the business is growing much faster. Equipment for logic below 14nm and DRAM below 18nm is effectively unlicensable; the affiliates rule, which extends controls to companies at least 50% owned by blacklisted ones, was expected by Applied to cut about $110M from fiscal Q4 2025 revenue and about $600M from fiscal 2026 before it was suspended; the suspension now runs to January 10, 2027. The second-order damage is worse than the first: by removing Applied from the advanced-node contest inside China, the controls have handed Naura and AMEC a protected domestic market in which to build mature-node deposition and etch businesses that will eventually compete abroad. Applied's $252.5M BIS settlement in February 2026 — with a three-year suspended denial order attached — is a reminder that the compliance cost is not just lost revenue. Investors should treat China as a source of revenue that shrinks by design, not a temporary dip.
Leading-Edge Transitions: Where Bull and Bear Actually Diverge
THE DECIDING VARIABLE
The industry is executing three simultaneous architectural transitions — gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistors, backside power delivery, and 3D chiplet/HBM stacking — and each adds process steps rather than removing them. That is the bull case in one sentence: Applied's Viva, Sym3 Z Magnum and Spectra tools are already qualified at multiple leading foundries, and management estimates Applied captures more than half the equipment value across the 15 equipment-driven steps in an HBM stack. The bear rebuttal is that each extra dollar is moving the other way. High-NA EUV lets a foundry replace multi-patterning sequences — several deposition, etch and CMP passes — with a single lithography exposure. Resolution: if the extra steps from GAA, backside power and packaging outrun what lithography removes, Applied's ~19% WFE share stabilises or grows, as its 2030 target of more than 2 extra points assumes, and 18.2x fiscal 2027 estimates looks undemanding. If it does not, Applied is a high-quality but volatile cyclical business bought near a peak in spending. The fall since June did not answer this question; it moved the price from pricing the bull case to pricing something closer to a cautious middle. The fiscal Q4 print supplied a 2027 view (equipment demand up more than 30%) but not a dollar figure, and progress toward the 2030 share target will only show over several years.
