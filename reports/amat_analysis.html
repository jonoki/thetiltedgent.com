01 Company Overview
Applied Materials is the world's largest supplier of materials engineering equipment for semiconductor manufacturing — the deposition, etch, chemical mechanical planarization (CMP, a polishing step that flattens wafer surfaces between layers), ion implantation, thermal processing, and process-control tools that physically build a transistor layer by layer. It is the only vendor with meaningful share across essentially every non-lithography step, which gives it roughly 19–20% of global wafer fab equipment (WFE) spending — second overall only to ASML, whose extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography monopoly makes it #1 by revenue.
Fiscal 2025 (ended October 26, 2025) revenue was $28.37B, up 4% YoY, with GAAP EPS of $8.66 and non-GAAP EPS of $9.42 on net income of $7.00B. Growth then inflected hard: Q2 FY26 (April quarter) delivered a record $7.91B, +11% YoY, and management guided fiscal Q3 to $8.95B ±$500M — roughly +23% YoY — after raising its calendar-2026 semiconductor equipment growth outlook from "more than 20%" to "more than 30%". The stock has re-rated violently on that reset: up ~113% year-to-date in 2026 and roughly 3.4x off its September 2025 lows, lifting Applied to a ~$435B market capitalization and roughly the 32nd most valuable listed company in the world.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Broadest process portfolio ~50,000 tool installed base $6.4B services annuity Ion implant ~70% share PVD & CMP leadership Co-development with foundries Integrated multi-step platforms ~20,000 patents
Applied's moat is a breadth-plus-integration moat rather than a single-product monopoly. No customer can build a leading-edge fab without Applied's deposition, CMP and implant tools, and once a tool is qualified into a process flow the switching cost is enormous: requalifying a deposition step at a new vendor means months of yield learning on a node where a single point of yield can be worth hundreds of millions of dollars. That qualification lock-in then compounds into the AGS annuity, where roughly two-thirds of $6.4B in revenue recurs regardless of new-tool orders. Breadth also lets Applied sell integrated solutions that competitors structurally cannot — moving a wafer between deposition, treatment and metrology under a single vacuum, which becomes more valuable as gate-all-around and backside power delivery add process steps. Management estimates Applied captures more than half the equipment value in the 15 semiconductor-process steps required to build an HBM stack. Finally, scale matters: Applied's R&D budget (~$3.5B/yr) is larger than the total revenue of most single-product equipment vendors, and its co-development labs with TSMC, Samsung, Intel and SK Hynix mean it is designing tools for nodes that are still five years from production.
Key Vulnerabilities

1. China concentration and export controls. China was 27% of Q2 FY26 revenue (and 30%, or $2.1B, in Q1 FY26), down from close to 40% in 2023. Applied has quantified roughly $710M of revenue impact from tightened US restrictions — including the "affiliate rule" that extends controls to subsidiaries of listed entities — and previously guided to a ~$600M FY2026 hit. Equipment for logic below 14nm and DRAM below 18nm effectively cannot be licensed to Chinese customers. Worse, restrictions are simultaneously subsidising domestic Chinese competitors: Naura and AMEC are taking mature-node deposition and etch share inside China, permanently shrinking Applied's serviceable market there.

2. Value migration toward lithography. As nodes shrink, an increasing share of incremental WFE dollars flows to ASML's High-NA EUV rather than to deposition and etch. ASML's 2026 revenue (~€44B) will be roughly 1.7x Applied's, and ASML's ~30% WFE share has expanded while Applied's has drifted from the low-20s toward ~19%. High-NA also enables single-patterning where multi-patterning previously required extra deposition/etch cycles — a direct substitution risk for Applied's core tools at the leading edge.

3. Customer concentration and cycle timing. A handful of buyers — TSMC, Samsung, Intel, SK Hynix, Micron and SMIC — account for the large majority of Semiconductor Systems revenue, and each can defer a fab by two quarters with a single board decision. Applied's own order book now stretches ~8 quarters, which management frames as visibility but which equally means a large portion of the FY27–FY28 consensus is already committed and therefore vulnerable to cancellation or push-out if AI compute demand normalises. Note that Q2 FY26 FCF collapsed 80% YoY to $210M as inventory and capex built ahead of the ramp — the classic pre-peak working-capital signature.

4. Regulatory and compliance overhang. Applied paid a $252.5M penalty to the Bureau of Industry and Security in February 2026 for illegally exporting semiconductor manufacturing equipment, accompanied by a suspended denial order that would restrict Applied's own export privileges if compliance conditions are breached; it is waived only after three years of clean conduct. The DOJ and SEC closed their parallel subpoena-driven probes without enforcement action, but the suspended order is a low-probability, very-high-severity tail risk for a company that ships from US soil into Asia.

Notable One-Off Items
Applied's recent GAAP results are unusually noisy, and the gap between GAAP and non-GAAP runs in both directions — investors should not take TTM GAAP EPS of $10.63 at face value.

Q1 FY26 (Jan 2026): a $253M charge for the legal settlement with the US Department of Commerce over export-controls compliance, offset by a $484M unrealized gain on strategic investments. The net effect flattered GAAP EPS ($2.54, +75% YoY) while non-GAAP EPS was flat at $2.38 — a 68-cent divergence in the "good" direction.

Q2 FY26 (Apr 2026): GAAP EPS of $3.51 versus non-GAAP $2.86, again driven mainly by investment gains rather than operations. Roughly 23% of reported GAAP earnings in the last two quarters is non-operating.

FY2025: a $181M restructuring charge announced in Q4 (roughly 4% of the workforce), a $659M tax benefit from remeasuring deferred tax assets under Singapore incentives, and a $403M valuation allowance against corporate alternative minimum tax credits. These three tax and restructuring items largely offset but individually distort quarterly comparisons.

Ongoing, not one-off: the ~$710M export-control revenue impact and the $110M of Q4 FY25 product that went unshipped under the affiliate rule are best treated as a permanent reduction in the addressable China market, not a timing item. The suspended BIS denial order carries no accounting charge but is a material contingent liability disclosed in the filings.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Applied is the broadest-exposure toll booth on a WFE market that has been revised from $140B to the low $150B range for 2026 and is guided higher again for 2027, with DRAM the fastest-growing segment — precisely where Applied's mix has swung (34% of systems revenue). HBM economics multiply the effect: each stack consumes 3–4x the DRAM wafer starts per bit, requires 19 process steps of which 15 are equipment-driven, and Applied captures more than half that value. Order visibility has stretched from 3–6 months to eight quarters, advanced packaging is guided up >50%, gross margin just hit a 25-year high of 50.0%, and the company converts at 33% ROIC with a net-cash balance sheet. At 1.29x PEG the stock is not expensive against its own growth if this is a multi-year capacity cycle rather than a spike.

Bear Case

At 35.8x forward earnings against a 5-year average near 21x, the market is paying a structural-growth multiple for a business that has never escaped its cycle. The tell is in the cash flow: Q2 FY26 free cash flow fell 80% YoY to $210M as inventory and capex built ahead of the ramp — the classic late-cycle working-capital signature. China, still 27% of revenue, is shrinking by regulatory design while Naura and AMEC take domestic share, and ~$710M of revenue is already gone. Meanwhile the incremental WFE dollar is migrating toward ASML's High-NA EUV, which can substitute litho for the multi-patterning deposition and etch steps Applied sells. A single 2027 WFE guide below the low-$150B run-rate would strip 30–40% from the multiple, and one Buy-rated analyst (RBC) already carries a target below the current price.

Top 3 Risks
01
Cycle-timing risk on a peak multiple. Semicap equipment is a capacity business, and capacity cycles end. Applied is trading at 35.8x forward earnings on the assumption that the memory/AI buildout runs through at least 2028; the eight-quarter backlog that management presents as visibility is equally a concentration of cancellable orders. With a beta of 1.62 and options pricing a ±9.9% earnings move, the drawdown potential on a single disappointing WFE datapoint is severe — the stock already fell 31% from its June high on no news at all.
02
China: a quarter of revenue under active political control. China was 27% of Q2 FY26 sales, down from ~40% in 2023, with ~$710M of quantified revenue impact and a flat-to-slightly-higher outlook for calendar 2026. Rules can tighten unilaterally and without notice — the affiliate rule alone stranded $110M of shipments in one quarter. The suspended BIS denial order from the $252.5M February 2026 settlement is a low-probability but company-altering tail: a compliance breach within three years could restrict Applied's own export privileges.

  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
The Memory Supercycle Is the Whole Story
EMERGING TAILWIND
For three years AI drove semiconductor content without driving semiconductor capacity — which is why WFE was roughly flat through 2024–25 even as NVIDIA's revenue exploded. That broke in late 2025. DRAM is in genuine physical shortage, with supply constrained through 2026 and into H1 2027 and price relief not expected until late 2027 at the earliest. The response has been the largest memory capex commitment in the industry's history: Samsung and SK Hynix have earmarked ~$518B for four new memory fabs plus $52B for a dedicated HBM packaging hub, and Micron has guided every quarter of fiscal 2027 to above $10B of capex. Lam has already raised calendar-2026 WFE from $140B to the low $150B range and expects DRAM to be the fastest-growing segment again in 2027. For Applied this is close to a best-case mix: DRAM jumped from 26% of FY25 systems revenue to 34% in Q1 FY26, HBM requires 3–4x the wafer starts per delivered bit, and advanced packaging — where Applied guides to >50% growth in 2026 — is the fastest-growing sub-segment of all.
China Export Controls: A Permanent, Not Cyclical, Haircut
ONGOING HEADWIND
China has fallen from close to 40% of Applied's revenue in 2023 to 27% in Q2 FY26, and the decline is policy-driven rather than demand-driven. Equipment for logic below 14nm and DRAM below 18nm is effectively unlicensable; the affiliate rule extended controls to subsidiaries of listed entities and stranded $110M of shipments in a single quarter; Applied has quantified roughly $710M of revenue impact and guided China plus global ICAPS to flat-to-slightly-higher for calendar 2026. The second-order damage is worse than the first: by removing Applied from the advanced-node contest inside China, the controls have handed Naura and AMEC a protected domestic market in which to build mature-node deposition and etch businesses that will eventually compete abroad. Applied's $252.5M BIS settlement in February 2026 — with a three-year suspended denial order attached — is a reminder that the compliance cost is not just lost revenue. Investors should treat the China contribution as a structurally shrinking annuity, not a cyclical trough.
Leading-Edge Transitions: Where Bull and Bear Actually Diverge
THE DECIDING VARIABLE
The industry is executing three simultaneous architectural transitions — gate-all-around (GAA) nanosheet transistors, backside power delivery, and 3D chiplet/HBM stacking — and each adds process steps rather than removing them. That is the bull case in one sentence: Applied's Viva, Sym3 Z Magnum and Spectra tools are already qualified at multiple leading foundries, and management estimates Applied captures more than half the equipment value across the 15 equipment-driven steps in an HBM stack. The bear rebuttal is that the marginal dollar is moving the other way. High-NA EUV lets a foundry replace multi-patterning sequences — several deposition, etch and CMP passes — with a single litho exposure, and ASML has guided to 30% low-NA EUV and DUV immersion capacity expansion for 2027 on top of ~€44B of 2026 revenue that is already 1.7x Applied's. Resolution: if step-count inflation from GAA, backside power and packaging outruns litho substitution, Applied's ~19% WFE share stabilises or grows and the current 35.8x forward multiple is defensible on a rising base. If it does not, Applied is a high-quality, high-beta, mid-teens-growth business trading at twice its historical multiple into a capacity peak. The November FY26 print and first FY27 WFE framing is where that question starts getting answered.
