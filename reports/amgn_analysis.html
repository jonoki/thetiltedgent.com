01 Company Overview

Amgen is one of the original biotechnology companies — founded in 1980, it commercialised some of the first blockbuster recombinant proteins (Epogen, Neupogen) and today sells roughly two dozen medicines across cardiovascular, bone health, inflammation, oncology and rare disease, plus a large portfolio of biosimilars. Full-year 2025 revenue was $36.75B, up +9.95% YoY; TTM revenue through Q3 2026 is $38.90B (+8.2%). Q3 2026, reported after the close on November 3, 2026, delivered revenue of $10.37B (+8%) and non-GAAP EPS of $6.45 against a $5.74 Nasdaq consensus, and management raised full-year guidance to $38.9–39.7B of revenue (from $38.2–39.4B) and $22.90–23.70 of non-GAAP EPS (from $22.30–23.50).

Two things define this stock, and they pull in opposite directions. The first is MariTide (maridebart cafraglutide), a once-monthly injectable obesity candidate now in Phase 3, a challenger to Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk and by a wide margin the largest swing factor in the valuation; Reuters reports that the first Phase 3 results are expected next year. The second is the erosion of the legacy base: Prolia and Xgeva fell 45% and 47% in Q3 2026 as biosimilars launched globally, taking the pair to $920M from $1.68B a year earlier. Layered on top is a balance sheet still carrying the $27.8B Horizon Therapeutics acquisition, with $54.0B of debt at September 30, and an IRS transfer-pricing dispute in which notices for 2010–2015 seek about $8.7B of additional tax plus $2.0B of penalties, before interest. AMGN closed at $460.42 on November 6, 2026, up +45.9% over twelve months and +40.7% year-to-date, making it the 45th-largest company in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation.

MariTide is a peptide–antibody conjugate combining GLP-1 receptor agonism with GIP receptor antagonism, mechanistically the opposite of Lilly's tirzepatide on the GIP arm and a genuine scientific bet. Amgen says its long-acting design supports starting with monthly dosing and staying on the drug with as few as four or six doses a year, versus weekly for Wegovy and Zepbound. That is the commercial thesis: fewer injections, better real-world persistence (in a JAMA Network Open study of U.S. patients, 64.8% of those without type 2 diabetes stopped within a year), and potentially easier supply economics.

Phase 2 data showed weight loss in the region of ~20% at 52 weeks at the top dose in patients without diabetes, with no clear plateau, but with gastrointestinal tolerability (nausea and vomiting, concentrated on first dose) as the clear weakness and lower weight loss in the type-2-diabetes cohort. The Phase 3 MARITIME programme now spans obesity with and without diabetes, heart failure, switching from weekly drugs, cardiovascular outcomes and sleep apnoea, with the last two fully enrolled; two more diabetes studies start in Q4 2026. Reuters reports that first Phase 3 results are expected next year; Fierce Biotech, citing ClinicalTrials.gov, gave the start of 2027 for MARITIME-1's primary readout. On the Q3 call, Jay Bradner said: "We will have more to share around the efficacy and tolerability of MariTide at the time that these studies read out."

Amgen's moat is industrial before it is intellectual. Large-molecule manufacturing is genuinely hard — living cell lines, multi-year facility qualification, regulator-approved processes that cannot be changed casually — and Amgen has been doing it for more than four decades, across sites in Thousand Oaks, Juncos (Puerto Rico), West Greenwich (Rhode Island), New Albany (Ohio), Holly Springs (North Carolina) and Singapore. That capability is what lets the company simultaneously defend its own biologics and profitably attack everyone else's with biosimilars — a position no pure-play originator or pure-play biosimilar house can occupy. Layered on top are the ordinary pharmaceutical moats: patents on the molecules and their formulations, periods of regulatory exclusivity (including for rare-disease drugs), and the scale to contract directly with the three PBMs that process nearly 80% of U.S. prescriptions (FTC, 2023 data). The deCODE Genetics subsidiary in Iceland — population-scale human genetic data — is a real, if slow-building, discovery advantage; MariTide's GIP-antagonist rationale traces partly to human genetics rather than mouse models.

The honest qualification is that none of this moat protects revenue after a patent expires. Amgen's history is one of enormous franchises that eventually shrank sharply — Epogen, Neupogen, and now Prolia — replaced by new ones bought or discovered just in time. The moat determines how profitably the company operates, not whether any given product survives.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The denosumab cliff is here. Prolia and Xgeva together were $6.5B in 2025, about 18% of revenue, and in Q3 2026 they fell to $920M, down 45% from a year earlier as multiple biosimilars launched globally. History is unkind: Humira's global sales fell about 79% from 2022 to 2025. Denosumab is given in the clinic and the practice buys and bills for it, which slowed the first year's decline (Prolia still grew 1% in 2025), but that cushion is now going, and 2027 is the year analysts are watching.

2. MariTide is an all-or-nothing bet inside a $249B company. Much of the +46% twelve-month move is what investors will pay for a chance at a large obesity drug, not delivered earnings. If MARITIME Phase 3 confirms ~20% weight loss with manageable tolerability, the monthly-dosing differentiation is worth many billions of peak revenue. If tolerability drives discontinuation rates materially above Lilly's and Novo's, or if efficacy lands closer to 15%, that value could go close to zero and the stock could trade back toward the low-teens multiple of forward earnings the legacy business alone would command. There is very little middle ground in how the market will price the readout.

3. Leverage restricts the response. $54.0B of total debt against $14.35B of shareholders' equity, a debt-to-equity ratio near 3.8×, is a direct legacy of funding Horizon with borrowings. It is serviceable on $12.0B of operating cash flow, and management has cut debt by roughly $10.6B since 2023, but it constrains Amgen's ability to do the large acquisition that has historically been its answer to a patent cliff. Amgen made no share repurchases in Q3 2026 and guides to no more than $3.0B this year. The company is paying down debt just as its patents expire.

4. Policy exposure on two fronts. Enbrel's Medicare price took effect in January 2026, and Otezla's negotiated price of $1,650 per 30-day supply (against a $4,722 list price, per a Mondaq analysis of the CMS figures) takes effect on January 1, 2027. On most-favored-nation pricing, Amgen said in December 2025 that it is taking actions that satisfy the administration's July 2025 letter, according to its 2025 Form 10-K. Section 232 tariffs on imported patented drugs, proclaimed in April 2026, are the other exposure, though biosimilars are at 0% for now. Amgen's large Puerto Rico manufacturing base is a partial hedge on tariffs and, at the same time, the origin of its tax dispute.

5. Tavneos and a data breach. The FDA's drug centre proposed on April 27, 2026 to withdraw approval of Tavneos, saying it has not been shown to be effective, alleging that pivotal trial results were manipulated and citing cases of fatal liver injury; the New England Journal of Medicine retracted the pivotal study on June 29, 2026. Amgen requested a hearing on June 1, submitted supporting materials on July 23, and says the drug has a favorable benefit-risk profile. Tavneos was $105M in Q3, about 1% of revenue, so the commercial stake is small and the reputational one is larger. Separately, Amgen disclosed on July 31, 2026 that data held with third-party cloud providers, including proprietary data and patient health information, had been taken; it says the incident is not reasonably likely to have a material impact on its financial condition or results.

Notable One-Off Items

AMGN's GAAP earnings are a poor guide to the underlying business, and three distortions account for most of the gap.

Acquisition amortisation. The $27.8B Horizon deal (closed October 2023) added a large block of acquired intangible assets. Amgen's amortisation of finite-lived intangibles was $2.7B in the first nine months of 2026, against $3.4B in the same period of 2025 (10-Q, Note 2), which is a large part of why GAAP EPS of $17.24 sits below non-GAAP EPS of $23.18. It is a genuine economic cost (Amgen paid for those assets) but not a cash expense in the current period. FY2025 GAAP EPS rose 88%, which Amgen attributes to higher revenues and net unrealized gains on its stake in BeOne Medicines, partly offset by higher operating expenses that included $1.2B of Otezla impairment charges.

Gains on equity stakes. Q3 2026 GAAP results included $1.47B of net gains from equity investments, and Amgen's GAAP guidance assumes $1.70 a share of such gains for the year. Non-GAAP EPS strips them out.

The IRS transfer-pricing dispute. The IRS asserts that Amgen understated U.S. taxable income by allocating too much profit to its Puerto Rico manufacturing operations. Notices of deficiency cover 2010–2012 (about $3.6B of additional federal tax, plus interest) and 2013–2015 (about $5.1B of additional tax, plus interest, and about $2.0B of penalties): $10.7B together before interest, or roughly 4% of market capitalisation. The IRS has also proposed similar adjustments for 2016–2018, in a draft received in April 2026 and reissued in May and July, and asserted penalties for those years in a September 2026 draft. Amgen disagrees and intends to contest them, and says it continues to believe its accrual for income tax liabilities is appropriate (Q3 2026 Form 10-Q, Note 4).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Amgen has a monthly obesity candidate in Phase 3 aimed at a market forecasters put at about $100B by 2030, and MariTide's dosing attacks the incumbents' greatest weakness: in a JAMA Network Open study, 64.8% of patients without type 2 diabetes stopped within a year. Meanwhile the base keeps growing even as denosumab shrinks: revenue rose 9.95% in FY2025 and 8% in Q3 2026 with Prolia down 45%, on Repatha +37%, Evenity +39%, Uplizna +191% and Imdelltra +128%. ROIC is 20.5%, free cash flow is $9.6B, and guidance went up again. At 19.8× 2026 guided earnings with a 2.2% dividend and 0.42 beta, you pay a pharma multiple for a defensive cash generator and get the obesity programme on top.

Bear Case

You are paying 26.7× trailing earnings and a 3.4 PEG for a business analysts forecast growing EPS 6–8% a year; the premium rests on a 2027 Phase 3 readout whose Phase 2 predecessor was part of a 22% slide from the August 2024 high to December. Denosumab, $6.5B in 2025, fell 45% in Q3, with Prolia missing forecasts; Enbrel and Otezla are under Medicare price pressure and Merck's pill now competes with Repatha. $54.0B of debt (3.8× equity) constrains the acquisition response. The stock trades 9% above the average target with 18 Holds and 3 Sells, a Tax Court decision on an IRS claim of $10.7B before interest could come within months, and monthly RSI at 77.6 is in the upper part of its five-year range. The downside now looks larger than the upside.

MariTide Phase 3 failure or an underwhelming profile. The MARITIME readouts in 2027 are close to all-or-nothing for the valuation. Efficacy materially below ~20%, or discontinuation rates that negate the monthly-dosing convenience argument, would remove the hope of a large obesity drug that helped drive a 46% twelve-month move. Layer on IRS notices seeking $8.7B of tax and $2.0B of penalties before interest, with a Tax Court decision possible from late 2026, and the risk of a single large hit to the balance sheet is not captured by a 0.42 beta.
  Industry Commentary
The three macro dynamics that matter most for Amgen specifically, right now.
The Economics of Entering Obesity Third
EMERGING TAILWIND
Forecasts for this market have come down: Reuters reported in February 2026 that 2030 estimates had fallen about 30% to roughly $100B, with Goldman Sachs at $105B, and that the long-held $150B target had moved out to 2035 for some analysts. Lilly leads with Zepbound and Mounjaro ($36.5B combined in 2025, up 122%, per its 10-K) and Novo Nordisk follows with Ozempic and Wegovy at DKK 206B, up 15% in kroner. The question for Amgen is not whether it can beat tirzepatide on weight loss, but whether third place in a $100B market is worth what the stock already assumes. Third place at 10% share is $10B of revenue at biologic margins, about a quarter of the $38.9B Amgen does today. The path runs through differentiation, not superiority: monthly (or less frequent) dosing versus weekly, aimed squarely at the one-year discontinuation rate of about 65% seen in patients without diabetes, plus the manufacturing capacity to actually supply it; Amgen has been adding U.S. manufacturing capacity in Ohio and North Carolina. The counter-argument is equally concrete: by 2028 the market will contain oral semaglutide, orforglipron, retatrutide, CagriSema and a queue of Chinese-originated assets, payers will have consolidated their approved-drug lists to two or three agents, and the last company in may find the price it can charge has collapsed. Entering third means entering into a price war you did not start.
Biosimilars Have Come for the Biologics Era — Including Amgen's Own
ONGOING HEADWIND
A wave of biologic patents is expiring through 2030, and Amgen sits on both sides of it. On the losing side: Prolia and Xgeva ($6.5B in 2025) are now losing nearly half their sales year over year, Enbrel's net price is falling on Medicare price setting, and Otezla faces generics from 2028. The precedents are brutal: Humira went from $21.2B in 2022 to $4.5B in 2025; Stelara fell 41% to $6.08B in 2025 as biosimilars arrived. The mitigating detail for denosumab was channel: it is given in the clinic and billed by the practice, not filled at a retail pharmacy, so switching required a practice to change its purchasing rather than a benefit manager to change a coverage tier. That is why 2025 erosion ran below the Humira template; Q3 2026's −45% shows the cushion going. On the winning side, Amgen's biosimilars summed to roughly $977M in Q3 2026, up 26%, on the manufacturing cost base to make the economics work at biosimilar price points. On balance this is still a headwind: the products being lost carry originator margins and the products being gained carry biosimilar margins.
U.S. Drug Pricing and Tax: Where the Policy Risk Actually Sits — Bull vs. Bear Framing
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Three policy regimes now operate at once. Inflation Reduction Act Medicare negotiation delivered its first maximum fair prices on January 1, 2026, with Enbrel among them and with Otezla's taking effect on January 1, 2027. On most-favored-nation pricing, Amgen's 2025 Form 10-K says it announced in December 2025 actions that satisfy the administration's July 2025 letter. And Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs, proclaimed in April 2026 at up to 100% on imported patented drugs and their ingredients, leave biosimilars at 0% for now and bear on plants outside the U.S. customs territory, such as Amgen's in Singapore; Puerto Rico is inside that territory.

The bull framing: Amgen is better positioned than most. Its fastest-growing assets — Repatha (already a volume-over-price strategy with heavily discounted net pricing), Tezspire, Imdelltra and most of the Horizon rare-disease portfolio — are either already low-priced, sold through specialty pharmacies, or too small for negotiation selection. Its large and expanding U.S. manufacturing base (Ohio, North Carolina, Rhode Island) is a direct tariff hedge, and its biosimilar unit is aligned with policymakers' stated goal of lowering drug costs.

The bear framing: the same Puerto Rico footprint that hedges tariffs is the subject of an IRS transfer-pricing case seeking about $8.7B of tax and $2.0B of penalties, before interest, in U.S. Tax Court, with a decision possible from late 2026. That is roughly 4% of market capitalisation. More broadly, prices are now negotiated with the federal government as well as with payers, which is why large-cap pharma trades on low forward multiples compared with the Nasdaq-100 figure in Section 04. Amgen at 19.8× is not obviously mispriced against that backdrop; it is priced as a pharma company with a lottery ticket, and the lottery ticket is the whole argument.
