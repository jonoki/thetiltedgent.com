01 Company Overview

Amgen is the original biotechnology company — founded in 1980, it commercialised the first blockbuster recombinant proteins (Epogen, Neupogen) and today sells roughly two dozen medicines across cardiovascular, bone health, inflammation, oncology and rare disease, plus one of the largest biosimilar portfolios in the industry. Full-year 2025 revenue was $36.75B, up +9.95% YoY; TTM revenue through Q2 2026 is $38.10B (+9.1%). Q2 2026, reported August 4, 2026, delivered revenue of roughly $10.0B (+9.5%) and non-GAAP EPS of $6.29 against a $5.62 consensus, and management raised full-year revenue guidance to $38.2–39.4B.

Two things define this equity, and they pull in opposite directions. The first is MariTide (maridebart cafraglutide), a once-monthly injectable obesity candidate now in Phase 3 — the only credible third entrant into a market currently owned by Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk, and by a wide margin the largest swing factor in the valuation. The second is the erosion of the legacy base: Prolia and Xgeva lost U.S. exclusivity in 2025 and now face a queue of approved denosumab biosimilars, while Enbrel continues its long decline. Layered on top is a balance sheet still carrying the $27.8B Horizon Therapeutics acquisition — $57.3B of total debt — and an unresolved IRS transfer-pricing dispute with claimed deficiencies exceeding $15B before penalties. AMGN closed at $416.18 on August 12, 2026, up +46.5% over twelve months and +27.2% year-to-date, making it roughly the 52nd-largest U.S. public company and the 86th-largest globally by market capitalisation.

MariTide is a peptide–antibody conjugate combining GLP-1 receptor agonism with GIP receptor antagonism — mechanistically the opposite of Lilly's tirzepatide on the GIP arm, and a genuine scientific bet rather than a me-too. Its half-life supports once-monthly or less-frequent dosing versus weekly for Wegovy and Zepbound, which is the entire commercial thesis: fewer injections, better real-world persistence (roughly two-thirds of GLP-1 starters discontinue within a year), and potentially easier supply economics.

Phase 2 data showed weight loss in the region of ~20% at 52 weeks at the top dose in patients without diabetes, with no clear plateau — competitive with tirzepatide — but with gastrointestinal tolerability (nausea and vomiting, concentrated on first dose) as the clear weakness, and lower weight loss in the type-2-diabetes cohort. The Phase 3 MARITIME programme, running since 2025, is explicitly designed around dose-escalation schedules intended to blunt that first-dose effect. Topline Phase 3 readouts are expected in 2027. Efficacy percentages, dosing intervals and readout timing here are drawn from previously disclosed Phase 2 results and programme descriptions and should be verified against Amgen's latest clinical disclosures — this is the fastest-moving fact set in the file.

Amgen's moat is industrial before it is intellectual. Large-molecule manufacturing is genuinely hard — living cell lines, multi-year facility qualification, regulator-approved processes that cannot be changed casually — and Amgen has been doing it longer than anyone, across sites in Thousand Oaks, Juncos (Puerto Rico), West Greenwich (Rhode Island), New Albany (Ohio), Holly Springs (North Carolina) and Singapore. That capability is what lets the company simultaneously defend its own biologics and profitably attack everyone else's with biosimilars — a position no pure-play originator or pure-play biosimilar house can occupy. Layered on top are the ordinary pharmaceutical moats: composition-of-matter and formulation patents, orphan and regulatory exclusivities, and the scale to contract directly with the three PBMs that control roughly 80% of U.S. commercial lives. The deCODE Genetics subsidiary in Iceland — population-scale human genetic data — is a real, if slow-compounding, discovery advantage; MariTide's GIP-antagonist rationale traces partly to human genetics rather than mouse models.

The honest qualification is that none of this moat protects revenue after a patent expires. Amgen's history is one of enormous franchises that eventually went to zero — Epogen, Neupogen, and now Prolia — replaced by new ones bought or discovered just in time. The moat determines how profitably the company operates, not whether any given product survives.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The denosumab cliff is here, not coming. Prolia and Xgeva together were roughly $6.1B in 2025, about 17% of revenue, at franchise-leading margins. U.S. exclusivity lapsed in early 2025 and at least five biosimilars are approved. History is unkind: Humira fell ~79% in three years; Remicade, Rituxan and Herceptin each lost the majority of their revenue within three years of entry. Amgen's own guidance implicitly assumes a slower glide path because denosumab is administered in-office with a fragmented buy-and-bill channel, but the direction is not in doubt and 2027 is where the arithmetic bites hardest.

2. MariTide is a binary sitting inside a $225B market cap. A meaningful share of the +46% twelve-month move is obesity option value, not delivered earnings. If MARITIME Phase 3 confirms ~20% weight loss with manageable tolerability, the monthly-dosing differentiation is worth many billions of peak revenue. If tolerability drives discontinuation rates materially above Lilly's and Novo's — or if efficacy lands closer to 15% — the option is worth close to zero and the multiple re-rates back toward the low-teens forward earnings the legacy business alone deserves. There is very little middle ground in how the market will price the readout.

3. Leverage restricts the response. $57.3B of total debt against $11.7B of shareholders' equity — a debt-to-equity ratio near 4.9× — is the highest leverage in large-cap biotech, a direct legacy of funding Horizon with borrowings. It is serviceable on $12.5B of operating cash flow, and management has repaid roughly $7B since 2023, but it materially constrains Amgen's ability to do the large acquisition that has historically been its answer to a patent cliff. The company is deleveraging into the teeth of its own LOE wave.

4. Policy exposure on two fronts. Enbrel was selected in the first Medicare negotiation cycle with maximum fair prices effective January 2026; Otezla, Prolia and Xgeva are plausible candidates for later cycles. Separately, most-favored-nation reference pricing proposals and Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs remain unresolved. Amgen's large Puerto Rico manufacturing base is a partial hedge on tariffs — and, simultaneously, the origin of its tax dispute.

Notable One-Off Items

AMGN's GAAP earnings are among the least representative in large-cap pharma, and three distortions account for nearly all of it.

Horizon acquisition amortisation. The $27.8B deal (closed October 2023) created roughly $28B of identifiable intangibles and goodwill. Amortisation of those intangibles runs at approximately $4–5B per year through cost of sales and operating expense, which is why GAAP EPS of $16.09 sits so far below non-GAAP EPS near $23. It is a genuine economic cost (Amgen paid cash for those assets) but it is not a cash expense in the current period, and it steps down over time — the primary reason FY2025 GAAP net income nearly doubled from FY2024 without a comparable change in the business.

Acquired IPR&D and restructuring. FY2024 GAAP results were depressed by acquired IPR&D charges and by a multi-year cost-transformation programme running alongside Horizon integration — net income fell to $4.09B (12.2% margin) on revenue that grew 18.6%. Anyone comparing FY2024 to FY2025 or to TTM figures without adjusting for these items will draw the wrong conclusion about margin trajectory.

The IRS transfer-pricing dispute — a material unreserved contingency. The IRS has asserted that Amgen understated U.S. taxable income by allocating too much profit to its Puerto Rico manufacturing operations. Notices of deficiency cover 2010–2015 (approximately $10.7B of additional tax) and 2016–2018 (approximately $5.1B), with penalties sought on top — a headline exposure comfortably above $15B, or roughly 7% of market capitalisation. Amgen is contesting both in U.S. Tax Court, believes its positions are correct, and has not established a reserve for the claimed amounts; a related securities class action over the adequacy of the original disclosure has also been litigated. This is genuinely binary and genuinely large, it is not reflected in any consensus EPS number, and case timing has slipped repeatedly. Deficiency amounts and procedural status are as previously disclosed and should be verified against Amgen's most recent 10-Q contingencies footnote.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Amgen is the only company with a credible third entrant into a $130–150B obesity market, and MariTide's monthly dosing attacks the incumbents' single greatest weakness — roughly two-thirds of GLP-1 patients discontinue within a year. Meanwhile the "melting" base isn't melting: revenue grew 9.95% in FY2025 and 9.5% in Q2 2026, ROIC is 19.8%, free cash flow is $10.2B, and denosumab erosion has so far run below the bear case. At 17.8× forward earnings with a 2.4% dividend and 0.41 beta, you are paying a pharma multiple for a defensive cash generator and receiving the obesity option approximately free.

Bear Case

You are paying 25.9× trailing earnings and a 3.4 PEG for a business the sell-side models growing EPS at 5% — the entire premium is a 2027 Phase 3 readout whose Phase 2 predecessor cost the stock 22%. Roughly $6.1B of high-margin denosumab revenue is in structural decline with five biosimilars launched, Enbrel and Otezla are following, and $57.3B of debt (4.9× equity) constrains the acquisition response. The stock trades 12% above consensus target with 15 Holds and 3 Sells, an unreserved IRS claim above $15B sits off-model, and monthly RSI at ~76 is near the top of its five-year range. The asymmetry has flipped.

01
MariTide Phase 3 failure or an underwhelming profile. The MARITIME readouts in 2027 are close to binary for the multiple. Efficacy materially below ~20%, or discontinuation rates that negate the monthly-dosing convenience argument, would remove the option value that drove a 47% twelve-month move. The November 2024 Phase 2 reaction — −22% peak-to-trough — is the working template, and the stock is starting from a much higher base this time.
02
Denosumab erosion accelerating. Prolia and Xgeva were ~$6.1B in 2025 at the company's best margins, and at least five biosimilars are now approved. Erosion has been slower than feared through the first year, which is precisely why an acceleration would be a genuine surprise to the current price — consensus has begun extrapolating the gentle glide path. Enbrel (Medicare-negotiated since January 2026) and Otezla (generics from 2028) sit behind it.
03
Leverage plus the IRS contingency. $57.3B of gross debt and $43.3B net against $11.7B of book equity is the most levered balance sheet in large-cap biotech, and it is being repaid rather than redeployed — Amgen cannot easily buy its way out of a patent cliff the way it did in 2023. Layer on IRS deficiency notices exceeding $15B before penalties, unreserved and in Tax Court, and the tail risk on the balance sheet is not adequately reflected in a 0.41 beta.
  Industry Commentary
The three macro dynamics that matter most for Amgen specifically, right now.
The Economics of Entering Obesity Third
EMERGING TAILWIND
The incretin market did roughly $60B in 2026 and sell-side models put it at $130–150B by the early 2030s — larger than the entire oncology market was a decade ago. Lilly holds roughly 55% share with Zepbound and Mounjaro (~$34B combined in 2025, growing ~55%) and Novo roughly 40% (~$28B, growing ~10% as it loses share). The relevant question for Amgen is not whether it can beat tirzepatide on weight loss — it probably cannot — but whether third place in a $140B market is worth more than the $8–10B of enterprise value the option is currently carrying. Third place at 10% share is $14B of revenue at biologic margins, which for a company doing $38B today is transformative. The path there runs through differentiation, not superiority: monthly (or less frequent) dosing versus weekly, aimed squarely at the ~65% one-year discontinuation rate that is the industry's dirty secret, plus the manufacturing capacity to actually supply it — Amgen has been building U.S. fill-finish capacity in Ohio and North Carolina explicitly for this. The counter-argument is equally concrete: by 2028 the market will contain oral semaglutide, orforglipron, retatrutide, CagriSema and a queue of Chinese-originated assets, payers will have consolidated formularies to two or three agents, and the marginal entrant may find the price it can charge has collapsed. Entering third means entering into a price war you did not start.
Biosimilars Have Come for the Biologics Era — Including Amgen's Own
ONGOING HEADWIND
The 2023–2030 biologic patent cliff is the largest in pharmaceutical history and Amgen sits on both sides of it. On the losing side: Prolia and Xgeva (~$6.1B) lost U.S. exclusivity in early 2025 with five-plus biosimilars approved, Enbrel is in terminal decline, and Otezla faces generics from 2028 — cumulatively more than a quarter of current revenue is in structural erosion. The precedents are brutal: Humira went from $21.2B (2022) to under $5B in three years; Stelara lost more than half its revenue in eighteen months. The mitigating structural detail for denosumab is channel — it is a physician-administered, buy-and-bill product rather than a retail-pharmacy fill, and switching an in-office injectable requires a practice to change its purchasing, not a PBM to change a formulary tier. That is why first-year erosion has run below the Humira template, and why the market has been willing to pay up. On the winning side, Amgen's own biosimilar unit did roughly $3.0B in 2025, +30%, and is one of the few in the industry with the manufacturing cost base to make the economics work at biosimilar price points. Net-net this is still a headwind — the products being lost carry originator margins and the products being gained carry biosimilar margins — but Amgen is more hedged against the cliff than any of its peers.
U.S. Drug Pricing and Tax: Where the Policy Risk Actually Sits — Bull vs. Bear Framing
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Three policy regimes now operate at once. Inflation Reduction Act Medicare negotiation delivered its first maximum fair prices on January 1, 2026 — Enbrel was a first-cycle selection — with Otezla, Prolia and Xgeva plausible in later cycles. Most-favored-nation reference pricing, which would benchmark U.S. prices against the lowest developed-market price, remains an active proposal. And Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs threaten the import economics of offshore manufacturing, which for Amgen means Puerto Rico and Singapore.

The bull framing: Amgen is better positioned than most. Its fastest-growing assets — Repatha (already a volume-over-price strategy with heavily discounted net pricing), Tezspire, Imdelltra and the entire Horizon rare-disease portfolio — are either already low-priced, specialty-channel, or too small for negotiation selection. Its large and expanding U.S. manufacturing base (Ohio, North Carolina, Rhode Island) is a direct tariff hedge, and its biosimilar unit is structurally aligned with policymakers' stated goal of lowering drug costs. Policy pressure that compresses the industry's pricing power arguably helps the low-cost producer.

The bear framing: the same Puerto Rico footprint that hedges tariffs is the subject of an IRS transfer-pricing case with claimed deficiencies above $15B before penalties, unreserved, in U.S. Tax Court. That is roughly 7% of market capitalisation sitting entirely outside consensus estimates, on an indeterminate timetable. More broadly, the terminal-margin assumption for U.S. pharma has to come down: prices are now negotiated with the federal government rather than with payers alone, which is precisely why large-cap pharma trades in the mid-to-high teens on forward earnings while the NASDAQ-100 trades near 27×. Amgen at 17.8× is not obviously mispriced against that backdrop — it is priced as a pharma company with a lottery ticket, and the lottery ticket is the whole argument.
