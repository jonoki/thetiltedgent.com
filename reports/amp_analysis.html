01 Company Overview

Ameriprise Financial is a US wealth and asset manager with an insurance and annuity business attached to it. About 10,000 financial advisors — a mix of employees and franchisees — sit at the centre of it, and the firm reported record firm-wide AUM/AUA/AUM&A of $1.81 trillion at Q2 2026, up 14% YoY. FY2025 revenue was $18.91B, up 5.5% from $17.93B, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $19.84B, up 9.1%. By market capitalisation ($48.90B) it ranks roughly #514 globally.

The central debate on this page is not growth — it is what the 13.4× earnings multiple is pricing. Ameriprise earns a 55.0% return on equity ex-AOCI and returned 91% of operating earnings to shareholders last quarter, yet trades at a discount to the S&P 500 on trailing P/E (13.37 vs 26.11) and at a discount to its own asset-manager peer group. Part of that is deserved — the reported ROE is flattered by a thin equity base, the Asset Management segment is still in net outflow, and roughly $19B of client assets walk out the door this quarter as the Comerica relationship unwinds. Sections 03 and 04 take that apart. Note that several standard screening metrics are structurally meaningless for a financial and are marked n/m rather than quoted.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
10,000-ADVISOR DISTRIBUTION $732B WRAP ASSET BASE CLIENT SWITCHING COSTS CAPTIVE PRODUCT DISTRIBUTION $25B+ INTERNAL BANK SCALE OPERATING LEVERAGE 120-YEAR BRAND / TRUST

The moat is distribution, and it is narrower and more durable than it looks. Ameriprise does not have to win an asset-management beauty contest to earn a fee on a dollar — it has to keep the advisor, and the advisor keeps the client. Retail advisory relationships are famously sticky: moving an account means new paperwork, a tax event on unrealised gains in many cases, and the loss of a personal relationship that in practice is worth more to the household than 15 basis points of fee. That is why wrap assets grew 19% while the firm added only 79 net advisors — it is same-store growth, not headcount growth. The second-order effect is that the advisor force is also a captive distribution channel: annuities and Columbia Threadneedle funds reach clients through a salesforce that Ameriprise pays for once and monetises three times (advisory fee, product fee, cash spread). Very few competitors own all three layers. The third leg is the internal bank — $25B+ of assets and $28.8B of client cash — which converts idle cash into net interest income that a pure advisory platform has to hand to a custodian.

Key Vulnerabilities

Asset Management is in structural outflow
Net flows of $(6.5)B in Q2 2026 — the ninth-plus consecutive period of negative flows by the trend the comparisons imply, with institutional worsening to $(5.1)B. Columbia Threadneedle's AUM grew 10% purely on market appreciation. Active management losing share to passive is a decade-long secular trend, not a cyclical one, and margin improvement from a back-office project is a one-time offset, not a fix.
Revenue is levered to equity markets
Wrap fees, asset management fees and a large share of annuity fee income all bill against market values. With AUM/AUA at a record $1.81T, a 20% equity drawdown would take a roughly proportional bite out of the fee base with a cost structure that adjusts far more slowly. Beta of 1.13 understates the operating leverage; the 2022 drawdown to $237.68 and the 2025–26 slide to $444.40 both demonstrated it.
Cash-sweep economics and rate risk
$84B of total client cash, of which $46.6B has already migrated to third-party money market funds paying market rates rather than sitting on Ameriprise's balance sheet at a spread. Certificate balances fell to $7.4B "due to rate environment shifts." Cash sweep has also drawn regulatory and litigation attention across the brokerage industry. Falling short rates compress the spread; rising rates accelerate the migration. Neither direction is comfortable.
Channel concentration and transition risk
The Comerica relationship unwinds by end of Q3 2026, removing about $19B of client assets. The offsetting Huntington Bank onboarding (260 advisors, $28B of assets) is not expected until Q4 2026 / early 2027. That is a real, dated gap in the flow line, and the Comerica episode is a reminder that bank-channel partnerships are terminable contracts, not owned distribution.

Notable One-Off Items. Three things distort the headline numbers and deserve to be named. (1) The ROE is arithmetically flattered. A 63.4% GAAP ROE — 55.0% ex-AOCI — is not a normal outcome for a fee business; it reflects a book value per share of just $71.97 against a $553.53 price, an equity base that aggressive buybacks have shrunk (share count −5.22% YoY) faster than earnings. The business is genuinely capital-light and genuinely high-return, but the level of the ratio is partly a denominator story, and investors should not extrapolate 55% ROE into a valuation model as if it were a reinvestment rate. The same mechanic makes the reported 689% ROIC nonsense. (2) GAAP and adjusted EPS diverge in both directions. Q2 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $11.98 exceeded adjusted operating EPS of $11.07 — the market-impact adjustments on the annuity hedge book cut in favour of GAAP last quarter, and in prior quarters they have cut the other way (Q1 2025 diluted EPS was $5.83 against a far higher operating number, a −38% YoY GAAP swing that was hedge accounting, not business deterioration). Judge this company on adjusted operating EPS and check the reconciliation; judging it on GAAP quarterly EPS produces false signals. (3) A known $19B asset outflow is scheduled. The Comerica unwind completing by end of Q3 2026 removes roughly $19B of client assets, and the offsetting Huntington onboarding ($28B, 260 advisors) does not arrive until Q4 2026 / early 2027. Q3 flow optics will be worse than the underlying franchise, by design and on a known timetable.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen persistently and may be stretched; below 30 means the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a stock can stay "overbought" for months in a genuine uptrend. This one is computed on monthly closes with a 6-month lookback, so each reading reflects half a year of price action rather than a few days.

The five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $302.13 to the September 10, 2026 close of $553.53, AMP returned +83.2% on price alone — roughly 12.9% annualised. Dividends of approximately $29 per share paid over the window add roughly another 10 points of cumulative return (computed from reported DPS of $4.88, $5.30, $5.79 and $6.28 in FY2022–FY2025 plus partial-year payments, so treat it as an approximation). That compounding did not arrive smoothly. The path contains a 21% bear-market drawdown in 2022, a near-vertical 2023–24 re-rating, and then a second 23% drawdown from the November 2024 peak of $573.97 to the March 2026 trough of $444.40 — a fifteen-month round trip that went almost nowhere while earnings per share rose from $33.05 to over $41. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A capital-light advice franchise compounding at 12% revenue-per-advisor growth with a 28.9% pretax margin management calls sustainable, trading at 11.30× forward earnings against a 26.11× index. The firm converts nearly all of it to cash — $8.06B of free cash flow on ~$160M of capex — and hands 85–90% back, shrinking the share count 5.22% a year. Asset Management margin is up 370bps with retail outflows more than halved, and the Huntington onboarding (+$28B, 260 advisors) more than replaces the Comerica exit. Consensus 2026 EPS of $46.53 (+18.4%) at even a 14× multiple is $651.

Bear Case

You are paying 13.4× for a business whose revenue is a levered claim on equity market levels at an all-time high in assets, with an active asset manager still bleeding $(6.5)B a quarter and institutional outflows getting worse, not better. The headline 55% ROE is partly a shrunken-equity artefact, and buying back stock at $553 is a far worse use of capital than the $459 average paid last quarter. Eight of fourteen analysts say Hold, Morgan Stanley says Underweight at $489, and the average target implies 4.7% of upside. A 20% market drawdown takes a proportional bite out of fees with a fixed cost base beneath it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Equity market beta in the revenue line. With record AUM/AUA of $1.81T, wrap fees, asset management fees and annuity fee income all bill against market values. A sustained drawdown compresses revenue faster than the cost base can adjust, and the 2022 and 2025–26 episodes both showed the multiple compressing at the same time — a double hit.
02
Asset Management outflows do not stop. $(6.5)B of net outflow in a quarter where AUM rose 10% on market appreciation means the franchise is losing share of wallet. Institutional flows worsened YoY. Once the Q3 back-office cost saving annualises, there is no self-help lever left and the segment's earnings become a pure function of markets minus attrition.
03
Cash-sweep spread and rate direction. $46.6B of client cash has already moved to third-party money market funds and certificate balances fell to $7.4B on rate shifts. Falling short rates compress the spread on the $25B+ bank; rising rates accelerate further migration off balance sheet. Cash-sweep practices have also drawn regulatory and litigation scrutiny across the brokerage industry.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Active management's flow problem is not cyclical
Columbia Threadneedle's $(6.5)B of Q2 2026 net outflows sits alongside peers running the same treadmill: T. Rowe Price at $1.87T of AUM grew revenue 7.0% and trades at 10.80× earnings; Franklin Templeton at $1.83T grew 7.9%. All three are managing large, growing asset bases whose growth comes from markets rather than from winning mandates. The market has already priced this — the entire traditional active complex trades at single-digit-to-low-teens multiples while the index sits at 26×. For Ameriprise the saving grace is that Asset Management is only 18.3% of segment revenue and 19% of segment earnings; for a pure-play it is the whole story.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Client cash keeps voting with its feet
Of $84B of total client cash, $46.6B now sits in third-party money market funds paying market rates and $28.8B in on-balance-sheet cash, while certificate balances fell to $7.4B "due to rate environment shifts." This is the industry-wide cash-sorting dynamic: once retail clients learn that idle brokerage cash can earn a market yield elsewhere, the sweep-spread economics that padded 2023–24 brokerage earnings do not come back. Ameriprise's $25B+ internal bank still earns a real spread, but the marginal dollar of client cash is now priced, not free.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Advisor productivity as the operating-leverage story
Ameriprise added 79 net advisors in Q2 2026 — essentially nothing — yet grew AWM revenue 16% and lifted trailing revenue per advisor 12% to a record $1.203M. Management attributes part of this to technology: roughly 6,000 advisors are on the engagement toolset, and practices using the full suite reportedly save more than 30 hours a week of administrative work. If that holds, the wealth business scales revenue without scaling headcount, which is precisely what a 28.9% margin held flat through 16% growth implies.
BULL VS BEAR — THE FRAME
Is 13.4× a discount or a verdict?
The bull reads the multiple as a mispricing: a wealth-management compounder with a 55% ROE and an 18% forward EPS growth rate should not trade at half the index multiple, and the 5.22% annual share-count reduction does the rest of the work. The bear reads the same number as an accurate verdict on earnings quality — market-beta revenue, an outflowing asset manager, a shrinking-equity ROE and a known $19B asset exit this quarter. Both readings are consistent with every verified figure on this page. The resolution arrives on October 29, in two numbers: the AWM margin, and whether Asset Management flows are still negative.
