01 Company Overview

Ameriprise Financial is a US wealth and asset manager with an insurance and annuity business attached to it. More than 10,000 financial advisors sit at the centre of it, and total client assets (AUM/AUA/AUM&A) were $1.78 trillion at the end of Q3 2026, up 8% YoY but down 2% from the $1.81 trillion record at the end of Q2. FY2025 revenue was $18.91B, up 5.5% from $17.93B, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 2026 is $20.14B, up 8.8% (StockAnalysis). By market capitalisation ($42.61B) it ranks #261 in the S&P 500.

The central debate on this page is no longer growth — it is whether advisors keep choosing to stay. The quarter itself was solid: adjusted operating earnings per share rose 14% (22% before the annual unlocking charge), the company reports a 55.3% return on equity excluding AOCI, and it handed back 93% of operating earnings to shareholders. Yet the shares have fallen from $553.53 to $490.83 since the last edition, and at P/E of 11.05 on trailing earnings they sit well below the S&P 500's 25.10×. BofA's October 19 downgrade pointed to several well-known advisor practices leaving for rivals such as Charles Schwab and to the retention payments Ameriprise is using to keep others; management said on the October 29 call that retention among long-tenured advisors is in the high 90s percent and that there is no broad new program. Add an Asset Management arm still losing client money ($9.3B net outflow in the quarter) and a Comerica relationship whose roughly $19B of client assets have now mostly left, against Huntington Bank's roughly $28B arriving, and the question the market is pricing is plain. Sections 03 and 04 take it apart. Several standard screening numbers are structurally meaningless for a financial and are marked n/m rather than quoted.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
10,000+ ADVISOR DISTRIBUTION $729B WRAP ASSET BASE CLIENT SWITCHING COSTS CAPTIVE PRODUCT DISTRIBUTION $24.7B INTERNAL BANK SCALE

The moat is distribution, and the last two months have tested it. Ameriprise does not have to win an asset-management beauty contest to earn a fee on a dollar — it has to keep the advisor, and the advisor keeps the client. Retail advisory relationships are sticky: moving an account means new paperwork, a tax bill on unrealised gains in many cases, and the loss of a personal relationship. That is why wrap assets grew 12% in the year to September while the firm kept adding experienced advisors (79 in Q3) — it is same-store growth, not only headcount growth. The advisor force is also a captive distribution channel: annuities and Columbia Threadneedle funds reach clients through a salesforce that Ameriprise pays for once and earns on three times (advisory fee, product fee, cash spread). The internal bank — $24.7B of assets, with $27.0B in cash-sweep balances — converts idle client cash into net interest income that a pure advisory platform has to hand to someone else. What BofA's October 19 note questions is how durable the first leg is: it says several prominent advisor practices (Rise, Fathom and StackStone, as summarised by Yahoo Finance) are moving to rivals such as Charles Schwab, and that Ameriprise is using cash retention bonuses structured as forgivable loans and selective platform fee cuts to hold others. On the Q3 call the CFO said Ameriprise is not making changes and the CEO said there is no program, and management put retention among long-tenured advisors in the high 90s percent. The two accounts are on record side by side; the next few quarters of advisor and flow numbers will show which is closer.

Key Vulnerabilities

Advisors can leave, and taking clients with them
BofA cut the stock to Underperform on October 19 (target $445 from $570) on advisor attrition, and expects retention payments and a 25% rise in transition-assistance loans to squeeze wealth-management margins near term. Organic growth, it argued, trails LPL Financial and Raymond James. Management's answer on the call: retention is in the high 90s percent and there is no broad new program. The AWM margin of 29.3% (down 20 basis points) is the number that would show strain first.
Asset Management is in structural outflow
Net outflows of $(9.3)B in Q3 2026 against $(3.4)B a year earlier, with institutional clients pulling $(5.5)B. Assets under management and advisement grew only 4% and fell from $759B at the end of Q2 to $743B. Active management losing ground to cheaper index funds is a decade-long trend, not a cyclical one.
Revenue rises and falls with equity markets
Wrap fees, asset management fees and a large share of annuity fee income all bill against market values. With total client assets at $1.78T and the S&P 500 at 7,652 on September 30 (up 14% on the year, per the release), a 20% market fall would take a roughly proportional bite out of the fee base while the cost base adjusts more slowly. Beta of 1.12 (a measure of how much the stock moves with the market) points the same way.
Cash-sweep economics and interest rates
Total client cash was $81B, down 5% on the year, as money moved into third-party money market funds and out through the Comerica transition; cash-sweep balances were $27.0B (from $28.8B at Q2). Certificate balances declined and lower short-term rates weighed on cash earnings (Q3 call). The CFO said the usual pattern is for balances to fall in Q3 and build in Q4. BofA flags the longer-term risk that low yields on uninvested cash push clients to higher-yielding alternatives and force Ameriprise to pay more on sweep cash.
Bank-channel contracts end
Comerica's roughly $19B of client assets have mostly left (CFO, Q3 call); a small portion is still to go. The replacement, Huntington Bank, completed moving its 258 advisors to Ameriprise on October 19 (CFO, Q3 call), with $28B of client assets transferring through early 2027 (release), with 29 more advisors and about $4B from Huntington's Cadence Bank deal due in mid-2027. Bank partnerships are terminable contracts, not owned distribution.

Notable One-Off Items. Three things distort the headline numbers and deserve to be named. (1) The ROE is arithmetically flattered. A 72% trailing GAAP ROE — 55.3% on the company's own adjusted measure that excludes AOCI — is not a normal outcome for a fee business. Total equity was $5.11B at September 30 against $6.46B a year earlier, with accumulated other comprehensive losses of $2.64B, while the share count fell to 87.0M from 93.1M. That puts book value at roughly $59 a share (our calculation: $5.11B ÷ 87.0M) against a $490.83 price. The business is genuinely capital-light and high-return, but the level of the ratio is partly a story about a small equity base, and investors should not extrapolate it as a reinvestment rate. (2) GAAP and adjusted EPS differ. Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $12.32 exceeded adjusted operating EPS of $11.26, because the market-impact adjustments on the annuity hedge book cut in GAAP's favour this quarter, and the annual unlocking charge of $0.81 a share depressed the adjusted number. Judge the company on adjusted operating EPS and check the reconciliation in the release. (3) Comerica distorts the flow numbers. The reported $(8.5)B of AWM client net flows becomes +$5.3B excluding Comerica, whose roughly $19B of client assets have now mostly left. Huntington's $28B, arriving through early 2027, will flatter the flow numbers by the same logic.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen persistently and may be stretched; below 30 means the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a stock can stay “overbought” for months in a genuine uptrend. This one is computed on monthly closes with a 6-month lookback, so each reading reflects half a year of price action rather than a few days.

The five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $302.13 to the October 30, 2026 close of $490.83, AMP returned +62.5% on price alone (dividends not included) — roughly 10.2% a year (our calculation: 1.6246 to the power of one-fifth). The path was not smooth. It contains a 21% bear-market drawdown in 2022 (to $237.68 in June), a sharp 2023–24 rally to a November 2024 peak of $573.97, and then a 22.6% fall from that peak to a March 2026 low of $444.40 (month-end closes) — a sixteen-month slide while earnings per share rose from $33.05 in FY2024 to $44.42 over the latest twelve months. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A capital-light advice franchise with record revenue per advisor ($1.24M, +13%) and a 29.3% wealth-management margin that management expects to hold, trading at 10.64× 2026 consensus earnings of $46.11 against an index at 19.62×. It converts nearly all of its earnings to cash — $7.33B of free cash flow on $152M of capex — and hands 93% of operating earnings back, shrinking the share count 6.6% in a year, with a new $5.5B buyback authorization. Retention in the high 90s percent among long-tenured advisors, as management tells it, and Huntington's $28B of client assets more than replace Comerica's roughly $19B. Even a 14× multiple on $46.11 is $646.

Bear Case

You are paying 11× for a business whose revenue is tied to stock-market levels, whose advisors are being courted away by rivals, and whose active asset manager lost $(9.3)B of client money in the quarter against $(3.4)B a year earlier. BofA calls it Underperform with a $445 target, citing retention payments that will squeeze margins; Morgan Stanley is Underweight at $483. The 72% trailing GAAP ROE is partly a small-equity story: equity is down to $5.11B from $6.46B. Eight of fourteen analysts say Hold, and a 20% market fall would take a proportional bite out of fees with a cost base that adjusts slowly.

Top 3 Risks

01
Advisor attrition and the cost of keeping advisors. The distribution moat only holds while advisors stay. BofA expects retention payments and transition-assistance loans to compress wealth-management margins, and the AWM margin was already down 20 basis points to 29.3% in Q3. Management says retention is in the high 90s percent and that it is not making broad changes.
02
Equity market beta in the revenue line. With $1.78T of client assets, wrap fees, asset management fees and annuity fee income all bill against market values. A sustained drawdown compresses revenue faster than costs can adjust, and the 2022 fall (−21.3% to June 2022) and the 2024–26 slide (−22.6% to March 2026) hit the share price far harder than earnings: FY2022 earnings per share fell only 2.7%, and they rose through the 2024–26 slide.
03
Asset Management outflows and cash-sweep pressure. $(9.3)B of net outflow in the quarter means the franchise is losing share of client money, with institutional clients pulling $(5.5)B. Total client cash is down 5% on the year and lower short-term rates weighed on cash earnings; BofA warns that low cash yields could push clients to higher-paying alternatives.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The fight for advisors
On the Q3 call the CFO said recruiting from rival firms remains competitive and described some competitors' packages as taking seven to eight years to pay back in cash. BofA argues that running an independent advisory firm is becoming more attractive and that wirehouse (large full-service bank) recruiting is slowing advisors' moves to independent broker-dealers. Ameriprise's answer is a platform pitch — revenue per advisor of $1.24M, up 13%, a record — rather than the highest cheque.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Active management's flow problem is not cyclical
Columbia Threadneedle's $(9.3)B of Q3 2026 net outflows sits alongside peers growing revenue on market gains: T. Rowe Price grew trailing revenue 6.9% and trades at 10.53× trailing earnings, and Franklin Templeton grew 7.9% and trades at 21.52× trailing and 10.29× forward earnings (our calculations from StockAnalysis at the October 30 close). For Ameriprise the saving grace is that Asset Management is only 18.5% of segment revenue and 22% of segment earnings; for a pure-play it is the whole story.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Bank partnerships bring whole advisor teams
The Huntington deal is one of the largest of its kind the company has done, the CEO said on the Q3 call: roughly 260 advisors and $28B of client assets, with a further 29 advisors and about $4B from Cadence Bank due in mid-2027. Management said it has room to add more financial-institution partners. Unlike recruiting individual advisors, these deals bring client assets in bulk, though bank contracts can also end, as Comerica's did.
BULL VS BEAR — THE FRAME
Is 11× a discount or a verdict?
The bull reads the multiple as a mispricing: a wealth business earning a 55% adjusted ROE, growing earnings per share in the high teens, should not trade at less than half the index multiple, and the 6.6% annual drop in the share count does the rest of the work. The bear reads the same number as a fair verdict on earnings quality — market-linked revenue, an outflowing asset manager, a small-equity ROE and an advisor force that rivals are bidding for. Both readings fit every figure on this page. The next test is the Q4 2026 report: the wealth margin, and whether client flows recover once Comerica is gone and Huntington's assets are in.
