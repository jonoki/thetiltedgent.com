01 Company Overview

American Tower is the world's largest independent owner and operator of multitenant communications real estate, with a portfolio of more than 148,000 communications sites across the Americas, Europe, Africa and Asia-Pacific, plus a US interconnection-dense data center platform acquired through CoreSite. It leases vertical space on towers and rooftops to wireless carriers, broadcasters and government tenants under long-duration contracts with built-in escalators, and structures the business as a REIT. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $10.645B, up 5.1% YoY, with AFFO per share of $10.76. It ranks as the #312 most valuable public company globally and the second-largest constituent of the specialty-REIT industry behind Equinix.

The economics are unusual for real estate: a tower is a fixed-cost asset whose marginal tenant carries almost no incremental cost, so each additional carrier on an existing structure drops through at very high margin. That is why American Tower runs a 67.0% adjusted EBITDA margin on FY2025 revenue while carrying $37.2B of debt — the asset base is capital-intensive up front and cash-generative forever after. The central tension in the equity today is that this long-duration, bond-like cash stream is being re-rated by interest rates at the same time that US organic growth has been throttled by carrier-consolidation churn.

The cash engine and the problem child simultaneously. American Tower leases space on US and Canadian macro towers to AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile under contracts that typically run 5–10 years with roughly 3% annual escalators, producing revenue that is contractual, recurring and largely insensitive to the economic cycle. The reason the bull case is not simply "wait for churn to lap." CoreSite is a US interconnection-dense colocation platform — the kind of facility where networks, clouds and enterprises cross-connect rather than the kind where hyperscalers park bulk training capacity. That positioning has become genuinely valuable in the AI cycle: management stated on the Q2 2026 call that nine of the top ten AI companies and three of the top five neoclouds are deployed in CoreSite facilities, and reported record leasing activity in the quarter. Q2 2026 revenue was $297M, +13.4% YoY, and the company raised its full-year data center growth expectation to roughly 15%, up 200 basis points from prior guidance, contributing ~$25M of the ~$110M full-year property revenue guidance raise. American Tower is funding this deliberately: $695M of 2026 data center development spending inside a $1,805–1,915M total capex envelope, meaning roughly 37% of group capital expenditure is going to a segment that is only ~10% of revenue. At ~10% of the mix, CoreSite cannot yet offset a flat US tower business — but it is compounding at 3–4x the group rate and it is where incremental capital is being pointed.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Irreplaceable Sites Zoning Barriers Contractual Escalators Colocation Operating Leverage Switching Costs Global Scale · 148k Sites CoreSite Interconnection

The moat is physical and legal rather than technological. A macro tower sits on a specific parcel that cleared local zoning, environmental and structural review — permissions that in most developed markets have become materially harder to obtain than when the original grid was built, which means the existing asset base is closer to irreplaceable than depreciable. Once a carrier's radios are on a structure, moving them costs money, requires new permits and creates a coverage gap during the transition, so tenants renew rather than relocate; contracts run 5–10 years with roughly 3% escalators embedded, which converts the asset into an inflation-linked annuity. The economics compound through colocation: the second and third tenant on a tower add revenue against a nearly unchanged cost base, which is how American Tower sustains a 67.0% adjusted EBITDA margin and a 45.5% operating margin on infrastructure. CoreSite adds a second, different moat — interconnection density, where value accrues to whichever facility already hosts the most networks, a network effect that is very hard to replicate with new construction. The vulnerability is that none of this protects against the two things actually driving the stock: the number of independent tenants willing to pay, and the discount rate applied to the cash they pay.

Key Vulnerabilities

Customer concentration in a three-carrier US market. The US tenant base is effectively AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile. Every consolidation event permanently removes a paying tenant from a shared structure, and the resulting decommissioning shows up as churn for years afterward. Sprint's absorption into T-Mobile and DISH/EchoStar's site exits are the live examples: management attributes essentially the entire gap between ~4% underlying tower growth and the 0.7% US & Canada organic tenant billings growth reported in Q2 2026 to this dynamic. There is no commercial remedy — the tenant simply no longer exists.

The balance sheet is the business model, and it is rate-exposed. American Tower carries $37.19B of total debt against $1.76B of cash, for $35.43B net debt and 4.9x net leverage — against its own ~5x ceiling, with no meaningful headroom. Reported D/E of 4.39x and a P/B of 21.9x are artefacts of decades of depreciation against assets that have not actually lost value. 93% of debt is fixed-rate with a 5.2-year weighted-average maturity, which buys time but not immunity: every maturity that rolls refinances at rates well above the coupons set in the 2019–2021 era, and that is a permanent step-down in AFFO growth relative to the pre-2022 trajectory.

Emerging-market currency and macro exposure. 37% of revenue sits outside the US and Canada, much of it in Brazil, Mexico, Nigeria, India-adjacent APAC markets and Africa. FY2025 booked an $809.4M FX loss against a $308.3M FX gain in FY2024 — a ~$1.1B swing in reported results driven purely by translation. Latin America is simultaneously contributing negative organic growth (−2.4% in Q2 2026, guided to ~−3% for FY2026). The company has been shrinking this exposure (India 2024, Philippines and KTBL 2026), which reduces volatility but also removes the highest-growth-rate assets it owned.

The growth pivot is small and capital-hungry. Data centers are ~10% of revenue but absorb ~$695M of 2026 capex, roughly 37% of total. If CoreSite leasing decelerates — because AI capacity demand normalizes, because hyperscalers bring interconnection in-house, or because power constraints slow deliveries — American Tower is left with a flat US tower business, a shrinking international footprint and 4.9x leverage, with the growth narrative removed.

Notable One-Off Items

Currency translation is the largest distortion, by far. FY2025 recorded a foreign currency loss of $809.4M versus a $308.3M gain in FY2024 — a swing of roughly $1.1B in reported results with no cash-economic content. It reversed again in Q2 2026: a $42.1M FX gain against a $(484.0)M loss in the prior-year quarter, which is why Q2 2026 net income attributable to common stockholders "grew" 136.5% to $868M and why TTM EPS shows +162% growth. Every reported earnings growth rate on this company for the last two years should be re-checked against AFFO before it is used.

The ATC India divestiture — confirmed closed. American Tower closed the sale of 100% of ATC India to Data Infrastructure Trust, a Brookfield-sponsored InvIT, on September 12, 2024, for approximately INR 210 billion (~$2.5B) — $2.2B of final closing proceeds plus ~$320M from debenture monetization and receivables. Proceeds went to debt repayment, including retirement of the India term loan. The transaction carried a $1.2B loss recognized in 2024, India was reclassified to discontinued operations, and pro-forma continuing-operations AFFO/share was reset to ~$9.95. The effect on the growth profile is real and permanent: India was a large-volume, low-margin, high-churn, currency-exposed market, so its removal raised group margins and reduced volatility while removing a large tower count and a source of unit growth. The current low-single-digit organic growth profile is partly a consequence of that deliberate trade.

Straight-line revenue normalization. Non-cash straight-line revenue fell to $101M in FY2025 from $277.6M in FY2024. This is a $177M reduction in reported property revenue with no cash impact — it mechanically depresses the reported growth rate and is worth adding back when comparing FY2025 to FY2024.

Further portfolio pruning in 2026. ATC Philippines ($75.6M) and the KTBL controlling interest ($6.9M) were sold in June 2026. Small in dollars, but they continue the pattern of shrinking the international tail and they subtract from reported revenue growth in future comparisons.

Services revenue distortion. FY2025 services revenue of $340M grew 75.3% off a low base. This flatters total revenue growth relative to underlying property performance and is not a recurring run-rate.

No material goodwill impairment was identified in the FY2025 or Q2 2026 releases reviewed for this analysis. Absence of a disclosure here should not be read as confirmation that none exists — verify against the 10-K and 10-Q.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 suggests the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 suggests it has been sold off hard and may be washed out. It describes momentum, not value.

The five-year trajectory is one long de-rating with two failed recoveries. From a monthly close of $265.41 in September 2021 to $174.49 on August 19, 2026, AMT lost 34.3% of its price, and from the December 2021 cycle peak of $292.50 to the September 2023 trough of $164.45 it fell 43.8%. Dividends materially soften that — the shares yield 4.10% today and distributions per share grew 4.9% in 2025 to $6.80 — but the total return over the period still lands far behind the index. For context on the same recent window: AMT's 52-week price change is −14.1% while SPY returned +20.9% including dividends over the past year, roughly 35 percentage points of underperformance in twelve months. Against tower peers the picture is less lonely — Crown Castle is −22.2% and SBA Communications −12.6% over one year — which is the tell: this is a sector de-rating driven by rates and carrier capex, not a company-specific failure. The one peer that ran away, DigitalBridge at +43.4%, is the one the market values on data centers.

Mechanically, what happened is a discount-rate story. In 2021 a contractual, inflation-escalating, 3%-growing cash stream with 4.9x leverage was worth ~$292 when the 10-year Treasury yielded well under 2%. When the risk-free rate repriced through 2022–2023, the same cash stream was worth $164 — nothing about the towers changed. The 2023–2024 rally to $232.56 was a rate-cut trade, and it faded. The 2026 leg down to $163.57 in June is a different animal: that one was fundamental, driven by carrier-consolidation churn crushing organic tenant billings growth to 1.7%. The stock has since recovered to $174.49 on the Q2 beat and the data center guidance raise, sitting above its 50-day average of $172.58 but below its 200-day of $177.91 — technically unresolved, and 18.8% below the 52-week high.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 15.7x forward P/AFFO and collecting a 4.10% yield covered at a ~65% AFFO payout for an irreplaceable global asset base with 73.8% gross margins and 67% adjusted EBITDA margins. The growth suppression is identifiable and finite: Sprint/DISH decommissioning churn accounts for essentially all of the gap between 0.7% reported US organic tenant billings growth and management's ~4% underlying run-rate, and it is expected to ease materially in 2027 — at which point US growth re-accelerates by several hundred basis points with no new leasing required. Meanwhile CoreSite is compounding at ~15% with record leasing and nine of the top ten AI companies deployed, and the company has raised guidance twice in 2026. Every one of 25 covering analysts has a target above the current price.

Bear Case

Strip out the currency noise and this is a business growing organic tenant billings at 1.7% with AFFO/share guided to +3.0% at the midpoint, carrying $35.4B of net debt at 4.9x leverage against its own ~5x ceiling, earning an 8.03% ROIC against a marginal refinancing cost that has risen sharply since the coupons on its existing stack were set. The US tenant market is three carriers and shrinking; Latin America is contractionary at −3% guided; the international portfolio is being sold down piece by piece, which removes volatility but also removes growth. Data centers are the only genuine growth engine and they are ~10% of revenue while consuming ~37% of capex. If long rates stay elevated, the multiple does not recover and you are left clipping a 4% coupon on a low-single-digit compounder that has lost 34% of its price in five years.

Top 3 Risks

01
Churn does not roll off on schedule. The entire bull case rests on 2027 US organic tenant billings growth re-accelerating from ~0.5% as Sprint/T-Mobile and DISH/EchoStar decommissioning laps. If carriers extend the wind-down, or if a further consolidation event removes another tenant, the "temporary" de-rating becomes the permanent growth rate and the current 15.7x forward P/AFFO is not cheap — it is fair value for a 1% grower.
02
Rates stay higher for longer and the refinancing wall bites. $37.19B of gross debt at 4.9x net leverage, 5.2-year weighted-average maturity. 93% fixed-rate protects the near term but guarantees the repricing arrives in tranches; the December 2025 issue came at 4.700%. Rising interest expense subtracts directly from AFFO, and a higher discount rate simultaneously compresses the multiple applied to it — the same variable hits both the numerator and the denominator.
03
Emerging-market currency and Latin American contraction. 37% of revenue is non-US, and FY2025 alone swung ~$1.1B in reported results on translation ($809.4M loss vs a $308.3M prior-year gain). LatAm organic tenant billings are guided to ~−3% on elevated Brazil churn. Continued divestitures reduce the exposure but also shrink the asset base, and each sale removes a growth market in exchange for a one-time deleveraging benefit.
  Industry Commentary
Interest Rates & the Valuation of Long-Duration REITs
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Tower REITs are the closest thing in equities to a levered, inflation-linked perpetual bond: contractual revenue, ~3% escalators, 20-year asset lives, and 35% of enterprise value funded by debt. That construction makes them exquisitely sensitive to the long end of the curve, in both directions and through two separate channels. Channel one is the discount rate — AMT went from $292.50 in December 2021 to $164.45 in September 2023, a 43.8% decline, with no deterioration in tenant billings; the cash flows did not change, the rate used to value them did. Channel two is interest expense — $37.19B of gross debt against $7.04B of EBITDA means every 100bp of incremental refinancing cost on a rolling 5.2-year maturity schedule compounds directly into AFFO. The company has done what it can: 93% fixed-rate, $11.1B of liquidity, leverage held flat at 4.9x. What it cannot do is create growth fast enough to outrun a structurally higher cost of capital. The 8.03% ROIC against a 4.7% new-issue coupon leaves a spread, but a much thinner one than the business was built on.

The Carrier Capex Cycle & Consolidation Churn
ONGOING HEADWIND

The 5G capex wave that funded 2019–2022 leasing has passed its peak, and the US carrier market has simultaneously consolidated from a four-plus-player field to three. These are different problems that arrived together. Lower capex means fewer new amendments; consolidation means negative leasing as acquired networks are decommissioned — Sprint's absorption into T-Mobile, and DISH/EchoStar's retreat from its build-out. The arithmetic in Q2 2026: 0.7% US & Canada organic tenant billings growth against management's stated ~4% underlying demand, with FY2026 guided to ~0.5%. The same disease shows up across the sector — Crown Castle's revenue is −4.6% YoY and its stock −22.2% over twelve months, worse than AMT on both counts, while SBA at +5.4% revenue growth is the relative winner. Latin America is running the same playbook a cycle later, with Brazil consolidation churn pushing that segment to −2.4% organic in Q2 2026 and ~−3% guided for the year. The critical unknown is duration: management says the headwind eases substantially in 2027. That claim, not the 5G narrative, is what the equity is actually underwriting.

Data Centers & Edge Compute — The Growth Pivot, and What It Decides
EMERGING TAILWIND

CoreSite is the swing factor in how this stock gets valued for the rest of the decade. Interconnection-dense colocation has become strategically important in the AI cycle — models trained in bulk hyperscale facilities still have to be served close to networks and users, which is precisely what CoreSite's footprint does. The evidence so far is real: $297M of Q2 2026 revenue, +13.4% YoY, record leasing, ~15% full-year growth guided (up 200bp), nine of the top ten AI companies deployed, and $695M of 2026 development capex behind it. The bull framing: a ~10%-of-revenue segment growing at 3–4x the group rate, funded by 37% of capex, eventually becomes large enough to lift group AFFO growth from ~3% into mid-single digits — at which point the market stops valuing AMT as a bond proxy and starts valuing it alongside Equinix and Digital Realty, whose peers command a 36.2x industry-average P/E versus AMT's 24.0x. The bear framing: 10% of revenue cannot offset 49% of revenue growing at zero, the capital is being diverted into a market with well-capitalized incumbents and real power constraints, and if AI-adjacent leasing normalizes American Tower will have spent several billion dollars to arrive at the same flat growth profile with less financial flexibility. The next four quarters of CoreSite leasing decide which of those is true.
