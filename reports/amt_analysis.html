01 Company Overview

American Tower, one of the largest global REITs, is an independent owner, operator and developer of multitenant communications real estate, with a portfolio of more than 147,000 communications sites (Q3 2026 release) across the Americas, Europe, Africa and Asia-Pacific, plus a US interconnection-dense data center platform acquired through CoreSite. It leases vertical space on towers and rooftops to wireless carriers, broadcasters and government tenants under long-duration contracts with built-in escalators, and structures the business as a REIT. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $10.645B, up 5.1% YoY, with AFFO per share of $10.76. It ranks #146 in the S&P 500 by market value and is the second-largest company in the specialty-REIT industry behind Equinix. On October 27, 2026 it reported Q3 results (revenue $2.785B, +2.5%; AFFO per share $2.87, +3.2%), raised its full-year outlook for the third time this year, and the board added $2.0B to the share-buyback authorization.

The economics are unusual for real estate: a tower is a fixed-cost asset whose marginal tenant carries almost no incremental cost, so each additional carrier on an existing structure drops through at very high margin. That is why American Tower runs a 67.0% adjusted EBITDA margin on FY2025 revenue while carrying $37.6B of debt (Sep 30, 2026) — the asset base is capital-intensive up front and cash-generative forever after. The central tension in the equity today is that this long-duration, bond-like cash stream is repriced whenever interest rates move, at the same time that US organic growth has been held back by carrier-consolidation churn (carriers removing equipment from shared towers after mergers and shutdowns).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Irreplaceable Sites Zoning Barriers Contractual Escalators Shared-Tower Economics Switching Costs Global Scale · 147k Sites CoreSite Interconnection

The moat is physical and legal rather than technological. A macro tower sits on a specific parcel that cleared local zoning, environmental and structural review — permissions that in most developed markets have become materially harder to obtain than when the original grid was built, which means the existing asset base is closer to irreplaceable than depreciable. Once a carrier's radios are on a structure, moving them costs money, requires new permits and creates a coverage gap during the transition, so tenants renew rather than relocate; contracts run 5–10 years with roughly 3% escalators embedded, which converts the asset into an inflation-linked annuity. The economics compound through colocation: the second and third tenant on a tower add revenue against a nearly unchanged cost base, which is how American Tower sustains a 67.0% adjusted EBITDA margin and a 45.5% operating margin on infrastructure. CoreSite adds a second, different moat — interconnection density, where value accrues to whichever facility already hosts the most networks, a network effect that is very hard to replicate with new construction. The vulnerability is that none of this protects against the two things actually driving the stock: the number of independent tenants willing to pay, and the discount rate applied to the cash they pay.

Key Vulnerabilities

Customer concentration in a three-carrier US market. The US tenant base is effectively AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile. Every consolidation event permanently removes a paying tenant from a shared structure, and the resulting decommissioning shows up as churn for years afterward. Sprint's absorption into T-Mobile and DISH/EchoStar's site exits are the live examples: on the Q3 call the CFO put US & Canada organic growth at about 4% excluding DISH churn, against the 0.0% US & Canada organic tenant billings growth reported in Q3 2026. There is no commercial remedy — the tenant simply no longer exists.

The balance sheet is the business model, and it is rate-exposed. American Tower carries $37.58B of total debt against $1.87B of cash at Sep 30, 2026, for $35.71B net debt and 4.9x net leverage — near the top of the 3x–5x target range the CFO cited on the Q3 call. Reported D/E of 4.64x and a P/B of 24.7x are artefacts of decades of depreciation against assets that have not actually lost value. Debt matures in tranches, which buys time but not immunity: in September 2026 the company priced $1.6B of new notes at 5.300%, 5.560% and 5.750% (Sep 9 8-K) and used the proceeds to repay $600M of 1.450% notes, so every maturity that rolls refinances at rates well above the coupons set in the 2019–2021 era — a permanent step-down in AFFO growth relative to the pre-2022 trajectory.

Emerging-market currency and macro exposure. About 38% of FY2025 revenue (37.6%) came from outside the US and Canada, much of it in Brazil, Mexico, Nigeria, India-adjacent APAC markets and Africa. FY2025 booked an $809.4M FX loss against a $308.3M FX gain in FY2024 — a ~$1.1B swing in reported results driven purely by translation. Latin America is simultaneously contributing negative organic growth (−2.1% in Q3 2026, guided to ~−3% for FY2026). The company has been shrinking this exposure (India 2024, Philippines and KTBL 2026), which reduces volatility but also removes the highest-growth-rate assets it owned.

The growth pivot is small and capital-hungry. Data centers are ~10% of revenue but absorb ~$695M of 2026 capex, roughly 38% of the total outlook. If CoreSite leasing decelerates — because AI capacity demand normalizes, because hyperscalers bring interconnection in-house, or because power constraints slow deliveries — American Tower is left with a flat US tower business, a shrinking international footprint and 4.9x leverage, with the growth narrative removed.

Notable One-Off Items

Currency translation is the largest distortion, by far. FY2025 recorded a foreign currency loss of $809.4M versus a $308.3M gain in FY2024 — a swing of roughly $1.1B in reported results with no cash-economic content. Q3 2026 net income attributable to common stockholders fell 10.3% to $766M and diluted EPS fell 9.9% to $1.64, including a $71.5M unrealized loss on equity investments (against a $10.9M gain a year earlier); the Q3 outlook lowers full-year net income by $30M at the midpoint for equity security losses. Every reported earnings growth rate on this company for the last two years should be re-checked against AFFO before it is used.

The ATC India divestiture — confirmed closed. American Tower closed the sale of 100% of ATC India to Data Infrastructure Trust, a Brookfield-sponsored InvIT, on September 12, 2024, for approximately INR 210 billion (~$2.5B) — $2.2B of final closing proceeds plus ~$320M from debenture monetization and receivables. Proceeds went to debt repayment, including retirement of the India term loan, and India was reclassified to discontinued operations. The effect on the growth profile is real and permanent: India was a large-volume, low-margin, high-churn, currency-exposed market, so its removal raised group margins and reduced volatility while removing a large tower count and a source of unit growth. The current low-single-digit organic growth profile is partly a consequence of that deliberate trade.

Straight-line revenue normalization. Non-cash straight-line revenue fell to $101M in FY2025 from $277.6M in FY2024. This is a $177M reduction in reported property revenue with no cash impact — it mechanically depresses the reported growth rate and is worth adding back when comparing FY2025 to FY2024. In Q3 2026 straight-line revenue was a negative $24.0M against +$27.7M a year earlier.

Further portfolio pruning in 2026. ATC Philippines ($75.6M) and the KTBL controlling interest ($6.9M) were sold in June 2026. Small in dollars, but they continue the pattern of shrinking the international tail and they subtract from reported revenue growth in future comparisons. On October 27, 2026 the company also agreed to sell its Canadian subsidiaries for about C$288M (about $202M), closing expected in Q4 2026.

Services revenue distortion. FY2025 services revenue of $340M grew 75.3% off a low base. This flatters total revenue growth relative to underlying property performance and is not a recurring run-rate.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 suggests the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 suggests it has been sold off hard and may be washed out. It describes momentum, not value.

The five-year trajectory is one long decline with two failed recoveries. From a monthly close of $262.48 in November 2021 to $187.78 on October 30, 2026, AMT lost 28.5% of its price, and from the December 2021 cycle peak of $292.50 to the September 2023 trough of $164.45 it fell 43.8%. Dividends materially soften that — the shares yield 3.81% today and distributions per share grew 4.9% in 2025 to $6.80 — but the total return over the period still lands far behind the index. For context on the same recent window, price against price (dividends excluded for both): AMT is +4.6% over the 52 weeks to October 30, 2026 ($179.45 to $187.78) while SPY is +11.2% ($679.83 to $755.72), roughly 6.5 percentage points of underperformance in twelve months. Against tower peers AMT looks better — Crown Castle is −2.9% and SBA Communications −2.1% over the same year — which suggests the weakness was a sector story driven by interest rates and carrier spending, not a company-specific failure. DigitalBridge, the data center investor that was the one peer to run away in the previous edition, was delisted on September 30, 2026 after its acquisition by SoftBank Group (StockAnalysis).

Mechanically, what happened is a discount-rate story. In 2021 a contractual, inflation-escalating, 3%-growing cash stream with 4.9x leverage was worth ~$292 when the 10-year Treasury yielded well under 2%. When the risk-free rate repriced through 2022–2023, the same cash stream was worth $164 — nothing about the towers changed. The 2023–2024 rally to $232.56 was a rate-cut trade, and it faded. The 2026 leg down to $163.57 at the June month-end came in the month American Tower terminated its DISH agreement (June 2); the Q2 results that followed on July 28 showed organic tenant billings growth of 1.7%. The shares then closed at $161.06 on October 1, the lowest close of the past 52 weeks, jumped 9.3% in the single session of October 9 ($166.73 to $182.24), and finished October at $187.78 — +14.7% for the month, after the Q3 print and the third outlook raise. That leaves the price above its 50-day average of $175.87 and its 200-day of $177.41 and 3.3% below the highest close of the past 52 weeks ($194.12 on June 5, 2026).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 16.9x forward P/AFFO and collecting a 3.81% yield covered at a ~65% AFFO payout for an irreplaceable global asset base with 73.5% gross margins and 67% adjusted EBITDA margins. The growth suppression is identifiable and finite: DISH churn accounts for the gap between 0.0% reported US & Canada organic tenant billings growth in Q3 and about 4% without it, and management expects growth to accelerate in 2027 — at which point US growth re-accelerates by several percentage points with no new leasing required. Meanwhile CoreSite revenue grew 17.4% in Q3 with record leasing expected for 2026, and the company has raised guidance three times in 2026. Every one of 25 covering analysts has a target above the current price.

Bear Case

Strip out the currency noise and this is a business growing organic tenant billings at 1.1% with AFFO/share guided to +3.2% at the midpoint, carrying $35.7B of net debt at 4.9x leverage, near the top of its 3x–5x target range, earning a 7.90% ROIC against a marginal refinancing cost that has risen sharply since the coupons on its existing stack were set. The US tenant market is three carriers and shrinking; Latin America is contractionary at −3% guided; the international portfolio is being sold down piece by piece, which removes volatility but also removes growth. Data centers are the only genuine growth engine and they are ~10% of revenue while consuming ~38% of capex. If long rates stay elevated, the multiple does not recover and you are left clipping a 4% coupon on a low-single-digit grower whose shares are still 28.5% below their November 2021 close.

Top 3 Risks

01
Churn does not roll off on schedule. The entire bull case rests on 2027 US organic tenant billings growth re-accelerating from ~0.5% as Sprint/T-Mobile and DISH/EchoStar decommissioning laps. If carriers extend the wind-down, or if a further consolidation event removes another tenant, the "temporary" selloff becomes the permanent growth rate and the current 16.9x forward P/AFFO is not cheap — it is fair value for a 1% grower.
02
Rates stay higher for longer and the refinancing wall bites. $37.58B of gross debt at 4.9x net leverage (Sep 30, 2026); debt matures in tranches, which protects the near term but means the repricing keeps arriving; the September 2026 notes priced at 5.300%–5.750%. Rising interest expense subtracts directly from AFFO, and a higher discount rate simultaneously compresses the multiple applied to it — the same variable hits both the numerator and the denominator.
03
Emerging-market currency and Latin American contraction. About 38% of FY2025 revenue is non-US, and FY2025 alone swung ~$1.1B in reported results on translation ($809.4M loss vs a $308.3M prior-year gain). LatAm organic tenant billings are guided to ~−3% on elevated Brazil churn. Continued divestitures reduce the exposure but also shrink the asset base, and each sale removes a growth market in exchange for a one-time deleveraging benefit.
  Industry Commentary
Interest Rates & the Valuation of Long-Duration REITs
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Tower REITs are the closest thing in equities to a levered, inflation-linked perpetual bond: contractual revenue, ~3% escalators, 20-year asset lives, and about a third of enterprise value funded by debt. That construction makes them exquisitely sensitive to the long end of the curve, in both directions and through two separate channels. Channel one is the discount rate — AMT went from $292.50 in December 2021 to $164.45 in September 2023, a 43.8% decline, with no deterioration in tenant billings; the cash flows did not change, the rate used to value them did. Channel two is interest expense — $37.58B of gross debt against $7.33B of annualized Q3 adjusted EBITDA means every percentage point of incremental refinancing cost compounds into AFFO as maturities roll. The company has done what it can: $10.6B of liquidity and leverage held at 4.9x at Sep 30, 2026. What it cannot do is create growth fast enough to outrun a structurally higher cost of capital. The 7.90% ROIC against September 2026 new-issue coupons of 5.30%–5.75% leaves a spread, but a much thinner one than the business was built on.

The Carrier Capex Cycle & Consolidation Churn
ONGOING HEADWIND

The 5G capex wave that funded 2019–2022 leasing has passed its peak, and the US carrier market has simultaneously consolidated from a four-plus-player field to three. These are different problems that arrived together. Lower capex means fewer new amendments; consolidation means negative leasing as acquired networks are decommissioned — Sprint's absorption into T-Mobile, and DISH/EchoStar's retreat from its build-out. The arithmetic in Q3 2026: 0.0% US & Canada organic tenant billings growth against about 4% excluding DISH churn (CFO, Q3 call), with FY2026 guided to ~0.5%. The same disease shows up across the sector — Crown Castle's trailing revenue is $4.08B (the previous edition showed $4.16B) and its stock is −2.9% over twelve months to Oct 30, 2026, against SBA at −2.1% and AMT at +4.6% (prices only). Latin America is running the same playbook a cycle later, with Brazil consolidation churn pushing that segment to −2.1% organic in Q3 2026 and ~−3% guided for the year. The critical unknown is duration: management says 2026 is the low point for US & Canada and growth accelerates in 2027. That claim, not the 5G narrative, is what the equity is actually underwriting.

Data Centers & Edge Compute — The Growth Pivot, and What It Decides
EMERGING TAILWIND

CoreSite is the swing factor in how this stock gets valued for the rest of the decade. Interconnection-dense colocation has become strategically important in the AI cycle — models trained in bulk hyperscale facilities still have to be served close to networks and users, which is precisely what CoreSite's footprint does. The evidence so far is real: $313M of Q3 2026 revenue, +17.4% YoY, about 16% full-year growth guided (up from about 15% at Q2), and $695M of 2026 development capex behind it. The bull framing: a ~10%-of-revenue segment growing at 3–4x the group rate, funded by 38% of capex, eventually becomes large enough to lift group AFFO growth from ~3% into mid-single digits — at which point the market may stop valuing AMT as a bond proxy and starts valuing it alongside Equinix and Digital Realty, whose industry peers trade on a 37.7x average P/E versus AMT's 26.4x. The bear framing: 10% of revenue cannot offset 49% of revenue growing at zero, the capital is being diverted into a market with well-capitalized incumbents and real power constraints, and if AI-adjacent leasing normalizes American Tower will have spent several billion dollars to arrive at the same flat growth profile with less financial flexibility. The next four quarters of CoreSite leasing decide which of those is true.
