01 Company Overview

Amazon.com is the world's largest e-commerce company and largest cloud infrastructure provider, operating a retail marketplace that captures roughly 40% of US online spending alongside Amazon Web Services (AWS), the #1 cloud platform with ~30% global share. Full-year 2025 revenue reached $716.9B, up 12% YoY, and the trailing twelve months through Q2 2026 hit $775.7B (+15.8%) as AWS reaccelerated to 37% growth — its fastest in 18 quarters. Amazon crossed the $3 trillion market cap threshold in early August 2026, ranking approximately #5 globally, and is the largest US company by revenue (#1 on the Fortune 500 by sales among tech peers).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat

AWS Switching Costs
Marketplace Network Effects

First-Party Shopper Data → Ads
Logistics Cost Advantage

Amazon's moat is a set of interlocking flywheels no single competitor replicates end-to-end. The fulfillment network (1,000+ facilities, the largest last-mile fleet in the US) makes same-day delivery an entry barrier measured in hundreds of billions of dollars; Prime converts that speed into subscription lock-in and higher purchase frequency; marketplace scale attracts 2M+ third-party sellers who fund the network via fees and then buy ads against unmatched first-party purchase data. On the cloud side, AWS benefits from classic enterprise switching costs — data gravity, retraining, and re-architecture — now reinforced by custom silicon: Trainium offers AI training at a claimed 30–40% better price-performance than comparable GPU (graphics processing unit) instances, and the $8B+ Anthropic partnership anchors multi-year committed demand. The result: a company where the low-margin retail business finances infrastructure, and infrastructure (AWS + ads, roughly 75%+ of operating income) generates the profits.

Key Vulnerabilities
›
Cloud share erosion. Azure has grown faster than AWS for most of the past three years and Microsoft's OpenAI distribution gives it an enterprise AI wedge; Google Cloud and Oracle are winning AI workloads too. AWS's Q2 2026 reacceleration to 37% narrows but does not close the growth gap — sustained share loss would compress the multiple on Amazon's most profitable business.
›
Regulatory overhang. The FTC's (Federal Trade Commission) monopolization suit — alleging anti-discounting tactics and coercive fulfillment bundling — is scheduled for trial in February 2027. The EU's Digital Markets Act imposes ongoing conduct remedies, and Amazon already paid $2.5B in 2025 to settle the FTC's Prime enrollment case. Structural remedies are a tail risk; conduct remedies could still dent marketplace and ads economics.
›
Capex / AI overbuild risk. TTM capex of ~$173B (up 64% YoY) has pushed free cash flow negative. If AI inference demand grows slower than the buildout assumes, Amazon owns a depreciating mountain of servers — depreciation is already the fastest-growing expense line, and useful-life assumptions on AI hardware are untested through a downcycle.
›
Low-cost cross-border disruption. Temu and Shein reset consumer price expectations in discretionary categories. Amazon's answer (Amazon Haul, direct-from-China listings) defends share but at dilutive margins, and tariff/de-minimis policy swings cut both ways — helping against Chinese rivals but raising Amazon's own first-party and seller costs.

From roughly $174 in August 2021, AMZN was cut in half by the post-COVID normalization and rate shock, bottoming at $84 in December 2022 (−52% from the start, −56% from the 2021 peak) as e-commerce growth stalled and 2022 brought Amazon's first annual loss since 2014 (Rivian write-downs). That trails the NASDAQ-100 (~+90–100% over the same window, boosted by semiconductors) and roughly matches or slightly beats the S&P 500 (~+55–65%) — nearly all of AMZN's net 5-year gain arrived in the last 13 months. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

AWS is compounding at 37% with a 39% margin and a $169B run rate, advertising is growing 26% at software-like margins, and retail margins are still early in their robotics/regionalization expansion — three simultaneous margin flywheels. If AI demand holds, today's $173B capex converts into years of high-return revenue, Trainium cuts the cost curve versus GPU-dependent rivals, and Kuiper adds an option on a new business. At ~1.47 PEG with a $324.94 consensus target, the growth is arguably not fully priced.

Bear Case

Free cash flow is negative for the first time in a decade, debt is up $63B in six months, and the stock trades 3% off all-time highs with a monthly RSI near 80 — priced for AI perfection just as guidance implies revenue decelerating to 9–12%. Any AI digestion phase strands capacity and crushes the AWS multiple; Azure is still growing at least as fast off a similar base; and the February 2027 FTC trial hangs over the marketplace/ads profit pools. Reported earnings quality is flattered by a $53B paper gain.

1.
AI capex-return mismatch. ~$173B TTM capex (+64% YoY) against negative FCF is the single biggest swing factor — if AWS growth slows before the buildout pays back, both earnings and the multiple compress together.
2.
Antitrust outcomes. FTC v. Amazon goes to trial February 2027; adverse conduct remedies could raise seller churn, cap take rates (~50%+ effective fees per Marketplace Pulse estimates), and limit ad placement economics. EU DMA compliance costs continue regardless.
3.
Consumer and tariff sensitivity. ~75% of revenue still touches the consumer; tariff pass-through, a softening labor market, or trade-policy whiplash hits units, seller economics, and ad budgets simultaneously — with a 1.45 beta amplifying the stock's reaction.
  Industry Commentary — What Matters Right Now
The Hyperscaler Capex Supercycle Is Eating Free Cash Flow
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The big four cloud providers will spend an estimated $450–500B+ on AI infrastructure in 2026; Amazon alone is running ~$173B TTM and guiding higher (estimates ran ~$16B ahead of consensus at mid-year). Amazon's TTM FCF is negative $7.6–11.6B (source-dependent) versus +$38B two years ago, and long-term debt rose $63B in six months to ~$129B. Power, not chips, is becoming the binding constraint — AWS has raised EC2 prices twice in 2026 on scarcity. The entire sector's equity story now depends on inference demand materializing on schedule; depreciation from this vintage of spend will pressure margins through 2028 either way.

Enterprise AI Demand Is Broadening Beyond Model Labs
EMERGING TAILWIND

Cloud infrastructure spending grew ~28% YoY on a ~$390B base, and AWS's re-acceleration from 12% (2023 trough) to 37% (Q2 2026) shows the AI spend translating into hyperscaler revenue, not just NVIDIA's. Amazon says AI and custom chips each exceed $25B revenue run rates, and the Anthropic relationship (equity stake now marked up by $53.4B) doubles as an anchor customer for Trainium — a hedge against GPU cost inflation that Microsoft and Google are replicating less aggressively. Retail media (Amazon's ad model) is simultaneously the fastest-growing category in advertising, pulling budgets from search and linear TV at 40–50%+ incremental margins.

Tariffs, the Value Consumer, and the Regulatory Clock — Bull vs Bear Frame
ONGOING HEADWIND

Trade policy keeps repricing e-commerce economics: de-minimis elimination hurt Temu/Shein more than Amazon, but tariffs raise Amazon's own costs and its sellers' costs, and the value-seeking consumer is trading down. Meanwhile the FTC trial clock (Feb 2027) starts discounting into the stock in coming quarters. Bull frame: Amazon is the share gainer in every storm — scale absorbs tariffs, Haul blunts discounters, ads and AWS make retail margin pressure almost irrelevant to consolidated earnings, and consensus sees $1T+ revenue by 2028. Bear frame: a 29.5x forward P/E with negative FCF, decelerating H2 guidance (9–12%), an overbought tape, and a courtroom date is a fragile combination if any leg — AI demand, the consumer, or the legal calendar — breaks the wrong way.
