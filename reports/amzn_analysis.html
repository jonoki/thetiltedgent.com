01 Company Overview

Amazon.com is the world's largest online retailer and the largest cloud-computing provider: next to a retail marketplace that captures roughly 40% of US online spending, it runs Amazon Web Services (AWS), which rents computing power and AI services to businesses. Full-year 2025 revenue reached $716.9B, up 12% YoY, and revenue over the twelve months to September 2026 was $797.5B (+15.4%). In Q3 2026 AWS grew 39%, its fastest pace in nearly five years per Amazon, and the company's 13.9% operating margin (operating profit as a share of revenue) was its highest ever. The strain is cash: Amazon expects to spend about $220B on capital projects in 2026, mostly AI data centers, so over the last twelve months it spent $42.4B more than its operations brought in. The stock passed a $3 trillion market value in early August 2026; at the Nov 2 close it is worth about $2.66T.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Fulfillment Network Scale
Prime Flywheel
AWS Switching Costs
Marketplace Network Effects

Logistics Cost Advantage

Amazon's moat is a set of interlocking flywheels no single competitor copies end to end. The fulfillment network makes same-day delivery an entry barrier measured in hundreds of billions of dollars; Prime turns that speed into subscription lock-in and more frequent orders; marketplace scale draws third-party sellers who pay fees to use the network and then buy ads against Amazon's own purchase data. On the cloud side, AWS customers face classic switching costs (moving their data, retraining staff, rebuilding software), now reinforced by Amazon's own chips, which Jassy said saved customers more than $2B in Q3 2026 against the GPUs (graphics processing units) most AI work runs on, and by Anthropic's pledge of more than $100B of AWS spending over ten years. The result: the low-margin retail business finances the infrastructure, and AWS alone produced 58% of segment operating income over the twelve months to September 2026.

Key Vulnerabilities
›
Cloud share erosion. Azure has grown faster than AWS for most of the past three years and Microsoft's OpenAI ties give it an opening with corporate AI buyers; Google Cloud and Oracle are winning AI workloads too. In the September quarter Azure grew 48% and Google Cloud 103% (Microsoft and Alphabet releases), against 39% at AWS, so AWS's acceleration narrows the gap with Azure but does not close it; sustained share loss would cut what investors pay for Amazon's most profitable business.
›
Regulatory overhang. The FTC's (Federal Trade Commission) 2023 monopolization suit — alleging anti-discounting tactics and coercive fulfillment bundling — goes to trial on March 29, 2027; on Oct 9, 2026 the judge refused Amazon's request to move it to June (MLex). A second suit, filed Aug 31, 2026 by the FTC and 22 states, alleges Amazon overcharged advertisers by more than $20B since 2019 through hidden reserve prices in its ad auctions; Amazon calls it misguided and says it will defend itself (CNBC; Q3 2026 10-Q). The EU's Digital Markets Act adds conduct rules, and Amazon paid $2.5B in 2025 to settle the FTC's Prime enrollment case. A break-up is an unlikely worst case; court-ordered limits on how Amazon runs the marketplace could still dent its marketplace and ad profits.
›
Capex / AI overbuild risk. Capex over the twelve months to September 2026 was $210.7B net of proceeds, up $94.8B on a year earlier, turning free cash flow into a $42.4B outflow, and Jassy expects to spend meaningfully more in 2027. If demand for running AI models grows slower than the buildout assumes, Amazon owns a fast-depreciating fleet of servers whose useful lives are untested through a downturn, and long-term debt has already risen to $173.1B from $65.6B at the end of 2025.
›
Low-cost cross-border disruption. Temu and Shein reset consumer price expectations in discretionary categories. Amazon's answer (Amazon Haul, direct-from-China listings) defends share but at lower margins, and swings in tariffs and the de-minimis rule (the US duty exemption for small parcels) cut both ways — helping against Chinese rivals but raising Amazon's own first-party and seller costs.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

AWS is growing 39% at a 39% margin with a $184B run rate and a $578B backlog, and Amazon projects over 40% growth in both 2027 and 2028; advertising is guided back to high-teens growth in Q4; and retail margins are still widening as delivery gets faster and cheaper. If AI demand holds, today's ~$220B a year of capex turns into years of high-return revenue, Amazon's own chips cut costs against rivals that rely on bought GPUs, and Amazon Leo adds an option on a new business. At 26.8x the FY2026 adjusted EPS consensus and a $327.08 average target, 33% above the price, the growth is arguably not fully priced.

Bear Case

Free cash flow over twelve months is a $42.4B outflow, long-term debt has risen $107.5B this year to $173.1B, and 2027 capex will be meaningfully higher again. The stock is 14.5% below its August high and up only 0.6% in a year against 10.3% for SPY. Any pause in AI demand strands capacity and hits the value investors put on AWS; Azure is still growing fast; and two FTC cases — the March 2027 monopoly trial and the August 2026 ad-pricing suit — hang over the marketplace and ads profit pools. Reported earnings are flattered by tens of billions of dollars of paper gains on Anthropic.

1.
AI capex-return mismatch. $210.7B of TTM capex (net of proceeds, up $94.8B YoY) against a $42.4B FCF outflow is the single biggest swing factor — if AWS growth slows before the buildout pays back, earnings and what investors will pay for them fall together.
2.
Antitrust outcomes. FTC v. Amazon goes to trial March 29, 2027, and the FTC's August 2026 ad-auction suit seeks penalties and restitution; court-ordered limits could push sellers to leave, cap the fees Amazon charges them, and cut what it earns from ad placements. EU DMA compliance costs continue regardless.
3.
Consumer and tariff sensitivity. The two retail segments still bring in 79.8% of revenue (twelve months to September 2026); tariff pass-through, a softening labor market, or trade-policy whiplash hits units, seller economics, and ad budgets simultaneously — with a 1.46 beta amplifying the stock's reaction.
  Industry Commentary — What Matters Right Now
The Big Cloud Providers' AI Spending Is Eating Free Cash Flow
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The big cloud providers are spending hundreds of billions of dollars a year on AI infrastructure; Amazon alone expects about $220B of cash capex in 2026, $75.9B of it in Q3, and meaningfully more in 2027. Its free cash flow over the twelve months to September was an outflow of $42.4B, against an inflow of $14.8B a year earlier, and long-term debt rose to $173.1B from $65.6B at the end of 2025 (including £4.25B of sterling bonds sold in September). The sector's stock-market story now depends on demand for running AI models arriving on schedule; depreciation from this wave of spending will weigh on margins for years either way.

Enterprise AI Demand Is Broadening Beyond Model Labs
EMERGING TAILWIND

Cloud infrastructure spending grew 43% year over year in Q2 2026, to about $500B over twelve months (Synergy Research Group), and AWS's growth climbing from 12% (2023 trough) to 39% (Q3 2026) shows AI spending turning into cloud revenue, not just chip sales for NVIDIA. AWS's backlog of $578B is growing triple digits, Amazon says, and Anthropic, whose stake gave Amazon most of its 2026 investment gains, has pledged more than $100B of AWS spending over ten years, making it an anchor customer for Amazon's Trainium chips and a hedge against the cost of bought GPUs. Retail media (ads sold on a retailer's own site, Amazon's ad model) is among the fastest-growing categories in advertising, pulling budgets from search and TV.

Tariffs, the Value Consumer, and the Regulatory Clock — Bull vs Bear Frame
ONGOING HEADWIND

Trade policy keeps repricing e-commerce economics: ending the de-minimis duty exemption for small parcels hurt Temu and Shein more than Amazon, but tariffs raise Amazon's own costs and its sellers' costs, and the value-seeking consumer is trading down. Meanwhile two FTC cases run on the clock: the monopoly trial on March 29, 2027 and the ad-pricing suit filed in August 2026. Bull frame: Amazon is the share gainer in every storm — scale absorbs tariffs, Haul blunts discounters, and ads and AWS make retail margin pressure almost irrelevant to company-wide earnings. Bear frame: 26.8x FY2026 adjusted earnings with free cash flow deeply negative, 2027 spending set to rise again, and two court cases is a fragile combination if any leg — AI demand, the consumer, or the legal calendar — breaks the wrong way.
