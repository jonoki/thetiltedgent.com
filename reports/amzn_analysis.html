Company Overview

What Amazon actually is in 2026 — and who runs it.

Amazon operates the largest e-commerce marketplace in the Western world and, through Amazon Web Services (AWS), the largest cloud infrastructure business on earth. Trailing-twelve-month revenue reached $775.7B, up +15.8% YoY, and second-quarter 2026 net sales of $200.6B marked the first $200 billion quarter in company history. It is the largest US retailer by online sales (~38% of US e-commerce), the largest cloud provider globally (~30% infrastructure share), the third-largest digital advertiser, and — as of 2026 — the No. 1 company on the Fortune 500 by revenue.

The story of 2026 is a re-rating: AWS growth reaccelerated to +37%, the fastest in 18 quarters, on AI infrastructure demand. Amazon simultaneously became one of the largest holders of frontier-model equity in the world through its Anthropic and OpenAI stakes — a position that both supercharged reported earnings and injected substantial volatility into them.

Notable Executives & Owners

Chief Executive Officer
Andy Jassy

Amazon veteran since 1997 who built AWS from a side project into a ~$169B annualized run-rate business before succeeding Jeff Bezos as CEO in July 2021. Has been a net buyer of the stock, adding roughly 30,000 shares after a recent equity award.

CFO since 2015 and one of the longest-tenured finance chiefs in mega-cap tech; credited by Fortune with steering the cost discipline that lifted operating margin from near-zero in 2022 to 13.7% in Q2 2026 while defending a $220B capital budget.

Bezos · Vanguard · BlackRock

Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Where the durability is — and where it is thinnest.

Sources of Moat

Scale Economics Logistics Network Density Marketplace Network Effects AWS Switching Costs Prime Ecosystem Lock-In Proprietary Data & Closed-Loop Attribution Custom Silicon (Trainium / Graviton) Capital-Intensity Barrier

Amazon's core advantage is that its three big businesses subsidise and reinforce each other in ways a single-purpose competitor cannot match. The retail flywheel — more selection draws more buyers, which draws more third-party sellers, which lowers unit costs and funds faster delivery — is decades old and now nearly impossible to replicate at cost. The genuinely modern moat is capital intensity: a $220B 2026 capital budget and a $169B trailing-twelve-month property-and-equipment spend build a barrier that only three or four companies on earth can clear. On top of that sits switching cost. AWS customers with data gravity, IAM policies, and years of infrastructure-as-code do not migrate casually, and the $496B contracted backlog converts that inertia into visible forward revenue. Finally, vertical integration into silicon — Trainium for training, Inferentia for inference, Graviton for general compute — lets AWS price AI capacity below Nvidia-only competitors while protecting margin, which is precisely how it won a 2-gigawatt commitment from OpenAI.

Key Vulnerabilities

Free Cash Flow Has Gone Negative

Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow swung to roughly −$7.6B from +$18.2B a year earlier, and long-term debt rose $63.2B in six months to $128.9B. Amazon is now funding an AI buildout partly with borrowed money, and several sell-side houses model negative FCF through 2027. If AI cloud demand normalises before the depreciation schedule matures, the return on that capital is exposed.

Cloud Share Is Being Chipped At From Above and Below

AWS's 37% growth was celebrated — but Azure grew 43% and Google Cloud grew 82% in the same quarter. Google Cloud's backlog rose ~390% YoY to $514B, now larger than AWS's $496B. AWS remains roughly 30% of the market against Azure's 24% and Google's 13%, but it is the slowest grower of the three and has been capacity-constrained relative to peers for several quarters.

Regulatory Overhang: FTC Monopolisation Trial

The FTC's structural case in the Western District of Washington before Judge John Chun — alleging Amazon overcharges sellers, retaliates against off-platform price competition, and degrades search results — has been rescheduled to March 2027 after earlier delays. Remedies sought include conduct restrictions on marketplace fee bundling; a worst case touches the third-party seller services line that generates $46.8B a quarter.

Earnings Quality Distorted by Equity-Stake Marks

Q2 2026 net income of $62.6B included $53.4B of non-operating income from revaluing the Anthropic stake, against $15.9B of net discrete tax expense. Those marks are non-cash and reverse in both directions. With a further $50B committed to OpenAI, reported GAAP EPS is now substantially a function of private AI-company valuations, which makes trailing P/E nearly meaningless as a comparison tool.

Retail Consumer & Input-Cost Sensitivity

Roughly 79% of revenue is still retail-linked and exposed to tariffs, discretionary spending, and freight. Management explicitly raised 2026 capex to $220B in part because of higher memory (DRAM/HBM) costs — component inflation is now flowing directly into the cost base of the very business that is supposed to deliver operating leverage.

Notable One-Off Items

Material — earnings are heavily distorted this quarter

1. Anthropic revaluation gain of $53.4B. Q2 2026 non-operating income was driven almost entirely by marking Amazon's Anthropic stake to a higher private valuation. This is non-cash, non-recurring in the accounting sense, and reversible. Net income of $62.6B (+245% YoY) versus operating income of $27.5B (+43%) illustrates the gap.

2. Net discrete tax expense of $15.9B accompanied the revaluation, partially offsetting it.

3. $50B OpenAI investment completed ($15B upfront plus a $35B final tranche closed in July 2026) at a $730B OpenAI valuation. This creates a second large mark-to-market exposure in future quarters.

4. Capex guidance raised to $220B for FY2026, explicitly attributed in part to higher memory-chip costs — a cost-inflation item, not a one-off, but new versus prior guidance.

Practical implication: use operating income, operating margin and free cash flow to judge the business. Trailing P/E, net margin and ROE are all currently overstated.

Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

AWS has reaccelerated to 37% with a $496B backlog and a 39.4% operating margin, meaning the AI capex is showing up as contracted revenue rather than speculative capacity. Advertising compounding at 26% off an ~$80B run rate is the highest-margin dollar in the company and still under-monetised in streaming and sports. At 27x forward earnings — roughly 44% below its own ten-year average and in line with the NASDAQ-100 — investors are paying an index multiple for the only company that is simultaneously #1 in cloud, #1 in Western e-commerce, and #3 in digital advertising.

Bear Case

Free cash flow is negative $7.6B and heading lower against a $220B capital budget funded partly with $63B of new debt in six months, while ROIC of 11.9% sits well below the index at ~19% — the returns on this buildout are unproven. Reported earnings are unreliable: $53.4B of Q2 profit was a non-cash Anthropic mark, and a further $50B is now tied to OpenAI's private valuation. Meanwhile AWS is the slowest-growing of the big three clouds, Google Cloud's backlog has passed it, and the stock is at an all-time high with 59 of 62 analysts positive and almost no short interest — sentiment is as good as it gets.

Top 3 Risks

  1. The AI capex cycle fails to earn its cost of capital. $220B of 2026 spend converts into depreciation over the following three to five years. If AI cloud demand normalises before that base is absorbed — or if memory and power costs keep inflating — operating margin compresses just as the depreciation peaks, and negative free cash flow extends well past 2027.
  2. Cloud share erosion to Azure and Google Cloud. AWS grew 37% while Azure grew 43% and Google Cloud grew 82%, with Google's backlog now larger than AWS's. Amazon has been capacity-constrained for several quarters. Losing the frontier-model workload tier would undermine both the growth rate and the pricing power that justifies today's multiple.
  3. Regulatory action on marketplace economics. The FTC monopolisation trial in March 2027 targets exactly the seller-fee and price-parity mechanics that generate $46.8B a quarter at high incremental margin. Even a conduct-only remedy that unbundles FBA from Buy Box eligibility would hit the most profitable part of the retail business, and a separate FTC Prime case remains live.

Industry Commentary

The macro and sector dynamics that matter most to Amazon right now.

Compute and Memory Supply Is the Binding Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Every hyperscaler is now supply-limited rather than demand-limited. Jassy stated plainly that AI demand will outpace supply through 2027. Amazon raised its 2026 capital budget to $220B — up from $169B trailing, itself +64% YoY — and explicitly attributed part of the increase to higher memory prices (DRAM and HBM, the high-bandwidth memory stacked next to AI accelerators). This is a genuine cost shock working through the whole sector: it raises the capital required per unit of AI capacity, lengthens payback, and pushes free cash flow negative across the industry. Google Cloud posted its first negative quarterly free cash flow in years for the same reason. The strategic response — vertical integration into Trainium and Graviton silicon — is the main reason AWS can commit 2 gigawatts of capacity to OpenAI at a price Nvidia-only competitors would struggle to match.

Cloud Is Accelerating, Not Maturing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The consensus assumption that cloud infrastructure was a maturing, mid-teens-growth market has been decisively broken. Enterprise cloud spend hit ~$129B in Q1 2026 alone, up ~35% YoY — the ninth consecutive quarter of accelerating growth — against a total market of roughly $900B. AI now accounts for ~19% of cloud spending, up from 8% in 2023, and it is lifting the whole market rather than redistributing share. That is why all three hyperscalers accelerated in the same quarter (AWS 37%, Azure 43%, Google Cloud 82%) — a pattern that essentially never happens in a share-war. For Amazon this matters more than for peers, because AWS supplies roughly 60% of consolidated operating income from 21% of revenue.

Retail Demand, Tariffs and the Rate Path
ONGOING HEADWIND

Roughly 79% of Amazon's revenue is still tied to retail and advertising, both of which key off consumer spending. The July 2026 non-farm payroll print came in at 57,000 against a 110,000 forecast — soft enough to raise Fed cut expectations, which supports long-duration equity valuations through a lower discount rate but signals weakening end demand for the retail business. Tariff policy remains a live input cost for imported goods and has already produced one 22% drawdown in this stock (spring 2025). The pattern to watch is whether AWS strength can continue to offset retail softness the way it did in Q2.

Framing the Debate: Bull vs Bear on the Same Facts
THE CORE DISAGREEMENT

Both sides of this trade agree on the numbers and disagree entirely on what they mean. The bull reads $220B of capex and $496B of backlog as the same fact: Amazon is pre-selling capacity it has not yet built, negative free cash flow is a timing artefact of growth, and 39.4% AWS margins prove the unit economics work even mid-buildout. The bear reads $220B of capex and 11.9% ROIC as the same fact: Amazon is capitalising an arms race it cannot exit, the backlog is concentrated in a handful of AI labs whose own funding is circular (Amazon invested $50B in OpenAI, which then committed ~$100B back to AWS), and negative FCF plus $63B of new debt in six months is what a capacity trap looks like at the top. The resolution arrives not at the next print but roughly eight quarters out, when the 2026 capex vintage is fully in the depreciation line and either AWS revenue has outrun it or it has not.
