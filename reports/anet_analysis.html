01 Company Overview

Arista Networks designs and sells high-performance Ethernet switching, routing, and network software for AI clusters, cloud data centers, campus networks, and service-provider backbones — all running a single, unmodified software image called EOS (Extensible Operating System, a Linux-based network OS with a published state database). Inside the giant data centers of the hyperscalers (the largest cloud companies) it has become the default alternative to Cisco built on chips bought from outside suppliers, known as merchant silicon, and it is among the largest Ethernet suppliers to AI back-end fabrics, which until recently were dominated by InfiniBand. IDC put it at 18.7% of data-center Ethernet switch sales in Q2 2026, behind Nvidia at 20.4% and ahead of Cisco at 18.2%. FY2025 revenue was $9.01B, up 28.6% YoY, and the business has since accelerated: TTM revenue through Q3 2026 is $11.89B, up 40.7% YoY, with Q3 2026 alone at $3.66B (+58.5%), the fastest growth in its history as a public company. Management now guides 2026 revenue to $13.3B and 2027 to about $18.5B. At roughly $216B of market capitalization Arista joined the S&P 100 in September 2026, remarkable for a firm with just 5,115 employees (roughly $2.32M of TTM revenue per employee).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Single EOS Binary Image CloudVision Lock-In Merchant Silicon Leverage Hyperscaler Design-Ins 20-Year Codebase, No Forks $2.32M Revenue / Employee Debt-Free, $14.4B Net Cash

Arista's moat is architectural rather than legal. Because EOS is a single, unmodified software image running across every platform — from a 48-port campus switch to a 400-terabit AI spine — a hyperscaler qualifies the software once and inherits it everywhere, and Arista avoids maintaining the many separate software versions that make Cisco's products expensive to support. That structural choice is why 5,115 employees can support $11.9B of trailing revenue and 44% operating margins while spending far less on R&D in absolute dollars than Cisco or Nvidia. The second layer is the qualification cycle itself: getting a switch into a Microsoft or Meta AI fabric takes long joint engineering, congestion-control tuning, and failure-mode testing; once in, displacing it mid-cluster is operationally reckless. The third layer is merchant silicon — by riding Broadcom's Tomahawk/Jericho roadmap rather than building custom ASICs, Arista converts a fixed cost into a variable one and gets to market on new speed grades (800G, now 1.6T) at least as fast as vertically integrated competitors, while keeping ROIC extraordinarily high. Finally, CloudVision makes customers depend on Arista's management software, and its support and subscription contracts are part of the $6.84B of total deferred revenue. The moat's honest limit: Arista does not own the silicon, so anything Broadcom sells to Arista, Broadcom will also sell to a maker of unbranded switches at a lower price.

Customer Concentration — the dominant single risk

Arista's 10-K says two end customers each passed 10% of revenue in each of the last three years: 26% and 16% in FY2025, up from 20% and 15% in FY2024. The 10-K does not name them; Next Platform's analysis identifies them as Microsoft (26%, about $2.34B) and Meta (16%, about $1.44B). That is about 42% of the company in two customers, up from 35% a year earlier. On the Q3 call CEO Jayshree Ullal said she expects one or two more customers to pass 10% in 2026, making at least three and maybe four. These are also the most sophisticated buyers alive: they design their own racks, publish their own switch specs (SONiC/OCP), and negotiate with full knowledge of Arista's costs.

NVIDIA Vertical Integration

Nvidia sells Spectrum-X Ethernet and NVLink/InfiniBand as part of an integrated rack-scale system (GB200/GB300 NVL72 and successors). When the GPU vendor bundles the fabric, ships it pre-validated, and warrants end-to-end performance, the incremental friction of buying a third-party spine rises. Nvidia's data-center networking revenue reached $14.8B in the quarter ended April 2026, up 199%, more than five times Arista's first-quarter revenue. Arista's counter — open Ethernet, multi-vendor GPU support, and the UEC standard — is credible, but it is a fight against the highest-margin, best-capitalized company in the supply chain.

White-Box / Merchant Silicon Commoditization

Arista's chips come from Broadcom. So do Celestica's, Accton's, and Quanta's. When a hyperscaler runs SONiC (an open-source network OS originally from Microsoft) on an unbranded switch from a contract manufacturer, it captures most of Arista's hardware value at a fraction of the gross margin. GAAP gross margin fell from 64.06% in FY2025 to 62.93% in Q2 2026, then recovered to 63.58% in Q3, and management guides non-GAAP gross margin of 62–64% for 2027 even as revenue grows about 39%. CFO Chantelle Breithaupt said product cost inflation outran supply-chain savings in Q3, with customer mix also a factor. The bull rebuttal is that AI fabrics are hard enough that operators pay for EOS; the bear rebuttal is that this was said about front-end networks too.

Valuation & the Capital-Spending Cycle

At 45.8× trailing earnings and 28.5× the FY2027 consensus estimate (P/E), the share price still assumes several more years of fast growth. Arista's revenue depends almost entirely on other companies' capital budgets, and its two largest customers are among the most-watched capital spenders in the S&P 500. On month-end closes the stock lost 33% from January to March 2025 and 22% from October 2025 to March 2026; from October 9 to November 5, 2026 it fell 27% close to close, including a 10.8% drop two sessions after the fastest-growing quarter in its history as a public company.

Notable One-Off Items

Five distortions are worth carrying in your head when you look at the reported numbers:

1. Tariffs and component costs. The 2025–2026 US tariff regime on imported electronics and components is a live cost. It sits inside cost of goods sold rather than appearing as a one-off. On the Q3 call CFO Chantelle Breithaupt pointed to product cost inflation, only partly offset by supply-chain savings; non-GAAP gross margin was 64.0% against 65.2% a year earlier.

2. Inventory and purchase commitments. Inventory is $3.21B, up from $2.5B a quarter earlier, and non-cancellable purchase commitments reached $11.4B at September 30, 2026 (from $9.7B at June 30), with $9.3B due within 12 months and a multiyear memory agreement among them. This is a deliberate bet on 2027 demand. If a hyperscaler defers, the risk is an inventory write-down and a purchase-commitment charge landing in a single quarter.

3. Stock-based compensation and a one-off gain. Stock-based compensation was $138.1M in Q3 2026, about 3.8% of revenue. Normally that leaves GAAP EPS below non-GAAP EPS. This quarter it ran the other way ($1.22 GAAP against $1.20 non-GAAP) because GAAP net income includes a $134.5M gain on strategic investments, which non-GAAP excludes. The trailing P/E above carries that gain; the consensus EPS behind the forward P/E is non-GAAP.

4. Interest income is a real earnings line. With $14.36B in cash and marketable securities and zero debt, Arista reported $138.6M of non-GAAP other income in Q3, about $555M a year at that pace. At the quarter's 20.9% tax rate that is about $0.09 of EPS a quarter, or roughly 8% of FY2026 consensus EPS (our calculation). It is genuine, but it depends on interest rates rather than on selling switches, and lower rates would quietly trim EPS growth.

5. Stock split. Arista executed a 4-for-1 stock split effective December 2024. Every price, EPS, and range figure on this page is split-adjusted. A pre-split quote near $400 in older commentary corresponds to roughly $100 on today's basis.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Arista is the pure-play toll booth on AI networking at the exact moment Ethernet won the back-end fabric argument against InfiniBand, and growth is accelerating — from +28.6% in FY2025 to +40.7% TTM to +58.5% in Q3 2026, with FY2026 guided to $13.3B (~+48%) and FY2027 to about $18.5B (+38–40%). It does this with 63% gross margins, a 40.0% net margin, $5.02B of free cash flow, zero debt and $14.4B of cash and securities. AI fabric revenue is guided to double to about $7B in 2027 and campus to reach about $2B, and a PEG near 1 says the price is not extended relative to the growth being delivered.

Bear Case

You are paying 45.8× trailing earnings for a hardware company with about 42% of FY2025 revenue in two customers, both of which design their own switches and both of which can pause a build with one budget meeting. Deferred revenue stopped growing in Q3 ($6.8B) while revenue grew 58.5%, management's 2027 gross-margin range of 62–64% sits below the 65.0% non-GAAP margin of the first nine months of 2025, purchase commitments have reached $11.4B, Nvidia sells a validated Ethernet fabric alongside its GPU racks, and unbranded-switch makers buy the same Broadcom silicon Arista does. With 29 of 29 analysts on StockAnalysis at Buy or better and short interest at 0.97% at the Oct 15 settlement, almost everyone who could be bullish already is, and the shares have fallen 22–33% three times since January 2025.

01
Customer concentration. Two end customers, identified by Next Platform as Microsoft (26%) and Meta (16%), were about 42% of FY2025 revenue, up from 35% in FY2024, and Cloud & AI Titans was 48%. The Enterprise sector (32%) is smaller than those two accounts combined, so diversification would not cover both cutting orders together. Management expects at least three and maybe four customers above 10% in 2026, which helps only if the newcomers add to the total rather than replace it.
02
Nvidia's vertical bundle and merchant-silicon commoditization. Nvidia ships Spectrum-X pre-integrated with its rack-scale GPU systems, and its networking revenue reached $14.8B in the quarter ended April 2026, while Celestica, Accton, and Quanta buy the identical Broadcom chips Arista uses and sell them at contract-manufacturer margins with open-source SONiC on top. Arista's differentiation is EOS and CloudVision — real, but using software to defend a hardware price is a position that tends to shrink, visible already in the 1.2-percentage-point gross-margin decline from FY2025 to the last twelve months.
03
A sharp fall on any capital-spending wobble. At 28.5× FY2027 consensus earnings (39.0× FY2026), with consensus modelling about 37% EPS growth into 2027 and nearly every analyst at Buy, the stock is priced for the outlook to be met. Arista has had three falls of 22–33% since January 2025, the latest around its fastest-growing quarter as a public company. A 1.64 beta and a 45.8× trailing P/E mean the downside on a growth disappointment is large: a fall to the ~32× networking-industry average on this page would imply about a 30% price decline at unchanged earnings.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
Ethernet Won the AI Back-End
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two years ago the AI back-end network — the fabric connecting GPUs to each other during training — was overwhelmingly InfiniBand, a proprietary Nvidia/Mellanox technology. That argument is effectively over: the Ultra Ethernet Consortium standardized the congestion-control and lossless-delivery features that InfiniBand used to monopolize, and hyperscalers preferred a fabric they could source from multiple vendors. Arista is a leading beneficiary: management targets about $3.5B of AI fabric revenue in 2026 and about $7B in 2027, and on the Q3 call CEO Jayshree Ullal put the total market Arista can reach above $200B by 2030. The catch is that Nvidia read the same memo and pivoted Spectrum-X to Ethernet, so Arista won the protocol war and now has to win the vendor war inside it.
Big Tech's Capital Spending Is the Whole Model
ONGOING HEADWIND
Arista has no consumer business, and only about 14% of its revenue comes from recurring service contracts; the rest depends on new hardware orders. About 48% of FY2025 revenue came from a handful of Cloud & AI Titans whose combined 2026 AI capital expenditure runs into the hundreds of billions and is set annually by boards that read the same headlines everyone else does. The record is plain: the 33% fall from January to March 2025 and the 22% fall from October 2025 to March 2026 (month-end closes) both came while Arista's own revenue growth was above 27%. Networking is also a late-cycle line item within a data-center build: GPUs are ordered first and switches are installed as clusters come online.
Merchant Silicon Cuts Both Ways — Bull vs. Bear
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Arista's decision not to build its own chips is why it earns 40.0% net margins on 5,115 employees — Broadcom does the silicon R&D, Arista does the software and the systems engineering. The bull framing: that asset-light model produces ~$2.32M of revenue per employee, ~240% reported ROIC (about 29% on total equity), $5.02B of free cash flow, and the ability to ride every Tomahawk/Jericho generation without a fabrication bet — with EOS and CloudVision as the durable differentiator that a white-box vendor cannot copy. The bear framing: Broadcom sells the same chip to Celestica and Accton, Microsoft open-sourced SONiC precisely to commoditize the software layer, and Nvidia now bundles a validated fabric with the GPUs. In that world Arista's 63% gross margin is a target competitors have every reason to attack, and the 1.2 percentage points already lost between FY2025 and the last twelve months is the first data point in a long series rather than a one-time shift in what it sold. Which framing is right is, more than growth or market size, the actual question in this stock at 46× earnings.
