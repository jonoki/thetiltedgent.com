01 Company Overview

Aon plc is one of the two dominant global insurance and reinsurance brokers, sitting between commercial clients who need to transfer risk and the insurers and capital markets who underwrite it. It earns commissions and fees for placing property & casualty and reinsurance cover, and consulting/advisory fees in health benefits and retirement/wealth — an asset-light, capital-efficient model that carries no underwriting risk on its own balance sheet. The company reports through two segments, Risk Capital and Human Capital, but manages and discloses growth through four solution lines: Commercial Risk, Reinsurance, Health and Wealth.

Full-year 2025 revenue was $17.181B, up 9% year over year from $15.698B, with organic revenue growth of 6% — the metric the market actually trades on, since it strips out acquisitions, disposals and currency. Adjusted operating margin expanded to 32.4% from 31.5%, adjusted diluted EPS rose 9% to $17.07, and free cash flow grew 14% to $3.218B. At a $68.2B market cap Aon ranks #188 in the S&P 500 and is the second-largest listed insurance broker by revenue, behind Marsh & McLennan ($17.68B of revenue over the last twelve months, against Marsh's $28.46B and Gallagher's $15.77B, per StockAnalysis).

The most recent quarter (Q3 2026, reported before the open on October 28, 2026) showed revenue of $4.101B, up 3% reported and +6% organic; sales of the NFP Wealth business and Stroz Friedberg took 3 points off the reported figure. Commercial Risk and Reinsurance each grew 7% organically, Health 6% and Wealth 4%. Adjusted operating margin was 27.0%, up 70 bps (0.7 percentage points); adjusted EPS of $3.36 rose 10% and edged the $3.34 consensus on Nasdaq's earnings calendar (a 0.6% surprise). The shares rose 4.4% on the day, closing at $315.72, and 1.9% more the next session. Management said it remains confident in its 2026 guidance on a standalone basis, which leaves out the USI deal's costs (Q3 call, as summarised by MarketBeat).

The bigger event came earlier, on August 31, 2026: Aon agreed to buy USI Insurance Services, a US middle-market broker owned by KKR and other shareholders, for $17.0B ($16.7B after about $278M of tax benefits), roughly 14.5 times USI's trailing adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation) including expected savings. USI has about $3B of annual revenue and more than 10,500 staff. Aon is paying with debt, not shares: it sold $13.5B of senior notes in mid-September and has paused buybacks. Aon expects the deal to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals, to make about $395M of annual savings and extra revenue by its own estimate, to dilute adjusted EPS in 2027 and to add to it from 2028 (Aon release, Aug 31, 2026; Insurance Business for the 2027 dilution). The shares fell 9.5% that day, from $355.40 to $321.52, and ended September at $276.77 before recovering to $321.67 by October 30.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
GLOBAL DUOPOLY SCALE
PROPRIETARY RISK DATA
CAT MODELLING & ANALYTICS
MULTI-YEAR CLIENT STICKINESS
AON BUSINESS SERVICES PLATFORM
REINSURANCE MARKET ACCESS
ASSET-LIGHT / NO UNDERWRITING RISK

Aon's moat is scale and data that feed each other and are very hard to rebuild. Global corporates and insurance carriers need a broker that can place a program across dozens of jurisdictions and hundreds of markets simultaneously; only two or three firms can do that, and the placement volume Aon sees gives it a proprietary view of loss experience, pricing and terms that it monetises back to clients as analytics and catastrophe modelling. Retention is structurally high because switching brokers mid-program is operationally disruptive and risks coverage gaps, and because the broker's institutional knowledge of a client's claims history is genuinely difficult to transfer. The economics are exceptional: 48.4% gross margin, 22.2% net margin, 43.4% ROE and 16.5% ROIC on a business that requires essentially no fixed capital and takes no underwriting risk. Aon Business Services — the shared technology, data and operations layer sitting under both segments — is the internal mechanism that converts that scale into margin, and management credits it with funding growth investments. Layered on top is the 3x3 Plan, a three-year strategic roadmap to accelerate the Aon United strategy through targeted investment in talent, technology and analytics, coupled with the Accelerating Aon United restructuring program.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Commission revenue is levered to a pricing cycle Aon does not control. Roughly half of revenue sits in Commercial Risk, where a large share of income is a percentage of premium. Marsh's Global Insurance Market Index showed global commercial rates down 5% in Q3 2026, the ninth consecutive quarterly decline (after 6% in Q2), with property down 10% and cyber down 4% (thirteenth straight quarterly decline); US rates fell 1%, against 2% in Q2. Management argues organic growth tracks nominal GDP more than price, and the record supports that so far (6% organic in Q3), but the arithmetic of a shrinking premium base is a persistent drag while insurance capacity remains abundant.

2. Balance sheet leverage, now from two deals. The $13.0B NFP deal was funded with $7.0B of cash and assumed liabilities plus $6.0B of Aon equity (19.0 million shares). Total debt came down from $17.016B at end-2024 to $15.249B at end-2025, then jumped: Aon's Q3 10-Q shows $28.04B of borrowings at September 30, 2026 ($26.32B long-term and $1.72B short-term), after $13.4B of new debt in nine months, mostly the September bond sale for USI. On StockAnalysis's measure (which also counts lease obligations) the debt-to-equity ratio is 2.83, more than three times the 0.78 peer median. Aon says it will put debt repayment first after the USI close and aims for 2.8–3.0 times debt to EBITDA within about 24 months; S&P Global Ratings affirmed its A- rating but moved the outlook to negative, and Fitch placed the rating on its new notes on negative watch (Insurance Business). It also limits buybacks (none in Q3) and further M&A, and means Aon's high ROE is partly a product of a thin equity base.

3. Fiduciary investment income shrinks as rates fall. Brokers hold client premium in fiduciary accounts and keep the interest. That was a windfall in the high-rate years and is now reversing: full-year 2025 fiduciary investment income was $271 million, and Q3 2026 fell 8% year over year to $69 million (from $75 million; $182 million for nine months against $208 million), as lower interest rates more than offset higher average balances. This is high-margin, essentially costless revenue, so every dollar lost drops straight out of operating income.

4. Key-person and execution risk around the CFO seat. Edmund Reese left as CFO effective immediately on Aug 17, 2026, and the seat was still held on an interim basis at the October 30 close. Replacing a finance chief while closing a $17B acquisition, running a multi-year restructuring program and refinancing a much larger debt load widens the range of outcomes on cost and cash-flow targets; Aon reaffirmed its 2026 guidance with the CFO announcement and, on the Q3 call, said it remains confident in its standalone 2026 guidance (MarketBeat summary).

Notable One-Off Items

Aon's GAAP numbers carry meaningful non-recurring distortion, and the gap between the 25.3% FY2025 GAAP operating margin and the 32.4% adjusted operating margin is almost entirely these items:

Accelerating Aon United restructuring. FY2025 program expense was $365 million ($129M of it in Q4 2025). Program expense was $93 million in Q3 2026 against $32 million a year earlier, mainly from workforce changes, and $281 million for the nine months (against $236 million). Net savings were $20 million in Q3 (Q3 release), which added about 50 basis points to the adjusted margin, and Aon still targets $100 million of savings in 2026 (Q3 call, MarketBeat summary). The 2025 10-K puts annualized savings at about $450 million by the end of 2027. Charges should taper as the program winds down, which would lift GAAP earnings into 2027.

NFP transaction and integration costs. $77 million in FY2025 (down to $11 million in Q4 2025) — meaningfully lower than the 2024 run-rate, indicating the acquisition-cost drag is largely behind the P&L.

USI transaction costs. Aon booked $25 million of USI transaction costs in Q3 2026 and expects about $160 million in total, mostly in the third and fourth quarters (Q3 call, MarketBeat summary). Fourth-quarter interest expense is expected to be about $410 million, against $206 million in Q3, because of the new debt. The deal's costs and the interest are left out of the standalone 2026 guidance.

NFP Wealth business disposal. Aon divested NFP's wealth arm. Q2 2026 free cash flow fell 34% year over year to $483 million, and for the first half Aon pointed to the cash tax payment on the NFP Wealth sale and working capital (Q2 release). Q3 free cash flow recovered to $1.097B (+2%).

Currency. Aon expects currency to be a negligible effect on adjusted EPS in Q4 2026 and a favorable effect of about $0.42 per share for full-year 2026 (Q3 release; $0.02 in Q3 itself). That is roughly 2% of expected 2026 EPS, currency rather than operations — worth adjusting for when comparing adjusted EPS growth to organic revenue growth.

Reported-versus-organic gap. FY2025 reported revenue growth of 9.4% ($17.181B against $15.698B) against 6% organic meant roughly a third of headline growth came from acquisitions and currency. In 2026 the gap has reversed: the first nine months show +4% reported against +5% organic, because the NFP Wealth and Stroz Friedberg sales are removing revenue. In Wealth Solutions Q3 revenue fell 21% reported while growing 4% organically.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Aon is growing organic revenue 5–6% a year and expanding adjusted margins while commercial insurance prices fall — nine consecutive quarters of rate declines, property down 10% in Q3 (Marsh) — which is the strongest available evidence that the business is driven by exposure growth, new business and analytics rather than premium inflation. New business added 11 percentage points to Q3 organic growth and has added 9–11 points for ten straight quarters, with retention in the mid-90% range (Q3 call, MarketBeat summary). It does this at 48% gross margin, 22% net margin and 16% ROIC with no underwriting risk and almost no capital intensity, and it trades at 17.6× trailing and 16.8× forward earnings, a discount to both its peer median and the index. NFP and now USI would create what Aon calls a $6.5B US middle-market platform, where commissions are higher and retention stickier, with $395M of run-rate gains targeted by Aon's own estimate, and the stock has already given back a third of its value from the February 2025 high at the September low.

Bear Case

Roughly half of revenue is a percentage of a premium base that is shrinking, and there is no visible end to the soft market while capacity is abundant. Fiduciary investment income — high-margin, costless revenue — is down 8% in Q3 and falls further with every rate cut. Most of the current margin gain is restructuring savings (about 50 of Q3's 70 bps), an engine that switches off in 2027. The balance sheet now carries about $28B of debt, a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.83 against a 0.78 peer median, S&P has moved its outlook to negative and Fitch has put its rating on the new notes on negative watch; Aon has stopped buying back stock and says USI will dilute adjusted EPS in 2027, so earnings per share growth depends on synergies arriving on schedule. The CFO seat is still interim during a $17B integration. At an average target of $360 the street is paying for a recovery that depends on deal execution.

01
The soft P&C market outlasts the offsets. Global commercial rates fell 5% in Q3 2026, the ninth straight quarterly decline, with property −10% and cyber −4%; only casualty is rising (+1%). Aon's organic growth has held at 6% so far, but if declines steepen or new business slows, commission compression flows through with almost no cost offset. Commercial Risk is 49.4% of revenue.

Once the program completes, Aon must deliver margin progress from growth, USI synergies and Aon Business Services productivity alone — an unproven proposition in a soft market, and the point at which the CFO vacancy matters most.
  Industry Commentary
The P&C Soft Market Is Now Structural, Not Cyclical Noise
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Marsh's Global Insurance Market Index (released Oct 21, 2026) put global commercial insurance rates down 5% in Q3 2026 — the ninth consecutive quarterly decline, after a 6% fall in Q2. Property fell 10% and cyber 4% in its thirteenth straight quarter of declines, while casualty rose 1%. Every region was down year on year: IMEA −14%, UK −8%, Canada, Europe and Asia −6% each, US −1% (against −2% in Q2). Marsh attributes the decline to ample capacity and insurer competition, with cheaper reinsurance also a factor. For a broker, this is a direct hit to the commission base. Aon's counter-argument is that organic growth correlates with client capital investment and nominal GDP rather than with rate, and its numbers back that up so far — 6% organic in both 2024 and 2025, 6% in Q3 2026. The drag is arithmetic and persistent; the narrower US decline is the only sign of the pace easing.
Rate Cuts Are Draining Fiduciary Investment Income
ONGOING HEADWIND
Brokers earn interest on client premium held in fiduciary accounts between collection and remittance. It is pure-margin revenue with essentially no associated cost, which made it a big boost to earnings through the high-rate years — Aon booked $271 million in FY2025. It is now reversing. Q3 2026 fiduciary investment income was $69 million, down 8% year over year (from $75 million), as lower interest rates more than offset higher average balances. The mechanic is unforgiving in both directions: because there is no cost base, every dollar of lost fiduciary income falls straight out of operating income and works against the guided margin expansion. It also compounds with the soft market — falling premiums mean smaller fiduciary balances and lower rates on them. Lower rates do reduce interest expense on floating debt, but Aon's borrowings are now mostly fixed-rate notes, including the $13.5B sold in September 2026 at 5.35% to 6.45%, and Q3 interest expense of $206 million was flat on a year earlier, with about $410 million expected in Q4.
Broker Consolidation Into the Middle Market — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND
The defining industry trend is the large brokers buying up the fragmented US middle market, where commission rates are higher, competition is less price-transparent, and retention is stickier than in global large-account broking. Aon bought that position wholesale with NFP for $13.0B in April 2024 and agreed on Aug 31, 2026 to add USI for $17.0B; on the Q3 call Aon described the combination as a $6.5B US middle-market platform (MarketBeat summary). Gallagher (+30.5% TTM revenue growth) and Brown & Brown (+27.5%) are executing the same playbook with their own M&A, which bids up prices for everyone.

Bull framing: the middle market is genuinely less rate-sensitive, so consolidating into it is the correct structural hedge against the soft cycle, and Aon trades at 16.8× forward earnings. Bear framing: the strategy is capital-hungry at a time when Aon carries the most debt for its equity of the five big listed brokers here (debt-to-equity 2.83 vs a 0.78 peer median), buying firm after firm makes underlying growth harder to judge from outside, and rising purchase prices mean new deals earn less than NFP did. The test comes in the organic growth line over the next four quarters: if middle-market mix keeps Commercial Risk organic growth at 6–7% while global rates keep falling, the bull case is proven. If organic growth slips toward 3–4%, the debt will look like a mistake made at the top of a cycle.
