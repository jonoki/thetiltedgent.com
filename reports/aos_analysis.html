01 Company Overview

A. O. Smith was founded in Milwaukee in 1874 as a baby-carriage and bicycle-parts maker and later built car frames. Today it sells something nobody thinks about until it fails: the water heater. It is the largest manufacturer and marketer of water heaters in North America, and it also sells boilers, heat pumps, tanks and water-treatment systems under brands including A. O. Smith, State, Lochinvar, Hague and Aquasana. FY2025 revenue was $3.83B, up 0.32% on FY2024, with diluted EPS of $3.85. At the September 21, 2026 close of $56.96, the equity is worth about $7.74B (our calculation: 135.91M shares × $56.96).

The pitch has always been the replacement cycle. Management puts replacement at roughly 80–85% of US residential water-heater demand. When a tank bursts in the basement, the homeowner does not wait for a better interest rate. That steadiness funded 33 consecutive years of dividend growth (per StockAnalysis). The problem is that steady has turned into stuck:

  • Revenue has sat between $3.75B and $3.85B in every year from FY2022 to FY2025.
  • China, once the growth engine, has shrunk from $922M of sales in FY2021 to $625M over the trailing twelve months.
  • In 2026 the US residential market softened, and steel costs rose about 20% year over year in Q2.

The stock closed at $56.96, down 36.6% from its September 2024 month-end high of $89.83. The question for the table: is this a quality franchise in a cyclical trough, or a mature business whose one growth story (China) is about to be written off?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

#1 NA water-heater maker 80–85% replacement demand Wholesale & retail shelf space Lochinvar boiler brand Pass-through pricing ROIC 23.0% 33 yrs dividend growth

The moat is distribution plus urgency. When a water heater fails, the plumber installs whatever the local wholesaler has on the shelf that day, and A. O. Smith's brands hold a large part of that shelf. Replacement demand does not wait for the housing cycle. That is how the company earns returns most manufacturers would envy: ROE of 27.1% and ROIC of 23.0% over the trailing twelve months.

The weakness is that the same structure caps growth. A replacement market grows with the installed base and with housing formation, and no faster. To beat that, a company needs price, adjacent products (boilers, water treatment, valves) or new geographies (China, India). In 2026 steel is eating the price gains, water treatment is being restructured, and China is under review.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China has gone from growth engine to write-down candidate. China revenue fell from $922.4M in FY2021 to $624.8M over the twelve months to June 2026, a 32% drop. It fell another 28% in local currency in Q2. The CEO says every option is open: a sale, a partnership or restructuring it alone. Any of those could bring a loss on exit or an impairment, and none is in the guidance.
02
Price and cost are nearly cancelling out. Management said Q2 price-cost was only "slightly positive". The full-year outlook assumes steel about 15% above 2025 levels, with second-half steel inflation "somewhat higher than the first half". It also expects new tariffs to cost modestly more than the ones they replaced. The 4–7% price increase is smaller than recent ones and arrives mid-Q3.
03
Q2 borrowed from Q3. Customers buying ahead of the price increase, plus boiler early-buy orders, pulled demand forward. Management has already said Q3 EPS will be below both Q2 and Q4. A weak quarter that has been guided can still move the stock when it prints.
04
The balance sheet is being put to work. Debt rose to $637.5M at June 30, 2026, after the Leonard Valve term loan (net debt $456M; debt-to-capital 25.7%). At FY2025 year-end total debt was $203.9M, and cash roughly matched it (StockAnalysis). Leverage is still modest at 0.84x debt/EBITDA, but acquisitions and buybacks are now partly funded with debt.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures:

  • The $22.6M pre-tax restructuring and impairment charge in North America water treatment in Q2 2026. It explains the gap between GAAP EPS of $0.91 and adjusted EPS of $1.03, and between 2026 GAAP guidance ($3.60–$3.75) and adjusted guidance ($3.70–$3.85).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 15.9x trailing and 15.2x forward earnings, with an 8.3% free-cash-flow yield and a 2.53% dividend. The business earns a 23% return on invested capital, has raised its dividend for 33 years, and is retiring nearly 4% of its shares a year. People replace the core product when it breaks rather than choosing to buy it: 80–85% of US residential demand is replacement, and emergency replacement is holding up. Boilers are growing at double digits, Leonard Valve adds a new platform, and a 4–7% price increase lands in the second half. The October China decision could remove the business that has dragged on results since 2021, leaving a higher-margin company centred on North America. Every analyst target, including the two Sells, is above the current price.

Bear Case

This is a no-growth company. Revenue has been between $3.75B and $3.85B for four years and TTM EPS is flat. EPS is only holding up because of buybacks, which are now partly funded with debt. The US residential market is guided down, Q2 pulled demand from Q3, steel is up about 20%, and the new price increase is smaller than past ones. China has lost about a third of its revenue since 2021, and an exit could come with an impairment. J.P. Morgan and Goldman rate it a Sell, and 9.7% of the float is sold short. Part of the market clearly thinks the high returns on capital belong to a franchise that has stopped compounding. At 15.9x, a business with flat earnings is not obviously cheap.

Top 3 Risks

01
The China outcome. The strategic assessment ends by the Q3 call, and the CEO says "all outcomes are still on the table". Guidance excludes any result. A sale below book value, a write-down or a costly restructuring would hit GAAP earnings. Keeping the business means living with double-digit revenue declines in local currency.
02
US residential water-heater volumes. Management has already moved its industry outlook from "flat to down low single digits" to "down low single digits". Proactive replacement, about 30% of replacement demand, depends on consumer confidence, and new construction is weak. Another cut to the volume outlook would put the bottom of the $3.70–$3.85 EPS guide at risk.
03
Price-cost squeeze. The full-year plan assumes steel about 15% above 2025, rising in the second half, and tariffs that cost modestly more than before. The price increase is "on the lower end" of recent ones and started late. If competitors discount to win share in a soft market, the damage shows up in the 24.4% adjusted North America margin.
Industry Commentary
China's consumer-appliance slump
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A. O. Smith sells water heaters and water purifiers into a Chinese appliance market that management calls "challenging, particularly in the premium segment." The company's product-line data (StockAnalysis) shows China revenue of $922.4M in FY2021, then $839.1M, $835.1M, $791.9M and $689.5M in FY2022–FY2025, and $624.8M over the latest twelve months. Revenue has fallen for four years running, and Q2 2026 was down 28% in local currency.

The company has been reviewing its options for almost a year. The CEO said it has had "a lot of great discussions with a lot of different potential parties." For a US investor, this is the clearest either-or in the stock. The outcome decides whether AOS is valued as a North American replacement franchise, or as one still carrying a shrinking Chinese appliance business.

Steel, tariffs and the US replacement cycle
ONGOING HEADWIND

Water heaters are mostly steel. Management put steel costs up about 20% year over year in Q2 2026 and assumes about 15% above 2025 for the full year, with tariffs a further headwind. This time the price increase is 4–7%, "on the lower end" of recent ones. It arrives while volumes are soft and the CEO describes "a highly competitive environment". Emergency replacement is not optional, so volumes will not collapse. The risk is to margins, not to revenue.

Boilers, valves and the "water management" pivot
EMERGING TAILWIND

The fastest-growing parts of A. O. Smith are its commercial and residential boilers (+21% in Q2 2026, +12% in the first half) and the newly acquired Leonard Valve (about $70M of sales targeted for 2026). Management presents these, alongside its investment in tankless and heat-pump water heating, as the start of a wider water-management platform.

The bull framing: these faster-growing adjacent businesses, plus a possible exit from China, could turn a flat company into a mid-single-digit grower with better margins and a buyback.

The bear framing: the boiler line, including Leonard Valve, is $329M of TTM revenue, under 9% of the company, and part of Q2's boiler growth was pulled forward. It cannot offset a soft water-heater market by itself.

The test is whether full-year boiler growth lands inside the guided 6–8% after a 12% first half, and what the China decision leaves behind.
