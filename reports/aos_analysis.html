01 Company Overview

A. O. Smith was founded in Milwaukee in 1874 as a baby-carriage and bicycle-parts maker and later built car frames. Today it sells something nobody thinks about until it fails: the water heater. It is the largest manufacturer and marketer of water heaters in North America, and it also sells boilers, heat pumps, tanks and water-treatment systems under brands including A. O. Smith, State, Lochinvar, Hague and Aquasana. FY2025 revenue was $3.83B, up 0.32% on FY2024, with diluted EPS of $3.85. At the October 30, 2026 close of $63.07, the equity is worth about $8.47B (our calculation: 134.33M shares × $63.07; the 134.33M is the 25.86M Class A and 108.47M Common shares on the cover of the Q3 10-Q, as of October 27).

The pitch has always been the replacement cycle. Management puts replacement at roughly 80–85% of US residential water-heater demand. When a tank bursts in the basement, the homeowner does not wait for a better interest rate. That steadiness has funded more than 30 consecutive years of dividend increases (company release, October 29, 2026). The problem is that steady has turned into stuck:

  • Revenue has sat between $3.75B and $3.86B in every year from FY2022 to FY2025, and the nine months to September 30, 2026 were $2,890.4M against $2,917.7M a year earlier.
  • China, once the growth engine, has shrunk from $922M of sales in FY2021 to $613M over the trailing twelve months, and is now being sold.
  • US residential water-heater volumes are still falling, and the CFO expects steel and freight costs each to rise about 15% in 2026 against 2025.

The China answer. On October 27, 2026 the company agreed to sell an 81% interest in its China business to CPE, an alternative asset manager, valuing the business at about $400M on a cash-free, debt-free basis before royalties. It keeps 19% and licenses the A. O. Smith brand to the business under a long-term agreement; including the present value of those licensing payments it puts the total at more than $500M. The company plans to bring back about $80M of cash held in China and to use the after-tax proceeds and that cash mainly for share buybacks. Closing is expected in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals. From the fourth quarter China will be reported as discontinued operations (a business held for sale, shown on its own line), with earlier periods restated. China was about 16% of trailing twelve-month sales (our calculation).

The stock closed at $63.07, down 29.8% from its September 2024 month-end high of $89.83 and up 11.9% from the September 2026 month-end close of $56.37. The question for the table has changed. It is no longer whether the company will deal with China. It is what a smaller, North America-heavy A. O. Smith is worth when its main business, US residential water heaters, is shrinking by volume.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

#1 NA water-heater maker 80–85% replacement demand Wholesale & retail shelf space Lochinvar boiler brand Pass-through pricing ROIC 23.0% 30+ yrs dividend growth

The moat is distribution plus urgency. When a water heater fails, the plumber installs whatever the local wholesaler has on the shelf that day, and A. O. Smith's brands hold a large part of that shelf. Replacement demand does not wait for the housing cycle. That is how the company earns returns most manufacturers would envy: ROE of 27.0% and ROIC of 23.0% over the trailing twelve months.

The weakness is that the same structure caps growth. A replacement market grows with the installed base and with housing formation, and no faster. To beat that, a company needs price, adjacent products (boilers, water treatment, valves) or new geographies. In 2026 steel and freight are eating the price gains and water treatment is being restructured. China, the one big geography, is being sold, which leaves a company that depends more heavily on the North American replacement market.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China is leaving, but not yet gone. China revenue fell from $922.4M in FY2021 to $612.9M over the twelve months to September 2026 (our calculation), a 34% drop, and it fell 17% in local currency in Q3. The sale to CPE closes only in the first half of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals. New guidance covers the company without China, so what the business earns until closing, and what the 19% stake and royalties add afterwards, is not in it.
02
Price and cost are nearly cancelling out. North America segment margin fell 60 basis points in Q3 as lower water-heater volumes and higher steel and freight costs outweighed boiler volume, pricing and tariff refunds. The CFO expects steel and freight to rise about 15% each in 2026 and said Q4 will see some margin pressure. A price increase of up to 9.5% starts in December; the CEO said on the call that competitors have announced similar price changes with similar timing.
03
Volumes are shrinking. The company's outlook for US residential water-heater industry volume is down low single digits for 2026, and its own water-heater sales fell in Q3. Emergency replacement is steady. Proactive replacement, where a homeowner swaps an old tank before it fails (about 30% of replacement demand, per the CFO on the Q2 call), is the part that depends on consumer confidence.
04
The balance sheet is being put to work. Debt was $673.1M at September 30, 2026 ($41.0M current), against cash of $234.5M, so net debt was $438.6M (our calculation); debt-to-capital was 27.0%. At FY2025 year-end total debt was $203.9M (StockAnalysis). The increase came from the Leonard Valve term loan. Debt is still less than one year of EBITDA (profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation), and the China proceeds are meant to go largely to buybacks.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures:

  • The $22.6M pre-tax restructuring and impairment charge in North America water treatment, all booked in the first half of 2026. It explains the gap between nine-month GAAP EPS of $2.68 and $2.80 excluding the charge. The company expects the full-year charge at about $20M and leaves it out of adjusted guidance.
  • Buybacks. Q3 average diluted shares were 3.4% below a year earlier, which is why Q3 EPS fell 1% while net earnings fell 4%.
  • Tariff refunds. Q3 included refunds of tariffs paid earlier under IEEPA, which partly offset cost pressure; the CFO does not expect anything material from them in Q4.

Looking further back, FY2022 diluted EPS of $1.51 (against $3.02 in FY2021) reflects a Q4 2022 loss of $0.78 per share.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 17.6x trailing and 18.3x forward earnings (the forward estimate sits inside the new guidance, which leaves China out), with a 6.6% free-cash-flow yield and a 2.41% dividend. The business earns a 23% return on invested capital, has raised its dividend for more than 30 years, and is retiring about 3% to 4% of its shares a year. People replace the core product when it breaks rather than choosing to buy it: 80–85% of US residential demand is replacement. Boilers are growing at double digits, with the full-year guide raised to 10–12%, Leonard Valve adds a new platform, and a further price increase of up to 9.5% lands in December. The China sale removes the business that has dragged on results since 2021, brings back about $80M of cash, and is meant to fund buybacks. What is left is a higher-margin company centred on North America.

Bear Case

This is a no-growth company. Revenue has been between $3.75B and $3.86B for four years and the nine months to September were down 0.9%. EPS is only holding up because of buybacks, which are now partly funded with debt. US residential volumes are guided down, steel and freight are each up about 15%, North America margin fell 60 basis points in Q3, and the CFO flagged more pressure in Q4. The sale closes only in the first half of 2027 and still needs regulatory approval. J.P. Morgan and Goldman rate it a Sell, 8 of 13 analysts say Hold, and 10.3% of the float is sold short. Part of the market clearly thinks the high returns on capital belong to a franchise that has stopped growing. At 17.6x, a business with flat earnings is no longer obviously cheap after the October rebound.

Top 3 Risks

01
The China closing and what is left. The sale needs regulatory approvals and is not expected to close before the first half of 2027. If it slips or changes, the without-China guidance and the buyback plan built on the proceeds change with it. Until closing, China's declining sales stay in the reported results.
02
US residential water-heater volumes. Management's outlook is industry volume down low single digits, and its own water-heater sales fell in Q3. Proactive replacement depends on consumer confidence, and new construction is weak. Another cut to the volume outlook would put the $3.42–$3.52 EPS guide at risk.
03
Price-cost squeeze. The full-year plan assumes steel and freight each about 15% above 2025. The December increase of up to 9.5% only works if competitors follow. If they discount to win share in a soft market, the damage shows up in the 23.6% North America segment margin.
Industry Commentary
China's consumer-appliance slump, and the exit
RESOLVED, NOT CLOSED

A. O. Smith sells water heaters and water purifiers into a Chinese appliance market that management called "challenging, particularly in the premium segment" on the Q2 call. The company's product-line data (StockAnalysis) shows China revenue of $922.4M in FY2021, then $839.1M, $835.1M, $791.9M and $689.5M in FY2022–FY2025, and $612.9M over the twelve months to September 2026 (our calculation). Revenue has fallen for four years running, and Q3 2026 was down 17% in local currency.

On October 27, 2026 the company agreed to sell 81% to CPE rather than keep fighting for share. For a US investor, this settles the clearest either-or in the stock: AOS will be valued as a North American replacement franchise with a 19% minority stake in a Chinese business, not as a company carrying a shrinking Chinese appliance operation. The deal still has to close.

Steel, tariffs and the US replacement cycle
ONGOING HEADWIND

Water heaters are mostly steel. The CFO expects steel and freight costs each to rise about 15% in 2026 against 2025. A price increase of up to 9.5% starts in December, while volumes are soft. Emergency replacement is not optional, so volumes will not collapse. The risk is to margins, not to revenue.

Boilers, valves and the "water management" pivot
EMERGING TAILWIND

The fastest-growing parts of A. O. Smith are its commercial and residential boilers (+13% excluding Leonard Valve in Q3 2026) and the Leonard Valve acquisition ($46.6M of sales in nine months). Management presents these, alongside its investment in tankless and heat-pump water heating, as the start of a wider water-management platform.

The bull framing: these faster-growing adjacent businesses, plus the China exit, could turn a flat company into a mid-single-digit grower with better margins and a buyback.

The bear framing: the boiler line, including Leonard Valve, is $354M of TTM revenue, under 10% of the company. It cannot offset a soft water-heater market by itself.

The test is whether full-year boiler growth lands inside the raised 10–12% guide, and how much margin the remaining company earns once China is out.
