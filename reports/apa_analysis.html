01 Company Overview

APA Corporation is Apache with a new nameplate. The company was founded in Minneapolis in 1954 as Apache Oil with $250,000 of seed money, went public in 1969 and moved to Houston in 1992; on March 1, 2021 it reorganised so that APA Corporation became the holding company, every Apache share converted one-for-one into an APA share, and APA took over the ticker. It is an E&P: it finds oil and gas, produces it and sells it at the market price. That makes it a price-taker in the purest sense. Its assets are the Permian Basin in Texas, which it added to heavily with the $4.5B all-stock Callon Petroleum acquisition closed April 1, 2024 (about 70 million new APA shares), a long-running gas and oil business in Egypt, legacy fields in the UK North Sea that it plans to shut down, and exploration positions in Suriname, Alaska and Uruguay.

The last five years read like a hand of Hold'em where the board changes the result more than the player does. The stock went from a $25.77 month-end close in November 2021 to $47.01 in May 2022 on the post-invasion oil spike, drifted down for three years to a $15.54 close in April 2025, a 67% drawdown, then climbed past the old peak to a $52.82 month-end close in October 2026. It gave back 15.5% in the first week of November and closed at $44.63 on November 6, 2026, still up 187% from the low. Two months did much of the 2026 work: March, +39.7%, and October, +27.2%, as crude climbed during the US-Iran war. Management's part of the story is cost and debt. It targets $500M of annual run-rate cost savings by year-end 2026, repaid $451M of debt in Q3 on top of $752M in the first half, generated $699M of free cash flow in Q3 2026 and now expects about $2.9B for the full year. The main question for the stock: analysts on average see 2026 EPS of $6.87 falling to $5.44 in 2027 (Yahoo Finance, read November 9, 2026). At $44.63 you are paying 6.5x the 2026 estimate and 8.2x the lower 2027 one. How much of today's earnings is the oil price and how much is the leaner company?

Apache has operated the Forties field since 2003. In November 2024, after the UK raised its Energy Profits Levy by three points to 38% (a 78% headline tax rate on oil profits) and extended it to March 2030, the company said "the onerous financial impact of the EPL, combined with the substantial investment that will be necessary to comply with regulatory requirements, makes production of hydrocarbons beyond 2029 uneconomic." An E&P cannot set its price. What it can control is how much it produces per dollar spent and how much of the cash reaches equity holders after the lenders.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

~10 years Permian inventory Egypt concession position GranMorgu 750M+ bbl discovery Alaska infrastructure (Savant deal pending) $500M cost-savings programme

Commodity producers don't have moats in the Buffett sense. Nobody pays more for APA's barrel than for anyone else's. What an E&P can have is low costs and good rock: acreage that makes money at oil prices where higher-cost producers do not, and resource it has found before anyone else. APA has both in pieces. The Permian inventory is deep, Egypt is a large concession position built over decades, and GranMorgu is a discovery few companies of APA's size hold. The Savant deal, if it closes, adds a second kind of advantage: owning the pipe and the plant next to its own acreage on the North Slope, so its development costs do not include paying someone else's tariff. The weakness is that none of this sets the price. Every dollar of margin on this page moves with crude.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The earnings are a function of the oil price. Analysts on average expect EPS to fall 20.8%, from $6.87 in 2026 to $5.44 in 2027, and revenue to fall 13.0% (Yahoo Finance, read Nov 9, 2026). Analysts are forecasting a lower oil price, not a weaker company. APA realised $92.02 a barrel for its oil in Q3, and it works in both directions: +39.7% in March, +27.2% in October, then −15.8% in the three sessions to Nov 4 as WTI fell 15.4%.
02
In Egypt, the state is the buyer. Egypt makes up 17% of reserves and a large share of gas volume, which is why reported production exceeds adjusted. Pricing (nearly half the gas is on 2024-revised terms) and a contract formula that trims adjusted barrels when oil prices rise are two moving parts. Neither is in management's full control.
03
The UK exit has a cost. Stopping production before 2030 ends the North Sea cash stream and leaves decommissioning obligations.
04
Exploration spend earns nothing until 2028. GranMorgu first oil is mid-2028, the Alaska wells are planned for the 2026–27 winter season and the Uruguay well for 2027. Until then, capital goes in and no revenue comes out, and exploration wells can come up dry.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $44.63 you pay 7.3x trailing earnings, 2.9x EV/EBITDA and a ~13.9% trailing free-cash-flow yield for a company that is cutting $500M of annual cost, has taken $2.8B of debt off the balance sheet since the end of 2024, has raised its 2026 free-cash-flow outlook by $600M to about $2.9B, has lowered its net-debt target to $2B, expects about $650M more of buybacks by year-end, and has three exploration and development bets: GranMorgu (750M+ barrels, first oil mid-2028), Alaska with its own infrastructure if the Savant deal closes, and Uruguay. Even on the 2027 average estimate of $5.44 EPS, the multiple is 8.2x. If GranMorgu arrives on time, APA gains a major new production source just as the North Sea one ends.

Bear Case

The stock is up 82% since December 2025, mostly on an oil price move rather than a change in the business, and it lost 15.8% in three sessions when WTI fell 15.4%. Analysts already expect 2027 EPS to fall 20.8% and revenue 13.0%, and three of 28 rate it a Strong Sell. The preliminary 2027 budget rises about $400M to $2.5B, with about 60% of the increase going to exploration and GranMorgu, which do not pay before 2028. Fourth-quarter U.S. oil is expected to ease. Egypt means relying on a state buyer to pay, and the UK leaves decommissioning costs. A company whose earnings depend on the oil price is always cheapest on trailing numbers just before the price turns.

Top 3 Risks

01
Crude price reversal. WTI ended October at $104.12 and was $90.83 by November 6. If the extra price buyers pay for fear of supply disruption comes out of oil, the 2026 and 2027 EPS estimates fall with it. Analysts already expect a 2027 decline.
02
Egypt pricing and volumes. A contract formula that trims adjusted barrels when oil rises, nearly half the gas on renegotiated terms, and a state buyer. Any change to pricing or payment terms goes straight to cash flow.
03
Execution on long-dated projects. GranMorgu (mid-2028), Alaska (2026–27 winter wells, Savant closing subject to regulatory approval) and Uruguay (2027) take capital now and return it later, if the geology and schedules hold.
Industry Commentary
Geopolitics sets the price
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

APA's 2026 chart follows the oil price. The March surge was attributed to "Middle East turbulence" adding "an oil risk premium" (StocksToTrade, Mar 26, 2026), and the 7.4% fall in U.S. crude on November 2 came as hopes grew for a diplomatic end to the US-Iran war (Gulf News, Nov 3, 2026). An E&P can lower its cost per barrel. It cannot change what the barrel sells for, and the shape of the estimates (2026 up, 2027 down) shows analysts treating today's price as temporary.

Fiscal regimes: UK windfall tax, Egyptian gas pricing
ONGOING HEADWIND

APA's two international businesses show two different outcomes for fiscal terms. In the UK, a 38% Energy Profits Levy (78% headline rate) extended to March 2030 made post-2029 production "uneconomic" in the company's words, so APA is leaving. In Egypt, revised 2024 pricing covering nearly half of gas production has helped support investment and gas growth (gross gas up about 10% from a year earlier in Q4 2025). The government's terms decide whether a country keeps attracting capital.

Doing more with less
EMERGING TAILWIND

This part of the story does not depend on the oil price. APA got down to four rigs this year and raised its U.S. oil guide. It reached $350M of savings two years early and raised the target to $500M. Annualised interest expense is down more than $180M as debt is repaid. The bull framing: these are permanent improvements that lower APA's breakeven, so the next downturn does less damage. The bear framing: the CFO tied the $600M outlook raise partly to stronger commodity prices (MarketBeat's call summary), APA plans to add two rigs around the turn of the year, and cost programmes do not keep producing savings forever. A lower-cost company still loses money if the oil price falls far enough.
