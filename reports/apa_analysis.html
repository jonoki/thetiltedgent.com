01 Company Overview

APA Corporation is Apache with a new nameplate. The company was founded in Minneapolis in 1954 as Apache Oil with $250,000 of seed money, went public in 1969 and moved to Houston in 1992; on March 1, 2021 it reorganised so that APA Corporation became the holding company, every Apache share converted one-for-one into an APA share, and APA took over the ticker. It is an E&P: it finds oil and gas, produces it and sells it at the market price. That makes it a price-taker in the purest sense. Its assets are the Permian Basin in Texas, which it added to heavily with the $4.5B all-stock Callon Petroleum acquisition closed April 1, 2024 (about 70 million new APA shares), a long-running gas and oil business in Egypt, legacy fields in the UK North Sea that it plans to shut down, and exploration positions in Suriname, Alaska and Uruguay.

The last five years read like a hand of Hold'em where the board changes the result more than the player does. The stock went from a $21.43 month-end close in September 2021 to $47.01 in May 2022 on the post-invasion oil spike, drifted down for three years to a $15.54 close in April 2025, a 67% drawdown, then rose again to $43.81 on September 21, 2026, up 182% from that low. Much of the 2026 move came in one month: March 2026, +39.7%, as Middle East tension pushed crude higher. Management's part of the story is cost and debt. It targets $500M of annual run-rate cost savings by year-end 2026, has repaid $752M of bonds in the first half of 2026, and generated $738M of free cash flow in Q2 2026 alone. The main question for the stock: the Street's consensus has 2026 EPS at $6.15 falling to $4.39 in 2027. At $43.81 you are paying 7.1x the peak-year estimate and 10.0x the fade-year one. How much of today's earnings is commodity price and how much is the leaner company?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

~10 years Permian inventory Egypt concession position GranMorgu 750M+ bbl discovery Alaska infrastructure (Badami, Nutaaq) Low-cost operating base 56-year dividend record

Commodity producers don't have moats in the Buffett sense. Nobody pays more for APA's barrel than for anyone else's. What an E&P can have is position on the cost curve and in the ground: acreage that makes money at lower prices than the marginal producer, and resource it has found before anyone else. APA has both in pieces. The Permian inventory is deep, Egypt is a large concession position built over decades, and GranMorgu is a discovery few companies of APA's size hold. The Savant deal adds a second kind of advantage: owning the pipe and the plant next to its own acreage on the North Slope, so its development costs do not include paying someone else's tariff. The weakness is that none of this sets the price. Every dollar of margin on this page moves with crude.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The earnings are a function of the oil price. Consensus has EPS rising 63% to $6.15 in 2026 and then falling 28.6% to $4.39 in 2027. Revenue is expected to drop 9.6%. The Street is forecasting a price fade, not company deterioration. The March 2026 rally, +39.7% in a month, was driven by Middle East tension, and that works in both directions.
02
Egypt is a sovereign counterparty. Egypt makes up 17% of reserves and a large share of gas volume, which is why reported production exceeds adjusted. Pricing (half the gas is on 2024-revised terms) and deferred low-pressure volumes at Khafre are two moving parts. Neither is in management's full control.
03
The UK exit has a cost. Stopping production before 2030 ends the North Sea cash stream and leaves decommissioning obligations. The size of those obligations was not sourced this session.
04
Exploration spend earns nothing until 2028. GranMorgu first oil is mid-2028, the Alaska wells are planned for the 2026–27 winter season and the Uruguay well for 2027. Until then, capital goes in and no revenue comes out, and exploration wells can come up dry.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $43.81 you pay 9.3x trailing earnings, 3.3x EV/EBITDA and a ~13.7% trailing free-cash-flow yield for a company that is cutting $500M of annual cost, has taken $2.3B of debt off the balance sheet since the end of 2024, has just authorised 40M more shares of buybacks, and has three option bets in development: GranMorgu (750M+ barrels, first oil mid-2028), Alaska with its own infrastructure, and Uruguay. Even on the 2027 consensus fade to $4.39 EPS, the multiple is 10x. If GranMorgu arrives on time, APA gains a major new production source just as the North Sea one ends.

Bear Case

The stock is up 79% since December 2025, mostly on an oil price move rather than a change in the business. Consensus already expects 2027 EPS to fall 28.6% and revenue 9.6%, the average target is only 4.7% above the price, and three of 27 analysts rate it a Strong Sell. Monthly RSI is 72.9. Revenue missed in Q2 while cost savings delivered the beat, and there is only so much cost left to cut. Egypt is a sovereign-credit exposure, the UK leaves decommissioning costs, and the growth projects do not pay before 2028. A company whose earnings depend on the oil price is always cheapest on trailing numbers just before the price turns.

Top 3 Risks

01
Crude price reversal. The March 2026 rally was geopolitical. If the risk premium comes out of oil, the 2026 EPS estimate of $6.15 falls with it. Consensus already assumes a 2027 decline.
02
Egypt pricing and volumes. Low-pressure gas deferred at Khafre, half the gas on renegotiated terms, and a state buyer. Any change to pricing or payment terms goes straight to cash flow.
03
Execution on long-dated projects. GranMorgu (mid-2028), Alaska (2026–27 winter wells, Savant closing subject to regulatory approval) and Uruguay (2027) take capital now and return it later, if the geology and schedules hold.
Industry Commentary
Geopolitics sets the price
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

APA's 2026 chart follows the oil tape. The March surge was attributed to "Middle East turbulence" adding "an oil risk premium" (StocksToTrade, Mar 26, 2026). An E&P can lower its cost per barrel. It cannot change what the barrel sells for, and the consensus shape (2026 up, 2027 down) shows the market treating today's price as temporary.

Fiscal regimes: UK windfall tax, Egyptian gas pricing
ONGOING HEADWIND

APA's two international businesses show two different outcomes for fiscal terms. In the UK, a 38% Energy Profits Levy (78% headline rate) extended to March 2030 made post-2029 production "uneconomic" in the company's words, so APA is leaving. In Egypt, revised 2024 pricing covering about half of gas volumes has helped support investment and gas growth (about +10% in 2025). The government's terms decide whether a country keeps attracting capital.

Doing more with less
EMERGING TAILWIND

This part of the story does not depend on the oil price. APA went from five rigs to four and raised its U.S. oil guide. It reached $350M of savings two years early and raised the target to $500M. Annualised interest expense is down more than $155M as debt is repaid. The bull framing: these are permanent improvements that lower APA's breakeven, so the next downturn does less damage. The bear framing: Q2 revenue missed by about $230M and savings closed the gap, and cost programmes do not keep producing savings forever. A lower-cost company still loses money if the oil price falls far enough.
