01 Company Overview

Air Products and Chemicals is the world's third-largest industrial gas company, supplying atmospheric gases (oxygen, nitrogen, argon) and process gases (hydrogen, helium, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, syngas) to refining, chemicals, metals, electronics, energy, medical and food customers across roughly 50 countries. It also designs and builds cryogenic and gas-processing equipment — air separation units, natural gas liquefaction trains, and liquid hydrogen and helium transport and storage systems. Fiscal 2025 (year ended September 30, 2025) revenue was $12,037.3 million, down 0.5% from FY2024's $12,101 million; trailing-twelve-month revenue through the June 2026 quarter has since recovered to $12.60 billion, up 4.5%.

At a $67.52 billion market capitalization the company ranks roughly #384 globally and #188 among US-listed companies. The investment story right now is not the underlying gas business — that is compounding at high-single-digit adjusted operating income growth with ~25% margins — but the strategic reset being executed by CEO Eduardo Menezes, who has spent his tenure unwinding the previous management's very large clean-hydrogen commitments. Across fiscal 2025 and the June 2026 quarter, Air Products has booked approximately $6.6 billion of pre-tax project-exit and impairment charges and cut its capital spending run-rate from roughly $5 billion toward a long-term target of $2.0–2.5 billion a year.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
On-Site Take-or-Pay Contracts Pipeline Network Density Energy Cost Pass-Through 15–20 Yr Contract Duration Oligopoly Structure Ultra-High-Purity Qualification Cryogenic Process Technology

The industrial gas moat is one of the most durable in the industrial economy, and it is structural rather than brand-based. Roughly half of Air Products' revenue comes from on-site and pipeline supply: the company builds an air separation unit or HyCO plant inside or adjacent to a customer's refinery, steel mill or fab, and sells the output under a take-or-pay contract (the customer pays a minimum monthly facility fee regardless of how much gas it actually consumes) typically running 15 to 20 years, with energy and inflation pass-through clauses that transfer natural gas and power cost volatility to the customer. That combination produces utility-like cash flows with almost no volume risk and almost no commodity risk on the contracted base — which is why the segment operating margins sit at 28–30% across Americas, Asia and Europe even in a weak industrial cycle.

Layered on top is network density. Once a pipeline grid exists — Air Products' US Gulf Coast hydrogen pipeline system is the largest in the world — incremental customers can be served at very low marginal cost, and a competitor must build an entire grid to compete for a single plant. The industry is a genuine oligopoly: Linde, Air Liquide, Air Products and Nippon Sanso together control the large majority of global supply, with Air Products holding an estimated ~15% global share versus Linde at ~30% and Air Liquide at ~25% (share figures are estimates, not disclosed). In electronics, the moat is qualification: ultra-high-purity gases must be validated against a specific fab process, and switching suppliers mid-node is a risk semiconductor manufacturers rarely take. Finally, the equipment franchise — coil-wound heat exchangers and cryogenic process technology for LNG — is a real engineering asset with a small competitive set. What the last two years have proved is that this moat does not extend to merchant clean hydrogen: when Air Products tried to build multi-billion-dollar plants without contracted offtake, none of the structural advantages applied, and the company took roughly $6.6 billion of pre-tax charges learning that.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Capital allocation credibility. The single largest overhang is that the market no longer takes management's project economics at face value. Two separate multi-billion-dollar write-down events — roughly $3.7 billion pre-tax across FY2025 and $2.9 billion pre-tax in Q3 FY2026 — destroyed a meaningful fraction of a decade's retained earnings. In February 2025 management explicitly stated it did not anticipate additional material cancellations; sixteen months later it cancelled the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex. Until the new capex framework has been delivered for several consecutive years, APD will carry a valuation discount to Linde.

2. Balance sheet and free cash flow strain. Trailing-twelve-month FCF is negative $301.4 million, because $4.57 billion of operating cash flow is being consumed by legacy megaproject capex. Debt-to-equity is 1.10 with $18.27 billion of total debt against $1.00 billion of cash, and the current ratio is a thin 1.08. Adjusted net-debt/EBITDA of 2.1x is manageable, and the NEOM deconsolidation should remove ~$5.2 billion, but there is no buffer for a second surprise. Management has signalled buybacks are unlikely before late 2027 or early 2028.

3. Cyclical and geographic demand exposure. Merchant and packaged gas volumes track industrial production directly. Europe delivered −2% volume in the June quarter with all reported growth coming from energy pass-through and currency; the Americas saw 2% lower energy cost pass-through. A meaningful share of consolidated growth is currently arithmetic rather than operational, and a US or Chinese industrial slowdown would expose that.

4. Concentration in the electronics bet. Having exited clean hydrogen, the growth story now rests heavily on one end market: $2.4 billion of the $3.0 billion traditional industrial gas backlog is electronics, roughly 80%, and management says two-thirds or more of the opportunity pipeline is electronics. Semiconductor capex is famously cyclical. Swapping green-hydrogen concentration risk for semiconductor-fab concentration risk reduces, but does not eliminate, the underwriting problem.

Notable One-Off Items

APD's GAAP numbers are effectively unusable for valuation right now, and the gap between GAAP and adjusted results is the largest single analytical issue in this name. Investors should work from adjusted figures and treat the charges as real but non-recurring economic losses already sunk.

Fiscal 2025 project exits — approximately $3.7 billion pre-tax. Following a board-initiated portfolio review, Air Products announced on February 24, 2025 that it would exit three US projects: the World Energy sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) expansion in Paramount, California; the 35 metric-tonne-per-day green liquid hydrogen facility in Massena, New York (killed by regulatory changes excluding existing hydroelectric power from clean-hydrogen tax credits, plus slow hydrogen-mobility adoption); and a carbon monoxide project in Texas on unfavourable economics. The initial estimate was a pre-tax charge "not to exceed $3.1 billion"; the three projects ultimately carried $2.4 billion. Adding $425 million for two Chinese coal gasification projects moved to held-for-sale and $755 million of smaller energy-transition exits gives roughly $3.6 billion of project exit costs, of which $737.4 million fell in Q4 FY2025. Separately FY2025 absorbed $123.7 million for a global cost-reduction plan and $86.3 million of shareholder-activism costs. Result: a GAAP full-year operating loss of $877.0 million and a GAAP loss per share of $1.74, against adjusted EPS of $12.03 — a swing of nearly $14 per share.

Fiscal Q3 2026 — approximately $2.9 billion pre-tax ($2.2 billion after tax, $9.92 per share). On June 30, 2026 the company announced it would not proceed with the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex, the flagship blue-hydrogen and blue-ammonia project already under construction, and would discontinue the Casa Grande, Arizona zero-carbon liquid hydrogen facility plus several smaller clean-energy distribution projects. The quarter therefore reported a $2.1 billion GAAP operating loss (a negative 66.3% operating margin) and a $6.47 GAAP loss per share — while adjusted EPS was $3.47, up 12% year over year and above the top end of guidance, on adjusted operating income of $810 million and a 25.6% adjusted operating margin. Management intends to recover value by redeploying or selling Louisiana equipment; any proceeds are upside not currently in estimates.

Other distortions. Currency contributed roughly 1 point to Q3 FY26 consolidated sales growth and 3 points in Europe, so reported European growth overstates underlying demand. Energy cost pass-through added 3 points in Europe but subtracted 2 points in the Americas — a pure accounting flow-through with no margin content. Restructuring under the global cost-reduction plan continues. Finally, once NEOM is commissioned and deconsolidated, both the balance sheet and the equity-affiliate line will restate materially; comparisons across that boundary will not be like-for-like.

Five-year trajectory. On a dividend-adjusted basis APD went from $227.13 in September 2021 to $303.22 at the August 19, 2026 close — a total return of roughly +33.5% over five years, or about 5.9% a year. Over the identical window the S&P 500 (SPY, dividend-adjusted) went from $402.36 to $769.98, a total return of roughly +91.4%. APD therefore underperformed the index by close to 58 percentage points across the period, and it did so while paying a rising dividend and running 28–30% segment operating margins — the entire shortfall is attributable to capital misallocation, not to the operating business. The path was not a steady drift: the stock made a record close of $322.52 in January 2025 on activism and management-change optimism, then fell 26% to $237.85 by October 2025 as the scale of the write-downs became clear, before recovering 27% to current levels as the reset delivered adjusted earnings growth. The 52-week change of +5.23% against SPY's +20.87% one-year total return shows the underperformance is ongoing, not purely historical.

Relative performance. Against sector peers the picture is equally unflattering. Linde trades at a trailing P/E of 31.05 and Air Liquide at 30.28, both with positive GAAP earnings and Linde compounding revenue at 6.6% and net income at 7.9%. APD has no meaningful trailing multiple at all. On forward earnings the gap narrows sharply — 21.29x for APD versus 25.73x for Linde — which is precisely the mispricing Bernstein is underwriting. The bull case is a multiple convergence trade; the bear case is that the discount is a rational discount for a management team that has twice surprised the market with multi-billion-dollar losses.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Strip out the write-downs and Air Products is a high-quality industrial gas oligopolist growing adjusted operating income 9% with a 25.6% adjusted operating margin, 28–30% segment margins, a 0.75 beta, and 51 consecutive years of dividend increases — trading at 21.3x forward earnings versus Linde's 25.7x. The reset is now largely complete: $6.6 billion of bad capital has been recognised, capex falls from ~$5B to ~$3.5B to ~$3.0B and eventually $2.0–2.5B, NEOM deconsolidation removes ~$5.2B of net debt, and free cash flow inflects sharply positive. Meanwhile the replacement growth engine is arguably better than the one discarded: a $3.0 billion traditional backlog that is 80% electronics, with $1.5 billion of wins in six months into a semiconductor super cycle, all on contracted 15–20 year take-or-pay terms. If the multiple simply converges toward peers, that alone is 15–20% upside before any earnings growth.

Bear Case

Management credibility is the asset that was actually impaired. In February 2025 the company stated it did not anticipate further material cancellations, then took another $2.9 billion charge sixteen months later; investors have no independent way to verify that the remaining book is clean. The balance sheet has no slack — FCF is negative $301 million, D/E is 1.10 on $18.27 billion of debt, the current ratio is 1.08, and buybacks are off the table until late 2027 at the earliest. Underlying demand is soft: European volumes fell 2% and Americas energy pass-through fell 2%, meaning much of the reported 5% growth is currency and cost flow-through, not units. And at ~22.6x FY2026 adjusted earnings with the stock in the top quintile of its 52-week range and consensus targets clustered just 10–13% above spot, a great deal of the recovery is already in the price.

Top 3 Risks
01
Further impairments on the residual project book. Two Chinese coal gasification assets are already held for sale at a $425 million charge, and NEOM remains a consolidated, partially complete 2.2 GW electrolysis asset whose economics depend on green ammonia clearing prices Air Products does not control. The Yara agreement removes volume risk but explicitly leaves Air Products with the spread between its fixed offtake cost and market price. A third write-down event would likely break the current re-rating.
02
Semiconductor cycle reversal. Roughly 80% of the $3.0 billion backlog and two-thirds of the opportunity pipeline are electronics. Fab construction is the most capex-cyclical end market in industrials; a memory or foundry digestion phase would not cancel existing take-or-pay contracts but would freeze new bookings and remove the growth narrative that is currently supporting the multiple.
03
Free cash flow inflection slips. The entire bull case is timing-dependent: the capex step-down, the NEOM deconsolidation and the resumption of buybacks all land in FY2027–FY2028. Construction overruns at NEOM, a delayed commissioning, weaker Louisiana equipment recovery proceeds, or a rise in maintenance capex would push the inflection out — and with the dividend consuming roughly $1.6 billion a year against $18.27 billion of debt, a delay is not costless.
  Industry Commentary
The Retreat From Speculative Green-Hydrogen Capex
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The single most important sector development of the last two years is the collapse of the merchant clean-hydrogen investment thesis, and Air Products is its most expensive case study. Between fiscal 2025 and the June 2026 quarter the company recognised roughly $6.6 billion of pre-tax project exit and impairment charges — approximately $3.7 billion in FY2025 (including $2.4 billion for the World Energy SAF, Massena NY and Texas CO projects, $425 million for two Chinese gasification assets and $755 million of smaller energy-transition exits) and $2.9 billion in Q3 FY2026 for the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex and Casa Grande. The proximate causes were regulatory — the exclusion of existing hydroelectric generation from clean-hydrogen tax credit eligibility killed Massena outright — but the structural cause was building supply without contracted demand. Air Liquide signalled the same cooling. The lesson embedded in APD's numbers is that the industrial gas moat only functions when a creditworthy counterparty signs a 15-year take-or-pay contract first.
Soft Industrial Production and the Quality of Reported Growth
ONGOING HEADWIND
Industrial gas volumes are one of the cleanest real-time proxies for manufacturing activity, and the signal is mixed at best. In the June 2026 quarter consolidated sales rose 5% but only 3 points came from volume; 1 point was price and 1 point currency. Europe was worse: +3% energy pass-through, +3% currency, +2% price, −2% volume, with operating margin down 90 bps — the region's reported 6% growth contained no underlying demand growth at all. In the Americas, energy cost pass-through subtracted 2 points. Air Liquide's TTM revenue was down 1.3%. The read-through is that European heavy industry — steel, glass, refining, chemicals — remains structurally impaired by energy costs, and that merchant/packaged gas will not be a source of growth until industrial production genuinely reaccelerates. What protects margins in the meantime is the contracted on-site base, not the cycle.
Electronics Gas Demand — The Replacement Growth Engine
EMERGING TAILWIND
Every new semiconductor fab requires enormous volumes of ultra-high-purity nitrogen and hydrogen plus specialty and dopant gases, supplied on the same on-site, take-or-pay model that makes refinery contracts attractive — but with better growth and higher switching costs, because gases are qualified against a specific process node. Air Products has $2.4 billion of its $3.0 billion traditional backlog in electronics and booked over $1.5 billion of electronics wins in six months, with air separation unit awards in Taiwan and South Korea; Menezes has called it a semiconductor "super cycle" and says two-thirds or more of the pipeline is electronics. Asia, where this demand concentrates, grew operating income 18% with margin up 210 bps in the June quarter. Linde reported record Q2 sales and EPS on the same driver, so this is an industry-wide tailwind rather than a company-specific claim.

Bull vs bear framing: the bull argues Air Products has swapped an uncontracted, politically-dependent hydrogen build-out for a contracted, customer-funded electronics build-out at roughly one-third the capital intensity, and deserves Linde's multiple. The bear replies that concentration risk has been relocated rather than removed, that semiconductor capex is the most violently cyclical spending line in industrials, and that a management team which mis-underwrote $6.6 billion of projects two years ago should be given a discount rate, not the benefit of the doubt, on the next $3 billion.
