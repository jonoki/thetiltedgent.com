01 Company Overview

Air Products and Chemicals is the world's third-largest industrial gas company, supplying atmospheric gases (oxygen, nitrogen, argon) and process gases (hydrogen, helium, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, syngas) to refining, chemicals, metals, electronics, energy, medical and food customers across roughly 50 countries. It also sells equipment to third parties, such as air separation units (plants that split air into oxygen, nitrogen and argon). Fiscal 2026 (year ended September 30, 2026) revenue was $12,990.9 million, up 7.9% from FY2025's $12,037.3 million.

At a $67.88 billion market capitalization the company ranks #181 in the S&P 500. The investment story right now is not the underlying gas business — adjusted operating income rose 11% in fiscal 2026 to $3.2 billion at a 24.4% adjusted margin (profit before one-off charges) — but the strategic reset being executed by CEO Eduardo Menezes, who has spent his tenure unwinding the previous management's very large clean-hydrogen commitments. Across fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026, Air Products has booked approximately $6.9 billion of pre-tax project-exit and impairment charges ($3,747.0 million in FY2025 and $3,151.9 million in FY2026, per the income statement in the November 5, 2026 release) and cut its capital spending from roughly $5 billion to $3.3 billion in fiscal 2026, with $3.0–$3.5 billion guided for fiscal 2027.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
On-Site Take-or-Pay Contracts Pipeline Network Density Energy Cost Pass-Through 15–20 Yr Contract Duration Oligopoly Structure Ultra-High-Purity Qualification Cryogenic Process Technology

The industrial gas moat is one of the most durable in the industrial economy, and it is structural rather than brand-based. Roughly half of Air Products' revenue comes from on-site and pipeline supply: the company builds an air separation unit or HyCO plant inside or adjacent to a customer's refinery, steel mill or fab, and sells the output under a take-or-pay contract (the customer pays a minimum monthly facility fee regardless of how much gas it actually consumes) typically running 15 to 20 years, with energy and inflation pass-through clauses that transfer natural gas and power cost volatility to the customer. That combination produces utility-like cash flows with almost no volume risk and almost no commodity risk on the contracted base — which is why the segment operating margins sit at 28–30% across Americas, Asia and Europe even in a weak industrial cycle.

Layered on top is network density. Once a pipeline grid exists, as with Air Products' US Gulf Coast hydrogen pipeline system, incremental customers can be served at very low marginal cost, and a competitor must build an entire grid to compete for a single plant. The industry is a genuine oligopoly: Linde, Air Liquide, Air Products and Nippon Sanso together control the large majority of global supply, with Air Products holding an estimated ~15% global share versus Linde at ~30% and Air Liquide at ~25% (share figures are estimates, not disclosed). In electronics, the moat is qualification: ultra-high-purity gases must be validated against a specific fab process, and switching suppliers on a running production process is a risk semiconductor manufacturers rarely take. What the last two years have proved is that this moat does not extend to merchant clean hydrogen: when Air Products tried to build multi-billion-dollar plants without customers signed up to buy the output, none of the structural advantages applied, and the company took roughly $6.9 billion of pre-tax charges learning that.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Capital allocation credibility. The biggest problem is that investors no longer take management's project forecasts at face value. There have been two separate multi-billion-dollar write-down events: roughly $3.7 billion pre-tax in FY2025 and $3.2 billion pre-tax in FY2026 (including $2.9 billion in Q3). In February 2025 management explicitly stated it did not anticipate additional material cancellations; sixteen months later it cancelled the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex. Until the new plan for capital spending (capex) has been delivered for several consecutive years, APD will carry a valuation discount to Linde.

2. Balance sheet and free cash flow strain. Free cash flow in fiscal 2026 turned positive but only barely: FCF was $476.8 million ($4,680.7 million of operating cash flow less $4,203.9 million of plant additions, per the cash-flow statement), against $1.6 billion of dividends paid. Debt-to-equity is 1.06 with $18.23 billion of total debt (StockAnalysis, includes leases) against $1.33 billion of cash, and the current ratio is a thin 1.07 ($5,016.4 million of current assets against $4,708.9 million of current liabilities). The NEOM deconsolidation should remove ~$5.2 billion of net debt, but there is no buffer for a second surprise. No share buybacks are included in the fiscal 2027 outlook; Menezes said on the November 5 call they could come once free cash flow allows, subject to board approval.

3. Cyclical and geographic demand exposure. Merchant and packaged gas volumes track industrial production directly. In the September 2026 quarter consolidated volumes rose 9% (Americas +26%, Asia +3%, Europe +1%), but sales growth also includes energy pass-through (+6 points in Europe, which adds sales but not profit) and currency, and the adjusted operating margin fell 150 basis points to 24.1% on higher costs. The Americas jump also included a significant asset sale tied to an early contract termination, so part of the reported growth is not from selling more gas, and a US or Chinese industrial slowdown would expose that. The first fiscal 2027 outlook is slower than fiscal 2026: adjusted EPS of $14.15–$14.50, up 5% to 8%, against 12% growth in fiscal 2026.

4. Concentration in the electronics bet. Having exited clean hydrogen, the growth story now rests heavily on one end market. At the July results management put the traditional industrial gas backlog at about $3.0 billion; on the November 5 call it was $3.3 billion, with a significant portion of the capital for electronics customers, and the release reports over $2.0 billion of new electronics wins. Spending on chip plants is famously cyclical. Swapping a heavy bet on green hydrogen for a heavy bet on chip plants reduces, but does not remove, the risk of depending on one market.

Notable One-Off Items

APD's GAAP numbers are still distorted for valuation, and the gap between GAAP and adjusted results is the biggest single thing to understand about this stock. Investors should work from adjusted figures and treat the charges as real but non-recurring economic losses already sunk.

Fiscal 2025 project exits — approximately $3.7 billion pre-tax. Following a board-initiated portfolio review, Air Products announced on February 24, 2025 that it would exit three US projects: the World Energy sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) expansion in Paramount, California; the 35 metric-tonne-per-day green liquid hydrogen facility in Massena, New York (killed by regulatory changes excluding existing hydroelectric power from clean-hydrogen tax credits, plus slow hydrogen-mobility adoption); and a carbon monoxide project in Texas on unfavourable economics. The initial estimate was a pre-tax charge "not to exceed $3.1 billion"; the three projects ultimately carried $2.4 billion. Adding $425 million for two Chinese coal gasification projects moved to held-for-sale and $755 million of smaller energy-transition exits gives roughly $3.6 billion of project exit costs, of which $737.4 million fell in Q4 FY2025. Separately FY2025 absorbed $123.7 million for a global cost-reduction plan and $86.3 million of shareholder-activism costs. Result: a GAAP full-year operating loss of $877.0 million and a GAAP loss per share of $1.74, against adjusted EPS of $12.03 — a swing of nearly $14 per share.

Fiscal Q3 2026 — approximately $2.9 billion pre-tax ($2.2 billion after tax, $9.92 per share). On June 30, 2026 the company announced it would not proceed with the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex, the flagship blue-hydrogen and blue-ammonia project already under construction, and would discontinue the Casa Grande, Arizona zero-carbon liquid hydrogen facility plus several smaller clean-energy distribution projects. The quarter therefore reported a $2.1 billion GAAP operating loss (a negative 66.3% operating margin) and a $6.47 GAAP loss per share — while adjusted EPS was $3.47, up 12% year over year and above the top end of guidance, on adjusted operating income of $810 million and a 25.6% adjusted operating margin. Management intends to recover value by redeploying or selling Louisiana equipment; any proceeds were upside not in estimates at the time.

Fiscal 2026 in total — $3.15 billion pre-tax. The income statement in the November 5, 2026 release shows $3,151.9 million of business and asset actions for the year (against $3,747.0 million in FY2025), leaving GAAP operating income of $23.3 million and GAAP diluted EPS of $2.39 against adjusted EPS of $13.45. The fourth quarter carried roughly $0.2 billion of those charges, mostly on two Chinese coal gasification plants: one was sold in October 2026 and the other was fully written down.

Other distortions. Currency contributed 1 point to Q4 consolidated sales growth (+2 in Asia, −1 in Europe). Energy cost pass-through added 6 points to Europe's Q4 sales and cost that region about 200 basis points of margin — costs that pass through sales without adding profit. Restructuring under the global cost-reduction plan continues. Finally, once NEOM is commissioned and deconsolidated, both the balance sheet and the equity-affiliate line will change materially; comparisons across that boundary will not be like-for-like.

Five-year trajectory. APD went from $304.26 in December 2021 to $304.84 at the November 6, 2026 close — a price return of roughly +0.2% over five years, essentially flat, before dividends. Over the identical window the S&P 500 (SPY) went from $474.96 (December 2021 month-end close) to $735.29 (November 6, 2026 close), a price return of roughly +54.8%. APD therefore lagged the index by roughly 55 percentage points on a like-for-like price basis — and it did so while paying a rising dividend and running 28–30% segment operating margins; much of the shortfall traces to the project write-downs rather than the operating business. The path was not a steady drift: the stock reached its highest month-end close, $335.26 in January 2025, on activism and management-change optimism, then fell 28% to $242.59 by October 2025 as the scale of the write-downs became clear, before recovering 26% to current levels as the reset delivered adjusted earnings growth. Over the past year the picture has reversed: from the November 6, 2025 close ($258.79) the stock is up +17.8% against +9.7% for SPY (price returns, closes to the November 6, 2026 close).

Relative performance. Against sector peers the picture is mixed. Linde trades at a trailing P/E of 34.2 ($515.25 close on November 6 ÷ $15.05 EPS) and Air Liquide at 33.8, both with positive GAAP earnings, and Linde's revenue grew 8.2% over the trailing twelve months to September 2026. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Strip out the write-downs and Air Products is a high-quality supplier in an industry run by a few large companies, growing adjusted operating income 11% with a 24.4% adjusted operating margin, 28–30% segment margins, a 0.67 beta, and 44 consecutive years of dividend increases — trading at 21.3x forward earnings versus Linde's ~27.0x. The reset is now largely complete: $6.9 billion of bad capital has been recognised, capex fell from $5.1B to $3.3B and is guided to $3.0–$3.5B, NEOM deconsolidation removes ~$5.2B of net debt, and free cash flow has turned positive ($477 million in fiscal 2026). Meanwhile the replacement growth engine is arguably better than the one discarded: a $3.3 billion traditional backlog with over $2.0 billion of new electronics wins, typically on contracted 15–20 year terms. If the valuation closed even half the gap to Linde's forward multiple, that alone is about 13% upside before any earnings growth (halfway from 21.3x to 27.0x is about 24.1x; 24.1 ÷ 21.3 − 1).

Bear Case

Management credibility is the asset that was actually impaired. In February 2025 the company stated it did not anticipate further material cancellations, then took another $2.9 billion charge sixteen months later; investors have no independent way to verify that the remaining book is clean. The balance sheet has no slack — FCF was just $477 million, D/E is 1.06 on $18.23 billion of debt, the current ratio is 1.07, and no buybacks are in the fiscal 2027 outlook. Growth is slowing: the first fiscal 2027 outlook is adjusted EPS up 5% to 8% against 12% in fiscal 2026, and the Q4 adjusted margin fell 150 basis points. And at ~22.7x fiscal 2026 adjusted earnings with the stock about 80% of the way up its 52-week range and the average target about 12% above the share price, a great deal of the recovery is already in the price.

Top 3 Risks
01
Further impairments on the residual project book. One of the two Chinese coal gasification plants was sold in October 2026 and the other was fully written down in the fourth quarter, but NEOM remains a consolidated, partially complete electrolysis plant of more than 2 GW whose economics depend on green ammonia market prices Air Products does not control. The Yara agreement removes the volume risk, but Menezes said in July that the price risk is still there. A third write-down event would likely break the current recovery in the shares.
02
Semiconductor cycle reversal. A significant portion of the $3.3 billion backlog and, as Menezes said in July, about two-thirds of the opportunity pipeline are electronics. Chip-plant construction is among the most cyclical parts of industrial spending; a pause while memory or contract chipmakers absorb new capacity would not cancel existing take-or-pay contracts but would freeze new bookings and remove the growth story that is supporting the share price.
03
The free cash flow turnaround slips. The entire bull case is timing-dependent: the capex step-down, the NEOM deconsolidation and the resumption of buybacks all land in FY2027–FY2028. Construction overruns at NEOM, a delayed commissioning, weaker Louisiana equipment recovery proceeds, or a rise in maintenance capex would push the turnaround out — and with the dividend consuming roughly $1.6 billion a year against $18.23 billion of debt, a delay is not costless.
  Industry Commentary
The Retreat From Speculative Green-Hydrogen Capex
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The single most important sector development of the last two years is the collapse of the merchant clean-hydrogen investment thesis, and Air Products is its most expensive case study. Between fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026 the company recognised roughly $6.9 billion of pre-tax project exit and impairment charges — $3.7 billion in FY2025 (including $2.4 billion for the World Energy SAF, Massena NY and Texas CO projects, $425 million for two Chinese gasification assets and $755 million of smaller energy-transition exits) and $3.2 billion in FY2026, of which $2.9 billion came in Q3 for the Louisiana Clean Energy Complex and Casa Grande. The proximate causes were regulatory — the exclusion of existing hydroelectric generation from clean-hydrogen tax credit eligibility killed Massena outright — but the structural cause was building supply without contracted demand. The lesson embedded in APD's numbers is that the industrial gas moat only functions when a creditworthy counterparty signs a 15-year take-or-pay contract first.
Mixed Industrial Demand and the Quality of Reported Growth
MIXED SIGNAL
Industrial gas volumes are one of the cleanest real-time gauges of manufacturing activity, and the signal is mixed. In the September 2026 quarter consolidated sales rose 12%, with 9 points from volume and 1 point each from price, energy pass-through and currency, though the Americas' 26% volume jump included a significant asset sale tied to an early contract termination. Europe was less strong: +6% energy pass-through, +2% price, +1% volume, −1% currency, with operating margin down 190 basis points — most of the region's reported 8% growth is higher energy costs passed on to customers rather than underlying demand. Air Liquide's revenue over the twelve months to June 2026 was down 1.3%. The read-through is that European heavy industry — steel, glass, refining, chemicals — remains structurally impaired by energy costs, and that merchant gas will not be a source of growth until industrial production genuinely reaccelerates. What protects margins in the meantime is the contracted on-site base, not the cycle.
Electronics Gas Demand — The Replacement Growth Engine
EMERGING TAILWIND
Every new semiconductor fab requires enormous volumes of ultra-high-purity nitrogen and hydrogen plus specialty and dopant gases, supplied on the same on-site, take-or-pay model that makes refinery contracts attractive — but with better growth and higher switching costs, because gases are qualified against a specific process node. Air Products' release reports over $2.0 billion of new electronics wins and the call put the industrial gas backlog at $3.3 billion, with a significant portion for electronics customers; at the July results it was about $3.0 billion, with projects in Taiwan and South Korea cited. Asia, where this demand concentrates, grew operating income 10% in the September quarter with margin up 150 basis points.

Bull vs bear framing: the bull argues Air Products has swapped an uncontracted, politically-dependent hydrogen build-out for a contracted electronics build-out, and deserves a valuation like Linde's. The bear replies that concentration risk has been relocated rather than removed, that spending on chip plants is among the most cyclical in industry, and that a management team which misjudged $6.9 billion of projects over two years should not get the benefit of the doubt on the next $3 billion.
