01 Company Overview

AppLovin Corporation is a pure-play advertising-technology company that sells machine-learning-driven performance advertising to app developers, e-commerce merchants and, increasingly, CTV (connected TV) advertisers. Its core asset is AXON, a self-learning ad-matching and bidding engine that sits underneath the AppLovin Ads self-serve platform, the MAX in-app bidding exchange, the Adjust measurement suite, and Wurl's CTV distribution business. Following the June 2025 completion of the divestiture of its Apps (mobile-games studio) business — which produced a $106 million pre-tax gain and left prior periods restated for discontinued operations — AppLovin reports essentially a single advertising business.

The economics are extraordinary and are the whole argument. FY2025 revenue was $5,481M, up 70.0% YoY; TTM revenue through Sep 30, 2026 is $7,609M (+57.8%) on just 898 employees — roughly $8.5 million of revenue per employee. TTM net profit margin is 65.89%, gross margin 88.78%, and Q3 FY2026 adjusted EBITDA margin was 84%. At $129.62B market cap the company ranks #89 in the S&P 500, even with the shares 41.9% below their December 2025 close.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
AXON SELF-LEARNING LOOP EXCHANGE + BIDDER + MMP STACK ~50% MOBILE GAME UA SHARE 88.8% GROSS MARGIN 898 EMPLOYEES / $8.5M REV EACH 84% ADJ. EBITDA MARGIN $4.88B TTM FREE CASH FLOW

AppLovin's moat is a data loop that keeps improving, not a patent or a brand. AXON observes the outcome of every impression it buys through MAX and measures through Adjust, so each dollar of advertiser spend improves the model that prices the next dollar. Because the company owns all three layers, the model learns from results almost at once — an advantage a standalone DSP buying other companies' ad space cannot match. The financial signature of that loop is the point: 88.78% gross margin, 77.78% operating margin, 65.89% net margin, 84% adjusted EBITDA margin, and $4.88B of TTM free cash flow on $7.61B of revenue. Operating cash flow of $4.90B and free cash flow of $4.88B are almost the same, meaning capital expenditure is very small — this is software economics with almost no physical drag. ROIC of 137.63% and ROE of 210.09% are real but flattered by a book-equity base that buybacks have shrunk to about $3.3B. The honest framing: the returns are extraordinary and the denominators are small.

The vulnerability is that this same closed architecture is what makes the business hard for outsiders — including short sellers, auditors, and regulators — to verify independently.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The self-attribution / verification gap. AppLovin runs the auction, the bidding model, and — via Adjust — a measurement layer. An advertiser evaluating AppLovin's reported performance is substantially relying on AppLovin's own accounting of the conversion. This is a structural feature, not a proven abuse, but it is why four separate firms have been able to publish critical reports that outsiders cannot easily disprove. Fuzzy Panda, Muddy Waters and Culper Research are short sellers (investors who profit if the shares fall); CapitalWatch, a financial publisher, followed in January 2026. The allegations across them include improper user-data collection, violation of platform service agreements, and mechanisms that install apps on user devices without consent; CapitalWatch additionally alleged ties to a Southeast Asian money-laundering operation. CEO Adam Foroughi has called the short-seller reports false and misleading, and AppLovin demanded that CapitalWatch retract its report. CapitalWatch apologised for its claims about a major shareholder, APP shares rose 13.2% on Feb 9, 2026 as that circulated, and Barron's reported on Feb 13 that it had recanted the entire report. The remaining claims are allegations.

2. Regulatory and platform-rule exposure. On Oct 6, 2025, press reports said the SEC was investigating AppLovin's data-collection practices; the stock fell sharply that day. On the Aug 5, 2026 Q2 call, CFO Matt Stumpf said the SEC had concluded its inquiry with no recommended action. That closes the specific inquiry. It does not close the underlying exposure — AppLovin's model depends on signal that Apple and Google can restrict by policy at any time, and privacy regulation in the US and EU keeps tightening around exactly the device data AXON consumes. A private lawsuit is new since the last edition: Pomerantz LLP, a plaintiffs’ law firm, announced a securities class action in the US District Court for the Northern District of California (No. 26-cv-10584) on behalf of investors who bought between Feb 12 and Aug 5, 2026. It alleges AppLovin and certain officers misled investors about how consistently its AI models were improving and about a generative-AI video tool that faced delays. AppLovin’s 10-Q says that at Sep 30, 2026 it had no material expected losses from legal proceedings that it had to record or disclose.

3. Concentration in a single, maturing market. AppLovin does not give the split between gaming and consumer, but Wells Fargo estimates it already takes about half of mobile-game user-acquisition spending. When a business takes half of the spending in a market, more growth has to come from the market growing or from a new market. Q2 FY26 showed that risk: revenue of $1,924M landed inside the $1,915–$1,945M guide but about $16.3M (0.8%) below consensus, adjusted EBITDA was $1M under the low end of its range, and BofA downgraded on Aug 11. Q3 eased the worry, with gaming up nearly 50%, and consumer is the designated second engine, still early.

4. What heavy buybacks do to the balance sheet. Debt-to-equity reads 1.07, which looks high. The underlying picture is milder: $3.52B long-term debt against $3.17B cash is roughly $0.34B net debt against $6.41B of TTM adjusted EBITDA — under 0.1× net debt to adjusted EBITDA, as management also said on the call. The ratio is high because buybacks have shrunk book equity, not because of balance-sheet stress. The more useful question is price paid: $1.0B in Q1 2026 at about $455 a share, $551.3M in Q2 at about $501, and $1.4B in Q3 at about $341. The first two sit well above the $391.22 close, the third below it.

Notable One-Off Items

Apps divestiture (June 2025). AppLovin completed the sale of its mobile-games (Apps) business in June 2025, recognising a $106 million pre-tax gain. Prior periods are restated for discontinued operations — this is why the historical revenue series shows FY2022 at $2,817M and FY2023 at $1,842M (a reported −34.6%). That decline is not a business contraction; it is the games revenue leaving the continuing-operations line. Any growth comparison spanning 2022–2023 is meaningless without this adjustment. Note: the buyer was Tripledot Studios. AppLovin received $715.6 million in total at the June 30, 2025 close: $430.6 million in cash and Tripledot shares valued at $285.0 million, about 20% of Tripledot’s fully diluted equity (Q2 2025 10-Q).

Discontinued-operations drag, 2025. Net income for the first nine months of 2026 rose 75% ($3,911M against $2,231M), but the 2025 figure carries about a $100M loss from discontinued operations. On continuing operations alone the rise is 68% ($3,911M against $2,331M), which is the cleaner comparison.

Buybacks stepped up as the price fell. Long-term debt stands at $3.52B. In Q3 AppLovin repurchased and withheld 4.1M shares for $1.4B (about $341 a share), up from 1.1M shares for $551.3M in Q2 (about $501). CFO Matt Stumpf said the step-up came because "we saw this dislocation in the stock price versus the actual intrinsic value of the company." The board added $3.2B to the authorization, taking it to $3.6B at the end of October. Since the programme began, the company has repurchased and withheld 104M shares for $9.4B, an average of $90, per the call.

Stock-based compensation. Adjusted EBITDA of $1,842M in Q3 excludes $84.2M of stock-based compensation among other items, and the four-quarter total is $333.9M. GAAP net income of $1,439M in Q3 already counts that cost, so the $5.01B TTM GAAP net income figure is the conservative read and is the one behind the P/E above.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The economics are genuinely rare: 88.78% gross margin, 84% adjusted EBITDA margin, $4.88B TTM free cash flow and 137.63% ROIC, all produced by 898 people. Revenue grew 55% in Q3, $100M above the top of guidance, and management says the model rollout behind it is only partial. Gaming, the part Wells Fargo said was nearing a growth ceiling, grew nearly 50%, and consumer spend grew triple digits. The SEC inquiry closed in August 2026 with no recommended action, and one critical report was retracted. Even after +44.0% in two sessions the stock is 47.0% below its 52-week high at 18.90× 2027 consensus EPS and a 0.98 PEG. The board added $3.2B to the buyback authorization, with net debt of only $0.34B.

Bear Case

The stock rose 71.2% in five sessions, and Q4 guidance implies growth slowing to 8–10% from Q3 (Q3 was +14%). Foroughi himself expects "bigger Q4s followed by tougher Q1s" as e-commerce grows. Consensus for 2027 is +25.1% revenue and +19.3% EPS, below management’s long-term aim of about 30%. At about half of mobile-game user-acquisition spending (Wells Fargo’s estimate), AppLovin has limited easy share left to take. UBS’s Stephen Ju raised on the call that some advertisers fear e-commerce bidders will push up auction prices (Foroughi said such industry checks tend to be flawed). Consumer remains unquantified: AppLovin gives no advertiser count and Foroughi said it is too early for figures that predict the future. Underneath sits the verification problem: AppLovin operates the exchange, the bidder and the measurement layer, which is why four outside firms have been able to publish critical reports that outsiders cannot readily disprove. Most of those allegations remain neither proven nor disproven.

Top 3 Risks
01
Growth slows faster than the market now hopes. Consensus FY2026 EPS of $17.35 and revenue of $8.34B (+52.1%) sit behind this page’s forward numbers, and FY2027 consensus has revenue of $10.43B (+25.1%) and EPS of $20.70 (+19.3%). Those estimates assume the new models keep working and consumer keeps scaling. If gaming flattens or consumer scales more slowly, the estimates come down and today’s P/E turns out higher than it looks, and management has said that model gains won’t land on a perfect quarterly schedule.
02
The disclosure and verification gap is itself the risk — separate from any allegation. AppLovin runs the auction, the bidding model, and the measurement layer, and does not disclose revenue by vertical, e-commerce advertiser counts, or self-serve adoption. Even with the SEC inquiry concluded and one critical report retracted, an investor cannot independently verify the attribution quality that produces 65.89% net margins. That opacity invites further short reports, and means any future adverse disclosure would land on a market with no independent baseline to anchor against. This is a risk about what cannot be checked, not an assertion that anything is wrong.
03
Platform and privacy dependency, plus a 2.50 beta. AXON's edge depends on device- and app-level signal that Apple and Google can restrict unilaterally by policy, and that privacy regulators on both sides of the Atlantic continue to narrow. A single platform rule change could impair the model without any regulatory finding at all. On top of that, with a beta of 2.50, 4.26% of float short at the Oct 15 settlement and a 71.2% rise in five sessions, position size matters as much as the thesis: this stock has moved about 2.5× the market in both directions.
  Industry Commentary
AI-Driven Ad Targeting and Measurement Is Consolidating Budgets, Not Distributing Them
EMERGING TAILWIND
The defining shift in performance advertising is that machine-learning bidders have made the auction, not the creative, the primary source of return — and the models with the most closed-loop outcome data win. AppLovin's financials are the clearest evidence available: 88.78% gross margin, 77.78% operating margin, and $7,609M of TTM revenue produced by 898 employees (about $8.5M per head). That is not a media business's cost structure; it is a software business's. The catch, and it is the central industry tension, is that the same dynamic concentrates budgets into a handful of closed systems that grade their own results — AppLovin, Meta Advantage+, Google PMax, Amazon — and advertisers increasingly cannot compare them on a common measurement standard. Whoever measures, wins the credit.
Privacy and Platform Rules Remain the Sector's Live Wire — Even After a Clean SEC Outcome
ONGOING HEADWIND
The reported SEC inquiry into AppLovin's data-collection practices — first reported on Oct 6, 2025 — was described by CFO Matt Stumpf on Aug 5, 2026 as concluded with no recommended action. That is a clean outcome for this specific matter and should be read as such. But the structural exposure it highlighted has not changed: mobile ad-tech economics rest on device and in-app signal that Apple and Google control by policy and that privacy regulators continue to constrain. A platform rule change requires no investigation, no finding, and no notice period. For a company whose entire margin structure derives from signal quality, that is the most under-appreciated tail risk in the sector — larger, arguably, than any of the short-seller allegations, most of which remain unproven in either direction.
Can 84% Adjusted EBITDA Margins Survive Contact With E-Commerce? — Bull vs Bear
THE DECIDING QUESTION
Bull framing: AppLovin delivered an 84% adjusted EBITDA margin in Q3 while growing 55%, and guides to 84–85% for Q4 with data-center costs falling as a share of revenue. If margins hold anywhere near that while e-commerce scales, 18.90× 2027 earnings is modest for a business that turns this much revenue into cash: operating cash flow and free cash flow were $4.90B and $4.88B.

Bear framing: These margins were earned in mobile gaming, where AppLovin owns the exchange, the bidder, and the measurement. In e-commerce it owns none of the purchase signal — Amazon and Shopify-adjacent players do — and it is relying on partnership deals to bring in advertisers rather than running its ads everywhere. Serving mid-market merchants means more support, more creative production, and lower spend per account. The 84% margin is not a law of physics; it is a function of a specific market structure that does not obviously transfer.

Where it stands: Q3 gave the bull side its first evidence, with the margin at 84% while consumer grew triple digits. CFO Matt Stumpf said "We manage this business to EBITDA dollars and free cash flow rather than to a margin percentage," so a lower margin would not by itself mean the plan had failed. If Q4 and Q1 show consumer holding up and revenue growth near the 30% long-term aim, the bull case strengthens. If growth falls well short of consensus, or margin guidance drifts toward 80%, the bear case becomes the base case.
