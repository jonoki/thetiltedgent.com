01 Company Overview

Aptiv has now split itself in two twice. The company began life as General Motors' in-house parts arm, became Delphi when GM spun it out in 1999, shed its powertrain and aftermarket businesses as Delphi Technologies in December 2017 — and renamed what was left Aptiv. On April 1, 2026 it did it again: the EDS wiring-harness business was distributed to shareholders as Versigent PLC (NYSE: VGNT), one Versigent share for every three Aptiv shares held on the March 17, 2026 record date. Before leaving, Versigent paid Aptiv a cash dividend of about $1.9B, which Aptiv used to redeem $1,847M of senior notes early.

What remains — "New Aptiv", in management's own phrase — is a smaller, higher-margin business: the pro forma company had $12.40B of FY2025 net sales against $20.40B for the whole of old Aptiv. Its pitch is that connectors, sensors, vehicle compute and software sell into robots, drones and factories as well as cars. The market has not bought it yet. The stock closed March 2026 at $69.44, opened April at $60.16 as the Versigent value left, and has fallen a further 27.7% since the April month-end to $43.57 — most of that on August 4, 2026, when a second-quarter beat came with a $300M cut to full-year revenue guidance and the shares fell 16.6% in a day. The debate now: is this an industrial-technology company trading at an auto-supplier multiple, or an auto supplier with an industrial-technology slide deck?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Multi-year OEM programme awards Design-in switching costs Wind River software platform NVIDIA edge-AI collaboration Global manufacturing footprint Current ratio 2.02

An automotive component is chosen years before the car ships and then built for the life of the model. Once a part is designed into a platform, replacing it means re-validating it, so the supplier holds the revenue as long as the vehicle sells — which is why $10B of first-half bookings matters more than one quarter's sales. That lock-in protects share, not volume: if the automaker builds fewer cars, the supplier sells fewer parts, and a car-maker's delayed launch is the supplier's delayed revenue. The August cut was exactly that: $150M from customer production schedules, $100M from delayed launches and ramps.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China. $150M of the $300M revenue cut came from customer production schedules — local Chinese carmakers and European carmakers exporting to China, per the CFO on the Q2 call. China was +5% in Q2 but −4% for the first half.
02
Forecasting credibility. The first guidance issued as New Aptiv was cut at the first full quarter. Management conceded it had been too optimistic about launches and ramps. The next guide will be discounted until it is beaten.
03
Cash conversion. First-half free cash flow was −$196M. Operating cash flow from continuing operations was $82M for the half, against $531M a year earlier. The full-year $625–725M FCF guide depends on a second half that the revenue guide has just lowered.
04
The growth business is not growing yet. Intelligent Systems — the segment behind the robotics, drone and edge-AI narrative — was flat in Q2. The June rally priced the narrative; the August print priced the numbers.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported figures. First, discontinued operations: EDS results are restated as discontinued for every period presented, so year-on-year continuing figures compare like with like, while TTM screens do not. Discontinued operations lost $50M in Q2 2026. Second, the Q3 2025 loss: old Aptiv reported a net loss of $355M in that quarter (StockAnalysis), which is why FY2025 GAAP EPS was only $0.75 and pro forma New Aptiv FY2025 diluted EPS was −$0.20 (8-K/A pro forma, April 2026). The cause of the loss was not established from the sources fetched. Third, adjusted vs GAAP: Q2 2026 adjusted EPS was $1.63 against GAAP continuing $1.40, and the FY2026 guides are $5.60–5.80 adjusted vs $4.06–4.26 GAAP. The forward multiple you quote depends on which you pick.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Read this chart with the spin in mind. Neither digrin nor Yahoo Finance adjusts APTV's history for the Versigent distribution; the "adjusted" columns equal the raw closes. So every point before April 2026 is the price of a company that still owned EDS. The spin shows up as a gap: $69.44 at the March 31 close, $60.16 at the April 1 open — $9.28, or 13.4% of the March price. That approximates what one-third of a Versigent share was worth, not a loss to holders. On raw prices APTV is down 70.8% from the September 2021 close of $148.97 to $43.57. Scaling the pre-spin history by that gap (our approximation, using the April 1 open rather than an official adjustment factor) gives about −66%. Either way it is a lost five years, and it started long before the spin. The October 2021 close of $172.89 is the high of the window; no month-end since has come close.

The 6-period monthly RSI is 19.9 at the September 21 close — the lowest reading in the five-year series, below August 2026's 20.6 and June 2022's 21.6. The April 2026 reading of 25.8 is partly an artefact: the RSI calculation sees the spin gap as a price fall. The 10-month moving average is about $62.92, 44% above the current price, and that average still contains pre-spin prices. What the chart does show cleanly is the post-spin path: $60.26 at the end of April, a narrative-driven run to $67.94 in May and an intraday $78.49 in June, then four straight down months to a September close that is the lowest point on the chart.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 7.6x consensus 2026 earnings and roughly 6x guided adjusted EBITDA for a business with a 14.4% adjusted operating margin that is still rising, $10B of first-half bookings against a $20B target, a debt load cut by about $2.1B with Versigent's cash, and a buyback of more than $600M planned for 2026 on a $9B market cap. The trailing P/E of 39 is an accounting artefact of a spin and a 2025 loss, not a price. Every one of 20 covering analysts has a target at or above $55. If the second half delivers the lowered guide and Intelligent Systems starts growing, the multiple has room to double before it looks demanding for a company selling into robots, drones and edge AI as well as cars.

Bear Case

New Aptiv cut its first real guide at its first full quarter, blaming the same China and launch-timing risks every auto supplier carries. The "industrial technology" segment was flat, and the robotics-and-drones ambition is about $300M of revenue several years out — under 3% of this year's sales. First-half free cash flow was negative. The shares have fallen 27.7% since the first post-spin month-end, set a five-year low, lost a FTSE index bid and drew a J.P. Morgan downgrade. A 7.6x multiple is what the market pays for a cyclical auto supplier. The bear view: that is the right multiple, and the targets have not caught up.

Top 3 Risks

01
Auto production volumes, led by China. Half of the $300M revenue cut came from customer production schedules. On the call, management said its earlier assumption of Chinese government stimulus had not played out. It does not control the fix.
02
Cash conversion. −$196M of first-half free cash flow against a $625–725M full-year guide. A miss on second-half cash would hit the buyback, and the buyback is a large part of the per-share story.
03
Narrative risk in Intelligent Systems. The June rally was priced on edge AI, robotics and drones; the segment behind it was flat in Q2 and $50M of the cut was software timing. If the growth business keeps reporting flat, the market will value New Aptiv on its component volumes alone.
Industry Commentary
China's domestic car market and the export channel
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

On the Q2 call Aptiv's CEO put domestic Chinese retail car sales at about 20% below the prior year and said local carmakers were cutting second-half production; the CFO added that European carmakers exporting to China were revising schedules too. For a component supplier this arrives as lower volumes on programmes it has already won. Aptiv's own regional numbers show the swing: China +5% in Q2 but −4% for the half, with EMEA −8% in the quarter.

Launch delays across the industry
ONGOING HEADWIND

$100M of Aptiv's cut came from delayed launches and ramps. Per the call, management now expects launches to add about 200 basis points of growth in the second half, 100bp less than before. A supplier books revenue when the customer's new vehicle actually ships, so one automaker's late model moves revenue out of this year's guide at every supplier on the programme.

Edge AI and the non-automotive pivot
EMERGING TAILWIND

The spin was designed to leave a company whose sensors, compute and software can be sold outside the car. Evidence so far: non-automotive revenue grew 12% in Q2 (per the call), a first commercial drone award worth about $500M over five years, and the NVIDIA Jetson collaboration. The bull framing: the market is valuing a nascent industrial-tech franchise at zero, and every new non-auto award raises the floor. The bear framing: a $300M multi-year robotics-and-drones ambition against $12.7B of sales is a rounding error, and in June the stock paid up for exactly this story before giving it all back.
