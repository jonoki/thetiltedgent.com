01 Company Overview

Alexandria is the landlord to the lab bench. Founded in 1994 by Joel S. Marcus and Jerry M. Sudarsky and public since 1997, it built a REIT dedicated to life-science real estate: laboratory and office campuses clustered next to research universities in what it calls its AAA innovation clusters, Greater Boston, the San Francisco Bay Area, San Diego, Seattle, Maryland, Research Triangle and New York City. It owns 36.0 million rentable square feet of operating properties, 80% of its annual rental revenue comes from its "Megacampus" platform, and 57% comes from investment-grade or publicly traded large-cap tenants (all as of June 30, 2026). GICS files it under Office REITs; economically it is a specialist in a building type (wet labs with heavy ventilation, power and floor loading) that ordinary office landlords cannot easily supply.

For a REIT the earnings line to read is FFO, not EPS, and at Alexandria the two tell completely different stories. GAAP says the company lost $6.04 per share over the last twelve months, mostly because of write-downs: $2.20B of real-estate impairments in 2025 and another $222.5M in Q2 2026. FFO per share as adjusted, the company's own measure, says it still earns cash: $7.84 over the last four quarters. The problem is the direction. FFO as adjusted was $9.47 in 2024 and $9.01 in 2025, and is guided to $6.35–$6.45 for 2026, roughly 29% below 2025 at the midpoint. The dividend followed: cut 45% in December 2025 from $1.32 to $0.72 a quarter. The stock closed at $222.96 in December 2021 and at $53.34 on September 21, 2026. The market has priced the landlord as if the biotech slowdown that emptied some of its space is permanent. The question this report works through is whether an 86.9%-occupied portfolio at under 7x trailing FFO is a value, or a portfolio still marking itself down.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Cluster locations near research universities Megacampus scale (80% of ARR) Specialised lab build-out 57% investment-grade / large-cap tenants 7.7-yr average lease term 97% leases with annual escalators 9.7-yr average debt term

The moat is location plus specification. A lab tenant needs to be within reach of the talent, universities and venture money of a handful of clusters, and it needs a building engineered for wet labs. Alexandria spent three decades assembling those buildings in those places, and long leases with fixed escalators lock in the rent. What the moat does not protect against is supply and demand inside the cluster. When tenants have more choice, the landlord pays for it at renewal, and renewal rents of −9.6% cash in the first half of 2026 are that bill. The moat protects the franchise; it does not protect the price per square foot.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Occupancy is still falling. 90.9% → 87.7% → 86.9% over two quarters, with year-end guidance of 86.2–87.8%. On a 36.0M RSF portfolio, each point of occupancy is roughly 360,000 square feet (our arithmetic) of empty space still carrying taxes and operating costs.
02
Asset values keep being marked down. $2,202.8M of real-estate impairments in 2025 and $222.5M in Q2 2026 (plus $12.4M of impairments on non-real-estate venture investments in Q1 2026). Impairments are excluded from FFO as adjusted, but each one is the company saying part of its balance sheet is worth less than it was carried at.
03
The funding plan depends on selling. The 2026 capital plan leans on $2.1–$3.7B of dispositions. If buyers demand lower prices, Alexandria either accepts them, which would support the bear view of its assets, or sells less and borrows more.
04
Tenant funding cycle. 43% of annual rental revenue comes from tenants that are not investment-grade or large-cap public companies (the complement of the reported 57%). Those tenants are more exposed to the life-science funding climate.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items dominate the GAAP numbers. First, impairments: $2,202.8M of real-estate impairment charges in 2025 and $222.5M in Q2 2026, which is why GAAP shows a loss while FFO stays positive. Second, a $366.4M gain on early extinguishment of debt in Q1 2026, from February tender offers that repurchased $1.33B of senior-note principal; it turned Q1 GAAP EPS positive ($2.10 per the release; $2.11 on StockAnalysis). Third, the dividend reset on December 3, 2025, which the company said preserves about $410 million of liquidity a year. None of the three changes what a tenant pays in rent, which is why FFO, occupancy and renewal spreads are the numbers to watch.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $191.07 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $53.34, ARE lost 72.1% of its price, and 76.1% from the December 2021 month-end high of $222.96. Dividends softened that, but not by much against a decline of nearly three-quarters; total return was not calculated. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 6.8x trailing FFO and 8.3x the 2026 guide for the specialist lab landlord, with a 5.40% yield covered more than twice by guided FFO (45% payout). The damage is visible and quantified: $2.4B of impairments since the start of 2025, the dividend reset, and $1.33B of notes bought back in a tender. Leasing volume rose 60% in Q2, cash renewal spreads improved from −15.8% in Q1 to −4.3% in Q2, 80% of rent comes from Megacampus assets, 57% from investment-grade or large-cap tenants, and leases run 7.7 years on average with escalators on 97% of them. If occupancy stabilises in the high 80s and the disposition program closes at reasonable prices, the market is pricing a trough as a new normal.

Bear Case

Every trend that feeds FFO is still pointed down: occupancy 90.9% → 86.9% in six months, same-property NOI −10.6%, FFO from $9.47 (2024) to $6.40 guided (2026), and a guidance midpoint that implies a weaker second half of 2026 than the first. Cheap on P/FFO is what a shrinking FFO stream looks like, and selling $2.9B of assets to fund construction and debt reduction shrinks it further. The impairments are the company confirming some assets are worth less than it carried them at; disposition prices could confirm it again. The average analyst target is $53.00 — the Street sees no upside either.

Top 3 Risks

01
Occupancy and rent reset. Occupancy at 86.9% with year-end guidance as low as 86.2%, and renewal rents still negative on a cash basis. If tenant demand in the clusters stays soft, the rent roll resets lower as leases roll.
02
Execution on $2.1–$3.7B of dispositions. The capital plan requires selling assets. Weak prices force a choice between dilutive sales, more leverage (7.0x net debt and preferred to adjusted EBITDA, 2Q26 annualised, against a 5.6x–6.2x 4Q26 target) or less construction.
03
Further write-downs. $2.2B of impairments in 2025 and $222.5M in Q2 2026 show carrying values are still being revised. More write-offs would not hit FFO as adjusted directly, but they erode book equity and credibility.
Industry Commentary
A tenant's market in lab space
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Alexandria's own numbers describe the market: occupancy down four points in six months, same-property NOI down about a tenth, renewal rents below expiring rents. Market-wide lab vacancy and new-supply figures for the clusters were not retrieved in the sources fetched for this report, so the size of any overhang is described here only through Alexandria's reported results.

Rates and the REIT cost of capital
ONGOING HEADWIND

A REIT that funds development needs its cost of capital below its development yield. At 6.8x trailing FFO, new equity is expensive, which is why the 2026 plan is funded with asset sales and retained cash (a 42% FFO payout) rather than share issuance. The balance sheet has room — $3.60B of liquidity and a 9.7-year average debt term — but leverage is targeted at 5.6x–6.2x net debt and preferred to adjusted EBITDA for 4Q26, not lower.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull framing: 6.8x FFO and a 5.40% covered yield for a portfolio concentrated in the main US life-science clusters is a price for permanent decline, and leasing volume, renewal spreads and a recovering RSI suggest the decline is slowing. The bear framing: FFO is guided down about 29% this year, occupancy guidance has been lowered once already, and the business is shrinking itself to fund itself. The way to decide is not the multiple. It is two numbers reported every quarter: occupancy and cash renewal spreads. When occupancy stops falling and cash spreads turn positive, FFO has found its floor.
