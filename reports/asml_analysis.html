01 Company Overview

ASML Holding N.V. builds the photolithography machines that print circuit patterns onto silicon wafers — and it is the only company on earth that makes EUV (extreme ultraviolet lithography: a machine that uses 13.5-nanometre light, generated by vaporising tin droplets with a high-power laser, to etch features far smaller than conventional light can resolve). No EUV tool means no leading-edge logic or DRAM below roughly the 7nm class. TSMC, Samsung and Intel all buy from the same supplier because there is no second supplier.

Full-year 2025 revenue was €32,667M, up 15.58% year over year, producing net income of €9,609M and diluted EPS of €24.71. On a TTM basis through September 2026, revenue is €39,474M ($44.98B) with net income of €13,474M ($15.36B) and diluted EPS of €34.97 ($39.85). Market capitalisation is $635.0B (our calculation: 383.10M shares × the $1,657.44 close on Oct 15, 2026). ASML ranks #13 in the Nasdaq-100 by market value.

The latest quarter, reported on Oct 14, 2026, was a record: €11.66B of sales (+55% on a year earlier), a 57.5% gross margin and €4.96B of net income, about €0.5B of it a one-off gain on ASML's stake in the French AI company Mistral AI. The bigger news was in the CEO's words: customers keep raising their demand forecasts, some want to place orders for 2029 and even 2030, and ASML now expects the factory output it has planned for 2027 and 2028 to fall short of what customers want. The shares still fell 3.9% that day.

The most important thing to hold in mind while reading the rest of this page: every income-statement and segment number ASML publishes is in euros. Every price, market cap, valuation multiple and analyst target is in dollars. Mixing them is the single most common way an ASML write-up goes wrong — a €40.82 consensus EPS is not a $40.82 consensus EPS.

What the machine actually does. Chipmaking is photography at absurd scale: a pattern on a mask is projected through optics onto a light-sensitive coating on a silicon wafer, and the smallest feature you can print is bounded by the wavelength of the light. The industry ran 193nm light for two decades and bought resolution with tricks — immersing the lens in water, and splitting one layer into two, three or four separate exposures ("multi-patterning"), each of which costs a mask, a pass, cycle time and yield. EUV replaces the light itself with 13.5nm radiation, a ~14× shorter wavelength, which collapses several patterned passes back into one.

Why nobody has copied it in twenty years. EUV is absorbed by air and by glass, so the entire optical path must run in vacuum and use mirrors, not lenses — mirrors polished by Carl Zeiss to a flatness that is the standard reference example of the most precise surfaces ever manufactured. The light source is not a lamp: droplets of molten tin are fired across a chamber tens of thousands of times a second and hit twice by a high-power CO₂ laser to strike a plasma. The mask must be reflective, defect-free and pellicle-protected. Each of those is a separate decades-long supply chain, and ASML controls the integration of all of them. This is not a patent moat that expires; it is an accumulated-know-how-and-supplier-lock moat, which is why two well-capitalised competitors (Canon, Nikon) that still sell DUV never shipped an EUV tool.

Unit economics. ASML recognised revenue on 48 EUV systems (NXE and EXE combined) in FY2025 on €11,600M of EUV system sales — an average selling price of roughly €242M per EUV system. That is the honest derived number; ASML does not publish per-model pricing.

High-NA — the next generation. High-NA EUV (a higher "numerical aperture" optical system, ASML's EXE:5000-series, which resolves finer features than the standard low-NA NXE tools) moved from pilot to production during this cycle. ASML's Q4 2025 EUV system sales included two High-NA systems, per CFO Roger Dassen on the Q4 2025 call; CEO Fouquet stated on the Q4 2025 call that Intel had announced "the qualification and acceptance of their EXE:5200B system, which will be used in high-volume manufacturing for their leading-edge nodes," and that "more systems" would be released to customers in 2026. ASML's Jul 15, 2026 release recorded the milestone as "High NA EUV reaches new readiness milestone with first high-volume Logic product": Intel 18A layers qualified on High-NA for high-volume manufacturing. On the Q3 2026 call Fouquet added that "Samsung has also announced that they will be using High NA in high volume manufacturing starting 2028," and on Sep 8, 2026 ASML and TSMC announced an initiative to move the industry to large-format photomasks (the stencils the machine projects; 12-inch, per the Q3 presentation) for High-NA. Cost per High-NA unit: ASML's results do not break it out. Then-CEO Peter Wennink put it at €300–350M per unit in November 2022 (The Elec); the anchor in ASML's own numbers is the €242M FY2025 blended EUV ASP above, and High-NA sells above that blend.

Capacity: ASML expects to ship around 65 low-NA EUV systems in 2026 and plans to raise low-NA EUV capacity by about 30% for 2027 (CFO Dassen, July 2026). In October 2026 it said even its planned supply will not fully meet 2027 and 2028 demand, and that it is investigating ways to raise 2028 capacity beyond the 30% increase it had announced.

DUV (deep ultraviolet) is the 193nm and 248nm generation that prints everything not requiring EUV — power management, analogue, automotive, image sensors, mature-node logic, and the non-critical layers of even the most advanced chips. It is the majority of exposure steps in a modern fab by count. Immersion DUV ("ArFi") tools, where water fills the gap between the final lens and the wafer to raise the effective numerical aperture, are the high-value end of this line. Also included here is ASML's metrology and inspection business (e-beam and optical tools that measure whether the patterning actually worked).

Unlike EUV, DUV genuinely has competitors — Canon and Nikon both sell lithography scanners — but ASML is the volume and technology leader, particularly in immersion. ASML sold 300 new and 27 used lithography systems in FY2025 across all technologies. DUV matters strategically for a second reason: it is the product line that can legally ship to more destinations than EUV, which makes it the focal point of export-control policy.

Capacity: ASML expects about 130 immersion DUV shipments in 2026 and intends to raise immersion capacity by 30% in 2027 (CFO Dassen, July 2026); in October 2026 it said it is investigating ways to raise 2028 capacity beyond the 30% increase it had announced.

Installed Base Management is revenue earned on machines ASML has already sold: service and maintenance contracts, spare parts, field options, and performance upgrades that raise the throughput or resolution of a tool already bolted to a customer's fab floor. A lithography scanner is a twenty-year asset costing tens or hundreds of millions of euros; the customer cannot afford downtime and cannot service it themselves, so the aftermarket is effectively captive.

The numbers make the point better than the adjective. IBM revenue was €8,193M in FY2025, up 26% year over year, and represented 25.1% of total net sales. In the three quarters since, it has been a larger share of a bigger business: €2,488M in Q1 2026 (28.4% of €8,767M), €2,762M in Q2 2026 (29.6% of €9,326M) and €3,289M in Q3 2026 (28.2% of €11,662M), with about €3.7B guided for Q4. Quarter to quarter that share moves partly because system revenue is lumpy — but the trend is real and it is the reason ASML's downside quarters are less ugly than a pure capital-equipment company's should be. Roughly three euros in ten now arrive from an annuity rather than from a purchase order. Management guided on the Q4 2025 call to "another year of revenue growth" for IBM in 2026, and said Q2 2026 revenue and gross margin were "both above guidance, driven primarily by higher than expected Installed Base Management sales." In Q3 it happened again: IBM sales came in above guidance on demand for productivity upgrades, which Fouquet said "drove the gross margin above guidance at 57.5%."

There is a compounding mechanic here worth naming: every system ASML ships permanently enlarges the base that generates this revenue. The 300 new systems sold in 2025 are 300 new annuities. That is why the segment can grow 26% in a year when total revenue grew 15.6%.

Structural note: ASML is the only party that can service an ASML scanner. Competitor share in this segment is zero by construction.

The case that ASML is the most genuinely monopolistic large company in the S&P 500's orbit does not rest on rhetoric — it rests on a count. There is exactly one commercially available EUV lithography system, and ASML makes it. Advanced logic and DRAM below roughly the 7nm class cannot be manufactured economically without one. Every leading-edge fab operator on the planet must therefore transact with the same Dutch vendor, and the vendor's own numbers show what that permits: 54.34% gross margin, 34.14% net margin, 65.64% ROE and 92.13% ROIC on a TTM basis, against a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.09. A 92% ROIC with almost no borrowing is the financial signature of a company that does not have to negotiate.

The durability of it is the more interesting question, and the answer is that EUV's barrier is not one thing you could invent around. It is the vacuum optical column, the Zeiss mirror sets, the tin-droplet plasma light source, the reflective mask ecosystem, the resist chemistry, the metrology to prove any of it worked, and — most of all — the twenty years of field data on how the assembly behaves in a real fab at real throughput. A competitor starting today would need not just the physics but the supply chain, and much of that supply chain has been built to ASML's specification and effectively for ASML alone. Two decades of Canon and Nikon not shipping an EUV tool is the empirical test, and it has already been run. The €38.8B backlog reported at end-2025 is the commercial expression of the same fact: customers pre-commit years of capital because there is no alternative queue to join. In October 2026 that queue got longer: ASML said some customers want to place orders for 2029 and even 2030, and that its planned supply for 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet demand.

The uncomfortable corollary is that a monopoly this complete stops being a purely commercial asset and becomes a geopolitical instrument. ASML's shipping list is a lever that governments want to hold, which is precisely why the company's most material risk is not competitive at all.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. China export controls and a ~20% revenue dependency. On the Q4 2025 investor call CEO Christophe Fouquet said the company expects "the China region's share in our total net sales in 2026 to be in line with our current system backlog, which is around 20 percent." One customer region supplying a fifth of revenue would be a concentration risk in any business; here it is a fifth of revenue sitting inside an active policy dispute. In the Q1 2026 release Fouquet stated that the guidance range "accommodates potential outcomes of ongoing discussions around export controls" — the company itself is treating the rule set as an open variable rather than a settled parameter. On the Q3 2026 call (Oct 14) CFO Roger Dassen repeated the figure: "The China part of the total business we expect to be around 20%." These rules have been revised repeatedly. Do not act on any assumed rule state — verify it against the Dutch government and US BIS notices directly before sizing this risk. What is not in doubt is the exposure (~20% of 2026 sales) and management's own characterisation of the rules as unresolved.

2. Customer concentration among a handful of fabs. ASML sold 300 new lithography systems in all of FY2025, and recognised revenue on just 48 EUV systems. That is not a market with thousands of buyers; leading-edge logic is essentially three companies and leading-edge DRAM is essentially three more. A single large customer deferring a fab build — or accelerating one — visibly moves ASML's quarter. Concentration cuts both ways with a monopoly: ASML cannot be replaced, but it also cannot replace a lost customer, because there are no spare ones.

3. Capital-equipment cyclicality, which the technology position does not repeal. This is the vulnerability investors most often reason themselves out of. ASML sells wafer-fab equipment — capital goods bought in waves tied to customers' construction budgets, not consumable goods bought every month. FY2024 revenue grew +2.56% immediately after FY2023's +30.16%, with no change whatsoever in the competitive position. The share price is more honest about this than the narrative: the stock closed October 2024 down 19.3% in a single month, and closed March 2025 at $662.63, 18.5% below its October 2021 close. A monopoly on the tool does not confer a monopoly on the customer's capex committee.

4. Bookings opacity and valuation. Net new system orders ("bookings") are the forward indicator that moves this stock more than reported revenue, because revenue is just backlog converting on a schedule already known. The last bookings figure ASML disclosed was Q4 2025: €13.2B, of which €7.4B EUV and €5.8B non-EUV, alongside the €38.8B year-end backlog. The Q2 and Q3 2026 press releases contain no bookings or backlog figure at all; the Q3 release says only that order-book momentum "continues to increase quarter on quarter this year." Less frequent disclosure of the most predictive metric, on a stock trading at 41.6× trailing earnings, means the market is pricing forward demand with a longer information lag than it used to have.

Notable One-Off Items

ASML's statements show no large write-down, litigation charge or acquisition distortion. Five items are worth flagging as distortions to naive comparisons:

A one-off gain in Q3 2026. The Q3 statement of operations carries a €509.7M gain on equity investments, before tax, plus €134.7M of profit from equity-method investments (companies ASML owns a minority stake in). CFO Dassen said about €500M of Q3 net income came from revaluing the first tranche of ASML's Mistral investment. Without it, Q3 earnings and the trailing P/E would look less flattering.

Guidance revised upward three times in nine months. FY2026 total net sales guidance moved from €34–39B (Jan 28, 2026) to €36–40B (Apr 15, 2026) to €43–45B (Jul 15, 2026) to €44–45B (Oct 14, 2026), with the gross-margin guide lifted from 51–53% to 54–56% and then to around 56%. The Q4 guide of €14.2–15.2B is what the full-year range implies after €29.76B of sales in the first three quarters. Any trailing growth rate understates the current year.

FY2023 free cash flow of €3,288M against net income of €7,839M. A €4.5B gap, driven by working capital and customer-payment timing on a capital-equipment order book rather than by earnings quality. FY2022 FCF was €7,205M and FY2025 was €11,085M — the series is genuinely lumpy and single-year FCF multiples on this company are close to meaningless.

Bookings disclosure changed. The Q4 2025 release and call carried explicit net-bookings figures (€13.2B in Q4, €28,035M for FY2025) and a €38.8B backlog. The Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026 releases contain neither. The most decision-relevant forward metric is now disclosed less often.

Two data sources disagree on the analyst picture — see Section 05. That is a source discrepancy, not a fact, and it is flagged rather than reconciled.

In plain English: RSI compares the size of recent up-months to recent down-months on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has run hard and is called "overbought"; below 30 it has been beaten down and is called "oversold". On monthly data these are slow, coarse signals — useful for spotting exhaustion, useless for timing.

Five-year trajectory. ASML closed October 2021 at $812.88 and closed at $1,657.44 on Oct 15, 2026 — a price return of +103.9% over the chart's window, roughly 15.4% a year before dividends. Over the same dates SPY returned +68.05% and QQQ +88.55% (price only). That headline number hides the shape entirely, and the shape is the lesson. From the October 2021 close to the August 2025 close of $742.62, the stock returned −8.6% in almost four years. Then it more than doubled: +123.2% from that August 2025 close to Oct 15, 2026.

Nearly four years of going nowhere, then a double in about fourteen months. That is the empirical answer to whether a technological monopoly protects you from a capital-equipment cycle. It does not. It protects the business; the equity still trades the cycle. Anyone who bought the moat story in mid-2021 was correct about the company and waited four years to be paid.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

ASML is the only supplier of EUV lithography on earth, and no advanced logic or DRAM node below roughly 7nm is manufacturable without it — a position no competitor has replicated in twenty years of trying. The financials confirm it: 92.13% ROIC, 65.64% ROE, 34.14% net margin, net cash, and 0.38% short interest. Management has raised FY2026 guidance three times in nine months, from €34–39B to €44–45B against FY2025's €32.7B — implied growth of 35–38% — and Q3 2026 delivered a record €11.66B with a 57.5% gross margin, with Q4 guided to €14.2–15.2B. Installed Base Management, the recurring service-and-upgrade leg, is now ~28–30% of revenue and grew 26% in FY2025. ASML says its planned supply for 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet demand, some customers want to order for 2029 and 2030, and it is studying a 2028 capacity increase beyond 30%: a multi-year demand statement in capital form.

Bear Case

You are paying 41.6× trailing earnings, flattered by a one-off €0.5B gain in Q3, for a capital-equipment company after a 64% 52-week move, and capital equipment is cyclical no matter how good the technology is — FY2024 revenue grew 2.56% right after FY2023 grew 30.16%, and the stock fell 8.6% from October 2021 to August 2025 while the moat was fully intact the whole time. The market's response to a record quarter and a raised outlook was a 3.9% fall on the day. Around 20% of expected 2026 sales come from a region governed by export rules management itself calls unresolved. The customer list is a handful of fabs: 300 systems sold in an entire year, 48 of them EUV, so a single deferred fab is a visible quarter. And the metric that actually moves the stock — net bookings — no longer appears in the quarterly release, so the market is pricing forward demand on a longer lag than it used to.

Top 3 Risks
01
China export controls on a ~20% revenue base. CEO Fouquet guided China to "around 20 percent" of 2026 total net sales, in line with the system backlog, CFO Dassen repeated "around 20%" on the Q3 2026 call, and Fouquet separately said guidance "accommodates potential outcomes of ongoing discussions around export controls." The rules have been revised repeatedly, which is itself the risk: a fifth of revenue is governed by a variable neither the company nor an outside analyst controls. Verify the live Dutch and US rule state before sizing this.
02
Customer concentration and order lumpiness. 300 new systems and 48 EUV systems in all of FY2025, sold to a leading-edge customer base of roughly a half-dozen companies. There is no diversification available: ASML cannot replace a lost customer because there are no spare ones. Quarterly results swing on individual fab decisions, and the disclosure that would let you see those decisions early — bookings — is no longer in the quarterly release.
03
Cyclicality plus a demanding multiple. 41.6× trailing earnings prices in the current upcycle continuing. The five-year monthly series shows a −49% drawdown on monthly closes (Oct 2021 – Sep 2022), a −34% round trip in four months (2024), and a near-four-year stretch of negative return (Oct 2021 – Aug 2025) — all with the monopoly fully intact. A monopoly on the tool is not a monopoly on the customer's capex committee, and the equity has repeatedly proved it.
  Industry Commentary
EUV is the physical bottleneck of the AI build-out
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every advanced AI accelerator and every HBM stack that feeds it is patterned on an ASML EUV tool. There is no second source and no workaround at these dimensions. ASML recognised revenue on just 48 EUV systems in FY2025 expects to ship around 65 low-NA EUV systems in 2026, with about 30% more capacity planned for 2027 — meaning the world's supply of leading-edge patterning capability is measured in dozens of machines per year and is set by one company's factory schedule. When ASML says its planned 2027 and 2028 supply will not fully meet demand, and that it is investigating more than the 30% increase it had announced for 2028, that is the industry's growth ceiling being negotiated in public.
Export controls: a fifth of revenue on a political variable
ONGOING HEADWIND
China is expected at ~20% of 2026 total net sales per the CEO in January and the CFO in October, tracking the system backlog; and management explicitly built "potential outcomes of ongoing discussions around export controls" into its own guidance range. Because ASML is a genuine choke point, its shipping list is a policy instrument, and both Dutch and US authorities have exercised that leverage before. Anyone underwriting this name should read the live licensing position directly rather than a summary of it.
The bull and bear both start from the same number
EMERGING TAILWIND
FY2026 guidance went €34–39B → €36–40B → €43–45B → €44–45B in nine months, with gross margin guided from 51–53% to around 56%. The bull reads that as demand outrunning even the monopolist's own forecasts — capacity being added ~30% across both EUV and DUV immersion for 2027 and still short of 2027–28 demand by ASML's own account, Installed Base Management compounding at 26% and now ~28–30% of sales, High-NA in high-volume manufacturing at Intel with Samsung to follow from 2028. The bear reads the same sequence as the top of a cycle: guidance raises cluster at peaks, wafer-fab equipment orders are cyclical by construction, and 41.6× trailing earnings leaves little room for the digestion year that followed the last surge — FY2024's +2.56% after FY2023's +30.16%. Both readings are consistent with the data. The disagreement is entirely about where in the cycle this sits, which is why targets run from $869.15 to $2,888.
