01 Company Overview

Atlas Copco makes the machines that sit in the back room of almost every factory: air compressors (Compressor Technique), vacuum pumps for chip fabs, labs and food packaging (Vacuum Technique), industrial power tools and assembly systems for car plants and general industry (Industrial Technique), and portable compressors, generators and pumps for construction sites and rental fleets (Power Technique). Founded in Stockholm in 1873, it spun off its mining and rock-drilling arm as Epiroc in June 2018 and has since built a second engine in vacuum through the earlier purchases of Edwards (2014) and Leybold (2016). In 2025 it booked revenues of SEK 168.3B (−5%, with currency alone taking 6 points off), an operating margin of 20.3% and basic EPS of SEK 5.43. About 38% of revenue is service — maintenance, parts and rental on an installed base of machines that run around the clock.

The story of 2026 is the order book. In Q2 orders jumped 27% to a record SEK 50.95B; in Q3 2026 they rose 29% to SEK 52.34B (25% organic, meaning before currency and acquisitions), higher again, while revenue reached a record SEK 47.92B (+11% organic) and the operating margin rose to 20.8%. Two-thirds of the extra orders came from one place: vacuum orders rose 87%, with semiconductor and flat-panel orders at what the report calls "another record level". CEO Vagner Rego put it plainly in his comment on the report: "The overall demand for Atlas Copco Group's products, services and solutions was very strong, and order intake increased significantly." The market wanted more. The report also said organic orders were essentially unchanged from Q2, and the A share fell 7.7% on the day. At SEK 186.70 it trades at 32.9 times trailing earnings, up 12.4% since the end of 2025 but 12.8% below its October 6 high. The debate is simple to state: is a 1.09x book-to-bill (orders over revenue) the plateau of a chip-equipment upswing that converts into revenue through 2027, or the top of a cyclical spike? Management's own outlook — that customer activity will "remain at the current level" — is unchanged for a second quarter.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base + service (38% of revenue) Global leader in vacuum & abatement ROCE 24% vs 8% cost of capital Serial acquirer: 126 deals 2021–25 Multi-brand portfolio (Edwards, Leybold, Chicago Pneumatic, Desoutter) Energy-efficiency engineering Low leverage (net debt/EBITDA 0.3)

The moat is the installed base. A compressor or a fab vacuum pump is a small part of the customer's capital budget but a large part of their uptime risk, and once it is installed the owner buys service, parts and upgrades from the maker for a decade. That is how a compressor business earns a 72% return on capital employed and why the group as a whole clears its own 8.0% cost-of-capital benchmark three times over at 24%. The second layer is the operating model: four business areas, dozens of divisions, each free to buy small distributors and specialists — 29 acquisitions in 2025 alone — and bolt them onto a global service network. The vulnerability is that none of this protects against the cycle. Equipment is still 62% of revenue, and when customers stop building fabs or car plants, equipment orders fall regardless of the moat.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The growth is semiconductor-led. Electronics was 60% of Vacuum Technique's 2025 orders, and vacuum delivered about two-thirds of the Q3 2026 order increase (SEK 7.7B of SEK 11.8B). Chip-equipment spending moves in sharp cycles; in 2025 the same business area saw revenue fall and took restructuring charges. A record order quarter is the high-water mark of a cycle as often as the start of one.
02
Acquisitions dilute returns. Compressor Technique's return on capital employed has fallen from 80% to 72% in a year and its Q3 margin from 25.3% to 23.6%; the Q2 report blamed that area's margin decline mainly on recently acquired companies, and the Q3 report says acquisitions weighed on the group margin. The group's ROCE is 24%, down from 25%. Each deal is small; the cumulative effect of dozens a year is visible.
03
The krona cuts reported growth. Most revenue is earned abroad — Asia/Oceania and North America alone are 62% — but reported in SEK. Currency took 6 points off 2025 revenue and SEK 3.4B off operating profit; in the first nine months of 2026 it cut orders by 5% and operating profit by SEK 1.5B, though it added 1% in Q3 alone.
04
Power Technique's capital intensity. The rental fleet is growing through acquisitions and its return on capital employed has slipped to 13% from 15% a year earlier, the lowest in the group — and the area changes president on January 1, 2027.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items move the figures. First, currency: a stronger krona took SEK 3.4B off 2025 operating profit and about 0.6 points off the margin, and kept cutting reported growth in 2026 (−5% on nine-month orders), though it added 1% in Q3. Second, items affecting comparability: SEK −0.8B in 2025, of which SEK −0.7B was restructuring in Vacuum and Industrial Technique; in Q3 2026, SEK −245M, of which SEK −113M from the provision for share-based incentive programmes, which moves with the share price, and SEK −132M of restructuring in Industrial Technique's automotive business in Germany — the adjusted margin of 21.3% strips them out. Third, the extra distribution: the FY2025 payout of SEK 5.00 per share (SEK 24.4B in total) is 92% of 2025 EPS, well above the 50% policy, because SEK 2.00 of it is a one-off return of surplus capital.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — an expensive stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of SEK 137.95 to the October 27, 2026 settled close of SEK 186.70, the A share gained 35.3% on price, behind the OMXS30's 42.2% over the same dates. Holders also received SEK 15.00 per share in dividends and SEK 2.00 per share through the 2022 redemption; added back without reinvestment, that lifts the five-year gain to roughly 48%. The path was anything but smooth: a 39% fall from the December 2021 close to the June 2022 low of SEK 95.57, a two-year climb to SEK 200.80 in May 2024, a slide to the SEK 150 area through most of 2025 as revenue shrank, and a 2026 that included the sharpest monthly fall of the window — −16.4% in March, against −9.1% for the OMXS30 — then six straight monthly gains from April to September, and a 9.5% drop in October to the 27th, through the SEK 2.50 dividend and the Q3 report.

It ran hotter in February 2026 (81.7), just before March's 16.4% fall, and peaked at 84.7 in May 2024, which was followed by a 25% slide over the next eleven months. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The cycle has turned and the order book proves it. Q3 2026 orders rose 25% organically to SEK 52.34B, above Q2's record, with a 1.09x book-to-bill; revenue reached a record SEK 47.92B and service grew in every business area and region. Vacuum — the group's semiconductor lever — took orders up 87% and its margin to 22.2%; Industrial Technique's adjusted margin recovered to 20.6%. Revenue lags orders, so 2027 revenue is being booked now, and consensus expects EPS to rise from SEK 6.27 in 2026 to SEK 7.67 in 2027 (Yahoo Finance consensus, not on the reported EPS basis). The business underneath earns 24% on capital against an 8% cost of capital, carries net debt of only 0.3x EBITDA, has returned SEK 24.4B to shareholders in 2026, and keeps adding small bolt-on acquisitions — 29 of them in 2025. After the October fall the average analyst target sits 21% above the price.

Bear Case

At 32.9x trailing earnings, the price still assumes the upswing lasts. Trailing EPS of SEK 5.67 is still below 2024's SEK 6.11, TTM revenue is up only 2.7%, and organic orders stopped growing from Q2 to Q3 — the market marked the stock down 7.7% on report day even with orders above consensus. Two-thirds of the order growth came from semiconductor vacuum, the most cyclical corner of the group, where 2025 brought falling revenue and restructuring. Management itself guides only that activity will "remain at the current level". Meanwhile Compressor Technique, the profit engine, saw its margin fall to 23.6% and its ROCE to 72%, Power Technique's ROCE is 13% with a new president coming, and the krona cut nine-month orders by 5%. If chip customers push out deliveries, the multiple has a long way to fall.

Top 3 Risks

01
Semiconductor capex reverses. Electronics was 60% of Vacuum Technique's 2025 orders and vacuum supplied about two-thirds of Q3's order growth. A pause in fab construction would hit orders first and margins second, as the capacity being added now becomes under-used.
02
Valuation leaves little room. 32.9x trailing and 29.8x 2026 consensus earnings. Q3 showed what happens when orders merely hold "at the current level": organic orders flat on Q2, and a 7.7% fall on report day despite record revenue.
03
Currency and trade. Most revenue is earned abroad and reported in SEK; a stronger krona cut 2025 operating profit by SEK 3.4B. In the 2025 annual report Rego wrote that "Tariffs, for example, increase costs and complexity, ultimately affecting consumers," and that rules blocking cross-border trade are more concerning; the group's answer is to produce where it sells.
Industry Commentary
The semiconductor equipment upswing
EMERGING TAILWIND

Electronics was 60% of Vacuum Technique's 2025 orders, which makes its order intake a read on chipmakers' equipment spending. Atlas Copco's Q3 2026 report describes semiconductor and flat-panel vacuum orders at "another record level", after a record in Q2, driven by several key accounts across all major regions and up on the prior quarter too, led by Asia. The group's orders rose 39% in both North America and Asia/Oceania in local currency. Because much of the cost base is fixed, volume moves profit faster than revenue, in both directions: Vacuum Technique's margin rose 3.7 points to 22.2% on volume even as it paid for a capacity ramp-up. The question for 2027 is whether these are orders for fabs that will be equipped on schedule; vacuum orders ran 36% ahead of vacuum revenue in Q3, and that backlog only becomes revenue if the fabs proceed.

A strong krona and a fragmented trade map
ONGOING HEADWIND

Atlas Copco reports in SEK but, per the CEO's 2025 letter, has operations in about 70 countries and a presence in 180. In 2025 currency took 6 points off revenue growth and SEK 3.4B off operating profit, and in the first nine months of 2026 it reduced orders by 5% and operating profit by SEK 1.5B. That is the effect of converting foreign sales into kronor, not lost business, but it is why reported revenue fell in 2025 while organic revenue was nearly flat. On trade, the CEO's 2025 letter describes a "local for local" footprint — "Our ambition is to produce where we sell and source where we produce" — as the hedge against tariffs and protectionism. The Middle East was under 4% of 2025 revenue and energy costs are well below 1% of revenue, per the Q3 report's risk section.

Paying up for quality — bull and bear framing
VALUATION DEBATE

Atlas Copco has the profile of a high-quality business: a 24% ROCE, a near-50% payout policy, low debt and a steady stream of bolt-on acquisitions. The bull framing: two quarters of record-level orders, recovering margins in Vacuum and Industrial Technique and a consensus that sees EPS rising about 22% in 2027 justify paying 24x 2027 earnings for a business of this quality, and a book-to-bill above 1 means the revenue is already partly secured. The bear framing: the multiple is being paid on trailing earnings still 7% below their 2024 peak, the growth is concentrated in the most cyclical end-market and has stopped rising quarter on quarter, and management will not commit to more than steady activity. By October 27 the stock was 9.5% below its September month-end, including a 7.7% fall on report day. The January 28, 2027 Q4 report — vacuum orders, the outlook sentence and the dividend proposal — is where the two framings meet next.
