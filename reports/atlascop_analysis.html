01 Company Overview

Atlas Copco makes the machines that sit in the back room of almost every factory: air compressors (Compressor Technique), vacuum pumps for chip fabs, labs and food packaging (Vacuum Technique), industrial power tools and assembly systems for car plants and general industry (Industrial Technique), and portable compressors, generators and pumps for construction sites and rental fleets (Power Technique). Founded in Stockholm in 1873, it spun off its mining and rock-drilling arm as Epiroc in June 2018 and has since built a second engine in vacuum through the earlier purchases of Edwards (2014) and Leybold (2016). In 2025 it booked revenues of SEK 168.3B (−5%, with currency alone taking 6 points off), an operating margin of 20.3% and basic EPS of SEK 5.43. About 38% of revenue is service — maintenance, parts and rental on an installed base of machines that run around the clock.

The story of 2026 is one quarter. In Q2 2026 orders jumped 27% to SEK 50.95B (26% organic, meaning before currency and acquisitions), the highest order intake the group has reported, while revenue rose 9% to SEK 44.97B and the operating margin held at 20.6%. Almost half of the extra orders came from one place: vacuum orders rose 60%, driven by what the report calls record order intake from the semiconductor industry, with gas and process compressors for marine LNG and air separation adding a second leg. CEO Vagner Rego put it plainly in his comment on the report: "The demand for Atlas Copco Group’s products, services and solutions improved significantly, particularly from the semiconductor industry." The market had already paid ahead: at SEK 209.80 the A share trades at 38.4 times trailing earnings, up 26% since the end of 2025 and 1.2% below its 52-week high. The debate is simple to state: is a 1.13x book-to-bill (orders over revenue) the start of a multi-year chip-equipment upswing that converts into revenue through 2027, or a cyclical spike that a 38x multiple has already capitalised? Management's own outlook — that customer activity will "remain at the current level" — is the most conservative voice in the room.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base + service (38% of revenue) Global leader in vacuum & abatement ROCE 24% vs 8% cost of capital Serial acquirer: 126 deals 2021–25 Multi-brand portfolio (Edwards, Leybold, Chicago Pneumatic, Desoutter) Energy-efficiency engineering Low leverage (net debt/EBITDA 0.5)

The moat is the installed base. A compressor or a fab vacuum pump is a small part of the customer's capital budget but a large part of their uptime risk, and once it is installed the owner buys service, parts and upgrades from the maker for a decade. That is how a compressor business earns a 74% return on capital employed and why the group as a whole clears its own 8.0% cost-of-capital benchmark three times over at 24%. The second layer is the operating model: four business areas, dozens of divisions, each free to buy small distributors and specialists — 29 acquisitions in 2025 alone — and bolt them onto a global service network. The vulnerability is that none of this protects against the cycle. Equipment is still 62% of revenue, and when customers stop building fabs or car plants, equipment orders fall regardless of the moat.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The growth is semiconductor-led. Electronics was 60% of Vacuum Technique's 2025 orders, and vacuum delivered about half of the Q2 2026 order increase. Chip-equipment spending moves in sharp cycles; in 2025 the same business area saw revenue fall and took restructuring charges. A record order quarter is the high-water mark of a cycle as often as the start of one.
02
Acquisitions dilute returns. Compressor Technique's return on capital employed has fallen from 82% to 74% in a year and its Q2 margin from 25.0% to 24.1%, a margin decline the report attributes mainly to recently acquired companies. The group's ROCE is 24%, down from 26%. Each deal is small; the cumulative effect of dozens a year is visible.
03
The krona cuts reported growth. Most revenue is earned abroad — Asia/Oceania and North America alone are 62% — but reported in SEK. Currency took 6 points off 2025 revenue and SEK 3.4B off operating profit; in H1 2026 it cut orders by 8% and revenue by 7% against the prior year.
04
Power Technique's capital intensity. The rental fleet is growing through acquisitions and its return on capital employed has slipped to 13% from 16%, with the Q2 report naming operational inefficiencies — and the area's president retires at year-end.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The cycle has turned and the order book proves it. Q2 2026 orders rose 26% organically to a record SEK 50.95B with a 1.13x book-to-bill, every business area grew orders by double digits organically, and service grew everywhere. Vacuum — the group's semiconductor lever — took orders up 60% and its margin back to 21.0%; Industrial Technique's margin recovered to 20.0%. Revenue lags orders, so 2027 revenue is being booked now, and consensus already expects EPS to rise from SEK 6.24 in 2026 to SEK 7.50 in 2027 (Yahoo Finance consensus, not on the reported EPS basis). The business underneath earns 24% on capital against an 8% cost of capital, carries net debt of only 0.5x EBITDA, is returning SEK 24.4B to shareholders in 2026, and keeps adding small bolt-on acquisitions — 29 of them in 2025.

Bear Case

At 38.4x trailing earnings, the price already assumes the upswing lasts. Trailing EPS of SEK 5.46 is still below 2024's SEK 6.11, TTM revenue is down 1.8%, and the stock trades 5.7% below the average target, with RSI above 70 for four straight months. Half of the order growth came from semiconductor vacuum, the most cyclical corner of the group, where 2025 brought falling revenue and restructuring. Management itself guides only that activity will "remain at the current level". Meanwhile acquisitions are diluting Compressor Technique's margin and returns, Power Technique's ROCE is down to 13% with a leadership change coming, and a strong krona keeps eating reported growth. If chip customers push out deliveries, the multiple has a long way to fall.

Top 3 Risks

01

Industry Commentary
The semiconductor equipment upswing
EMERGING TAILWIND

Electronics was 60% of Vacuum Technique's 2025 orders, which makes its order intake a read on chipmakers' equipment spending. Atlas Copco's Q2 2026 report describes semiconductor vacuum orders at "a new record level", driven by several key accounts across all major regions and up sharply on the prior quarter too. The order surge showed up regionally where fabs are being built — North America +49% and Asia/Oceania +47% in local currency. For the group this is operating leverage in both directions: Vacuum Technique's margin rose 2.1 points to 21.0% on volume even as it paid for a capacity ramp-up. The question for 2027 is whether these are orders for fabs that will be equipped on schedule; vacuum orders ran 37% ahead of vacuum revenue in Q2, and that backlog only becomes revenue if the fabs proceed.

A strong krona and a fragmented trade map
ONGOING HEADWIND

Atlas Copco reports in SEK but, per the CEO's 2025 letter, has operations in about 70 countries and a presence in 180. In 2025 currency took 6 points off revenue growth and SEK 3.4B off operating profit, and in H1 2026 it reduced orders by 8% and revenue by 7%. That is translation, not lost business, but it is why reported revenue fell in 2025 while organic revenue was nearly flat. On trade, the CEO's 2025 letter describes a "local for local" footprint — "Our ambition is to produce where we sell and source where we produce" — as the hedge against tariffs and protectionism. The Middle East is under 4% of revenue and energy costs well below 1%, per the Q2 report's risk section.

The compounder premium — bull and bear framing
VALUATION DEBATE

Atlas Copco has the profile of a quality compounder: a 24% ROCE, a near-50% payout policy, low debt and a steady stream of bolt-on acquisitions. The bull framing: a record order book, recovering margins in two of four business areas and a consensus that sees EPS rising about 20% in 2027 justify paying 28x 2027 earnings for a business of this quality, and a book-to-bill above 1 means the revenue is already partly secured. The bear framing: the multiple is being paid on trailing earnings still 11% below their 2024 peak, the growth is concentrated in the most cyclical end-market, management will not commit to more than steady activity, and the stock has already risen 28.6% from its March month-end close. The October 22 Q3 report — specifically vacuum orders and the outlook sentence — is where the two framings meet.
