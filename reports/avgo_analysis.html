01 Company Overview

Broadcom designs the custom ASIC accelerators (which it calls XPUs) and Ethernet networking silicon behind much of the non-NVIDIA half of the AI buildout, alongside a franchise portfolio of broadband, wireless, and storage chips and a sticky enterprise software arm anchored by VMware. In fiscal 2025 (ended November 2, 2025) the company generated $63.9B in revenue, up 24% YoY, and its trailing-twelve-month revenue has since accelerated to $75.5B (+32%) as AI semiconductor sales more than doubled. At a $2.01T market cap, Broadcom is now the 6th-largest US company — bigger than Meta and Tesla — and roughly the 7th-largest in the world, all without belonging to the "Magnificent Seven."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Custom ASIC Co-Design Scale SerDes & 3.5D Packaging IP Ethernet Switching Standard $73B+ AI Backlog VMware Enterprise Lock-In Multi-Year Hyperscaler Deals FCF Machine (43% Margin)

Broadcom's moat is the combination of irreplaceable interconnect IP and switching costs measured in years. Its SerDes libraries, co-packaged optics, and 3.5D packaging know-how are why hyperscalers designing their own AI chips still route the hardest parts through Broadcom — together with Marvell it controls roughly 95% of the custom AI ASIC co-design market, with Broadcom holding the dominant ~70–75% of that. A custom XPU program takes 18–36 months from engagement to deployment, making design wins effectively multi-generation annuities (Google is on its seventh TPU generation with Broadcom since 2014). Layer on Tomahawk/Jericho's ~80% share of merchant datacenter switch silicon — the default alternative to NVIDIA's proprietary InfiniBand/NVLink stack — plus VMware's grip on enterprise virtualization (~70%+ share, embedded in mission-critical workloads that are costly to migrate), and Broadcom monetizes AI whether or not any single accelerator vendor wins. The whole engine converts 43% of revenue into free cash flow, funding both a 15-year dividend growth streak and continued M&A capacity.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Extreme customer concentration. A handful of hyperscalers — Google, Meta, OpenAI, ByteDance — drive nearly all AI revenue, and the top customers each represent 10%+ of total sales. A single canceled or in-housed XPU program (or a Google decision to dual-source TPU design, as periodically rumored with MediaTek) would leave a crater in guidance that no other customer could backfill quickly.
▸
NVIDIA's counterattack. NVIDIA still holds ~70% of AI accelerators, iterates on an annual cadence (Blackwell → Rubin), and its NVLink Fusion program now invites custom chips into NVIDIA racks — a direct play to commoditize Broadcom's niche. If GPU total cost of ownership closes the ~65% inference gap, the custom-silicon thesis weakens.
▸
AI capex cyclicality and counterparty risk. The Q3 guide assumes $16B of quarterly AI revenue, much of it tied to OpenAI — a customer with roughly $1.4T of announced multi-vendor compute commitments against a low-double-digit-billions revenue base. Any financing hiccup or industry capex digestion pushes backlog to the right.
▸
VMware backlash and regulatory watch. Aggressive price increases (customers report 2–5x renewal quotes) have driven defections to Nutanix, Proxmox, and hyperscaler-native stacks, plus EU antitrust complaints from cloud providers (CISPE). Software growth of 9% depends on renewals holding at high prices.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

Broadcom is the only at-scale alternative to NVIDIA for AI compute, with ~70–75% of the custom ASIC co-design market, ~80% of merchant AI Ethernet switching, a $73B+ backlog, and AI revenue guided to triple to $16B in a single quarter. If management hits its ">$100B AI revenue in 2027" ambition, today's 27× forward P/E and 0.57 PEG make this the cheapest mega-cap AI compounder — with a 43% FCF margin and 15 straight dividend hikes as ballast.

▼ Bear Case

At 71× GAAP trailing earnings and 26.8× sales, perfection is priced in: the June quarter proved record results can still knock the stock down 12%. Revenue hangs on a handful of hyperscalers and one aggressively-financed AI lab (OpenAI); NVIDIA's NVLink Fusion aims squarely at commoditizing custom silicon; XPU mix dilutes gross margin; and VMware's 9% growth rests on price hikes that are actively driving customers to rivals and regulators.

01
AI capex air pocket. Q3 guidance of $29.4B (+84%) embeds hyperscaler and OpenAI spending plans that could be deferred with little notice; even a one-quarter digestion phase would compress both estimates and the multiple simultaneously.
02
Customer concentration / program loss. Losing or sharing a single flagship XPU program (Google TPU dual-sourcing rumors resurface regularly) would remove a multi-billion-dollar annuity that no near-term design win could replace.
03
Margin mix and leverage. Lower-margin XPU racks are growing far faster than high-margin software and legacy chips, pressuring the 76% gross margin, while $64.9B of debt keeps the balance sheet sensitive to any FCF disappointment.
  Industry Commentary: What's Moving the Chessboard
The Custom-Silicon Inflection Is Real
EMERGING TAILWIND

Custom ASICs are projected to reach 27.8% of the AI server market in 2026, growing ~44.6% YoY — nearly triple the ~16.1% growth of merchant GPUs — because at production inference scale, custom silicon can deliver up to a 65% total-cost-of-ownership advantage. Every major hyperscaler now runs a custom program (Google TPU v7, Meta MTIA, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia, OpenAI's Broadcom-built XPU), and Broadcom + Marvell control ~95% of the co-design market. NVIDIA still holds ~70% of AI accelerators overall, but the marginal dollar of inference capex is increasingly contested — and Broadcom's Ethernet networking wins even in mixed GPU/XPU clusters.

AI Capex Financing Circularity
ONGOING HEADWIND

Hyperscaler capex is tracking toward roughly $600B+ in 2026, and OpenAI alone has announced on the order of $1.4T in multi-year compute commitments (Broadcom, NVIDIA, AMD, Oracle, CoreWeave) against a revenue base in the low tens of billions — increasingly funded by vendor equity stakes, debt, and circular arrangements that unsettle credit analysts. Broadcom's $73B+ backlog is a genuine asset, but backlogs can be rescheduled. The Q3 print (Sep 2) is effectively a referendum on whether the 2027 spending trajectory survives contact with financing reality.

The Valuation Referendum: Bull vs Bear Framing
WHAT IT MEANS FOR AVGO

Bulls read the setup as: 0.57 PEG, ~66% consensus FY26 revenue growth, forward P/E in line with the NASDAQ-100 — the market is under-pricing a company compounding EPS at 50–70% with monopoly-grade positions in two AI chokepoints (co-design and Ethernet). Bears read the same numbers as: $2.01T of market cap resting on 3–4 customers, a GAAP P/E of 71×, gross margin structurally declining as hardware mix rises, and a stock that already fell 12% on a record quarter. Both agree on the mechanism: AVGO is now a leveraged bet on the durability of the 2026–2027 AI capex cycle, with the Sep 2 earnings report as the next roll of the dice.
