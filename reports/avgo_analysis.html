01 Company Overview

Broadcom designs the custom ASIC accelerators (which it calls XPUs) and Ethernet networking silicon behind much of the non-NVIDIA half of the AI buildout, alongside a franchise portfolio of broadband, wireless and storage chips and an enterprise software arm anchored by VMware. Fiscal Q3 2026 (ended August 2) brought revenue of $29.6B, up 86% YoY, of which $16.7B was AI semiconductors (+221%); trailing-twelve-month revenue is now $89.1B, up roughly 49%. Management now talks in gigawatts and fiscal years ahead: about $115B of AI revenue in fiscal 2027 and $230B in fiscal 2028, with Anthropic set to become its largest XPU customer in 2027. The market has not paid for the ambition yet: at $362.66 the company is worth about $1.73T (our calculation, 4.77B shares), down from $2.01T at the previous edition and roughly ninth-largest in the world.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Custom ASIC Co-Design Scale SerDes & 3.5D Packaging IP Ethernet Switching Standard Secured Wafer, Substrate & HBM Supply VMware Enterprise Lock-In Multi-Year Hyperscaler Deals FCF Machine (44% Margin)

Broadcom's moat is the combination of irreplaceable interconnect IP and switching costs measured in years. Its SerDes libraries, co-packaged optics, and 3.5D packaging know-how are why hyperscalers designing their own AI chips still route the hardest parts through Broadcom — together with Marvell it controls roughly 95% of the custom AI ASIC co-design market, with Broadcom holding the dominant ~70–75% of that. A custom XPU program takes 18–36 months from engagement to deployment, making design wins effectively multi-generation annuities (Google is on its seventh TPU generation with Broadcom since 2014). Layer on Tomahawk/Jericho's ~80% share of merchant datacenter switch silicon — the default alternative to NVIDIA's proprietary InfiniBand/NVLink stack — plus VMware's grip on enterprise virtualization (~70%+ share, embedded in mission-critical workloads that are costly to migrate), and Broadcom monetizes AI whether or not any single accelerator vendor wins. The Q3 call added a newer moat: supply. Tan framed the fiscal-2027 target as capacity Broadcom has already "secured" in leading-edge wafers, substrates and HBM memory, and said "Our demand actually exceeds this outlook." The whole engine converts about 44% of revenue into free cash flow ($39.4B over the last twelve months), funding the dividend and $5.6B of long-term debt paydown in Q3 alone.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Extreme customer concentration — now with AI labs at the top. Six XPU customers drive the AI business, and the 2027–28 plan leans on two AI labs: Anthropic, which management says will be the largest XPU customer in 2027, and OpenAI. Unlike Google or Meta, neither funds its compute from an established profit stream. A single canceled, delayed or in-housed program (or a Google decision to dual-source TPU design) would leave a crater that no other customer could backfill quickly.
▸
NVIDIA's counterattack. NVIDIA still holds ~70% of AI accelerators, iterates on an annual cadence (Blackwell → Rubin), and its NVLink Fusion program now invites custom chips into NVIDIA racks — a direct play to commoditize Broadcom's niche. If GPU total cost of ownership closes the ~65% inference gap, the custom-silicon thesis weakens.
▸
Financing and counterparty risk. In June Broadcom launched the AI XPV Platform with Apollo and Blackstone — an initial $35B tranche funding more than 1 gigawatt of Anthropic compute, sized for 20+ gigawatts through 2028. Per 24/7 Wall St., citing Broadcom's 10-Q, Broadcom backstops the lease payments for five years with maximum exposure of up to $29B on the initial transaction, and a Bank of America note estimated the vehicle could reach $370B of senior debt by mid-2029. The stock fell 5.9% the day that note circulated. If a lab's funding falters, the risk comes back to Broadcom's balance sheet.
▸
VMware backlash and regulatory watch. Aggressive price increases (customers report 2–5x renewal quotes) have driven defections to Nutanix, Proxmox, and hyperscaler-native stacks, plus EU antitrust complaints from cloud providers (CISPE). The Q3 jump to +29% software growth depends on renewals holding at high prices.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

The business just did what bulls asked for and the stock went down. AI revenue is guided from $16.7B to $21.7B in one quarter, management says fiscal-2027 supply is already secured for ~$115B of AI revenue and sees $230B in fiscal 2028, and Tan says demand exceeds that outlook. At $362.66 the stock trades at 18.7× FY27 consensus EPS — well below its own trailing P/E of 46× — with a 44% FCF margin, falling gross debt and a CEO targeting more than $30 of EPS in fiscal 2028. If even most of that lands, the −25% drawdown from the June high close is the opportunity.

▼ Bear Case

The 2027–28 plan now rests heavily on two AI labs, Anthropic and OpenAI, and part of it on a Broadcom-backstopped financing vehicle that one bank estimates could reach $370B of debt. When the lab that is about to become your largest customer publicly argues for slowing AI down, the stock falls 4.8% in a day — that is what concentration looks like. Gross margin is guided down again to ~73%, the Q4 revenue guide came in under consensus, and at 19× sales and 46× GAAP trailing earnings the stock is still priced for the targets to arrive on time.

01
AI capex air pocket. Q4 guidance of $34.8B (+93%) and the $115B fiscal-2027 AI target embed AI-lab and hyperscaler spending plans that could be deferred with little notice; even a one-quarter digestion phase would compress both estimates and the multiple simultaneously.
02
Customer concentration and counterparty credit. Anthropic becoming the largest XPU customer in 2027, partly funded through the XPV Platform whose lease payments Broadcom reportedly backstops, ties Broadcom's revenue and contingent liabilities to the same counterparty. Losing or sharing a flagship program would remove a multi-billion-dollar annuity that no near-term design win could replace.
03
Margin mix and leverage. Lower-margin XPU racks are growing far faster than high-margin software and legacy chips, taking consolidated gross margin from 75% in Q3 to a guided ~73% in Q4, while $59.4B of debt plus the lease backstop keep the balance sheet sensitive to any FCF disappointment.
  Industry Commentary: What's Moving the Chessboard
The Custom-Silicon Inflection Is Real
EMERGING TAILWIND

Custom ASICs are projected to reach 27.8% of the AI server market in 2026, growing ~44.6% YoY — nearly triple the ~16.1% growth of merchant GPUs — because at production inference scale, custom silicon can deliver up to a 65% total-cost-of-ownership advantage. Every major hyperscaler now runs a custom program (Google TPU v7, Meta MTIA, Amazon Trainium, Microsoft Maia, OpenAI's Broadcom-built XPU, and Anthropic's TPU v8i deployment), and Broadcom + Marvell control an estimated ~95% of the co-design market. NVIDIA still holds ~70% of AI accelerators overall, but the marginal dollar of inference capex is increasingly contested — and Broadcom's Ethernet networking wins even in mixed GPU/XPU clusters.

AI Capex Financing Circularity
ONGOING HEADWIND

The question moved from how much AI costs to who carries the debt. Broadcom's own answer is the AI XPV Platform (launched June 9, 2026): Apollo and Blackstone lead an initial $35B tranche that funds more than 1 gigawatt of Anthropic compute, sized for 20+ gigawatts through 2028. Bank of America's Tom Curcuruto estimated the vehicle could carry $370B of senior debt by mid-2029, including roughly $150B of new issuance in 2027 alone, and the stock fell 5.9% the day the note circulated. A month later Anthropic's CEO argued in an essay for slowing the pace of AI progress, and chip stocks fell with Broadcom. Tan's answer, the same day on CNBC, was that the long-term targets had not changed; the market's answer is still pending.

The Valuation Referendum: Bull vs Bear Framing
WHAT IT MEANS FOR AVGO

Bulls read the setup as: 18.7× FY27 consensus earnings for a company guiding AI revenue to double in each of the next two fiscal years, with supply already secured — the market is pricing the risk, not the plan. Bears read the same numbers as: $1.73T of market cap resting on six XPU customers, two of them AI labs; gross margin guided lower again; a Q4 guide below consensus; and a financing structure that puts Broadcom's balance sheet behind its customers' leases. Both agree on the mechanism: AVGO is a leveraged bet on the 2027–2028 AI build-out being funded on schedule, and the business has changed far less than the price has. The Q4 print, expected in December, is the next roll of the dice.
