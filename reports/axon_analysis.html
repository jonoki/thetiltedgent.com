01 Company Overview

Axon Enterprise sells conducted energy weapons (CEWs — handheld devices that deliver an incapacitating electrical current, sold under the TASER brand), body-worn cameras, in-car video, drones and counter-drone systems to police departments, and then sells those same departments the cloud software that stores, indexes and manages the evidence those devices produce. FY2025 revenue was $2,779.5M, up 33% from $2,082.5M in FY2024. Q3 2026 revenue was $908.6M, up 28% year over year, short of what analysts expected, and management now guides to about 30% growth for 2026 after pushing three federal counter-drone deployments (about $130M) out of the year. Axon is classified by data providers under Industrials / Aerospace & Defense; its market-cap rank in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 is in the header.

The framing that matters for this business: Axon began life as TASER International, a hardware company, and the hardware is still there — but the economics have migrated. Software & Services was $437.0M of Q3 2026 revenue (+43%) at a 71.4% gross margin, against 43.0% for the Connected Devices hardware segment. The devices are increasingly the razor; EVIDENCE.COM and the bundled software plans are the blades. The quarter that sank the stock was a hardware and timing miss: software accelerated while the drone deals slipped. Sections 02 and 04 quantify that split rather than asserting it.

EVIDENCE.COM is the cloud repository where body-camera footage, in-car video, digital case files and chain-of-custody records live. Around it Axon sells tiered software bundles — the AI Era Plan, Axon 911 (dispatch software) and Axon Fusus (real-time crime centre integration). Q3 2026 segment revenue grew 43% to $437.0M, faster than the 36% of Q2, and the segment is now 48% of the company's sales. Segment gross margin was 71.4%, down from 73.8% a year earlier, which Axon put down mainly to scaling new products and more professional-services revenue (Q3 shareholder letter). CEO Rick Smith said on the Q3 call: "Our AI products have already passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue." The lock-in mechanism is specific rather than rhetorical: a department's evidence archive is a set of legally sensitive records subject to statutory retention periods and chain-of-custody rules, so switching vendors means migrating material that may be subpoenaed. Add public-agency procurement inertia and multi-year contracts, and the practical switching cost is far above the sticker price of the software. Axon's moat is not the TASER patent estate and it is not the camera hardware, both of which are replicable. It is that the customer's legally sensitive records live inside Axon's cloud, inside Axon's workflow. A police department that wants to switch vendors must migrate an evidence archive subject to statutory retention schedules and chain-of-custody requirements, re-train officers on new capture and tagging procedures, and re-certify the whole process with prosecutors who have to be able to authenticate the material in court. That is not a software migration; it is a legal exercise. Layer on a customer type that runs multi-year competitive procurements and rarely re-bids a working system mid-term, and the practical churn rate collapses. The numbers confirm it: 126% net revenue retention and $15.8B of future contracted bookings growing 39% at Q3 2026. The moat is measurable, and the software side kept widening through the bad quarter — Software & Services grew 43% by selling more products into accounts Axon already owns.

Customer concentration in law-enforcement budgets. 85% of Q3 2026 revenue ($772.0M of $908.6M) came from the United States, overwhelmingly from public safety agencies. Those budgets are set by elected bodies and move with public sentiment about policing. A durable political shift toward defunding, or a wave of municipal fiscal stress, hits the whole revenue base at once — this is not diversified end-market risk.

Lumpy federal drone orders. Q3 showed a new weak spot: a few large federal counter-drone contracts can move a quarter. Three Dedrone deployments worth about $130M slipping out of 2026 was enough to cut the year's growth outlook from 32–34% to about 30%. CFO Brittany Bagley said on the call: "So we're resetting how we guide."

Valuation as its own vulnerability. Even after the fall, the trailing P/E is 97× GAAP earnings, so a growth slowdown still costs the share price heavily. The stock is −49.68% over the trailing 52 weeks (Nov 7, 2025 close to Nov 9, 2026 close), against +12.18% for SPY over the same dates. See Section 04.

Stock-based compensation and GAAP dilution. FY2026 SBC is now guided to about $570–600M. Against Q3 GAAP net income of $57.0M, SBC of $156.4M in the same quarter is about 2.7 times reported profit. It is the main reason GAAP earnings look nothing like the adjusted figures analysts model, and the Skydio deal adds new Axon shares on top.

Cash conversion is still the question. Free cash flow is now guided to $350–400M for 2026, down from a prior target of $450M, but the first nine months produced an outflow of $74.5M, so Q4 alone has to bring in roughly $425–475M (our arithmetic). Trailing-twelve-month FCF is $80.9M against $225.5M of operating cash flow. Debt also rose: notes outstanding were $2.9B of principal at Sep 30 after September's convertible issue, against $1.64B of cash and short-term investments.

Notable One-Off Items

Stock-based compensation is the single largest distortion, and it is only partly one-off. Q3 2026 SBC was $156.4M; FY2025 SBC was $634.2M; FY2026 is now guided to about $570–600M. Part of it relates to the Employee XSP program and the CEO Performance Award — a multi-tranche, milestone-gated equity grant to founder-CEO Rick Smith that vests only on hitting escalating market-capitalisation and operational targets. It has two effects investors should price separately: it suppresses GAAP earnings (Q3 GAAP net income $57.0M / $0.68 EPS vs. non-GAAP $144M / $1.72), and it dilutes share count. When an analyst quotes a forward P/E in the 30s or 40s, they are using the non-GAAP number that adds this back. On GAAP the trailing multiple is 97×.

Guidance reset. In November Axon cut the year's revenue outlook to about $3.61–3.63B, set Q4 revenue at $990M–$1.01B and Q4 adjusted EBITDA (its adjusted measure of operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) at $245–250M, lowered planned 2026 capital spending to $130–150M, and set 2026 free cash flow at $350–400M, below its prior target of $450M, citing a build-up of inventory (Q3 shareholder letter).

Acquisitions and investments. Dedrone (counter-drone) and Fusus (real-time operations) are acquired businesses now embedded in the reported lines, so Platform Solutions' +106% is part organic, part acquired; Axon does not split the two in the headline release. Skydio is different: Axon owns a minority stake, so Skydio's sales are not in Axon's revenue.

Also note: GAAP net income ($258.5M TTM) far exceeds GAAP operating income ($68.0M TTM), because items below operating profit did the work: "other income, net" was $255.7M in the first nine months of 2026 alone. Treat the trailing P/E denominator with that in mind.

What the multiple actually implies

Stating it numerically, and not softening it. At $303.18, market capitalisation is ~$24.7B (81.43M shares at Oct 30, 2026 × close) and enterprise value is ~$25.9B ($24.7B + $2.9B notes − $1.64B cash and short-term investments; our calculation). Against $3.42B TTM revenue that is 7.6× sales, about half the 14.7× of the previous edition. Against the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance ($924–929M) it is ~28× EV/adjusted EBITDA.

Run the arithmetic forward. On FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $7.11 the stock trades at 42.6×. For that to fall to 25× without the share price falling, earnings must rise about 1.7×. Holding margins flat, that takes 2.0 years at 30% annual growth, 2.4 years at 25%, and 2.9 years at 20%. In August the same test needed earnings to triple, about five years of 25–30% growth; the price now asks for two to three. The catch is that Q3 was the quarter growth slipped below 30%. In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward. Above 70 means the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 means it has been beaten down and may be washed out. This is computed on monthly closes, so each reading covers half a year of price action.

The five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 month-end close of $168.79 to the Nov 9, 2026 close of $303.18, AXON returned +79.6% on price alone — about 12.6% a year, with no dividend — against +65.2% for SPY and +72.5% for QQQ over the same dates (price only). But the path is the story, not the endpoint. The stock fell 44.8% from November 2021 to its June 2022 trough of $93.17, then rose almost ninefold to a peak monthly close of $827.94 in June 2025, and has since given back 63.4%. The trailing 52-week change is −49.68%, against +12.18% for SPY. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A near-monopoly position in a budget-funded market with multi-year contracts, and the numbers prove the lock-in rather than assert it: 126% net revenue retention, $15.8B of future contracted bookings growing 39%, and ARR of $1.8B growing 40%. The miss was in hardware timing, not software: Software & Services accelerated to 43% growth in Q3, and AI products passed $100M of annual recurring revenue. Software gross margin of 71.4% against 43.0% for devices means every point of mix shift is a point of company margin. Piper Sandler said after the print that it does not believe Axon has lost business. And the price has halved: 7.6× sales and 32× FY2027 consensus EPS, against 14.7× sales in August. If growth holds at 25–30%, the 25× test in Section 04 is two to three years away rather than five.

Bear Case

Growth is no longer only a valuation argument. Q3 revenue missed, growth fell to 28%, international revenue slipped to 15% of the total, and the 2026 outlook came down to about 30% on a handful of federal drone contracts — proof that a few large orders can now move the quarter. The stock still trades at 97× trailing GAAP earnings and ~28× EV/adjusted EBITDA. GAAP economics are thin — 1.99% operating margin, 1.06% ROIC — because $570–600M of annual stock-based compensation takes nearly all operating profit and dilutes holders, and GAAP net income leans on $255.7M of nine-month "other income". The balance sheet now carries $2.9B of notes, and about $1.2B is going into a minority stake in Skydio, partly paid in new Axon shares. Nine-month free cash flow was negative, so the lowered $350–400M full-year target rests on one quarter.

01
Execution on the reset. Management cut the outlook once on federal timing. Another miss — Q4 revenue below $990M, the Dedrone deployments slipping again, or net revenue retention falling below its 126% — would test the analysts who kept their Buy ratings while cutting targets by 4–33%.
02
Concentration in politically determined budgets. 85% of Q3 revenue was U.S., overwhelmingly public safety, with federal agencies now a swing factor through Dedrone. These budgets are appropriated by elected bodies and move with public sentiment about policing. International is the diversification answer but fell to 15% of revenue in Q3 from 18% in Q2.
03
Stock-based compensation, dilution, capital allocation and cash conversion. $570–600M of FY2026 SBC against $57.0M of Q3 GAAP net income means the gap between the adjusted numbers analysts model and the GAAP numbers shareholders own is large and persistent. Add new shares for Skydio, a convertible that can turn into shares above about $652, and TTM free cash flow of only $80.9M, and the rate at which $15.8B of contracted bookings converts into shareholder cash is the least well-evidenced part of the story.
  Industry Commentary
Public-safety budgets are the whole end market
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
$772.0M of Q3 2026's $908.6M — 85% — came from the United States, dominated by state and local law enforcement, with federal counter-drone orders now large enough to move a quarter. That is not a diversified customer base; it is mostly public money, appropriated by city councils, state legislatures and Congress, responsive to public opinion about policing. The offsetting fact is duration: $15.8B of future contracted bookings, and of the $10.2B the accounts count as remaining performance obligations, only about 20–25% is expected to be recognised in the next twelve months. Long contracts do not make the end market safe, but they make it slow — a sentiment shift takes years to reach the income statement. Timing is the exception, as Q3 showed.
AI is turning the archive into a product
EMERGING TAILWIND
CEO Rick Smith said on the Q3 2026 call that Axon's AI products "have already passed $100 million in annual recurring revenue", and Software & Services growth sped up to 43%. The strategic logic is tight: AI-assisted report writing and video search are only valuable if you already hold the footage, and Axon holds it. That converts a storage cost centre into a premium upsell — and it is visible in the 126% net revenue retention. The cost side shows up too: Software & Services gross margin fell from 73.8% to 71.4% year over year, which Axon put down mainly to scaling new products and more professional-services revenue.
Bull vs. bear, framed by the same facts
ONGOING HEADWIND
Until August the two sides agreed on the operating numbers and argued only about price. Q3 changed that: growth slipped to 28% and the outlook came down, so the bear now has a business argument too. The brokers' answer is visible in the ratings table: after the print every firm on it kept its rating and cut its target, Craig-Hallum downgraded to Hold, and the average target fell to $541.32 from $693.40 in August. The bull reads 126% NRR, $15.8B of contracted bookings and 43% software growth and sees a timing problem at half the August price. The bear reads 97× trailing GAAP earnings, 1.06% ROIC, $570–600M of annual stock pay and new debt, and sees a premium stock whose growth has started to slow. The −49.68% 52-week return shows which argument the market has been running with.
