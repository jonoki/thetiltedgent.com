01 Company Overview

AstraZeneca is a Cambridge, UK-based maker of prescription medicines, and the largest drug company on the London market: the FTSE 100's third-biggest member by market value at the end of August 2026. It earns its money in cancer (Oncology, 46% of first-half 2026 revenue), in heart, kidney, lung and immune diseases, and in rare diseases. FY2025 Total Revenue was $58.74B, up 9% (8% at CER), with reported EPS of $6.60 and Core EPS of $9.16. "Core" is the company's adjusted measure: it adds back restructuring and the amortisation and impairment of acquired drug rights, which are large at a company that buys much of its pipeline. Management's stated target is $80 billion of Total Revenue in 2030.

2026 changed how Americans own it. Until January 30 the US line was a Nasdaq ADS worth half an ordinary share; since February 2, 2026 the ordinary shares themselves trade on the NYSE under the same AZN ticker, alongside London and Stockholm. The stock closed that month at $208.45, its highest month-end in five years, and has since fallen to $156.90 (−24.7%). Along the way a heart-disease trial for Wainua failed in July and the diabetes, heart and kidney pill Farxiga lost US exclusivity. The business kept growing through it: first-half 2026 revenue rose 9% to $30.67B and Core EPS 12% to $5.21. The debate is whether a pipeline the CEO says has more than twenty high-value readouts due within 18 months can outrun patent expiries, China price cuts and US drug-pricing policy.

The second quarter itself was a modest beat on profit: Core EPS of $2.63 against the $2.50 consensus compiled by MarketBeat (+5.2%), on Total Revenue of $15.38B, a shade under the $15.44B expected; full-year guidance was reconfirmed. The weeks since have mixed trial results with deal-making. Two more late-stage cancer trials fell short (eVOLVE-Lung02 was stopped on August 17 and SERENA-4 missed on September 14), while five Phase III trials came in positive: Tagrisso with Orpathys in two lung-cancer trials, Enhertu in HER2-mutant lung cancer, Tezspire in eosinophilic esophagitis (an allergic disease of the gullet) and Imfinzi with Amgen's Imdelltra in small cell lung cancer. AstraZeneca completed the Zegfrovy lung-cancer licence from Dizal ($600M upfront), sold €2.55B of bonds, and on September 29 agreed to invest $2 billion in Summit Therapeutics for about 12% of the company.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Patent-protected oncology franchise ADC partnership with Daiichi Sankyo Rare-disease pricing power (Alexion) 183-project pipeline Emerging-markets reach (27% of revenue) Core operating margin 34%

A drug company's moat is a stack of patents with expiry dates, so the question is never whether the moat leaks, but whether new medicines refill it faster than old ones lose protection. AstraZeneca's case rests on breadth: no single product is more than 13% of revenue (Farxiga in H1 2026), it runs late-stage programmes across cancer, heart, lung and rare disease at once, and emerging markets bring in 27% of revenue. The cost of that breadth is R&D at 25% of revenue on a reported basis (23% Core) in H1 2026, and a balance sheet that keeps buying in assets: $3.33B of intangible-asset purchases in the half, including a $1.1B upfront payment to CSPC Pharmaceuticals. The buying continued after June: a $600M upfront payment to Dizal for Zegfrovy (completed September 1) and a $2B equity investment in Summit Therapeutics (agreed September 29), with €2.55B of new bonds priced on August 24 in four tranches maturing 2030 to 2038.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Farxiga is going off-patent in the US. The company's single largest product by H1 2026 sales ($3,998M) fell 19% at CER in Q2 as US exclusivity ended. Every quarter now removes revenue that new launches must replace.
02
US drug pricing. The half-year gross margin reflected the US government agreement announced in 2025, and Farxiga is subject to the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare price negotiation; the US Supreme Court declined in May 2026 to hear AstraZeneca's challenge to that law. The US is 42% of revenue.
03
China is shrinking at constant currency. Revenue there was flat in dollars and down 13% at CER in Q2 2026 as volume-based procurement cuts prices on mature drugs. The company has been building there (a Beijing R&D centre opened in 2025), which raises the stakes.
04
Late-stage misses. Between the Q1 and H1 2026 results three Phase III or registrational readouts missed their primary endpoints: CARDIO-TTRansform (Wainua in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, a heart-muscle disease), EMERALD-2 (Imfinzi after liver-cancer surgery) and TMA-313 (Ultomiris). The CEO's own framing is that the $80B target "assumes successes and setbacks"; the stock fell 5.7% on the day of the CARDIO-TTRansform update. Two more followed after the H1 results: eVOLVE-Lung02 (volrustomig with chemotherapy in first-line lung cancer) was discontinued on August 17 when the independent data monitoring committee judged it unlikely to meet either primary endpoint, and SERENA-4 (Etcamah with palbociclib in first-line breast cancer) missed its primary endpoint on September 14, a week after Etcamah's US approval for patients whose tumours develop ESR1 mutations on first-line therapy.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $156.90 you pay 16.2x trailing Core earnings and 15.3x this year's consensus for a company guiding to 2026 revenue growth at a mid-to-high single-digit rate at CER and Core EPS growth at a low double-digit rate, with a 34% Core operating margin and oncology growing 15%. Ten of eleven analysts in the S&P Global panel rate it a buy and the lowest target is 17% above the price. The pipeline is broad enough that single failures, like CARDIO-TTRansform, dent rather than break the $80B 2030 plan, and more than twenty high-value readouts are due within 18 months; since the H1 results five Phase III trials have come in positive (SAFFRON, DESTINY-Lung04, CROSSING, SANOVO, DeLLphi-305) and Etcamah won US approval. The NYSE listing lets US investors own the ordinary shares directly.

Bear Case

The two biggest markets are both turning against the old portfolio. Farxiga, the single largest product, is losing US exclusivity and fell 19% at CER in Q2; US pricing now runs through a government agreement and the IRA's Medicare negotiation; China fell 13% at CER in Q2. Reported earnings are about 30% below Core, so the trailing P/E on IFRS profit is 23.3, not 16. Net debt rose $3.5B in six months to $26.9B while the company keeps paying upfront for licensed assets. Three late-stage misses between the Q1 and H1 results, and two more since (eVOLVE-Lung02, SERENA-4), show what a busy readout calendar can also deliver; the spending has not slowed either, with $2B committed to Summit in September, and the stock has fallen 24.7% from its February high with the RSI not yet oversold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Patent expiries faster than launches. Farxiga ($3,998M in H1 2026, 13% of product revenue) is losing US exclusivity, Soliris fell 22% at CER, and Brilinta's sales fell 66% in the half. The new launches (Datroway, Truqap, Baxfendy, Wainua) must scale quickly to fill the gap.
02
US pricing and China procurement. 42% of revenue is US and 11% China. The 2025 US government agreement already shows in gross margin, the Supreme Court has ended the company's IRA challenge, and China's volume-based procurement keeps cutting prices on mature drugs.
03
Binary trial readouts on a leveraged balance sheet. More than twenty high-value readouts are due within 18 months; three late-stage readouts missed between the Q1 and H1 2026 results and two more by mid-September. Net debt of $26.9B is 1.3x EBITDA — manageable, but rising as licensing deals are paid upfront.
Industry Commentary
US drug pricing after the 2025 agreements
ONGOING HEADWIND

AstraZeneca's H1 2026 announcement lists the implementation of the US government agreement announced in 2025 among the factors moving its gross margin, alongside price adjustments on drugs past exclusivity. Separately, the Inflation Reduction Act lets Medicare negotiate prices on older drugs, Farxiga among them; AstraZeneca's legal challenge ended when the US Supreme Court denied review in May 2026. For a company earning 42% of its revenue in the US, pricing is now set partly in Washington. The bull answer is volume: an agreed framework removes uncertainty, and new medicines launch at prices the framework allows.

Antibody-drug conjugates and combination deals
EMERGING TAILWIND

Antibody-drug conjugates, antibodies that carry chemotherapy straight to the tumour, are one of the fastest-growing classes in cancer, and AstraZeneca's two with Daiichi Sankyo are already material: Enhertu grew 32% at CER to $1,719M in H1 2026 and Datroway grew more than sixfold from a small base. The September 29 agreement to invest $2B in Summit Therapeutics and the October 2 collaboration pairing Datroway with Summit's ivonescimab, starting in triple-negative breast cancer, show the next step: combinations of a conjugate with an immune drug, which the company's oncology R&D head calls "a core pillar of our oncology strategy". The trade-off is economics: partnered products share their profits, which is part of why the reported gross margin sits around 82% rather than higher.
