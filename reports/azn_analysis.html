01 Company Overview

AstraZeneca is a Cambridge, UK-based maker of prescription medicines, listed in London, Stockholm and New York. It earns its money in cancer (Oncology, 47% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026), in heart, kidney, lung and immune diseases, and in rare diseases. FY2025 Total Revenue was $58.74B, up 9% (8% at CER), with reported EPS of $6.60 and Core EPS of $9.16. "Core" is the company's adjusted measure: it adds back restructuring and the amortisation and impairment of acquired drug rights, which are large at a company that buys much of its pipeline. Management's stated target is $80 billion of Total Revenue in 2030.

2026 changed how Americans own it. Until January 30 the US line was a Nasdaq ADS worth half an ordinary share; since February 2, 2026 the ordinary shares themselves trade on the NYSE under the same AZN ticker, alongside London and Stockholm. The stock closed that month at $208.45, its highest month-end in five years, slid to $161.47 by the end of September as a heart-disease trial for Wainua failed in July and the diabetes, heart and kidney pill Farxiga lost US exclusivity, then climbed back to $186.58 by November 2, 10.5% below the February month-end. The business kept growing through it: revenue in the first nine months of 2026 rose 8% (7% at CER) to $46.88B and Core EPS 14% to $8.00. The debate is whether a pipeline the CEO said in July has more than twenty high-value readouts due within 18 months can outrun patent expiries, China price cuts and US drug-pricing policy.

The third quarter, reported on October 30, was a clear beat on profit: Core EPS of $2.79, up 17% (19% at CER), against Nasdaq's consensus of $2.60 (+7.3%), on Total Revenue of $16.21B, up 7%. Full-year guidance was reconfirmed, and the company said it intends to raise the dividend declared for 2026 to $3.30 a share. The stock rose 7.0% close to close that day in New York, and 7.0% in London. Trial news since the summer has leaned positive: the company counts 12 positive Phase III programme readouts so far in 2026, including Tagrisso with Orpathys in two lung-cancer trials, Enhertu in HER2-mutant lung cancer, Tezspire in eosinophilic esophagitis (an allergic disease of the gullet), and overall-survival gains presented at the ESMO cancer congress in October for Imfinzi with Amgen's Imdelltra in small cell lung cancer and for sonesitatug vedotin in gastric cancer. Against that, eVOLVE-Lung02 was stopped on August 17, SERENA-4 missed on September 14 and HICKORY, a trial of efzimfotase alfa in the rare bone disease hypophosphatasia, fell short of statistical significance on its overall walking-test result. AstraZeneca completed the Zegfrovy lung-cancer licence from Dizal ($600M upfront), sold €2.55B of bonds, and on October 5 completed a $2 billion investment in Summit Therapeutics for about 12% of the company.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Patent-protected oncology franchise ADC partnership with Daiichi Sankyo Rare-disease pricing power (Alexion) 180-project pipeline Emerging-markets reach (27% of revenue) Core operating margin 35%

A drug company's moat is a stack of patents with expiry dates, so the question is never whether the moat leaks, but whether new medicines refill it faster than old ones lose protection. AstraZeneca's case rests on breadth: no single product is more than 13% of revenue (Tagrisso, 12.5% in the first nine months of 2026, just ahead of Farxiga), it runs late-stage programmes across cancer, heart, lung and rare disease at once, and emerging markets bring in 27% of revenue. The cost of that breadth is R&D at 24% of revenue on a reported basis in the first nine months of 2026, and a balance sheet that keeps buying in assets: purchases of drug rights drove the nine-month investing outflow, including a $1,104M upfront payment to CSPC Pharmaceuticals in Q2. The buying continued after June: a $600M upfront payment to Dizal for Zegfrovy (completed September 1) and a $2B equity investment in Summit Therapeutics (agreed September 29, completed October 5), with €2.55B of new bonds priced on August 24 in four tranches maturing 2030 to 2038.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Farxiga is going off-patent in the US. The company's second-largest product ($5,603M in the first nine months of 2026, behind Tagrisso) fell 15% at CER as US exclusivity ended, and the heart, kidney and diabetes area it leads fell 18% in Q3. Every quarter now removes revenue that new launches must replace.
02
US drug pricing. Gross margin in the first nine months again reflected the US government agreement announced in 2025, and Farxiga is subject to the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare price negotiation; the US Supreme Court declined in May 2026 to hear AstraZeneca's challenge to that law. The US is 42% of revenue.
03
China under price pressure. Revenue there fell 3% at CER in the first nine months of 2026 (up 3% in dollars) as volume-based procurement cuts prices on mature drugs; after −13% at CER in Q2, Q3 was up 3% at CER. The company has been building there (a Beijing R&D centre opened in 2025), which raises the stakes.
04
Late-stage misses. Between the Q1 and H1 2026 results three Phase III or registrational readouts missed their primary endpoints: CARDIO-TTRansform (Wainua in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy, a heart-muscle disease), EMERALD-2 (Imfinzi after liver-cancer surgery) and TMA-313 (Ultomiris). The CEO's own framing is that the $80B target "assumes successes and setbacks"; the stock fell 5.7% on the day of the CARDIO-TTRansform update. Two more followed after the H1 results: eVOLVE-Lung02 (volrustomig with chemotherapy in first-line lung cancer) was discontinued on August 17 when the independent data monitoring committee judged it unlikely to meet either primary endpoint, and SERENA-4 (Etcamah with palbociclib in first-line breast cancer) missed its primary endpoint on September 14, a week after Etcamah's US approval for patients whose tumours develop ESR1 mutations on first-line therapy. The 9M results added HICKORY (efzimfotase alfa in hypophosphatasia, short of significance overall) and a discontinued Ultomiris trial, AWAKE.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $186.58 you pay 18.4x trailing Core earnings and 18.3x this year's consensus for a company that has just reconfirmed 2026 guidance of revenue growth at a mid-to-high single-digit rate at CER and Core EPS growth at a low double-digit rate, with a 35% Core operating margin and oncology growing 16%. Q3 Core EPS rose 19% at CER and beat Nasdaq's consensus by 7.3%. Nine of twelve analysts in the S&P Global panel rate it a buy, with an average target 10.8% above the price. The pipeline is broad enough that single failures, like CARDIO-TTRansform, dent rather than break the $80B 2030 plan: the company counts 12 positive Phase III programme readouts so far in 2026 and first approvals for four new medicines (Etcamah, Baxfendy, Klygefa and Amlypave). The NYSE listing lets US investors own the ordinary shares directly.

Bear Case

The two biggest markets are both turning against the old portfolio. Farxiga is losing US exclusivity and fell 15% at CER in the first nine months, and the heart, kidney and diabetes area fell 18% in Q3; US pricing now runs through a government agreement and the IRA's Medicare negotiation; China fell 3% at CER over the nine months. Reported earnings are about 30% below Core, so the trailing P/E on IFRS profit is 26.2, not 18. Net debt was $25.8B at September 30, $2.5B more than at the end of 2025, before the $2B Summit payment in October, and the company keeps paying upfront for licensed assets. Three late-stage misses between the Q1 and H1 results, and more since (eVOLVE-Lung02, SERENA-4, HICKORY), show what a busy readout calendar can also deliver; and after a 15.6% October the lowest target in the S&P Global panel, $158, sits 15% below the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Patent expiries faster than launches. Farxiga ($5,603M in the first nine months of 2026, 12% of Total Revenue) is losing US exclusivity, Soliris fell 26% at CER, and Brilinta's sales fell 62% at CER. The new launches (Datroway, Truqap, Baxfendy, Wainua) must scale quickly to fill the gap.
02
US pricing and China procurement. 42% of revenue is US and 12% China. The 2025 US government agreement already shows in gross margin, the Supreme Court has ended the company's IRA challenge, and China's volume-based procurement keeps cutting prices on mature drugs.
03
Binary trial readouts on a leveraged balance sheet. More than twenty high-value readouts are due within 18 months; three late-stage readouts missed between the Q1 and H1 2026 results and more have since (SERENA-4, HICKORY, the stopped eVOLVE-Lung02). Net debt of $25.8B is 1.2x EBITDA — manageable, but upfront licensing payments and the $2B Summit stake keep adding to it.
Industry Commentary
US drug pricing after the 2025 agreements
ONGOING HEADWIND

AstraZeneca's 9M 2026 announcement again lists the implementation of the US government agreement announced in 2025 among the factors moving its gross margin. Separately, the Inflation Reduction Act lets Medicare negotiate prices on older drugs, Farxiga among them; AstraZeneca's legal challenge ended when the US Supreme Court denied review in May 2026. For a company earning 42% of its revenue in the US, pricing is now set partly in Washington. The bull answer is volume: an agreed framework removes uncertainty, and new medicines launch at prices the framework allows.

Antibody-drug conjugates and combination deals
EMERGING TAILWIND

Antibody-drug conjugates, antibodies that carry chemotherapy straight to the tumour, are one of the fastest-growing classes in cancer, and AstraZeneca's two with Daiichi Sankyo are already material: Enhertu grew 33% at CER to $2,688M in the first nine months of 2026, won EU approval for use after surgery in HER2-positive early breast cancer on October 27, and Datroway more than quadrupled to $176M. The $2B investment in Summit Therapeutics (agreed September 29, completed October 5) and the October 2 collaboration pairing Datroway with Summit's ivonescimab, starting in triple-negative breast cancer, show the next step: combinations of a conjugate with an immune drug, which the company's oncology R&D head calls "a core pillar of our oncology strategy". The trade-off is economics: partnered products share their profits, which is part of why the reported gross margin sits around 82% rather than higher.
