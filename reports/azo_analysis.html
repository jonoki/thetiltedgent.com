01 Company Overview

AutoZone sells replacement auto parts, maintenance items and accessories through 8,031 stores: 6,863 in the United States, 1,001 in Mexico and 167 in Brazil. It is the largest specialty parts retailer in the American aftermarket by store count. Fiscal 2026 (52 weeks ended August 29, 2026) brought the company's first year above $20 billion: revenue of $20.34B, up 7.4% on FY2025's $18.94B, with a record 374 new stores. The company is worth $46.0B at the September 23 close; at the September 25 close, companiesmarketcap.com ranked it #539 in the world by market value.

The central debate has moved. For two years it was whether earnings had stalled. Diluted EPS went $149.55 (FY2024) → $144.87 (FY2025) → $152.55 in FY2026, finally above the old high. But the fourth quarter carried a one-off $96 million ($4.43 a share) refund of IEEPA tariffs. Take it out and FY2026 EPS is about $148.12, still short of 2024 (our arithmetic). LIFO inventory accounting pulls the other way: it charged $192 million of non-cash inflation cost to FY2026 gross profit. The live question is now traffic. DIY same-store sales fell 0.6% in the quarter as customer transactions dropped 5% or more, which CEO Phil Daniele called "not typical in this industry". Management's first FY2027 outlook assumes those customers come back. At 18.6× trailing earnings, the bull says the market is pricing a stall that never came. The bear says the refund flattered a quarter in which sales missed and the 2028 store target was cut, with shareholders' equity at negative $2.50B.

AutoZone sells wholesale to independent garages, dealerships and fleet operators. This is the growth story and the reason the revenue line held up while DIY slipped. Commercial sales rose 8.6% in Q4 to $1.91 billion: just over 8% a month for the first three months, then well over 9% in August. In the quarter it was 34% of domestic auto parts sales. The moat is inventory placement, not brand. A failed alternator is a non-discretionary purchase: the customer barely shops on price and needs the part today, so they pay whoever has it. AutoZone's advantage is that it has the part. It runs 6,863 domestic stores backed by 172 mega-hubs, and a mega-hub typically carries more than 100,000 SKUs, the slow-moving parts no single store can justify stocking. The network took decades and about $1.5 billion of capital spending in FY2026 alone; the plan for FY2027 is around $1.65 billion. It produces an after-tax ROIC of 35.8% on the company's own definition, with a 52.3% gross margin for the year. Those are economics closer to a specialty distributor than a big-box chain. ROIC is down from 41.3% a year earlier as the new stores absorb capital before they mature; management says it will hold it "in the mid-30s area". The other half of the model is the payables float. Net inventory per store was negative $107 thousand at year-end, and accounts payable covered 111.1% of inventory, so vendors finance the shelf. That is how a company with negative book equity generated $3.30B of operating cash in FY2026.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The DIY customer is being priced out, a trip at a time. Same-SKU inflation ran mid-single digits in Q4 and the DIY ticket rose about 5%, but transactions fell 5% or more. The CEO said a drop that size is "not typical in this industry". He added: "That lower-end consumer is definitely pressured and probably will continue to be pressured for some time." The FY2027 outlook assumes inflation eases to about 4% and transactions recover toward the 1–3% decline the CEO called the historical norm. If the customer keeps deferring repairs, the ticket alone cannot carry the comp.

2. Negative equity removes the margin for error. Shareholders' equity is −$2.50 billion, against $9.08 billion of debt and $326.1 million of cash. The company's adjusted-debt measure is $12.50B, at 2.5× EBITDAR. This is the deliberate result of decades of buybacks ($2.03B in FY2026 alone), not distress. But it means the model depends on uninterrupted access to debt markets and on credit ratings tied to that leverage target. A sustained earnings decline would force the buyback down, and the buyback is a large part of EPS growth.

3. O'Reilly is still growing faster, and management conceded the point. O'Reilly's trailing revenue is up 8.5% against AutoZone's 7.4% for FY2026, and ORLY trades at 27.3× trailing earnings versus AZO's 18.6×. On the call, UBS's Michael Lasser pointed to "a player in the industry that is driving same-store sales growth well ahead of AutoZone"; Daniele agreed the comps had "maybe not been as high as we would have liked." Commercial growth slowing from 10.4% in Q3 to 8.6% in Q4 is the number to watch.

4. The headline numbers are noisy. Q4 carried a $96 million tariff refund ($4.43 a share), a $70 million currency tailwind to sales ($0.87 a share) and a LIFO charge far smaller than a year earlier ($15M vs $80M). Excluding LIFO in both years, the CFO put Q4 EBIT growth at 4.4% and EPS growth at 8.5%, and that figure still includes the refund. Take the refund out as well and EBIT fell about 3% (our arithmetic, both before LIFO: $1,235.9M vs $1,276.1M). Operating expenses deleveraged 101 basis points on growth spending. The international reported comp of +10.7% was +1.3% in constant currency. A reader who takes the +15.1% EPS headline at face value is reading mostly one-offs and currency.

RSI compares the size of recent monthly gains to recent monthly losses on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 conventionally mean the stock has risen far and fast enough to be called "overbought"; below 30 means the opposite. It measures momentum, not value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

A first thing to say about this chart: the y-axis is in thousands of dollars. AutoZone has not split its stock since a 2-for-1 in April 1994, so a single share costs $2,843.34, one of the highest share prices on the NYSE. Percentage moves that would look ordinary on a $50 stock show up here as three-figure dollar swings. The $51.39 fall on September 23 was a 1.78% day. The $697.5 million spent on buybacks in Q4 bought just 223,000 shares. The whole company, 16.17 million shares, is worth $46.0 billion.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $1,784.84 to September 23, 2026 at $2,843.34, AZO returned +59.3%, all of it price, since there are no dividends, and much of it manufactured by a shrinking share count. The shape matters more than the total. Four of those five years were a near-uninterrupted grind higher, to an all-time monthly close of $4,290.24 in September 2025: +140% in four years with a beta of 0.34, exactly the low-volatility compounder the market wanted. The last twelve months undid a third of it. From that peak the stock is down 33.7%, and the current price is below every month-end close since May 2024's $2,769.94.

The de-rating is almost entirely multiple, not earnings. At the September 2025 peak, trailing EPS was FY2025's $144.87, so the stock traded at about 29.6×. EPS has since risen 5.3% to $152.55, and the multiple has fallen to 18.6×. Two step-downs did most of the damage. The first was a 20.9% drop across October–December 2025. The second was a −20.8% month in May 2026, from $3,704 to $2,935 around the Q3 report. The Q4 print did not start a third leg: the stock ended its two post-print sessions 1.4% above the pre-print close of $2,803.25.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A business with a 35.8% ROIC, 52% gross margins, non-discretionary demand and 8,031 stores now trades at 18.6× trailing earnings, more than eight turns below O'Reilly, in the year EPS finally set a new high ($152.55). Commercial grew 10.6% for the year and exited Q4 at well over 9%. DIY went from negative to flat by August. Management has given a concrete FY2027 framework: flat to low-single-digit domestic comps, high-single to low-double-digit commercial growth, about 400 stores. It bought back $2.03B of stock last year at an average of $3,496, well above today's price, and has $1.61B still authorized. If DIY transactions normalise as inflation eases, the 34% de-rating was a multiple story with nothing broken underneath.

Bear Case

Strip out $4.43 of tariff refunds and FY2026 EPS is about $148, still below 2024, and Q4 missed on sales. DIY comps turned negative on a 5%-plus drop in transactions. Commercial growth slowed from 10.4% to 8.6%. ROIC fell from 41.3% to 35.8%. The FY2028 store target was cut from roughly 500 to 430. Management's own outlook depends on the pressured low-income customer coming back. The buyback that props up EPS runs on a balance sheet with −$2.50B of equity. Eleven target cuts, and no rating changes, suggest the sell side is still walking its numbers down rather than defending a floor.

Top 3 Risks

01
DIY transactions do not come back. The FY2027 outlook assumes ticket growth of about 4% and a recovery in traffic from a 5%-plus decline. The CFO said of the outlook that "most of this is predicated on inflation." If inflation eases but customers keep deferring repairs, domestic comps sit near flat. Meanwhile SG&A is planned to grow about 8% in total, which deleverages the P&L, and there is no tariff refund to fill the gap.
02
Negative equity constrains the response to weakness. AutoZone has −$2.50B of shareholders' equity, $9.08B of debt ($12.50B on its adjusted measure, at 2.5× EBITDAR), $326.1M of cash and a current ratio of 0.90. There is no book cushion. Debt is capped by leverage targets tied to its credit ratings, so a sustained profit decline would force the buyback down before the balance sheet could repair. That would remove the main support under EPS at exactly the wrong moment.
03
Share loss to O'Reilly in the commercial channel. DIFM is where the growth is (+8.6% in Q4, 29% of revenue) and where ORLY competes hardest. Growth per commercial program slowed to +2.7% from +4.5% in Q3. If the gap in comps persists, the eight-turn valuation discount is the market pricing a real competitive outcome, not an opportunity.

Industry Commentary

CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Sector-wide multiple compression
Auto-parts retail has de-rated as a group. Over the twelve months to September 23: AZO −31.0%, AAP −31.6%, ORLY −17.2%, while the S&P 500 (SPY price return) rose 15.8%. ORLY at $85.90 sits near its 52-week low of $82.59, and AAP's trailing revenue is shrinking (−1.3%). When companies with very different growth rates and balance sheets fall together, the market is repricing the category's growth assumption, not reacting to one operator. Within the group, AutoZone has now fallen 14 points further than O'Reilly. One reading is that the market is separating the two on comps, in which case the discount can persist even if AutoZone executes.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Inflation: tariffs, refunds, LIFO and fuel
Same-SKU inflation ran mid-single digits in Q4, and management expects it to average about 4% in FY2027. Higher prices lift the ticket, but they also cost transactions: Daniele said higher oil and gas prices "likely dampened our traffic and sales results for most of the quarter." The accounting runs in both directions. LIFO took $192 million out of FY2026 gross profit, and the plan for FY2027 is $85–90 million. The IEEPA refund put $96 million back in Q4. Daniele argued that "while the tariff refunds may appear to be a windfall, the reality is that the refund environment was deflationary, and the industry likely would have raised prices faster if both retailers and suppliers hadn't received cost relief from those refunds." Pricing across the industry remained, in the CFO's words, "pretty disciplined and rational".
EMERGING TAILWIND
An older fleet, and miles still being driven
The demand drivers have not gone away. The CFO pointed to "more expensive, new and used vehicles that continue to drive the average age of vehicles higher". The CEO said that despite higher fuel prices "we haven't necessarily seen a tail off in the miles driven yet". Weather also turned: hotter temperatures late in the quarter lifted the hot-weather categories. Daniele expects "a normal to tougher winter", and said of the weather: "Both hot and cold are good for us." The bull and bear cases both fit these facts. The swing variable for FY2027 is whether the stretched DIY customer returns once inflation eases. On the call, management said that after spells of heavy traffic declines, transactions have historically rebounded.
