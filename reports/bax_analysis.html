01 Company Overview

Baxter makes the unglamorous things a hospital cannot run a shift without: IV solutions, infusion pumps and administration sets, parenteral (IV) nutrition, injectable drugs, inhaled anaesthetics, surgical products, patient monitoring and connected-care systems. FY2025 revenue from continuing operations was $11.24B, up 6% reported and 3% operationally; GAAP EPS from continuing operations was −$1.75 against adjusted EPS of $2.27. In the third quarter of 2026 (to September 30) sales were $2.98B, up 5% reported and organically, GAAP EPS was $0.57 and adjusted EPS $0.58, and the company raised its full-year outlook for the second time this year. At the October 30, 2026 close the whole company was worth about $11.90B — roughly one year of sales.

This is a story about a company that spent five years rearranging its own furniture. In December 2021 Baxter bought Hillrom for $10.5B in cash — about $12.5B including assumed debt — to push its digital-health and connected-care offering across hospitals, homes and other care settings. A run of sales followed: the BioPharma Solutions contract-manufacturing unit went to Advent International and Warburg Pincus for $4.25B (completed October 2, 2023, now Simtra BioPharma Solutions), and the Kidney Care business — dialysis therapies — was sold to Carlyle for $3.8B, closing January 31, 2025 as the standalone company Vantive. What remains is a hospital-products company with a large debt load, a flagship infusion pump on a shipment hold, an FDA Warning Letter at its main U.S. drug plant, a dividend cut 94% to a penny a quarter, and a CEO just over a year into the job. The share price fell from an $85.84 month-end close in December 2021 to $23.00 — a 73% decline from that peak. Because the portfolio changed so much, long-run per-share series (revenue, EPS, dividends) are not like-for-like: FY2021 revenue of $12.15B and FY2022's −17.2% "growth" on StockAnalysis reflect divestiture restatement, not a collapse in demand.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

IV solutions manufacturing scale Pump installed base → recurring sets Hospital purchasing relationships Regulatory approvals as barrier Medically essential, low-ticket products Patient-monitoring & connected-care footprint

The moat is essentialness plus manufacturing. Sterile fluids and infusion consumables are low-price, high-volume, heavily regulated products that hospitals order every day; building sterile capacity is slow and approval-gated, so the incumbent keeps the business unless it stumbles. The weakness is that the same regulation cuts both ways: when a device fails in the field, the regulator can freeze a franchise faster than any competitor could. Baxter's recent history is a lesson in that — the moat protects the category, not the company's execution within it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The Novum IQ pump. In July 2025 the FDA published an early alert on a recall of certain Novum IQ large-volume pumps; MedTech Dive reported the issues were tied to 79 serious injuries and 2 deaths, including underinfusion when stepping up flow rates. The Q3 2026 10-Q, filed October 29, still describes the July 2025 stop on distributing and installing the pump in the U.S. and Canada, except where medically necessary. A pump that cannot be placed is a pump that does not pull through years of disposable-set sales.
02
Leverage. Debt and finance leases of $8.60B against $1.98B of cash at September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q) and TTM EBITDA of $1.83B (StockAnalysis) — about 3.6x net debt/EBITDA (our calculation), down from about 4.0x at the last edition after the August bond buyback. Management aims for about 3x by the end of 2026 on its own measure. That ratio is why the dividend went to $0.01 and why spare cash goes to debt, leaving little room for acquisitions or a pump-program reset.
03
Margin erosion. Gross margin has slid from 40.35% in FY2023 to 35.73% in FY2025 and 33.37% over the twelve months to September 2026; Q3 2026's adjusted gross margin of 38.3% was 1.1 points lower than a year earlier. Some of that is portfolio mix after the divestitures; some is cost. Either way, it is the line a turnaround has to reverse.
04
Credibility. FY2025 adjusted EPS guidance was cut in October 2025 and the year still came in below the reduced range. 2026 has gone the other way, with raises in July and October, though July's leaned on a one-off tariff refund. On the October call the CFO listed 2027 pressures — inflation, tariffs, the end of Vantive transition services, product exits and the cephalosporin exit — ahead of 2027 guidance in February (MarketBeat's account of the call).
05
Drug-plant quality. On October 20, 2026 the FDA posted a Warning Letter to Baxter's Round Lake, Illinois plant — per BioSpace one of its three main drug plants and the only one on the U.S. mainland. BioSpace reports the FDA found that controls failed to stop cephalosporin from reaching areas where other drugs are made, and that management oversight of drug manufacturing was inadequate. Baxter said it "takes the Warning Letter seriously and is committed to comprehensively addressing the issues raised" (BioSpace, October 22 update). The stock closed down 1.92% that day.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the $485M goodwill impairment on Front Line Care in Q4 2025, which drove that quarter's −$2.01 GAAP EPS from continuing operations and still sits inside the TTM figure. Second, the IEEPA tariff refund worth $0.11 per share in Q2 2026 adjusted EPS — a benefit that is by nature non-recurring. Third, in Q3 2026, a $253M pre-tax gain on the early extinguishment of debt (the bonds were bought back below face value), which lifts GAAP EPS and is excluded from adjusted EPS, alongside $39M of restructuring costs for the cephalosporin exit, also excluded (per MarketBeat's account of the call). The Kidney Care business is reported as discontinued, so total-company and continuing-operations figures differ slightly (nine-month 2026 GAAP EPS: $0.78 total, $0.80 continuing). On the balance sheet, Baxter announced cash tender offers in August 2026 and, per its August 18 release, accepted $863.9M of principal across its 2032, 2043, 2046 and 2051 notes for an aggregate purchase price of no more than the $600M offer cap, settling August 20 (sum of the four series accepted: $421.99M + $132.20M + $72.74M + $236.93M). Buying back low-coupon long bonds below par is a sensible use of cash; the release says the tender retired $864M of debt in the quarter, and the September 30 balance sheet ($8.60B of debt and finance leases) is after it.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 30, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $78.96 to the October 30, 2026 close of $23.00, BAX lost 70.9% of its price; over the same dates the S&P 500 fund SPY rose 64.6% and the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ 78.8% (price only). Dividends do not rescue it: adding back the $4.18 per share of dividends with ex-dates in the window (Yahoo Finance dividend history; simple sum, not reinvested) brings the total return to roughly −66% — an estimate. The shape is a staircase down: a 2022–2023 slide after the Hillrom deal, a failed 2024 recovery, a July 2025 collapse, a floor in March 2026, a sharp mid-2026 rebound and a slide back since the summer.

The 6-period monthly RSI recorded its lowest reading of the window, 15.6, in February 2023, and a second capitulation at 17.2 in July 2025. It peaked at 68.2 in July 2026 — the highest reading in the series, just short of overbought — and has cooled to 50.8 after the September and October declines. Unlike most broken charts, the trend has turned: the 10-month moving average is about $21.41, below the current price, and the stock has closed above it every month since June.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 11x the FY2026 consensus of $2.09 and roughly one times sales for a company whose products hospitals cannot do without. The mess is mostly identified and priced: the portfolio has been simplified to two segments, the dividend has been cut to free cash for debt, $863.9M of bond principal was retired in August for no more than $600M, free cash flow reached $705M in the first nine months of 2026, and guidance has been raised twice this year rather than cut. A CEO with an operations background and a new CFO from a medtech peer are in place. If the Novum IQ pump returns to market and margins recover even part of the way toward FY2023's 40% gross margin, the earnings power is well above a $12B market cap.

Bear Case

Cheap has been the thesis for years and it has cost holders 71% over five. The July raise leaned on an $0.11 tariff refund, and Q3 adjusted EPS fell 16% on a higher tax rate. Gross margin has fallen about seven points since FY2023, the flagship pump remains on a shipment hold first disclosed in 2025, after a recall linked to serious injuries and deaths, the FDA has now sent a Warning Letter to the main U.S. drug plant, and leverage near 3.6x EBITDA limits what management can do about any of it. Twelve of fifteen analysts sit at Hold and one at Sell; three cut their targets after the print, consensus for 2027 ($2.05) sits below 2026 ($2.09), and the stock fell 7.7% on a beat-and-raise day. A turnaround that needs a device reset, margin repair and deleveraging all at once has a lot of ways to disappoint.

Top 3 Risks

01
Regulatory risk, devices and drugs. The Novum IQ hold remains in place, and the Round Lake drug plant now has an FDA Warning Letter. A longer hold, further field actions or FDA escalation would erode the pump installed base that drives recurring set and solution sales, and unresolved plant findings can delay approvals and supply.
02
Earnings quality. FY2026 adjusted EPS benefited from a one-off tariff refund, and GAAP and adjusted figures swing apart quarter to quarter (Q2 2026: $0.26 vs $0.56; Q3 2026's $0.57 GAAP included a $253M debt gain). Management has already flagged 2027 pressures, and the refund will not repeat.
03
Balance sheet. $8.60B of debt and finance leases at September 30, 2026, about 3.6x net debt/EBITDA (our calculation). Deleveraging depends on sustaining free cash flow while funding any pump remediation.
Industry Commentary
Device quality as the binding constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

In hospital infusion, the product is only as good as its field record. MedTech Dive reported in July 2025 that the Novum IQ large-volume pump — cleared by the FDA in April 2024 as part of Baxter's expansion push — was subject to a recall tied to 79 serious injuries and 2 deaths, with failure modes including underinfusion when transitioning to higher flow rates and misloaded tubing. A pump recall does not just cost pump sales: hospitals tend to standardise on one platform, and a platform they cannot deploy is a platform a competitor gets to replace. The Q3 2026 10-Q still describes the hold, and in October the FDA added a Warning Letter on the Round Lake drug plant, so the quality problem is no longer confined to devices. Until the hold lifts, the most valuable razor in Baxter's razor-and-blades model is sitting in a warehouse.

Portfolio surgery and what it leaves behind
ONGOING HEADWIND

Baxter bought Hillrom for $10.5B, then sold BioPharma Solutions for $4.25B and Kidney Care for $3.8B. Net, the company swapped two businesses — one of them its historic dialysis franchise — for a hospital-equipment platform whose Front Line Care unit has since taken a $485M impairment. The divestitures funded debt reduction, but they also removed diversification, and the "Other" line of Vantive supply sales is a reminder that the separation is still being unwound. The Q2 2026 segment recast, which (per GuruFocus) folds Pharmaceuticals into MPT, is the latest step; it simplifies management and makes the historical segment data harder to compare.

The turnaround scorecard — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull framing: the pieces a turnaround needs are showing up in order — new CEO (named July 2025), dividend reset (November 2025), a quarter that took the big write-down at once (Q4 2025), a low bar for 2026 (flat organic growth guide), then a beat and raise (Q2 2026), a debt tender and a new CFO. Stocks bottom when the bad news stops surprising, and March 2026's $16.80 may have been that point. The bear framing: the raise was flattered by a tariff refund, the pump problem is not solved, and analysts who mostly rate it Hold, at a $27.50 average target, are not pricing in a clean recovery either. The way to judge it is not the P/E; it is organic growth versus guidance, gross-margin direction, and a date for the Novum IQ return. The October 29 read: organic growth of 5% beat the guide, adjusted gross margin fell 1.1 points, and no return date for the pump. The next read is the fourth-quarter report and the first 2027 guidance in February.
