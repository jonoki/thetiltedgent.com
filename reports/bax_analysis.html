01 Company Overview

Baxter makes the unglamorous things a hospital cannot run a shift without: IV solutions, infusion pumps and administration sets, parenteral (IV) nutrition, injectable drugs, inhaled anaesthetics, surgical products, patient monitoring and connected-care systems. FY2025 revenue from continuing operations was $11.24B, up 6% reported and 3% operationally; GAAP EPS from continuing operations was −$1.75 against adjusted EPS of $2.27. At the September 21, 2026 close the whole company was worth about $11.85B — roughly one year of sales.

This is a story about a company that spent five years rearranging its own furniture. In December 2021 Baxter bought Hillrom for $10.5B in cash — about $12.5B including assumed debt — to push its digital-health and connected-care offering across hospitals, homes and other care settings. A run of sales followed: the BioPharma Solutions contract-manufacturing unit went to Advent International and Warburg Pincus for $4.25B (completed October 2, 2023, now Simtra BioPharma Solutions), and the Kidney Care business — dialysis therapies — was sold to Carlyle for $3.8B, closing January 31, 2025 as the standalone company Vantive. What remains is a hospital-products company with a large debt load, a flagship infusion pump on a shipment hold, a dividend cut 94% to a penny a quarter, and a new CEO just over a year into the job. The share price fell from an $85.84 month-end close in December 2021 to $22.93 — a 73% decline from that peak. Because the portfolio changed so much, long-run per-share series (revenue, EPS, dividends) are not like-for-like: FY2021 revenue of $12.15B and FY2022's −17.2% "growth" on StockAnalysis reflect divestiture restatement, not a collapse in demand.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

IV solutions manufacturing scale Pump installed base → recurring sets Hospital purchasing relationships Regulatory approvals as barrier Medically essential, low-ticket products Patient-monitoring & connected-care footprint

The moat is essentialness plus manufacturing. Sterile fluids and infusion consumables are low-price, high-volume, heavily regulated products that hospitals order every day; building sterile capacity is slow and approval-gated, so the incumbent keeps the business unless it stumbles. The weakness is that the same regulation cuts both ways: when a device fails in the field, the regulator can freeze a franchise faster than any competitor could. Baxter's recent history is a lesson in that — the moat protects the category, not the company's execution within it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The Novum IQ pump. In July 2025 the FDA published an early alert on a recall of certain Novum IQ large-volume pumps; MedTech Dive reported the issues were tied to 79 serious injuries and 2 deaths, including underinfusion when stepping up flow rates. The Q2 2026 release still describes a "shipment and installation hold" on the pump. A pump that cannot be placed is a pump that does not pull through years of disposable-set sales.
02
Leverage. Total debt of $9.67B against $2.15B of cash (StockAnalysis) and TTM EBITDA of $1.88B — about 4.0x net debt/EBITDA (our calculation). That ratio is why the dividend went to $0.01 and why every spare dollar goes to debt, leaving little room for acquisitions or a pump-program reset.
03
Margin erosion. Gross margin has slid from 40.35% in FY2023 to 35.73% in FY2025 and 34.10% on a TTM basis. Some of that is portfolio mix after the divestitures; some is cost. Either way, it is the line a turnaround has to reverse.
04
Credibility. FY2025 adjusted EPS guidance was cut in October 2025 and the year still came in below the reduced range. The FY2026 raise leaned partly on a one-off tariff refund. A management team rebuilding trust needs clean beats, not helped ones.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the $485M goodwill impairment on Front Line Care in Q4 2025, which drove that quarter's −$2.01 GAAP EPS from continuing operations and still sits inside the TTM figure. Second, the IEEPA tariff refund worth $0.11 per share in Q2 2026 adjusted EPS — a benefit that is by nature non-recurring. Third, discontinued operations: the Kidney Care business is reported as discontinued, so StockAnalysis's total-company net income (−$1.06B TTM) and EPS (Q2 2026: $0.24) differ slightly from the company's continuing-operations figures (Q2 2026: $0.26). On the balance sheet, Baxter announced cash tender offers in August 2026 and, per its August 18 release, accepted $863.9M of principal across its 2032, 2043, 2046 and 2051 notes for an aggregate purchase price of no more than the $600M offer cap, settling August 20 (sum of the four series accepted: $421.99M + $132.20M + $72.74M + $236.93M). Buying back low-coupon long bonds below par is a sensible use of cash; it also means the $9.67B debt figure above is before that reduction.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 11x the FY2026 consensus of $2.05 and roughly one times sales for a company whose products hospitals cannot do without. The mess is mostly identified and priced: the portfolio has been simplified to two segments, the dividend has been cut to free cash for debt, $863.9M of bond principal was retired in August for no more than $600M, first-half free cash flow swung from −$144M to +$257M, and guidance is now being raised rather than cut. A new CEO with an operations background and a new CFO from a medtech peer are in place. If the Novum IQ pump returns to market and margins recover even part of the way toward FY2023's 40% gross margin, the earnings power is well above a $12B market cap.

Bear Case

Cheap has been the thesis for four years and it has cost holders 71%. The July raise leaned on an $0.11 tariff refund; strip it out and Q2 adjusted EPS fell from $0.59 to $0.45. Gross margin has fallen more than six points since FY2023, the flagship pump remains on a shipment hold first disclosed in 2025, after a recall linked to serious injuries and deaths, and leverage near 4x EBITDA limits what management can do about any of it. Eleven of thirteen analysts sit at Hold, and the stock has already given back 11.8% of its summer rally in three weeks. A turnaround that needs a device reset, margin repair and deleveraging all at once has a lot of ways to disappoint.

Top 3 Risks

01
Infusion-pump regulatory risk. The Novum IQ hold remains in place. A longer hold, further field actions or FDA escalation would erode the pump installed base that drives recurring set and solution sales.
02
Earnings quality. FY2026 adjusted EPS benefited from a one-off tariff refund, and the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is wide (Q2 2026: $0.26 vs $0.56). If organic growth slips back toward the original "about flat" guide, the raise unwinds.
03
Balance sheet. $9.67B of debt before the August tender, ~4.0x net debt/EBITDA (our calculation). Deleveraging depends on sustaining free cash flow while funding any pump remediation.
Industry Commentary
Device quality as the binding constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

In hospital infusion, the product is only as good as its field record. MedTech Dive reported in July 2025 that the Novum IQ large-volume pump — cleared by the FDA in April 2024 as part of Baxter's expansion push — was subject to a recall tied to 79 serious injuries and 2 deaths, with failure modes including underinfusion when transitioning to higher flow rates and misloaded tubing. A pump recall does not just cost pump sales: hospitals tend to standardise on one platform, and a platform they cannot deploy is a platform a competitor gets to replace. The Q2 2026 release still describes the hold. Until it lifts, the most valuable razor in Baxter's razor-and-blades model is sitting in a warehouse.

Portfolio surgery and what it leaves behind
ONGOING HEADWIND

Baxter bought Hillrom for $10.5B, then sold BioPharma Solutions for $4.25B and Kidney Care for $3.8B. Net, the company swapped two businesses — one of them its historic dialysis franchise — for a hospital-equipment platform whose Front Line Care unit has since taken a $485M impairment. The divestitures funded debt reduction, but they also removed diversification, and the "Other" line of Vantive supply sales is a reminder that the separation is still being unwound. The Q2 2026 segment recast, which (per GuruFocus) folds Pharmaceuticals into MPT, is the latest step; it simplifies management and makes the historical segment data harder to compare.

The turnaround scorecard — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull framing: the pieces a turnaround needs are showing up in order — new CEO (September 2025), dividend reset (November 2025), a kitchen-sink quarter with an impairment (Q4 2025), a low bar for 2026 (flat organic growth guide), then a beat and raise (Q2 2026), a debt tender and a new CFO. Stocks bottom when the bad news stops surprising, and March 2026's $16.80 may have been that point. The bear framing: the raise was flattered by a tariff refund, the pump problem is not solved, and a Hold-heavy Street at a $28.73 average target is not pricing in a clean recovery either. The way to judge it is not the P/E; it is organic growth versus guidance, gross-margin direction, and a date for the Novum IQ return. All three get their next read on October 29.
