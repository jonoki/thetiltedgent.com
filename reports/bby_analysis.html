01 Company Overview

In 1966 Richard Schulze and James Wheeler opened a hi-fi stereo shop in St. Paul called Sound of Music. It became Best Buy in 1983 with seven stores, outlived big-box rivals such as Circuit City, and is now one of the few national chains in the United States whose whole reason to exist is selling you the gadget in person. The company operates about 1,117 locations across the US and Canada after leaving China (2011), Europe (2012) and Mexico (2020). FY2026 revenue was $41.69B, up 0.39%, with GAAP EPS of $5.04. At $93.22 the market values it at $19.55B — roughly #1,193 in the world — which is less than half of one year's sales.

The shape of the story is a long hangover followed by a sudden sober morning. Revenue peaked at $51.76B in FY2022, the pandemic year when everyone bought a laptop and a bigger TV at once, then fell three years running to $41.53B in FY2025 — a 20% slide in the top line. The stock followed: from a $122.24 month-end close in October 2021 to $60.49 in April 2026, a 50.5% drawdown. Then 2026 turned. Comparable sales grew 2.0% in Q1 FY27 and 4.1% in Q2, both ahead of the company's own outlook, management lifted the full-year outlook from "−1% to +1%" comps to "+1.9% to +3.0%", and the stock is up 54% from the April close. All of this is happening while the company changes its chief executive and its chief financial officer in the same six months. The debate: is this the start of a new upgrade cycle — AI PCs, AI glasses, a new Marketplace and an advertising business — or a cyclical bounce in a low-margin retailer that the market has already paid for? The consensus price target sits 7% below the current price.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

National store footprint (~1,117) Vendor relationships with big tech brands Geek Squad services & install ~1/3 historical US TV unit share Omnichannel: 33% of US sales online Retail media data (Best Buy Ads)

The moat is the showroom. Technology brands need somewhere a customer can see the screen, try the headset and get the thing installed, and Best Buy is the last nationwide specialist doing that at scale — which is why Bonfig's 27 years of relationships with major technology companies are cited as a qualification for the top job. What the showroom does not protect is price: the product on the shelf is identical to the one sold online, so gross margin sits in the low twenties and operating margin near 4%. The bet on Marketplace and Ads is an attempt to earn money from the traffic itself rather than only from the box.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Thin margins, cyclical demand. A 4.31% trailing operating margin leaves little room for error. Revenue fell 10.55% in FY2023 and kept falling for two more years; a replacement-cycle retailer lives and dies on whether consumers feel like upgrading.
02
Expenses rising with the investment phase. Q2 SG&A grew to 19.7% of sales from 19.4%, with management citing compensation, advertising and investment in Ads and Marketplace as the drivers. The payoff from those platforms is not yet disclosed.
03
Leadership turnover at the top. New CEO on November 1, a CFO who joined on August 19, and the incoming CEO taking over in the holiday quarter. Strategy continuity is likely (he is an insider), but guidance credibility rests with a team that has not yet reported a quarter together.
04
Part of the beat was one-off. Q2 included about $34M of tariff refunds. Stripping it out does not erase the beat, but it is not a recurring earnings stream.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the prior-year restructuring charges: Q2 FY26 carried $114M, while Q2 FY27 had a $6M reversal — which is why TTM GAAP EPS growth of 65.7% overstates the underlying improvement. Adjusted Q2 EPS was $1.47. Second, tariff refunds of about $34M helped Q2. Third, GAAP versus adjusted is close this quarter (GAAP EPS $1.48, adjusted $1.47), so the adjustments are not doing heavy lifting. Buybacks are modest: $36M in Q2 and about $300M planned for FY27, roughly 1.5% of the market cap, against $203M paid in dividends in Q2 alone.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 close of $105.71 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $93.22, BBY lost 11.8% of its price. Dividends change the picture: per-share dividends for FY2023 through FY2026 alone sum to $14.76, and three $0.96 payments have gone ex in FY27, so a holder collected at least $17.64 a share — putting the total return, without reinvestment, at roughly +5% (estimate). Five years of owning Best Buy paid you its dividend and not much else. The path between was anything but flat: a 50% slide from the $122.24 October 2021 month-end to the $60.49 April 2026 low, then a 54% rebound in five months.

The 6-period monthly RSI now reads 70.4 — just across the overbought line for the first time since September 2024 (77.6), which was the last month-end close above $100. The low of the window was 18.5 in June 2022. The 10-month moving average is about $73.22, $20 below the price: the long-term trend has turned up decisively. Momentum is stretched rather than broken — the question is whether earnings catch up with the price or the price comes back to the average.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

After four years of shrinking sales, comps have turned positive two quarters running — +2.0% then +4.1% — and management raised the full-year comp guide by about 2.5 points at the midpoint. Gross margin is expanding (23.9% vs 23.2%) as services, the Marketplace and Best Buy Ads add higher-margin dollars, and the man who built those businesses takes over as CEO on November 1. You pay about 13–14x forward earnings, collect a 4.12% dividend and a ~9% free-cash-flow yield, and own a 26% ROIC business. If AI PCs and new devices drive a genuine replacement cycle, a 4.4–4.5% operating margin on growing sales compounds, and the Hold-heavy consensus has room to be upgraded.

Bear Case

The stock is up 54% from April and trades 7% above the average analyst target, with 19 of 25 analysts at Hold, RSI above 70 and 7.55% of the float short. The operating margin is 4.3% — a rounding error in either direction moves earnings sharply — and Q2 showed SG&A growing faster than sales, with part of the beat from $34M of tariff refunds. International is shrinking. The company enters its most important quarter with a new CEO and a CFO in her first months. Revenue is still 18% below FY2022, and the history of this chart is that rallies above $100 have not held.

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation has run ahead of the Street. $93.22 against an $86.70 average target and a $100 high target leaves little margin for a disappointing holiday quarter; the Q2 reaction showed how quickly the market punishes a beat that comes with higher costs.
02
Margin fragility. On ~$42B of revenue, 10bps of operating margin is roughly $42M of operating profit. Expense growth for Marketplace, Ads and compensation, plus the non-recurring tariff refunds, make the 4.4–4.5% full-year guide the number to watch.
03
Execution through a double leadership change. CEO handover on November 1 and a CFO who joined on August 19, landing in the holiday quarter. An insider successor lowers the strategic risk, but a new team resetting guidance is a common pattern after a CEO change.
Industry Commentary
New categories vs. the post-pandemic hangover
EMERGING TAILWIND

Consumer electronics runs on replacement cycles. The pandemic pulled years of laptop, TV and appliance purchases into 2020–21, and Best Buy spent FY2023–FY2025 paying for it: revenue fell from $51.76B to $41.53B. The FY27 turn is being led by exactly the categories a new cycle would show up in — computing & mobile phones +6.8% and consumer electronics +5.6% in Domestic Q2 comps — with AI glasses and trading cards named as emerging drivers. Traditional gaming and entertainment (−6.3%) are the weak spots. The question for the sector is whether this is a cycle or a blip; two quarters is not yet a cycle.

Retail media and marketplaces — the margin escape route
INVESTMENT PHASE

Selling the same product as everyone else caps a retailer's gross margin. Selling access to shoppers — ads placed by the brands on the shelf — and taking a commission on third-party sellers' goods does not. Best Buy launched its US Marketplace and has been scaling Best Buy Ads under Bonfig, and in Q2 FY27 both were credited with gross-profit growth while also raising expenses. Neither line is separately disclosed, so investors cannot yet size the payoff. Disclosure of these businesses under the new CEO would be a meaningful change in how the stock can be valued.
