01 Company Overview

Becton, Dickinson and Company is a 129-year-old medical technology manufacturer whose products — syringes, needles, IV catheters, infusion pumps, medication-dispensing cabinets, prefillable drug-delivery systems and interventional devices — sit inside essentially every hospital in the developed world. Fiscal 2025 (year ended September 30, 2025) revenue as then reported was $21.84B, up 8.2% from $20.18B, with net income of $1.68B and diluted EPS of $5.82. The company ranks roughly #498 globally by market capitalisation.

That FY2025 figure is now a historical artefact, and understanding why is the whole point of this page. On February 9, 2026, BD completed the separation of its Biosciences & Diagnostic Solutions business and its combination with Waters Corporation. Those operations are now reported as discontinued operations, prior periods have been restated, and what remains — "New BD" — is a smaller, four-segment, roughly $19B-revenue medical-device company. The central debate is whether that smaller company is a cleaner, faster-compounding asset that the market has not yet re-rated, or a low-growth commodity-supplies business that has just given away its most interesting growth engine and is left carrying $64.15B of enterprise value against $48.05B of equity value — that is, a balance sheet where debt still does a lot of the talking.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
129-YEAR INSTALLED BASE GPO CONTRACT LOCK-IN FDA-QUALIFIED MANUFACTURING PRIMARY-CONTAINER QUALIFICATION CLINICAL WORKFLOW EMBEDDING SCALE IN COMMODITY CONSUMABLES 54-YEAR DIVIDEND RECORD

BD's core advantage is not technological — it is procedural. A hospital that standardises on Pyxis cabinets has built medication-safety policy, nurse training, pharmacy audit trails and electronic-health-record integrations around a specific vendor's hardware; a pharmaceutical company that qualifies a BD prefillable syringe as the primary container for a biologic has written that component into a regulatory filing and cannot change it without a submission. These are switching costs measured in years of validation work, not in purchase price. Layer on manufacturing scale in products where the unit economics are measured in cents, distribution through group purchasing organisations that prefer to consolidate vendors, and a 54-consecutive-year record of dividend increases that anchors a specific, very patient shareholder base — and you have a business that is extraordinarily hard to displace and equally hard to accelerate. The beta of 0.28 is the market's summary of exactly that.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The growth ceiling is structural, not cyclical. Nine-month fiscal 2026 FXN growth by segment runs +1.5%, +4.2%, +1.6% and +5.3%. Strip out currency and the blended organic rate is roughly 3–4%. No amount of operational execution turns a hospital-consumables business into a double-digit grower; the only levers are mix shift toward Interventional and BioPharma, price, and acquisition. That is a slow machine, and the FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $12.62–$12.72 leans heavily on margin and buyback rather than volume.
02
Leverage is doing real work in the equity story. Enterprise value of $64.15B against a $48.05B market cap implies roughly $16B of net debt, and the current ratio is 0.87, meaning current liabilities exceed current assets. BD paid down $2,696M of debt and repurchased $2,250M of stock in nine months — a $4.9B capital-allocation program at a company generating $2.59B of free cash flow. That gap was funded by separation proceeds, which is a one-time source.
03
The dividend payout ratio on GAAP earnings is above 100%. At 126.09% against $4.20 of annual dividend, BD is currently paying out more than it earns on a GAAP basis. On adjusted EPS of roughly $12.65 the payout is a comfortable 33%, and the separation-related charges are the reason for the gap — but a 54-year dividend record is exactly the kind of asset a company will defend past the point of financial logic, and that constrains flexibility.
04
Regulatory concentration in Connected Care. BD has already lived through a multi-year FDA remediation on the Alaris infusion platform. Infusion pumps are among the most heavily scrutinised device classes in the United States, and a single recall or consent-decree event in a segment producing a quarter of revenue would do damage disproportionate to its weight — as it did the last time.

Notable One-Off Items

Fiscal 2026 is, for reporting purposes, a broken year, and almost every backward-looking ratio on this page is affected. (1) The Biosciences & Diagnostic Solutions separation and Waters combination completed February 9, 2026; all historical results for that business are now discontinued operations and prior periods have been restated, which is why FY2025's reported $21.84B and the restated continuing-operations base of roughly $18.9B both circulate as "BD revenue." (2) The nine months to June 30, 2026 carry a $276M after-tax loss from discontinued operations. (3) Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended March 31, 2026 — the quarter containing the separation) printed a net loss of $311M, −$1.11 per diluted share, despite $695M of operating income. (4) TTM net income of $942M against FY2025's $1,678M is therefore a transaction artefact, not a collapse in the operating business. (5) The 126.09% dividend payout ratio is a direct consequence of the same depressed GAAP denominator. Use adjusted EPS guidance of $12.62–$12.72 and segment FXN growth as the operating read; use GAAP only if you are willing to unpick the charges.

RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own history ("overbought"); below 30 means the opposite ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a year, and an expensive one can stay overbought. Here it is calculated on monthly closes, so each point represents a month of trading rather than a day.

The five-year record is unambiguous and unkind. From the October 2021 close of $239.59 to $176.42 on September 10, 2026, BDX lost 26.4% of its price — roughly −5.9% annualised — during a period in which the broad US market compounded strongly. Dividends of roughly $3.60–$4.20 a year soften that to something closer to −12% on a total-return basis before accounting for the Waters shares distributed in February 2026, which are not captured in the price line at all. Even generously adjusted, this is five years of capital destruction relative to almost any alternative, and it is the necessary starting point for any bull case.

The shape matters more than the endpoints. For the first two and a half years — October 2021 through roughly March 2025 — BDX traded in a remarkably tight band between about $220 and $280, behaving exactly as a 0.28-beta hospital-supplies name should: it ignored the 2022 bear market almost entirely, and it ignored the 2023–24 melt-up equally. The damage is concentrated in a single eleven-month stretch. From the March 2025 close of $229.06 the stock fell to $147.12 by May 2026 — a −35.8% decline, including a −16.7% month in May 2025 that is the single worst month in the entire window. Measured from the August 2023 high close of $279.45, the peak-to-trough drawdown is −47.4%.

What has happened since May 2026 is the actual investment question. The stock bottomed at $147.12, ground higher through June and July, and then gapped up after the August 6 earnings beat to close August at $188.11 — a +27.9% move off the low in three months, in a stock with a beta of 0.28. That is not a defensive-name move; that is a re-rating attempt. The September pullback to $176.42 has given back roughly 40% of the August advance. The monthly RSI panel below the price chart shows the mechanism: the indicator collapsed into oversold territory through the first half of 2026 and has since snapped back toward and through neutral. Momentum has turned; whether it holds depends entirely on the November guidance.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A simplified four-segment BD trading at 13.3× forward earnings with a 2.38% yield, a 54-year dividend record and a 0.28 beta is priced as a declining business, but all four segments grew in Q3 and the two highest-quality ones — Interventional (+6.4%) and BioPharma Systems (+6.6%) — grew fastest. Management beat its own Q3 expectations on revenue, margin and EPS and raised the FY2026 adjusted EPS floor to $12.62. The separation removed the most cyclical, most tariff-exposed part of the portfolio and funded roughly $4.9B of debt repayment and buyback in nine months. If November's FY2027 guidance frames mid-single-digit organic growth with margin expansion, the stock re-rates toward the $195–$204 target cluster without anything heroic happening.

Bear Case

Strip out currency and BD grew 4.4% FXN in its best recent quarter, with its largest segment at +1.5% FXN over nine months. That is a business whose terminal growth rate is roughly nominal GDP, carrying roughly $16B of net debt, a 0.87 current ratio, ROIC under 8% and a GAAP payout ratio above 100%. Seven of fourteen analysts rate it Hold, and four of the five most recent target raises came without a rating change — the sell side is marking to market, not converting. Five years of −26% price returns were not an accident of sentiment; they were a correct assessment of a company that grows slowly, acquires expensively and has now sold the business that gave it optionality. The last dividend increase was +0.96%.

Top 3 Risks

01
FY2027 guidance disappoints in November. The whole August rally is a bet on what management says about the standalone company. Anything framed as low-single-digit organic growth with flat margins removes the re-rating premise, and the stock has already given back 40% of that advance in anticipation.
02
Leverage constrains the response to a bad year. With roughly $16B of net debt, a sub-1.0 current ratio and $2.59B of TTM free cash flow against a $1.14B annual dividend obligation, BD has limited capacity to simultaneously defend the dividend, continue buying back stock, deleverage and acquire growth. Something gives, and the 54-year dividend streak is the least likely thing to be sacrificed.
03
Regulatory or recall event in Connected Care or Medical Essentials. Infusion pumps and medication-dispensing systems are among the most heavily regulated device categories in the US, and BD has been through a multi-year FDA remediation on Alaris before. A repeat in a segment producing a quarter of revenue would dwarf any of the operating variables discussed on this page.
Industry Commentary
Hospital consumables are a nominal-GDP business, and currency is currently hiding it
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The single most important number on this page is the gap between reported and foreign-currency-neutral growth. Q3 fiscal 2026 revenue grew +5.4% reported but +4.4% FXN; over nine months, Medical Essentials grew +3.3% reported but +1.5% FXN and BioPharma Systems +4.1% reported but +1.6% FXN. A weak dollar is contributing roughly a point to two points of headline growth across the company, and it is contributing most in the two segments with the weakest underlying rate. Hospital procedure volumes grow with population, ageing and utilisation — call it low single digits — and group purchasing organisations exist specifically to suppress price. This is the structural ceiling every bull case on BD has to argue around, and it is why the multiple is 13× rather than 25×. The honest framing: BD's organic growth rate is roughly 3–4%, and every basis point above that has to come from mix, M&A, or the currency.

The injectable-drug boom is a components business, and BD owns a seat
EMERGING TAILWIND

Every self-administered biologic, every GLP-1 pen and every autoinjector requires a qualified primary container and delivery system — and once a component is written into a drug's regulatory filing, changing it requires a submission. The market values that dynamic generously: West Pharmaceutical Services carries a $24.19B market cap on $3.33B of revenue growing 12.4%, at 44× earnings. BD's BioPharma Systems segment produced $1,689M over nine months and accelerated to +6.6% in Q3 — half of West's revenue base, in the same category, valued inside a 13× conglomerate multiple. The separation of Biosciences shows management is willing to break the company apart when the discount becomes indefensible. That precedent is now on the record, and BioPharma Systems is the obvious next candidate if its acceleration holds.

Bull versus bear: the November print settles it
ONGOING HEADWIND

The bear reads a company that lost 26% of its value over five years, grows at 4% ex-currency, earns under 8% on invested capital, carries $16B of net debt, and has just sold its most differentiated division to fund a buyback — a classic value trap where cheapness is the correct price of low growth and high leverage. The bull reads a stock that fell 47% peak to trough, bottomed in May 2026, rallied 28% on a single earnings beat, trades at 13.3× guided earnings with a 2.38% covered-on-adjusted-earnings dividend, and is about to publish its first clean full-year baseline as a simpler business. Both readings use the same facts. The discriminating variable is the FY2027 organic growth and margin guidance due in early November 2026: mid-single-digit organic with margin expansion validates the bull; low-single-digit and flat confirms the bear. Everything else on this page is positioning around that one disclosure.
