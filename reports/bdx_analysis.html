01 Company Overview

Becton, Dickinson and Company is a 129-year-old medical technology manufacturer whose products — syringes, needles, IV catheters, infusion pumps, medication-dispensing cabinets, prefillable drug-delivery systems and interventional devices — are used in hospitals and in drug makers’ production lines. Fiscal 2026 (year ended September 30, 2026) revenue was $19.46B, up 4.9% (3.9% excluding currency effects). Net income from continuing operations was $1.42B, or $5.06 per diluted share; after a $271M loss on the discontinued business, total diluted EPS was $4.09, and adjusted EPS was $12.70 (BD Q4 fiscal 2026 release, November 5, 2026).

The year is the first built on the smaller company. On February 9, 2026, BD completed the separation of its Biosciences & Diagnostic Solutions business and its combination with Waters Corporation. Those operations are reported as discontinued operations, and what remains — “New BD” — is a four-segment medical-device company with about $19.5B of revenue. The debate has not changed: is it a cleaner, faster-growing business, or a slow-growth supplies business carrying about $73.3B of enterprise value against $57.71B of equity value (our calculation: market cap plus roughly $15.6B of net debt)? Management’s first answer, the fiscal 2027 guidance, is steady rather than faster: low-single-digit revenue growth and adjusted EPS growth of 3% to 5%. The stock has risen 20% since the last edition without the business changing much: the market now pays 16.0 times expected fiscal 2027 earnings, against 13.3 times at the September 10 close.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
129-YEAR INSTALLED BASE BUYING-GROUP CONTRACT LOCK-IN FDA-QUALIFIED MANUFACTURING PRIMARY-CONTAINER QUALIFICATION CLINICAL WORKFLOW EMBEDDING SCALE IN COMMODITY CONSUMABLES 55 YEARS OF DIVIDEND INCREASES

BD’s core advantage is not technological — it is procedural. A hospital that standardises on Pyxis cabinets has built medication-safety policy, nurse training, pharmacy audit trails and electronic-health-record links around one vendor’s hardware; a pharmaceutical company that qualifies a BD prefillable syringe as the primary container for a biologic has written that component into a regulatory filing and cannot change it without a new submission. These are switching costs measured in years of validation work, not in purchase price. Add manufacturing scale in products where the profit on each unit is measured in cents, buying groups that prefer fewer vendors, and a record of dividend increases that BD describes as 55 consecutive fiscal years (November 5, 2026 announcement) — and you have a business that is hard to displace and equally hard to speed up. The beta of 0.28 is the market’s summary of exactly that.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The growth ceiling is built into the business; it is not a phase. Fiscal 2026 growth excluding currency by segment ran +2.6%, +3.6%, +3.3% and +5.9%, and +3.9% for the company. For fiscal 2027 BD guides to low-single-digit revenue growth. No amount of execution turns a hospital-supplies business into a double-digit grower; the levers are selling relatively more Interventional and BioPharma products, price, and acquisition. The fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guide of $13.10–$13.30 (+3.1% to +4.7%) is growth in profit per share roughly in line with revenue, not faster.
02
Debt weighs heavily on the case for the stock. Debt of $16.35B (long-term $13.42B plus current $2.92B) against $725M of cash implies roughly $15.6B of net debt, and the current ratio is 0.87. In fiscal 2026 BD repaid $2,696M of debt, repurchased $2,433M of stock and paid $1,161M of dividends — $6.3B against $2.93B of free cash flow. The cash flow statement shows a $3,916M net distribution from the spin-off, one-time money that covers the gap and does not repeat.
03
The dividend payout ratio on GAAP earnings is above 100%. At 103.7% ($4.24 annual dividend ÷ $4.09 total GAAP EPS), BD pays out slightly more than it earns on a GAAP basis. On adjusted EPS of $12.70 the payout is a comfortable 33%, and the separation and impairment charges explain the gap — but a 55-year record is the kind of asset a company defends past the point of financial logic, which constrains flexibility. The latest increase was about 1% ($1.05 to $1.06 a quarter).
04
Regulatory concentration in Connected Care. BD has already lived through a multi-year FDA remediation on the Alaris infusion platform. Infusion pumps are among the most heavily scrutinised device classes in the United States, and a single recall or consent-decree event in a segment producing a quarter of revenue would do damage out of proportion to its weight — as it did the last time.
05
Tariff relief is conditional and unquantified. The October 6 agreement gives BD relief from any future Section 232 tariffs on its products, but only if it meets milestones it has not disclosed, and BD has not put a number on the financial effect. No medical-device-specific Section 232 tariffs had been announced as of that report (MedTech Dive, October 6, 2026).

Notable One-Off Items

Fiscal 2026 is, for reporting purposes, a broken year, and many backward-looking ratios on this page are affected. (1) The Biosciences & Diagnostic Solutions separation and Waters combination completed February 9, 2026; the business is discontinued operations, which is why fiscal 2025’s reported $21.84B and the smaller continuing-operations base both circulate as “BD revenue.” (2) Fiscal 2026 carries a $271M loss from discontinued operations, taking total GAAP EPS to $4.09 against $5.06 from continuing operations. (3) The year includes $450M of non-cash asset impairments tied to BD’s “Excellence Unleashed” strategy. (4) The second quarter (ended March 31, 2026, the quarter containing the separation) printed a net loss of $311M, −$1.11 per diluted share, on just $93M of operating income, after $533M of integration, restructuring and transaction expense. (5) A $66M benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds lifted the fourth quarter and the year. (6) The 103.7% GAAP payout ratio is a consequence of the same depressed denominator. Use adjusted EPS ($12.70 for fiscal 2026; $13.10–$13.30 guided for fiscal 2027) and segment FXN growth as the operating read; use GAAP only if you are willing to unpick the charges.

RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own history ("overbought"); below 30 means the opposite ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a year, and an expensive one can stay overbought. Here it is calculated on monthly closes, so each point represents a month of trading rather than a day.

From the December 2021 close of $251.48 to $211.88 on November 6, 2026, BDX lost 15.7% of its price — about −3.5% a year — while the S&P 500 gained 54.8% and the Nasdaq-100 65.4% over the same window (Yahoo Finance index closes, December 31, 2021 to November 6, 2026, price only). The BDX figure is price only: it leaves out dividends, and it does not count the Waters shares that holders received in February 2026, which are not in the price line at all. Even so, this is a long stretch of lagging the market, and it is the starting point for any bull case.

The shape matters more than the endpoints. From December 2021 through March 2025 — more than three years — BDX traded in a tight band, with month-end closes between about $220 and $280, behaving as a hospital-supplies stock that barely follows the market (a beta of 0.28) should: it ignored the 2022 bear market and the 2023–24 rally alike. The damage is concentrated in one fourteen-month stretch. From the March 2025 close of $229.06 the stock fell to $147.12 by May 2026 — a −35.8% decline, including a −16.7% month in May 2025 that is the single worst month in the window. Measured from the August 2023 high close of $279.45, the peak-to-trough drawdown is −47.4%. Both falls use the actual closes on this chart, so they include the roughly 21% of value that left the share price when Waters shares were distributed in February 2026. Adjusted with Yahoo Finance’s 1.272 factor for that distribution, they are about −18% and −33%.

What has happened since May 2026 is the actual investment question. The stock bottomed at $147.12, climbed through the summer and closed August at $188.11 after the Q3 beat, slipped to $179.00 in September, then rose to $197.88 in October and $211.88 on November 6 — +44.0% off the low in about five months, in a stock with a beta of 0.28. The business did not change that much: fiscal 2027 guidance is for low-single-digit revenue growth. What changed is the price paid for it: 16.0 times expected fiscal 2027 earnings, up from 13.3 times at the September 10 close. The monthly RSI panel shows the momentum: it sat in oversold territory (below 30) from March to June 2026 and was 71.0 at November 6, just over the 70 line.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A simplified four-segment BD at 16.0× expected fiscal 2027 earnings with a 2.00% yield, 55 straight years of dividend increases and a 0.28 beta is still priced like a slow business, but every segment grew in fiscal 2026, Interventional (+6.8%, +7.6% in Q4) and BioPharma Systems (+6.5% in Q4) are growing fastest, and free cash flow rose 42.5% to $2.93B. Management hit its August guidance on adjusted EPS ($12.70), guided $13.10–$13.30 for fiscal 2027, and has secured conditional relief from future Section 232 tariffs in return for a $19B U.S. investment plan. If the December 10 Investor Day frames mid-single-digit organic growth with margin gains, the $224 average target becomes a floor rather than a ceiling.

Bear Case

Strip out currency and BD grew 3.9% in fiscal 2026, with its largest segment at +2.6% and Connected Care slowing to +1.6% in the fourth quarter. Management’s own fiscal 2027 outlook is low-single-digit revenue growth, and its adjusted EPS guide is +3% to +5%. That is a business growing roughly with the economy, carrying about $15.6B of net debt, a 0.87 current ratio, a return on invested capital near 6% and a GAAP payout ratio above 100%. The stock is up 20% since September 10 and the price has gone from 13.3 to 16.0 times expected earnings on unchanged growth; the average target is only 5.7% above the close; six of 16 analysts say Hold and Barclays says Sell at $165. The last dividend increase was about 1%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Investors are already paying more; the growth has not moved. The rise from 13.3 to 16.0 times expected earnings assumes steady execution. Guidance of low-single-digit revenue growth with adjusted EPS up 3% to 5% leaves little room for a miss, and the December 10 Investor Day has to give investors a reason to pay more.
02
Leverage constrains the response to a bad year. With roughly $15.6B of net debt, a sub-1.0 current ratio and $2.93B of free cash flow against a $1.16B annual dividend bill, BD has limited capacity to defend the dividend, keep buying back stock, pay down debt and fund a $19B U.S. investment at the same time. Something gives, and the 55-year dividend streak is the least likely thing to be sacrificed.
03
Regulatory or recall event in Connected Care or Medical Essentials. Infusion pumps and medication-dispensing systems are among the most heavily regulated device categories in the US, and BD has been through a multi-year FDA remediation on Alaris before. A repeat in a segment producing a quarter of revenue would outweigh any of the operating variables discussed on this page.
Industry Commentary
Hospital supplies grow with the economy, and guidance says so
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The most important numbers on this page are the gap between reported and foreign-currency-neutral growth and the fiscal 2027 outlook. Fiscal 2026 revenue grew +4.9% reported but +3.9% excluding currency; Medical Essentials grew +4.0% reported but +2.6% FXN and BioPharma Systems +4.8% reported but +3.3% FXN. For fiscal 2027 BD guides to low-single-digit revenue growth with a currency drag of about 0.7 points. Hospital procedure volumes grow with population, ageing and utilisation — low single digits — and buying groups exist specifically to hold prices down. That is the ceiling every bull case on BD has to argue around, and it is why the stock trades at 16 times expected earnings rather than 25. The honest framing: BD’s underlying growth rate is roughly 3–4%, and anything above that has to come from a richer product blend, acquisitions or currency.

The injectable-drug boom is a components business, and BD owns a seat
EMERGING TAILWIND

Every self-administered biologic, every GLP-1 pen and every autoinjector requires an approved primary container and delivery system, and once a component is written into a drug’s regulatory filing, changing it requires a new submission. The market values that dynamic generously: West Pharmaceutical Services carries a $27.85B market cap on $3.40B of revenue growing 12.6%, about 8.2 times sales (our calculation). BD’s BioPharma Systems segment produced $2,437M in fiscal 2026 and grew +6.5% in the fourth quarter — about 72% of West’s revenue base, in the same category, valued inside a company that trades at 3.0 times sales overall. The separation of Biosciences shows management is willing to break the company apart. If the pick-up in BioPharma Systems holds, it is a natural candidate for the same question.

Guidance is out, and the stock has already paid for it
WHAT TO WATCH

The September edition said the November guidance would settle the argument. It did not settle it; it narrowed it. Guidance of low-single-digit revenue growth and adjusted EPS of $13.10–$13.30 landed about where analysts already were: the $13.20 midpoint was 0.8% below the consensus cited by StockStory on the day, and analysts now sit at $13.21. Neither the bull nor the bear got much new information on growth. What moved is the price: 20% higher since September 10, taking the stock from 13.3 to 16.0 times expected earnings. The bear reads a company whose share price fell 47% peak to trough before adjusting for the Waters distribution, grows about 4% excluding currency, earns about 6% on invested capital and carries $15.6B of net debt, whose stock has now run ahead of its growth. The bull reads a stock that bottomed in May, has a conditional shield against future tariffs and a path to mid-single-digit growth in Interventional and BioPharma. The next information is the December 10 Investor Day.
