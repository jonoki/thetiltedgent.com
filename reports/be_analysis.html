01 Company Overview

Bloom Energy builds solid oxide fuel cells — refrigerator-sized "Energy Servers" that sit on a customer's site and turn natural gas, biogas or hydrogen into electricity without burning it. For most of its public life that was a hard sell: a clean-ish power box competing with a grid that was cheaper and already there. Revenue grew at 10–23% a year from FY2021 to FY2024 and the company lost money every one of those years — GAAP net losses of $164M, $301M, $302M and $29M.

Then the grid stopped being "already there". AI data centers need hundreds of megawatts now, and a utility interconnection takes years. Bloom's pitch became time to power: installation in three to four months, against four to seven years for a grid connection (The Motley Fool), with an Oracle project delivered in 55 days. The orders followed — up to 1 GW with AEP (Nov 2024), a $5B Brookfield partnership (Oct 2025) expanded to a $25B framework (Jun 30, 2026), and a master services agreement for up to 2.8 GW with Oracle (mid-April 2026). Q2 2026 revenue was $1,065.4M, the first billion-dollar quarter in the company's history; Q3 2026 (released October 22) beat it at $1,235.8M, up 138.1%, and management raised its full-year revenue guide to $4.4–$4.6B. Trailing twelve-month GAAP EPS is now $1.63. The stock went from a $9.60 month-end close in October 2024 to a $302.70 month-end peak in June 2026, entered the S&P 500 on September 21, 2026 (replacing Molson Coors), and closed at $203.92 on October 26 after falling 11.24% the day after the Q3 report.

The central debate is not whether the demand is real — the income statement says it is. It is what a 125x trailing multiple is paying for: a durable new power-generation category, or a bridge technology whose window closes once the grid and turbines catch up. The October sell-off answered part of that: the earnings rose faster than expected, and investors chose to pay less for each dollar of them. Underneath the growth sit three facts investors should hold in the same hand: two customers were about 43% and 23% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, the second a related party (a company with ownership or other ties to Bloom, per the 10-Q); operating cash flow turned negative in Q3 (−$27.1M) while capital spending rose; and the company faces a securities class action over its disclosure of Chinese scandium in its supply chain.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Time to power (months, not years) Proven SOFC at scale US manufacturing base Brookfield $25B financing framework Oracle / AEP reference deployments Installed-base service annuity

Bloom's moat is not that fuel cells are the cheapest electrons — historically they have not been. It is that they are the fastest electrons you can get at scale. When a data center's alternative is waiting years for a grid connection, the value of power is set by the revenue of the idle GPUs, not by the utility tariff. Bloom has spent two decades manufacturing the one on-site technology that can be dropped in modularly, and the Brookfield framework solves the customer's other problem — who pays for it up front. The vulnerability is the mirror image: a moat built on scarcity lasts only as long as the scarcity.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Two customers are most of the company. About 43% and 23% of revenue in the first nine months of 2026 came from two customers, the second a related party (Q3 10-Q). In the second quarter alone one customer, not the related party, was about 73% of revenue (Q2 10-Q/A). A change in either relationship would move group revenue more than anything else in this report.
02
Scandium and the class action. On July 8, 2026 Hunterbrook Media published a report alleging Bloom relied on Chinese scandium, some of it routed through intermediaries in Thailand, Japan and South Korea; the stock fell 5.7% that day. A securities class action (Nevins v. Bloom Energy Corporation, N.D. Cal., No. 26-cv-07944) covers purchases from February 27, 2025 to July 8, 2026. Bloom rejected the report's conclusions and said in an 8-K on July 9 that its scandium supply "is not dependent on China". These are allegations, not findings.
03
The window can close. Bloom sells speed. If utilities, gas turbines and grid build-out catch up with data-center demand, the premium for on-site power in months shrinks — and so does the case for paying fuel-cell prices.
04
Dilution from convertibles. Shares outstanding rose 33.17% YoY (StockAnalysis), and Q3 diluted weighted shares were 324.3M against 234.9M a year earlier (release). The 0% notes due November 2030 (carrying value $2,450.9M at September 30, Q3 10-Q) convert at 5.1290 shares per $1,000 of principal, about $194.97 a share (Bloom's pricing release, October 30, 2025). $2.2B of notes were priced, with an option for $300M more, so full conversion means roughly 11.3M–12.8M new shares, about 4% of the October 19 count of 295.66M. Conversions of the 3.0% Green Notes added 7,796,169 shares in the first nine months of 2026. Long-run per-share series understate how much the company has grown in absolute terms and overstate how much of it belongs to each share.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 26, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a stock in a genuine re-rating can stay overbought for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $31.26 to the October 26, 2026 settled close of $203.92, BE rose 552% on price (no dividends). But none of that happened in the first three years. The stock fell from $31.26 to a $8.77 month-end low in February 2024 — a 72% loss over more than two years of cash burn — and was still at $9.60 in October 2024, before the AI-power story arrived. Everything since is a different company in the same ticker: +186% in November 2024 on the AEP deal, a near-vertical run from $18.32 in April 2025 to $132.16 in October 2025, then +109% in April 2026 on Oracle, to a $302.70 month-end peak in June 2026.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Power is the binding constraint on AI, and Bloom sells the one thing a data center cannot buy from a utility: power in months. The evidence is in the numbers, not the narrative — Q3 revenue +138.1% to a record $1,235.8M, gross margin up 453 bps to 33.8%, GAAP operating income of $225.4M against $7.8M a year earlier, TTM free cash flow of $436.7M from a company that burned $456M in 2023, a ~$20B backlog at end-2025, and financing partners (Brookfield's $25B framework, the Nebius structure) that take the capital burden off customers. Management raised the full-year guide again, to $4.4–$4.6B. Consensus has adjusted EPS going from $3.26 in 2026 to $5.31 in 2027; if the growth holds, a 63x forward multiple falls to 38x in a year — and the price has already come down 33% from the June month-end while the estimates went up.

Bear Case

At 125x trailing GAAP earnings, 16x sales and 100x EV/EBITDA, the price still assumes the boom lasts — for a business where two customers were about 43% and 23% of nine-month revenue. Q3 operating cash flow was −$27.1M even as reported profit hit a record, and capital spending rose to $153.5M. The moat is scarcity of grid power, and scarcity is what markets are built to fix. A securities class action alleges misleading disclosure of Chinese scandium in the supply chain. Share count rose 33.17% in a year, and roughly 11–13M more shares sit behind the 2030 convertibles at their initial conversion price of about $195. The stock has already shown what a bad month looks like — −32% in July 2026, −26.4% so far in October — and with a 3.78 beta it will show it again. Most post-print target changes were cuts, and the low target is $102.

Top 3 Risks

01
Customer concentration. About 43% and 23% of nine-month 2026 revenue from two customers (the second a related party), and 70% of receivables in three at September 30. Any delay, renegotiation or financing hiccup at those counterparties flows straight into product revenue.
02
Valuation leaves little room for a stumble. At 63x FY2026 consensus EPS, a miss against the $4.4–$4.6B guide — or even a lumpy quarter from product shipments — reprices the stock by far more than the earnings change. October 2026 showed the other version: a beat and a raise, and still −11.24% the next day.
03
Supply chain and litigation. The scandium allegations touch both a critical input for solid oxide cells and the credibility of management disclosure. The class action is at an early stage (the lead-plaintiff deadline was September 28, 2026); outcomes and any supply-chain changes are unknown.
Industry Commentary
Time to power: the grid gap for AI data centers
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

AEP said in November 2024 that it expected 20 GW of new load across its footprint by the end of the decade. Grid connections are quoted at 4–7 years; Bloom installs in 3–4 months. That gap is the entire market. Sikander Rashid, Brookfield's global head of AI infrastructure, framed it as "behind-the-meter power solutions are essential to closing the grid gap for AI factories." As long as the gap exists, the buyer's comparison is not fuel cell versus grid tariff — it is fuel cell versus idle GPUs.

Critical minerals and supply-chain scrutiny
ONGOING HEADWIND

Solid oxide fuel cells use scandium, and the July 2026 Hunterbrook report put Bloom's sourcing under a spotlight at the same time that US policy is focused on Chinese critical-mineral dependence. Bloom's position, in an 8-K filed July 9, 2026, is that its scandium supply is not dependent on China and that it can see enough supply to build 25 GW of fuel cells a year. The market will want that shown, not stated.

The price of being the answer
VALUATION RISK

In twelve months Bloom went from a perennial money-loser to an S&P 500 member priced at 125x trailing earnings — down from 298x at our September 18 edition, because earnings rose and the price fell. The bull framing: trailing multiples are the wrong lens for a company more than doubling revenue with rising margins; on 2027 consensus the forward multiple is 38x. The bear framing: every successful bridge technology gets priced as if the bridge were permanent, and the three variables that decide this — the concentrated customer, the scandium question and the pace of grid build-out — are all outside the income statement. The adjudication is the order book: backlog and new-customer diversification, reported quarter by quarter.
