01 Company Overview

Bunge is the plumbing of the world's food system. It buys soybeans, canola, sunflower seed, wheat and corn from farmers, stores them, ships them, and crushes the oilseeds into two products that go to two very different customers: meal for animal feed and oil for kitchens, food manufacturers and — increasingly — renewable diesel refineries. It is a 208-year-old company (founded 1818) that now lists a Swiss holding company on the NYSE: Bunge Limited of Bermuda became Bunge Global SA, domiciled in Geneva, on November 1, 2023, keeping the ticker BG, while the corporate headquarters sits in St. Louis. It joined the S&P 500 on March 15, 2023, taking the seat vacated by the failed Signature Bank.

Then, on July 2, 2025, Bunge closed its combination with Viterra — the grain-handling business that owned elevators, ports and origination networks from Canada to Argentina to Australia. DTN reported the deal at about $18 billion, including roughly $9.8B of assumed Viterra debt, paid with approximately 65.6 million new Bunge shares (about $5.3 billion at closing) plus about $2.0 billion in cash (Bunge 8-K). That one transaction rewrote every number on this page. Quarterly revenue went from $12.77B (Q2 2025) to $24.04B (Q2 2026); trailing revenue is now $93.47B, and the third quarter of 2026 ($23.80B) was the first like-for-like comparison with a Viterra quarter ($22.16B, up 7.4%). The diluted share count went from 136M to 195M — even after Bunge finished a $2 billion buyback tied to the deal in Q2 2026. And the thing that did not scale is the margin: Bunge earns cents on each dollar of grain it moves. TTM net margin is 1.24%, gross margin 5.12%. This is a business where a one-point change in crush spreads matters more than a ten-point change in revenue.

The stock has told that story in two halves. Its month-end closes stayed between $74 and $85 through the first nine months of 2025, then it went from $89.08 at end-2025 to $127.20 at end-March 2026 — up 43% in a quarter. It gave a chunk back in June (to $106.73), recovered to $116.21 in August, and closed October 30, 2026 at $96.64, 28% below the June high. Management's full-year adjusted EPS guide is $9.25–$9.75, raised on July 29 and kept on October 29. At that midpoint the stock trades near 10.2x adjusted earnings — but near 16x trailing GAAP earnings of $5.94. That gap between the adjusted and the reported number is where this report spends its time.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Origination footprint at both ends Port & storage assets Crush capacity in 3 bean belts Risk-management book Scale among a few global houses 208 years of counterparties

Bunge's advantage is physical. Crushing plants, port terminals and country elevators take years to permit and build, and their value comes from where they sit: next to the farmer at origin, or next to the feed mill and refinery at destination. DTN counted 55 port terminals and more than 350 grain storage facilities in the combined network. The Q2 2026 release credits Viterra with an "expanded" origination footprint in soybeans, softseeds and grain. The other half of the moat is information. A company that sees grain flows from Canada, Argentina, Brazil, the US, Europe and Australia at the same time trades better than one that sees only one. Heckman's Q2 line — the platform "did exactly what it was designed to do" amid "shifting trade flows" — is that argument in plain words. What the moat does not do is set the price. Bunge takes the crush margin the market gives it. In the best years that is a great business, and in the worst years a merely adequate one.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Margins it does not control. Soybean and softseed crushing earned 90% of Q3 2026 adjusted segment EBIT. Both depend on the spread between seed and its products, which is set by crop sizes, livestock demand and biofuel policy. FY2023 diluted EPS was $14.87; FY2025 was $4.91. The plants were largely the same.
02
Reported earnings are noisy by design. Bunge hedges physical positions with derivatives and marks both to market. This mark-to-market timing added $1.67 per share to GAAP EPS in Q2 2026 ($3.47 GAAP vs $2.00 adjusted) and took $0.28 off it in Q3 ($1.64 GAAP vs $2.56 adjusted, with $0.64 of one-off items as well). The effect is supposed to reverse over time, but in a single quarter the gap can be most of the adjusted number: Q1 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.35 against $1.83 adjusted.
03
Integration and dilution. The share count is 28.4% above a year ago (192.14M outstanding, StockAnalysis), although weighted diluted shares in Q3 2026 were 194M against 198M a year earlier. For per-share value to rise, Viterra's earnings have to more than make up for the ~65.6M new shares and the extra corporate cost ($151M adjusted Corporate and Other loss in Q3, against $167M a year earlier).
04
Working capital eats cash. Operating cash flow was −$2,302M in the first nine months of 2026 (−$503M a year earlier), and inventories were $16.6B at September 30 against $13.2B at year-end. TTM free cash flow is −$2.66B (StockAnalysis). Bunge's inventories are mostly RMI ($14.7B of $16.6B at September 30), which can be turned into cash. But total debt rose to $16.7B from $14.1B at year-end, and the business still needs its debt markets open to operate.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported figures. First, mark-to-market timing: it took $0.28 per share off Q3 2026 GAAP EPS (against $0.87 in Q3 2025) and added $1.67 in Q2 2026. Second, one-off gains and charges: $0.64 per share in Q3 2026, including an $82M loss on the sale of two Brazilian sugarcane mills ($66M impairment and $16M of hedge losses), $10M of Ukraine-Russia war charges in Softseed and a $13M tax valuation allowance. Third, Viterra acquisition and integration costs: $21M net in Q3 2026 and $88M net in the nine months, excluded from adjusted EPS. Together these are why Q3 GAAP EPS from continuing operations was $1.64 against $2.56 adjusted. On September 1, 2026 Bunge agreed to sell the two ex-Viterra mills (Rio Vermelho and Nova Unialco) to COFCO International; terms were not disclosed and the deal needs regulatory approval. Assets held for sale were $753M at September 30, up from $191M at year-end. In the prior year, Q2 2025 included a $155M pre-tax gain ($118M after tax) on the sale of the North American corn milling business.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $92.64 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $96.64, BG gained 4.3% in price (closing prices, dividends not included). For most of the five years the stock went nowhere. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Viterra made Bunge the largest origination-plus-processing platform outside the private giants just as crush margins recovered. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS rose 12.8% to $2.56 on a diluted share count 2% smaller than a year earlier. Soybean adjusted EBIT rose to $565M from $478M and softseed to $329M from $275M. Management kept the guide at $9.25–$9.75, and the consensus of $9.53 puts the stock at 10.1x forward, with a 2.98% dividend. The $2B deal buyback is done, a director bought in size at $105.53, and every one of the nine analysts StockAnalysis tracks has a target above the October 30 close. If the adjusted run-rate is real, the 16x trailing P/E is simply out of date.

Bear Case

Adjusted earnings exclude mark-to-market losses and integration costs, but the cash does not. TTM free cash flow is −$2.66B, nine-month operating cash flow was −$2.30B, total debt is $16.7B, and net interest will cost $640–670M this year. The GAAP numbers show 1.24% net margins, 6.92% ROE and 5.48% ROIC. FY2023 EPS of $14.87 became $4.91 by FY2025 on largely the same plants, which is what a cyclical peak looks like. Grain Merchandising, the segment Viterra enlarged most, earns about 1.3% on sales and its adjusted EBIT fell 27.5% in Q3; Tropical Oils' fell 68.6%. If the crush margin turns, the larger share count leaves less earnings per share.

Top 3 Risks

01
Crush-margin cyclicality. Two crushing segments earned 90% of Q3 2026 adjusted segment EBIT. Their spread depends on crop sizes, livestock feed demand and biofuel policy in the US, Brazil, Argentina and the EU. Bunge's plants and its hedging can soften a downturn but not prevent one.
02
Integration on a 28.4% larger share count. Viterra's per-share value depends on grain-segment margins above ~1% and on corporate cost ($151M adjusted in Q3 vs $167M a year earlier) not growing again.
03
Trade policy and geopolitics. The Q2 release lists the war in Ukraine and sanctions on Russia, tariff and trade policy, and biofuel regulation among its main risks. Heckman's own quote cites "shifting trade flows". A merchant can profit when trade flows change. It loses when trade is simply blocked.
Industry Commentary
Oil demand has become policy-driven
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

A soybean is mostly meal by weight, but oil is increasingly what sets the crush margin. Renewable diesel and biodiesel mandates have made vegetable oil an energy product, so crush economics now depend partly on policy decisions about fuel. Bunge's Q2 2026 release names "biofuels regulation" among its key risk factors and credits "improving market conditions" and "a more favorable market environment" for the soybean and softseed gains. The mechanism is simple: more mandated oil demand means more beans crushed and a wider spread, and the processor with plants in every major bean belt benefits most.

Consolidation among the big four
COMPETITIVE SHIFT

Global grain trading is concentrated in a handful of houses, among them ADM, Bunge, Cargill, Louis Dreyfus and COFCO International. Viterra was another large one, and folding it into Bunge reduced the number of large players by one. The September 2026 mill sale shows the group trading assets among themselves: COFCO buying Viterra's old Brazilian sugar mills from Bunge. Fewer, larger players can mean steadier margins through the cycle. It also means more regulatory attention.

Two P/E ratios, one company
READ THE FOOTNOTES

BG trades at 16.27x trailing GAAP EPS and about 10.1x forward adjusted EPS. Both numbers are correct. The difference comes from three things: the mark-to-market swings Bunge adjusts out (from −$1.28 per share in Q1 2026 to +$1.67 in Q2 2026), integration costs ($88M after tax in the first nine months of 2026), and two weak quarters (Q4 2025 and Q1 2026) still in the trailing window. For merchant-processors the market tends to look through GAAP noise, because the hedges genuinely do reverse. But the adjusted figure also excludes a real cost of doing the deal. The Q3 print narrowed the gap but did not close it ($1.64 GAAP against $2.56 adjusted). The test is Q4 2026: if GAAP and adjusted EPS converge, the forward multiple was right and the stock is cheap. If they keep diverging, the trailing multiple is the better measure.
