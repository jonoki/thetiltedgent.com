01 Company Overview

Bunge is the plumbing of the world's food system. It buys soybeans, canola, sunflower seed, wheat and corn from farmers, stores them, ships them, and crushes the oilseeds into two products that go to two very different customers: meal for animal feed and oil for kitchens, food manufacturers and — increasingly — renewable diesel refineries. It is a 208-year-old company (founded 1818) that now lists a Swiss holding company on the NYSE: Bunge Limited of Bermuda became Bunge Global SA, domiciled in Geneva, on November 1, 2023, keeping the ticker BG, while the corporate headquarters sits in St. Louis. It joined the S&P 500 on March 15, 2023, taking the seat vacated by the failed Signature Bank.

Then, on July 2, 2025, Bunge closed its combination with Viterra — the grain-handling business that owned elevators, ports and origination networks from Canada to Argentina to Australia. DTN reported the deal at about $18 billion, including roughly $9.8B of assumed Viterra debt, paid with approximately 65.6 million new Bunge shares (about $5.3 billion at closing) plus about $2.0 billion in cash (Bunge 8-K). That one transaction rewrote every number on this page. Quarterly revenue went from $12.77B (Q2 2025) to $24.04B (Q2 2026); trailing revenue is now $91.82B, up 80.5%. The diluted share count went from 136M to 195M — even after Bunge finished a $2 billion buyback tied to the deal in Q2 2026. And the thing that did not scale is the margin: Bunge earns cents on each dollar of grain it moves. TTM net margin is 1.10%, gross margin 4.92%. This is a business where a one-point change in crush spreads matters more than a ten-point change in revenue.

The stock has told that story in two halves. Its month-end closes stayed between $74 and $85 through the first nine months of 2025, then it went from $89.08 at end-2025 to $127.20 at end-March 2026 — up 43% in a quarter. It gave a chunk back in June (to $106.73) and now sits at $112.61. Management's full-year adjusted EPS guide is $9.25–$9.75, raised on July 29. At that midpoint the stock trades near 11.9x adjusted earnings — but near 22x trailing GAAP earnings of $5.13. That gap between the adjusted and the reported number is where this report spends its time.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Origination footprint at both ends Port & storage assets Crush capacity in 3 bean belts Risk-management book Scale among a few global houses 208 years of counterparties

Bunge's advantage is physical. Crushing plants, port terminals and country elevators take years to permit and build, and their value comes from where they sit: next to the farmer at origin, or next to the feed mill and refinery at destination. DTN counted 55 port terminals and more than 350 grain storage facilities in the combined network. The Q2 2026 release credits Viterra with an "expanded" origination footprint in soybeans, softseeds and grain. The other half of the moat is information. A company that sees grain flows from Canada, Argentina, Brazil, the US, Europe and Australia at the same time trades better than one that sees only one. Heckman's Q2 line — the platform "did exactly what it was designed to do" amid "shifting trade flows" — is that argument in plain words. What the moat does not do is set the price. Bunge takes the crush margin the market gives it. In the best years that is a great business, and in the worst years a merely adequate one.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Margins it does not control. Soybean and softseed crushing earned 88% of Q2 2026 adjusted segment EBIT. Both depend on the spread between seed and its products, which is set by crop sizes, livestock demand and biofuel policy. FY2023 diluted EPS was $14.87; FY2025 was $4.91. The plants were largely the same.
02
Reported earnings are noisy by design. Bunge hedges physical positions with derivatives and marks both to market. In Q2 2026 this mark-to-market timing added $1.67 per share to GAAP EPS ($3.47 GAAP vs $2.00 adjusted). The effect is supposed to reverse over time, but in any single quarter GAAP and adjusted EPS can differ by more than the whole adjusted number.
03
Integration and dilution. The share count is up 42.6% YoY. For per-share value to rise, Viterra's earnings have to more than make up for the ~65.6M new shares and the extra corporate cost ($148M adjusted Corporate EBIT loss in Q2 vs $81M). Management's quantified synergy figure was not found in the sources fetched this session.
04
Working capital eats cash. Operating cash flow was −$1,126M in H1 2026, and inventories rose by $2,235M. TTM free cash flow is −$711M (StockAnalysis). Bunge's inventories are mostly RMI ($13.3B of $15.5B at June 30), which can be turned into cash. But the business still needs its debt markets open to operate.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported figures. First, mark-to-market timing: +$1.67/share in Q2 2026 (−$0.69 in Q2 2025). This is why Q2 GAAP EPS was $3.47 against $2.00 adjusted. Second, Viterra acquisition and integration costs: $39M after tax in Q2 2026 and $80M in H1 2026, excluded from adjusted EPS. Third, disposals: Q2 2025 included a $155M pre-tax gain ($118M after tax) on the sale of the North American corn milling business. On September 1, 2026 Bunge agreed to sell two ex-Viterra Brazilian sugarcane mills (Rio Vermelho and Nova Unialco) to COFCO International. Terms were not disclosed, and the deal needs regulatory approval. Any gain or loss is likely to go through "certain gains & charges".

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Viterra made Bunge the largest origination-plus-processing platform outside the private giants just as crush margins recovered. Q2 2026 adjusted EPS rose 53% to $2.00 on a share count 43% larger. Softseed adjusted EBIT went from $14M to $255M. Management raised the guide to $9.25–$9.75, and the consensus of $9.64 puts the stock at 11.7x forward, with a 2.56% dividend. The $2B deal buyback is done, a director bought in size at $105.53, and every one of the nine analysts StockAnalysis tracks has a target above today's price. If the adjusted run-rate is real, the 22x trailing P/E is simply out of date.

Bear Case

Adjusted earnings exclude mark-to-market losses and integration costs, but the cash does not. TTM free cash flow is −$711M, H1 operating cash flow was −$1.13B, debt is $15.2B, and net interest will cost $620–660M this year. The GAAP numbers show 1.10% net margins, 7.18% ROE and 5.09% ROIC. FY2023 EPS of $14.87 became $4.91 by FY2025 on largely the same plants, which is what a cyclical peak looks like. Grain Merchandising, the segment Viterra enlarged most, earns about 1% on sales and just had its outlook cut. If the crush margin turns, the larger share count leaves less earnings per share.

Top 3 Risks

01
Crush-margin cyclicality. Two crushing segments earned 88% of Q2 2026 adjusted segment EBIT. Their spread depends on crop sizes, livestock feed demand and biofuel policy in the US, Brazil, Argentina and the EU. Bunge's plants and its hedging can soften a downturn but not prevent one.
02
Integration on a 42.6% larger share base. Viterra's per-share value depends on grain-segment margins above ~1% and on corporate cost ($148M adjusted in Q2 vs $81M) not growing further. A quantified synergy target was not found in the sources fetched this session, so this report cannot track progress against one.
03
Trade policy and geopolitics. The Q2 release lists the war in Ukraine and sanctions on Russia, tariff and trade policy, and biofuel regulation among its main risks. Heckman's own quote cites "shifting trade flows". A merchant can profit when trade flows change. It loses when trade is simply blocked.
Industry Commentary
Oil demand has become policy-driven
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

A soybean is mostly meal by weight, but oil is increasingly what sets the crush margin. Renewable diesel and biodiesel mandates have made vegetable oil an energy product, so crush economics now depend partly on policy decisions about fuel. Bunge's Q2 2026 release names "biofuels regulation" among its key risk factors and credits "improving market conditions" and "a more favorable market environment" for the soybean and softseed gains. This report did not source specific 2026 mandate volumes, so no figure is given. The mechanism, though, is simple: more mandated oil demand means more beans crushed and a wider spread, and the processor with plants in every major bean belt benefits most.

Consolidation among the big four
COMPETITIVE SHIFT

Global grain trading is concentrated in a handful of houses, among them ADM, Bunge, Cargill, Louis Dreyfus and COFCO International. Viterra was another large one, and folding it into Bunge reduced the number of large players by one. The September 2026 mill sale shows the group trading assets among themselves: COFCO buying Viterra's old Brazilian sugar mills from Bunge. Fewer, larger players can mean steadier margins through the cycle. It also means more regulatory attention. Details of the conditions attached to the Viterra approval were not in the sources fetched this session.

Two P/E ratios, one company
READ THE FOOTNOTES

BG trades at 21.95x trailing GAAP EPS and about 11.7x forward adjusted EPS. Both numbers are correct. The difference comes from three things: the mark-to-market swings Bunge adjusts out (−$0.69 to +$1.67 per share in a single quarter), integration costs ($80M after tax in H1 2026 alone), and two weak post-merger quarters in the trailing window. For merchant-processors the market tends to look through GAAP noise, because the hedges genuinely do reverse. But the adjusted figure also excludes a real cost of doing the deal. The test is Q3 and Q4 2026: if GAAP and adjusted EPS converge, the forward multiple was right and the stock is cheap. If they keep diverging, the trailing multiple is the better measure.
