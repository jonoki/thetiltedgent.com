01 Company Overview

Biogen is a Cambridge, Massachusetts neuroscience company that spent two decades as the franchise in multiple sclerosis and is now trying to become something else before that franchise runs out. It sells MS drugs (Tysabri, Vumerity, Avonex, Tecfidera, Plegridy), rare-disease drugs (Spinraza for spinal muscular atrophy, Skyclarys for Friedreich's ataxia, Qalsody for a genetic form of ALS), co-commercialises the Alzheimer's antibody Leqembi with Eisai, sells Zurzuvae for postpartum depression, and — since May 14, 2026 — owns Apellis and its eye and kidney drugs Syfovre and Empaveli. FY2025 revenue was $9.89B, up 2.2%, after four straight years of decline from $10.98B in FY2021.

The story is a relay race. The old runner — MS — is slowing: Q2 2026 MS revenue fell 13% to $963M as generic Tecfidera (−53%) and European Tysabri competition bite. The new runners — what management calls the Growth Portfolio — delivered $1.06B, up 24%, and for the second straight quarter out-earned the legacy MS business. The question for the stock is whether the handoff is clean. On price, the market has voted yes for eighteen months: from an April 2025 month-end low of $121.08 the stock has risen 78% to $215.50. On earnings, the picture is murkier — trailing GAAP EPS is just $5.64, because Biogen is paying for its future up front: acquired R&D charges, Apellis inventory step-ups and restructuring pushed Q2 2026 GAAP EPS down to $0.66 against a non-GAAP $3.60. That gap between what the business earns and what the accounts show is the central thing to understand here.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Neurology commercial footprint Rare-disease pricing power Antisense platform (Ionis partnership) Ocrevus royalty stream Biologics manufacturing scale 78% gross margin

Pharma moats are patents with an expiry date, and Biogen has lived through what happens when one lapses: Tecfidera revenue halving in a single year. The durable part of the moat is not any one molecule but the neurology machine — the relationships with neurologists, the infusion-based neurology channel that both Tysabri and Leqembi rely on, and the antisense know-how behind Spinraza, Qalsody and the pipeline candidates licensed from Ionis. The vulnerability is that a machine needs products to run, and every product in the portfolio has a clock on it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The MS cliff keeps eroding. MS fell 13% in Q2 2026 and is still 35% of revenue. Generic Tecfidera and European Tysabri competition are named by the company as drivers. If the decline accelerates — a US Tysabri biosimilar would be the obvious trigger — the Growth Portfolio has to run faster just to keep revenue flat.
02
Leqembi is a shared, slow launch. $184M a quarter in global in-market sales, of which Biogen books half, three years after the Clarity AD readout. It competes against Lilly's anti-amyloid antibody, requires infusion or injection plus brain-scan monitoring, and its economics are split with Eisai. The subcutaneous Iqlik form is the next lever.
03
Buying the future costs today's earnings. ~$3.00 of 2026 EPS goes to acquired IPR&D and milestones and ~$0.85 to Apellis dilution. Net debt is now $6.8B. Business development that works looks brilliant in 2029; business development that does not leaves a levered company with a shrinking core.
04
Index removal removed a buyer. Biogen was dropped from the NASDAQ-100 effective December 22, 2025. It remains an S&P 500 member, but a large price-insensitive bid left the register.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP earnings in 2026 are a poor guide to the business. In Q2 2026 alone, $164M of acquired IPR&D, $200.6M of Apellis inventory fair-value step-up, $153.6M of intangible amortisation and $170.5M of restructuring took net income from what would have been a normal quarter to $97.5M. Non-GAAP adjustments here are doing heavy lifting, so the honest way to read Biogen is on free cash flow ($2.70B TTM) and on the underlying-business guide, with the caveat that "acquired IPR&D" is a recurring feature of a company that buys its pipeline.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $282.99 to the September 18, 2026 settled close of $215.50, BIIB lost 23.8% of its price, with no dividends to soften it. But the five-year number hides two completely different stocks. The first fell from a $305.17 high (November 2022) to a $121.08 low (April 2025) — a 60% decline over two and a half years as Aduhelm was abandoned, Leqembi launched slowly and MS revenue eroded. The second has risen 78% from that low in seventeen months as the Growth Portfolio started to show up in the numbers.

The 6-period monthly RSI captures both. It sat below 30 every month from August 2024 to July 2025, bottoming at 7.8 in April 2025 — about as washed-out as a monthly reading gets. It then swung to 81.4 in June 2026 and reads 70.8 now, right on the overbought line. The price is well above its 10-month moving average of about $196.75. Momentum is strong and stretched; the July 2026 dip after a clean earnings beat is the first sign that good news is already in the price.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The handoff is working. The Growth Portfolio grew 24% to $1.06B and now out-earns legacy MS; Skyclarys (+29%), Zurzuvae (+52%) and Qalsody (+60%) are compounding; Apellis adds two commercial products with $250M+ of synergies targeted. Strip out the one-off acquisition charges and the stock trades at about 13x management's underlying 2026 EPS guide and an 8.5% free-cash-flow yield. Five registrational readouts in the next four quarters give multiple shots on goal, and a Leqembi subcutaneous form could finally widen the Alzheimer's funnel. Revenue guidance went from decline to growth in one quarter.

Bear Case

The stock is up 78% from the low with RSI at 70.8, and the July beat-and-raise was met with a 6.1% monthly decline — the market is paying for success that has not fully arrived. MS still falls 13% a year on 35% of revenue; Leqembi's Biogen share is $64M a quarter three years after its landmark trial; nearly a fifth of revenue is royalties on someone else's drug. Management is buying growth with a now-levered balance sheet ($6.8B net debt) and GAAP EPS of $5.64 puts the stock at 38x trailing. If the pipeline readouts disappoint, the company is left with a shrinking core and acquired assets it paid full price for.

Top 3 Risks

01
Pipeline binary events. Five registrational readouts in four quarters, starting with litifilimab in SLE at end-2026. Late-stage biotech readouts fail often, and a miss on the lead immunology asset would undercut the diversification story.
02
MS erosion accelerates. Down 13% in Q2 2026, driven by generics and European Tysabri competition. Any new US generic or biosimilar entry would widen the hole the Growth Portfolio must fill.
03
Integration and leverage. Apellis ($5.41B) and RayThera (up to $1B) in four months took cash from $3.0B to $1.3B and net debt to $6.8B. Missing the $250M synergy target or Syfovre sales thresholds would make 2027 accretion slip.
Industry Commentary
The patent cliff as a business model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Big biotech runs on a treadmill: every product loses exclusivity, and the company must replace it before it does. Biogen's version is unusually visible because its MS franchise was so dominant. Tecfidera's −53% in Q2 2026 is the end state of a generic entry; Tysabri's European erosion is the next chapter. Revenue fell from $10.98B in FY2021 to $9.68B in FY2024 before stabilising at $9.89B in FY2025 — a company running hard to stand still. The 2026 guidance raise to mid-single-digit growth is the first time in this window the treadmill has been outrun.

Alzheimer's: the market that keeps not arriving
ONGOING HEADWIND

Anti-amyloid antibodies were supposed to be the largest new drug market in neurology. Three years after Clarity AD, Leqembi does $184M a quarter globally, split between Eisai and Biogen. The friction is operational — diagnosis, brain scans for amyloid and for side-effect monitoring, infusion capacity — and the competition from Lilly is direct. Subcutaneous dosing (Iqlik) lowers the infusion barrier; it does not remove the diagnostic one. Biogen's next-generation bet is diranersen, a tau-targeting agent for which a Phase 3 start is planned.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

On GAAP numbers Biogen looks expensive at 38x; on free cash flow it looks cheap at an 8.5% yield; on consensus non-GAAP it sits at 17x this year and 13x next ($215.50 ÷ $16.54 FY2027 consensus EPS = 13.0). The bull framing: the GAAP P/E is an artefact of paying for growth, and a company generating $2.7B of free cash a year on a ~$39B enterprise value is being priced as if the pipeline were worth little. The bear framing: "acquired IPR&D" recurs every year at a company that buys its pipeline, so adding it back flatters the true cost of staying in business — and the 78% rally has already moved the stock from pricing in failure to pricing in a reasonable amount of success. The adjudicator is the next four quarters of readouts, not the multiple.
