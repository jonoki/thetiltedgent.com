01 Company Overview

Biogen is a Cambridge, Massachusetts neuroscience company that spent two decades as the franchise in multiple sclerosis and is now trying to become something else before that franchise runs out. It sells MS drugs (Tysabri, Vumerity, Avonex, Tecfidera, Plegridy), rare-disease drugs (Spinraza for spinal muscular atrophy, Skyclarys for Friedreich's ataxia, Qalsody for a genetic form of ALS), co-commercialises the Alzheimer's antibody Leqembi with Eisai, sells Zurzuvae for postpartum depression, and — since May 14, 2026 — owns Apellis and its eye and kidney drugs Syfovre and Empaveli. FY2025 revenue was $9.89B, up 2.2%, after four straight years of decline from $10.98B in FY2021.

The story is a relay race. The old runner — MS — is slowing: Q3 2026 MS revenue fell 13% to $919M, with generic Tecfidera down 51% and Avonex down 17%. The new runners — what management calls the Growth Portfolio — delivered $1.26B, up 49% in the first full quarter with the Apellis drugs, and now make up 63% of what the company calls core pharmaceutical revenue. Total revenue rose 11% to $2.80B. "Our third quarter results demonstrate that Biogen has returned to growth," CEO Christopher Viehbacher said in the October 28 release. On price, the market has voted yes for eighteen months: from an April 2025 month-end low of $121.08 the stock has more than doubled, up 106% to $249.65. On earnings, the picture is murkier — trailing GAAP EPS is just $2.34, because Biogen is paying for its future up front. Acquired R&D charges ($284M in Q3, mostly the RayThera purchase) hit both measures of profit; on top of that, Apellis inventory step-ups, amortisation of acquired drug rights and restructuring took Q3 2026 to a GAAP loss of $0.14 a share against a non-GAAP $2.62. That gap between what the business earns and what the accounts show is the central thing to understand here.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Neurology commercial footprint Rare-disease pricing power Antisense platform (Ionis partnership) Ocrevus royalty stream Biologics manufacturing scale 74% gross margin

Pharma moats are patents with an expiry date, and Biogen has lived through what happens when one lapses: Tecfidera revenue halving in a single year. The durable part of the moat is not any one molecule but the neurology machine — the relationships with neurologists, the infusion-based neurology channel that both Tysabri and Leqembi rely on, and the antisense know-how behind Spinraza, Qalsody and the pipeline candidates licensed from Ionis. The vulnerability is that a machine needs products to run, and every product in the portfolio has a clock on it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The MS cliff keeps eroding. MS fell 13% in Q3 2026 and is still 33% of revenue. Generic Tecfidera (−51%) is the biggest drag. If the decline accelerates — a US Tysabri biosimilar would be the obvious trigger — the Growth Portfolio has to run faster just to keep revenue flat.
02
Leqembi is a shared, slow launch. $202M a quarter in global in-market sales, of which Biogen books $68.4M as its collaboration share, four years after the Clarity AD readout. It competes against Lilly's anti-amyloid antibody, requires infusion or injection plus brain-scan monitoring, and its economics are split with Eisai. The subcutaneous Iqlik form is the next lever.
03
Buying the future costs today's earnings. About $3.20 of 2026 EPS goes to acquired R&D, upfront and milestone charges, plus Apellis dilution. Net debt was $6.4B at September 30. Business development that works looks brilliant in 2029; business development that does not leaves a levered company with a shrinking core.
04
Index removal removed a buyer. Biogen was dropped from the NASDAQ-100 effective December 22, 2025. It remains an S&P 500 member, but the funds that track the Nasdaq-100, which buy whatever is in the index regardless of price, had to sell their shares.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP earnings in 2026 are a poor guide to the business. In Q3 2026 alone, $283.8M of acquired R&D, upfront and milestone expense (mostly the RayThera purchase), $194.8M of Apellis inventory fair-value step-up, $160.2M of intangible amortisation and $82.1M of restructuring and cost-saving charges turned the quarter into a GAAP net loss of $20.6M, against non-GAAP net income of $391.4M. Nine-month acquired R&D charges reached $481.8M. For the full year, management's guide carves out about $3.20 per share of acquired R&D and milestones plus the Apellis dilution. Non-GAAP adjustments here are doing heavy lifting, so the honest way to read Biogen is on free cash flow ($2.07B TTM) and on the underlying-business guide, with the caveat that "acquired IPR&D" is a recurring feature of a company that buys its pipeline.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 29, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $266.68 to the October 29, 2026 settled close of $249.65, BIIB lost 6.4% of its price, with no dividends to soften it; over the same dates SPY, the S&P 500 fund, rose 67.5% on price. But the five-year number hides two completely different stocks. The first fell from a $305.17 high (November 2022) to a $121.08 low (April 2025) — a 60% decline over two and a half years as Aduhelm was abandoned, Leqembi launched slowly and MS revenue eroded. The second has risen 106% from that low in eighteen months as the Growth Portfolio started to show up in the numbers.

The 6-period monthly RSI captures both. It sat below 30 every month from August 2024 to July 2025, bottoming at 7.8 in April 2025 — about as washed-out as a monthly reading gets. It then swung to 81.4 in June 2026 and reads 83.2 now, the highest reading in the series. The price is well above its 10-month moving average of about $205.15. Momentum is strong and stretched. One reading: twice now — July after Q2, and October 28 after Q3 — a clean beat has been met with a down day or month, a sign that much of the good news was in the price beforehand.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The handoff is working. Revenue grew 11% in Q3; the Growth Portfolio grew 49% to $1.26B and is now 63% of core pharmaceutical revenue; Spinraza is growing again (+23%), Skyclarys (+34%), Zurzuvae (+53%) and Qalsody (+42%) keep climbing, and Syfovre's injections hit a record in the first full quarter under Biogen. Strip out the acquisition charges and the stock trades at about 15x management's underlying 2026 EPS guide and a 5.6% free-cash-flow yield. The SLE readouts in December and three more Phase 3 readouts in 2027 give several shots on goal, and the Leqembi subcutaneous form could finally widen the Alzheimer's funnel.

Bear Case

The stock has more than doubled from the low with RSI at 83.2, the average analyst target ($251) is barely above the price, and both of the last two beats were met with a down day or month — the market is paying for success that has not fully arrived. MS still falls 13% a year on a third of revenue; Leqembi's Biogen share is $68M a quarter four years after its landmark trial; nearly a fifth of revenue is royalties on someone else's drug. Management is buying growth with a now-levered balance sheet ($6.4B net debt) and GAAP EPS of $2.34 puts the stock at 107x trailing. If the pipeline readouts disappoint, the company is left with a shrinking core and acquired assets it paid full price for.

Top 3 Risks

01
Pipeline yes-or-no events. Two Phase 3 lupus readouts for litifilimab in December 2026, then cutaneous lupus and kidney-transplant rejection in the first half of 2027 and Dravet syndrome in Q3 2027. Late-stage biotech readouts fail often, and a miss on the lead immunology asset would undercut the diversification story.
02
MS erosion accelerates. Down 13% in Q3 2026, led by generic Tecfidera (−51%). Any new US generic or biosimilar entry would widen the hole the Growth Portfolio must fill.
03
Integration and leverage. Apellis ($5.41B, closed May 14) and RayThera (up to $1B, closed August 6) took cash from $3.0B at the end of 2025 to $1.3B at September 30; net debt was $6.4B at September 30. Missing the $250M synergy target or Syfovre sales thresholds would make 2027 accretion slip.
Industry Commentary
The patent cliff as a business model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Big biotech runs on a treadmill: every product loses exclusivity, and the company must replace it before it does. Biogen's version is unusually visible because its MS franchise was so dominant. Tecfidera's −51% in Q3 2026 is the end state of a generic entry. Revenue fell from $10.98B in FY2021 to $9.68B in FY2024 before stabilising at $9.89B in FY2025 — a company running hard to stand still. Q3 2026's 11% revenue growth, and a 2026 guide for a mid-single-digit increase, are the first time in this window the treadmill has been outrun.

Alzheimer's: the market that keeps not arriving
ONGOING HEADWIND

Anti-amyloid antibodies were supposed to be the largest new drug market in neurology. Four years after Clarity AD, Leqembi does $202M a quarter globally, split between Eisai and Biogen. The friction is operational — diagnosis, brain scans for amyloid and for side-effect monitoring, infusion capacity — and the competition from Lilly is direct. Subcutaneous dosing (Iqlik) lowers the infusion barrier; it does not remove the diagnostic one. Biogen's next-generation bet is diranersen, a tau-targeting agent for which a Phase 3 start is planned.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

On GAAP numbers Biogen looks expensive at 107x; on free cash flow it looks fair at a 5.6% yield; on consensus non-GAAP it sits at 20x this year and 15x next ($249.65 ÷ $16.30 FY2027 consensus EPS = 15.3). The bull framing: the GAAP P/E is an artefact of paying for growth, and a company generating $2.1B of free cash a year on a ~$43B enterprise value is being priced as if the pipeline were worth little. The bear framing: "acquired IPR&D" recurs every year at a company that buys its pipeline, so adding it back flatters the true cost of staying in business — and the 106% rally from the April 2025 low has already moved the stock from pricing in failure to pricing in a reasonable amount of success. The adjudicator is the next four quarters of readouts, not the multiple.
