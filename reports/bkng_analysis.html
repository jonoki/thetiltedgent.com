01 Company Overview

Booking Holdings is the largest online travel agency (OTA) group in the world, operating Booking.com, Priceline, Agoda, KAYAK, OpenTable and Rentalcars.com. The company is an asset-light marketplace: it takes a commission on accommodation, flight, car-rental and restaurant transactions rather than owning inventory, which produces a gross margin of 87.2% and FCF of $9.24B on $28.13B of trailing revenue.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $26.92B, up 13.4% YoY, with operating income of $9.49B (35.2% operating margin). Revenue for the twelve months through September 30, 2026 is $28.13B (+8.0%), or $29.03B (+11.5%) before the $900M reduction booked for a US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) matter involving Priceline; TTM net income is $7.18B (+42.3%). The company is worth $119.49B at the November 6 close (743.70M shares at October 26, from the 10-Q cover). In April 2026 Booking carried out its first-ever forward stock split, 25-for-1, effective April 6: the last close before it was $4,194.31 on April 2, or $167.77 on the new share count.

The engine of the group and the largest accommodation marketplace on earth, with particular density in Europe where fragmented, independent and boutique properties have no realistic direct-booking alternative. Booking.com's supply advantage — millions of listed properties spanning hotels, apartments, hostels and homes — is the moat: a European traveler searching a 12-room guesthouse in Umbria will find it on Booking.com and essentially nowhere else at scale. The Connected Trip is Booking's central strategic bet: bundle accommodation, flights, ground transport (Rentalcars.com), restaurant reservations (OpenTable, whose "Gold Tables" perk for its top loyalty members launched July 2026) and attractions into one itinerary settled through Booking's own payments platform. On the Q3 2026 call, CFO Ewout Steenbergen said Connected Trip transaction growth "accelerated to almost 20% year-over-year" and that it "represented a low double-digit mix of Booking.com's total transactions" — so the strategy is real but not yet large. CEO Glenn Fogel said those transactions grew three times faster than Booking.com's overall transactions from a relatively small base. Its purpose is defensive as much as offensive: an itinerary with four linked components and stored payment credentials is far harder for an AI agent, a price-comparison site or a hotel's direct channel to pull apart than a single-night hotel reservation. Booking has also pushed into niche communities, e.g. the DogPack pet-friendly travel partnership announced around the April 2026 split.

Booking's moat is supply density combined with conversion efficiency. Every additional independent property listed on Booking.com makes the site more useful to a traveler, and every additional traveler makes the listing more valuable to the property — a textbook two-sided network, but its strength varies enormously by geography. In Europe, where the lodging base is fragmented across small independents with no loyalty program and no meaningful direct channel, the network is very hard to attack and Booking keeps its commission rates. In North America, where Marriott and Hilton concentrate supply behind loyalty walls, the same network is materially weaker. Layered on top is a second, less obvious advantage: Booking is one of the largest buyers of performance advertising on the planet, and its conversion data lets it bid more accurately per click than smaller rivals — so the marketing channel that looks like a cost is also a barrier to entry. Marketing was 4.8% of gross bookings in Q3 2026. The merchant-model transition adds a third layer, turning Booking from a referral business into a payments business that holds the customer's card, the itinerary and the cash between payment and stay. The financial signature of all this is an ROIC of 81.95% and $9.24B of trailing FCF on essentially no owned hard assets.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. AI agents as a new front door. This is the central bear argument. If travellers begin their journey inside a general-purpose AI assistant rather than a search box, the discovery step Booking currently rents from Google could move to an agent Booking neither owns nor advertises on. KAYAK is the most directly exposed asset; Booking.com is exposed through what it pays to win customers. The fear became concrete on September 23, 2026, when BKNG fell 5.07% (close to close) on the day of the selloff in travel booking stocks that 24/7 Wall St. tied to Meta's Muse AI agent, which it reported can complete a booking directly with an airline or hotel. Management now gives a size for this traffic. On the Q3 call CFO Ewout Steenbergen said: "LLM referrals have accelerated over the last several weeks, but still account for significantly less than 1% of Booking.com's room nights." CEO Glenn Fogel said the company works with Meta, OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Amazon. That is small for now, and it is the number to track at each print.

2. Regulators on both sides of the Atlantic. Europe is both Booking's profit pool and its regulatory risk. The company operates under the EU's Digital Markets Act gatekeeper regime and carries a $469M liability for a Spanish competition fine that is suspended pending appeal. On September 9, 2026 the EU General Court upheld the block on its €1.63B Etraveli flight-booking acquisition, and on October 21 the European Commission opened a formal Digital Services Act investigation into how Booking.com handles fake listings, phishing and the labelling of paid listings. In the US, the $1.0B charge in Q3 is for an FTC matter at Priceline that is not yet settled. Remedies that restrict pricing terms, force choice screens or limit data reuse would hit exactly the geography where the economics are best.

3. Demand is slowing again. Q3 2026 room nights grew +7%, ahead of the +3% to +5% guided in August. But the Q4 guide is room nights +1% to +3%, gross bookings +2% to +4% and adjusted revenue 0% to +2%, with adjusted EBITDA about flat. On the call Steenbergen said "we saw some moderation in global demand trends toward the end of the third quarter, driven by international travel", and he also pointed to October disruption from the Middle East conflict, higher airfares and local unrest in some European cities. The guidance assumes the direct and indirect effects of the Middle East conflict last through year-end. Travel is cyclical and post-pandemic normalization has removed the easy comparisons.

4. Buybacks have pushed equity below zero. Relentless buybacks have driven stockholders' equity to −$10.41B and book value per share to about −$13.99. That is a deliberate choice, not distress — the company holds $15.81B of cash against $20.06B of debt and generates $9.24B of FCF — but it makes ROE, D/E and price-to-book uncomputable, leaves no balance-sheet cushion against a genuine travel shock, and makes automated stock screens misread it. It also runs ahead of cash generation: about $12.0B of buybacks in the last twelve months against $9.24B of free cash flow, with cash down $1.39B and debt up $1.33B in the first nine months of 2026.

Notable One-Off Items

Items below operating income distorted FY2025. Operating income rose 25.0% in FY2025 to $9.49B, yet GAAP net income fell 8.1% to $5.40B and EPS fell 4.1% to $6.62. Total other expense widened to $1.30B from $82M (10-K), and a higher income-tax bill accounts for the rest of the gap. FY2025 net margin therefore fell to 20.1% from 24.8% in FY2024, and much of the rise in trailing net margin to 25.5% and in trailing EPS by 50% comes from measuring against that weak period. Analysts expect adjusted EPS, which strips out such items, to grow 14.3% in FY2026, well below the +50% headline.

The FTC charge in Q3 2026. Booking recorded a $900M reduction in revenue and a $100M accrued loss in general and administrative expenses for an FTC matter involving Priceline, a $1.0B charge before tax that it excludes from adjusted revenue, adjusted net income and adjusted EBITDA. The company says gross bookings are unaffected. No settlement has been agreed; the 10-Q says litigation remains possible and the FTC may seek penalties and restitution above these amounts. Reported Q3 revenue fell 1.3% to $8.893B and GAAP net income fell 1.1% to $2.717B; adjusted revenue rose 9% to $9.793B.

Buyback-driven negative equity. Stockholders' equity of −$10.41B and book value per share of about −$13.99 are the accumulated result of repurchasing far more stock, at far higher prices, than retained earnings support. Diluted weighted-average shares fell 7.5% from a year earlier to 753M, and cash paid for buybacks was about $12.0B over the last twelve months, roughly 10% of today's market value. With the 1.05% dividend the total cash return is about 11% of market value. This is the single most important reconciliation item on the page: ROE, D/E and P/B are all mechanically meaningless for BKNG, and any stock screen or peer comparison that uses them will misrank the stock in both directions.

Elevated 2026 investment spend. Management committed roughly $700M above baseline in 2026 for generative AI, the Connected Trip platform, market expansion and advertising — a deliberate margin drag in 2026, set against Transformation Program savings of about $250M in 2025 (when adjusted EBITDA margin reached 36.9%) and an expected $500M to $550M in 2026 (Q4 2025 earnings call). Transformation costs were $23M in Q3 and $78M year to date.

Currency and geopolitics. With the majority of gross bookings originating outside the United States, reported growth carries a meaningful currency effect. For Q4 the company guides gross bookings at +2% to +4% reported against +4% to +6% in constant currency (with exchange-rate moves stripped out), so currency is now a drag of about two points. Management also cited the indirect effects of the Middle East conflict and higher airfares as headwinds into the fourth quarter.

Regulatory and tax matters. The 10-Q carries a $469M liability for the Spanish competition fine (suspended pending appeal) and describes local-tax claims in France and a Dutch pension matter, alongside the new European Commission investigation and the FTC matter. These can each move earnings in a single quarter.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying the world's largest travel marketplace — 87.2% gross margin, 25.5% net margin even after a $1.0B FTC charge, 81.95% ROIC and $9.24B of free cash flow — at 15.4x this year's expected earnings and a 1.08 PEG (price-to-earnings divided by expected growth), cheaper than both its travel peers and the Nasdaq-100. Q3 beat the company's own guidance on room nights, gross bookings, adjusted revenue and adjusted EBITDA. AI-assistant referrals are still significantly less than 1% of Booking.com room nights, Europe's fragmented independent-lodging base is a supply advantage no chain and no agent can copy quickly, and about $12.0B of buybacks in twelve months is roughly 10% of today's market value. Thirty of 37 analysts rate it Buy with an average target of $215.80 (+34.3%). If room-night growth steadies near mid-single digits once the October disruption passes, today's price leaves room for investors to pay more for each dollar of earnings.

Bear Case

Q4 room nights are guided to just +1% to +3% after +7% in Q3, adjusted revenue to 0% to +2% and adjusted EBITDA to about flat. Management says demand softened toward the end of Q3, and its guidance assumes the Middle East conflict's effects on travel last through year-end. AI-assistant referrals are small but, in the CFO's words, have accelerated over the last several weeks; if discovery moves to assistants the company does not control, the cost of winning customers rises and commissions get squeezed from both ends. Negative $10.41B of stockholders' equity leaves no balance-sheet cushion for a real travel shock, and buybacks are running ahead of free cash flow. The FTC matter is unsettled, a European Commission investigation is new, and the +50% TTM EPS growth headline is largely a weak 2025 comparison rather than genuine acceleration.

Top 3 Risks
01
AI agents cutting Booking out. If generative-AI assistants become the default entry point for trip planning, Booking loses control of how it finds customers and either pays more for traffic or accepts fewer sales per visit. The CFO puts AI-assistant referrals at significantly less than 1% of Booking.com's room nights but says they have accelerated over recent weeks, and the September 23 selloff that 24/7 Wall St. tied to Meta's Muse shows how quickly the threat can reach the stock price. KAYAK is the first casualty; Booking.com's marketing efficiency is the second.
02
A demand slowdown. The Q4 guide of +1% to +3% room nights follows +7% in Q3. If international travel, airfares and the Middle East conflict keep weighing on bookings, growth falls toward the guided rate and 15.4x forward earnings stops looking cheap. Travel demand is genuinely cyclical.
03
Regulation and legal exposure. Booking earns its best economics in the market with the most aggressive platform regulation: Digital Markets Act obligations, the new Digital Services Act investigation, a $469M Spanish competition liability and multi-jurisdiction tax proceedings can each affect European profit. In the US the FTC matter at Priceline is accrued at $1.0B and unresolved. Because the company's equity base is negative, there is little accounting cushion to absorb a large one-time settlement.
  Industry Commentary
AI agents and the travel discovery layer
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the debate behind BKNG's valuation. Online travel has always been a two-stage business: discovery (where do I go, what's available, what does it cost) and transaction (book it, pay for it, service it). Booking's economics historically came from owning stage two while renting stage one from Google. A capable AI assistant that plans an itinerary end-to-end attacks stage one directly; the September 23 selloff that 24/7 Wall St. tied to Meta's Muse agent showed how seriously investors take that. Booking's response has three parts: roughly $700M of above-baseline 2026 spend on generative AI and the Connected Trip; working with Meta, OpenAI, Google, Anthropic and Amazon, as the CEO told investors on the Q3 call; and the bet that whoever settles the payment, holds the cancellation policy and answers the 2 a.m. customer-service call keeps the customer. Connected Trip transactions grew almost 20% in Q3 and are deliberately hard to unbundle. The counter-evidence is that AI-assistant referrals, while still significantly less than 1% of Booking.com room nights, have accelerated in recent weeks, and that the stock now sells at 15.4x this year's expected earnings. Investors will want that referral number at every print.
Global travel demand normalization
ONGOING HEADWIND
The post-pandemic travel surge is fully behind us. Booking's revenue growth has stepped down — +56.0% (FY22), +25.0% (FY23), +11.1% (FY24), +13.4% (FY25) — and Q3 2026 adjusted revenue grew +9%, with Q4 guided to 0% to +2%. Room nights at +7% against gross bookings at +9.6% in Q3 show price, mix and currency adding to volume; the Q4 guide of +1% to +3% room nights reflects the softer demand management saw late in Q3 and the October disruption from the Middle East conflict, airfares and unrest in some European cities, and assumes the conflict's effects last through year-end. This is not a thesis-breaking dynamic — a marketplace growing room nights at mid-single digits with about 11% of market value returned to holders over the last twelve months is a perfectly good business — but it makes every incremental quarter a test of whether the slowdown is temporary or lasting.
European regulation vs. the merchant-payments build — bull and bear framed
EMERGING TAILWIND
Europe is the hinge. It is where Booking's supply moat is strongest, where its commissions are best, and where platform regulation is most aggressive — Digital Markets Act obligations, the new Digital Services Act investigation, challenges to rate parity (contract terms that stop hotels undercutting Booking on price elsewhere) and national tax proceedings all concentrate in the geography that generates the profit. The bear read: forced choice screens, parity bans and data-portability remedies erode European commissions at the moment unit growth is slowing and AI is attacking how customers are found, with no equity cushion (−$10.41B) to absorb a settlement. The bull read: regulation is a fixed cost that a $119B platform absorbs far more easily than a smaller rival, while the merchant-model transition (now about 75% of gross bookings) and the Agoda–Checkout.com payments buildout turn Booking from a commission collector into the company that holds the card, the itinerary and the cash between payment and stay. If that works, 15.4x this year's expected earnings for a business earning an 82% return on capital will look too cheap.
