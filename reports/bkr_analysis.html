01 Company Overview

Baker Hughes is one of the "big three" oilfield-services companies alongside SLB and Halliburton — except that, for the first time, only about half of it is oilfield services. The company runs two segments: Oilfield Services & Equipment (OFSE), the drilling, completions, artificial-lift and subsea business that rises and falls with upstream spending, and Industrial & Energy Technology (IET), which builds the gas turbines, compressors and liquefaction equipment behind LNG (liquefied natural gas) plants and, increasingly, behind gas-fired power for data centers. FY2025 revenue was $27.7B, down 0.3% from $27.8B in FY2024, but that flat headline hides the swap underneath: IET revenue grew +10% to $13.4B while OFSE fell −8% to $14.3B. At a $63.03B market cap the company ranks roughly #404 globally.

The debate on this page is a valuation identity crisis. In the twelve months to September 4, 2026 BKR is up 40.3%; SLB and Halliburton have run too, but BKR trades at 20.5× trailing earnings against Halliburton's 19.6× and a forward multiple of 23.2× — a premium the market is paying for IET's $37.1B record backlog and a $7.1B record IET order quarter in Q2 2026, not for rig count. On July 16, 2026 the company closed its $13.6B (enterprise value) all-cash acquisition of Chart Industries, moving further from the oil patch and toward industrial gas handling. The question is whether a company still earning half its revenue from a shrinking upstream cycle deserves to be priced like an industrial with a power-equipment story — and whether it just paid up, with borrowed money, to make that argument stick.

Chart (formerly NYSE: GTLS) makes cryogenic tanks, heat exchangers, air and gas handling and compression equipment — the "thermal management" plumbing that sits next to Baker Hughes' turbines and compressors in an LNG plant, a hydrogen project or a data-center cooling loop. The deal was announced in 2025 and closed on July 16, 2026, funded by $6.5B of U.S.-dollar senior notes, €3.0B of euro notes and new term loans. That financing is why the June 30, 2026 balance sheet shows an odd pair: $15.7B of cash against $16.3B of debt. The cash left the building in July. Chart's standalone revenue and margins were not verified from sources fetched, so no pro-forma figures appear here — but the Q3 2026 print on October 22 will be the first look at the combined company, and at whether IET's 20.6% margin survives the mix.

Also in motion: Waygate Technologies (industrial inspection) being sold to Hexagon for ~$1.45B cash; PSI business sold for ~$1.2B and Surface Pressure Control JV for $479M in 1H 2026. Portfolio churn is high — and it distorts GAAP earnings (see Section 04).
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

LNG liquefaction franchise $37.1B IET backlog Installed-base services annuity Gas turbine IP (ex-GE) NOC relationships (Middle East / LatAm) Subsea & artificial-lift technology Only OFS name with an industrial arm

The core advantage is that Baker Hughes owns the equipment layer of the LNG build-out and the service contracts that follow it. Once a liquefaction train is designed around a specific Baker Hughes compressor and turbine set, the plant owner is buying parts, upgrades and field service from Baker Hughes for 25 years — Gas Technology Services revenue was $831M in Q2 2026, and unlike equipment it does not lumpily depend on new-project sanctioning. The company's gas-turbine IP is the GE Oil & Gas heritage that competitors SLB and Halliburton do not have, which is why the data-center power opportunity landed in Baker Hughes' lap rather than theirs. And $40.1B of total remaining performance obligations — more than 1.4 years of revenue — is a degree of forward visibility that a pure oilfield-services company, priced off the next quarter's rig count, structurally cannot offer.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The data-center turbine market has bigger incumbents. GE Vernova ($250.9B market cap, $41.4B TTM revenue, +13%) and Siemens Energy (€124.4B, €41.8B, +9%) own the large-frame heavy-duty gas turbine market and are both sold out for years. Baker Hughes' NovaLT and aeroderivative machines are smaller, faster-to-deploy units — a real niche for behind-the-meter data-center power, but a niche. If hyperscalers standardise on utility-scale plants, the order flow goes to GEV and Siemens.
▸
Half the company is still in a downcycle. OFSE revenue fell 8% in FY2025 and another 5% YoY in Q2 2026; segment EBITDA fell 11%. The company itself has flagged lower producer spending for 2026. IET's growth has so far been enough to hold total revenue flat — Q2 2026 total revenue was $6.74B, −2% YoY — but "flat" is what the premium multiple is currently buying.
▸
Leverage just went from zero to real. Pre-Chart, Baker Hughes carried $771M of net cash. Post-close, the roughly $10B-plus of new notes and loans are matched against $13.6B of acquired enterprise value, not against cash. Debt-to-EBITDA on the pre-deal gross figure was already 2.87×; the combined ratio depends on Chart's EBITDA, which was not verified here, and management has paused buybacks (none in Q2 2026).
▸
LNG order lumpiness. Gas Technology Equipment orders were $4.9B in one quarter — a number driven by a handful of Venture Global and Cheniere sanctioning decisions. LNG FIDs (final investment decisions) cluster; a quarter with none of them looks like a collapse even when the backlog is intact. The stock's June 2026 monthly close of $55.50, down 20% from April, shows how the market treats that lumpiness.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $25.08 to $63.50 on September 4, 2026, BKR has returned +153% in price alone — roughly 21% compounded over 4.9 years, before a dividend that ran from about $0.72 to $0.92 per year. The path had two very different halves. From late 2021 through the end of 2023 the stock was an oil-price proxy: a spike to $36.41 in March 2022, a slide to the 5-year monthly low of $20.96 in September 2022, then two years stuck between $27 and $36 while the company sorted out the post-GE cost base. From October 2024 the stock re-rated on a different story — IET orders, LNG sanctioning and, from 2025, data-center power — and roughly doubled from $38.08 to the April 2026 monthly-close high of $69.67. The 52-week change of +40.3% (stockanalysis.com) is consistent with the $45.40 August 2025 close on the chart. A NASDAQ-100 or S&P 500 total-return comparison over the identical window could not be verified from the sources fetched and is not stated; what can be said is that a 0.96 beta and a +153% five-year move mean most of the return was stock-specific re-rating rather than market drift, and that peers SLB (market cap +76% over 12 months) and Halliburton (+59%) have participated in the sector's recent run at least as strongly — BKR's premium is in its multiple, not its one-year return.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What the market is actually paying for. Strip the company into its parts. OFSE — $14.3B of FY2025 revenue at an 18.3% EBITDA margin, falling — is a business Halliburton-shaped investors value at roughly 15–20× earnings. IET — $13.4B at 18.5% rising to 20.6%, with $37.1B of backlog and orders running at double revenue — is a business GE Vernova-shaped investors value at 27× trailing and 45× forward. Baker Hughes trades at 20.5× trailing and 23.2× forward: the market has placed it about a third of the way from the oil-services multiple toward the power-equipment multiple. Every quarter in which IET orders stay above $5B and OFSE merely holds flat drags the blended multiple further along that line. Every quarter with an LNG sanctioning air-pocket drags it back.

The backlog arithmetic. $40.1B of remaining performance obligations against a $27.7B revenue run-rate is 1.45 years of revenue already contracted; for IET alone, $37.1B against $13.4B is 2.8 years. Book-to-bill in IET for Q2 2026 was $7,088M of orders on $3,291M of revenue — 2.15×. That is what "the market values it as an industrial" means in practice: visibility that oilfield services has never had. But equipment backlog converts at equipment margins, and Gas Technology Equipment was $1,524M of the $3,291M IET revenue in Q2 — the mix is tilting toward the lower-margin, longer-cycle part of the segment even as the segment margin rises. The 20.6% Q2 margin is the number most at risk from Chart's arrival.

The balance sheet changed on July 16. The June 30 snapshot — $15.7B cash, $16.3B debt, $771M net cash, 2.09 current ratio — describes a company that no longer exists. Roughly $10B-plus of new notes and loans went out the door for Chart. Buybacks stopped ($0 in Q2 2026 versus $384M for all of 2025). Disposal proceeds — PSI ~$1.2B, SPC JV $479M, Waygate ~$1.45B pending — are the de-levering plan. Investors should expect the October 22 balance sheet to show net debt in the low-teens billions (est.) and should judge the stock on that, not on the stockanalysis.com ratios above.

Bull Case

IET is the only U.S.-listed way to own LNG liquefaction equipment and small-frame gas turbines for data-center power in one ticker, and the order book proves demand: $7.1B in a quarter, $37.1B of backlog, a Horizon 2 target above $45B, and 20.6% margins already at the 2026 goal. Chart adds the thermal and cryogenic layer that makes Baker Hughes a full-scope supplier to LNG, hydrogen and data-center cooling. If IET keeps compounding at 10%+ on a rising margin, the company earns a GEV-style multiple on half its business and the stock's 23× forward is cheap. Meanwhile OFSE's 73% international skew shields it from the shale slowdown, ROIC of 16% doubles the cost of capital, and 24 analysts land at a $72 average target.

Bear Case

Half the company is in a downcycle the company itself has flagged — OFSE EBITDA −11% YoY — and the other half just booked an order quarter it cannot repeat, from a handful of LNG customers whose sanctioning decisions cluster. Trailing EPS of $3.11 includes ~$0.70 of disposal gains; on the $2.63 consensus the stock is 23× a business growing revenue 0.4%. The Chart deal replaced $771M of net cash with double-digit-billions of net debt at the top of the cycle, paused buybacks, and puts a 20.6% segment margin at risk of dilution. And in data-center power the incumbents are GE Vernova and Siemens Energy at $250B and €124B — Baker Hughes is the niche player, priced as if it were not.

Top 3 Risks

  Industry Commentary
Upstream Capex Is Contracting
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
All three large oilfield-services companies posted lower FY2025 revenue — SLB −1.6%, Halliburton −3.3%, Baker Hughes OFSE −8% — and Baker Hughes has publicly flagged lower producer spending for 2026. The U.S. rig count sits at 588 and flat. International, historically the stabiliser, fell 6% YoY for Baker Hughes OFSE in Q2 2026. This is not a Baker Hughes problem; it is the reason all three have spent the last two years buying their way into adjacent industrial markets — SLB's $3.4B Kelvion deal and Baker Hughes' $13.6B Chart deal are the same instinct at different scales. The constraint on the whole group is that the legacy business funds the pivot, and the legacy business is shrinking.
LNG Sanctioning Is Lumpy by Nature
ONGOING HEADWIND
A Venture Global six-block award or a Cheniere Sabine Pass Train 7 order is a multi-billion-dollar event that lands in one quarter. Baker Hughes' Gas Technology Equipment orders of $4.9B in Q2 2026 versus $1.5B of GTE revenue in the same quarter is what a sanctioning cluster looks like. The headwind is not demand — the backlog says demand is fine — it is that quarterly order prints become the stock's heartbeat, and a quiet quarter looks like a heart attack. Investors who bought after the record quarter should expect to be tested by a normal one.
Data Centers Want Gas Turbines, Now
EMERGING TAILWIND
Grid interconnection queues are years long; a gas turbine installed behind the meter is not. That is why Dynamis ordered 76 NovaLT16s (~1.3 GW) and Kodiak signed for up to 1.8 GW, and why roughly 7 GW of BRUSH generator awards landed in 2025. Siemens Energy explicitly credited AI data-center demand for record orders in its own Q3 2026, and GE Vernova's forward P/E of 45× says the market believes the cycle is long. Bull framing: Baker Hughes' small-frame, fast-deploy turbines are exactly what a hyperscaler needs for bridging power, and every unit sold adds 25 years of service revenue — the more the grid lags, the better. Bear framing: the heavy-duty incumbents are ten times Baker Hughes' size in power equipment; when utilities catch up with large-frame plants, "bridging" turbines get idled, and Baker Hughes' data-center revenue is not yet disclosed as a line item — so the size of the tailwind is, for now, an inference from GW announcements, not a reported number.
