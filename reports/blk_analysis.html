01 Company Overview

BlackRock is the world's largest asset manager, running $15.49 trillion of client assets as of September 30, 2026 across index and active strategies, the iShares exchange-traded fund (ETF) franchise, cash management, and a fast-growing private-markets platform. Revenue is overwhelmingly a function of assets under management (AUM) multiplied by an effective fee rate, so results rise and fall with market levels as well as with how much new money clients bring in. Full-year 2025 revenue was $24.22 billion, up 19% YoY; third-quarter 2026 revenue was $7.75 billion, up 19% again, and trailing-twelve-month revenue reached $28.54 billion with the GIP, HPS and Preqin acquisitions now fully in the base. Clients added $205 billion of net new money in the quarter and $869 billion over the last twelve months.

The company ranks #64 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization ($181.8B). Notably, its market cap is only about 1.2% of the assets it manages — a reminder that BlackRock earns fees on other people's money rather than risking its own. That model, which needs little capital of its own, produces a 24.2% net profit margin and a 47.8% as-adjusted operating margin in the third quarter, but it also means the earnings stream inherits the volatility of global equity and bond markets.

BlackRock's moat is a compounding scale advantage that is difficult to attack from below. In index products, cost per dollar managed falls almost linearly with AUM, and the largest ETFs attract the tightest bid-ask spreads and deepest options markets — which draws institutional users, which deepens liquidity further. iShares at $6.36 trillion, after another $135B of net inflows in Q3 2026, is that flywheel in numbers. Layered on top is Aladdin: once a client runs its risk, trading and reporting on BlackRock's operating system, switching means re-plumbing the entire investment process, which produces subscription revenue with very low churn and 12% ACV growth. The private-markets build — GIP for infrastructure, HPS for private credit, Preqin for data — extends both advantages into the fastest-growing, highest-fee corner of asset management, and the resulting mix shift is pushing the blended fee rate up rather than down, with 11% annualized organic base fee growth in Q3 2026 and 10% over the last twelve months. The result is a firm that is at once the low-cost provider of plain index funds and a manager whose average fee is rising through alternatives (private credit, infrastructure, private equity) — a combination no competitor currently holds.

Key Vulnerabilities

Market-beta dependence. Roughly 79% of revenue is a small percentage fee on AUM, and AUM moves with global equity and bond markets. A 20% drawdown in world markets mechanically removes a large share of base fees with almost no offsetting cost flexibility in the short run, and the stock's beta of 1.42 reflects that: with costs fixed in the short run, profit falls faster than fees. The March 2026 close of $961.71 — down 17.5% from the September 2025 peak — shows how quickly the multiple compresses when markets wobble.

Fee compression in core passive. Vanguard, State Street and Fidelity compete directly on expense ratio, and zero-fee or near-zero-fee index products have repeatedly reset the price floor. BlackRock's defence is mix — active ETFs, private markets, technology — but if new money keeps going mostly into the cheapest plain index funds, revenue grows more slowly than AUM. FY2025 distribution fees growing just 6% against 19% total revenue growth is a reminder that not every line participates in the story.

Acquisition integration and goodwill risk. The GIP, HPS and Preqin deals transformed the growth profile but also injected substantial purchase accounting: Q3 2026 intangible amortization alone was $276M (against $253M a year earlier), and FY2025 GAAP operating income fell 7% while as-adjusted operating income rose 18%. The $12.78 gap between FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS of $35.31 and as-adjusted EPS of $48.09 is the size of the adjustment investors are being asked to look through. If private-markets fundraising disappoints, that goodwill becomes an impairment candidate.

Political and regulatory exposure. As the largest holder of a substantial share of the US equity market, BlackRock is a standing target for anti-ESG state-level action, common-ownership antitrust theory (the argument that one investor owning stakes in competing companies dulls competition), and scrutiny of how index funds vote their shares. These rarely damage earnings directly but they generate headlines about lost client accounts, constrain product positioning, and raise the cost of doing business in politicised jurisdictions. The advisory relationship with regulators (FMA) simultaneously deepens the conflict-of-interest critique.

Notable One-Off Items

One-off distortions are material here and dominate the GAAP-versus-adjusted gap. First, acquisition-related intangible amortization: Q3 2026 amortization of intangible assets was $276M, against $253M a year earlier, now that the HPS transaction (closed July 2025) sits in both quarters. Second, integration and transaction costs from GIP, HPS and Preqin, which are the main reason FY2025 GAAP operating income declined 7% to $7.05B (29.1% GAAP margin) while as-adjusted operating income rose 18% to $9.60B (44.1% adjusted margin); in Q3 2026 the wedge was still 17.8 percentage points (30.0% GAAP against 47.8% as-adjusted). Third, nonoperating income: the release says the 38% rise in as-adjusted EPS also reflects higher nonoperating income in the quarter, which is why per-share growth ran ahead of the 27% rise in as-adjusted operating income. Fourth, performance fee lumpiness: Q3 2026 performance fees of $630M versus $516M a year earlier (+22%) depend on when gains are locked in, and do not set a new steady level. Finally, seed and co-investment marks — changes in the value of the firm's own money invested alongside clients — flow through nonoperating income and add quarter-to-quarter noise. Investors should anchor on as-adjusted EPS ($48.09 FY2025; $15.93 in Q3 2026) while remembering that the amortization being adjusted out is a real economic cost of having bought the growth.

Relative performance. Against the S&P 500, BLK's +18.8% price return since October 2021 has lagged badly — SPY, the largest S&P 500 index fund, returned +68.1% on price over the same window ($459.25 at the October 2021 month-end close to $771.79 on October 15, 2026). The gap opened in 2022: with revenue tied to both equity and fixed income AUM, BlackRock had no offsetting exposure in a year when both fell, and its shares fell harder than the index. It widened again over the past year (BLK −6.8% against SPY +16.0%). Against the asset-management peer group the picture reverses — BLK's 19% revenue growth in FY2025 and again in Q3 2026 is more than double the ~8% estimated industry average, and its 9–11% organic base fee growth is the outlier in an industry where mid-single-digit organic growth is considered strong. In short: BLK has been a poor way to own the stock market as a whole over five years, and an excellent way to own the consolidation of the asset-management industry. The private-markets mix shift is the mechanism by which management is trying to convert the second into the first.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

BlackRock is executing a genuine business-model upgrade: $336B of private-markets AUM with $68B raised over the last twelve months, Q3 performance fees up 22% YoY, technology ACV up 12%, and a blended fee rate that is rising rather than compressing — 11% annualized organic base fee growth in Q3 2026 and 10% over the last twelve months. That sits on top of a $6.36T iShares franchise that took in $135B of ETF net inflows in a single quarter. If GAAP converges toward the 47.8% as-adjusted operating margin as GIP/HPS/Preqin amortization rolls off, the 17.9x forward P/E on consensus earnings looks undemanding for a business growing revenue near 20% with a 0.23 debt-to-equity ratio and a $550M-a-quarter buyback.

Bear Case

Roughly 79% of revenue is a small percentage fee on market values, and a beta of 1.42 means a market drawdown hits earnings and multiple simultaneously — as March 2026's $961.71 close demonstrated. GAAP results are considerably weaker than the adjusted story: FY2025 GAAP operating income fell 7%, the GAAP operating margin was 30.0% in Q3 2026 against 47.8% as-adjusted, ROE has sagged to 12.3% on a goodwill-inflated equity base, and free cash flow declined to $3.52B from $4.70B in FY2024. The stock is down 6.8% over 52 weeks through record AUM and a run of earnings beats, yet still trades at 25.5x trailing GAAP earnings, so much of the private-markets optionality is already in the price. If fundraising slows or markets fall, earnings and the price-to-earnings multiple fall together.

Top 3 Risks

01
Market-level dependence with no natural hedge. With ~79% of revenue derived from AUM-linked fees across both equity and fixed income, a correlated drawdown in both asset classes — as in 2022, when the stock fell 41.7% from its October 2021 close to the September 2022 trough — removes base fees with essentially no cost offset. Beta of 1.42 quantifies the transmission.
02
Acquisition integration and goodwill impairment. The GIP/HPS/Preqin build cost real capital and is now embedded in a $12.78/share FY2025 gap between GAAP and as-adjusted EPS ($5.36 in Q3 2026 alone), $276M of quarterly intangible amortization, and an ROE of 12.3%. If private-markets fundraising or fee realization disappoints, the write-down risk lands on the equity base and the growth story simultaneously.
03
Fee compression and political pressure on the index franchise. Vanguard, State Street and Fidelity compete on price in the core index products that still make up the majority of AUM, while state governments pulling money over ESG, common-ownership antitrust theories and index-voting scrutiny impose an ongoing tax on the firm's largest and most visible business. The mix shift into private markets is the answer, but it currently offsets rather than escapes the pressure.
  Industry Commentary
The Migration from Public to Private Markets EMERGING TAILWIND

Capital formation is shifting away from public listings toward private credit, infrastructure and private equity, and fee rates in those channels are a multiple of index fee rates. BlackRock's response has been the most aggressive of any traditional manager: GIP for infrastructure, HPS for private credit, Preqin for the data layer. The result is $336B of private-markets AUM at September 30, 2026, $15.3B of Q3 net inflows, $68B raised over the last twelve months, and performance fees of $630M in Q3 against $516M a year earlier. Private markets are a large part of why BlackRock's organic base fee growth is running at 10–11% while the traditional industry grows mid-single digits.

Falling Index-Fund Fees vs. a Higher-Fee Mix ONGOING HEADWIND

Prices of plain index funds keep falling — Vanguard (~$12.0T), Fidelity ($7.8T) and State Street ($5.6T) all compete directly on expense ratio (the annual fee a fund charges), and the largest 20 managers ran 47% of the assets of the world's 500 largest at the end of 2024 (Thinking Ahead Institute), which concentrates the price war among a handful of giants with the scale to fight it. BlackRock's $6.36T ETF book (41.1% of AUM) sits at the centre of that pressure. The offset is mix: active ETFs, private markets and digital assets carry higher fee rates, so the blended rate has been rising even as individual product prices fall. FY2025 distribution fees growing only 6% against 19% total revenue growth is the clearest evidence that the low-fee legacy channels are no longer the growth engine.

Market-Level Dependence — Bull vs. Bear Framing CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Everything above is contingent on market levels. AUM of $15.49T generates fees as a percentage of value, so a 20% global drawdown removes roughly a fifth of the base fee pool in months, with a cost base that is only partly variable. Bull framing: that same gearing works in reverse — the +103.7% run from the September 2022 trough shows how violently the shares re-rate when AUM recovers, and the private-markets and technology lines now provide a less market-sensitive floor (technology ACV +12%, contracted and recurring). Bear framing: at 25.5x trailing earnings with a beta of 1.42, an investor buying BLK today is making an amplified bet on global asset prices at a full price, having already paid for the diversification that is supposed to soften it. Digital-assets AUM, $48.8B at June 30 and $79.6B at September 30, 2026, shows how fast a pool tied to the price of Bitcoin can grow or shrink.
