01 Company Overview

BMW builds premium cars under three brands — BMW, MINI and Rolls-Royce — makes BMW Motorrad motorcycles, and runs its own lending arm, Financial Services, which leased or financed 52.9% of the cars the group sold in the first half of 2026. In 2025 the group delivered 2,463,681 cars, booked revenue of €133.45B (down 6.3%), earned €10.24B before tax and €7.45B after it, or €11.89 of EPS per ordinary share. The car business ran at a 5.3% EBIT margin, below the 8–10% the company calls its strategic target.

2026 has been the year that gap widened. Late on June 16 BMW cut its car-business margin forecast from 4–6% to 1–3%, blaming a collapse in Chinese demand and the conflict in the Middle East; the shares fell 8.3% the next day and another 4.0% the day after. Second-quarter car sales in China fell 30.2%. In July BMW agreed a workforce restructuring programme with its works council, and at its September 29–30 Capital Market Day the new chief executive, Milan Nedeljković, set an interim target of a 3–5% margin in 2028 on the way back to 8–10% by the start of the 2030s. The stock went from €93.14 at the end of 2025 to €54.32 on October 2, 2026, down 41.7% — and that close was the lowest of the past twelve months. The market now values BMW at 5.2 times trailing earnings and 0.34 times book value, below the €42.6B of net financial assets the car business alone reports. The question for an owner: are these trough earnings on a cash-rich balance sheet, or the first years of a permanent squeeze from Chinese competitors in BMW's largest market?

One share class now. Until mid-2026 BMW had two listed classes: voting ordinary shares (BMW.DE, the line this report follows) and non-voting preference shares. Shareholders voted on May 13, 2026 to convert every preference share into an ordinary share of equal value; the change was registered on June 30, 2026, when trading in the preference shares stopped. BMW's share capital is now 615,810,431 ordinary shares, of which the company held 16,934,921 in treasury on June 30; the market cap above uses the 598,875,510 shares outstanding on that date.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Premium brands: BMW, M, MINI, Rolls-Royce Captive finance (52.9% penetration) Auto net financial assets €42.6B Neue Klasse platform "Technology-open" powertrains Global production footprint Family anchor shareholders

BMW's moat is pricing power from a brand people pay extra for, protected by a balance sheet that lets it keep investing through a bad year. It keeps selling petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric versions side by side — what management calls being technology-open — and the Neue Klasse brings its new technology across the whole range and all powertrains; at the Capital Market Day BMW said the electric iX3 had reached margin parity with combustion models in key markets. The captive bank keeps the customer inside the BMW system from first lease to trade-in. What the moat does not cover is China, where BMW itself describes the competition from local brands as intensifying and spreading across Asia-Pacific.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China is shrinking, and BMW with it. Second-quarter China sales fell 30.2%. The CFO on June 16: "We cannot operate in isolation of this market development and the associated intensity in the competitive field." BMW's plan is to make China local — expanding local production with the Neue Klasse, raising the share of its China-built cars developed specifically for Chinese buyers to at least 95% by 2030, and limiting imports to the highest-margin models.
02
Tariffs are a standing tax. Higher US duties and EU anti-subsidy duties cut the car margin by about 1.25 points in the first half, and BMW expects about 1.25 points for the full year (1.5 in 2025). The EU–US agreement in force since July 1 cut EU duties on US-built cars and parts from 10% to zero, which BMW has built into its outlook; BMW assumes no change to US duties on EU exports this year.
03
The cost base is German. The restructuring agreed with the works council in July carries a charge of up to 1.25 margin points in the second half of 2026. BMW is cutting 20% of its divisions and management roles and says a voluntary redundancy scheme for indirect staff in Germany pays back in about two years. On September 18 Volkswagen cut its own 2026 margin forecast to at most 1%; the whole German industry is cutting at once.
04
The bank is a lever both ways. Financing more than half of sales supports volume, but the jump in China penetration means BMW is stepping in where local lenders pulled back. Financial Services profit before tax was down 15.4% in the first half, and weaker used-car prices would hit lease returns.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort 2026's numbers. Tariffs took about 1.25 points off the car margin in the first half, partly offset in Q2 by refunds claimed on US tariffs collected under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act. BMW Brilliance amortisation — the write-down of the price paid for control of the Chinese joint venture — cost about €0.6B in the half (1.2 margin points), a non-cash charge that ends in time; BMW lists "the end of PPA" among the steps back to 8–10%. The workforce restructuring charge, up to 1.25 margin points, lands in the second half and is inside the 1–3% guide. And buybacks: the second tranche of BMW's €2B programme bought 8,252,475 ordinary shares for about €625M by June 26, and the third and final €625M tranche runs from July 1 to November 30 — at €54.32 that would be about 11.5M shares, roughly 1.9% of the shares outstanding (our calculation). BMW paid €2,654M in dividends in Q2 (€2,420M on ordinary and €234M on preference shares).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

The shape: a 2023 peak at €112.52, a long slide through 2024, a recovery to €93.14 by December 2025, then the 2026 fall, nearly half of it in the single month of June.

The deepest reading in the series was 18.2 in November 2024, and the stock rose 12.8% the following month — but RSI marks one-sided selling, not a floor. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 5.2 times trailing earnings, 8.3 times a forecast that already assumes EPS falls 45%, and a third of book value for a company with €42.6B of automotive net financial assets, a commitment to a "single A" credit rating, an above-€2.5B free-cash-flow target for a year of falling profit, and a buyback still running. Several 2026 drags are temporary by construction: the BMW Brilliance amortisation runs off, the restructuring charge is one-off, and the EU–US deal took EU duties on US-built cars to zero. The Neue Klasse is selling — the iX3 has drawn more than 100,000 orders in Europe since sales began in September 2025 — and BMW says it has reached margin parity with combustion models in key markets. If BMW only reaches the middle of its 2028 range, today's price assumes almost nothing of the way back to 8–10%, and every analyst target on the board is above the price.

Bear Case

The bear does not need the balance sheet to crack, only the earnings to stay down. China, BMW's largest single market, fell 30% in a quarter, and the reason — local brands selling more car for less — is structural, not cyclical. The new 2028 interim target of 3–5% is below the 4–6% BMW guided for 2026 as recently as March, so the recovery has moved further away. Much of the "net cash" is lent to the group's own bank and backs a leasing book exposed to used-car prices, so it cannot all be paid out. With German peers cutting at the same time, Europe is getting more competitive, not less. A dividend that pays out 30–40% of profit falls with profit: against the 8.1% trailing yield, the consensus FY2026 dividend of €2.50 is a 4.6% yield.

Top 3 Risks

01

Industry Commentary
China: from profit engine to a market to defend
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The numbers BMW's CFO gave on June 16 tell the story of 2026: in December 2025 the China Passenger Car Association expected the market to be flat; by mid-June it forecast −14.3%, and actual sales were down 19.4% through May. He said BMW had sold about 50,000 cars a month in China through 2025 and into 2026, then saw volumes fall 10% in the first quarter and 17.6% through May. Chinese brands are also selling more in other Asia-Pacific markets, which BMW says is spreading the pressure beyond China. BMW's answer is to become more Chinese: more locally built and locally developed models, exports of China-built cars to Southeast Asia under review, and price discipline over volume.

Germany's carmakers restructure at the same time
ONGOING HEADWIND

BMW's June warning was followed by Volkswagen's on September 18: an operating margin of at most 1% for 2026, down from 4.0–5.5%, with about €10B of one-off costs, mostly at Porsche, including a roughly €6B goodwill write-down (Reuters). Over twelve months Mercedes-Benz and Volkswagen preference shares each lost about 28%. All three groups face the same mix — China shrinking, US tariffs, high German labour and energy costs — and all three are cutting fixed costs together. That can restore margins across the industry, or it can turn Europe into the market where everyone discounts to keep plants full. BMW enters the cuts with €42.6B of automotive net financial assets and a commitment to keep its "single A" credit rating.

Electric cars that earn their keep
EMERGING TAILWIND

The Neue Klasse is BMW's bet that a new electronics architecture and a cheaper sixth-generation battery can make electric cars as profitable as petrol ones. The early evidence is good: the iX3, built at BMW's new plant in Debrecen, Hungary, launched in Europe in March; the CFO said on June 16 that the lower Gen6 battery-pack costs would support margins; and at the Capital Market Day BMW said the iX3, delivered since March 2026, had reached margin parity with combustion models in key markets. The new i3 followed in June, and the technology is being rolled out across more than 40 new or revised models by the end of 2027. If it works, BMW's electric growth in Europe (+38% in Q2) adds margin instead of diluting it — the one part of the 2026 story that is going better than planned.
