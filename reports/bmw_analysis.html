01 Company Overview

BMW builds premium cars under three brands — BMW, MINI and Rolls-Royce — makes BMW Motorrad motorcycles, and runs its own lending arm, Financial Services, which leased or financed 53.7% of the cars the group sold in the first nine months of 2026. In 2025 the group delivered 2,463,681 cars, booked revenue of €133.45B (down 6.3%), earned €10.24B before tax and €7.45B after it, or €11.89 of EPS per ordinary share. The car business ran at a 5.3% EBIT margin, below the 8–10% the company calls its strategic target.

2026 has been the year that gap widened. Late on June 16 BMW cut its car-business margin forecast from 4–6% to 1–3%, blaming a collapse in Chinese demand and the conflict in the Middle East; the shares fell 8.3% the next day and another 4.0% the day after. In July BMW agreed a workforce restructuring programme with its works council, and at its September 29–30 Capital Market Day the new chief executive, Milan Nedeljković, set an interim target of a 3–5% margin in 2028 on the way back to 8–10% by the start of the 2030s. On the evening of November 3 came the second cut: a 2026 car margin of −0.5% to +0.5%, because BMW will now book about 2.5 margin points of restructuring and model-phase-out costs in the fourth quarter, up from 1.25. Before those charges it still expects a margin above 2%. The third quarter itself came in at 3.4%, ahead of the 3.1% analysts had forecast and helped by a settlement with a supplier, while China sales fell 24.8% and the car business burned €566M of cash. The shares rose 5.5% on November 4, the day of the results, then gave part of it back by the end of the week. The stock went from €93.14 at the end of 2025 to €59.36 on November 6, 2026, down 36.3%, after a closing low of €52.18 on October 8. The market now values BMW at 5.9 times trailing earnings and 0.36 times book value (its net worth in the accounts), and the €35.1B market cap is below the €42.4B of net financial assets the car business alone reports. The question for an owner: are these trough earnings on a cash-rich balance sheet, or the first years of a permanent squeeze from Chinese competitors in BMW's largest market?

One share class now. Until mid-2026 BMW had two listed classes: voting ordinary shares (BMW.DE, the line this report follows) and non-voting preference shares. Shareholders voted on May 13, 2026 to convert every preference share into an ordinary share of equal value; the change was registered on June 30, 2026, when trading in the preference shares stopped. BMW's share capital is now 615,810,431 ordinary shares, of which the company held 24,676,720 (4.01%) in treasury on September 30; the market cap above uses the 591,133,711 shares outstanding on that date.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Premium brands: BMW, M, MINI, Rolls-Royce In-house financing (53.7% of new cars) Auto net financial assets €42.4B Neue Klasse platform "Technology-open" powertrains Global production footprint Family anchor shareholders

BMW's moat is pricing power from a brand people pay extra for, protected by a balance sheet that lets it keep investing through a bad year. It keeps selling petrol, diesel, hybrid and electric versions side by side — what management calls being technology-open — and the Neue Klasse brings its new technology across the whole range and all powertrains; at the Capital Market Day BMW said the electric iX3 had reached margin parity with combustion models in key markets. BMW's in-house bank keeps the customer inside the BMW system from first lease to trade-in. What the moat does not cover is China, where BMW itself describes the competition from local brands as intensifying and spreading across Asia-Pacific.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China is shrinking, and BMW with it. Third-quarter China sales fell 24.8%, after a 30.2% fall in the second quarter. The CFO on June 16: "We cannot operate in isolation of this market development and the associated intensity in the competitive field." BMW's plan is to make China local — expanding local production with the Neue Klasse, raising the share of its China-built cars developed specifically for Chinese buyers to at least 95% by 2030, and limiting imports to the highest-margin models.
02
Tariffs are a standing tax. Higher US duties cut the car margin by about 1.25 points in the third quarter and over the first nine months, and BMW expects about 1.25 points for the full year (about 1.5 in 2025). The EU–US agreement in force since July 1 cut EU duties on US-built cars and parts from 10% to zero, which BMW has built into its outlook; BMW assumes no change to US duties on EU exports this year.
03
The cost base is German. The restructuring agreed with the works council in July, together with phasing out older models and technology early, now carries a charge of about 2.5 margin points in the fourth quarter of 2026, up from the 1.25 BMW guided earlier. BMW is cutting 20% of its divisions and management roles, faster than first announced, and says a voluntary redundancy scheme for indirect staff in Germany pays back in about two years. AFP reported in July that the workforce will shrink by about 8,000 by the end of 2027, through voluntary exits offered to desk-based staff in Germany. On September 18 Volkswagen cut its own 2026 margin forecast to at most 1%; the whole German industry is cutting at once.
04
The bank is a lever both ways. Financing more than half of sales supports volume, but the jump in China penetration means BMW is stepping in where local lenders pulled back. Financial Services profit before tax was down 10.3% in the first nine months, the credit loss ratio rose to 0.35%, and BMW expects used-car prices to keep falling, which cuts what lease returns fetch.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort 2026's numbers. Tariffs took about 1.25 points off the car margin in the first nine months; the CFO expects refunds of US tariffs to help cash in the fourth quarter. BMW Brilliance amortisation — the write-down of the price paid for control of the Chinese joint venture — cost about €0.9B in the nine months (1.2 margin points), a non-cash charge that ends in time; BMW lists the end of this charge among the steps back to 8–10%. The restructuring and model-phase-out charge, about 2.5 margin points, lands in the fourth quarter and is inside the −0.5% to +0.5% guide; a settlement with a supplier over a brake-system warranty case helped the third quarter. And buybacks: BMW held 24,676,720 of its own shares on September 30, the third and final €625M tranche runs to November, and the CFO said the €2B programme will finish five months ahead of the original schedule, with about €1.25B returned through buybacks in 2026. BMW paid €2,654M in dividends in Q2 (€2,420M on ordinary and €234M on preference shares).

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 6, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of €84.98 to the November 6, 2026 close of €59.36, BMW lost 30.1% of its price. It paid €29.00 a share in dividends along the way (€5.80, €8.50, €6.00, €4.30 and €4.40 for 2021–2025), so a holder who pocketed them ended about 4% ahead before tax. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 5.9 times trailing earnings, 10.5 times an estimate that already assumes EPS falls by about half, and a little over a third of book value for a company with €42.4B of automotive net financial assets, a commitment to a "single A" credit rating, an above-€2.5B free-cash-flow target it reaffirmed on November 4, and a buyback finishing five months early. Several 2026 drags are temporary by construction: the BMW Brilliance amortisation runs off, the fourth-quarter restructuring charge is one-off, and BMW still expects a margin above 2% before it. The Neue Klasse is selling — the iX3 has drawn more than 100,000 orders in Europe within a year — and BMW says it has reached margin parity with combustion models in key markets. If BMW only reaches the middle of its 2028 range, today's price assumes little of the way back to 8–10%, and the average analyst target is 12.9% above the price.

Bear Case

The bear does not need the balance sheet to crack, only the earnings to stay down. China, BMW's largest single market, fell 24.8% in the third quarter after 30.2% in the second, and the reason — local brands selling more car for less — is structural, not cyclical. BMW has now cut its 2026 margin forecast twice in five months, and the 2028 interim target of 3–5% is below the 4–6% it guided for 2026 as recently as March, so the recovery has moved further away. S&P cut the credit rating to A− on November 5 with a negative outlook. Free cash flow was negative in the third quarter, so the above-€2.5B target needs about €1.8B in the fourth. Much of the "net cash" is lent to the group's own bank and backs a leasing book exposed to used-car prices, so it cannot all be paid out. The dividend is under review too: against the 7.4% trailing yield, BMW's usual 30–40% payout on the €5.68 FY2026 EPS estimate would be a 2.9–3.8% yield.

Top 3 Risks

01
China keeps falling. Q3 sales −24.8%, nine months −21.8%. Nedeljković on July 30: "The rapid deterioration of the market in China was the main trigger that forced us to adjust our guidance in June." Another leg down would test the above-2% margin BMW still expects before restructuring charges and question the 2028 interim target.
02
The restructuring costs more or delivers less. The charge has already doubled to about 2.5 points of margin, all of it due in the fourth quarter; the savings come later, and further decisions are not due until spring 2027. Volkswagen and Mercedes-Benz are cutting at the same time.
03
Trade policy and the Middle East. Tariffs already cost about 1.25 margin points a year. The Middle East conflict keeps energy and raw-material costs high and, BMW says, continues to affect consumer behaviour in numerous markets around the world.
Industry Commentary
China: from profit engine to a market to defend
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The numbers BMW's CFO gave on June 16 tell the story of 2026: in December 2025 the China Passenger Car Association expected the market to be flat; by mid-June it forecast −14.3%, and actual sales were down 19.4% through May. He said BMW had sold about 50,000 cars a month in China through 2025 and into 2026, then saw volumes fall 10% in the first quarter and 17.6% through May. Chinese brands are also selling more in other Asia-Pacific markets, which BMW says is spreading the pressure beyond China. The third quarter was no better: a Chinese market down 24.6% and BMW down 24.8%. BMW's answer is to become more Chinese: more locally built and locally developed models, exports of China-built cars to Southeast Asia under review, and price discipline over volume.

Germany's carmakers restructure at the same time
ONGOING HEADWIND

BMW's June warning was followed by Volkswagen's on September 18: an operating margin of at most 1% for 2026, down from 4.0–5.5%, with about €10B of one-off costs, mostly at Porsche, including a roughly €6B goodwill write-down (Reuters). Over twelve months Mercedes-Benz lost 28.6% and Volkswagen preference shares 24.8%. All three groups face the same mix — China shrinking, US tariffs, high German labour and energy costs — and all three are cutting fixed costs together. That can restore margins across the industry, or it can turn Europe into the market where everyone discounts to keep plants full. BMW enters the cuts with €42.4B of automotive net financial assets and a commitment to keep its "single A" credit rating, though S&P moved it to A− on November 5.

Electric cars that earn their keep
EMERGING TAILWIND

The Neue Klasse is BMW's bet that a new electronics architecture and a cheaper sixth-generation battery can make electric cars as profitable as petrol ones. The early evidence is good: the iX3, built at BMW's new plant in Debrecen, Hungary, launched in Europe in March; the CFO said on June 16 that the lower Gen6 battery-pack costs would support margins; and at the Capital Market Day BMW said the iX3, delivered since March 2026, had reached margin parity with combustion models in key markets. The 3 Series with its electric i3 version launched in October, and the technology is being rolled out across more than 40 new or revised models by the end of 2027. If it works, BMW's electric growth in Europe (+37.5% in Q3) adds margin instead of diluting it — the one part of the 2026 story that is going better than planned.
