01 Company Overview

Bristol-Myers Squibb discovers, develops, manufactures and sells prescription medicines across cancer, blood disorders, immunology, heart disease and — since the Karuna acquisition — neuroscience. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $48.19B, essentially flat year on year (−0.2% reported, −1% excluding currency). In the first nine months of 2026 revenue was $37.53B, +5.2% YoY, which puts TTM revenue at about $50.03B. It ranks #88 in the S&P 500 by market value, and it sits inside a 21-company "Drug Manufacturers — General" peer group with $4.61T of combined market value and $799.6B of combined revenue (StockAnalysis, market values at the October 30, 2026 close) — of which BMY is about 6.3% of revenue but only 2.8% of market value.

That gap is the entire story. Management reports the business in two buckets: a Growth Portfolio (Opdivo and the under-the-skin Opdivo Qvantig, Reblozyl, Breyanzi, Camzyos, Sotyktu, Krazati, Opdualag, Zeposia, Abecma, Cobenfy and others) that generated $26.41B in FY2025, +17% YoY, and a Legacy Portfolio (Eliquis, Revlimid, Pomalyst, Sprycel and older brands) that generated $21.79B, −15% YoY. In Q3 2026, reported on October 29, the Growth Portfolio rose 12% to $7.66B and Legacy rose 1% to $5.42B, held up by Eliquis (+25%) while the older cancer drugs kept losing sales to generics. The market prices BMY at 13.9× trailing earnings (P/E) and ~9.7× the midpoint of FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance because it doubts the Growth Portfolio can outrun what is coming for Eliquis and Opdivo.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
PATENT ESTATE IO FRANCHISE SCALE CELL-THERAPY MANUFACTURING 70.5% GROSS MARGIN $9.3B FREE CASH FLOW GLOBAL REGULATORY REACH SUBCUTANEOUS REFORMULATION

BMS's moat is the ordinary drugmaker's moat — time-limited monopolies granted by patents, protected by the cost and time of getting a drug approved, and paid for by very high profit on every pack sold — and the numbers show how powerful that is while it lasts. Gross margin is 70.50%, operating margin 32.35%, ROE 39.48% and ROIC 23.45%, on $9.29B of trailing free cash flow. Two elements are more durable than the patent estate itself. The first is manufacturing: Breyanzi and Abecma are made from each individual patient's own cells, an operational skill that generic and biosimilar makers cannot simply copy from a molecule sheet. The second is reformulation: Opdivo Qvantig moves intravenous Opdivo patients to an under-the-skin injection with its own, later exclusivity clock — $334M in Q3 2026, up more than 200%, an annual pace above $1.3B. Every patient switched before the intravenous form loses exclusivity is revenue that does not vanish on the cliff date. That is the moat BMS is building rather than merely defending.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. Concentration in two expiring assets. Eliquis ($14,443M FY2025) and Opdivo ($10,049M FY2025) together are 50.8% of company revenue and both lose exclusivity this decade. The Growth Portfolio grew 17% in FY2025 off a $26.41B base — roughly $4.5B of extra revenue a year. Replacing $24.5B therefore takes five-plus years of uninterrupted 15–17% growth, and that assumes nothing in the Growth Portfolio itself stumbles.

2. US drug pricing policy is now a dated headwind, not just a risk. Per the FY2025 10-K: Eliquis became subject to the Inflation Reduction Act's maximum fair price on January 1, 2026; Pomalyst follows on January 1, 2027; Orencia was selected for negotiation beginning 2028. That is three of BMS's largest products under government-set Medicare prices inside three years. On top of that, under a December 2025 agreement with the US government on most-favoured-nation pricing (tying US prices to lower prices abroad), BMS gives Eliquis free to Medicaid from January 1, 2026 and sells five drugs to cash-paying patients at about 80% off list price; in return it gets some US tariff relief until January 2029 and will not be subject to future US pricing mandates (FY2025 10-K).

3. Buying pipeline has been expensive, and most of the payoff is still in trials. BMS expensed $13.373B of acquired IPR&D in 2024, $3.721B in 2025 and $1.651B in the first nine months of 2026, $1.558B of it in Q3. The flagship asset from that spending, Cobenfy, is selling at about $230M a year. The newest deals — the Hengrui collaboration (13 early-stage assets; a $600M upfront paid in Q3 2026, a $175M payment due in 2027 and up to $14.3B in milestones, per the Q3 10-Q), a $500M anniversary payment to BioNTech and a $250M SystImmune milestone — are early-stage or still in trials, so they cannot add much revenue before the cliff arrives.

4. Debt limits the response. Total debt was $42.88B against $12.96B of cash and marketable securities at September 30, 2026 — net debt of $29.92B (Q3 release), about 1.6× trailing EBITDA of $19.05B (StockAnalysis). Debt-to-equity is 1.83. A company that needs to buy its way out of a patent cliff has less balance-sheet room to do so than the rivals it bids against, and it also pays a 4.03% dividend it is unlikely to cut.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Acquired IPR&D is the dominant distortion, and it is enormous. BMS expensed $13.373B of acquired in-process R&D in FY2024 — principally the Karuna transaction — which turned an otherwise profitable year into a GAAP net loss of $8,948M and GAAP EPS of −$4.41. FY2025 acquired IPR&D was $3.721B ($1.393B of it in Q4, mainly for the Orbital Therapeutics acquisition), leaving GAAP EPS of $3.46 against non-GAAP EPS of $6.15. In the first nine months of 2026 it was $1,651M versus $2,328M a year earlier — and $1,558M of that landed in Q3, from the Hengrui upfront and anniversary payment, a BioNTech anniversary payment and a SystImmune milestone (Q3 10-Q). BMS's non-GAAP EPS includes these charges too: Q3's $1.35 carried a $(0.58) hit, which is why it fell from $1.63 a year earlier. Anyone comparing BMY's earnings across years is comparing noise. Look at earnings before acquired R&D, or at free cash flow.

IPR&D impairments. Separate from acquired IPR&D expense, BMS wrote down $830M of research programmes in the first nine months of 2026, none of it in Q3 (Q3 release). These writedowns recur often enough at BMS that treating them as strictly one-off is generous.

Restructuring. The 2023 restructuring plan carries ~$2.5B of total expected charges through 2027, of which $1.9B had been incurred by September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q); it carries the exit costs of a productivity programme that targets about $2.0B of cost savings by the end of 2027 (FY2025 10-K), which CFO David Elkins said on the Q3 call is on track. FY2026 operating expense guidance stayed at ~$16.5B in October.

Eliquis and the IRA. Eliquis has been under maximum fair pricing since January 1, 2026, yet it grew 25% in Q3 2026, and CFO David Elkins guided to 20–25% growth for the full year. He also said fourth-quarter Eliquis sales will be hit by the coming loss of exclusivity in Europe. Pomalyst's maximum fair price starts January 1, 2027 and Orencia's negotiated price applies from 2028. Treat 2026 Eliquis growth as temporary, not a trend.

Business development. BMS paid the $600M Hengrui upfront in Q3 2026 and booked it, with a $175M payment due in 2027, as acquired IPR&D, against up to $14.3B of potential milestones across 13 early-stage assets (Q3 10-Q). With the $500M BioNTech and $250M SystImmune payments, that is most of Q3's $1,558M charge, and it is why the FY2026 non-GAAP EPS range fell even as revenue guidance rose.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The Growth Portfolio has already crossed over: $26.41B in FY2025 growing 17%, and $7.66B (+12%) in Q3 2026 — about 59% of revenue. Camzyos +42%, Breyanzi +46%, Reblozyl +22% and Opdivo Qvantig +200%-plus are real, growing franchises with long exclusivity runways, and Qvantig defuses part of the Opdivo cliff by moving patients to a later-expiring form. October brought a string of positive late-stage results (iberdomide, admilparant, iza-bren). Meanwhile the stock trades at 13.9× trailing and ~9.7× guided non-GAAP earnings against a 36.92× peer group, throws off $9.29B of free cash flow (7.3% of its market value) and pays a 4.03% dividend covered about 1.8 times. Management raised its FY2026 revenue range in July and again in October. If the cliff is merely severe rather than catastrophic, the valuation has room to double.

Bear Case

Eliquis ($14.4B) plus Opdivo ($10.0B) is 50.8% of FY2025 revenue facing loss of exclusivity this decade, and the CFO expects the European loss of Eliquis exclusivity to hit sales this quarter. The Growth Portfolio adds only about $4.5B a year — five-plus years of flawless growth just to stand still. Consensus already models revenue falling 2.9% in 2027, before the worst of it. The $13.4B of 2024 acquired R&D bought Cobenfy, which slipped to $57M in Q3 (about $230M a year), with its randomised Alzheimer’s-psychosis results not due until 2027; milvexian’s atrial fibrillation readout comes in Q1 2027. Eliquis is under IRA maximum fair pricing from January 2026, Pomalyst from 2027, Orencia from 2028. With $42.88B of debt and 1.83× debt-to-equity, the balance sheet cannot easily fund a rescue acquisition of the size needed. A low price on a shrinking base is not value — it is arithmetic.

01
The Eliquis cliff is unhedged and it is 30% of the company. The Hengrui deal ($600M upfront, up to $14.3B in milestones, 13 early-stage assets) points the right way but is far too early-stage to bridge 2028–2030.
  Industry Commentary
The patent cliff arithmetic facing big pharma
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Large drugmakers as a group face a wall of patent expiries late this decade, and BMY's version is unusually concentrated. Its two largest products — Eliquis at $14,443M and Opdivo at $10,049M in FY2025 — are 50.8% of revenue and both expire this decade. The offsetting engine is real but too small: the Growth Portfolio's FY2025 +17% on a $26.41B base is roughly $4.5B of extra revenue a year, while the Legacy Portfolio sheds roughly $3.3B a year at its FY2025 −15% rate. Together that produced the flat FY2025 (−0.2%) and the +5.2% of the first nine months of 2026. The problem is that today's Legacy decline is cushioned by an Eliquis that is still growing; when Eliquis turns down, the decline speeds up sharply while the Growth Portfolio's contribution grows only steadily. Consensus already sees this: revenue down 2.9% in 2027 (StockAnalysis, October 30, 2026).
US drug pricing policy and tariffs
ONGOING HEADWIND
The Inflation Reduction Act has moved from theory to dates for BMS. Per the FY2025 10-K: Eliquis under maximum fair pricing since January 1, 2026; Pomalyst from January 1, 2027; Orencia ($3,705M FY2025) selected for negotiation beginning 2028. Those three products had $20.9B of FY2025 sales. On top of that comes BMS's December 2025 agreement with the US government on most-favoured-nation pricing (tying US prices to lower prices abroad): free Eliquis for Medicaid and about 80% off list price for cash-paying patients on five drugs, in return for some tariff relief until January 2029 and no future US pricing mandates (FY2025 10-K). What follows when the tariff relief ends is unknown — and risk that cannot be measured is part of why a business with a 70.5% gross margin trades at 13.9× earnings while its peer group trades at 36.92×.
Does a low price compensate for a shrinking base?
THE CENTRAL QUESTION
Bull framing: at 13.9× trailing and ~9.7× guided non-GAAP EPS, with a 7.3% free-cash-flow yield and a 4.03% dividend covered about 1.8 times, the market is pricing in a permanent decline. It only takes the Growth Portfolio holding double-digit growth — +17% in FY2025, +12% in Q3 2026 — plus one successful large label expansion (Cobenfy in Alzheimer's psychosis) or one clean milvexian readout for the permanent-decline assumption to break. A beta of 0.18 means you are paid to wait with very little market risk. Bear framing: a low share price for a company whose biggest drugs have fixed expiry dates is not a mistake waiting to be fixed — it can be the right price. Paying under 10 times 2026 earnings is no bargain if 2029 earnings are half of 2026's, and the two readouts that could change the trajectory (milvexian in atrial fibrillation, Cobenfy in Alzheimer's psychosis) are both 2027 events. The honest resolution: this is a bet on when and whether the 2027 readouts and new deals pay off, not a bet that a cheap stock gets less cheap.
