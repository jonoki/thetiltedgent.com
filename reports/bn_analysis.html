01 Company Overview

Brookfield Corporation is a holding company that owns the controlling stake in one of the world's largest alternative-asset managers, an insurance and annuity business, and a stack of listed and private operating businesses in infrastructure, renewable power, private equity and real estate. It ranks #10 in Canada and #285 globally by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap, Sep 21, 2026), trades 1:1 on the NYSE and the TSX (home index: the S&P/TSX Composite), and reports in US dollars under IFRS. The consolidated income statement is enormous and nearly useless for valuation: TTM consolidated revenue of $80.69B produced just $1.26B of net income available to common shareholders, because the accounts consolidate every controlled fund and subsidiary and then hand most of the profit to non-controlling interests.

So this report leads with the two numbers Brookfield itself asks to be judged on. First, distributable earnings (DE): $2.61 per share over the last twelve months to June 30, 2026 ($2.39 before realised carried interest), up 7% per share before realisations. Second, plan value — management's sum-of-the-parts estimate of intrinsic value — which it put at $67 per share at its September 17, 2026 Investor Day, showing a "~40% margin of safety" against the share price. At the $37.98 close the stock trades at 14.6x trailing DE and a 43% discount to management's own plan value. The premise supplied for this report — a holdco with an alternative-asset flywheel, best read through DE and the plan-value discount — is confirmed by the company's filings. The whole debate is whether that 43% gap is a mispricing or a fair haircut for complexity, leverage inside the structure and the fact that the $67 is management's number, not the market's.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

$672B fee-bearing capital $210B deployable capital Permanent insurance float ($191B) Operator of real assets, not just allocator Westinghouse nuclear franchise Oaktree credit platform Corporate debt: 15-yr avg term

The moat is scale of capital plus operating capability in the same building. Brookfield's own framing in the Q2 letter: a data-centre project needs power generation, transmission, land, financing and long-term customer contracts, and few organisations can deliver all of them. That lets it bid on deals that are rarely marketed — the $100B Kentucky AI factory, the Westinghouse reactor programme — with $210B of deployable capital behind it. The insurance float then supplies long-duration money that does not redeem when markets wobble. The vulnerability is the mirror image: a structure this interconnected is hard for outsiders to value, and the market prices that difficulty as a discount.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Plan value is a management estimate. About $44 of the $67 sits in private holdings valued by Brookfield itself — carried interest at 10x a target annual carry, insurance at 15x earnings. The market is paying roughly $15 for that $44. If the carry targets slip or insurance multiples compress, the "discount" narrows from the wrong side.
02
Leverage inside the structure. Consolidated debt is $268.9B (StockAnalysis), almost all of it non-recourse borrowings of managed entities ($250.3B) rather than corporate debt ($14.7B). Non-recourse debt is ring-fenced by design, but in a rate or credit shock the equity cushions in the underlying assets shrink first, and Brookfield's stake is that equity. The company priced another $600M of 5.650% notes due 2031 on September 21, 2026.
03
Private-credit and insurance spread risk. The Q2 letter directly addresses "the risks in private credit" and argues Brookfield's exposure is concentrated in real-asset, asset-backed and opportunistic credit rather than sponsor-backed direct lending. With Oaktree fully owned and $191B of insurance assets partly invested in Brookfield strategies, a credit cycle would hit both fee income and the spread business at once.
04
Complexity keeps the buyer base narrow. BN is not in the S&P 500. Management's own letter says the simplified "New BN" structure should "strengthen our eligibility for inclusion in major U.S. and global equity indices." Until that happens, passive demand is limited and the discount has no mechanical closer.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, realised carried interest is lumpy by nature — $121M in Q2 2026 and $520M LTM — which is why Brookfield reports DE both before and after realisations; the "before" figure ($2.39/share LTM) is the cleaner run-rate. Second, acquisitions changed the base: Just Group (closed April 2026, $45B of insurance assets) and the Oaktree buy-in (closed July 2026) raise Wealth Solutions and Asset Management earnings mechanically, so part of the +15% per-share DE growth in Q2 is purchased, not organic. Third, the IFRS line is swung by fair-value changes and disposition gains: the LTM bridge from net income to DE removes $10.7B of depreciation, $3.2B of equity-accounted fair-value changes and $1.8B of disposition gains before stripping out the non-controlling interests' share. That is why GAAP EPS nearly doubled YoY (+86%) while DE per share before realisations rose 7%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying $67 of management-estimated value for $37.98, and $31 of that $67 is listed securities marked at market. After corporate debt, the market is paying about $15 per share for $44 of private holdings: carried interest, a $191B insurance platform growing earnings at over 20%, real estate in recovery and direct investments. DE per share before realisations grew 15% in Q2. Fee-bearing capital is up 19% to $672B. Management has bought back stock year to date at an average $42, ~37% below plan value, and the simplification could open the door to index inclusion. At 14.6x trailing DE, and 5.3x management's 2031 DE target, the stock needs only partial delivery of the plan to rerate. The momentum washout (monthly RSI 29.0, a five-year low) coincides with the most detailed value disclosure the company has made.

Bear Case

Holding-company discounts can last for decades, and this one exists for reasons. The $67 is Brookfield marking its own homework: carried interest valued at 10x a target, insurance at 15x, real estate on internal DCFs. Consolidated debt is $268.9B. Most of it is non-recourse, but it sits beneath the equity Brookfield owns. Private credit is under scrutiny just as Oaktree becomes wholly owned and the insurance book grows on Brookfield-managed assets. IFRS EPS of $0.54 gives a 70x P/E, and the stock trades below the sell-side average target of $54.40, itself well below $67. The DE growth rate is partly bought (Just Group, Oaktree) rather than organic. The market's verdict over the past year: −20.7% while the company reported record numbers.

Top 3 Risks

01
The discount does not close. The investment case rests on the market eventually valuing the private holdings closer to Brookfield's own marks. Without a mechanical catalyst such as index inclusion, a big realisation cycle or a simpler structure, the discount can persist while per-share value compounds underneath.
02
Credit and spread cycle. A private-credit downturn would hit Oaktree fee and carry income, the Wealth Solutions spread ($191B of insurance assets, 2.2% North American gross spread) and asset values under $250B of non-recourse debt at the same time. High beta (1.82) says the market already treats BN as a levered bet on these conditions.
03
Plan execution and carry timing. Management's five-year plan needs DE before realisations to more than double to $11.6B and carried-interest realisations to scale from $520M LTM. If realisations slip, the 24% total-DE CAGR target falls back towards the 17% core rate, and the $140 2031 plan value moves out with it.
Industry Commentary
Private credit under scrutiny
KEY DEBATE

The Q2 2026 shareholder letter spends a full section on it: "Much has been written lately about the risks in private credit. Concerns center on direct lending to sponsor-led, private-equity-backed borrowers and exposure to software businesses." Brookfield argues its book is concentrated in real-asset finance, asset-backed lending and opportunistic credit, and cites redemption requests at Oaktree Strategic Credit Fund falling below 5% "even as many others saw redemptions climb into the high double digits." That is a claim worth testing each quarter. The company calls the current moment "a healthy adjustment", not "a systemic problem", but for a firm that has just taken full ownership of Oaktree the claim will be checked in its own numbers.

Retirement money opening to private assets
EMERGING TAILWIND

Brookfield's letter points to U.S. federal policy changes "clearing a path" for private assets in defined-contribution plans, a pool it sizes at about $14 trillion, and to a collaboration with AllianceBernstein on custom DC solutions. Its insurance arm is the other channel into retirement money: annuity sales of about $5B a quarter, and a target of $375B of insurance assets by 2031. If even a small share of DC assets moves into private strategies, it is a new source of permanent capital for the whole alternative-asset industry. Brookfield is positioned on both sides, as manager and as insurer.

AI and power: the capital-intensity trade
ONGOING THEME

Brookfield's three stated megatrends are digitalisation, decarbonisation and deglobalisation. AI infrastructure touches all three: data centres need power, power needs generation and grid, and governments want it built domestically. Brookfield's exposure runs from a $100B Kentucky AI-factory project with the U.S. government to Westinghouse (the letter says its technology is used by over half of the world's operating reactors) and a $25B Bloom Energy fuel-cell framework. Per the Q2 call summary, an Nvidia compute-financing platform is at MOU stage. The bull reading is that these deals are too large for most competitors. The bear reading is that they are capital-intensive, long-dated and exposed to the durability of AI capex spending.
