01 Company Overview

BNP Paribas is Europe's largest bank by assets — $3.279 trillion on S&P Global's April 2026 ranking — and the sixth-largest company in the CAC 40 by index weight. It is a universal bank in the full sense: a wholesale bank for corporations and investors, retail banks in four "domestic markets" (France, Belgium, Italy and Luxembourg), consumer credit and car leasing across Europe, and a savings arm that runs insurance, wealth management and, since the July 2025 purchase of AXA Investment Managers, one of Europe's largest asset managers. FY2025 revenue was €51.2B (+4.9%), net income €12.2B (+4.6%) and EPS €10.29, with a RoTE of 11.6%.

The latest print is the second quarter of 2026, published July 23, 2026. Revenue rose 12.0% to €14.1B — in the CEO's words on the call, "a level we hadn't seen for a decade" — led by a record quarter in equity trading. Net income jumped 33.4% to €4.35B, beating the €4.21B average of 15 analyst estimates compiled by the bank (Reuters), though €858M of that was a one-off gain on the reworked Ageas insurance partnership. The bigger news was capital: the CET1 ratio reached 13.0%, the target BNP had set for the end of 2027, eighteen months early. Management's message since is about what comes next: a 2030 strategic plan due on February 2, 2027, a payout that "could" rise from 60% to 70% of earnings, and a yearly board decision on whether capital above 13% goes to shareholders or into investment.

The share price tells a two-part story. From a €57.90 month-end in October 2021 the stock climbed to €109.66 at the end of July 2026 and an intraday high of €113.82 on August 13. Since then it has given back 19.1% to €92.06 — €3.23 of that is the interim dividend, which went ex on September 24 — with no new results or guidance from the company since July. The rest came as France's political and fiscal worries pushed the extra yield on French government bonds over German ones to its highest since 2012, and as a global bond sell-off hit European banks on October 1. At 0.94x tangible book and under 8x trailing earnings, the market is pricing BNP more as a French sovereign proxy than as the diversified European bank management describes.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Diversified by business and country EMEA wholesale-banking leadership €2.59T savings & asset platform Four domestic retail franchises Cross-selling (one-third of revenue) Liquidity: LCR 149%, €521B reserve

BNP's advantage is breadth that feeds itself. Management says cross-selling between its businesses accounts for one third of revenue: the corporate client of the wholesale bank uses Securities Services, its employees bank with the retail network, and their savings flow into Cardif and the asset manager. That mix is also a hedge — when trading slowed, rates lifted retail; when rates fall, fees and markets usually carry more. On the call the CFO put the France question in one line: "I remind you, we have less than 10% of our profit before tax in France." Scale shows up in the balance sheet too: a 149% liquidity coverage ratio and €521B of immediately available liquidity at June 30, 2026.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
France's political and fiscal risk. Whatever the profit mix, the market treats BNP as a French bank. On August 27, 2026 the shares fell 4.8% close-to-close as French bank stocks were hit by political uncertainty ahead of the 2027 presidential election. By late September the gap between French and German 10-year yields was above 110bps, its highest since the 2012 euro-zone crisis, and credit-default swaps on French banks were at their highest since April 2025 (Reuters, Sep 25, 2026). Scope downgraded France in September, and Reuters reported Moody's could follow in late October.
02
The Sudan litigation. On October 17, 2025 a Manhattan federal jury found BNP liable to three Sudanese plaintiffs who testified about abuses under former President Omar al-Bashir's rule, awarding them a combined $20.5 million (Reuters). The plaintiffs' lawyers said the ruling opened the door for more than 20,000 Sudanese refugees in the US to seek damages; the shares fell 7.7% close-to-close on the next session, October 20. BNP is appealing to the US Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit (opening brief filed May 22, 2026), backed by amicus briefs from the governments of Switzerland and the United States, and says the trial court "fundamentally misconstrued Swiss law".
03
Costs still high for its returns. A 59.3% cost-income ratio in 1H26 is well above where management wants it (below 56% in 2028). On results day Jefferies expected the market to focus on "the perceived cost 'miss'" but suspected BNP "used the strong revenue result to front-load AXA IM integration costs". The 2027–2030 plan leans on a support-functions overhaul and AI to close the gap, against an addressable cost base of about €15B.
04
Pockets of rate and car-market exposure. Italy's BNL sells fixed-rate mortgages; asked about its margins, the CEO said: "It had some advantage in the previous cycle. It's a major complexity in the current cycle." Arval's used-car revenue is hostage to second-hand prices, which fell from March, and the Athlon deal enlarges that exposure.

BNP flags "exceptional items" itself. 1H26 carried +€870M of them after tax (+€657M pre-tax): the €858M gain on the Ageas/AGI transaction (closed April 28, 2026), a €372M revaluation of an equity stake (Allfunds) and a €168M revaluation in insurance, against €320M of restructuring and adaptation costs, €72M of IT reinforcement costs and a €219M legal-risk charge on financial instruments. RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

BNP is earning a 13.3% RoTE, already above its 12% target for 2026, and hit its 13% CET1 goal eighteen months early — so from 2027 the board decides each year whether surplus capital goes back to shareholders, on top of a payout of at least 60% (possibly 70% under the next plan), or into investment. Revenue grew 12% in 2Q26, retail banks in France and Belgium are compounding on deposit reinvestment, AXA IM gives it scale in asset management, and management targets double-digit EPS growth to 2028 with a 15% RoTE ambition for 2030. At 0.94x tangible book, under 8x earnings and a 6.3% trailing dividend yield, the market is paying little for that — and every one of the 19 targets on MarketScreener's page is above the price.

Bear Case

The discount is about France, and France is getting worse: the OAT–Bund spread is at a 2012-era high, a sovereign downgrade could follow, and analysts quoted by Reuters warn that a 2027 run-off between Marine Le Pen and Jean-Luc Mélenchon could hit French markets hard — whatever BNP's profit mix. The cost-income ratio is still near 60%, Arval and BNL are dragging, 2Q26 profit leaned on a one-off €858M gain, and the Sudan appeal is an open-ended legal tail. The chart has turned: three down months, the price below its 3- and 10-month averages, and a 19% drawdown from the August high.

Top 3 Risks

01

Industry Commentary
French banks trade on the sovereign
ACTIVE HEADWIND

A bank's funding cost and capital value are tied to its home government's credit, because banks hold their sovereign's bonds and markets assume the state stands behind them. Since August, French bank shares have moved with France's politics: on August 27 BNP closed 4.8% lower as investors weighed higher funding costs and weaker French growth ahead of the 2027 presidential election (TT News Agency). By late September the French–German 10-year spread was above 110bps and French bank CDS were at their highest since April 2025 (Reuters). BNP's answer is its mix — less than 10% of pre-tax profit in France, by the CFO's count — but share prices have not drawn that distinction.

Rates and the deposit tailwind
ONGOING TAILWIND

For euro-zone retail banks, the rate cycle is still paying out. BNP reinvests non-remunerated sight deposits "on the mid- to long-term end of the curve", in the CEO's words, and its rate-sensitive businesses (euro-zone commercial banks and Personal Finance) grew revenue 8.2% in 2Q26; management expects that environment "to extend well into our next strategic plan, taking us through to 2030". The flip side showed on October 1: when government bond yields jump to multi-year highs, bank shares sell off on valuation and credit worries even if income benefits.

Capital return as the valuation lever
KEY DEBATE

BNP trades at 0.94x tangible book against a peer median of 1.55x book, after spending capital on AXA IM and Athlon and building CET1 to its 13% target. With the target reached, the emphasis shifts: the CEO says anything above 13% "will be considered either for additional distribution, or additional investment, to be decided by the Board". Whether the 2030 plan in February commits to the 70% payout he floated is the main stock-specific catalyst; whether France lets the market price it is the main macro one.
