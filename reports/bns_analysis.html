01 Company Overview

Scotiabank's history in one sentence: chartered in Halifax in 1832, run today from 40 Temperance Street, Toronto, and for three decades the Canadian bank that bet on Latin America instead of the United States. It ranks #5 by market capitalisation in Canada (behind Royal Bank, TD, Shopify and BMO, per companiesmarketcap's country ranking) and #196 globally, at a $114.53B market value on the NYSE line. The shares trade 1:1 on the NYSE and the TSX; the S&P/TSX Composite is its home-market index.

The premise supplied for this report — "registered head office in Halifax, operational HQ Toronto" — is half verified. Toronto is confirmed as the executive address (company profile, results-release dateline) and Halifax as the 1832 founding city; a registered head office in Halifax is widely stated but was not confirmed on any page fetched this session, so this report does not assert it.

The story that matters is return on equity. For years Scotiabank earned the lowest ROE of the big Canadian banks, and its stock traded like it: the NYSE line fell from $72.34 (Feb 2022) to $40.47 (Oct 2023). Under CEO Scott Thomson (since February 2023) the bank has been pivoting capital from Latin America to North America: it bought a 14.9% stake in US regional bank KeyCorp for about US$2.8B (closed December 2024), filed in March 2026 to lift that to as much as 19.99%, and in Q1 FY2026 completed the sale of its Colombia, Costa Rica and Panama banking operations, booking a C$434M loss (C$377M after tax). The payoff showed up in Q3 FY2026 (May–Jul 2026): reported ROE of 14.1% (adjusted 14.2%) versus 12.2% a year earlier, clearing management's 14% medium-term target for the first time. On a trailing basis the gap to peers is still open — 11.46% TTM ROE for BNS versus 16.21% at Royal Bank and 12.83% at TD (stockanalysis) — and the stock has already run +46.85% in 52 weeks. The question is how much of the re-rating is left.

Currency: Scotiabank reports in Canadian dollars; the NYSE line trades in US dollars. Every income-statement figure on this page is C$ unless marked; every price, market cap, per-share valuation and USD EPS is US$, converted at the Bank of Canada rate of 1.4021 CAD per USD (0.7132 USD per CAD) for September 21, 2026. Earnings are generated in Canada, the US, Mexico and Latin America, so a weaker Canadian dollar and weaker Latin American currencies both reduce the USD value of the same earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Canadian big-bank oligopoly Low-cost deposit funding 19.4% domestic segment ROE KeyCorp option on US scale CET1 13.1% capital buffer C$474B wealth AUM

A Canadian bank's moat is structural: a concentrated domestic market in which five or six banks hold the deposits, the branch networks and the payment rails, and in which a customer's chequing account, mortgage and investments tend to stay at one institution for decades. Scotiabank's distinguishing choice has been where it put the rest of its capital. For thirty years that meant Latin America — higher margins, higher growth, and also higher loan losses, currency volatility and political risk, which is why the market paid less for each dollar of Scotiabank earnings. The KeyCorp stake is the other direction: a minority position in a US regional bank that gives exposure to US deposit and lending growth without paying a control premium, and with an option to grow it (the March 2026 filing to go up to 19.99%).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Credit costs are rising, not falling. PCL of C$1,079M in Q3 was 56 bps of loans, up from 51 bps a year earlier, and gross impaired loans grew quarter on quarter. The ROE improvement has come despite credit, not because of it. A Canadian consumer slowdown or a Latin American recession would hit this line first.
02
A minority stake is not control. 14.9% of KeyCorp (the most recent verified figure; a move toward 19.99% was filed in March 2026) earns an equity-accounted share of profit — C$77M in Q2 FY2026 — but Scotiabank does not run KeyCorp's balance sheet. KeyCorp's spokesperson said the larger stake "does not indicate any changes in the nature of the relationship between the two banks."
03
The remaining Latin America book still carries currency and political risk. The exits so far were the smaller Central American and Colombian operations; International Banking still earned C$766M in Q3, a quarter of the four lines' profit, and those earnings are translated into Canadian dollars at whatever the local currencies are worth.
04
Capital is being run down. CET1 fell 20 bps in one quarter to 13.1% as buybacks accelerated. That is a choice that lifts ROE — but it leaves a thinner buffer if credit costs rise further.

Notable One-Off Items

One item dominates: the C$434M pre-tax (C$377M after-tax) loss recognised in Q1 FY2026 on completing the sale of the Colombia, Costa Rica and Panama banking operations. It sits inside the trailing EPS of C$7.66 and the 11.46% TTM ROE, which therefore understate the run-rate that Q3's 14.1% ROE shows. Year-to-date reported net income was C$7,884M (diluted EPS C$6.00) versus C$5,552M (C$4.02) in the first nine months of FY2025. In Q3 itself, reported and adjusted results were close (C$2,953M vs C$2,973M; diluted EPS C$2.27 vs C$2.28), so adjustments are not doing heavy lifting in the latest quarter. Separately, the KeyCorp stake contributed C$77M to Q2 FY2026 net income (C$85M adjusted) as Scotiabank's share of KeyCorp's quarterly profit.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The turnaround is working and the trailing numbers still hide it. Q3 FY2026 ROE of 14.1% cleared the 14% target, Canadian Banking earns 19.4%, the productivity ratio is improving (52.7% from 53.6%), and the Q1 divestiture loss that weighs on the 11.46% TTM ROE falls out of the trailing figures next year. If ROE converges toward Royal Bank's 16%, a P/B of 1.77x against Royal Bank's 2.72x leaves room to re-rate, while a 3.37% yield and ongoing buybacks pay holders to wait. The KeyCorp stake, possibly heading to 19.99%, gives a growing US earnings stream without a control premium.

Bear Case

The re-rating has already happened: +46.85% in a year, a monthly RSI of 88.6, and a price 3.9% above the average target from 14 analysts whose consensus is Hold. Credit is moving the wrong way — PCL 56 bps against 51 bps a year earlier, impaired loans rising — while CET1 is falling as buybacks accelerate. One quarter at 14% ROE is not a run-rate, the fastest-growing line (Global Banking & Markets, +37%) is the most cyclical, and International Banking still leaves a quarter of earnings exposed to Latin American credit and currency.

Top 3 Risks

01
Credit cycle. PCL of C$1,079M (56 bps) in Q3 and rising gross impaired loans (C$7,801M). A turn in Canadian consumer credit or a Latin American downturn hits earnings before anything else.
02
Valuation and momentum already priced in. The stock trades above the average analyst target with a stretched monthly RSI. A good Q4 may be needed just to hold the price.
03
Currency translation. Earnings are in C$ and Latin American currencies; the NYSE line is in US$. At 1.4021 CAD per USD, each 5% move in the Canadian dollar changes the US$ value of the same C$ earnings by about 5%.
Industry Commentary
ROE convergence among the Canadian banks
EMERGING TAILWIND

On stockanalysis's trailing figures, Royal Bank earns 16.21% on equity, TD 12.83% and Scotiabank 11.46% — and the price-to-book ratios line up in the same order (2.72x, 2.26x, 1.77x). The market pays for ROE. Scotiabank's Q3 FY2026 print (14.1% reported) is the first evidence that it can close part of the gap, and the mechanism is visible: a domestic bank at 19.4% ROE, capital moving out of lower-return Central American markets, and buybacks shrinking the equity base. The next two quarters decide whether 14% is a level or a spike.

North America over Latin America
STRATEGIC SHIFT

For decades Scotiabank's pitch was growth from Latin America. Under Scott Thomson the pitch is return on capital from North America: a 14.9% KeyCorp stake (US$2.8B, closed December 2024) with a March 2026 filing to go to 19.99%, and the exit from Colombia, Costa Rica and Panama completed in Q1 FY2026 at a C$434M loss. Mexico and the remaining Latin American operations stay in International Banking, which earned C$766M in Q3 at a 15.7% ROE. The trade-off is growth for predictability — the market has so far rewarded it.

Credit costs as the swing factor
WATCH ITEM

Everything above assumes provisions stay near current levels. Scotiabank's Q3 PCL ratio was 56 bps, up from 51 bps a year earlier, and gross impaired loans grew quarter on quarter to C$7,801M, with credit-loss allowances at C$7.55B (per The Deep Dive). Revenue growth of 11.1% currently outruns that drift. If it did not, the ROE story would stall regardless of strategy.
