01 Company Overview

BNY is not a lender. It is the plumbing underneath the world's capital markets — a custodian, fund administrator, corporate trustee, clearer and collateral manager that holds or oversees $62.6 trillion in assets under custody and/or administration (AUC/A) and manages a further $2.2 trillion in assets under management (AUM) as of June 30, 2026. That is roughly 560× its own $111.84B market capitalisation: the balance sheet is a rounding error against the asset base it services. FY2025 revenue was $20.11B, up 8.4% on FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $21.42B, up 11.1%. Founded in 1784 by Alexander Hamilton's syndicate, it is the oldest bank in the United States and currently the world's #211 company by market capitalisation.

The central debate on this page is whether the last two years are a re-rating or a rate cycle. BNY has gone from a $38 stock in September 2022 to $164.84 — a +178% five-year run — while ROTCE climbed to 31.3% and the pre-tax margin to 39.8%. Bulls say the platforms reorganisation, the commercial model and AI-driven expense discipline have permanently changed the operating leverage of a business that spent a decade as a value trap. Bears say a fee business sitting on a very large, very cheap deposit base has just enjoyed the best net interest income environment in fifteen years, and the P/E has gone from single digits to 19×. Section 04 and Section 07 take that apart.

02 Core Businesses

BNY reports three operating segments plus an "Other" bucket. The mix below is the reported Q2 2026 segment revenue split ($5.667B of segment revenue against $5.698B of total consolidated revenue; the gap is eliminations and rounding). Before the segments, the single most important number on this page: fee revenue was $4.036B of the $5.698B quarter — 70.8% — against net interest income of $1.446B, 25.4%. For the full year 2025 the split was $14.4B fees / $4.9B NII, or 71.6% / 24.4%. This is a fee business with a bank attached, not the other way round.

Why rate cuts cut both ways here, in one sentence: lower rates shrink the spread BNY earns on its very large, very cheap client deposit base (NII falls), but they simultaneously stop the money-market fee waivers that force the asset-management arm to hand back fees when yields approach zero, and they typically lift trading, issuance and client transaction activity that the fee lines are paid on — so the two halves of the P&L lean against each other rather than moving together.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
$62.6T AUC/A scale Switching cost / operational lock-in Cheap non-interest-bearing deposits US Treasury clearing infrastructure G-SIB regulatory barrier Pershing wealth-platform network 241-year franchise / counterparty trust

The core advantage is not brand and it is not technology — it is that BNY is embedded in its clients' operating and regulatory machinery in a way that makes leaving genuinely expensive. A custody conversion for a large asset manager means re-plumbing NAV calculation, trade settlement, corporate actions, tax reclaims, regulatory reporting and collateral movements simultaneously, across dozens of markets, without a single settlement failure. Clients do it for strategy, almost never for price. That is why fee revenue grew 11% YoY in a quarter when the fee-per-asset rate is under permanent pressure: the assets keep compounding and the relationship keeps renewing. Layered on top is a funding advantage that no fintech can replicate — custody clients leave operating cash at BNY as a by-product of transacting, which is how a fee business ends up with a 1.45% NIM and $1.446B of quarterly NII it does not have to underwrite credit risk to earn. Finally, being a designated globally systemic bank is a cost, but it is also a wall: the capital, resolution-planning and operational-resilience burden makes the business practically un-enterable.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Fee compression is permanent and one-directional. Basis points charged per dollar of custodied asset have fallen for two decades and there is no mechanism by which they rise. BNY grows fee revenue by growing assets faster than the rate falls — which works while markets compound, and stops working the moment they do not. A flat market year would expose how much of the 11% fee growth is price and how much is beta.

2. Deposit balances are borrowed, not owned. The cheap deposit base is client operating cash that sits at BNY incidentally. It can be swept into money-market funds, Treasury bills or repo in days if the spread justifies it. Management noted deposit margin compression in Q2 2026 even while non-interest-bearing balances stayed flat — the balances held, the economics on them did not.

3. The rate cycle giveth. NII is guided to grow 12–13% in 2026, and it is the single largest contributor to the margin expansion that has driven the re-rating. A meaningful easing cycle removes that contribution. The fee-waiver relief and activity pickup described above offset part of it — but only part, and with a lag.

4. Capital ratios are being spent, not built. CET1 fell from 11.9% at year-end 2025 to 11.0% at June 30, 2026, and the Tier 1 leverage ratio from 6.0% to 5.9%, while the company ran an 87% total payout ratio. That is a deliberate, shareholder-friendly choice. It is also a buffer that only depletes in one direction, and it narrows the room available if a stress-test outcome, a regulatory change or a credit event demands capital back.

Notable One-Off Items. FY2025 carried roughly $90M of combined notable items — severance, litigation reserve adjustments and FDIC special-assessment effects — which is small against $20.1B of revenue and does not materially distort the trend. Two structural items matter more than any accounting one-off. First, guidance is explicitly given excluding notable items (revenue +10–11%, expenses +6–7%, ~400bps of positive operating leverage), so the reported GAAP path will differ. Second, the expense guide includes roughly $500M of incremental platform-operating-model investment against roughly $450M of targeted efficiency savings — that is close to a wash in 2026 and only becomes accretive if the savings persist after the investment stops. Finally, note the share count: average diluted shares fell from 716.7M in FY2025 to 692.2M in Q2 2026, and shares outstanding are down 3.97% YoY to 678.50M. That is a real contribution and a durable one at an 87% payout ratio, but it is capital return being counted as growth.

RSI compares the size of recent gains with the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 conventionally mean a stock has risen far and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means the opposite ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value — an overbought stock can keep rising, and an oversold one can keep falling. On monthly data it is a slow, structural signal rather than a trading one.

From $59.20 at the end of October 2021 to $164.84 on September 4, 2026, BNY returned +178.4% on price alone — before roughly five years of dividends. That single number hides two completely different stocks. The first three of those five years went essentially nowhere: from $59.20 in October 2021 the shares fell to $38.52 in September 2022 (−34.9%) and were still only $52.05 at the end of December 2023 — twelve percent below where they started, twenty-six months in. Everything in the five-year return was earned between January 2024 and today, a stretch over which the stock roughly tripled from $52.05 to $164.84.

The mechanism behind that split is the same mechanism described everywhere else on this page. In 2022 rising rates were a fear (deposit flight, unrealised securities losses, the March 2023 regional-bank scare that dragged every deposit-funded institution down together — BNY fell from $50.88 in February 2023 to $40.20 in May 2023, −21% in three months). From 2024 onward, rates that stayed higher for longer became a benefit rather than a threat, NII compounded, the platforms reorganisation started converting revenue growth into margin, and the buyback shrank the share count against a rising profit pool. The market re-rated the multiple at the same time as the earnings grew — which is why the price move is far larger than the earnings move.

Relative performance. Against its benchmark, the S&P 500, BNY's +57.5% twelve-month gain is a very large outperformance by any plausible index return over the same window, and the +178% five-year figure is well ahead of what a broad US index compounded over that period. Against the NASDAQ-100 — the second comparison and the harder test, given that index's AI-driven run — the five-year comparison is much closer, and BNY's outperformance is concentrated entirely in the last twenty months. Against its own sector, the read is cleaner and more informative: on trailing earnings BNY at 19.24× now trades at a ~16% premium to State Street (17.14×) and Northern Trust (16.05×), having historically traded near parity. Note also that both peers ran hard — State Street's market cap is up 66.5% and Northern Trust's 37.7% over twelve months — so this is a custody-bank re-rating, not a BNY-specific one. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A genuinely irreplaceable piece of market infrastructure — $62.6T of AUC/A, $2.2T of AUM — is finally being run for margin instead of scale. Pre-tax margin is 39.8% and ROTCE 31.3%, both up sharply YoY; guidance was raised to 10–11% revenue growth with ~400bps of positive operating leverage; and 70%+ fee revenue means the earnings base is far less rate-dependent than the "bank" label suggests. Rate cuts are not a one-way negative: fee waivers stop and client activity rises as NII compresses. Meanwhile an 87% payout ratio is shrinking the share count ~4% a year, a 19% dividend increase signals confidence, and a 0.98 PEG says the growth is not yet fully paid for.

Bear Case

The stock has tripled in twenty months and now trades at a 16% premium to its two listed peers, 24% above its 200-day, 0.6% from its high, against an average analyst target offering 1.5% upside and a low target implying −18%. The margin expansion driving the re-rating rests on NII guided to +12–13% in a cycle that will not repeat, and a sixth of Q2's EPS growth came from buybacks rather than earnings. CET1 fell 90bps in six months funding those buybacks. The $500M platform investment roughly cancels the $450M of savings this year, so the structural cost story is unproven. And management has already flagged Q3 as a "tough comp."

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation, not business. At 19.24× trailing and 17.15× forward with an average target of $167.33, the market has already paid for the operating-leverage story. The risk is not that BNY disappoints operationally — it is that it performs exactly as guided and the multiple contracts back toward the 16× peer level, which alone would take roughly 16% out of the price.
02
Rate-cycle reversal outpacing the offsets. NII growth of 12–13% is doing heavy lifting in 2026. Deposit margin compression was already visible in Q2 even with balances flat. The money-market fee-waiver relief and activity pickup that cushion a cutting cycle arrive with a lag and are smaller than the NII line they offset.
03
Capital return is the growth. An 87% payout ratio and a CET1 ratio that has fallen from 11.9% to 11.0% in six months is a policy, not a permanent condition. Any regulatory, stress-test or credit development that forces the buyback to pause removes roughly a sixth of reported EPS growth immediately — and would do so in a quarter when the stock is priced for continuation.
Industry Commentary
The two-sided rate trade
ONGOING HEADWIND

Custody banks are the only part of the financial sector where a rate cut is genuinely ambiguous. On the negative side, BNY earns $1.446B a quarter of net interest income at a 1.45% NIM on a deposit base it pays very little for; cuts compress that spread directly, and 2026 guidance of +12–13% NII growth becomes the toughest comparison in the P&L. On the positive side, when short rates approach zero the asset-management arm is forced to waive money-market fees so fund yields do not go negative — a drag that ran for years post-2008 and disappears as rates normalise downward from restrictive levels rather than toward zero — while lower rates typically lift debt issuance, trading volumes and collateral velocity, all of which the fee lines (70.8% of revenue) are paid on. The honest position is that the net effect depends on how far rates fall, not merely whether they do: a shallow easing cycle is close to neutral for BNY; a return to the zero bound is unambiguously negative.

Collateral, Treasury issuance and the plumbing trade
EMERGING TAILWIND

The structurally best thing about BNY right now has nothing to do with rates. Market and Wealth Services — which includes Clearance and Collateral Management, sitting at the centre of the US Treasury repo market — earned a 52.0% pre-tax margin in Q2 2026, up from 48.4%, on 12% revenue growth. That business is paid on activity and balances, not asset values. As long as US Treasury issuance stays at current levels and collateral turns over at current velocity, the volume base compounds independently of both the equity market and the rate path. This is the quiet reason the fee franchise is more durable than a "custody bank levered to markets" framing implies, and it is where most of the group's incremental operating leverage is generated.

Bull vs bear: is the operating leverage structural?
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Everything on this page reduces to one testable question, and 2027 will answer it. The bull case says the platforms operating model — described by management as "completed," with the focus moved "from implementation to realizing the benefits" — plus AI (roughly 40% of engineer-written code now AI-generated, headcount down 7% YoY) has permanently lowered the cost of serving a dollar of assets, and that the ~400bps of guided positive operating leverage is the new baseline rather than a peak. The bear case notes that 2026 contains roughly $500M of incremental platform investment against roughly $450M of efficiency savings — a wash — and that management explicitly declines to quantify AI economics, which makes the productivity claim unfalsifiable from the outside. The falsifiable test is simple and dated: if 2027 expense growth decelerates materially below revenue growth after the platform investment tapers, the structural case is proved and 19× is defensible. If expense growth stays at 6–7% because platform investment turns out to be a permanent line, then the last twenty months were a rate cycle wearing a transformation story, and the premium to State Street and Northern Trust closes.
