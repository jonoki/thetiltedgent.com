01 Company Overview

BP is one of the Western integrated oil majors: it pumps oil and gas from the Gulf of America to Azerbaijan, refines it, trades it and sells it through filling stations and the Castrol lubricants brand. FY2025 revenue was $189.3B, flat on 2024's $189.2B, and underlying replacement cost profit was $7.5B; reported profit attributable to shareholders was just $55M after write-downs. Then 2026 happened. Conflict in the Middle East pushed Brent to an average of $103.85 a barrel in Q2 2026 against $63.73 in Q4 2025, refining margins nearly doubled, and BP's second-quarter underlying profit came in at $5.7B, up 144% on a year earlier.

The price has been the easy part. The story of the last twenty months is a company being rebuilt under pressure. In February 2025, with the activist fund Elliott reported to hold about 5%, BP announced what it called a "fundamentally reset" strategy: more money for oil and gas, over $5B a year less for low-carbon businesses, $20B of asset sales and a net-debt target of $14–18B by end-2027. The leadership then turned over almost completely. Chief executive Murray Auchincloss left in December 2025; Meg O'Neill, previously chief executive of Australia's Woodside Energy, took over on April 1, 2026. Chair Albert Manifold, appointed in October 2025, was removed by the board on May 26, 2026 over what BP called "serious concerns" about governance and conduct, and Ian Tyler became chair. In February 2026 the board suspended share buybacks to put every spare dollar into the balance sheet. The question now is simple to state: with the debt target in sight a year early, when does the cash start coming back to shareholders, and in what form?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Integrated oil-to-forecourt chain Global trading arm Low unit cost ($6.62/boe) Gulf of America deepwater hubs bpx Energy US onshore LNG supply portfolio Forecourt and fuel brands

An oil major has no moat in the usual sense: nobody pays extra for BP's barrel. What it has is position. Production cost of $6.62 per barrel of oil equivalent in Q2 2026 is low enough to stay profitable at prices that hurt smaller producers, and the integrated chain means a spike in crude that squeezes one part of the business usually helps another. Q2 2026 showed it: refining and trading earned $3.2B, close to the whole oil-production segment's $3.6B, while plant outages held production back. The trading arm, which BP describes as "world-class" and which now sits across both new segments, earns from moving cargoes when markets are dislocated. The weakness is that none of this is unique. Shell, ExxonMobil, Chevron and TotalEnergies have the same tools, generally with stronger balance sheets, and over the year to October 2 every one of them rose more than BP.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The balance sheet comes before shareholders. Net debt is $22.3B, but once hybrid bonds, leases and Gulf of America settlement liabilities are added BP counts $53.6B of financial obligations at June 30, 2026 ($60.5B in March), and its guide is for that total to fall to $39–41B by end-2026 on its second-half price assumptions. O'Neill on the call: "$40 billion of total liabilities is still too much." Buybacks have been suspended since February 10, 2026, and the 30–40% of operating cash flow distribution guidance was retired with them.
02
Write-downs keep eating the profit. FY2025 underlying profit of $7.5B became $55M of reported profit after adjusting items, including about $4B of post-tax impairments in Q4 2025 alone. Q2 2026 carried another $0.8B. BP's low-carbon spending of earlier years is still being written down.
03
Leadership churn. Three chief executives since 2023 (Bernard Looney, Murray Auchincloss, Meg O'Neill) and three chairs in just over a year. The May 2026 removal of Albert Manifold came weeks after about 18% of shareholders voted against his election at the April AGM (BBC, May 26, 2026). A new team needs time to prove its plan; the market prices that uncertainty.
04
Operations slipped as the price rose. Upstream plant reliability fell to 92.4%, refining availability to 94.7% and process safety events rose to 18 in Q2 2026. In a quarter of $100+ Brent every lost barrel is expensive, and the guidance cut for 2026 production follows from it.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things separate BP's reported numbers from its underlying ones. First, impairments: about $4B post-tax in Q4 2025, mainly in low-carbon businesses, which turned FY2025's $7.5B underlying profit into $55M reported; Q2 2026 added $0.8B more. Second, inventory effects: when oil prices rise, fuel bought earlier is booked at a profit (a $3.2B after-tax gain in Q1 2026) and when they fall the reverse ($0.7B loss in Q2). Third, fair value accounting effects on LNG contracts and hybrid bonds swing with prices and currencies: favourable $1.0B pre-tax in Q2 2026. On the cash side, BP paid $1.1B of Gulf of America oil-spill settlement liabilities in Q2 (about $1.6B expected for 2026), redeemed $2.9B of hybrid bonds in June and plans to repay a further $1.0B of hybrid securities in Q3. Castrol's classification as held for sale also lowers depreciation until the sale completes.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $44.79 you pay 9.1 times trailing underlying profit and about 6 times this year's consensus, with a 4.6% dividend yield and a free-cash-flow yield above 13%. The heavy lifting on the balance sheet is close to done: net debt fell to $22.3B in June, the $14–18B target is now expected in 2026 rather than 2027, and $6B of Castrol money is still to come. Once the target is met, cash that went to debt can go to shareholders, and the new chief executive has named total shareholder return as the test of whether BP is becoming more valuable. A new, industry-experienced chair and CEO, a simpler two-segment structure and a portfolio being sold down to its best assets are the ingredients of a re-rating. Analysts moved that way in September: three upgrades in the space of a week.

Bear Case

The 2026 numbers are borrowed from a supply shock. Brent above $100 made Q2, and it is already lower in Q3 ($96.99), even if refining margins rose further. BP's own impairment testing assumes the Middle East disruption resolves before the end of 2026. When prices normalise, the company is left with about $40B of total financial obligations, falling production (2026 guidance below 2025), reliability problems, a long record of write-downs and no buyback. BP has changed chief executive twice and chair twice since 2023, and the stock's one-year lag behind every major peer says the market is not yet giving the benefit of the doubt.

Top 3 Risks

01
Oil and refining margins fall back. Each $1/bbl of Brent is about $340M of full-year pre-tax profit and each $1/bbl of refining margin about $450M. Q2 2026's $103.85 Brent and $29.6 margin are far above the price assumptions behind BP's 2027 targets, $70 Brent and a $10.8 margin (2024 real); a return toward them would remove most of the 2026 earnings jump.
02
Shareholder returns stay on hold. Buybacks were suspended in February 2026 and the distribution guidance retired. If the new financial frame prioritises debt and liabilities for longer than investors expect, the reason many held BP, its cash returns, stays limited to a dividend growing about 4% a year.
03
Execution under a new team. A new CEO, a cost programme, a new chair, new segment structure and a wave of asset sales all at once. Reliability already slipped in Q2 (92.4% upstream, 18 process safety events). A further incident, a sale that disappoints or another governance surprise would hit a stock that is still priced at a discount to peers for exactly these reasons.
Industry Commentary
The 2026 Middle East supply shock
DOMINANT DRIVER

bp's trading conditions data trace the year: Brent averaged $63.73 in Q4 2025, $81.13 in Q1 2026, $103.85 in Q2 and $96.99 in Q3. Refining margins moved further: bp's indicator margin went from $15.2/bbl in Q4 2025 to $29.6 in Q2 and $46.7 in Q3. The disruption also cost BP volume, with Middle East effects named in its production guidance cut. In its Q2 accounts BP raised the 2026 Brent assumption for impairment testing to $80 from $70 and said this assumes the supply disruptions resolve before year-end. For the whole industry, the earnings of 2026 depend on how long that takes.

Back to oil and gas
STRATEGIC SHIFT

The February 2025 reset cut planned low-carbon investment by more than $5B a year, and the 2026 actions continue the line: biogas and the North Sea up for sale, renewables moved into a capital-light Technology function, new gas positions in Abu Dhabi, Trinidad and Venezuela. The BBC noted in December 2025 that Shell and Equinor have also scaled back green-energy plans. The open question is whether BP, having arrived late to this strategy, can show the returns of peers who never left it.

Consolidation and the "prey" question
OPTIONALITY

When O'Neill's appointment was announced, Hargreaves Lansdown's Derren Nathan told the BBC that "BP is most frequently seen as prey rather than the hunter." The September 2026 deal letting Shell into two BP exploration prospects is a commercial partnership, not a merger step. For a shareholder the takeover angle is optionality, not a thesis: the board's stated plan is to close the gap by performance.
