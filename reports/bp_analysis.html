01 Company Overview

BP is one of the Western integrated oil majors: it pumps oil and gas from the Gulf of America to Azerbaijan, refines it, trades it and sells it through filling stations and the Castrol lubricants brand. FY2025 revenue was $189.3B, flat on 2024's $189.2B, and underlying replacement cost profit was $7.5B; reported profit attributable to shareholders was just $55M after write-downs. Then 2026 happened. Conflict in the Middle East pushed Brent, the benchmark crude oil price, to an average of $103.85 a barrel in Q2 2026 and $96.99 in Q3, against $63.73 in Q4 2025, and bp's refining indicator margin (its benchmark for what a barrel of crude earns once refined) went from $15.2 a barrel to $46.7. BP's third-quarter underlying profit came in at $8.5B, almost four times the $2.2B of a year earlier; reported profit was $4.7B after another $4.1B of write-downs.

The price has been the easy part. The story of the last twenty-one months is a company being rebuilt under pressure. In February 2025, with the activist fund Elliott reported to hold about 5%, BP announced what it called a "fundamentally reset" strategy: more money for oil and gas, over $5B a year less for low-carbon businesses, $20B of asset sales and a net-debt target of $14–18B by end-2027. The leadership then turned over almost completely. Chief executive Murray Auchincloss left in December 2025; Meg O'Neill, previously chief executive of Australia's Woodside Energy, took over on April 1, 2026. Chair Albert Manifold, appointed in October 2025, was removed by the board on May 26, 2026 over what BP called "serious concerns" about governance and conduct, and Ian Tyler became chair. In February 2026 the board suspended share buybacks to put every spare dollar into the balance sheet. It worked faster than planned: net debt was $13.9B at September 30, 2026, below the $14–18B range BP had set for end-2027, and the $6B Castrol sale closed on November 2. The question now is simple to state: when does the cash start coming back to shareholders, and in what form? O'Neill is due to set out her strategy at an update in New York on December 15, 2026.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Integrated oil-to-forecourt chain Global trading arm Low unit cost ($6.54/boe) Gulf of America deepwater hubs bpx Energy US onshore LNG supply portfolio Forecourt and fuel brands

An oil major has no moat in the usual sense: nobody pays extra for BP's barrel. What it has is position. Production cost of $6.54 per barrel of oil equivalent in Q3 2026 is low enough to stay profitable at prices that hurt smaller producers, and the integrated chain means a spike in crude that squeezes one part of the business usually helps another. Q3 2026 showed it: refining and trading earned $5.7B, more than the whole oil-production segment's $3.5B. The trading arm, which BP describes as "world-class" and which now sits across both new segments, earns from moving cargoes when markets are dislocated. The weakness is that none of this is unique. Shell, ExxonMobil, Chevron and TotalEnergies have the same tools, generally with stronger balance sheets, and over the year to November 2 every one of them rose more than BP.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The balance sheet comes before shareholders. Net debt is down to $13.9B, but once hybrid bonds, leases and Gulf of America settlement liabilities are added BP counted $44.3B of financial obligations at September 30, 2026, $9.3B less than in June, and the CFO expects about $33–35B by end-2026. O'Neill on the Q3 call: "The strong balance sheet sits at the core of our capital allocation framework." Buybacks have been suspended since February 10, 2026, with no shares repurchased in Q3, and the 30–40% of operating cash flow distribution guidance was retired with them.
02
Write-downs keep eating the profit. FY2025 underlying profit of $7.5B became $55M of reported profit after adjusting items, including about $4B of post-tax impairments in Q4 2025 alone. Q2 2026 carried another $0.9B and Q3 2026 $4.1B of net impairment charges before tax, $3.4B of them in gas & low carbon energy. BP's low-carbon spending of earlier years is still being written down.
03
Leadership churn. Three chief executives since 2023 (Bernard Looney, Murray Auchincloss, Meg O'Neill) and three chairs in just over a year. The May 2026 removal of Albert Manifold came weeks after about 18% of shareholders voted against his election at the April AGM (BBC, May 26, 2026). A new team needs time to prove its plan; the market prices that uncertainty.
04
Operations slipped in Q2, then recovered. Upstream plant reliability fell to 92.4%, refining availability to 94.7% and process safety events rose to 18 in Q2 2026, a quarter of $100+ Brent in which every lost barrel was expensive. Q3 brought them back to 95.3%, 96.5% and 8. One good quarter is not yet a record.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things separate BP's reported numbers from its underlying ones. First, impairments: about $4B post-tax in Q4 2025, mainly in low-carbon businesses, which turned FY2025's $7.5B underlying profit into $55M reported; Q2 2026 added $0.9B and Q3 2026 $4.1B of net impairment charges before tax, $3.4B of it in gas & low carbon energy (the October 13 trading statement had flagged $3–4.5B, RTÉ/PA reported). Second, inventory effects: when oil prices rise, fuel bought earlier is booked at a profit (a $3.2B after-tax gain in Q1 2026) and when they fall the reverse ($0.7B loss in Q2); Q3 brought a $2.0B gain before tax. Third, fair value accounting effects on LNG contracts and hybrid bonds swing with prices and currencies: favourable $1.0B pre-tax in Q2 2026. On the cash side, BP paid $1.1B of Gulf of America oil-spill settlement liabilities in Q2 (about $1.6B expected for 2026), redeemed $2.9B of hybrid bonds in June and a further $1.0B in Q3. Castrol's classification as held for sale lowered depreciation until the sale completed on November 2.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $25.96, the low of the window, to the November 2, 2026 close of $47.88, the ADS gained 84.4% on price. BP also paid about $8.35 per ADS in dividends over the period (Yahoo Finance dividend record), which lifts the total return to roughly 117% before reinvestment. Over the same window the S&P 500 (SPY) rose 65.1% on price and the FTSE 100 index 52.4%; BP's London line rose 84.4% in pence. Over the last year BP's +36.3% trails all four peers in this report: Shell +40.1%, Chevron +44.4%, TotalEnergies +49.1% and ExxonMobil +57.1%.

The chart has two halves. From 2022 to early 2025 BP traded a range between the high $20s and about $40, and spent most of 2024 falling: the 6-month RSI hit 19.2 in November 2024, its most oversold reading of the five years. Since the reset and the oil shock investors have been willing to pay more for it. March 2026 (+20.9%) and July 2026 (+22.4%, the best month in the series) bracket a 22% slide from the April 2026 month-end of $47.38 to $36.95 in June, through the chair's removal. October 2026 added 14.5% to a new month-end high of $50.39. RSI peaked at 86.7 in April 2026, was 70.3 at the end of October and now reads 62.2; the price sits above its 10-month average of $44.24. Momentum is positive without being stretched.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $47.88 you pay 6.5 times trailing underlying profit and about 5.8 times this year's consensus, with a 4.3% dividend yield and a free-cash-flow yield of about 18%. The heavy lifting on the balance sheet is done: net debt fell to $13.9B in September, below the $14–18B range BP had set for end-2027, and the $6B of Castrol money arrived on November 2. Cash that went to debt can now go to shareholders. A new, industry-experienced chair and CEO, a simpler two-segment structure and a portfolio being sold down to its best assets could persuade investors to pay more for each dollar of profit. Analysts lean that way: the StockAnalysis panel went from 9 Buy ratings in August to 11 in October, and Wells Fargo raised its target to $63 after the print.

Bear Case

The 2026 numbers are borrowed from a supply shock. Brent above $100 made Q2 and $97 made Q3, and a $46.7 refining margin is more than four times the $10.8 behind BP's 2027 targets. When prices normalise, the company is still left with $33–35B of total financial obligations on its own year-end forecast, falling production (2026 guidance below 2025), a long record of write-downs ($4.1B more in Q3) and no buyback yet. BP has changed chief executive twice and chair twice since 2023, and the stock's one-year lag behind every major peer says the market is not yet giving the benefit of the doubt.

Top 3 Risks

01
Oil and refining margins fall back. Each $1/bbl of Brent is about $340M of full-year pre-tax profit and each $1/bbl of refining margin about $450M. Q3 2026's $96.99 Brent and $46.7 margin are far above the price assumptions behind BP's 2027 targets, $70 Brent and a $10.8 margin (2024 real); a return toward them would remove most of the 2026 earnings jump.
02
Shareholder returns stay on hold. Buybacks were suspended in February 2026 and the distribution guidance retired. If the plan due on December 15 prioritises debt and liabilities for longer than investors expect, the reason many held BP, its cash returns, stays limited to a dividend growing about 4% a year.
03
Execution under a new team. A new CEO, a cost programme, a new chair, new segment structure and a wave of asset sales all at once. Reliability slipped in Q2 (92.4% upstream, 18 process safety events) before recovering in Q3 (95.3%, 8). A further incident, a sale that disappoints or another governance surprise would hit a stock that is still priced at a discount to peers for exactly these reasons.
Industry Commentary
The 2026 Middle East supply shock
DOMINANT DRIVER

bp's trading conditions data trace the year: Brent averaged $63.73 in Q4 2025, $81.13 in Q1 2026, $103.85 in Q2 and $96.99 in Q3. Refining margins moved further: bp's indicator margin went from $15.2/bbl in Q4 2025 to $29.6 in Q2 and $46.7 in Q3. The disruption also cost BP volume: its Q2 trading statement said reported production was lower because of it, and its 2026 production guidance includes the impact. In its Q2 accounts BP raised the 2026 Brent assumption for impairment testing to $80 from $70 and said this assumes the supply disruptions resolve before year-end; its Q3 accounts use $87. For the whole industry, the earnings of 2026 depend on how long that takes.

Back to oil and gas
STRATEGIC SHIFT

The February 2025 reset cut planned low-carbon investment by more than $5B a year, and the 2026 actions continue the line: biogas and the North Sea up for sale, renewables moved into the Technology function, new gas positions in Abu Dhabi, Trinidad and Venezuela. The BBC noted in December 2025 that Shell and Equinor have also scaled back green-energy plans. The open question is whether BP, having arrived late to this strategy, can show the returns of peers who never left it.

Consolidation and the "prey" question
POSSIBLE BONUS

When O'Neill's appointment was announced, Hargreaves Lansdown's Derren Nathan told the BBC that "BP is most frequently seen as prey rather than the hunter." The September 2026 deal letting Shell into two BP exploration prospects is a commercial partnership, not a merger step. For a shareholder a takeover is a possible bonus, not a reason to own the stock: the board's stated plan is to close the gap by performance.
