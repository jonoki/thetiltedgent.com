01 Company Overview

Brown & Brown is an insurance broker, not an insurer. It sells property & casualty, employee-benefits and specialty cover to businesses, public entities and individuals, and earns a commission on the premium it places — it never pays a claim. Founded in Florida in 1939 and still run from Daytona Beach by a Brown (CEO J. Powell Brown), it has spent decades compounding by acquisition. FY2025 revenue was $5.76B, up 22.49%, and trailing-twelve-month revenue to June 2026 is $6.66B, up 34.6%.

That growth rate is the trap in this report. Almost none of it is organic. On August 1, 2025 Brown & Brown closed its largest-ever deal, the $9.83B acquisition of Accession Risk Management Group — parent of retail broker Risk Strategies and wholesaler One80 Intermediaries — funded with roughly $4B of new equity and $4B of new debt, and adding about $1.7B of annual revenue. Strip the acquisitions out and Q2 2026 organic revenue fell 0.7%. The price tells you how the market scored that: from a $124.40 month-end close in March 2025 to $64.30 on September 21, 2026, down 48%. The question now is simple to state and hard to answer — is a broker whose organic growth has stalled on softening property rates, carrying a new $8B debt stack and 17% more shares, cheap at 20x trailing earnings, or merely no longer expensive?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

No underwriting risk Recurring renewal commissions Decentralised acquisition machine Niche program franchises (Arrowhead) Wholesale access for independent agents Founder-family ownership (13.9% insiders) 33 years of dividend growth

Brokerage is a good business: clients renew every year, the commission is a percentage of a premium that tends to rise with inflation and asset values, and the broker never pays a claim. The moat is distribution, meaning thousands of producers with local relationships plus wholesale and program franchises that carriers rely on to reach niche risks. The weakness is the flip side. That distribution walks out of the door with its people, and the revenue line moves with premium rates set by a market the broker does not control. When catastrophe-property rates rise, brokers look like growth companies. When they fall, as in 2026, brokers look like what they are, which is a leveraged bet on the insurance pricing cycle.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Premium-rate cycle. Specialty Distribution organic growth of −3.5% in Q2 2026 reflects pressure from falling catastrophe-property rates. A broker earns a share of the premium, so lower rates mean lower revenue even when every client renews.
02
Leverage after Accession. Total debt went from $4.06B (FY2024) to $8.07B (June 2026), against $918M of cash. Debt/EBITDA is 2.71x and interest coverage is 5.98x. Neither is alarming, but Q2 interest expense doubled to $100M, and that money is no longer available for acquisitions or buybacks.
03
Dilution. Shares outstanding rose 17.07% year over year because of the Accession equity raise. TTM GAAP EPS of $3.18 is down 8.6% even though net income rose 19.8%. Per-share value has not yet kept up with the growth in company size.
04
People risk. The company expects $50–60M of lost revenue in 2026 from employees leaving for a start-up competitor. In brokerage, producers take their books with them, and non-competes only slow that down.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Readings below 30 mean it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value, and a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $55.45 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $64.30, BRO gained 16.0% on price. That number hides two opposite moves. The stock more than doubled to $124.40 at the end of March 2025, when brokers were priced as compounders during a hard insurance market. It then lost 54.8% to a $56.25 May 2026 close, as organic growth faded, the Accession deal added shares and debt, and property rates softened. There were no share splits in the window (the last was 2:1 in March 2018). The chart uses unadjusted closes.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how long the decline lasted. It stayed below 30 for eight consecutive month-ends, from October 2025 to May 2026, and bottomed at 13.0 in May 2026, the most oversold reading of the five-year window. It recovered to 48.8 by August and is now 37.2 after the September drop. The 10-month moving average is about $67.63, above the current price. The June–August rebound got back to that average but did not hold above it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $64.30 you pay 14.4x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS and get a 6.7% free-cash-flow yield from a broker with no underwriting risk, a 35–37% adjusted EBITDAC margin, 33 straight years of dividend growth and a founding family that still owns a meaningful stake. The stock has already fallen 48% from its peak. Organic growth is near zero, but management guides to 1.5–2.5% in Retail and 2–4% in Specialty for the second half. Accession stops distorting the comparisons from Q3, synergies of $30–40M are modest and achievable, and the company is still buying back stock. If property pricing stabilises, a return to mid-single-digit organic growth on a much larger revenue base would re-rate a business that the market valued at twice this price 18 months ago.

Bear Case

Headline growth of 30% is bought, and the organic business shrank 0.7% in Q2. The purchase doubled debt to $8.07B, doubled quarterly interest expense to $100M and increased the share count by 17%, so TTM GAAP EPS is down 8.6%. Specialty Distribution, the high-margin half, is shrinking organically with margins down 400bps, while catastrophe-property rates, which drive its revenue, are falling. Producers are leaving for a start-up competitor with an estimated $50–60M of revenue. At 20x trailing GAAP earnings this is not a distressed valuation, and 14 of 19 analysts are at Hold. Brokers were priced for a hard market, and pricing for a soft one may not be finished.

Top 3 Risks

01
Soft insurance-pricing cycle. Broker revenue is a percentage of premium. Falling catastrophe-property rates already drove Specialty Distribution organic growth to −3.5% in Q2 2026. If the softening spreads to casualty lines, the second-half guidance of 2–4% will be missed.
02
Integration and talent retention at Accession. A $9.83B purchase of two producer-led businesses, Risk Strategies and One80, only works if the producers stay. The company has already flagged $50–60M of 2026 revenue lost to departures to a start-up competitor.
03
Leverage in a slower-growth world. Debt/EBITDA is 2.71x and interest coverage 5.98x. That is manageable while EBITDA grows. If organic growth stays near zero, the extra interest cost and the 17% dilution weigh on per-share earnings for longer than the deal model assumed.
Industry Commentary
The broker's hidden bet: premium rates
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Brokers are often described as safe businesses that take no insurance risk, and on the balance sheet that is true. The income statement is different. A commission is a percentage of the premium, so when rates rise the broker grows without winning a single new client, and when rates fall the broker shrinks while renewing every one. Brown & Brown's 2026 figures show this. Retail, which relies more on new business and exposure growth, managed +1.5% organic. Specialty Distribution, which places property catastrophe risk, fell 3.5% as those rates came down. In poker terms, the broker takes a rake on every pot and never plays a hand, but the rake is a percentage, and the pots are getting smaller.

Consolidation and the war for producers
ONGOING HEADWIND

Broker growth by acquisition, buying agencies at one multiple while being valued at a higher one, depends on the acquirer's own multiple staying high. BRO's forward P/E has fallen from 25.34 (FY2024 basis) to about 14 today, per StockAnalysis's forecast table, which narrows that arbitrage. At the same time, well-funded start-up brokers are hiring producers away from established firms, and the company has put a $50–60M figure on its 2026 exposure. The competition is now for producers as much as for clients.

The valuation reset — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

BRO trades at 9.98x EV/EBITDA, a 6.66% FCF yield and 14.4x forward adjusted earnings, against a forward multiple above 23x in every year from FY2022 to FY2024 on StockAnalysis's basis. The bull framing: the market has moved from pricing a hard market to pricing a soft one, and at these levels even flat organic growth plus buybacks and debt paydown produces a mid-single-digit per-share return before any re-rating. The bear framing: the old multiple assumed organic growth of high single digits, which the business is not producing now. Until Q3 and Q4 show organic growth back in positive territory without contingents, the discount reflects what the business is earning today.
