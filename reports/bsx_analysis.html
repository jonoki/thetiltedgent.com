01 Company Overview

Boston Scientific develops, manufactures and markets minimally invasive medical devices across interventional cardiology, electrophysiology, peripheral vascular, endoscopy, urology and neuromodulation, selling through two reporting segments — Cardiovascular and MedSurg. Fiscal 2025 net sales reached $20.074 billion, up 19.9% reported and 15.8% organic, a run driven overwhelmingly by the FARAPULSE pulsed-field ablation (PFA — a non-thermal catheter ablation technique that uses high-voltage electrical pulses instead of heat or cold) franchise and the WATCHMAN left-atrial appendage closure (LAAC) device.

That run has broken. The stock closed at $42.69 on October 29, 2026 — a $61.87B market value, about 60% below its 52-week high of $105.65 (November 13, 2025) and down 57.4% in a year in which the S&P 500 (through the SPY fund) rose 11.9%. Management has cut its 2026 sales growth forecast three times: from 10.5–11.5% (February 4) to 7.0–8.5% (April 22), 5.5–6.5% (July 29) and now about 4.25% (October 28; about 3.75% organic, which strips out currency moves and acquisitions). Two of the causes are the ones that broke the story earlier in the year: Boston Scientific is losing share in pulsed-field ablation to Medtronic, and the LAAC market it created has slowed sharply. The third is new: a cyberattack identified on August 25, 2026 shut down order processing, shipping and manufacturing until September 8, and management estimates it took about 3 points off third-quarter sales growth.

This is the entire investment case, and it is where the story broke. Cardiovascular houses Cardiology — interventional cardiology therapies, structural heart (including WATCHMAN LAAC), cardiac rhythm management (CRM) and electrophysiology (EP) — plus Peripheral Interventions. FY2025 grew 23.2% reported and 20.8% organic; by Q3 2026 the segment managed $3.406B, +1.9% reported and organic, with the cyberattack weighing on every business. Underneath, the quarter was starkly two-speed (growth rates from CEO Mike Mahoney on the Q3 call): interventional cardiology +19% and vascular therapies +1%, against electrophysiology −5% (U.S. −8%, international +2%), WATCHMAN down in the U.S. (−7%; international +4%) and cardiac rhythm management −2%.

Pulsed-field ablation — the category Boston Scientific created and no longer dominates. FARAPULSE was the first PFA system to scale, and the shift to PFA has been unusually fast: PFA went from 0% of atrial fibrillation (AF) ablation catheter spending before April 2023 to 78% by early 2026, and on the Q2 call Mahoney put U.S. PFA at "approximately 80% of the AFib market". Total AF ablation catheter spend reached $3.8 billion in 2025 on nearly 250,000 catheters (+20% unit growth). But Boston Scientific's share of that spend fell from 100% in 2023 → 83% in 2024 → 41% in early 2026, while Medtronic — with PulseSelect (Jan 2024) and the Affera Sphere-9 mapping-and-ablation catheter (Nov 2024, already 46% of Medtronic's own PFA mix) — took the lead at 48%. J&J's Varipulse holds ~11%; Abbott's Volt is ~1% and just beginning. On the Q3 call Mahoney said the company expects "continued erosion of our PFA share position until we launch FARAWAVE Ultra, now expected in early 2028" — later than the mid-2027 timing he gave in July. The rest of the defence is a product cycle too: entry into intracardiac echo (ICE) imaging, a market Mahoney put at more than $1 billion in July, planned for the second half of 2027; the FARAFLEX wide-area focal catheter, now planned for 2029; and the FARADIGM trial in paroxysmal and persistent AF.

WATCHMAN. The LAAC franchise (left-atrial appendage closure, a stroke-prevention implant) printed $535M in Q4 2025 (+29.4% YoY), slowed to about 4% in Q2 2026 and fell in the U.S. in Q3. The mechanism is a mix shift, not a share loss: roughly one-third of U.S. procedures are now performed alongside an ablation (growing 60%+ in Q2) while stand-alone procedures decline low-teens, and chief medical officer Ken Stein said on the Q2 call that "some of the confusion around CHAMPION certainly hasn't helped" — evidence from the OCEAN and CHAMPION-AF trials has reshaped who gets referred faster than the company expected. In July management assumed no improvement in 2027 absent a change in market conditions, and cut its weighted-average market growth rate (WAMGR — the blended growth of the markets it sells into) assumption to ~7–8%, roughly one point lower, when asked about the WATCHMAN slowdown. The next-generation WATCHMAN Elite device began enrolling patients in its Elite AF trial in Q3.

Boston Scientific's moat is built on the fact that interventional devices are bought by physicians, not by procurement, and physicians are slow to abandon a workflow they have mastered. A trained electrophysiologist running a FARAWAVE NAV workflow carries real switching costs: retraining, mapping-system integration, capital equipment and lab throughput risk. That has historically produced high recurring consumable revenue at a gross margin of 68.58% TTM (68.0% adjusted in Q3 2026, after 70.3% in Q2), above the 65.27% median of Medtronic, Abbott and Stryker. Layered on top is a genuine category-leadership strategy — the company deliberately competes only where it can be #1 or #2 — and a steady run of acquisitions that has bought its way into neighbouring markets: Axonics ($3.7B), Silk Road Medical (~$1.16B), Nalu Medical ($588M), a $1.5B / 34% stake in TAVR developer MiRus with an option over its transcatheter valve assets, and the pending $14.5 billion Penumbra acquisition, now expected to close early in the first quarter of 2027. Regulatory barriers are real — a PFA system requires large randomised trials, and the FARADIGM trial is now enrolling — and hospital contracting scale lets Boston Scientific bundle EP, structural heart and coronary into single agreements.

The critical qualification: the 2023–2025 evidence is that in PFA these barriers delayed competition by roughly 12–18 months and no longer. Boston Scientific went from 100% of PFA spend to 41% in under three years. Switching costs proved weaker than the market priced them, because Medtronic's Affera Sphere-9 combined mapping and ablation in one catheter and changed the workflow economics rather than merely matching them. A moat that shrinks that fast in the company's most important growth category is better treated as a head start than a lasting barrier — and Mahoney said on the Q3 call that he expects the share loss to continue until FARAWAVE Ultra launches in early 2028.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. U.S. electrophysiology share erosion. Medtronic leads PFA spend share at 48% versus Boston Scientific's 41% (early 2026), with J&J at 11% and Abbott's Volt only beginning. Electrophysiology sales fell 5% in Q3 2026 (U.S. −8%), and the main competitive answer, FARAWAVE Ultra, has slipped to early 2028 from mid-2027 — roughly five more quarters in which management itself expects share to keep falling.

2. LAAC market slowdown and CHAMPION-AF interpretation risk. WATCHMAN growth fell from +29.4% in Q4 2025 to about 4% in Q2 2026, and U.S. WATCHMAN sales fell 7% in Q3. Management attributes this to trial evidence (OCEAN, CHAMPION) shifting referrals toward combined procedures and higher-risk patients faster than it expected, and in July assumed no recovery in 2027. A demand reset driven by clinical evidence is far harder to reverse with salesmanship than a share loss is.

3. Operational resilience after the cyberattack. The incident identified on August 25, 2026 cut access to systems used to manufacture, process and ship orders; operations were back on September 8. Management estimates it cost about 3 points of Q3 sales growth and $0.07 of adjusted EPS, and expects a further 2–3 points and $0.04–$0.05 in Q4. Its September 8-K said "the timeline for a full operational recovery is uncertain", and the company does not expect a material effect on its long-term financial condition.

4. Acquisition-dependent growth and debt. Total debt was $12.62B against $539M of cash at June 30, 2026 — net debt of $12.08B (the Q3 balance sheet is not yet filed). The pending Penumbra deal adds roughly $11B of cash consideration funded by cash on hand and new debt, and is guided to cut adjusted EPS by $0.06–$0.08 in year one. FY2025 Urology growth of +23.1% reported versus +4.7% organic shows how much of headline growth is bought; with the shares down by more than half, paying for deals in stock gets much more expensive.

5. Product quality and regulatory exposure. In May 2026 the FDA announced a Class I recall — its most serious classification — of certain ACCOLADE-family pacemakers and CRT-Ps, which need a software update for a battery problem rather than removal (American Hospital Association, May 8, 2026). Cardiac rhythm management sales fell 2% in Q3 2026. Legacy litigation keeps running through the income statement: $194M of charges in FY2025 and $0.04 a share in Q3 2026.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between GAAP and adjusted results at Boston Scientific is unusually wide and unusually persistent, which is itself an analytical point: FY2025 GAAP EPS was $1.94 against adjusted EPS of $3.06 — a 37% gap. The FY2025 bridge: acquisition-related intangible amortisation $897M ($0.51/share), the largest single item and a direct consequence of the acquisition strategy; acquisition/divestiture-related charges $473M ($0.12/share), which include contingent-consideration remeasurement; restructuring charges $343M ($0.20/share); litigation-related charges $194M ($0.10/share), reflecting continuing legacy exposure including surgical mesh matters; goodwill and intangible impairment $46M ($0.02/share); EU MDR implementation costs $46M ($0.03/share); investment portfolio losses $26M ($0.02/share); and deferred tax expenses ($0.14/share), less $0.02 of discrete tax items.

Q3 2026 carried the same structure: GAAP EPS of $0.40 against adjusted EPS of $0.73, the gap made up per share of amortisation $0.14, restructuring $0.05, deferred tax expenses $0.05, acquisition/divestiture charges $0.04, litigation $0.04 and impairments $0.02, less $0.02 of investment gains. The cyberattack is not adjusted out: Monson said the adjusted figure includes "an approximate $0.07 unfavorable impact from the cyber incident". GAAP operating margin fell to 16.1% (operating income $828M on $5,138M of sales) from 20.7% a year earlier; adjusted operating margin was 26.5%, against 28.4% in Q2. Because amortisation of acquired intangibles recurs at a company that closes several deals a year — and Penumbra's $14.5B price is about to add a very large new block of it — treating it as "non-recurring" understates the true cost of the growth model. FCF ($3.63B over the twelve months to June 30; about $3.6B guided for 2026) is the cleaner measure of earnings.

Two further distortions: the effective tax rate over the last twelve months, 4.43%, is far below any sustainable rate and flatters the trailing net margin and P/E; and the completed $2 billion accelerated share repurchase (~40M shares) helped cut the Q3 diluted share count to 1,452.0 million from 1,495.5 million a year earlier — about 3% of earnings-per-share support that is not operating growth.

06 Upcoming Catalysts & News
Aug 25 – Sep 8, 2026
Cyberattack halts ordering, shipping and manufacturing
Boston Scientific identified a cybersecurity incident on August 25 that disrupted the systems used to process and ship customer orders (8-K, August 26). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A medtech business with real advantages is available at 13.5x this year's adjusted earnings guidance and 18.0x trailing GAAP earnings — about 46% below the Medtronic/Abbott/Stryker median — after a 57% fall in a year. Much of the Q3 damage was a one-off: management puts the cyberattack at about 3 points of growth and $0.07 of EPS, and operations were back by September 8. Underneath, interventional cardiology grew 19% and Neuromodulation 13% in Q3, adjusted gross margin was still 68.0%, and ROIC of 10.94% beats the 8.59% peer median.

Mahoney called 2027 "the trough in the cycle", and the setup for 2028 is crowded with launches: seven against a >$25B combined market, FARAWAVE Ultra, ICE imaging, Penumbra's ~$1.4B of thrombectomy revenue (expected to close in early Q1 2027), the MiRus TAVR option, and over $500M of planned restructuring savings. With most analysts' targets cut into the mid-$40s to mid-$50s and three downgrades already in, expectations are low.

Bear Case

Management has cut 2026 sales growth guidance from 10.5–11.5% to about 4.25% in nine months, guided Q4 sales down 1–3%, and says it will be challenged to grow sales and adjusted EPS in 2027 before Penumbra. Both engines that justified the premium are impaired at once and for different reasons: PFA share fell from 100% → 83% → 41% while Medtronic took the lead at 48%, electrophysiology sales fell 5% in Q3, and Mahoney expects share to keep falling until FARAWAVE Ultra arrives in early 2028 — later than promised in July. The LAAC market Boston Scientific created is slowing on clinical evidence that shifted referrals, and U.S. WATCHMAN sales fell 7% in Q3.

Meanwhile the balance sheet is about to absorb the largest deal in company history: $12.62B of debt against $539M of cash at June 30, a current ratio of 1.24 and quick ratio of 0.56, ~$11B of Penumbra cash consideration and $0.06–$0.08 of year-one dilution. A 4.43% trailing tax rate flatters profits, and the gap between GAAP and adjusted EPS ($0.40 against $0.73 in Q3) is about to widen with Penumbra's amortisation. With management saying growth itself will be a challenge in 2027, "cheap" may just be correctly priced.

  Industry Commentary
Pulsed-Field Ablation: A Category Turned Over in Under Three Years — and Its Competitive Response
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
PFA went from 0% of atrial fibrillation ablation catheter spending before April 2023 to 78% by early 2026 — on the Q2 call Mahoney put U.S. PFA at about 80% of the AFib market. The pool itself expanded to $3.8 billion in 2025 across nearly 250,000 catheters, growing 20% in units. In under three years it overtook the older standard, radiofrequency (heat) ablation. Boston Scientific created the category and captured 100% of it in 2023.

The instructive part is what happened next. Share went to 83% in 2024 and 41% by early 2026, with Medtronic taking the lead at 48% on the strength of Affera Sphere-9 — a combined mapping-and-ablation catheter that changed workflow economics rather than merely matching FARAPULSE — now 46% of Medtronic's own PFA portfolio. J&J's Varipulse holds 11%; Abbott's Volt is ~1% and ramping. FARAWAVE NAV is 32% of total PFA spend, original FARAWAVE 9%. By Q3 2026 Boston Scientific's electrophysiology sales were falling (−5%, U.S. −8%), and its next-generation catheter, FARAWAVE Ultra, had slipped to early 2028. The lesson generalises: in a category expanding this fast, being first buys 12–18 months, not a franchise.
Elective Procedure Volumes and the Evidence-Driven Demand Curve
ONGOING HEADWIND
Medtech demand is ultimately a procedure-volume business, and 2026 has demonstrated that the binding constraint is no longer capacity or reimbursement but clinical evidence. WATCHMAN growth collapsed from +29.4% in Q4 2025 to about 4% in Q2 2026 not because hospitals stopped implanting devices, but because the OCEAN and CHAMPION trial readouts shifted physician referral patterns toward combined procedures and higher-risk patients — on the Q2 call chief medical officer Ken Stein said "there was a much bigger impact than we anticipated". One-third of U.S. LAAC procedures are now done alongside an ablation, growing 60%+, while stand-alone volumes decline low-teens; by Q3 U.S. WATCHMAN sales were down 7%.

In July Boston Scientific cut its weighted-average market growth rate assumption to ~7–8%, down roughly one point. Regionally the picture diverges: in Q3 2026 U.S. sales grew 0.5% against EMEA +6.1% and Latin America/Canada +10.5% operationally (before currency moves), with Asia-Pacific −1.1%. Markets outside the U.S. are earlier in adopting these devices and less exposed to referral shifts driven by U.S. trial evidence.
Medtech Deal-Making: Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND
Boston Scientific has bought heavily: Silk Road Medical (~$1.16B), Axonics ($3.7B), Nalu Medical ($588M), a $1.5B / 34% stake in TAVR developer MiRus with an option over its valve assets, and now Penumbra at $14.5 billion — ~$1.4B of revenue and scaled entry into mechanical thrombectomy, expected to close in early Q1 2027.

Bull framing: at 18.0x trailing earnings — about 46% below the 33.2x median of Medtronic, Abbott and Stryker — the market is paying little for a deal-making record that has delivered 10.94% ROIC despite a decade of goodwill (the premium paid over the value of acquired assets). Penumbra plus MiRus plus seven internal launches against a >$25B market, combined with over $500M of restructuring savings over three years, is a credible path back to double-digit growth by 2028.

Bear framing: acquisitions have been standing in for organic growth, not adding to it — FY2025 Urology grew 23.1% reported on just 4.7% organic, and fell 3.1% organically in Q3 2026. Acquisition amortisation of $897M in 2025 is the recurring cost of that model and is about to grow, and $12.62B of debt against $539M of cash (June 30) leaves less room to buy the next problem's solution.
