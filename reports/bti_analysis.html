01 Company Overview

British American Tobacco — BAT — sells cigarettes (Lucky Strike, Dunhill, Kent, Pall Mall, Rothmans, and in the US Newport, Camel and Natural American Spirit) and a growing range of "smokeless" nicotine: Velo nicotine pouches, Vuse vapes and glo heated tobacco. It was formed in 1902 and owns Reynolds American, its US business, based in Winston-Salem, North Carolina. FY2025 revenue was £25.61B (down 1.0% reported, up 2.1% at constant currency, that is, with exchange-rate moves stripped out) with reported diluted EPS of 349.1p. The first half of 2026 brought revenue of £12.24B, up 1.4% (2.9% at constant currency), adjusted diluted EPS up 7.9% on BAT's own constant-currency, Canada-adjusted basis, and a full-year EPS outlook lifted to the middle of the 5–8% range. The ADS closed October 30, 2026 at $61.52, a market value of about $132.6B.

The hand BAT is playing is a slow switch. Combustibles, mostly cigarettes, still made 78% of H1 2026 revenue and pay for everything; "smokeless" products made 19.8%, up 1.6 points on FY2025, with 35.0 million adult consumers. The swing card is the US, which is 46% of revenue: two years ago it was the problem, and in H1 2026 US revenue grew 8.5% at constant currency while US pouch revenue more than tripled on Velo Plus. At its September 29–30, 2026 Capital Markets Day in Winston-Salem, management set out "Horizon 2030": the same 3–5% revenue, 4–6% adjusted operating profit and 5–8% adjusted EPS growth targets, mid-teens New Category growth to 2030 and a New Category contribution margin (the new products' profit after their direct costs, as a share of their revenue) of at least 30% by 2030. The ADS fell 2.1% on the first CMD session and closed the week at $52.64. Then, with no new figures from BAT, it rose 16.9% in four weeks to $61.52 as investors bought tobacco shares across the board: Altria and Philip Morris International rose 15.9% and 11.4% over the same dates while the S&P 500 (SPY) slipped 1.8%. The ADS now sits 8.6% below its 52-week high and yields about 5.3%. The debate is whether the pouch and vape growth arrives fast enough to outrun cigarette volumes that fell 4.6% in H1 2026, and whether the market will keep paying close to 13 times adjusted earnings for that.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Pricing power on an addictive product 11m+ retail outlets US FDA marketing authorisations Velo global pouch leadership Reynolds US distribution Regulation as a barrier to entry Investment-grade balance sheet

The moat is price. Cigarette volume fell 4.6% in H1 2026 and revenue still grew, because nicotine users absorb price rises that would sink almost any other consumer product — that is the "+6.8% price/mix" line. The second layer is permission: in the US every new vape or pouch needs FDA authorisation, a process Reynolds' general counsel Jeff Raborn described at the CMD this way: "Under the Act, the Tobacco Control Act, it should be 180 days. You have applications that have been pending for over five years." Slow approvals hurt BAT's innovation pipeline, but they also keep newcomers out, and when enforcement against illicit products tightens, the licensed incumbents collect the demand. The third is reach: more than 11 million retail outlets and 45 manufacturing sites, per Marroco's CMD remarks. The vulnerability is that each of these is set by governments — excise, flavour bans, enforcement — not by BAT.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
US cigarette trading-down. BAT's US cigarette volume share fell 0.8 percentage points in H1 2026 as the deep-discount segment grew; the H1 release says BAT has "actively increased investment in key markets in response to heightened competitive activity". H1 US combustibles revenue was helped by inventory moves and an excise drawback that the company expects not to repeat in H2.
02
APMEA has not turned. Revenue fell 6.3% and adjusted operating profit 16.5% at constant currency in H1 2026, on illicit trade and regulation in Bangladesh and Australia, inventory timing in Vietnam and heated-tobacco competition in Japan — a recovery that both the pre-close update and the H1 release called slower than expected.
03
Sterling earnings, dollar ADS. BAT earns most of its profit outside the UK and reports in pounds; the CMD release now expects a translational FX headwind of about 2–2.5% on FY2026 adjusted EPS growth. For a US holder the dividend arrives in sterling converted to dollars at each payment date, so the dollar payout moves with the pound.
04
Litigation tail. Reported profit swings on legal items: H1 2026 reported operating profit fell 15.8% partly because H1 2025 carried a £575M credit on the Canadian settlement provision. The Canadian settlement now takes 85% of the Canadian business's after-tax income (2025: 100%), which is why BAT presents an "adjusted for Canada" EPS.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort BAT's reported numbers. First, Canada: the court-approved settlement in Canada takes a percentage of the Canadian business's after-tax income (85% in 2026, 100% in 2025), so BAT's guided EPS is "adjusted for Canada" — 167.8p for H1 2026 at constant currency, up 7.9%. Second, disposals and legal credits: H1 2025 contained the £904M ITC share-sale gain and £333M ITC Hotels gain; H1 2026 contained a £149M credit from settling historical litigation with ITG Brands and a £315M finance-cost credit plus a £95M tax credit from UK Supreme Court rulings in the FII GLO tax case. Third, Fit2Win, the restructuring programme: total one-off costs are now expected at about £950M (£620M cash), with £370M booked in H1 2026, for c.£700M of annualised savings by 2028 (raised from £600M). BAT said on June 29, 2026 that it expects to cut 5,500 jobs by the end of the year and outsource 3,500 more, about a fifth of a workforce of around 47,000 (City AM).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Even after October's 16.9% rise you are paying about 13 times adjusted trailing earnings and 12.7 times this year's consensus for a business yielding 5.3%, generating about £5.1bn of free cash a year even after a one-off £2.6bn Canada payment, buying back £1.3bn of stock in 2026 and with leverage heading into its 2.0–2.5x target. The US — 46% of revenue — has turned: revenue +8.5% and adjusted profit +10.1% at constant currency in H1, BAT's US pouch volume share over 30% (Marroco, CMD), Vuse at 55.9% of tracked US vapour. If the FDA's slow process and illicit-vape enforcement keep shifting demand to licensed sellers, and New Category margins climb from 13.8% toward the 30% target, earnings growth can sit at the middle of 5–8% for years while the dividend pays you to wait.

Bear Case

Seventy-eight percent of revenue is combustibles, volume is falling 4.6% a year, and the price increases that hold revenue up are pushing US smokers into deep-discount brands where BAT is under-represented. APMEA's profit fell 16.5% in H1 and management has twice called the recovery slower than expected. Heated tobacco is shrinking. Management itself guides to the lower end of the revenue and profit ranges for 2026 and declined at the CMD to talk up the top end. The reported EPS is noisy with ITC gains, litigation credits and restructuring, and the sterling dividend is exposed to the pound for a US holder. And October's rally came with no new numbers from BAT: the ADS rose with Altria and Philip Morris International, and the lowest analyst target ($63) is now only 2.4% above the price and the $70.37 average 14.4% above it, so much of the upside analysts saw at the last edition has already been paid for.

Top 3 Risks

01
Regulation and excise, everywhere at once. Regulatory changes in Poland cut AME vapour revenue 13.9%; illicit trade and regulation hit Bangladesh and Australia; US product approvals take years. Every one of BAT's categories is priced by governments, and the 2030 plan assumes 30% of illicit US vape value returns to legal sellers.
02
The US combustibles cushion thins in H2. H1 US cigarette revenue was helped by inventory moves and an excise drawback that management says will not repeat, while US investment rises for Velo Max, Vuse flavours and cigarette pricing. A weaker H2 in the largest profit pool would land just as APMEA is supposed to recover.
03
New Categories have to earn, not just grow. The 30%-by-2030 contribution-margin target is the bridge between "growing" and "replacing cigarette profit". H1 2026 was 13.8%. Heated Products is in its "peak investment year", and Modern Oral is entering newer markets where Marroco said there will be "a cost of entry".
Industry Commentary
Nicotine pouches are the growth market
EMERGING TAILWIND

BAT expects nicotine revenue in its addressable markets to grow about 4% a year from 2025 to 2030, with double-digit growth in New Categories over low-single-digit combustibles, and Modern Oral industry revenue to almost triple by 2030 (CMD, September 29, 2026). Marroco noted that Sweden's adults use about 12 pouches a day against a global average of four — the bull case for the category in one number. BAT's own pouch revenue grew 65.9% in H1 2026 and its volume share in its top markets reached 39.2%. The question for investors is price: pouches carry about four times a cigarette's gross profit per unit, per Marroco, but newer markets carry "a cost of entry".

Illicit vapes and the FDA
ONGOING HEADWIND

The legal US vape market competes with unauthorised disposables that BAT puts at about £7bn of value. At the CMD, Reynolds' general counsel Jeff Raborn called the FDA's May 8 guidance "a positive, what we would consider interim step" and said applications have been pending for more than five years against the Tobacco Control Act's 180 days. Marroco said that with a more level playing field BAT could beat its 30% New Category margin target. The read-across is industry-wide: licensed manufacturers gain when enforcement bites, and lose time and share when it does not.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
THE DEBATE

At $61.52 the ADS trades at 15.8x reported and about 12.8x adjusted trailing EPS, 12.7x FY2026 consensus, with a 5.33% dividend yield and a ~5% free-cash-flow yield — up from 13.7x, 11.1x and 10.9x, and down from a 6.15% yield, at the October 2 price. October showed how the stock trades as a group: with no new BAT figures, it rose with Altria and Philip Morris International as investors looked for steady earnings and dividends while Treasury yields jumped and the wider market fell (Barchart, via Yahoo Finance, October 12, 2026). The bull framing: a company guiding to the middle of 5–8% EPS growth, with more than £50bn of free cash planned over 2024–2030 and leverage back in its target range by year-end, is still priced at about 13 times adjusted earnings, a multiple that assumes little growth. The bear framing: that yield is what a melting cigarette franchise looks like when the replacement products are still a sixth of revenue and earn a fraction of the margin. The way to settle it is not the multiple; it is two lines BAT reports every half — the smokeless share of revenue (19.8%) and the New Category contribution margin (13.8%). Both rose in H1 2026.
