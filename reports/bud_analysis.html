01 Company Overview

AB InBev is the world's largest brewer by sales. Its megabrands, as the company calls them, are Budweiser, Corona, Stella Artois and Michelob Ultra; in the US it also sells Bud Light and Busch Light, and it has a growing "Beyond Beer" line of canned cocktails such as Cutwater. It was built by deals: Belgium's Interbrew merged with Brazil's AmBev in 2004 to form InBev, InBev bought Anheuser-Busch in 2008, and the group then took over Mexico's Grupo Modelo and, in 2016, SABMiller. FY2025 revenue was $59.32B, up 2.0% organic but down 0.8% in dollars, on volumes that fell 2.3%. Normalized EBITDA was $21.22B, a 35.8% margin, and the company's own headline underlying EPS was $3.73.

The story of the last five years is pricing without volume. Beer consumption has been declining in Europe and the US, and AB InBev has grown revenue by charging more and selling pricier brands: in the second quarter of 2026 revenue per hectolitre rose 4.2% while total volume rose only 0.9%. After volumes fell 2.3% in 2025, 2026 brought growth back, helped by the FIFA World Cup co-hosted by the US, Mexico and Canada, and the shares went with it: the NYSE ADS closed 2025 at $64.04 and reached a five-year month-end high of $86.48 on July 31, 2026. Since that day the stock has given back 15.1% to $73.43. At that price it trades at 15.5 times trailing reported earnings and 17.8 times the company's underlying earnings, carrying $64.2B of net debt, 2.86 times a year of EBITDA. The open question for the October 29 third-quarter print is whether the World Cup volume lift lasts once the tournament is over.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brand portfolio (Corona, Michelob Ultra, Stella) Share gained or held in ~70% of markets (2Q26) BEES Marketplace Scale in sponsorships (FIFA World Cup) Brewing & can-making scale Pricing power (rev/hl +4.2%)

Beer is heavy, cheap and local, so whoever owns the breweries, the trucks and the relationship with the corner shop in a given country tends to stay on top. AB InBev's moat is that it holds that kind of position in many markets at once, and that its brands let it raise prices: revenue per hectolitre rose 4.2% in 2Q26, and the company says it gained or held market share in about 70% of its markets. The 49.7% EBITDA margin in Middle Americas is what that looks like at its best. The weakness is that a moat around beer does not help if people drink less beer: the moat protects share and price, not volume.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Volume is the weak link. FY2025 volumes fell 2.3%; the first half of 2026 grew just 0.8% in a World Cup year, and even that 2Q26 figure came in a touch below expectations. Bloomberg's report on the quarter put it plainly: beer makers face a slowdown in drinking in key markets, tied to cost-of-living pressure and health concerns. Price and mix can carry growth for a while, but every year of price increases on flat volume makes the next one harder.
02
China keeps shrinking. Volumes in China fell 9.7% in 2Q26 after a 7.4% fall in 2Q25, and Asia Pacific EBITDA fell to $990M in the first half from $1,056M. It is the one region dragging the group.
03
Debt went up this year, not down. Net debt rose to $64.2B at June 30, 2026 from $60.9B at the end of 2025, in a half that included the $2.9B container-plant buy-in, $1.30B of buybacks and the final dividend. The ratio to EBITDA still improved, to 2.86x from 3.27x a year earlier, because EBITDA grew. With a $6B buyback under way, debt reduction now depends on EBITDA growth more than on repayment.
04
Controlled company, and the controllers are selling some. The founding-family foundation, its family vehicles and the shareholders bound to it by voting agreements (including Altria and BEVCO) control 52.3% of the votes, so outside shareholders cannot change the board's direction. On August 3, 2026 one family vehicle, Eugenie Patri Sebastien (E.P.S.), sold about 10 million shares at €73.10 in a block trade, 2.8% below the previous close, for €731M.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between reported and underlying profit is unusually wide this year, and it runs in the flattering direction. AB InBev hedges the cost of its share-based pay programmes with derivatives, and when its own share price rises those derivatives book mark-to-market gains: about $1.4B in 2Q26 and $2.03B in the first half. That is why reported profit attributable to shareholders more than doubled to $3,751M in 2Q26 (from $1,676M) while underlying profit rose to $2,390M (from $1,950M), and why basic EPS of $1.90 in the quarter sits far above underlying EPS of $1.21. The same mechanism reverses when the share price falls, as it has since July. Hyperinflation accounting, restructuring costs and disposals were small by comparison. The trailing P/E in the table uses reported EPS as the house rule requires; the underlying P/E of 17.8 is the cleaner comparison.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $56.38 to the October 2, 2026 close of $73.43, the ADS gained 30.2% on price, dividends excluded. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The world's largest brewer is growing profit faster than its industry: 2Q26 EBITDA +5.8% against +4.8% expected, underlying EPS +23.4%, gross margin up to 57.5%, Brazil back to growth and Mexico at record volumes. Free cash flow was $11.33B in 2025, a 7.6% yield on today's market value, and it is funding a $6B buyback while net debt to EBITDA has come down to 2.86x from 3.27x a year earlier. At 16.4 times this year's consensus underlying EPS and 9.5 times EBITDA, the stock is cheaper than it was in July, with every one of the eleven polled analysts at Hold or better and an average target 32% above the price. Consensus has underlying EPS rising another 10% in 2027, to $4.93; if volume keeps growing after the World Cup, price and mix can deliver it.

Bear Case

Strip out the World Cup and the core problem is unchanged: people are drinking less beer. Volumes fell 2.3% in 2025 and grew only 0.9% in a tournament quarter that still missed expectations; China volumes fell 9.7% in the quarter. Reported earnings are flattered by $2.03B of first-half hedging gains that disappear when the share price falls, and net debt rose $3.3B in the half to $64.2B. The founding-family vehicles control the company and one of them sold €731M of stock just after the peak. After a 35% run in seven months the price has already rolled over below its 10-month average; a soft 3Q26 without the tournament could take it further.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume after the World Cup. 3Q26 consensus already assumes organic volume growth of only 0.3%. If volumes turn negative again, the price-and-mix model has to carry all of the growth, and consensus EBITDA growth of 3.9% for the quarter leaves little room.
02
China and emerging-market currencies. China volumes fell 9.7% in 2Q26. Elsewhere, half the revenue comes from Latin America, where reported dollar results swing with local currencies: first-half revenue grew 11.5% reported against 5.7% organic, which the company put down to positive currency translation. That can reverse as quickly.
03

Industry Commentary
Less drinking, more paying
STRUCTURAL HEADWIND

Brewers worldwide are contending with a slowdown in drinking in key markets, which Bloomberg's report on the 2Q26 results tied to cost-of-living pressure and concerns over alcohol's health effects. The industry's answer is the one AB InBev is running: sell fewer, pricier drinks, push premium brands, and grow no-alcohol beer and canned cocktails. It shows in the share prices: over five years Heineken is down 22.8%, Carlsberg 18.3%, Molson Coors 20.5%, Asahi 13.0%, Tsingtao 35.6% and China Resources Beer 67.9%. AB InBev, up 30.2%, is the exception in that group.

The World Cup year
ONE-OFF TAILWIND

The 2026 FIFA World Cup was co-hosted by the US, Mexico and Canada; AB InBev's North America and Middle Americas regions, which include those three countries, supplied 53% of its first-half revenue, and the company ran World Cup activations across its markets. It credits the tournament with supporting Michelob Ultra's growth in the US and opening a path for the brand in Latin America. The question for investors is how much of 2026's volume recovery was the tournament. In the same quarter China volumes fell 9.7%. The 3Q26 consensus of +0.3% volume suggests analysts expect the lift to fade.

Beyond Beer and no-alcohol
EMERGING TAILWIND

Two small lines are growing fast: no-alcohol beer revenue rose 27% in 2Q26 and Beyond Beer, about 3% of 2025 revenue according to Bloomberg, rose 44%, with the BeatBox acquisition adding a US ready-to-drink brand. In the US, the Cutwater cocktail brand grew triple digits. These products target the same health-conscious drinkers who are leaving beer, so they are a hedge on the core business; at their current size they cannot yet offset a decline in it.
