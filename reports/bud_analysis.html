01 Company Overview

AB InBev is the world's largest brewer by sales. Its megabrands, as the company calls them, are Budweiser, Corona, Stella Artois and Michelob Ultra; in the US it also sells Bud Light and Busch Light, and it has a growing "Beyond Beer" line of canned cocktails such as Cutwater. It was built by deals: Belgium's Interbrew merged with Brazil's AmBev in 2004 to form InBev, InBev bought Anheuser-Busch in 2008, and the group then took over Mexico's Grupo Modelo and, in 2016, SABMiller. FY2025 revenue was $59.32B, up 2.0% organic but down 0.8% in dollars, on volumes that fell 2.3%. Normalized EBITDA was $21.22B, a 35.8% margin, and the company's own headline underlying EPS was $3.73.

The story of the last five years is pricing without volume. Beer consumption has been declining in Europe and the US, and AB InBev has grown revenue by charging more and selling pricier brands: in the third quarter of 2026 revenue per hectolitre rose 4.2% while total volume rose only 0.4%. After volumes fell 2.3% in 2025, 2026 brought growth back, helped by the FIFA World Cup co-hosted by the US, Mexico and Canada, and the shares went with it: the NYSE ADS closed 2025 at $64.04 and reached a five-year month-end high of $86.48 on July 31, 2026. Since that day the stock has given back 7.1% to $80.38. At that price it trades at 16.1 times trailing reported earnings and 18.5 times the company's underlying earnings, carrying $64.2B of net debt at June 30 (the latest figure the company has published), 2.86 times a year of EBITDA. The October 29 third-quarter print gave a first answer on whether the World Cup volume lift lasts once the tournament is over: total volume still grew 0.4%, but beer volume slipped 0.1% and China fell 16.8%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brand portfolio (Corona, Michelob Ultra, Stella) Share gained or held in ~70% of markets (2Q26) BEES Marketplace Scale in sponsorships (FIFA World Cup) Brewing & can-making scale Pricing power (rev/hl +4.2%)

Beer is heavy, cheap and local, so whoever owns the breweries, the trucks and the relationship with the corner shop in a given country tends to stay on top. AB InBev's moat is that it holds that kind of position in many markets at once, and that its brands let it raise prices: revenue per hectolitre rose 4.2% in both 2Q26 and 3Q26, and the company said at 2Q26 that it gained or held market share in about 70% of its markets. The 50.0% nine-month EBITDA margin in Middle Americas is what that looks like at its best. The weakness is that a moat around beer does not help if people drink less beer: the moat protects share and price, not volume.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Volume is the weak link. FY2025 volumes fell 2.3%; the first nine months of 2026 grew just 0.7% in a World Cup year, and the third quarter, once the tournament was over, grew 0.4% with beer volume down 0.1%. Bloomberg's report on the 2Q26 results put it plainly: beer makers face a slowdown in drinking in key markets, tied to cost-of-living pressure and health concerns. Price and mix can carry growth for a while, but every year of price increases on flat volume makes the next one harder.
02
China keeps shrinking. Volumes in China fell 9.7% in 2Q26 after a 7.4% fall in 2Q25, and 16.8% in 3Q26, when the company says it was managing inventory levels in a challenging industry. Asia Pacific EBITDA was down 13.8% in the nine months. It is the one region dragging the group.
03
Debt went up this year, not down. Net debt rose to $64.2B at June 30, 2026 from $60.9B at the end of 2025, in a half that included the $2.9B container-plant buy-in, $1.30B of buybacks and the final dividend. The ratio to EBITDA still improved, to 2.86x from 3.27x a year earlier, because EBITDA grew. With a $6B buyback under way ($2.6B completed by October 26) and the interim dividend raised to €0.40 a share from €0.15, debt reduction now depends on EBITDA growth more than on repayment.
04
Controlled company, and the controllers are selling some. The founding-family foundation, its family vehicles and the shareholders bound to it by voting agreements (including Altria and BEVCO) control 52.3% of the votes, so outside shareholders cannot change the board's direction. On August 3, 2026 one family vehicle, Eugenie Patri Sebastien (E.P.S.), sold about 10 million shares at €73.10 in a block trade, 2.8% below the previous close, for €731M.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between reported and underlying profit is unusually wide this year, and it has mostly run in the flattering direction. AB InBev hedges the cost of its share-based pay programmes with derivatives, and when its own share price rises those derivatives book mark-to-market gains: $2.03B in the first half of 2026 and $1.23B over the nine months, because the third quarter, with the share price lower, booked an $800M loss (against a $947M loss in 3Q25). In 3Q26 profit attributable to shareholders was $1,597M (from $1,054M) against underlying profit of $2,396M (from $1,970M), and basic EPS of $0.81 sat below underlying EPS of $1.21, the reverse of the second quarter, when basic EPS of $1.90 sat far above it. Hyperinflation accounting, restructuring costs and disposals were small by comparison. The trailing P/E in the table uses reported EPS as the house rule requires; the underlying P/E of 18.5 is the cleaner comparison.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 30, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $61.22 to the October 30, 2026 close of $80.38, the ADS gained 31.3% on price, dividends excluded. Most of that came in 2026: for four years the stock went sideways between a $45.16 low (September 2022) and a $70.49 high (May 2025), and as late as January 2025 it closed at $49.18. The run from the December 2025 close of $64.04 to the July 2026 close of $86.48 was 35.0% in seven months. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The world's largest brewer is growing profit faster than its industry: 3Q26 underlying EPS +22.4% to $1.21 against $1.15 expected, organic revenue +4.9% against +4.1% expected, volume still growing after the tournament, Brazil and Mexico expanding, and the 2026 outlook confirmed. Free cash flow was $11.33B in 2025, a 7.0% yield on today's market value, and it is funding a $6B buyback ($2.6B done by October 26) and a larger interim dividend (€0.40, from €0.15) while net debt to EBITDA had come down to 2.86x at June 30 from 3.27x a year earlier. At 18.2 times this year's consensus underlying EPS and 9.9 times EBITDA, the stock is cheaper than it was in July, with every one of the twelve polled analysts at Hold or better and an average target 18% above the price. Consensus has underlying EPS rising another 10% in 2027, to $4.85; if volume keeps growing after the World Cup, price and mix can deliver it.

Bear Case

Strip out the World Cup and the core problem is unchanged: people are drinking less beer. Volumes fell 2.3% in 2025; in the quarter after the tournament they grew only 0.4% with beer volume down 0.1%, China volumes fell 16.8% and US EBITDA fell 4.0%. The EBITDA margin slipped 0.34 percentage points organically, which the company ties to a step-up in sales and marketing spending and higher commodity costs. Reported earnings include $1.23B of nine-month hedging gains that reverse when the share price falls (the third quarter booked an $800M loss), and net debt rose $3.3B in the first half to $64.2B. The founding-family vehicles control the company and one of them sold €731M of stock just after the peak. The price has recovered to $80.38 but is still 7.1% below July, and management says it is working to end the year with normal inventories in China.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume after the World Cup. The first quarter after the tournament delivered volume growth of 0.4% (company-compiled consensus +0.3%), but beer volume fell 0.1% and the growth came from non-beer products and Middle Americas. If volumes turn negative again, the price-and-mix model has to carry all of the growth, and EBITDA growth of 3.9% in the quarter, with the margin down, leaves little room.
02
China and emerging-market currencies. China volumes fell 16.8% in 3Q26 and 9.7% in 2Q26. Elsewhere, half the revenue comes from Latin America, where reported dollar results swing with local currencies: nine-month revenue grew 10.7% reported against 5.4% organic, a gap that includes currency translation. That can reverse as quickly.
03
Leverage and control. At $64.2B (June 30), net debt was 2.86 times EBITDA, and it rose in the first half. The board answers to a controlling group with 52.3% of the votes, and further sales by family vehicles, like the August block, can weigh on the shares.
Industry Commentary
Less drinking, more paying
STRUCTURAL HEADWIND

Brewers worldwide are contending with a slowdown in drinking in key markets, which Bloomberg's report on the 2Q26 results tied to cost-of-living pressure and concerns over alcohol's health effects. The industry's answer is the one AB InBev is running: sell fewer, pricier drinks, push premium brands, and grow no-alcohol beer and canned cocktails. It shows in the share prices: over five years Heineken is down 17.0%, Carlsberg 8.5%, Molson Coors 9.0%, Asahi 4.0%, Tsingtao 41.0% and China Resources Beer 69.3%. AB InBev, up 31.3%, is the exception in that group.

The World Cup year
ONE-OFF TAILWIND

The 2026 FIFA World Cup was co-hosted by the US, Mexico and Canada; AB InBev's North America and Middle Americas regions, which include those three countries, supplied 53% of its first-half revenue, and the company ran World Cup activations across its markets. It credits the tournament with supporting Michelob Ultra's growth in the US and opening a path for the brand in Latin America. The question for investors is how much of 2026's volume recovery was the tournament. In 2Q26, the quarter the tournament began, China volumes fell 9.7%. The first quarter after the tournament, 3Q26, grew volume only 0.4%, against the +0.3% the company-compiled consensus expected, with beer volume down 0.1%.

Beyond Beer and no-alcohol
EMERGING TAILWIND

Two small lines are growing fast: no-alcohol beer revenue rose 35% in 3Q26 (27% in 2Q26) and Beyond Beer, about 3% of 2025 revenue according to Bloomberg, rose 19% in 3Q26 (44% in 2Q26), with the BeatBox acquisition adding a US ready-to-drink brand. In the US, the Cutwater cocktail brand grew triple digits. These products target the same health-conscious drinkers who are leaving beer, so they are a hedge on the core business; at their current size they cannot yet offset a decline in it.
