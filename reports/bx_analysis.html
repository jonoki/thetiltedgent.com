01 Company Overview

Blackstone is the world's largest alternative asset manager, running $1.346 trillion of AUM across four segments: Real Estate, Private Equity, Credit & Insurance, and Multi-Asset Investing. The economics are two-layered — a recurring management-fee stream on $961.6B of fee-earning AUM, plus performance revenue that is realised only when investments are sold, which is why the GAAP income statement swings violently from year to year.

Most recent full year (FY2025): revenue of $14.21B, up 12.2% YoY, with GAAP net income of $3.02B and GAAP EPS of $3.87. But the numbers management and the sell side actually run the business on were Fee-Related Earnings (FRE) of $5.7B ($4.67/share) and Distributable Earnings (DE) of $7.1B ($5.57/share). Blackstone ranks #126 globally and #74 in the US by market capitalisation, and sits inside the S&P 500 (added September 2023).

The most recent quarter (2Q26, reported 23–24 July 2026) was a clean beat: DE of $1.977B ($1.52/share, +26% YoY), FRE of $1.783B ($1.43/share, +20–22% YoY), record AUM of $1,346.3B (+11%), perpetual capital of $555.6B (+15%), and quarterly inflows of $68.3B. Despite that, the stock is still down 15.8% over the trailing 52 weeks.

The BREIT redemption episode, stated plainly. BREIT is a semi-liquid, non-traded REIT sold largely to wealth-channel investors. It is structured with repurchase caps — roughly 2% of net asset value per month and 5% per quarter. When rates spiked and listed REITs fell far below BREIT's appraised marks in late 2022, redemption requests exceeded those caps and Blackstone prorated withdrawals for months into 2023. Investors got some money back each month, but not all of what they asked for. The mechanism worked exactly as documented, and no gate was "broken" — but it made the central bear-case point about semi-liquid retail vehicles concrete: liquidity in these products is a promise conditioned on other people not asking at the same time, and appraisal-based NAVs can lag observable public-market prices for a long time. The recovery is now measurable rather than rhetorical: on the 2Q26 call management reported BREIT net flows were the best in nearly four years, repurchase requests fell 42% YoY and 33% sequentially, NAV rose ~7% over the year to $57B, and the vehicle has returned 9.4% net annualised since inception, roughly 40% above the public REIT index over that span. Data centres are ~27% of the portfolio.
Data centres / QTS. The single largest value driver at the firm right now is the digital-infrastructure platform anchored by QTS. On the 2Q26 call management put total data-centre platform value — including facilities under construction — at $185B, up from roughly $130B earlier in the year, and named QTS the largest single source of appreciation firm-wide. They expect to lease more than three times as much capacity this year as in any prior year. Blackstone also listed BXDC (Blackstone Digital Infrastructure Trust), raising $2.0B. Schwarzman went as far as calling the stock "one of the least expensive ways to participate in the AI trend." That framing is a double-edged sword: it is the clearest bull catalyst and simultaneously the clearest source of concentration risk if AI capex expectations reset.

Blackstone's moat is not a product — it is the cost of capital on the fundraising side. At $1.346T of AUM and $262.5B of trailing-twelve-month inflows, it is the default allocation for pension funds, sovereign wealth funds and insurers that need to write a $500M–$2B ticket without doing diligence on an unproven manager. That scale compounds in three ways. First, it lets Blackstone underwrite transactions almost no one else can finance alone — a $185B data-centre platform is not assembled by a mid-sized sponsor. Second, it converts into pricing power on the liability side: $555.6B of perpetual capital — 48% of fee-earning AUM and growing 15% a year — means roughly half the fee base does not have to be re-raised every few years. Third, it gives Blackstone the distribution to reach retail and insurance money that traditional drawdown funds structurally cannot: BREIT, BCRED, BXDC and the insurance mandates are all attempts to convert a wholesale business into something closer to an annuity.

The financial fingerprint of that moat is visible in the metrics table below: ROE of 31.37% and a 22.73% net profit margin against a median of roughly 5% for the listed alternative-manager peer group. Blackstone converts revenue to profit at four times the rate of its closest comparables because it does not need much balance sheet to do it — total assets are only $49.9B against $1.35T of managed money. But the moat protects the fee stream far better than it protects the earnings multiple, and the last twelve months are the proof: FRE grew 22% and the stock still fell 15.8%.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Real estate is shrinking while everything else grows. Real Estate AUM fell 3% YoY to $314.1B — the only segment in decline — even as it still supplies 32.3% of management fees. That gap means fee revenue in the segment is being carried by a base that is not replenishing. Commercial real estate marks remain appraisal-driven and lag observable transaction prices; management acknowledged declines in life-science office in 2Q26, offset by data centres. If the data-centre bid cools before the office and life-science drag is fully written down, the segment's fee base compresses from both ends.

2. Semi-liquid retail vehicles carry structural liquidity mismatch. BREIT and BCRED promise periodic liquidity on portfolios of assets that take months or years to sell. The 2022–23 BREIT episode showed what happens when requests exceed the 2%-monthly / 5%-quarterly caps: proration. The current data is genuinely good — repurchase requests down 42% YoY, best net flows in nearly four years — but the structure has not changed. A sharp rate move or a public-market dislocation can re-run the same script, and the headline risk is disproportionate to the AUM at stake because it is the retail channel that generates press coverage.

3. Private credit is now the growth engine — and the most crowded trade in finance. Credit & Insurance is $469.3B and growing 15%. The reported 7% trailing-twelve-month gross return in non-investment-grade private credit is fine, but it has been earned in a benign default environment with wide spreads. Spread compression from competing capital lowers future returns; a genuine default cycle tests marks that are, again, model-derived rather than traded. Regulators and the IMF/BIS have repeatedly flagged the opacity of the asset class. Blackstone is better positioned than most, but it is not insulated from a sector-wide de-rating.

4. Earnings quality is a presentation choice, and the dividend rides on it. GAAP EPS was $3.87 in FY2025 while DE was $5.57 and FRE $4.67. The dividend of $4.74/share works out to a payout ratio of 117% of GAAP earnings but well under 100% of DE. That is defensible — DE is the right denominator — but it means the dividend is variable by design and falls when realisations fall. Investors who bought BX for a 3.3% yield should understand they own a distribution that management has explicitly structured to fluctuate with the exit environment, not a fixed coupon.

Notable One-Off Items

Unrealised performance-fee marks are the dominant distortion. In 2Q26, GAAP total revenue was $5.044B while total segment revenue — the operating measure — was $3.802B. The $1.24B gap is principally unrealised appreciation on fund investments and accrued carry flowing through the income statement. Cumulatively, $7.5B of net accrued performance revenue ($6.00/share, +13% YoY) sits unrealised on the balance sheet. None of it is cash yet.

The FY2021→FY2022 revenue collapse was not operational. Revenue fell from $22.18B to $8.02B (−63.9%) and GAAP EPS from $8.13 to $2.36 (−71.0%). Fee-earning AUM did not fall by anything like that; the swing was reversal of 2021's enormous unrealised marks. Any screen that ranks BX on multi-year GAAP revenue or EPS growth is measuring mark-to-market noise.

Large discrete realisations in 2Q26. The Digital Realty joint venture and Lumina dispositions contributed roughly $6.6B of the quarter's $31.8B of realisations, and were the main reason Real Estate segment DE rose 32% YoY in a quarter when the segment's AUM fell 3%. Realisation-driven DE is inherently lumpy — management explicitly guided realisations to slow sequentially in 3Q26.

Real-estate valuation marks. Values appreciated modestly overall in 2Q26, with data-centre appreciation (QTS named as the largest single driver firm-wide) offsetting declines in life-science office. These are appraisal-based, not transaction-based, and are the single most subjective input in the reported numbers.

Balance-sheet and buyback items. BXDC's listing raised $2.0B. Share repurchases were only 0.2M shares in the quarter (0.8M over the trailing twelve months) — buybacks are immaterial here; $1.7B of the quarter's capital return went out as dividends. Total assets of $49.9B at 30 June 2026.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 means it has been sold off hard and may be washed out. It says nothing about whether the stock is cheap — it is a momentum gauge, not a valuation one.

The five-year trajectory has been a round trip with a lot of damage in the middle. From the September 2021 close of $116.34 to the August 24, 2026 close of $143.64, the price return is +23.5% — roughly 4.3% annualised. Dividends added meaningfully on top (FY2025 alone paid $4.74/share, a 3.3% yield at the current price), but a precise five-year total-return figure was not verified from a live source in this session and is therefore not asserted here. What the price path shows is three distinct regimes: a blow-off into November 2021, a brutal 48% de-rating into December 2022 as rates rose and real-estate marks came under attack, and then a two-and-a-half-year recovery that peaked in November 2024 and has since given back a quarter of its value.

The last twelve months have been the hard part. The stock is down 15.75% over 52 weeks, and drew down from $190.09 (52-week high) to $101.73 (52-week low) — a 46% peak-to-trough range inside a single year, on a business whose fee-related earnings grew 22%. That divergence between operating results and share price is the central fact of the current setup. Price now sits above both the 50-day ($129.22) and 200-day ($130.94) moving averages after the post-earnings rally, and the monthly RSI has moved back into the upper half of its range — momentum has turned, but from a deeply oversold February 2026 base rather than from strength.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The recurring engine is working: FRE grew 22% YoY to $1.783B in 2Q26, AUM hit a record $1.346T, perpetual capital reached $555.6B (48% of fee-earning AUM, +15%), and inflows of $68.3B in a single quarter show the fundraising franchise is intact. Management has guided to double-digit base management fee growth returning in 2027. Meanwhile the stock is down 15.8% over twelve months, so an investor today buys 22% FRE growth at 22.4× forward earnings with a 3.3% variable yield, plus $7.5B ($6.00/share) of unrealised carry as a call option on the exit window reopening — and a $185B data-centre platform that management argues the market is not paying for.

Bear Case

The stock already trades above the $142.57 consensus target, 48% of analysts are on Hold, and the highest-quality house on the tape (Goldman) has raised its target twice without upgrading off Neutral. The growth is now concentrated in private credit — the most crowded trade in finance, priced for a benign default environment — while Real Estate, still a third of management fees, is the only shrinking segment at −3% AUM. Marks in both businesses are model- and appraisal-derived rather than traded. GAAP EPS of $4.47 covers the $4.74 dividend at only 94%, and the payout is explicitly variable and tied to realisations that management has already guided lower for 3Q26.

Top 3 Risks
01
Private credit cycle risk. Credit & Insurance is $469.3B and the primary growth engine, but its 7% trailing-twelve-month gross return was earned in a low-default environment. A genuine credit cycle would hit marks that are model-derived, compress spreads across the industry, and slow the insurance and BCRED inflows that currently supply roughly half of all firm inflows. A sector-wide de-rating would hit BX regardless of its own credit performance.
02
Real-estate marks and the rate path. Real Estate AUM is down 3% YoY at $314.1B while still generating 32.3% of management fees. Appraisal-based valuations lag transactions; life-science office is already marking down and only data-centre appreciation is offsetting it. Higher-for-longer rates simultaneously depress marks, slow realisations (which drive the dividend) and revive redemption pressure in BREIT.
03
Concentration in the AI/data-centre trade. The $185B data-centre platform is the largest single driver of appreciation firm-wide, QTS is explicitly named as such, and management is marketing the stock as an AI proxy. That is a real asset with real leases — but it means a reset in AI capex expectations would hit Blackstone's real-estate marks, its realisation pipeline, its retail-vehicle NAVs and its narrative simultaneously. Diversification across four segments is less protective than it looks when one theme drives the appreciation in the largest of them.
  Industry Commentary
The Public-to-Private Capital Shift
EMERGING TAILWIND
Capital keeps migrating out of public markets and into private vehicles, and Blackstone is the largest single beneficiary. The scale is now hard to argue with: $1,346.3B of AUM, $262.5B of inflows over the trailing twelve months, and $228.1B of dry powder waiting to be deployed. The strategically important part is not the headline number but the composition — $555.6B of perpetual capital, up 15% YoY and now 48% of fee-earning AUM. Perpetual capital does not have to be re-raised, which converts a lumpy fundraising business into something with subscription-like characteristics. That is the mechanism behind management's guidance for double-digit base management fee growth in 2027. It is also the reason FRE deserves a higher multiple than the GAAP earnings the screens show: $5.7B of FY2025 FRE against $3.02B of GAAP net income is not an accounting quirk, it is the durable half of the business being obscured by the volatile half.
Private Credit at Scale — Growth Engine and Systemic Question Mark
ONGOING HEADWIND
Private credit has gone from a niche to the largest segment at Blackstone in under a decade: $469.3B of AUM, +15% YoY, $31.0B of inflows in a single quarter, $83.6B of dry powder. Direct lending took $13.3B and infrastructure/asset-based credit $9.9B in 2Q26 alone. The bull argument is that banks retrenched and someone has to lend; the bear argument is that so much capital has arrived so quickly that spreads have compressed and underwriting standards have loosened across the industry, and that nobody can independently verify marks on loans that never trade. Blackstone's own reported numbers are reassuring — 1% gross return in the quarter, 7% over twelve months, management describing credit performance as stable — but they are self-reported, model-based, and drawn from a period with very few defaults. The valuation risk here is not that Blackstone's book goes bad; it is that the market decides the entire asset class deserves a lower multiple, which is arguably part of what the last twelve months of share-price action already reflect.
Commercial Real Estate, Rates, and the Semi-Liquid Retail Vehicle — Bull vs Bear
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is where the bull and bear cases actually meet. The bull framing: Blackstone has already rotated the portfolio out of the damaged parts of commercial real estate — data centres, logistics and rental housing now make up nearly 80% of the global equity portfolio. Real Estate segment distributable earnings rose 32% YoY to $745M on $6.6B of realisations. BREIT has demonstrably healed: repurchase requests down 42% YoY and 33% sequentially, best net flows in nearly four years, NAV up ~7% to $57B, 9.4% net annualised since inception against a public REIT index it has beaten by roughly 40%. Rate cuts from here would lift marks, accelerate realisations and revive flows all at once.
The bear framing: segment AUM still fell 3% YoY — the only segment shrinking — while supplying 32.3% of management fees, so the fee base is eroding underneath the earnings. Valuations remain appraisal-based and management has confirmed life-science office is still marking down. And the structural point from 2022–23 has not been engineered away: BREIT's liquidity is capped at roughly 2% of NAV monthly and 5% quarterly, and when requests exceeded those caps investors were prorated for months. That worked as designed, but "worked as designed" is precisely the bear case — a semi-liquid vehicle sold to wealth-channel investors delivers full liquidity only when few people want it. Higher-for-longer rates would compress marks, stall realisations and re-test that mechanism simultaneously. Which of these two framings is right is, more than anything else, a view on the path of long-term interest rates and on whether AI-driven data-centre demand holds.
