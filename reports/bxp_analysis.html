01 Company Overview

BXP is the landlord of the American trophy office. Founded in Boston in 1970 by Mortimer Zuckerman and Edward Linde, listed in June 1997 as Boston Properties and renamed BXP, Inc. on July 1, 2024 — a name that no longer ties it to just one of its six regions (Boston, Los Angeles, New York, San Francisco, Seattle and Washington, DC). At June 30, 2026 it owned or had interests in 164 properties totalling about 51.1 million square feet: 143 office, 13 retail, 7 residential (four under construction) and one hotel. It is a REIT, so the number that matters is not GAAP earnings but FFO: $1.78 per diluted share in Q2 2026, up from $1.71, with full-year 2026 guidance raised to $6.99–$7.05.

Here is the hand the market has been dealt. Five years ago this stock closed September 2021 at $108.35; it now sits at $64.13. Along the way the company took $1.22 per share of non-cash impairments in Q3 2025, cut its quarterly dividend from $0.98 to $0.70 in September 2025, and put roughly 30 non-core properties up for sale to fund development. And yet the operating line has turned: occupancy has risen three quarters running, from 86.0% (Sep 2025) to 86.7% (Dec 2025), 87.4% (Mar 2026) and 88.4% (Jun 2026), leasing ran at 1.8 million square feet in Q2 on a 9.9-year average term, and the flagship 343 Madison Avenue tower is roughly half pre-leased with a $1.2B construction loan in hand. The stock trades at about 9.1x forward FFO with a 4.37% yield that FFO covers about 2.5 times. The debate is simple to state: is office occupancy recovering fast enough to out-run the cost of refinancing cheap 2010s-era debt at 2026 rates?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Six-city gateway CBD footprint ~10-year lease terms In-house development platform Unsecured bond market access Scale: 51.1M sq ft Flight-to-quality tenant demand

The office market after 2020 split in two: commodity buildings that nobody wants, and the newest, best-located towers that tenants consolidate into. BXP's moat is being on the right side of that split at scale — its CBD portfolio runs 90.7% occupied against 88.4% for the whole book, and new leases are being signed for roughly a decade. The development platform compounds the advantage: a landlord that can build the next trophy tower (343 Madison) and pre-lease half of it before delivery is selling a product that older stock cannot match. The moat protects occupancy and rent. It does not protect the cost of capital — and for a landlord carrying $16.36B of debt, that is the other half of the equation.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage in a higher-rate world. Total debt of $16.36B against a $11.39B market cap; debt/EBITDA of 10.0x on StockAnalysis's basis and 7.94x net debt/EBITDAre on the company's. Every maturity that rolls from a 2010s coupon to a 2026 coupon takes FFO with it — the 2.75% → 6.05% refinancing is the template.
02
Asset values have already been marked down. Non-cash impairments of $1.22 per share in Q3 2025 and $0.10 per share in Q2 2026 (Sumner Square, Washington, DC, ahead of an anticipated sale). Impairments do not touch FFO, but they are the accounting admission that some buildings are worth less than they cost — and the disposition programme sells into that market.
03
The dividend has been cut once already. $0.98 to $0.70 a quarter in September 2025 (−28.6%), with the CEO telling investors it would free roughly $50M of capital a quarter. The new dividend is well covered by FFO (about 40%), but income investors who owned it for the payout were the ones who paid for the reset.
04
Development risk is concentrated. 343 Madison is a $2B project with about 50% pre-leased and delivery not expected until late 2029. The remaining half has to be leased into whatever the Manhattan office market looks like three years from now, with a floating-rate construction loan at SOFR + 2.50% until milestones cut it to + 2.25%.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP earnings over the last two years are a list of one-offs. Q4 2024: a net loss of $1.45 per share, which is why FY2024 EPS was just $0.09. Q3 2025: non-cash impairment charges of $1.22 per share, producing a −$0.77 quarter. None of these flow through FFO, which is precisely why FFO is the lens: FFO per share moved in a narrow band — $7.10 (FY2024), $6.85 (FY2025), $6.99–$7.05 guided for FY2026 — while GAAP EPS swung from $0.09 to $1.74.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay about 9.1x guided FFO for a CBD-weighted portfolio of premier office buildings, at a point where every operating signal has turned: occupancy up three quarters in a row to 88.4%, leased at 91.3% (tenants already signed but not yet paying), 1.8 million square feet signed in Q2 on ten-year terms, FFO guidance raised, and a 4.37% dividend covered about 2.5 times by FFO. The dividend reset and the impairments are behind it; the disposition programme is cutting leverage toward the lower 7s; 290 Binney is delivering an 8.9% cash yield and 343 Madison is half pre-leased. If occupancy reaches 91% by end-2027 as guided, FFO grows into a multiple that is still near its trough.

Bear Case

The bear case is arithmetic: $16.36B of debt is being refinanced from 2–3% coupons into 6%, and each roll takes FFO with it — $700M at 330 bps wider is about $23M a year before anything else happens. FFO per share has gone from $7.10 in 2024 to $6.85 in 2025 and is guided back only to about $7.02; the dividend has already been cut 28.6%; and at least $1.32 per share of impairments in the last four quarters say the buildings being sold are worth less than they cost. A 7.09% short interest is money betting that occupancy gains stall near 90% while interest costs keep climbing, and that 343 Madison has to lease its second half into an uncertain 2029 Manhattan.

Top 3 Risks

01
Refinancing cost. The 2.75% → 6.05% roll on the 2026 notes is the pattern for the rest of the maturity ladder. With net debt at 7.94x EBITDAre, interest expense can absorb much of the occupancy gain.
02
Occupancy stalls. The guided path is close to 90% at end-2026 and 91% at end-2027. The Q3 print on October 27 is the first test; a miss would hit the bull case at its core.
03
Development and disposition execution. 343 Madison ($2B, ~50% pre-leased, late 2029) and a multi-billion-dollar disposition programme both depend on office capital markets staying open — for leasing on one side and for buyers on the other.
Industry Commentary
Rates are the office landlord's second tenant
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For a leveraged landlord, the bond market sets two prices at once: what the buildings are worth and what it costs to hold them. The worst month in BXP's five-year series (June 2022, −20.0%) came in the year rates rose. The August 2026 financing puts a number on today's level: 6.05% for ten-year unsecured money, versus 2.75% on the notes it replaces. Until that gap closes, higher occupancy is partly offset by interest expense.

Flight to quality — the split office market
ONGOING HEADWIND

Office demand has not recovered evenly; it has concentrated. BXP's CBD portfolio at 90.7% occupied versus 88.4% overall shows the spread inside one landlord, and the company is selling the weaker end — suburban office, land and non-core assets — to fund the stronger end. On the Q2 call, management described demand picking up from technology, AI, defence and cybersecurity clients across its markets (paraphrased). The headwind is for everyone else's buildings; the question for BXP is how much of its own portfolio sits on the wrong side of the line — and the impairments suggest it is not zero.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $64.13 the stock is priced at about 9.1x guided FFO with a 4.37% yield covered roughly 2.5x. The bull framing: this is trough pricing for a portfolio whose occupancy is rising and whose best development has just come in at an 8.9% cash yield; every point of occupancy on 51.1 million square feet is real rent. The bear framing: a single-digit FFO multiple is fair for a landlord whose earnings are being eaten by refinancing, whose FFO per share is still below 2024, and whose dividend was cut a year ago. The way to decide is not the P/FFO today; it is two numbers reported every quarter — occupancy against the 90%/91% path, and net debt/EBITDAre against the lower-7x goal.
