01 Company Overview

Carrier Global makes and services the equipment that moves heat: residential and commercial air conditioners, furnaces, heat pumps, chillers, building controls, and truck-and-container refrigeration. It is the surviving core of what was, at its April 2020 spin-off from United Technologies (now RTX), a company with three businesses — HVAC, refrigeration, and fire & security. Fire & security and commercial refrigeration were sold in 2024 (transport refrigeration stayed), and two more side businesses, Riello (July 1, 2026) and NORESCO (August 3, 2026), have now been sold. FY2025 revenue was $21.75B, down 3.3% from $22.49B in FY2024; trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 is $22.79B, up 3.3% from $22.06B a year earlier. At a $43.88B market capitalisation (818.16M shares × $53.63) it trades 30% below its 52-week high of $76.76.

The third quarter turned the story from orders without profit to profit with a catch. Sales rose 12% to $6,263M (organic +15%), total company orders rose about 35% (data-centre orders about 300%), the backlog of booked-but-undelivered orders passed $8.5B (up 60%), and all four segments earned a higher adjusted operating margin than a year ago. The catches: roughly $130M of the quarter's operating profit was refunds of previously paid import tariffs (the release cites refunds under the IEEPA emergency-powers law), worth about $0.10 of the $0.87 adjusted EPS; and Carrier took a $3.1B non-cash goodwill impairment on its Climate Solutions Europe unit — an accounting write-down of the premium paid for past acquisitions, made when the business is judged worth less than the books say. It drove a GAAP operating loss of $2,273M and a GAAP loss of $3.16 a share. Management raised the sales outlook but trimmed the middle of its adjusted EPS range. Sections 02 and 04 take it apart.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED BASE + AFTERMARKET CONTRACTOR & DEALER NETWORK APPLIED CHILLER ENGINEERING CARRIER / VIESSMANN / TOSHIBA BRANDS REFRIGERANT REGULATORY SCALE DESIGNED INTO DATA-CENTRE PROJECTS TRANSICOLD COLD-CHAIN SERVICE

The real moat in HVAC is not the box; it is who gets called when the box breaks. Chillers and rooftop units run for fifteen to twenty-five years, and over that life the service, parts and controls revenue can exceed the original equipment sale — at much higher margin and with far less cyclicality. That installed base is defended by a contractor and dealer network that is expensive to build and slow to switch, because the mechanic who knows your equipment is the person who recommends the replacement. Layer on regulatory scale: refrigerant transitions (the industry's periodic forced move to lower global-warming-potential refrigerants, most recently A2L-class products) impose redesign and certification costs that large manufacturers absorb more easily than small ones. For applied commercial equipment, and specifically for data-centre cooling, being specified into the engineering design early is close to a lock for the project's life — which is why data-centre orders up about 300% in Q3 2026 mean more than a single quarter's number usually does.

In Q3 the moat shows up in margins: all four segments earned more per sales dollar than a year earlier. Two things limit how much to read into it. About $130M of the profit was refunds of tariffs paid earlier, and over nine months the group adjusted margin is still 15.5% against 16.8%.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Profit quality. Q3 adjusted operating profit of $1,091M (margin 17.4%) includes about $130M of tariff refunds. Without them (our arithmetic) it would be about $961M, a 15.3% margin against 14.8% a year earlier. The nine-month adjusted operating profit is $2,780M, down 2% from $2,837M. Group gross margin is 24.38% over the trailing twelve months against 26.8% in FY2024.
▸
Residential HVAC is housing, and housing is rates. About half the business is Americas, much of it residential replacement and new construction. Replacement demand is defensive; new-construction and discretionary-upgrade demand is not. Residential sales rose more than 30% in Q3, a strong comparison to defend next year, and a high-rate environment hits both housing turnover and the willingness to finance a new system.
▸
A big write-down on the biggest acquisition. The $3.1B Europe impairment says the European business is worth less than the books carried. It is non-cash and excluded from adjusted results, but it removed $3.3B of goodwill (from $15,501M to $12,209M, which also reflects currency and sold businesses) and lowered shareholder equity to $10,421M. The FY2024 net income of $5,604M on $22.49B of revenue — a 24.9% net margin — was likewise overwhelmingly gains from selling businesses, so GAAP earnings here swing in both directions for non-operating reasons.
▸
Leverage. At September 30, 2026 Carrier had $1,482M of short-term borrowings and $9,992M of long-term debt against $1,354M of cash: net debt of $10,120M (release), roughly flat from $10,278M at year-end. Debt-to-equity is 1.14 on stockanalysis.com's total debt (which includes lease obligations), up from 0.92 in the previous edition mainly because the write-down shrank equity. The current ratio is 1.00 and the quick ratio 0.60.

Notable One-Off Items

Large non-recurring items run in both directions across years, which is why the five-year earnings series is close to uninterpretable at face value.

Q3 2026: a $3.1B goodwill impairment and $130M of tariff refunds. The release reports a GAAP operating loss of $2,273M (stockanalysis.com's $689M operating income leaves the write-down out) and a $2,593M net loss attributable to shareowners; it reports a GAAP loss of $3.16 per share from continuing operations against adjusted EPS of $0.87. Total pre-tax adjustments were $3,364M. The tariff refunds added about $0.10 to adjusted EPS (net of about $0.02 for minority holders), so on a like-for-like basis adjusted EPS would be about $0.77 (our arithmetic) — below the $0.81 consensus. The effective adjusted tax rate was 23.5%.

FY2024 net income of $5,604M on $22,486M of revenue. A 24.9% net margin for a maker of air conditioners is not an operating result; the gains from business sales produced diluted EPS of $6.15 against operating income of only $2,330M. Free cash flow that year was $44M, against $2,168M the year before and $2,121M the year after. FY2022 net income of $3,534M (EPS $4.11) on declining revenue of $17,288M is the same effect a cycle earlier.

Q4 2025 was a near-wipeout quarter: revenue $4,837M (−6.0% YoY), operating income $93M, net income $53M, EPS $0.06. It is still in the trailing twelve months until the Q4 2026 report, and it sets up an easy comparison for the fourth quarter.

Portfolio exits are finished: Riello (July 1) and NORESCO (August 3) closed in the third quarter, bringing in $395M of cash in Q3 and $410M over nine months. The release expects divestitures to cost about 2% of 2026 sales growth: roughly $225M of revenue from Riello and $125M from NORESCO year over year. Segment figures above are on the current four-segment presentation.

Capital returns: $597M in Q3 ($399M of repurchases and $198M of dividends) and $1,742M over nine months ($1,144M of repurchases against $2,413M a year earlier, plus $598M of dividends). Management expects to return about $2.3B in 2026. Shares outstanding are down 5.21% year over year to 818.16M.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY  ·  AS OF OCTOBER 30, 2026
RSI measures how much of recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 conventionally suggests a run has been unusually one-sided to the upside ("overbought"); below 30 suggests the opposite ("oversold"). It is a description of momentum, not a forecast.

Five years, round trip. A share bought at the October 2021 month-end close of $52.23 is worth $53.63 today — a price return of roughly +2.7% over five years, or about 0.5% a year before dividends. Over the same window the stock touched $80.49 (September 2024 month-end) and $35.56 (September 2022 month-end), a 2.3× spread between the best and worst month-end prices in a business that sells air conditioners. With a beta of 1.29 (Section 04), the shares have moved more than the market, not like a defensive stock.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Orders up about 35% with data-centre orders up about 300%, a backlog above $8.5B (up 60%) and organic sales up 15% show demand converting into revenue now, not someday. All four segments earned a higher margin than a year ago, free cash flow was $674M in the quarter and about $2B is guided for the year, the portfolio sales are done, and the company is returning about $2.3B in 2026 with 5.2% fewer shares outstanding than a year earlier. At 18.75× forward earnings (16.8× on 2027), with the lowest published analyst target ($55) above the price and the shares 30% below their high, the setup is an industrial with a real tailwind priced for doubt.

Bear Case

About $130M of the quarter's profit was refunds of tariffs paid earlier; without them adjusted EPS was about $0.77 (our arithmetic) against a $0.81 consensus, and the Americas margin was lower than a year ago. Guidance implies a Q4 adjusted operating margin near 12% and EPS of $2.85–$2.90, not above the old $2.90 despite about $900M more full-year sales. The $3.1B Europe write-down says an acquisition was overpaid for; debt is $11.88B with a 1.00 current ratio; trailing GAAP earnings are negative, and the Q3 loss stays in them until the Q3 2027 report; and every target change from Sep 30 to Oct 30 was a cut. Transportation is shrinking and the Asia-Pacific margin is still down 2.7 points over nine months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margins without the refunds. The Q4 guidance implies a margin near 12%. If the underlying margin on rising sales is lower than last year's, as it was in the Americas segment in Q3, the case that demand is back weakens: the extra sales would be earning less per dollar. The Americas margin excluding refunds (about 18.6% against 19.7%) is the number to track.
02
Backlog that converts slowly or at poor prices. Record orders in commercial HVAC and data centres are long-lead, engineered projects. Between order and revenue lie cancellation risk, customer spending cuts, and fixed-price contracts signed before a cost move. An order book taken at bad prices is a liability disguised as an asset.
03
Leverage, housing and Europe. $11.5B of borrowings against $1.35B of cash, a thin current ratio and a residential business that grew more than 30% in Q3 leave limited room for a bad season. Europe has now been written down once; weaker heat-pump subsidies or demand could bring a second charge.
Industry Commentary
Data-centre cooling is the growth engine
TAILWIND

The most concrete number in Carrier's Q3 2026 disclosure is data-centre orders up about 300%, inside total company orders up about 35% and commercial HVAC orders up about 60%, with backlog above $8.5B. Computer racks that draw large amounts of power give off heat that ordinary air cooling cannot handle economically, which pushes operators toward chillers and engineered cooling systems, the long-lived, specified-in equipment where Carrier's engineering and installed-base service model is strongest. The caveat is timing: these orders convert to sales over quarters to years, which is why 2026 sales guidance rose to $23.8B–$24.0B from about $23B while orders rose much faster.

Costs, tariffs and price are still the constraint
WATCH ITEM

The release's own margin footnotes show how much the quarter leaned on refunds and a one-time correction: about $120M of tariff refunds in the Americas, a one-point benefit in Europe from correcting a prior-period item, tariff refunds in Transportation. Over nine months the group's adjusted operating margin is 15.5% against 16.8%, and the Asia-Pacific and Americas segments are both still below last year (11.2% against 13.9%, and 21.0% against 23.2%). Q3 showed volume and orders; the next two quarters will show how much of that reaches profit without refunds.

A climate-only company, built by selling the rest — now finished
PHASE COMPLETE

Since the 2020 spin, Carrier has sold fire & security, commercial refrigeration, Riello (July 1, 2026) and NORESCO (August 3, 2026). Revenue was $21.75B in FY2025 against $20.61B in FY2021, and trailing twelve-month revenue is now $22.79B: modest aggregate growth across five years of portfolio churn, with FY2022 and FY2024 earnings distorted by sale gains and 2026 earnings by a write-down.

Bull framing: the subtraction is done, what remains is a focused climate business with record orders and a data-centre tailwind, and 18.75× forward earnings is cheap for it. Bear framing: five years of reorganising have produced little net revenue growth, a price return of about 0.5% a year before dividends since October 2021, and now a $3.1B acknowledgement that Europe was overvalued. Both readings use the same numbers; the first full year of 2027 guidance is where they separate.
