01 Company Overview

Carrier Global makes and services the equipment that moves heat: residential and commercial air conditioners, furnaces, heat pumps, chillers, building controls, and truck-and-container refrigeration. It is the surviving core of what was, at the 2020 spin-off from Raytheon, a three-legged conglomerate — HVAC, refrigeration, and fire & security. Two of those three legs are gone. FY2025 revenue was $21.75B, down 3.3% from $22.49B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $22.11B, down 1.6% year over year. At a $46.69B market capitalisation it sits in the upper-middle tier of U.S. industrials, and it trades 26% below its 52-week high of $76.76.

The central debate is a timing mismatch, and it is unusually clean. Orders in Q2 2026 rose roughly 40% year over year — commercial HVAC up about 65%, data-centre-related orders up more than 300% — against reported revenue growth of 3.9% and organic growth of 3%. At the same time every one of the four reported segments saw its adjusted operating margin fall year over year, by between 70 and 350 basis points. So the order book says one thing and the income statement says another. Either the backlog converts into revenue and the margin compression proves to be mix-and-investment noise on the way to a genuine data-centre cooling cycle, or the orders are being bought with price and the margin is the true signal. Sections 02 and 04 take that apart segment by segment.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED BASE + AFTERMARKET CONTRACTOR & DEALER NETWORK APPLIED CHILLER ENGINEERING CARRIER / VIESSMANN / TOSHIBA BRANDS REFRIGERANT REGULATORY SCALE DATA-CENTRE THERMAL SPEC-IN TRANSICOLD COLD-CHAIN SERVICE

The real moat in HVAC is not the box; it is who gets called when the box breaks. Chillers and rooftop units run for fifteen to twenty-five years, and over that life the service, parts and controls revenue can exceed the original equipment sale — at much higher margin and with far less cyclicality. That installed base is defended by a contractor and dealer network that is expensive to build and slow to switch, because the mechanic who knows your equipment is the person who recommends the replacement. Layer on regulatory scale: refrigerant transitions (the industry's periodic forced migration to lower global-warming-potential refrigerants, most recently to A2L-class products) impose redesign and certification costs that large manufacturers absorb more easily than small ones, and each transition tends to consolidate share upward. For applied commercial equipment, and specifically for data-centre thermal management, being specified into the engineering design early is close to a lock for the project's life — which is what makes data-centre orders up more than 300% in Q2 2026 more meaningful than a single quarter's number usually is.

What the moat demonstrably does not do right now is protect price. All four segments lost margin year over year in the same quarter. A moat that cannot hold margin during a period of record backlog is a moat under test.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Universal margin compression. CSA −260bps, CSE −70bps, CSAME −350bps, CST −160bps, all in Q2 2026. When every segment in every geography moves the same direction, the cause is usually structural — input cost, tariffs, price competition or deliberate investment — not local execution. Group gross margin is 24.69% on a TTM basis and operating margin just 8.19%, against a full-year guide implying roughly 15% on an adjusted basis. That gap between GAAP and adjusted is where the argument lives.
▸
Residential HVAC is housing, and housing is rates. Roughly half the business is Americas, and a large share of that is residential replacement and new construction. Replacement demand is defensive; new-construction and discretionary-upgrade demand is not. A high-rate environment suppresses both housing turnover and the willingness to finance a $12,000 system, and no amount of data-centre order growth substitutes for it in the near term.
▸
Serial portfolio surgery obscures the trend line. Commercial refrigeration and fire & security sit in discontinued operations, Riello exited July 1, 2026, NORESCO is announced. Each move makes the remaining business cleaner and each one makes multi-year comparisons less reliable. FY2024 net income of $5,604M on $22.49B of revenue — a 24.9% net margin for a building-products company — was overwhelmingly divestiture gains, not operations. Anyone anchoring on that year's EPS of $6.15 is reading an artefact.
▸
Leverage against a shrinking earnings base. Debt-to-equity of 0.92, total debt $12.39B against $1.34B of cash, and a current ratio of 1.02 with a quick ratio of just 0.55. Enterprise value of $57.74B is 24% above market cap. This is a levered balance sheet being deleveraged with divestiture proceeds while TTM operating income falls 14.0%. It is manageable, not comfortable.

Notable One-Off Items

There are large non-recurring distortions in this history, and unlike most companies they run in both directions across years — which is why the five-year earnings series below is close to uninterpretable at face value.

FY2024 net income of $5,604M on $22,486M of revenue. A 24.9% net margin for a maker of air conditioners is not an operating result. FY2024 was the year of portfolio exits, and the gains flowed through net income, producing diluted EPS of $6.15 against operating income of only $2,330M. Free cash flow that same year was $44M — effectively zero — against $2,168M the year before and $2,121M the year after. Accounting income and cash income pointed in opposite directions by an order of magnitude. Use FY2024 for nothing.

FY2022 net income of $3,534M (EPS $4.11) on declining revenue of $17,288M is the same phenomenon a cycle earlier. Between them, FY2022 and FY2024 make the trailing five-year EPS chart look like a sawtooth that has nothing to do with the business.

Q4 2025 was a near-wipeout quarter: revenue $4,837M (−6.0% YoY), operating income $93M, net income $53M, diluted EPS $0.06 — down 97.8% year over year. That single quarter is doing an enormous amount of work in dragging the TTM figures down, and it will drop out of the trailing twelve months after the Q4 2026 report in early 2027. Anyone modelling the TTM P/E forward should account for that mechanical improvement.

Discontinued operations and exits: commercial refrigeration and fire & security are reported as discontinued; Riello exited July 1, 2026; NORESCO announced. Each restatement changes the comparative base. The segment figures in Section 02 are from the current four-segment presentation and are internally consistent; year-over-year comparisons that cross a restatement boundary are not.

Capital returns: approximately $640M returned to shareholders in Q2 2026 via dividends and repurchases combined, with shares outstanding down 5.05% year over year to 824.33M. Unlike many industrials, this buyback is genuinely shrinking the count.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY  ·  AS OF SEPTEMBER 10, 2026
RSI measures how much of recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 conventionally suggests a run has been unusually one-sided to the upside ("overbought"); below 30 suggests the opposite ("oversold"). It is a description of momentum, not a forecast.

Five years, round trip. A share bought at the October 2021 month-end close of $52.23 is worth $56.64 today — a price return of roughly +8.4% over five years, or about 1.6% a year before dividends. Over the same window the stock touched $80.49 (September 2024 month-end) and $35.56 (September 2022 month-end). That is a 2.3× spread between the best and worst month-end prices in a business that sells air conditioners. Carrier has been, in practice, a high-beta trade on rates, housing and industrial sentiment wearing the clothes of a defensive compounder.

The shape matters more than the endpoints. The 2022 drawdown was a rates event — the whole building-products complex de-rated as mortgage rates rose. The 2023–2024 climb, from $40.90 in May 2023 to $80.49 in September 2024, was a +97% move in sixteen months on the electrification-and-heat-pump thesis plus a cleaner, post-divestiture portfolio story. Everything since has been a giving-back: from $80.49 down to $52.84 by December 2025, a −34% decline, then a violent, low-quality recovery through the first half of 2026 — $56.31 in March, $73.35 in June, back to $61.81 in July, $58.22 in August, and $56.64 now. Four consecutive months of double-digit percentage swings in both directions is not the trading pattern of a settled thesis.

The RSI panel makes the same point technically. The 6-period monthly RSI has spent 2026 whipsawing rather than trending, which is characteristic of a stock where two plausible narratives — record orders versus collapsing segment margins — are being repriced against each other every quarter.

What can be said from sourced data: a −10.76% twelve-month price change against a market that has not fallen, combined with a stock sitting 24% of the way up its own 52-week range and 26% below its high, is consistent with material underperformance rather than a market-wide drawdown. The forward P/E of 18.40 against an estimated ~21 for the index says the market is pricing CARR at a discount to the broad market on forward earnings — unusual for a business with a secular data-centre narrative attached, and the clearest single expression of the market's scepticism about whether those forward earnings arrive.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Orders up ~40% with commercial HVAC up ~65% and data-centre orders up more than 300% is not a rounding artefact — it is a record backlog that has to convert into revenue somewhere in the next several quarters. Free cash flow of $1.93B (1.58× GAAP net income, guided to ~$2B for the year) says the cash engine is intact even while accounting earnings are depressed by Q4 2025 and by divestiture noise. At 18.4× forward earnings with consensus growth of +11% in 2026 and +15% in 2027, and with the lowest published analyst target ($60) sitting above the current price, the setup is a de-rated industrial with a secular tailwind and a shrinking share count (−5.05% YoY).

Bear Case

Every single segment lost adjusted operating margin year over year — CSA −260bps, CSAME −350bps, CST −160bps, CSE −70bps — during the same quarter that produced the record orders. That combination is what you see when volume is being bought with price. ROIC of 7.03% is below any plausible cost of capital, the balance sheet carries $12.39B of debt against $1.34B of cash with a 0.55 quick ratio, and TTM revenue is still declining 1.6%. A 40.76× trailing multiple on a business earning below its cost of capital requires the recovery to be near-certain, and the July–September 2026 price action says the market no longer thinks it is.

Top 3 Risks

01
The margin decline proves structural, not transitional. If Q3 2026 (October 27) shows another quarter of across-the-board segment margin compression, the "investing ahead of backlog" explanation fails and the market will re-price CARR as a commoditising manufacturer facing Asian price competition rather than a secular-growth climate play. CSAME's 350bps decline is the tell to watch.
02
Backlog that never converts, or converts unprofitably. Record orders in commercial HVAC and data centres are long-lead, engineered projects. Between order and revenue lie cancellation risk, hyperscaler capex revision risk, and — critically — fixed-price contracts signed before a tariff or input-cost move. An order book taken at bad prices is a liability disguised as an asset.
03
Leverage plus a housing downturn. $12.39B of debt, a 1.02 current ratio and 0.55 quick ratio leave limited room for a bad residential season. Roughly half of revenue is Americas, materially exposed to housing turnover and rate-sensitive replacement demand. Interest cost is already the reason net margin (5.52%) sits so far below operating margin (8.19%); a demand shock would compound it.
Industry Commentary
Data-centre thermal management is the real growth vector
EMERGING TAILWIND

The single most concrete number in Carrier's Q2 2026 disclosure is data-centre orders up more than 300% year over year, inside total company orders up ~40% and commercial HVAC orders up ~65%. AI compute density has made cooling a first-order constraint on data-centre construction rather than a back-office utility: high-density racks reject heat that traditional air cooling cannot handle economically, which pushes operators toward applied chillers, liquid cooling loops and engineered thermal systems — exactly the high-margin, spec-in, long-lived equipment where Carrier's applied engineering and installed-base service model are strongest. This demand is also unusually well-funded and unusually rate-insensitive compared with the housing demand that has historically driven the stock. The caveat is duration: these orders sit in backlog and convert over quarters to years, which is precisely why a 40% order increase produced only 3% organic revenue growth in the same period.

Price competition is arriving faster than the mix shift
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Four segments, four different geographies, four different end-market mixes — and all four lost adjusted operating margin in the same quarter. CSAME, the segment most directly exposed to Chinese manufacturers competing on price in Asia-Pacific and Middle East tenders, lost the most (−350bps to 11.8%, and −420bps to 10.8% on a six-month basis). Europe, at 7.2%, is already earning less than a third of the Americas' margin on a fifth of group revenue. Group gross margin of 24.69% and operating margin of 8.19% on a TTM basis are both below where this business was two years ago. The synchronised nature of the decline is what makes it a constraint rather than a stumble: local execution problems do not occur in four places at once.

A pure-play built by subtraction — and what that means for the two cases
ONGOING HEADWIND

Since the 2020 spin, Carrier has removed fire & security, commercial refrigeration, Riello (July 1, 2026) and now NORESCO. Revenue is $21.75B in FY2025 against $20.61B in FY2021 — essentially flat across five years while the company churned its portfolio. That is the cost of the strategy: five years of no aggregate growth, two years (FY2022, FY2024) of accounting earnings so distorted by disposal gains that FY2024 posted a 24.9% net margin alongside $44M of free cash flow, and a trailing earnings base too damaged to support the 40.76× trailing multiple the screens display.

Bull framing: the subtraction is finished or nearly so, what remains is a focused climate business with record orders and a genuine AI-adjacent tailwind, proceeds pay down the $12.39B debt load, and 18.4× forward earnings for that is cheap. Bear framing: the company has spent five years reorganising and has flat revenue, declining margins in every geography, a 7.03% ROIC below its cost of capital, and a share price that has returned ~1.6% a year before dividends since October 2021. Both readings use the same verified numbers. The October 27 print is where they separate.
