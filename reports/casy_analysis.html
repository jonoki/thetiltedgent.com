01 Company Overview

Casey's is a convenience-store chain out of Ankeny, Iowa, with 2,959 stores at July 31, 2026, selling gasoline, groceries, cold drinks and, unusually, a lot of pizza. That last part is the whole investment case. Most of the revenue is fuel; most of the profit is not. In the quarter to July 31, 2026, fuel was 65.6% of revenue but 36.1% of gross profit, while prepared food and dispensed beverage — pizza, breakfast, fountain drinks — was 8.7% of revenue and 23.6% of gross profit, at a 59.3% margin. Casey's is a restaurant that happens to own the gas pumps out front, and the market pays for it that way.

Fiscal 2026 (year ended April 30, 2026) was the best year in the company's history on every line: revenue $17.56B, +10.2%, net income $714.5M, +30.9%, diluted EPS $19.16. A large part of the backdrop is the Fikes Wholesale (CEFCO) acquisition — 198 stores, $1.145B all-cash, announced July 2024 and closed in November 2024 — the largest deal in Casey's history, which pushed the footprint deep into Texas and the Gulf South. On April 9, 2026 the company joined the S&P 500, replacing Hologic. The stock closed July 2026 at $871.00, its highest month-end ever. Seven weeks later it is $585.34 — down 32.8%. Nothing broke in the numbers: the September 8 quarter beat on earnings by 59 cents a share. What broke was the multiple. The central question is whether a 28x earnings multiple on a fuel-and-pizza retailer was ever the right price, or whether the market simply lost its nerve after one soft same-store sales print.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Prepared food at 59% margin 2,959-store footprint 100% company-owned stores Vertical integration ~11M rewards members M&A roll-up capacity 26 years of dividend growth

The moat is the kitchen. A pizza oven turns a fuel stop into a food stop, and it lets Casey's earn a 59.3% gross margin on prepared food — against 12.0% on fuel revenue in the same quarter. The model appears to travel: remodelled stores showed about a 30% prepared-food sales lift, per coverage of the Q1 call. At the June 24, 2026 Investor Day the company described itself as 100% company-owned and vertically integrated, with about 11 million rewards members. The vulnerability is that 36% of gross profit still depends on a fuel margin the company does not control, and that the other 60% depends on a rural and small-town consumer who is, per management, already buying fewer gallons per trip.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Fuel margin is the swing factor. Q1 margin of 47.8¢ ran above the plan's "mid 40s." The CFO said daily margins ranged from the 30s to the 60s in the quarter. A reversion of three cents on roughly 3.7B gallons a year at Q1's pace is about $110M of gross profit (our arithmetic). That is likely why management did not raise FY2027 guidance after a +17.1% EBITDA quarter.
02
Inside sales are slowing. Prepared-food same-store sales went from 5.6% to 4.8% and grocery from 3.8% to 2.7% year on year. Each is still positive, but the stock was priced for acceleration, not deceleration. Customers trading down to regular fuel and higher-ethanol blends are a sign of a stretched consumer.
03
Labour, card fees and insurance. Opex rose 8.0%, faster than the 5%–7% full-year guide, with credit-card fees up from $71.7M to $85.7M as pump prices rose. Higher fuel prices inflate card fees automatically, whether margins hold or not.
04
The multiple was the product. At the July month-end of $871.00 the stock was valued like a compounder. At $585.34 it is still 28.2x trailing earnings — versus 15.55x for Murphy USA and 16.35x for Couche-Tard. Even after a 32.8% fall, Casey's carries a premium that needs the prepared-food story to keep working.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business just had its best quarter ever: EPS +27.7%, EBITDA +17.1%, inside margin 42.2%, fuel margin 47.8¢, and a Fikes integration running ahead of schedule with remodelled stores seeing about a 30% prepared-food lift. Guidance of 8%–10% EBITDA growth looks conservative after a +17.1% first quarter, and management usually updates after Q2. The stock has fallen 32.8% from its July month-end without a single reported number getting worse on an absolute basis. Every one of the 20 analyst targets is above the price, the low being $687. A 400-store pipeline, a $1B buyback authorisation and a ~$2B three-year free cash flow target give the company plenty of ways to compound per-share value.

Bear Case

The quarter's beat came from fuel margin — the one line Casey's does not control — while the part the market pays a premium for slowed: prepared-food same-store sales fell to 4.8% from 5.6%, grocery to 2.7% from 3.8%, and customers are buying fewer gallons per trip. Opex grew 8.0%, ahead of the 5%–7% guide. The new plan targets roughly half the EBITDA growth of the last one (8%–10% versus 16%), yet the stock still trades at 28.2x trailing earnings, nearly double Murphy USA. Analyst targets are anchored to the old price and are being cut in $105–$225 steps. The monthly trend has turned down for the first time in five years. Paying a premium multiple for decelerating growth is how a quality stock becomes a merely fair one.

Top 3 Risks

01
Fuel-margin reversion. Q1's 47.8¢ is above the plan's "mid 40s." Fuel is 36% of gross profit; if margins revert while opex keeps rising at 5%–8%, EBITDA growth falls back toward the bottom of the 8%–10% guide.
02
Inside same-store sales slowing further. 3.2% in Q1 against a 2%–5% guide and a 4.1% market expectation. A second quarter near the bottom of the range would challenge the premium multiple, which rests on prepared food.
03
Multiple compression still has room. At 28.2x trailing earnings versus 15.55x for Murphy USA and 16.35x for Couche-Tard, a move toward peer multiples would be a larger drawdown than the one already taken — even with earnings still growing.
Industry Commentary
Fuel volatility: a tailwind that looks like a risk
SWING FACTOR

Convenience-store fuel margins usually widen when wholesale prices fall and compress when they rise, because pump prices lag. Q1 FY2027 was the unusual case of higher prices and higher margins: revenue per gallon up about a third, margin up 6.8¢ to 47.8¢. The CFO's description of daily margins from the 30s to the 60s says the average hid a lot of volatility. The consumer side is visible in the data: same-store gallons −0.3%, fewer gallons per trip, trading down to regular and higher-ethanol blends. Credit-card fees, which scale with the pump price, rose from $71.7M to $85.7M. For Casey's, high fuel prices are a margin gift that costs a little traffic and a lot of card fees.

Consolidation: the roll-up runway
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Casey's grows by buying and rebuilding smaller chains. It added 504 stores in FY24–FY26 and targets at least 400 more in FY27–FY29, about half through acquisitions. The Fikes playbook — buy stores, install the kitchen, see a ~30% prepared-food lift — is the template. Peers are pursuing the same idea at different scales: StockAnalysis notes Couche-Tard is pursuing an $8.6B deal for Żabka Group, and Murphy USA is piloting an autonomous foodservice platform. When every fuel retailer wants to become a food retailer, the price of good stores goes up and the prepared-food edge narrows.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
THE DEBATE

At $585.34 CASY trades at 28.2x trailing and 27.4x forward earnings, 15.34x EV/EBITDA, and a 3.0% free-cash-flow yield. The bull framing: a business that grew EPS 31% last year and 28% last quarter, with a 16% EBITDA CAGR record, has never been a 15x fuel retailer, and the sell-off punished a sales miss of under a point while earnings beat by 9%. The bear framing: the premium was built on accelerating inside sales and a 16% EBITDA CAGR; the new plan targets 8%–10%, inside sales are decelerating, and the quarter's beat came from fuel. Paying 28x for 8%–10% growth needs the prepared-food engine to keep taking share. The way to tell which view is right is not the P/E.
