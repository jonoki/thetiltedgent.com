01 Company Overview

CATL makes batteries — the cells and packs inside electric cars and trucks, and the large battery systems that power grids use to store solar and wind energy. It is the biggest player in both. Citing SNE Research, its 2026 interim report puts CATL's share of the world's EV battery installations at 40.2% for January–May 2026, up 2.2 percentage points on a year earlier, and says it ranked first in global energy-storage battery shipments for January–June 2026. Overseas customers named in the same report include Volkswagen, Stellantis, BMW, Volvo and Toyota. FY2025 revenue was RMB 423.70B, up 17.04%, on 661 GWh of lithium-ion battery sales, and net profit attributable to shareholders rose 42.28% to RMB 72.20B. The first half of 2026 was faster still: revenue RMB 276.92B (+54.80%) and net profit RMB 43.28B (+41.98%).

Two listings, one company. The A shares have traded on Shenzhen's ChiNext board since June 11, 2018 (ticker 300750); this report uses them because that is where most of the shares trade — 4.41B A shares against 0.22B H shares. On May 20, 2025 CATL added a Hong Kong listing (3750), selling 155,915,300 H shares at HK$263.00 and raising HK$41.0B, earmarked for its Hungary plant and general purposes; on April 30, 2026 it placed another 62,385,000 H shares at HK$628.20 for gross proceeds of about HK$39.19B. On October 22, 2026 the H shares closed at HK$338.40 — about CNY 288.00 at that day's HKD/CNY close of 0.8511 (Yahoo), 31% above the A-share price for the same claim on the same company. The stock's story this year is a round trip that kept going: the A shares closed April at CNY 436.00 and touched an intraday high of CNY 468.75 on May 7, fell 19.9% in September as reports showed several Chinese carmakers spreading battery orders to rivals, then lost another 24.3% in October to the 22nd, to CNY 220.41, after a September quarter in which profit grew far more slowly than sales. That close is about half the April one, and puts the company at 11.45x trailing earnings, while the average target on MarketScreener's 30-analyst panel is about twice the price. The quarter settled one question and opened another: sales kept growing, but the margin did not hold.

The latest quarter. The September quarter, released after the Shenzhen close on Tuesday, October 20, brought revenue of RMB 165.22B (+58.58%) but net profit of only RMB 21.25B (+14.54%), 5.8% less than in the June quarter. The Seoul Economic Daily called it the slowest profit growth in seven quarters, against a market forecast of 41.2%. Basic earnings per share were CNY 4.62 (+12.68%). Gross margin — what is left of sales after the direct cost of making the batteries — fell to about 20.7% in the quarter (our calculation from the nine-month and half-year figures), from 25.8% a year earlier; for the nine months it was 22.72%, down 2.59 points. The consumption tax is booked below gross profit, under taxes and surcharges, which rose to RMB 2.21B in the quarter from RMB 0.60B a year earlier; counting that line, the quarter's margin was about 19.3% (our calculations from the quarterly and interim reports; Dow Jones reported the same 19.3%). At its results briefing the company put the pressure on profit down to a shift in product mix, the consumption tax on lithium batteries that resumed at 2% on September 1, and the cut in the export VAT rebate on batteries from 9% to 6% in April (Global Times; CnEVPost). CFO Zheng Shu said the tax is built into product prices, so the extra cost is hard to pass on without renegotiating prices with customers (CnEVPost). The tax rises to 4% in September 2027 and the rebate goes entirely in January 2027. On volume, the company said EV and storage battery sales together exceeded 260GWh in the quarter, about a quarter of it storage, and the company said it is talking with customers about sharing the tax burden and that demand in 2027 still looks promising (Economic Information Daily, via Eastmoney). The A shares fell 12.03% in the first session after the release (CNY 259.08 → 227.90 on Wednesday, October 21) and another 3.29% the next day. CATL gave no revenue or profit forecast.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale: 40.2% of world EV battery use #1 in storage shipments Chemistry breadth (LFP, NMC, sodium-ion) 60,319 patents held or pending Global carmaker qualification Upstream mines & recycling Net cash ~RMB 184B

The moat is cost and qualification at scale. A battery cell has to pass years of safety and durability testing before a carmaker puts it in a model, so a supplier that is already qualified across dozens of brands, on three continents, is hard to replace quickly. CATL also spent RMB 18.52B on R&D in the first nine months of 2026 (+22.89%) and sits on enough cash to keep building plants through a price war. The weakness is that a battery is still a component its customers would rather make or multi-source: the best-run carmakers have every incentive not to depend on one supplier with a 40% world share and a gross margin more than double Li Auto's 9.4% second-quarter vehicle margin (CnEVPost, Sep 16, 2026).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customers are spreading their orders. CnEVPost reported on September 16, 2026 that Li Auto had moved all versions of its new L8, launched in June, to Sunwoda cells and plans to invest RMB 2.65B in Sunwoda's EV battery unit; that Xiaomi had widened its battery suppliers to four (CATL, FinDreams, CALB, Sunwoda), with its newest lineup supplied by Sunwoda and CALB; and that Aito had added CALB and Gotion. Xpeng made CALB its largest supplier several years ago. Each move is small against RMB 192B of half-year EV revenue; the pattern is what the market sold.
02
Profit per unit is falling. EV battery gross margin fell to 20.63% from 22.41% in H1 2026 and storage to 23.96% from 25.52%; in the September quarter group gross margin was about 20.7%, against 25.8% a year earlier (our calculation). In July management said profit per unit had stayed broadly stable over several quarters (investor-relations record). In October it named product mix, the battery consumption tax and the smaller export rebate as the causes, and CFO Zheng Shu said the tax is hard to pass on without renegotiating prices with customers (CnEVPost) — customers who now have other suppliers to negotiate with.
03
Cash flow lagged profit. Nine-month operating cash flow fell 23.60% while net profit rose 31.60%, and the September quarter added only about RMB 1.4B (our calculation); inventories reached RMB 144.77B, up 53.15% since December. In July the company said the build-up was stock laid in ahead of second-half demand (investor-relations record); in October it pointed to changed payment arrangements with suppliers (Economic Information Daily, via Eastmoney). The December quarter is the next check.
04
US policy risk. The Pentagon added CATL to its Section 1260H list of Chinese military companies in January 2025 (UPI, Jan 7, 2025); CATL called its inclusion a mistake, saying it does no military-related work. The designation was then a warning rather than a ban, but from June 30, 2026 Section 805 of the 2024 defense act bars the Pentagon itself from contracting with listed companies (Holland & Knight). CATL was not among the ten entities removed in the June 8, 2026 update, which added rivals BYD, CALB and EVE Energy (WilmerHale). Overseas revenue is 31.46% of the total and earns a higher margin than domestic sales, so access to Western markets matters to the profit mix.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items move the per-share and cash figures. First, new shares: the April 30, 2026 H-share placement added 62,385,000 shares and about HK$39.19B of cash, and the May 2025 Hong Kong listing added 155,915,300; the weighted share count behind EPS rose about 2.6% year on year in the first nine months (our calculation from reported profit and EPS), so EPS grew more slowly than profit (28.22% vs 31.60%). Second, non-recurring gains: profit excluding non-recurring gains and losses was RMB 57.88B in the first nine months of 2026 against RMB 64.53B reported (September quarter: RMB 18.86B against RMB 21.25B). Third, the special dividend: FY2025's distribution of 50% of profit included a one-off CNY 4.779 per share on top of the CNY 2.178 ordinary final, so the trailing yield overstates the run rate. The per-share chart and figures before April 2023 are adjusted for that month's bonus issue of 8 new shares for every 10 held.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At CNY 220.41 you pay 11.45x trailing and 11.4x consensus 2026 earnings for the world's largest battery maker — 40.2% of global EV battery use, first in storage — whose revenue rose 58.58% and profit 14.54% in the September quarter, with roughly RMB 184B of net cash, a ~5.9% free-cash-flow yield and a buyback that had taken in 18.5M shares by October 22. The company sold more than 260GWh of batteries in the quarter, about a quarter of it storage, a business that does not depend on any carmaker's sourcing decision — so the miss came from margin, and part of that is a tax the company says it is working out with customers how to share. The shares now trade below the CSI 300's 12.62x, and the average analyst target, even after the cuts, is about twice the price. If the tax is passed on through pricing over 2027, the market has priced a lasting margin loss that may prove temporary.

Bear Case

The September quarter showed what the September slide feared: revenue up 58.6% but profit up only 14.5%, and gross margin down to about 20.7% from 25.8% a year earlier. The battery consumption tax doubles to 4% in September 2027 and the export rebate goes in January 2027, and the CFO says the tax is hard to pass on without renegotiating prices — with carmakers that, as Li Auto, Xiaomi and Aito showed by September, now have other suppliers to turn to. Nine-month operating cash flow fell 23.6% while inventory rose 53% since December, and the company raised RMB 49.98B from bond issues in the nine months. Five years on, the stock is 37.9% below its October 2021 close even though profit rose 64% from 2023 to 2025 — the market has long refused to pay a growth multiple for a component maker, and losing pricing power is how that refusal gets justified.

Top 3 Risks

01
Customer diversification becomes a price war. By September 2026 Li Auto (Sunwoda cells for the L8), Xiaomi (Sunwoda and CALB for its newest lineup) and Aito (adding CALB and Gotion) had spread business to rivals; Xpeng made CALB its largest supplier years earlier (CnEVPost, Sep 16, 2026). If more carmakers follow or use the threat to renegotiate — while CATL is itself renegotiating prices to pass on the battery tax — the EV battery gross margin (20.63% in H1) is the line that pays.
02
Working capital and cash conversion. Inventories rose RMB 50.2B in nine months, to RMB 144.77B, and nine-month operating cash flow fell 23.60% to RMB 61.63B, with only about RMB 1.4B in the September quarter (our calculation). The company points to changed payment arrangements with suppliers (Economic Information Daily, via Eastmoney). Another quarter like it would mean profit no longer turns into cash at the old rate.
03
Geopolitics and overseas access. The Pentagon added CATL to its 1260H list in January 2025 and kept it there in the June 2026 update, and 31.46% of its revenue — the higher-margin part — comes from outside China, with plants under construction in Indonesia and Spain and the Hungary plant reported to have begun cell production only at the end of September, after environmental and workplace-safety problems (Caixin, Sep 30, 2026). Tighter Western rules on Chinese battery content would hit the most profitable part of the mix.
Industry Commentary
Carmakers want a second (and third) supplier
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

CnEVPost's September 16, 2026 report set out the pattern: Li Auto plans to invest RMB 2.65B in Sunwoda's EV battery business, which would make it the second-largest shareholder, and fitted every version of its new L8, launched in June, with Sunwoda cells; Xiaomi now buys from four battery makers, with its newest models supplied by Sunwoda and CALB; Aito added CALB and Gotion; Xpeng made CALB its largest supplier several years ago. The same article set CATL's 23.93% gross margin beside Li Auto's 9.4% vehicle margin. Car companies whose own margins are thin have a clear reason to share out battery orders, and the rivals are now big enough to take them. CATL's answer so far is product range (new Shenxing, Qilin, Freevoy and sodium-ion generations in 2026) and a domestic passenger-car share that, per its interim report, still rose 5.6 points to 46.7% in the first half. Officialdom has weighed in: on September 22 a news site under the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology called supplier diversification normal commercial practice that should not be labelled a move away from CATL or cast as carmakers against battery makers. Zeng Yuqun's own framing, in a video address to the 2026 World Power Battery Conference (SMM, September 3), was quality: carmakers launched more than 600 new models in China in the first half, nearly three a day, racing on speed, specifications and price, and in battery mass production small problems are magnified many times over, so every process has to be controlled from the design stage.

Energy storage: the second engine
ONGOING SHIFT

The interim report ties storage demand to growing electricity use (including AI data centres), wind and solar reaching 19.4% of global generation in January–May 2026 (Ember) and Chinese provinces adopting capacity tariffs that make storage projects pay. Global storage-battery shipments rose about 93% in the first half (SMM). CATL's storage revenue grew 87.54% to RMB 53.26B and is now 19.2% of the total. Storage buyers — utilities, developers and system integrators — are a different customer base from carmakers, which makes this line the natural offset to the EV sourcing problem, though its gross margin also slipped, to 23.96% from 25.52%.

Valuation after a 49% drop — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

From the April close to October 22 the A shares fell 49.4%. At CNY 220.41 they trade at 11.45x trailing earnings against 12.62x for the CSI 300, and the H shares in Hong Kong price the same company 31% higher. The bull framing: a market leader that grew profit 42% in 2025 and 32% in the first nine months of 2026, holding RMB 370B of cash and buying back its own stock, now trades below the average large Chinese company; the H-share premium shows international investors still value it above where the domestic market has left it. The bear framing: the A-share market is the larger and more liquid line and is pricing what it sees in China first — carmakers taking orders elsewhere, new battery taxes, margins and cash flow falling — and five years of decline say the market has never given a component maker the multiple its growth seems to deserve. The December quarter, the first full one with the tax, is the next test.
