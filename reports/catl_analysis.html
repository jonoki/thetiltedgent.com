01 Company Overview

CATL makes batteries — the cells and packs inside electric cars and trucks, and the large battery systems that power grids use to store solar and wind energy. It is the biggest player in both. Citing SNE Research, its 2026 interim report puts CATL's share of the world's EV battery installations at 40.2% for January–May 2026, up 2.2 percentage points on a year earlier, and says it ranked first in global energy-storage battery shipments for January–June 2026. Overseas customers named in the same report include Volkswagen, Stellantis, BMW, Volvo and Toyota. FY2025 revenue was RMB 423.70B, up 17.04%, on 661 GWh of lithium-ion battery sales, and net profit attributable to shareholders rose 42.28% to RMB 72.20B. The first half of 2026 was faster still: revenue RMB 276.92B (+54.80%) and net profit RMB 43.28B (+41.98%).

Two listings, one company. The A shares have traded on Shenzhen's ChiNext board since June 11, 2018 (ticker 300750); this report uses them because that is where most of the shares trade — 4.41B A shares against 0.22B H shares. On May 20, 2025 CATL added a Hong Kong listing (3750), selling 155,915,300 H shares at HK$263.00 and raising HK$41.0B, earmarked for its Hungary plant and general purposes; on April 30, 2026 it placed another 62,385,000 H shares at HK$628.20 for gross proceeds of about HK$39.19B. On September 30, 2026 the H shares closed at HK$484.20 — about CNY 413.65 at that day's HKD/CNY close of 0.8543 (Yahoo), 42% above the A-share price for the same claim on the same company. The stock's story this year is a round trip: the A shares closed April at CNY 436.00, touched an intraday high of CNY 468.75 on May 7, then fell 19.9% in September alone to CNY 291.11 as reports showed several Chinese carmakers spreading battery orders to rivals. At that close the company traded at 15.5x trailing earnings while every price target on MarketScreener's 29-analyst panel sat at least 54% higher. One side of that gap is wrong; the September 2026 quarter, the first with the customer shifts in it, is where the answer starts to show.

The latest quarter. The June quarter, released after the Shenzhen close on Friday, July 24, brought revenue of RMB 147.8B (+56.9%) and net profit of RMB 22.5B (+36.5%), ahead of the 29.7% profit growth analysts expected (LSEG SmartEstimate, as reported by Reuters). The A shares rose 4.44% in the first session after the release (CNY 383.01 → 400.00 on Monday, July 27). Margin was the soft spot: BOCOM International put second-quarter gross margin at 23.2%, 1.6 points below the first quarter, mainly because rising raw-material costs had not yet been passed on to customers. On the July 24 results call, where board secretary and vice president Jiang Li and CFO Zheng Shu answered questions, the company said, per its investor-relations record, that EV and storage battery volumes together rose about 60% in the half, with storage about a quarter of them, and that its plants were running close to full: 525GWh of effective capacity, with 764GWh under construction and most of it due to start production within two years. CATL gave no revenue or profit forecast for 2026, in the interim report or on the call; its outlook was that the storage market will keep growing fairly fast this year and next.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale: 40.2% of world EV battery use #1 in storage shipments Chemistry breadth (LFP, NMC, sodium-ion) 60,319 patents held or pending Global carmaker qualification Upstream mines & recycling Net cash ~RMB 225B

The moat is cost and qualification at scale. A battery cell has to pass years of safety and durability testing before a carmaker puts it in a model, so a supplier that is already qualified across dozens of brands, on three continents, is hard to replace quickly. CATL also spent RMB 11.38B on R&D in the first half of 2026 alone (+12.70%) and sits on enough cash to keep building plants through a price war. The weakness is that a battery is still a component its customers would rather make or multi-source: the best-run carmakers have every incentive not to depend on one supplier with a 40% world share and a gross margin more than double Li Auto's 9.4% second-quarter vehicle margin (CnEVPost, Sep 16, 2026).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customers are spreading their orders. CnEVPost reported on September 16, 2026 that Li Auto had moved all versions of its new L8, launched in June, to Sunwoda cells and plans to invest RMB 2.65B in Sunwoda's EV battery unit; that Xiaomi had widened its battery suppliers to four (CATL, FinDreams, CALB, Sunwoda), with its newest lineup supplied by Sunwoda and CALB; and that Aito had added CALB and Gotion. Xpeng made CALB its largest supplier several years ago. Each move is small against RMB 192B of half-year EV revenue; the pattern is what the market sold.
02
Profit per unit is drifting down. EV battery gross margin fell to 20.63% from 22.41% in H1 2026 and storage to 23.96% from 25.52%. Profit still rose 42% on volume, but if customers who now have alternatives use them to negotiate, the price cut lands on a margin that has already started to slip. Management's answer on the July 24 call was that profit per unit has stayed broadly stable over the past several quarters, with short-term swings from raw-material prices, spending on new businesses and product mix (investor-relations record).
03
Cash flow lagged profit. H1 2026 operating cash flow rose 2.61% while net profit rose 41.98%; inventories grew 38% in six months to RMB 130.82B. A building stockpile in a half when demand was strong is worth watching in a quarter when some customers are leaving. The company's explanation on the July 24 call: the build-up is mainly stock laid in ahead of second-half demand (investor-relations record). The September quarter will show whether that demand arrived.
04
US policy risk. The Pentagon added CATL to its Section 1260H list of Chinese military companies in January 2025 (UPI, Jan 7, 2025); CATL called its inclusion a mistake, saying it does no military-related work. The designation was then a warning rather than a ban, but from June 30, 2026 Section 805 of the 2024 defense act bars the Pentagon itself from contracting with listed companies (Holland & Knight). CATL was not among the ten entities removed in the June 8, 2026 update, which added rivals BYD, CALB and EVE Energy (WilmerHale). Overseas revenue is 31.46% of the total and earns a higher margin than domestic sales, so access to Western markets matters to the profit mix.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items move the per-share and cash figures. First, new shares: the April 30, 2026 H-share placement added 62,385,000 shares and about HK$39.19B of cash, and the May 2025 Hong Kong listing added 155,915,300; the weighted share count behind EPS rose about 3.3% year on year (our calculation from reported profit and EPS), so EPS grew more slowly than profit (37.43% vs 41.98%). Second, non-recurring gains: profit excluding non-recurring gains and losses was RMB 39.01B in H1 2026 against RMB 43.28B reported. Third, the special dividend: FY2025's distribution of 50% of profit included a one-off CNY 4.779 per share on top of the CNY 2.178 ordinary final, so the trailing yield overstates the run rate. The per-share chart and figures before April 2023 are adjusted for that month's bonus issue of 8 new shares for every 10 held.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At CNY 291.11 you pay 15.5x trailing and 14.1x consensus 2026 earnings for the world's largest battery maker — 40.2% of global EV battery use, first in storage — growing revenue 54.80% and profit 41.98% in its latest half, with roughly RMB 225B of net cash, a ~6.5% free-cash-flow yield and a buyback of RMB 20–40B under way. Storage, now 19.2% of revenue and growing 87.5%, does not depend on any carmaker's sourcing decision. The June quarter beat the profit consensus (+36.5% against +29.7% expected, LSEG via Reuters), management says its plants are running close to full, and JPMorgan and CLSA stood by the stock through the September fall. No customer loss has yet shown up in reported numbers; if Q3 margins hold, the market has priced a loss of franchise that has not happened.

Bear Case

A 40% world share and a gross margin more than double Li Auto's vehicle margin is exactly the position carmakers organise to break, and by September 2026 Li Auto, Xiaomi and Aito had shown they could. Margins in both main lines already fell in H1, and second-quarter gross margin slipped 1.6 points from the first as raw-material costs ran ahead of prices (BOCOM International); operating cash flow rose 2.6% against 42% profit growth, and inventory jumped 38% in six months. Analyst targets sit 54–175% above the price, and the latest dated target changes on MarketScreener predate the customer news. The stock went nowhere for five years even as profit rose 64% from 2023 to 2025 — the market has long refused to pay a growth multiple for a component maker, and losing pricing power is how that refusal gets justified.

Top 3 Risks

01
Customer diversification becomes a price war. By September 2026 Li Auto (Sunwoda cells for the L8), Xiaomi (Sunwoda and CALB for its newest lineup) and Aito (adding CALB and Gotion) had spread business to rivals; Xpeng made CALB its largest supplier years earlier (CnEVPost, Sep 16, 2026). If more carmakers follow or use the threat to renegotiate, the 20.63% EV battery gross margin is the line that pays.
02
Working capital and cash conversion. Inventories up RMB 36.3B and customer prepayments down RMB 12.8B in H1 2026 held operating cash flow growth to 2.61%. The company says the stock was laid in for second-half demand; the September quarter is the test. Another half like it would mean profit no longer turns into cash at the old rate.
03
Geopolitics and overseas access. The Pentagon added CATL to its 1260H list in January 2025 and kept it there in the June 2026 update, and 31.46% of its revenue — the higher-margin part — comes from outside China, with plants under construction in Indonesia and Spain and the Hungary plant reported to have begun cell production only at the end of September, after environmental and workplace-safety problems (Caixin, Sep 30, 2026). Tighter Western rules on Chinese battery content would hit the most profitable part of the mix.
Industry Commentary
Carmakers want a second (and third) supplier
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

CnEVPost's September 16, 2026 report set out the pattern: Li Auto plans to invest RMB 2.65B in Sunwoda's EV battery business, which would make it the second-largest shareholder, and fitted every version of its new L8, launched in June, with Sunwoda cells; Xiaomi now buys from four battery makers, with its newest models supplied by Sunwoda and CALB; Aito added CALB and Gotion; Xpeng made CALB its largest supplier several years ago. The same article set CATL's 23.93% gross margin beside Li Auto's 9.4% vehicle margin. Car companies whose own margins are thin have a clear reason to share out battery orders, and the rivals are now big enough to take them. CATL's answer so far is product range (new Shenxing, Qilin, Freevoy and sodium-ion generations in 2026) and a domestic passenger-car share that, per its interim report, still rose 5.6 points to 46.7% in the first half. Officialdom has weighed in: on September 22 a news site under the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology called supplier diversification normal commercial practice that should not be labelled a move away from CATL or cast as carmakers against battery makers. Zeng Yuqun's own framing, in a video address to the 2026 World Power Battery Conference (SMM, September 3), was quality: carmakers launched more than 600 new models in China in the first half, nearly three a day, racing on speed, specifications and price, and in battery mass production small problems are magnified many times over, so every process has to be controlled from the design stage.

Energy storage: the second engine
ONGOING SHIFT

The interim report ties storage demand to growing electricity use (including AI data centres), wind and solar reaching 19.4% of global generation in January–May 2026 (Ember) and Chinese provinces adopting capacity tariffs that make storage projects pay. Global storage-battery shipments rose about 93% in the first half (SMM). CATL's storage revenue grew 87.54% to RMB 53.26B and is now 19.2% of the total. Storage buyers — utilities, developers and system integrators — are a different customer base from carmakers, which makes this line the natural offset to the EV sourcing problem, though its gross margin also slipped, to 23.96% from 25.52%.

Valuation after a 33% drop — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At CNY 291.11 the A shares trade at 15.5x trailing earnings against 14.34x for the CSI 300, and the H shares in Hong Kong price the same company 42% higher. The bull framing: a market leader that grew profit 42% in 2025 and again in the first half of 2026, holding RMB 372B of cash and buying back its own stock is priced barely above the average large Chinese company; the H-share premium shows international investors value it well above where the domestic market has left it. The bear framing: the A-share market is the larger and more liquid line and is pricing what it sees in China first — carmakers taking orders elsewhere, margins edging down, cash flow lagging — and five flat years say the market has never given a component maker the multiple its growth seems to deserve. The Q3 2026 margins and cash flow are the next test.
