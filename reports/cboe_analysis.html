01 Company Overview

In April 1973 a handful of traders in Chicago started swapping standardised stock options on a floor carved out of the Chicago Board of Trade. Fifty-three years later that experiment is Cboe Global Markets: the home of SPX options, the operator of the VIX, and one of the largest US options and equities exchange groups. The company went public in June 2010, bought Bats Global Markets for roughly $3.2 billion in 2017, renamed itself Cboe Global Markets in October 2017, and joined the S&P 500 at the open on March 1, 2017.

The business is a toll road on risk. Every time someone hedges a portfolio, sells a covered call or trades a same-day SPX contract, Cboe collects a fee. In Q3 2026 that toll road produced net revenue of $712.2M, up 18%, and record diluted EPS of $4.08, up 43%, helped by a one-off $71.5M gain on selling Cboe Australia; adjusted EPS, which leaves out one-off items like that gain, was $3.63, up 36% and above the $3.43 analysts expected (Nasdaq consensus). Options alone were 66% of net revenue. The stock had closed June at $242.67 — down 27% in a single month — after the futures regulator approved a new kind of crypto contract that the market read as a threat to retail options volume. It has since climbed back: on September 29 Cboe and S&P Dow Jones Indices extended Cboe's exclusive S&P 500 options license through 2051, on October 30 Cboe reported its record quarter and raised its growth guidance for the third time this year, and the stock closed at $344.28 on November 3, 2026, 41.9% above the June close. The central question: is the SPX franchise a moat that new product wrappers cannot cross, or is the retail short-dated-options boom that powers the numbers a flow that can move elsewhere?

Two very different businesses under one heading. Index options — SPX, VIX and their minis — are proprietary: Cboe holds exclusive rights to list options on the S&P 500, now extended through 2051, so no rival exchange can compete for that order flow. The proprietary half is the moat; the multi-listed half is volume at a competitive price.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Exclusive SPX options license VIX franchise Liquidity network effect Proprietary market data Regulated clearing & exchange licenses Net cash balance sheet ($1.22B)

The moat is a contract and a crowd. The contract: Cboe's license from S&P Dow Jones Indices, extended by 25 years on September 29, 2026, gives it exclusive rights to list options on the S&P 500 index through 2051. The crowd: liquidity attracts liquidity. A market maker quoting SPX needs to be where every other hedger already is, and nobody else can list the contract anyway. That is why a 30% share of multi-listed options is a fight while SPX options are a franchise. The vulnerability is that exclusivity covers a contract, not a need. A trader who wants cheap leveraged exposure to the S&P 500 for the afternoon can, in principle, get it from a different wrapper on a different venue — and that is exactly the fear the market priced in June.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
New wrappers for the same exposure. On May 29, 2026 the CFTC cleared the way for perpetual crypto futures, the first available to US investors on domestic regulated exchanges (Reuters), and Cboe fell 9.8% on June 1 and 8.4% on June 2, 17.4% in two sessions (close to close). Raymond James analyst Patrick O'Shaughnessy on the new contracts: "The contracts are not designed for hedging, but rather retail-oriented speculation." CME has sued the CFTC over the approval (filed June 18, 2026). Cboe's answer is that the products do different jobs: on the Q3 2026 call Donohue said "[l]inear products like single-stock futures or perpetual futures may provide leverage," while "the vast majority of 0DTE trading happens in capped risk structures."
02
Retail and 0DTE concentration. 0DTE was a record 65% of SPX volume in Q3 2026, and Cboe estimates retail traders at 58% of SPX 0DTE volume, up from 53% a year earlier (Donohue, Q3 call). Retail flow is the most cyclical and the most mobile participant in any market. If it cools — or migrates — the fastest-growing revenue line reverses first.
03
Volatility dependence. Calm markets mean less hedging, and Cboe is paid per contract traded. Record 2026 volumes were earned in volatile markets; how long that lasts is not in Cboe's control.
04
Execution on the reset. Exiting listings, CEDX and Japanese equities, selling Australia and Canada, and cutting about 20% of the global workforce under a plan announced May 1, 2026 (Reuters), while simultaneously launching event contracts, KPI contracts, clearing and longer trading hours. Shrinking and building at the same time is where plans slip.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the comparisons. First, divestitures: Australia left the group on July 31, 2026 and Canada is pending, so reported growth includes a portfolio effect; management's organic guidance excludes Australia. Second, one-off items: Q3 2026 GAAP EPS ($4.08) is above adjusted EPS ($3.63) because it includes a $71.5M non-taxable gain on the Australia sale, partly offset by $16.7M of realignment costs and a $14.0M reserve on notes receivable tied to funding the Consolidated Audit Trail (the regulators' market-wide trade database); Q3 2025 had about $51M of investment gains. Third, the FY2022 base: diluted EPS of $2.19 was less than half FY2021's $4.92, so any multi-year EPS growth rate starting from 2022 flatters the trend. Buybacks picked up: $56.1M in Q3 2026 (about 211K shares at an average $266.17), and after the board added $550M on October 22 about $1.0B of authorisation was available as of October 28.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 3, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

Over the five years from the November 2021 month-end close of $128.94 to the November 3, 2026 settled close of $344.28, CBOE rose 167.0% on price — the price is 2.7 times its starting level — with no stock splits in the period. Dividends add to that: FY2022–FY2025 alone paid $9.12 per share. The shape is two regimes. From late 2021 to mid-2023 the stock went nowhere, bottoming at $112.31 in May 2022. Then came the 0DTE era: from June 2023 to May 2026 the monthly RSI closed above 70 in 23 of 36 months, peaking at 91.4 in November 2023, as same-day SPX options turned Cboe into a growth stock.

2026 broke the pattern. The stock closed May at $333.56, lost 27.2% in June, gained 27.8% in July, slipped 3.2% in August and 8.3% in September, then rose 21.9% in October — the month of the record Q3 — to a new best month-end for the window, $335.53. Monthly RSI is now 64.8, below the 70 "overbought" line; even in June it fell only to 41.0, short of oversold, which tells you how far above trend the stock had run first. The price is back above its 10-month moving average (about $302.26), which it had dropped below at the end of September.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 24.5x trailing and about 22x expected 2027 earnings for a business whose exclusive license on the world's most important index option now runs to 2051, with net revenue growing 18%, a 74.2% adjusted EBITDA margin, $1.22B of net cash, a 7.0% free-cash-flow yield and a dividend raised 19% this year. Guidance has been raised three times in 2026. The June selloff priced a threat — perpetual futures — that is crypto-native, under legal challenge by CME and, in management's framing, solves a different problem than a defined-risk option; since then 0DTE's share of SPX volume has hit a record 65%. Meanwhile Cboe is building its own new wrappers (event contracts, and KPI contracts aimed at a November launch) on an index nobody else can list, with about $1.0B of buyback authority behind the stock.

Bear Case

The record numbers are a volume boom, and volume booms end. 0DTE is a record 65% of SPX volume and retail traders an estimated 58% of SPX 0DTE volume — the most mobile flow in finance, now with a regulator willing to approve competing leveraged products. Volatility, the other driver, is outside Cboe's control. Consensus already expects adjusted EPS growth to slow from +35% in 2026 to +7.9% in 2027, which makes a 24.5x trailing multiple look full rather than cheap — and the price is within 3% of the average analyst target. Q3's reported EPS was lifted by a one-off $71.5M gain. Management is still exiting businesses and cutting staff while launching new products, and Cboe's share of on-exchange US stock trading keeps slipping (8.9% from 9.8%).

Top 3 Risks

01
Retail options flow migrates to new products or venues. The June 1–2 reaction (−17.4% in two sessions) was the market's estimate of this risk. If perpetual futures or other leveraged wrappers pull even a slice of retail 0DTE activity, the fastest-growing, highest-margin line shrinks first.
02
A calm market. Transaction revenue scales with hedging demand. A long stretch of low volatility would slow volumes regardless of market share. Consensus revenue growth for 2027 is only 4.6% (StockAnalysis).
03
Execution of the strategic reset. Divestitures, business closures and a planned ~20% cut to the global workforce (announced May 1), alongside new clearing, event-contract and extended-hours initiatives that need regulatory approval. Any stumble lands on a stock that has risen 42% since its June close.
Industry Commentary
Perpetual futures and the new-wrapper problem
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A perpetual future is a leveraged contract with no expiry date, born in crypto markets. After the CFTC cleared the way on May 29, 2026 for perpetual crypto futures on US-regulated exchanges, US exchange stocks sold off together over June 1–2: Cboe −17.4%, CME −8.4%, ICE −3.7% (close to close). The fear is not bitcoin; it is precedent — that the same wrapper could eventually carry equity-index exposure to retail traders who today use short-dated SPX options. CME Chairman and CEO Terry Duffy argues perpetuals are swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act, and CME sued the CFTC on June 18. Cboe's own counter is product design: on the Q3 call Donohue said "the vast majority of 0DTE trading happens in capped risk structures," and Hocking said 0DTE "has been tracking around 95% defined risk strategies kind of since inception." Both things can be true; the market will judge by volumes, not by arguments.

Volatility as a revenue input
ONGOING HEADWIND

Exchanges are one of the few businesses that earn more when markets are frightened. Cboe's low 0.41 beta reflects that: it tends to hold up when the index falls. The flip side: calm markets mean less hedging and fewer contracts traded. Record volumes in 2026 were earned in a volatile year. The multiple you pay depends on how much of that you believe is structural (the 0DTE habit, international demand for SPX access) and how much is weather.

Data and clearing — the recurring layer
EMERGING TAILWIND

Across the exchange group, the pattern of the last decade has been the same: trading is the attraction, data and infrastructure are the annuity. Cboe's Data Vantage line — $179.1M in Q3 2026, up 13% reported and about 16% organically, with full-year organic guidance raised to "mid teens" — is the part of the story that does not care about the VIX. The bull framing: each new product (event contracts, KPI contracts, longer trading hours) generates data and connectivity revenue on top of transaction fees, and Cboe is pursuing securities clearing registration to keep more of the value chain. The bear framing: Data Vantage is about a quarter of net revenue; the stock will still trade on 0DTE volumes. Four consecutive quarters of Data Vantage growth at or above the "mid teens" guide, alongside stable options market share, would be the evidence that the franchise is broadening rather than cresting.
