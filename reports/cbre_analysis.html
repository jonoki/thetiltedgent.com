01 Company Overview

CBRE Group is the largest commercial real estate services and investment firm in the world: it leases and sells buildings, runs and maintains them for corporate occupiers and landlords, manages construction and fit-out projects, and invests third-party capital in property. Fiscal 2025 gross revenue was $40.55B, up 13.4% year over year, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through Q3 2026 is about $44.9B (Q3 revenue up 12.7%, nine months up 15.5%). It is the largest company in its industry by revenue by a wide margin — $44.9B trailing against $27.4B for Jones Lang LaSalle, its nearest listed peer — and ranks #293 in the S&P 500.

Read the top line carefully, because management does not. CBRE guides on Core EPS and on net revenue — revenue less pass-through costs, the subcontracted vendor work that clients reimburse and that, in CBRE’s description, generally carries no margin — and those two measures, not gross revenue and not GAAP EPS, are the honest scoreboard. In FY2025, net revenue across the four segments was roughly $23.9B against $40.6B of gross revenue: about $16.7B, or 41% of the reported top line, is pass-through: subcontracted vendor work CBRE arranges for clients and bills back. Gross revenue growth therefore flatters the business, and the 3.07% net margin that falls out of it understates it. The central debate in this report: is CBRE a low-margin outsourcing contractor trading at 26× trailing GAAP earnings, or a high-return advisory franchise with a contractual services annuity bolted on, trading at roughly 15.7× its own FY2026 Core EPS guidance midpoint of $7.85? The gap between $4.68 of trailing GAAP EPS and $7.85 of guided Core EPS is where the entire argument lives.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Global scale — 155k employees
Multi-year contractual FM books
Transaction data flywheel
Producer network lock-in
Single-vendor enterprise outsourcing
Loan servicing annuity
Balance sheet for M&A roll-up

The core advantage is the only-one-who-can-do-all-of-it argument. A multinational occupier that wants one counterparty to lease its space, manage its buildings, run its capital projects and value its portfolio across dozens of countries has a shortlist of very few firms, and CBRE is the largest of them by revenue. That scale is self-reinforcing: contractual facilities and project work embeds CBRE inside a client's operations for years, which generates the transaction flow that feeds Advisory, which generates the data that wins the next mandate. The revenue is not sticky because clients love CBRE; it is sticky because switching a global facilities platform is an operational trauma. Against that, remember what scale does not buy: 4.7% segment operating margin on BOE gross revenue means very little pricing power inside the outsourced services itself. The moat protects the relationship, not the price.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest-rate and transaction-cycle beta. Advisory Services is 37% of net revenue and the highest-margin operating segment, and its leasing and capital-markets fees track property transaction volumes, which track the cost of debt. The FY2022–FY2023 experience is the proof: revenue growth collapsed to 3.6% in FY2023 and GAAP operating income fell from $1.51B to $1.12B. A renewed rate shock would compress the segment that carries the earnings recovery.
02
The GAAP-to-Core gap is structural, not transitional. FY2025 Core EPS of $6.38 versus GAAP EPS of $3.85 is a 66% premium, and the guided FY2026 Core EPS of $7.80–$7.90 sits against trailing GAAP EPS of $4.68. Much of the difference is amortisation of intangibles from a continuous acquisition programme — a real economic cost of the growth strategy, repeated every year. Investors paying 15.7× Core are implicitly agreeing that those charges never matter.
03
Low returns on the capital actually employed. ROIC of 4.87% against ROE of 16.2% says the equity return is being levered, not earned on the asset base. With debt-to-equity at 1.13 and enterprise value $9.0B above market cap, the acquisition roll-up has to keep clearing its cost of capital for the model to compound.
04
Client concentration in the growth engine. The fastest-growing demand — data centres, hyperscaler campuses, life sciences — is concentrated in a handful of very large corporate buyers, of which the April 2026 Meta "LevelUp" programme is the most visible. Multi-year enterprise programmes are excellent revenue until a single client re-plans its capital budget.

RSI measures how much of recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 conventionally mean a stock has run hard and may be due a pause; below 30 means it has been sold off hard. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Five years, two completely different stocks. From the October 2021 close of $104.08 to the October 26, 2026 close of $122.88, CBRE returned +18.1% with no dividends — an unremarkable headline that hides a −38% collapse, a +152% recovery and a −28% giveback. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Strip the pass-throughs and this is a $23.9B net-revenue business growing segment operating profit in every segment in Q3 2026 (+9.5% to +127%), with Core EPS guided to $7.80–$7.90 for FY2026 — 23% growth at the midpoint and a third consecutive year of double-digit Core growth. At $122.88 that is ~15.7× guided Core earnings for the undisputed scale leader in its industry, with 37% of net revenue in a high-margin Advisory franchise that is still early in a transaction-volume recovery, a contractual facilities book compounding on hyperscaler and life-sciences demand, no dividend obligation and ~$1.2B of buybacks already executed this year. The street's lowest published target, $141, is above the current price.

Bear Case

The number that pays shareholders is GAAP, and GAAP EPS is $4.68 — meaning the stock trades at 26.3× actual earnings while ROIC of 4.87% sits at or below any plausible cost of capital. The 66% gap between Core and GAAP is mostly amortisation from an acquisition programme that runs every single year, funded with debt at a 1.13 debt-to-equity ratio and a $750M note issue in April 2026. Advisory's recovery is a rate bet, not a franchise improvement, and the FY2023 experience shows how quickly that fee pool disappears. The market has already voted: the stock is down 28% from January's month-end close despite two guidance raises.

Top 3 Risks

01
Rate-driven relapse in Advisory. Thirty-seven percent of net revenue and the best margin in the company depend on transaction volumes that froze once already this cycle. A renewed move higher in long rates would take the Core EPS growth story with it.
02

Industry Commentary

The pass-through illusion CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Roughly $16.7B of CBRE's $40.6B FY2025 revenue is pass-through — subcontracted vendor work that clients reimburse, generally at no margin. Every ratio computed on gross revenue is therefore wrong in the same direction: an 18.5% gross margin, a 3.07% net margin and a 0.79 price-to-sales ratio (our calculation: $35.4B ÷ $44.9B) all describe a company that does not exist. On net revenue the FY2025 net margin is roughly 4.9% and Building Operations' segment operating margin is 10.2% rather than 4.7%. Any screen that ranks CBRE on gross-revenue margins will mis-price it, in either direction, permanently.

Data centres and the capex supercycle EMERGING TAILWIND

CBRE's Q3 release says infrastructure services revenue rose 50% to $4.5B on a trailing-twelve-month basis, and Building Operations & Experience grew Q3 2026 revenue 17.4% with critical infrastructure services up 80% in local currency. The April 2026 Meta "LevelUp" programme is a visible instance, and the Onnec deal (announced Oct 20) adds data-centre infrastructure services in Europe and Asia Pacific. This is CBRE's most credible claim to structural rather than cyclical growth: it is spending driven by compute demand, not by the cost of mortgage debt, and it sits in CBRE's contractual, multi-year work.

Bull vs bear, framed ONGOING HEADWIND

The whole position reduces to which earnings number you believe. Bulls buy 23% Core EPS growth at 15.7× guided Core, with segment operating profit up in all four segments in Q3 2026 (+9.5% to +127.4%) and a 40.6% average analyst target upside. Bears pay 26.3× GAAP for a business earning 4.87% on invested capital, funded with rising leverage, whose best margins depend on an interest-rate cycle it does not control. The market's own answer, so far in 2026: two guidance raises, and a share price 28% below January's month-end close.
