01 Company Overview

CBRE Group is the largest commercial real estate services and investment firm in the world: it leases and sells buildings, runs and maintains them for corporate occupiers and landlords, manages construction and fit-out projects, and invests third-party capital in property. Fiscal 2025 gross revenue was $40.55B, up 13.4% year over year, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through Q2 2026 stands at $43.71B, up 14.9%. It is the largest company in its industry by a wide margin — roughly 2.7× the market capitalisation of Jones Lang LaSalle, its nearest listed peer — and the world's 634th most valuable listed company.

Read the top line carefully, because management does not. CBRE guides on Core EPS and on net revenue — revenue less the pass-through costs it bills clients at or near zero margin inside its facilities-management contracts — and those two measures, not gross revenue and not GAAP EPS, are the honest scoreboard. In FY2025, net revenue across the four segments was roughly $23.9B against $40.6B of gross revenue: about $16.7B, or 41% of the reported top line, is pass-through — payroll and supplies CBRE fronts for clients and re-bills. Gross revenue growth therefore flatters the business, and the 2.98% net margin that falls out of it understates it. The central debate in this report: is CBRE a low-margin outsourcing contractor trading at 31× trailing GAAP earnings, or a high-return advisory franchise with a contractual services annuity bolted on, trading at roughly 17.6× its own FY2026 Core EPS guidance midpoint of $7.85? The gap between $4.38 of trailing GAAP EPS and $7.85 of guided Core EPS is where the entire argument lives.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Global scale — 155k employees
Multi-year contractual FM books
Transaction data flywheel
Producer network lock-in
Single-vendor enterprise outsourcing
Loan servicing annuity
Balance sheet for M&A roll-up

The core advantage is the only-one-who-can-do-all-of-it argument. A multinational occupier that wants one counterparty to lease its space, manage its buildings, run its capital projects and value its portfolio across dozens of countries has a shortlist of roughly three firms, and CBRE is larger than the other two combined on revenue. That scale is self-reinforcing: contractual facilities and project work embeds CBRE inside a client's operations for years, which generates the transaction flow that feeds Advisory, which generates the data that wins the next mandate. The revenue is not sticky because clients love CBRE; it is sticky because switching a global facilities platform is an operational trauma. Against that, remember what scale does not buy: 4.7% segment operating margin on BOE gross revenue means very little pricing power inside the outsourced services itself. The moat protects the relationship, not the price.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest-rate and transaction-cycle beta. Advisory Services is 37% of net revenue and the highest-margin operating segment, and its leasing and capital-markets fees track property transaction volumes, which track the cost of debt. The FY2022–FY2023 experience is the proof: revenue growth collapsed to 3.6% in FY2023 and GAAP operating income fell from $1.42B to $1.09B. A renewed rate shock would compress the segment that carries the earnings recovery.
02
The GAAP-to-Core gap is structural, not transitional. FY2025 Core EPS of $6.38 versus GAAP EPS of $3.85 is a 66% premium, and the guided FY2026 Core EPS of $7.80–$7.90 sits against trailing GAAP EPS of $4.38. Much of the difference is amortisation of intangibles from a continuous acquisition programme — a real economic cost of the growth strategy, repeated every year. Investors paying 17.6× Core are implicitly agreeing that those charges never matter.
03
Low returns on the capital actually employed. ROIC of 5.17% against ROE of 15.8% says the equity return is being levered, not earned on the asset base. With debt-to-equity at 1.18 and enterprise value $9.3B above market cap, the acquisition roll-up has to keep clearing its cost of capital for the model to compound.
04
Client concentration in the growth engine. The fastest-growing demand — data centres, hyperscaler campuses, life sciences — is concentrated in a handful of very large corporate buyers, of which the April 2026 Meta "LevelUp" programme is the most visible. Multi-year enterprise programmes are excellent revenue until a single client re-plans its capital budget.

Notable One-Off Items

Q4 2025 GAAP operating income of $14M against $505M in Q3 2025 and $431M in Q2 2025 is the single most distorting figure in the recent record — a quarter in which net income was nonetheless $416M, the highest of the five. That divergence is the signature of below-the-line and non-operating items, and it is exactly why CBRE's Core measures exist. Separately: the roughly $16.7B of FY2025 pass-through revenue is not a one-off but a permanent distortion of every margin ratio computed on gross revenue; Real Estate Investments earnings are lumpy by construction (Q2 2026 segment operating profit +68% on revenue −10.2%); and the $750M senior notes priced in April 2026 adds interest expense that will show up in GAAP but is excluded from nothing in Core. RSI measures how much of recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 conventionally mean a stock has run hard and may be due a pause; below 30 means it has been sold off hard. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Five years, two completely different stocks. From the October 2021 close of $104.08 to the September 10, 2026 close of $137.99, CBRE returned +32.6% with no dividends — an unremarkable headline that hides a −35% collapse, a +146% recovery and a −19% giveback. The 2022 leg was pure rate repricing: from $108.51 at the end of December 2021 to $67.51 in September 2022, a 37.8% drawdown, as the cost of debt destroyed transaction volumes and with them Advisory's fee pool. The stock then spent all of 2023 dead flat in a $69–$85 band while revenue growth slowed to 3.6%. The re-rating began in July 2024 — a 26% single-month move from $89.11 to $112.71 — and ran almost without interruption to $170.33 in January 2026, +152% off the low, as the Core EPS recovery became visible and the resegmentation reframed the business around contractual revenue. Since that peak the stock has given back 19%, including a −15% February 2026 month and a −12% May 2026 month, even as Q1 and Q2 2026 both beat and guidance was raised twice. That divergence — improving fundamentals, falling price — is the setup an investor is being asked to take a view on today.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Strip the pass-throughs and this is a $23.9B net-revenue business compounding segment operating profit at 25%+ in every segment in Q2 2026, with Core EPS guided to $7.80–$7.90 for FY2026 — 23% growth at the midpoint and a third consecutive year of double-digit Core growth. At $137.99 that is ~17.6× guided Core earnings for the undisputed scale leader in its industry, with 37% of net revenue in a high-margin Advisory franchise that is still early in a transaction-volume recovery, a contractual facilities book compounding on hyperscaler and life-sciences demand, no dividend obligation and ~$1.0B of buybacks already executed this year. The street's lowest published target, $144, is above the current price.

Bear Case

The number that pays shareholders is GAAP, and GAAP EPS is $4.38 — meaning the stock trades at 31.5× actual earnings while ROIC of 5.17% sits at or below any plausible cost of capital. The 66% gap between Core and GAAP is mostly amortisation from an acquisition programme that runs every single year, funded with debt at a 1.18 debt-to-equity ratio and a $750M note issue in April 2026. Advisory's recovery is a rate bet, not a franchise improvement, and the FY2023 experience shows how quickly that fee pool disappears. The market has already voted: the stock is down 19% from January despite two guidance raises.

Top 3 Risks

01
Rate-driven relapse in Advisory. Thirty-seven percent of net revenue and the best margin in the company depend on transaction volumes that froze once already this cycle. A renewed move higher in long rates would take the Core EPS growth story with it.
02
The Core-versus-GAAP credibility gap closes the wrong way. If the market decides that recurring intangible amortisation is a real cost — which it is — the relevant multiple is 31.5×, not 17.6×, and there is no earnings growth rate that makes 31.5× cheap for a 5.17% ROIC business.
03
Concentration in the growth engine. The fastest-growing mandates are large hyperscaler and enterprise programmes. Data-centre capital budgets are the most cyclical spending line in corporate America right now; a pause would hit BOE and Project Management simultaneously.

Industry Commentary

The pass-through illusion CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Roughly $16.7B of CBRE's $40.6B FY2025 revenue is pass-through — client payroll and supplies re-billed at essentially zero margin. Every ratio computed on gross revenue is therefore wrong in the same direction: an 18.6% gross margin, a 2.98% net margin and a 0.93 price-to-sales ratio all describe a company that does not exist. On net revenue the FY2025 net margin is roughly 4.8% and Building Operations' segment operating margin is 10.2% rather than 4.7%. Any screen that ranks CBRE on gross-revenue margins will mis-price it, in either direction, permanently.

Data centres and the capex supercycle EMERGING TAILWIND

Project Management grew revenue 19.1% in Q2 2026 and segment operating profit 27.8% — the fastest top-line growth in the company — on infrastructure, energy-transition and data-centre delivery. The April 2026 Meta "LevelUp" programme is the visible instance. This is CBRE's most credible claim to structural rather than cyclical growth: it is spending driven by compute demand, not by the cost of mortgage debt, and it lands in the two segments with contractual multi-year revenue.

Bull vs bear, framed ONGOING HEADWIND

The whole position reduces to which earnings number you believe. Bulls buy 23% Core EPS growth at 17.6× guided Core, with segment operating profit up 25%+ across all four segments and a 32.6% average analyst target upside. Bears pay 31.5× GAAP for a business earning 5.17% on invested capital, funded with rising leverage, whose best margins depend on an interest-rate cycle it does not control. The market's own answer, so far in 2026: two guidance raises, and a 19% lower share price.
