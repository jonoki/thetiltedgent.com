01 Company Overview

Cadence Design Systems sells the software, hardware and pre-built circuit blocks that chip and system companies use to design semiconductors. Its core market is EDA (electronic design automation — the software that lets engineers describe, simulate, place, route and verify a chip before it is manufactured), where Cadence and Synopsys form a two-firm structure that has proved remarkably durable. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $5.297 billion, up 14% year over year, with GAAP diluted EPS of $4.06 and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $7.14 (+20%). On a trailing-twelve-month basis revenue has since reached $6.13 billion (+17.6%), and after its third-quarter report on October 26, 2026 the company is guiding fiscal 2026 to $6.387–$6.417 billion, about 21% growth at the midpoint — partly bought, since the Hexagon Design & Engineering business it acquired in February 2026 was expected to add about $160 million this year. At $93.96 billion of market capitalisation Cadence ranks #127 in the S&P 500 and #50 in the Nasdaq-100.

The most striking fact about the duopoly is that Cadence carries a larger market capitalisation than Synopsys ($93.96B vs $92.35B at the same Oct 28, 2026 close; shares outstanding × close, our calculation) on less than two-thirds the revenue ($6.13B vs $9.42B trailing). The gap has narrowed from the previous edition as Synopsys’ shares rose. The market is paying for margin quality and organic growth rather than scale — a theme that runs through the rest of this report.

This is the digital and custom/analog design and verification stack — Virtuoso and Innovus for layout, Xcelium for simulation, Jasper for formal verification — plus, unusually for a "software" company, physical hardware. The Palladium line is emulation (special-purpose machines that model a chip design gate-for-gate so software can be run against it at near-real speed before any silicon exists) and the Protium line is prototyping (FPGA-based boxes that run a design far faster than emulation but with less debug visibility). Customers buy these to de-risk tapeout (the point at which a finished design is handed to a foundry for manufacture, after which a mistake costs a full mask set and months of schedule). Cadence has now called hardware a record quarter in each of the last several reports: 30+ new hardware customers added across FY2025 and 12 new hardware customers in Q2 2026, with expansion concentrated among hyperscalers and AI companies; the Q3 2026 release called hardware another record quarter, on larger deals with major customers and its hardware cloud offering. Core EDA revenue grew 20% year over year in Q3 2026.

Cadence does not break out hardware revenue as a separate line in the earnings releases reviewed here — it is reported inside Core EDA — so any specific hardware percentage would be fabrication. What the financials do show is the margin signature of a rising hardware mix: gross margin fell from 89.36% in FY2023 to 86.05% in FY2024 and 86.36% in FY2025, and 85.68% on a trailing basis. That 3.0-point compression in two years (3.7 points to the trailing figure) is what selling boxes does to a company whose licence business runs near 90%. It is also why Cadence's quarterly revenue is lumpier than a pure-licence model: hardware is recognised largely up front, is a capital purchase for the buyer, and clusters around customer design cycles rather than arriving in even monthly slices.

This is the deliberate attempt to grow the addressable market past semiconductors: PCB and package design, signal and power integrity, thermal simulation, computational fluid dynamics (numerically solving how air, liquid or heat moves around a physical object), electromagnetics solvers, and the Cadence Molecular Sciences work applying the same physics-simulation and compute stack to drug discovery and materials. The strategic logic is coherent — Cadence already sells simulation at scale to engineers, and the underlying maths and GPU infrastructure transfer — and the company has bought its way in: the Hexagon Design & Engineering acquisition (MSC Nastran, Adams), closed on February 23, 2026 for about €2.7 billion, 70% in cash and 30% in Cadence stock, joined the segment in Q1 2026, when it grew 18% year over year. In Q3 2026 the segment grew 30%, which the release puts down to PCB and advanced packaging and the Millennium platform; the year-ago quarter did not include Hexagon D&E.

Is it material yet? Honestly: not really, and it should not be underwritten as if it were. SD&A was 16% of FY2025 revenue, 17% in Q2 2026 and 16% in Q3 2026 — roughly $0.85 billion in FY2025 and about $1 billion a year at the Q3 2026 rate — and its FY2025 organic growth of 13% was the same as Core EDA's, i.e. it was not outgrowing the base business before acquisitions. The 18% Q1 2026 and 30% Q3 2026 figures are acquisition-assisted: Cadence expected Hexagon D&E to add about $160 million of 2026 revenue. Cadence does not size Cadence Molecular Sciences separately in any release reviewed here, which is itself informative: a business large enough to move the model usually gets its own number. Treat SD&A as a credible bet on a larger market, not as a segment currently carrying the valuation.

Why the duopoly persists. The barrier is not that Cadence's algorithms are secret — it is that a chip design flow is a multi-year, multi-hundred-engineer commitment. A design team builds scripts, methodology, regression suites and staff expertise around one vendor's toolchain, and those assets do not port. On top of that, foundries certify specific tool versions against specific process nodes: a design rule deck and a set of reference flows are qualified per node, so using an uncertified tool means the foundry will not stand behind manufacturability. And the consequence of getting it wrong is asymmetric — a tapeout failure costs a mask set and roughly a two-quarter schedule slip, against a tool-licence saving measured in single-digit millions. Rational engineering managers therefore do not switch mid-design, and often do not switch between designs either. The financial fingerprint of that is Cadence's $8.2 billion record backlog at September 30, 2026 (contracted revenue not yet recognised) and $4.2 billion of cRPO (remaining performance obligations expected to convert to revenue within twelve months) — equal to about 69% of trailing-twelve-month revenue already under contract for the next twelve months. Recurring revenue grew 18% year over year in Q3 2026. That is the real forward indicator for this business, far more than any single quarter's revenue.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. China and export control — a demonstrated, not theoretical, risk. Cadence resolved proceedings with the U.S. Department of Justice and the Bureau of Industry and Security with a one-time charge relating to $140.6 million of settlements, recognised in Q2 2025. Separately, temporary export restrictions imposed on May 23, 2025 cut China from 12% of revenue in Q2 2024 to 9% in Q2 2025. China has since recovered to 13% of FY2025, 13% in Q1 2026, 15% in Q2 2026 and 16% in Q3 2026 (18% in Q3 2025). The exposure is real, the policy is bidirectional, and the company has already paid a nine-figure penalty once.

2. Hardware makes the model lumpier than the multiple implies. Emulation and prototyping systems are capital purchases recognised largely up front. That is why gross margin has fallen 3.0 points from FY2023 to FY2025 and why quarterly revenue can swing on the timing of a few large box orders. A stock at 68x trailing earnings is priced like a business whose subscription revenue grows smoothly year after year; a meaningful part of the revenue is not like that.

3. Customer concentration in AI. Management explicitly attributes recent hardware and IP strength to hyperscalers and AI companies. That is a narrow, capex-cyclical buyer set. IP growing 24–40% year over year is excellent while the build-out runs; the same concentration works in reverse if accelerator programmes are deferred.

4. The adjacency thesis is unproven at the P&L level. System Design & Analysis is 16–17% of revenue and grew in line with Core EDA organically in FY2025; its 30% Q3 2026 growth includes the acquired Hexagon D&E business. If the market is paying a premium for a molecular-sciences and physical-simulation option, that option has not yet shown up as differential growth.

Notable One-Off Items

Four distortions matter when reading these numbers. First, the $140.6 million DOJ/BIS settlement charge taken in Q2 2025 crushed that quarter's GAAP operating margin to 19.0% from 27.7% a year earlier and GAAP EPS to $0.59 from $0.84 — it is the main reason FY2025 GAAP operating margin (28.2%) came in below FY2024 (29.1%) despite 14% revenue growth and a rising non-GAAP margin (44.6% vs 42.5%). Second, the Hexagon Design & Engineering acquisition (closed February 23, 2026) flatters System Design & Analysis growth from Q1 2026 onward; the 18% (Q1) and 30% (Q3) year-over-year figures are not organic. Third, Q3 2026 GAAP results carried restructuring charges (2.6 points of operating margin, $0.15 a share) and acquisition and integration costs (1.5 points, $0.09 a share), which is why GAAP operating margin fell to 26.2% from 31.8% and GAAP EPS was flat at $1.05 while non-GAAP EPS rose 9% to $2.11. Fourth, on the comparison side, Synopsys' +46.3% trailing revenue growth and its depressed 11.03% operating margin, 3.66% ROE and 2.23% ROIC are acquisition artefacts, not evidence that Cadence has out-executed it by 7x on returns; readers should compare organic growth and non-GAAP margins for a fair view. The gap is real but smaller than the raw ratios suggest.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Half of a genuine duopoly with contractual visibility most software companies would envy: $8.2B backlog and $4.2B cRPO — about 69% of trailing revenue already under contract for the next twelve months. Growth has stepped up: FY2025 +14%, Q1 2026 +18.7%, Q2 2026 +24%, Q3 2026 +22%, FY2026 guided to ~21% (part of it acquired), with IP up 24–40% and hardware setting records — and the EPS guide, cut in April, now stands at $8.37–$8.43, above February’s $8.05–$8.15. Unit economics are superb — 85.68% gross margin, 29.94% operating margin, 22.51% net margin, 17.09% ROIC — and vastly cleaner than Synopsys' post-acquisition 11.03% / 11.43% / 2.23%. And after a flat year (+0.34%) the multiple has already compressed while earnings compounded.

Bear Case

68.08x trailing and 40.8x the FY2026 guided non-GAAP EPS, with a PEG of 2.09 against Synopsys' 1.09. That price embeds smooth subscription compounding, but a growing share of revenue is emulation and prototyping hardware — capital purchases, recognised up front, lumpy by quarter, and visibly diluting gross margin from 89.36% (FY2023) to 85.68% today. The AI hyperscaler concentration driving the acceleration is capex-cyclical. China is back to 16% of revenue in a regime that already cost the company a $140.6M settlement and a revenue air pocket in 2025. And the SD&A adjacency that is supposed to widen the market Cadence can sell into grew in line with Core EDA organically in FY2025, and its 30% Q3 2026 growth is acquisition-assisted. GAAP EPS was flat year over year in Q3 after restructuring charges.

What the multiple implies. At $342.46 against the raised FY2026 non-GAAP guidance midpoint of $8.40, the stock trades at 40.8x forward earnings. Assume the price-to-earnings multiple falls over five years to a still-premium 25x. To merely hold the share price flat, EPS would have to compound at ~10.3% a year through 2031 ((40.8 ÷ 25)1/5 − 1). To deliver a 10% annualised return, EPS would have to compound at ~21.3% a year for five consecutive years ((40.8 ÷ 25 × 1.15)1/5 − 1). Cadence's non-GAAP EPS grew 20% in FY2025 and the raised guide implies about 17.6% growth in FY2026 ($8.40 vs $7.14), so the required rate is at or above the top of what the company has recently delivered — sustained for half a decade. That is the arithmetic behind the 2.09 PEG, and it is the number a buyer at this price is underwriting.

Top 3 Risks
01
Valuation risk. A PEG of 2.09 and 40.8x forward earnings leave little room for a growth stumble; the required ~21.3% five-year EPS growth rate for a 10% return exceeds the ~17.6% implied by current guidance. The last twelve months (+0.34%) show what multiple compression alone can do even while revenue grows 18–24% a quarter.
02
China and export controls. China is 16% of Q3 2026 revenue, up from 13% in FY2025. China fell to 9% of revenue in Q2 2025, the quarter of the May 23, 2025 restrictions, from 12% a year earlier, and the company has already paid $140.6M to settle DOJ and BIS proceedings. Policy is the single fastest-acting variable in the model.
03
Hardware lumpiness and AI-capex concentration. Palladium and Protium are large capital purchases from a narrow, cyclical buyer set — hyperscalers and AI companies by management's own attribution. Order timing can swing a quarter, and a pause in accelerator programmes would hit hardware, IP and Core EDA simultaneously rather than sequentially.
  Industry Commentary
The duopoly is structural — and the two halves have diverged sharply
ONGOING HEADWIND
Cadence and Synopsys are not simply rivals of similar shape. On the Oct 28, 2026 close, Cadence is worth $93.96B on $6.13B of trailing revenue; Synopsys is worth $92.35B on $9.42B (shares × close, our calculation). Cadence generates $389k of revenue per employee (15,753 staff at September 30, 2026) against Synopsys' $336k (28,000, per stockanalysis). Cadence's growth is mostly organic (+17.6% trailing, ~21% guided, including Hexagon D&E); Synopsys' +46.3% is acquisition-built, and it shows in the returns — ROIC 17.09% vs 2.23%, operating margin 29.94% vs 11.03%. The trade-off is direction of travel: Synopsys has bought a far larger simulation and analysis franchise outright, while Cadence is assembling one in pieces (Hexagon D&E) and still derives 69–70% of revenue from Core EDA. Buying Cadence is a bet on organic compounding at a premium multiple; buying Synopsys is a bet on integration.
Export control is now a permanent line item, not an event
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The sequence is instructive. China was 12% of Q2 2024 revenue. Temporary restrictions imposed May 23, 2025 pushed it to 9% in Q2 2025. Cadence simultaneously took a one-time charge relating to $140.6 million of DOJ and BIS settlements. By FY2025 China was back to 13%, held 13% in Q1 2026, reached 15% in Q2 2026 and 16% in Q3 2026 (18% in Q3 2025). So exposure has not been structurally reduced — it has recovered and grown, in an environment that has already cut China’s share by three points year over year in a single quarter and imposed nine-figure penalties. EDA is precisely the chokepoint technology export policy targets, because denying design tools denies future chips rather than current ones.
Bull vs bear, framed on the numbers that actually decide it
EMERGING TAILWIND
The bull needs three things to stay true: backlog keeps setting records (it has run $7.8B → $8.0B → $8.1B → $8.2B, while cRPO went $3.8B → $4.0B → $4.2B and then held at $4.2B in Q3); IP keeps compounding at 25–40% (it slowed to 24% in Q3); and the AI design cycle keeps pulling emulation hardware. If all three hold, ~21% revenue growth is defensible and 40.8x forward is expensive rather than absurd. The bear needs only one to break. Note what happened across the last two prints: Q2 2026 delivered +24% revenue and raised both the revenue and the EPS guide, yet the stock fell from $375.32 at the end of June to $330.19 at the end of September. Q3 raised both again, taking the EPS guide above February’s for the first time, and the stock rose 6.5% the next session. Similar prints have met opposite reactions; the flat cRPO is the number to watch next.
