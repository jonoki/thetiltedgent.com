01 Company Overview

CDW is the company that stands between the people who make technology and the people who have to buy it. It sells hardware, software and integrated IT solutions — cloud, security, hybrid infrastructure, advisory and managed services — to businesses, government agencies, schools and hospitals in the United States, the United Kingdom and Canada. It does not make the laptops, servers or licences it sells; it sources, configures, bundles, finances, installs and supports them. FY2025 net sales were $22.42B, up 6.79%, on net income of $1.07B and diluted EPS of $8.08. At $157.00 the market values the whole business at $19.60B (124.81M shares on the latest 10-Q cover × the close) — less than one year of sales.

That last fact is the whole shape of the company. CDW is a thin-margin, high-velocity business: a 21.0% trailing gross margin and a 4.5% net margin, applied to $24.5B of trailing sales. Small moves in margin are large moves in profit. For most of 2026 those moves went the wrong way: sales grew 9.2% in Q1 and 10.0% in Q2, yet the stock fell 20% on May 6 after the Q1 print and closed down 9.0% on August 5 after Q2, both times on a quarter in which revenue beat estimates and gross margin slipped. Q3 changed the reaction more than the pattern. Sales jumped 17.7% and gross margin fell further, to 20.5% from 21.9% a year earlier, but gross profit still grew 10.3%, adjusted EPS grew 20.4% and management raised its 2026 outlook; the stock rose 12.67% on November 4. The question underneath the stock is simple to state: in an AI-driven hardware upcycle, does the reseller in the middle keep enough of each sale as the mix shifts to lower-margin infrastructure? The stock set its 52-week low of $97.12 on May 12, 2026, and at $157.00 it is 61.7% above that low.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Procurement scale ($24.5B TTM sales) Vendor-neutral breadth Public-sector contract vehicles Account relationships Services & data capability (Lovelytics) 13 years of dividend increases

A reseller's moat is not the product — anyone can buy the same server from the same manufacturer. It is the relationship and the plumbing: the account team that knows a customer's environment, the contract vehicles that let a school district or agency buy without running a new tender, the logistics and configuration capacity to ship thousands of devices ready to use, and the breadth to put many vendors' products into one solution. That is why CDW's sales grew 17.7% in Q3 while gross margin fell 1.4 percentage points. The moat protects volume. It does much less for margin, because the manufacturers upstream and the large buyers downstream both have pricing power, and CDW sits between them.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Mix is moving toward lower-margin dollars. Year over year, gross margin fell 0.6 percentage points in Q1 2026, 0.7 in Q2 and 1.4 in Q3 (20.5% from 21.9%), though Q3 was 0.4 points above Q2. Hardware grew 21% in Q3, with data storage and servers more than doubling, and client devices grew 19% on higher prices and fewer units. CFO Albert Miralles said the drop was not like-for-like pressure but a result of selling more of particular products, while netted-down revenue reached 38.2% of gross profit, up 2.2 points. When the growth is in hardware, the gross-profit dollar grows slower than the sales dollar.
02
Cash conversion is improving but short of the goal. TTM free cash flow of $933.7M compares with $1,088M in FY2025 and $1,451M in FY2023. Adjusted free cash flow for the first nine months was 73% of adjusted net income, below the 80–90% long-term goal, though management said it has line of sight to its cash-flow objectives. A distributor in a growth phase funds inventory and receivables before it collects; if the cycle turns, that cash comes back — but only if the growth stops.
03
Debt is high and getting more expensive. $7.12B of total debt against $1.54B of cash at September 30, 2026 — net debt of $5.59B, about 28.5% of the equity market value, and 2.5x earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation on management's measure, inside its 2–3x target. In September CDW sold $1.5B of notes at 5.700% to 6.350%, partly to repay $1.0B of 2.670% notes due in December, and it raised its 2026 net interest expense outlook to $255M–$260M. Interest is covered 7.5x (StockAnalysis), so this is not a solvency issue, but it limits how much the balance sheet can absorb another margin step down.
04
An index exit removed a buyer. CDW was removed from the NASDAQ-100 in the annual reconstitution effective December 22, 2025. It remains an S&P 500 member, but one set of index funds that had to hold the stock has gone.
05
A CFO change is coming. Albert Miralles, CFO for five years, plans to retire in 2027 once a successor is in place (CDW release, August 5, 2026). CDW's 8-Ks through the banner date name no successor.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $189.36 to the November 6, 2026 settled close of $157.00, CDW lost 17.1% of its price. Dividends over the period add back $11.36 a share (19 quarterly payments, Yahoo Finance), which, with no reinvestment, takes the return to about −11.1% — still a losing five years. But the endpoints hide a round trip: the stock climbed to a $255.78 month-end in March 2024, 35% above the starting point, and then fell 52.7% to a $121.02 month-end in March 2026 — without a single year of falling EPS large enough to explain it. FY2024 diluted EPS was $7.97; FY2025 was $8.08. Earnings held; what investors would pay for each dollar of them fell.

The 6-period monthly RSI tells the story cleanly. It hit 83.4 at the March 2024 peak — the most overbought reading in the series — and then spent most of the next two years below 50 (19 of 24 months), bottoming at 14.4 in March 2026, the most oversold reading of the five years. It has since recovered to 59.6, in neutral territory. CDW trades above its 10-month moving average of $138.50: the long downtrend from 2024 has, at least for now, been interrupted.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Demand is not the problem, and Q3 showed profit can keep up. Net sales grew 17.7%, all four segments grew, gross profit grew 10.3% and adjusted EPS grew 20.4%. Management raised its 2026 outlook for gross profit and adjusted EPS, and CEO Christine Leahy called any buying ahead of need "immaterial, frankly". You pay 18.3x trailing GAAP earnings and 13.9x the 2026 consensus for adjusted earnings for a business earning a 15.3% return on invested capital against a 7.37% cost of capital, with 13 straight years of dividend increases and a shrinking share count. Lovelytics adds the data consulting that sits in front of AI projects, and the CFO says it lifts gross margin. Even after the rebound the stock is 38.6% below its March 2024 month-end, when sales were lower than today's.

Bear Case

The bear case does not dispute the growth; it disputes what CDW keeps of it. Gross margin fell 1.4 percentage points in Q3 to 20.5%, GAAP operating income grew 10.4% on 17.7% more sales, and management expects full-year gross margin below 2025. The growth is concentrated in hardware, and the CEO herself said a single piece of hardware or a GPU "can be dilutive to our underlying business margins of 21%." Year-to-date adjusted free cash flow was 73% of adjusted net income, below the 80–90% goal, yet buybacks and dividends took about 114% of it against a 50–75% target. TTM free cash flow of $933.7M is 36% below FY2023's $1,451M. Net debt is $5.59B, consensus revenue growth slows to 5.63% in 2027, and 7.23% of the float was sold short at the October 15 settlement.

Top 3 Risks

01
Gross-margin compression. Q3 2026 gross margin 20.5% vs 21.9%; nine months 20.5% vs 21.4%; management expects the full year below 2025. Revenue beat estimates while gross margin fell in both May (−20.3%) and August (−9.0%). November's +12.67% was a different reaction to the same margin trend, and the stock gave back 62% of that gain in the next two sessions.
02
Cash conversion and debt together. Adjusted free cash flow at 73% of adjusted net income for the year to date against an 80–90% goal, $5.59B of net debt, and shareholder payouts running at about 114% of that cash flow against a 50–75% target. Any of the three is manageable alone; together they narrow the room for buybacks, which have been doing much of the work in EPS growth.
03
Cycle timing. Consensus revenue growth falls from 12.74% in 2026 to 5.63% in 2027, and Q4 gross profit is guided to a seasonal low-to-mid-single-digit decline from Q3. Management says its orders and backlog show no flush of early buying; if the infrastructure spending of 2026 did pull demand forward, 2027 inherits a tougher comparison with the same margin problem.
Industry Commentary
The middleman's squeeze in an AI hardware cycle
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

An IT reseller earns a spread on other companies' products. When demand is broad — laptops, licences, networking, services — the spread is healthy. When demand concentrates in large infrastructure purchases, the spread narrows as a percentage even as the dollar volume grows. CDW's 2026 numbers fit that pattern: sales grew 17.7% in Q3, hardware grew 21%, data storage and servers more than doubled, and gross margin fell 1.4 percentage points. CEO Christine Leahy granted that infrastructure hardware "can be dilutive to our underlying business margins of 21%", while arguing it should be judged inside the wider solution, and CFO Albert Miralles said the drop came from selling more of certain products rather than from like-for-like pressure. The unresolved question is whether this is a phase that reverses as services and software catch up with hardware, or a new, lower level of margin for resellers as a whole. Professional and managed services returned to just 2% growth in Q3.

Buying the services layer
STRATEGIC SHIFT

Resellers' answer to a thinning product spread is to sell more of what it does itself. Futurum's analysis of the Lovelytics deal places it in a pattern: Insight Enterprises acquired specialist talent through Inspire11, and SHI invested in NStarX and set up AI and cyber labs. The logic, in Christine Leahy's words: "There is no AI strategy without a data strategy." On the Q3 call Albert Miralles said of Lovelytics, "Gross margins, for sure, accretive," and Leahy said CDW will operate it "almost separately for a period of time" and use CDW as a sales channel. Services carry higher margins than resold hardware, but they are also harder to scale, and a $525M base price buys a business of more than 600 people, in a company now at 14,600 people and $24.5B of sales. It is a direction, not yet a mix change.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

At the November 6 close CDW trades at 12.35x EV/EBITDA, 1.06x EV/sales and 0.80x price/sales (our calculation: EV, or enterprise value, is the $19.60B market cap plus StockAnalysis's $7.87B of debt and leases less $1.54B of cash; EBITDA is earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, $2.10B per StockAnalysis; sales are $24.52B), with a 4.8% free-cash-flow yield and a 1.73% dividend. The bull framing: the stock was priced near $256 in March 2024 on lower sales than today; margins have slipped by under one percentage point on a trailing basis, and the price has fallen 38.6%. Double-digit sales growth with margins that merely stop falling is not in the price. The bear framing: the March 2024 price was the anomaly — a reseller with a 4.5% net margin and 3.65x debt/EBITDA (StockAnalysis, leases included) should be valued like a distributor, not a technology company, and today's price simply reflects that. The deciding data are reported every quarter: gross margin and free cash flow. Q3 gross margin rose from Q2 but fell from a year earlier; Q3 cash flow was strong, and the year to date is still short of the goal.
