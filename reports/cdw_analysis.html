01 Company Overview

CDW is the company that stands between the people who make technology and the people who have to buy it. It sells hardware, software and integrated IT solutions — cloud, security, hybrid infrastructure, advisory and managed services — to businesses, government agencies, schools and hospitals in the United States, the United Kingdom and Canada. It does not make the laptops, servers or licences it sells; it sources, configures, bundles, finances, installs and supports them. FY2025 net sales were $22.42B, up 6.79%, on net income of $1.07B and diluted EPS of $8.08. At $146.41 the market values the whole business at $18.30B — less than one year of sales.

That last fact is the whole shape of the company. CDW is a thin-margin, high-velocity business: a 21.4% trailing gross margin and a 4.6% net margin, applied to $23.5B of trailing sales. Small moves in margin are large moves in profit — and 2026 has been a year of small moves in the wrong direction. Sales grew 9.2% in Q1 and 10.0% in Q2, the best growth in years, yet the stock fell 20% on May 6 after the Q1 print and lost as much as 16% pre-market on August 5 after Q2, both times because gross margin slipped while revenue beat. The question underneath the stock is simple to state: in an AI-driven hardware upcycle, does the reseller in the middle keep its cut, or does the cut get squeezed as mix shifts to lower-margin infrastructure and memory prices climb? Between those two prints the stock set its 52-week low of $97.12 on May 12, 2026, and it has since recovered 51% from that low.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Procurement scale ($23.5B TTM sales) Vendor-neutral breadth Public-sector contract vehicles Account relationships Services & data capability (Lovelytics) 12 years of dividend growth

A reseller's moat is not the product — anyone can buy the same server from the same manufacturer. It is the relationship and the plumbing: the account team that knows a customer's environment, the contract vehicles that let a school district or agency buy without running a new tender, the logistics and configuration capacity to ship thousands of devices ready to use, and the breadth to put many vendors' products into one solution. That is why CDW's sales grew double digits in Q2 while it lost share of each dollar. The moat protects volume. It does much less for margin, because the manufacturers upstream and the large buyers downstream both have pricing power, and CDW sits between them.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Mix is moving toward lower-margin dollars. Gross margin fell 60 bps in Q1 2026 (21.0% from 21.6%) and 70 bps in Q2. On the Q1 call CFO Albert Miralles traced most of the drop to "a lower proportion of netted down revenue": hardware grew 10% in Q1, with networking, servers and enterprise storage above 20%, while higher-margin software and services lagged. When the growth is in hardware, the gross-profit dollar grows slower than the sales dollar.
02
Cash conversion has weakened. TTM free cash flow of $860.2M compares with $1,088M in FY2025 and $1,451M in FY2023, and the Q2 call summary put conversion at 42%. A distributor in a growth phase funds inventory and receivables before it collects; if the cycle turns, that cash comes back — but only if the growth stops.
03
Leverage is real. $5.82B of total debt against $361.8M of cash at June 30, 2026 — net debt of $5.46B, about 30% of the equity market value. StockAnalysis puts debt/EBITDA at 3.11x and debt-to-equity at 2.63. Interest is covered 7.48x, so this is not a solvency issue, but it limits how much the balance sheet can absorb another margin step down alongside acquisitions such as Lovelytics.
04
Index exits removed a buyer. CDW was removed from the NASDAQ-100 in the annual reconstitution effective December 22, 2025. It remains an S&P 500 constituent, but one passive holder base has gone.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $182.02 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $146.41, CDW lost 19.6% of its price. Dividends over the period add back roughly $12 a share (our estimate from StockAnalysis's annual dividend-per-share figures), which takes the total return to roughly −13% — still a losing five years. But the endpoints hide a round trip: the stock climbed to a $255.78 month-end in March 2024, 40% above the starting point, and then fell 52.7% to a $121.02 month-end in March 2026 — without a single year of falling EPS large enough to explain it. FY2024 diluted EPS was $7.97; FY2025 was $8.08. The market did not re-price the earnings. It re-priced the multiple.

The 6-period monthly RSI tells the story cleanly. It hit 83.6 at the March 2024 peak — the most overbought reading in the series — and then spent most of the next two years below 50, bottoming at 14.5 in March 2026, the most oversold reading of the five years. It has since recovered to 52.5, squarely neutral. Unlike many 2026 decliners, CDW now trades above its 10-month moving average of about $135.50: the long downtrend from 2024 has, at least for now, been interrupted.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Demand is not the problem. Net sales grew 9.2% in Q1 and 10.0% in Q2 2026, three of four segments grew double digits or close to it, and management credits customers "advanc[ing] investments in infrastructure modernization, cloud, and AI-enabled technologies." You pay 17.6x trailing GAAP earnings and roughly 13x forward adjusted earnings for a business still earning a 15.3% return on invested capital against a 7.67% cost of capital, with 12 straight years of dividend growth and a shrinking share count. Lovelytics adds the data-consulting layer that sits in front of every AI project, and the stock has already absorbed two margin shocks and a NASDAQ-100 exit. If gross margin simply stops falling, the earnings power of 10% sales growth reaches the bottom line, and the stock is priced 42.8% below where it traded in March 2024 on lower sales.

Bear Case

The bear case does not dispute the growth; it disputes what CDW keeps of it. Gross margin fell 70 bps in Q2 to 20.1%, GAAP operating income grew 2.0% on 10.0% more sales, TTM free cash flow of $860.2M is down 41% from FY2023's $1,451M, and management guided second-half margins below 2025. The growth is concentrated in hardware and memory, where the manufacturer sets the price and the reseller passes it through. Net debt is $5.46B, consensus revenue growth slows to 3.67% in 2027, and a 7.30% short float says a meaningful part of the market expects this to be a volume boom that leaves the middleman with less, not more.

Top 3 Risks

01
Gross-margin compression. Q2 2026 gross margin 20.1% vs 20.8%; H1 20.5% vs 21.1%; management guided second-half margins below 2025. Twice in 2026 a revenue beat was overwhelmed by a margin miss — a −20.3% day in May and a −9.0% day in August.
02
Cash conversion and leverage together. FCF conversion of 42% in Q2 (per the call summary) against a typical 80–90%, $5.46B of net debt, and a ~$525M acquisition closing in Q3. Any of the three is manageable alone; together they narrow the room for buybacks, which have been doing much of the work in EPS growth.
03
Cycle timing. Consensus revenue growth falls from 8.11% in 2026 to 3.67% in 2027, and Q3 2026 is guided to a mid-single-digit sequential decline. If the AI-infrastructure and memory-driven buying of 2026 pulled demand forward, 2027 inherits a tougher comparison with the same margin problem.
Industry Commentary
The middleman's squeeze in an AI hardware cycle
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

An IT reseller earns a spread on other companies' products. When demand is broad — laptops, licences, networking, services — the spread is healthy. When demand concentrates in large infrastructure purchases and component prices are rising, the spread narrows: customers negotiate hard on big-ticket orders, manufacturers hold their own pricing, and the reseller's cut shrinks as a percentage even as the dollar volume grows. CDW's 2026 numbers fit that pattern. Sales grew about 10%, gross margin fell 60–70 bps, and the Q2 call summary noted memory price inflation and continuing product-delivery delays even as supply "normalized." CEO Christine Leahy put it plainly on the Q1 call: "tight memory supply and higher prices" were redirecting customer budgets toward infrastructure — networking, storage, servers, power and cooling. The unresolved question is whether this is a mix phase that reverses as services and software catch up with hardware, or a new, lower level of margin for the whole channel.

Buying the services layer
STRATEGIC SHIFT

The channel's answer to a thinning product spread is to sell more of what it does itself. Futurum's analysis of the Lovelytics deal places it in a pattern: Insight Enterprises acquired specialist talent through Inspire11, and SHI invested in NStarX and set up AI and cyber labs. The logic, in Christine Leahy's words: "There is no AI strategy without a data strategy." Services carry higher margins than resold hardware, but they are also harder to scale, and about $525M buys a business of just over 600 people — against a company with 14,800 employees and $23.5B of sales. It is a direction, not yet a mix change.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

CDW trades at 12.12x EV/EBITDA, 1.04x EV/sales and 0.78x price/sales, with a 4.7% free-cash-flow yield and a 1.72% dividend. The bull framing: the stock was priced near $256 in March 2024 on lower sales than today; margins have slipped by well under one percentage point, and the price has fallen 42.8%. A modest margin stabilisation on 8% sales growth is not in the price. The bear framing: the March 2024 multiple was the anomaly — a reseller with a 4.6% net margin, 3.11x debt/EBITDA and falling cash conversion deserves a distributor's multiple, not a technology multiple, and today's price is simply the market re-classifying the business. The deciding data are reported every quarter: gross margin and free cash flow. Neither has yet turned.
