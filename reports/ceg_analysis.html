01 Company Overview

Constellation Energy describes itself as "the largest private-sector power producer in the world and the nation's largest producer of clean and reliable energy," providing about 10% of the country's clean energy. It is an independent power producer (IPP) — not a rate-regulated utility — meaning earnings are driven by wholesale power prices, hedging, and long-dated bilateral contracts rather than an allowed regulatory return. Following the January 7, 2026 close of the Calpine acquisition, the combined company operates roughly 55 GW of generation capacity across nuclear, natural gas, geothermal, wind, solar and hydro, and serves about 2.5 million customer accounts. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $25.53B (+8.3% YoY); TTM revenue through Q3 2026 has jumped to $34.25B (+37.9% YoY), mostly because Calpine has been consolidated since January. In September and October it signed 20-year agreements with Amazon (690 MW from Calvert Cliffs in Maryland) and Google (backing about 890 MW of new nuclear capacity from upgrades to 11 existing reactors), and its Q3 release reported an agreement to buy a 609 MW gas plant in Rhode Island. Constellation ranks #121 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization (our calculation at the Nov 13, 2026 close).

The nuclear fleet is the earnings engine and the reason CEG is valued above other power producers. In Q3 2026 the fleet ran a 97.2% capacity factor (the share of its maximum possible output it actually produced, excluding the co-owned Salem and South Texas Project plants) and produced 46.7 terawatt-hours including its owned share of those two plants, with 20 planned refueling-outage days against 23 a year earlier, which matters because every outage day is lost output that costs almost nothing to produce. Nuclear output also carries a federal production tax credit (PTC) floor: an inflation-adjusted credit that pays more when power prices fall and shrinks to zero when they are high, so it limits the damage if prices drop.

Constellation acquired Calpine from Energy Capital Partners for a $16.4B equity value; the 10-Q records total consideration of $21.84B (50M shares valued at $17.60B plus $4.34B cash) with $12.55B of Calpine debt assumed and $11.11B of goodwill recognised. The deal bought an efficient combined-cycle gas fleet plus geothermal, and — strategically — presence in ERCOT (Texas) and California, the two markets where data-center and electrification load growth is fastest. It converts Constellation from a nuclear-plus-retail merchant into a 55 GW dispatchable-plus-carbon-free platform, which matters because hyperscalers increasingly want firm, 24/7 delivered power rather than intermittent output. DOJ remedies required divestitures: 4.4 GW across six plants (five in PJM, one in ERCOT) to LS Power for $5.0B (~$1,136/kW), plus an August 2026 agreement to sell Brazos Valley (606 MW, ERCOT) to LS Power for $860M (~$1,420/kW), which the company called a key step in meeting its regulatory commitments for the Calpine deal. Those sale prices are themselves a useful read-through: buyers are paying up for existing plants already connected to the grid. The gas fleet is still growing: the Q3 release reported an agreement to buy RISEC Holdings, owner of the 609 MW Rhode Island State Energy Center (a combined-cycle gas plant), from Shell Energy North America for $715M, subject to regulatory approvals.

This is the business investors are mainly paying for. Constellation sells power and energy services to about 2.5 million customer accounts, but the main value driver is the long-dated power purchase agreement (PPA) book with the big cloud and AI companies and other large corporates. September and October brought a run of new contracts. On Sep 30 Amazon signed a 20-year agreement for 690 MW from Calvert Cliffs, including a 190 MW expansion expected in 2030–2032, supporting more than $3B of investment. On Oct 6 Google signed a 20-year PPA anchoring more than $4.3B of uprates that add about 890 MW across 11 reactors in Illinois, Pennsylvania and New Jersey, the first expected in 2028, plus a separate 15-year agreement covering 2,700 MW in PJM (the grid operator for the Mid-Atlantic and parts of the Midwest). Constellation also signed its first "Peak+" deal: 270 MW for 20 years from the existing nuclear fleet starting in 2028, customer unnamed. "Our approach proves we can power the growing digital economy while strengthening our electric grid," CEO Joe Dominguez said in the Q3 release. That followed about 920 MW of nuclear PPAs reported with the Q2 results, with 15- to 20-year terms starting in 2029–2032, including 176 MW with Walmart. The anchor deal remains the 20-year Microsoft PPA signed in 2024 to restart Three Mile Island Unit 1 as the Crane Clean Energy Center (835 MW). Each new PPA turns power sold at market prices into contracted cash flow from a creditworthy buyer, which is how a generator earns a utility-like (or better) valuation.

Constellation's cheapest new megawatts do not come from greenfield build — they come from squeezing more output out of assets that already have interconnection rights. In September 2025 the company said uprates at its existing plants together with the Crane restart "could add up to 2,000 megawatts of new, clean and reliable baseload capacity to the grid," and September and October 2026 put buyers behind the first large blocks of uprates: about 890 MW of uprates anchored by Google and a 190 MW expansion at Calvert Cliffs backed by Amazon. In October the Department of Energy gave a conditional commitment to guarantee a loan of up to $5.0B for nuclear uprates, with a 30-year term at 0.375 percentage points over comparable Treasury yields. The Crane Clean Energy Center restart is the flagship: 835 MW of pressurized-water reactor capacity, targeted for 2027, supported by a $1B Department of Energy loan closed in November 2025, when the company said the site was more than 80% staffed with over 500 employees. Since then the NRC approved a fuel license amendment (Q2 2026 release), and on June 1, 2026 FERC approved a waiver transferring grid-connection rights from the Eddystone station to Crane, which keeps the restart from waiting on PJM transmission upgrades that could run past 2030. Wind, solar and hydro round out the carbon-free portfolio but are not separately material to earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Largest US Nuclear Fleet Irreplaceable Interconnection Rights Nuclear PTC Revenue Floor 20-Year Amazon & Google Contracts 97% Nuclear Capacity Factor 55 GW Multi-Market Scale NRC Licence & Operator Barrier

Constellation's moat is physical and regulatory rather than technological. Nuclear plants in the United States are effectively non-replicable at current cost and permitting timelines: the licensing, siting, spent-fuel handling and skilled-operator requirements mean the existing fleet is a closed set, and Constellation owns the largest piece of it. That scarcity has become an economic moat only recently, because hyperscaler demand for firm, carbon-free, 24/7 power co-located with grid interconnection turned a commodity into a differentiated product. The second layer is operational: a 97.2% capacity factor in Q3 2026, with 20 refueling-outage days against 23 a year earlier, means more output that costs almost nothing extra from plants whose fixed costs are already paid. Third, the nuclear PTC works one way: the inflation-adjusted credit tops up revenue if wholesale prices collapse, while Constellation keeps the full gain when prices are high. Fourth, the Calpine deal added dispatchable gas in ERCOT and California, letting Constellation sell shaped, firm products rather than raw nuclear output. Finally, the contract book itself is becoming a moat: 20-year agreements with Amazon and Google on top of Q2's 15- to 20-year deals lock in cash flows that competitors with smaller carbon-free fleets cannot offer at the same scale.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Narrative-dependent valuation. At a 22.5× trailing P/E against an XLU utility-sector 18.3× and an IPP peer median of 17.4× (Vistra and NRG; Talen has a loss), CEG carries a premium that rests on expectations of more data-center contracts rather than on regulated earnings. The stock is 26.4% below its 52-week high of $380.78 (Dec 12, 2025) and −16.5% over the past year, even after a record contracting quarter. On Nov 4, the day after the US midterm elections, it fell 6.7% close to close; Reuters named Constellation among the power companies most exposed to data-center projects in Texas as investors prepared for tougher scrutiny of the AI boom. Politics can move this premium as fast as contracts can.
▸
Commodity and hedge volatility. As an IPP, reported GAAP results swing with mark-to-market on the hedge book. Q2 2026 GAAP EPS fell to $1.42 from $2.67 even as adjusted operating EPS rose to $2.55 from $1.91; in Q3 the gap ran the other way, GAAP $5.19 against adjusted $5.06. H1 2026 fair-value changes on derivatives reduced operating cash flow by $589M. Investors are asked to lean on the company's own adjusted number.
▸
Leverage and integration risk from Calpine. Long-term debt was $19.04B at Sep 30, 2026 against $7.25B at year-end 2025, total debt is about $23.39B, cash fell to $603M from $3.64B, and $11.11B of goodwill was recognised at the Calpine close. New commitments add to the bill: more than $4.3B for the Google uprates, more than $3B at Calvert Cliffs and $715M for the Rhode Island plant, part of it to be funded by the conditional DOE loan. If the gap between power prices and gas costs narrows in ERCOT or California, or if the promised savings from combining the companies slip, that goodwill could be written down and the deal would add less to earnings per share.
▸
Regulatory and interconnection dependency. The Crane restart required a FERC waiver in June 2026 to move capacity interconnection rights from Eddystone; without it, PJM's transmission upgrades could have kept the unit from running fully until after 2030. Arrangements in which a data center takes power directly from a plant on site for data centers remain an unsettled area of FERC and state policy, and any adverse ruling on cost allocation or grid-charge treatment would directly damage the PPA economics that support the stock's premium valuation. The nuclear PTC itself is set by law and can be changed by Congress.

Notable One-Off Items. Constellation's FY2026 reported figures are heavily distorted, and the distortions run in both directions.

1. Calpine purchase accounting. The January 7, 2026 close introduced $11,107M of goodwill on total consideration of $21,835M (50M shares valued at $17,603M plus $4,342M cash), with $12,551M of debt assumed. At Jun 30, 2026 total assets had jumped to $98.25B from $57.25B and long-term debt to $19.11B from $7.25B. Every YoY comparison in FY2026 (TTM revenue +37.9%; nine-month net income attributable to common shareholders $3,938M versus $1,887M) is swollen by the deal and should not be read as growth of the existing business.

2. Mark-to-market on hedges. H1 2026 net fair-value changes on derivatives reduced operating cash flows by $589M; current derivative assets and liabilities stood at $2,167M and $716M. This is the primary reason Q2 GAAP EPS of $1.42 diverged so sharply from adjusted operating EPS of $2.55 (prior-year quarter: $2.67 GAAP versus $1.91 adjusted, so the gap flipped sign year over year). In Q3 2026 GAAP EPS of $5.19 ran slightly above adjusted $5.06.

3. Nuclear fuel, PTC pass-through and decommissioning trusts. Net realized and unrealized gains on the nuclear decommissioning trust funds (money set aside to dismantle the plants) were $419M in H1 2026 — a market-driven, non-operating item that flatters GAAP results when markets rise.

4. Divestiture gains. The DOJ-mandated sales — 4.4 GW across six gas plants to LS Power for $5.0B and Brazos Valley (606 MW) for $860M — are one-time proceeds at implied values of ~$1,136/kW and ~$1,420/kW respectively. They fund buybacks but are not recurring earnings.

Nuclear Credit Policy
The nuclear production tax credit is set by law and adjusted for inflation each year; it pays out when power prices are low and shrinks to zero when they are high. Because it limits the damage from falling prices, any legislative change to nuclear credits would change the whole risk profile of the stock, not just a few cents of EPS. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Constellation owns an irreplaceable 55 GW fleet anchored by the largest US nuclear position, running at a 97.2% capacity factor in Q3 2026. Demand is rising for the first time in decades: US generation hit a record 4,430 TWh in 2025 (+2.8%) after two flat decades, and September and October brought 20-year agreements with Amazon (690 MW) and Google (about 890 MW of uprates) plus a first Peak+ deal. Q3 adjusted EPS of $5.06 beat the $3.90 Nasdaq consensus by 29.7%, and management raised FY2026 guidance for the second time, to $12.50–$12.80, with the nuclear PTC limiting the damage if power prices fall and a conditional $5.0B DOE loan to fund uprates. At 20.9× FY2027 consensus earnings against a $338.25 average target, the stock is 26.4% below its 52-week high with no analyst carrying a sell rating — and CEG has the strongest balance sheet in the group at 0.71× debt-to-equity versus a 4.39× peer median.

Bear Case

CEG trades at 20.9× FY2027 consensus earnings and a 1.43 PEG versus an IPP peer median of 12.2× and 0.39 — a premium that rests on expectations of more contracts with the big cloud and AI companies, not on regulated returns. That premium has been shrinking: the stock is −16.5% over twelve months and fell 34.1% from its October 2025 monthly-close peak to June 2026 despite beating estimates, and it lost 6.7% the day after the midterm elections, when Reuters named it among the power companies most exposed to data-center projects in Texas. If PPA announcements slow, if FERC or state regulators restrict plants selling power directly to data centers on site, or if AI spending plateaus, the valuation could fall toward peers' with no operating setback at all (at the peer median's forward P/E, the same earnings would put the price about 42% lower). Meanwhile the fundamentals are noisier than the adjusted numbers suggest: Q2 GAAP EPS fell to $1.42 from $2.67 on hedge marks, H1 derivative fair-value swings cut operating cash flow by $589M, TTM free cash flow is $1.32B on a $98.5B company, long-term debt is $19.04B against $7.25B at the end of 2025, and new commitments (more than $4.3B of Google uprates, more than $3B at Calvert Cliffs, $715M for a Rhode Island gas plant) are still to be paid for. Crane slipping past 2027 would remove the most visible near-term catalyst.

01
A lower valuation if data-center contracting stalls. This is the dominant risk and deserves equal weight to the bull case. CEG's 20.9× FY2027 forward P/E is about 72% above the IPP median of 12.2×, a gap that exists because investors are paying today for megawatts they expect to be contracted later. A quarter without meaningful new PPAs, an adverse FERC ruling on plants selling power directly to data centers, or state politics turning against data centers (as the Nov 4 sell-off showed) could shrink that gap with earnings unchanged. The October 2025 to June 2026 fall of 34.1% on monthly closes happened through exactly this mechanism.
02
Commodity, hedge and capacity-auction volatility. As an IPP, Constellation has no regulator resetting its prices to cover costs. PJM EMAAC capacity reference prices moved from $125.71 to $289.67 — a 2.3× swing that can reverse. GAAP earnings swing with mark-to-market (revaluing hedges at current prices each quarter): Q2 GAAP EPS of $1.42 versus adjusted $2.55, Q3 $5.19 versus $5.06, and a $589M H1 hit to operating cash flow from derivative fair-value changes. The nuclear PTC limits the downside but is set by law, not by contract.
03
Calpine integration, leverage and goodwill impairment. Total debt of about $23.39B, long-term debt of $19.04B at Sep 30, 2026 against $7.25B at the end of 2025, cash down to $603M, and $11.11B of goodwill from a $21.84B purchase price create real balance-sheet sensitivity, with the Rhode Island purchase and billions of uprate spending still ahead. The company has not put a number on Calpine synergies. If ERCOT or California spark spreads shrink, or integration slips, the case that the deal adds to earnings per share and the goodwill value both come under pressure; TTM free cash flow of $1.32B leaves a limited cushion.
  Industry Commentary
AI Data-Center Power Demand and Behind-the-Meter Deals
EMERGING TAILWIND

US electricity generation reached a record 4,430 TWh in 2025, up 2.8% from 4,310 TWh in 2024 — a genuine break from roughly two decades of flat consumption, and EIA expects further growth through 2026–2027 led by commercial-sector data-center load. For a generator with spare interconnected capacity this is the most favourable demand backdrop in a generation. Constellation is turning it into contracts: about 920 MW of nuclear PPAs reported with the Q2 2026 results, on 15- to 20-year terms (including 176 MW with Walmart), then in September and October 20-year agreements with Amazon (690 MW) and Google (about 890 MW of uprates across 11 reactors) and a first 270 MW Peak+ deal. The open questions are regulatory and now political: selling power directly to a data center built at the plant remains contested at FERC and in state proceedings, Constellation itself needed a June 2026 FERC waiver to move grid-connection rights from Eddystone to Crane, and on Nov 4, the day after the midterm elections, power companies most exposed to Texas data-center projects sold off (Reuters). The demand is real; the rules for capturing it are still being written.

The Revaluation of Existing Nuclear
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

Five years ago US nuclear plants were closing for economic reasons — Three Mile Island Unit 1 shut in 2019. Today Constellation is spending to restart it as the 835 MW Crane Clean Energy Center under a 20-year Microsoft PPA, with a $1B DOE loan, a site more than 80% staffed by November 2025 and a 2027 target. That reversal is the industry story in one asset. The economics rest on three legs: hyperscaler willingness to pay a premium for firm carbon-free power; the nuclear production tax credit, an inflation-adjusted credit that works as a revenue floor; and the fact that uprates on existing licensed sites are far cheaper and faster than new build (the company said in 2025 that uprates and the Crane restart together could add up to 2,000 MW). September and October tested that third leg: Google is anchoring about 890 MW of uprates and Amazon a 190 MW expansion at Calvert Cliffs, and the Department of Energy offered a conditional loan guarantee of up to $5.0B to fund them. The vulnerability is that two of those three legs are policy-dependent. A change in the federal nuclear credit regime would not merely trim EPS; it would remove the downside protection that justifies paying a premium price for nuclear plants that sell at market prices at all.

Power Prices, Capacity Markets and the Bull/Bear Frame
ONGOING HEADWIND

Capacity markets have repriced violently — management cited a PJM EMAAC reference price of $289.67 against $125.71 a year earlier (Q2 2026 release), a 2.3× move. For an IPP, with no regulator resetting its prices, that flows straight to earnings in both directions. The bull framing: tight reserve margins plus record demand mean scarcity pricing persists, the PTC limits the downside if prices fall, Calpine's gas fleet lets Constellation sell shaped firm products into ERCOT and California, and a 0.71× debt-to-equity ratio versus a 4.39× peer median means CEG can fund growth when more heavily borrowed peers cannot. Management's two 2026 guidance raises, to $12.50–$12.80, rest on this. The bear framing, weighted equally: capacity prices that more than doubled can fall back; most of the independent power producers lost ground together over the past year (CEG −16.5%, Vistra −22.5%, NRG −41.6%; Talen +5.9%) while QQQ rose 11.8%, showing these stocks trade on sentiment about AI power demand; CEG's 20.9× FY2027 forward P/E and 1.43 PEG sit well above the peer median of 12.2× and 0.39; TTM free cash flow is $1.32B against a $98.5B market cap; and $11.11B of goodwill plus about $23.39B of debt came with a gas fleet whose value depends on the same spark spreads. If AI spending plateaus or PPA signings pause for two quarters, a slide toward peer valuations implies more downside than even the $240 low analyst target contemplates.
