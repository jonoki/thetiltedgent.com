01 Company Overview

CF Industries takes cheap North American natural gas and turns it into ammonia, then upgrades that ammonia into granular urea, UAN solution, ammonium nitrate and diesel exhaust fluid. The company calls itself the world's largest producer of ammonia. It started life in 1946 as the Central Farmers Fertilizer Company, a federation of farm co-operatives. It adopted a profit-first business model in 2002, ceased to be a co-operative when it listed in 2005, and joined the S&P 500 on August 27, 2008. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $7.94B, up 17.9%, and trailing EPS is $14.18, up 71.5%, earned by a company with just 2,900 employees.

The whole business is one spread: what the world pays for a ton of nitrogen, minus what CF pays for the gas to make it. In Q3 2026 CF's gas cost $2.87 per MMBtu, down from $2.96 a year earlier. The other side of the spread has been anything but steady. The conflict with Iran cut Middle East nitrogen exports this year, and in its Q2 release CF said that region normally supplies 25–30% of traded ammonia and 35–40% of traded urea. The stock rose from a $77.34 December 2025 close to $129.84 by the end of March 2026. By the end of June, CF said, prices were back at pre-conflict levels. Then they turned again: the Q3 release says global nitrogen prices "increased through the quarter" from the end of June on higher demand and tight supply. CF sold 6.5% more tons in Q3 than a year earlier, and sales rose 12.1% to $1.86B. Selling prices were mixed: ammonia $532 a ton against $418, but urea $423 against $450 and UAN $322 against $331. Gross margin was 38.7%, against 51.5% in the peak second quarter. The debate is how much of the $14.18 of trailing earnings is the low-cost franchise and how much is a war premium plus one-off gains.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

North American gas cost advantage Scale — world's largest ammonia producer 97% utilisation Distribution into the US Corn Belt Capacity is slow & costly to build Carbon capture & 45Q credits Free cash flow funds buybacks (shares −7.6% in a year)

A commodity producer's only lasting edge is its position on the cost curve. The world price of nitrogen is set by the last, most expensive ton the market still needs, often from a European plant paying European gas prices. CF makes its tons with US gas at around $3–4 per MMBtu and sells into the same market, so it earns the gap between its own cost and that producer's. The Q3 release says an estimated 37% of Europe's rated ammonia capacity was offline or running at reduced rates in October 2026. On the call Bohn said European shutdowns he had expected around 2030 could come sooner, by 12 to 18 months. CF's second advantage is that the gap takes years to close. Management says capacity under construction is not projected to keep pace with demand over the next four years. It puts mid-cycle EBITDA at $2.9B and expects its low-carbon projects to lift that to $3.3B by 2030. What the moat doesn't protect is the price. CF sets none of the prices it sells at.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The peak may already be behind it. Q3 gross margin was 38.7% against 51.5% in Q2, and urea sold for $423 a ton against $593. Consensus expects FY2026 EPS of $14.77 and then $11.28 for FY2027, a 23.6% drop, with revenue falling about 7.8%. On consensus numbers, the peak is this year.
02
The US gas advantage cuts both ways. Nine-month 2026 gas cost rose to $3.76 per MMBtu from $3.35, even though Q3 alone fell to $2.87. The advantage is a spread, and it narrows if US gas rises (for example from LNG export demand) or if European gas falls back.
03
Single-site concentration. One incident at Yazoo City in November 2025 took out a complex for more than a year, cut AN volume by two-thirds in Q3 and cost about six points of Q2 sales volume. The largest complex, Donaldsonville, sits on the Louisiana Gulf Coast.
04
Policy can move prices overnight. China's urea export quotas and price floors, introduced in May 2026 and since revised, can release or withhold millions of tons. On September 21, 2026, StockAnalysis carried a Barron's headline saying CF and Nutrien fell after President Trump said the US was working on a potash deal with Belarus. Potash is not a CF product, but the whole group trades together on the headline.
05
Legal challenges to Blue Point. Rural Roots Louisiana and Mount Triumph Baptist Church asked a federal court in July 2026 to block an expedited Army Corps of Engineers permit for the plant (10/12 Industry Report, July 6, 2026), and environmental groups sued in Louisiana over its air operating permit (QCIntel, September 4, 2026). Construction began in August on what is a $3.7B project.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Readings below 30 mean it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value, and a stock can stay overbought for a long time when the commodity underneath it is in a squeeze.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $60.59 to the November 6, 2026 close of $119.39, CF gained 97.0% on price. Dividends added $8.70 a share over the period (StockAnalysis dividend history, ex-dates from February 2022 through August 2026). The path was two nitrogen price shocks with a long quiet stretch between them. First came the 2022 surge. After that, the stock traded between roughly $61 and $93 at month-end from early 2023 until December 2025. Then 2026 brought a second surge: +20.5% in January, +6.8% in February and +30.4% in March, the largest monthly gain in the five-year series. By the end of March the stock was at $129.84.

The 6-period monthly RSI, computed on the series charted here, reached 84.5 in March 2026, just under its 2022 peak of 85.1 in May 2022. It now reads 55.7, close to neutral. The 10-month moving average of about $118.73 sits just below the price, so the long trend has flattened. The August 2026 month-end of $130.03 was the highest in the window, 19 cents above March, two months after the end of June, when, the company said, nitrogen prices had returned to pre-conflict levels. September then fell 11.4% and October recovered 7.0%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The bull case is that the moat is the gas spread, not the war. CF makes the world's key nitrogen molecule on $3–4 US gas and sells it at a price set by higher-cost European producers, 37% of whose ammonia capacity was offline or reduced in October. Management says new capacity will not keep pace with demand for four years, and that its projects can lift mid-cycle EBITDA from $2.9B to $3.3B by 2030. The balance sheet is conservative (0.41 debt/equity, 2.98 current ratio), free cash flow is about 12% of market value, the share count fell 7.6% in a year with $1.11B still authorised, and the dividend was raised 20% in July. Yazoo City returns in H1 2027, bringing back the lost volume. Blue Point One then gives CF a 2029 start in low-carbon ammonia with Japan's largest energy company as partner. At 8.4x trailing and 8.1x forward earnings, the bull argues, the market is pricing the windfall as the whole story and ignoring the franchise underneath.

Bear Case

Earnings of $14.18 a share came from an unusual year: a war-driven price spike, a $170M litigation gain and insurance recoveries. Q3 gross margin was already 12.8 points below Q2, and urea and UAN both sold below last year's prices. Consensus already has EPS falling about 24% to $11.28 in 2027, so the "cheap" forward multiple is 10.6x on the lower number. One complex has been down since November 2025, and capex rises to about $1.3B in 2026 (about $950M of it CF's share), which pulls cash away from buybacks. China's export authorizations of almost nine million tons and a single political headline can both move the price. Blue Point's permits are being challenged in court. With 13 of 22 analysts at Hold, the average target 8.3% above the price and three Sells, analysts largely see the stock as close to fully valued.

Top 3 Risks

01
Nitrogen prices can fall back. 2026's earnings rest on prices that have already moved: ammonia sold for $677 a ton in Q2 and $532 in Q3, and urea for $593 and $423. The 2022–2023 cycle shows how quickly that unwinds: revenue fell 40.7% in a year and the stock fell 43% from its peak month-end to its trough. The return of Middle East supply through the Strait of Hormuz is the swing factor.
02
Operational concentration. The Yazoo City outage shows how one site can move a whole segment. Any unplanned outage at the large Gulf Coast complexes during a price spike costs the most margin at exactly the wrong time, and insurance recovers cost, not lost windfall.
03
The gas spread narrows. Nine-month gas cost rose 12.2% year over year to $3.76 per MMBtu. A structurally higher US gas price or a collapse in European gas would compress the cost advantage the whole moat depends on.
Industry Commentary
The Iran conflict and Middle East supply
FADING TAILWIND

In its Q2 release CF said the Middle East normally supplies 25–30% of globally traded ammonia and 35–40% of globally traded urea. On the November call it estimated the market has lost about nine million metric tons of urea supply. The Q3 release estimates that only about half of average urea exports from the region have left the Persian Gulf since the conflict began. Management expects supply to stay below pre-conflict levels because of continued hostilities, difficulty transiting the Strait of Hormuz and the time needed to restore plants. Frost said that even if hostilities ended today it would take four to six months. The price response has been uneven: CF said prices were back at pre-conflict levels by the end of June, then rose through Q3 on higher demand and tight supply.

Europe's gas problem and China's export valve
PRICE-SETTING FORCES

Two outside forces set CF's selling price: the cost of the highest-cost producer and the policy of the largest one. Europe's ammonia plants are squeezed by sharply higher gas prices: the release estimates 37% of rated capacity was offline or running at reduced rates in October 2026, and about 35% of Trinidad's ammonia capacity is being permanently shut. China's urea export quotas and price floors, introduced in May 2026 and since revised, are the valve on the other side. CF projects Chinese urea exports in 2026 will be 2–4 million metric tons higher than in 2025, and Frost said authorizations now total almost nine million tons. On demand, India is expected to import more than 10 million metric tons of urea in 2026, and CF says nitrogen channel inventory is low throughout the global system, including North America.

Low-carbon ammonia — the long-term bet
EMERGING TAILWIND

CF describes itself as a manufacturer of "hydrogen and nitrogen products", and that wording reflects its long-term strategy. Ammonia carries hydrogen and can be shipped and burned as a fuel. JERA is Japan's largest energy company, and it has taken 35% of a $3.7B plant designed to capture 98% of its production CO2. That makes the end market credible, not only a projection. The economics depend on policy: CF already books 45Q credits on sequestered CO2 ($62M in the first nine months of 2026), and Yazoo City's own carbon-capture project is due to start in 2028. Two things remain to be proven: Blue Point One's first production, still three years out, and the price premium low-carbon ammonia will earn. Until then, the stock trades on today's nitrogen price.
