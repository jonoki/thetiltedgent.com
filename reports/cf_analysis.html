01 Company Overview

CF Industries takes cheap North American natural gas and turns it into ammonia, then upgrades that ammonia into granular urea, UAN solution, ammonium nitrate and diesel exhaust fluid. The company calls itself the world's largest producer of ammonia. It started life in 1946 as the Central Farmers Fertilizer Company, a federation of farm co-operatives. It adopted a profit-first business model in 2002, ceased to be a co-operative when it listed in 2005, and joined the S&P 500 on August 27, 2008. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $7.74B, up 20.0%, and trailing EPS is $13.44, up 76.6%, earned by a company with just 2,900 employees.

The whole business is one spread: what the world pays for a ton of nitrogen, minus what CF pays for the gas to make it. In Q2 2026 CF's gas cost $3.37 per MMBtu, essentially unchanged from $3.36 a year earlier. What changed was the other side. From mid-February 2026 the conflict with Iran cut Middle East nitrogen exports, and that region normally supplies 25–30% of traded ammonia and 35–40% of traded urea. CF's ammonia sold for $677 a ton against $452 a year earlier, and urea for $593 against $460. Gross margin reached 51.5% for the quarter. The stock rose from a $77.34 December 2025 close to $129.84 by the end of March 2026. The catch is that by the end of Q2, the company's own release says, seasonal demand declines and expected Middle East supply had "pushed nitrogen prices back to pre-conflict levels." The market is now pricing a fading windfall on top of a genuine low-cost franchise, and the debate is how much of the $13.44 is the franchise.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

North American gas cost advantage Scale — world's largest ammonia producer 98% utilisation Distribution into the US Corn Belt Capacity is slow & costly to build Carbon capture & 45Q credits FCF funds buybacks (−8.5% shares YoY)

A commodity producer's only lasting edge is its position on the cost curve. The world price of nitrogen is set by the marginal ton, often a European plant paying European gas prices. CF makes its tons with US gas at around $3–4 per MMBtu and sells into the same market, so it earns the gap between its own cost and the marginal producer's. That is why the release says high European gas "will further pressure marginal European nitrogen production and support the global clearing price." CF's second advantage is that the gap takes years to close. Management says capacity under construction will not keep pace with demand over the next four years, and that building new plants in other low-cost-gas regions has become more expensive. On that basis it has raised its mid-cycle EBITDA expectations. What the moat doesn't protect is the price. CF sets none of the prices it sells at.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The windfall is already fading. CF's own release says that by the end of Q2, seasonal demand declines and expected returning Middle East supply "pushed nitrogen prices back to pre-conflict levels." Consensus expects FY2026 EPS of $14.67 and then $10.70 for FY2027, a 27% drop, with revenue falling 11.9%. On consensus numbers, the peak is this year.
02
The US gas advantage cuts both ways. First-half 2026 gas cost rose to $4.01 per MMBtu from $3.52. The advantage is a spread, and it narrows if US gas rises (for example from LNG export demand) or if European gas falls back.
03
Single-site concentration. One incident at Yazoo City in November 2025 took out a complex for roughly 18 months, turned the AN segment loss-making and cost about six points of Q2 sales volume. The largest complex, Donaldsonville, sits on the Louisiana Gulf Coast.
04
Policy can move prices overnight. China's urea export quotas and price floors, introduced in May 2026 and since revised, can release or withhold millions of tons. On September 21, 2026, StockAnalysis carried a Barron's headline saying CF and Nutrien fell after President Trump said the US was working on a potash deal with Belarus. Potash is not a CF product, but the whole group trades together on the headline.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The bull case is that the moat is the gas spread, not the war. CF makes the world's key nitrogen molecule on $3–4 US gas and sells it at a price set by higher-cost European producers, who are facing sharply higher gas costs again. Management says capacity additions will not keep pace with demand for four years and has raised its mid-cycle EBITDA view. The balance sheet is conservative (0.41 debt/equity, 4.86 current ratio), the FCF yield is about 10%, the share count fell 8.5% in a year with $1.48B still authorised, and the dividend was just raised 20%. Yazoo City returns in H1 2027, bringing back the lost volume. Blue Point One then gives CF a 2029 option on low-carbon ammonia with Japan's largest energy company as partner. At 9.2x trailing and 8.4x forward earnings, the bull argues, the market is pricing the windfall as the whole story and ignoring the franchise underneath.

Bear Case

Earnings of $13.44 a share came from an unusual year: a war-driven price spike, a $170M litigation gain and insurance recoveries. The company itself says prices were back at pre-conflict levels by the end of June. Consensus already has EPS falling 27% to $10.70 in 2027, so the "cheap" forward multiple is 11.5x on the lower number. Volumes fell 15% in Q2 and one complex has been down since November. Capex rises to about $1.3B in 2026 (about $950M of it CF's share), which pulls cash away from buybacks. China's export policy and a single political headline can move the price by millions of tons. With 13 of 21 analysts at Hold, the average target 1.6% above the price and four Sells, the Street largely sees the stock as fully valued.

Top 3 Risks

01
Mean reversion in nitrogen prices. 2026's earnings rest on Q2 ammonia at $677 a ton and urea at $593. The 2022–2023 cycle shows how quickly that unwinds: revenue fell 40.7% in a year and the stock fell 43% from its peak month-end to its trough. The return of Middle East supply through the Strait of Hormuz is the swing factor.
02
Operational concentration. The Yazoo City outage shows how one site can move a whole segment. Any unplanned outage at the large Gulf Coast complexes during a price spike costs the most margin at exactly the wrong time, and insurance recovers cost, not lost windfall.
03
The gas spread narrows. First-half gas cost rose 14% year over year to $4.01 per MMBtu. A structurally higher US gas price or a collapse in European gas would compress the cost advantage the whole moat depends on.
Industry Commentary
The Iran conflict and Middle East supply
FADING TAILWIND

The Middle East normally supplies 25–30% of globally traded ammonia and 35–40% of globally traded urea. CF estimates the conflict with Iran has removed 4.0–4.5 million metric tons of urea and about 1 million metric tons of ammonia from traded supply so far. Management expects the region's supply to stay below pre-conflict levels because of continued hostilities, uncertainty around transit through the Strait of Hormuz and the time needed to restore plants. It also expects higher shipping costs from the region to persist. The price response has been shorter-lived than the supply loss. Prices rose from mid-February and were back at pre-conflict levels by the end of June, according to CF. Management's longer view is that Middle Eastern and Russian producers now carry "an enduring geopolitical risk premium", which would favour a North American producer.

Europe's gas problem and China's export valve
PRICE-SETTING FORCES

Two outside forces set CF's selling price: the cost of the highest-cost producer and the policy of the largest one. European gas has risen because of LNG supply uncertainty, heat waves and storage below the five-year average, which squeezes marginal European nitrogen output and supports the global clearing price. China introduced urea export quotas and price floors in May 2026, since revised, and CF projects 4–6 million metric tons of Chinese urea exports this year. Actual volumes will depend on Beijing's policy and on demand for fall restocking. On demand, CF expects India and Southeast Asia to import urea at or above second-half 2025 levels, and North American channel inventory to be below average going into 2027.

Low-carbon ammonia — the long-dated option
EMERGING TAILWIND

CF describes itself as a manufacturer of "hydrogen and nitrogen products", and that wording reflects its long-term strategy. Ammonia carries hydrogen and can be shipped and burned as a fuel. JERA is Japan's largest energy company, and it has taken 35% of a $3.7B plant designed to capture 98% of its production CO2. That makes the end market credible, not only a projection. The economics depend on policy: CF already books 45Q credits on sequestered CO2 ($43M in the first half of 2026), and Yazoo City's own carbon-capture project is due to start in 2028. Two things remain to be proven. Blue Point One's first production is still three years out, and the price premium low-carbon ammonia will earn has not been disclosed in any source we fetched. Until then, the stock trades on today's nitrogen price.
