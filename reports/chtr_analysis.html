01 Company Overview

Charter sells home internet, mobile, TV and phone service under the Spectrum brand. Effective August 19, 2026 it closed its combination with Cox Communications, which takes its footprint to 70.7 million homes and businesses passed at September 30, 2026. Within a year of closing, the parent company will rename itself Cox Communications while the consumer brand stays Spectrum. Before the deal, standalone Charter reported FY2025 revenue of $54.77B (−0.56%), and EPS still rose from $24.47 in FY2021 to $36.21 in FY2025. The reason was a shrinking share count, not a growing business. In Q3 2026 a $6.3B write-down turned that into a loss of $20.50 a share.

The story of this stock fits in one number: −81%. At the end of November 2021 CHTR closed at $646.28. On November 9, 2026 it settled at $121.20. The company still throws off a lot of cash before interest: pro forma Adjusted EBITDA, counting Cox for the whole period, was $27.3B over the last twelve months. The market is questioning whether the core product is shrinking. In the first quarter reported with Cox, internet customers fell by 233,000 on a pro forma basis, against 171,000 a year earlier. Charter also wrote down the value of its cable franchises by $6.3B, a test its 10-Q says was triggered by the sustained fall in its share price. Management's answer, announced with the results, is a new operating model and $2B a year of cost savings within 18 months. The debate is simple to state: can a converged internet-plus-mobile bundle, now sold across the Cox footprint, plus those savings, stop the broadband losses before $108.6B of debt becomes the main thing that matters to shareholders?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Wired network to 70.7M passings Scale after Cox (34.3M internet customers) Internet + mobile bundle Cable franchise rights Commercial fiber (Segra, Cox Business)

The moat is physical plant, the cable already running past the house. Building that network again from scratch is not realistic for most competitors. The problem is that a network moat protects only against rivals who have to dig. Fiber builders do dig, and fixed-wireless providers don't need to, because they reuse cell towers they already own. The numbers show the moat shrinking in practice: legacy Charter's customers as a share of the homes and businesses its network passes fell to 53.4% at June 30, 2026 from 55.7% a year earlier, and in Q3 the combined company lost 233,000 internet customers on a pro forma basis. The $6.3B write-down of Charter's cable franchises in Q3 is an accounting recognition that those rights are worth less than the books said. Management's answer is to make the bundle the moat: a customer with internet and mobile from Spectrum faces a bigger switching decision than one with internet alone. Mobile adding 508,000 lines in a quarter shows the bundle selling; the internet losses show it is not yet enough.

The core product is shrinking in customers and price. Pro forma internet revenue fell 3.7% in Q3 2026, with internet customers down 233,000 in the quarter and residential revenue per customer down 1.6%. The quarterly loss was bigger than the 171,000 of a year earlier.

Leverage leaves little room for error. Principal debt was $108.6B at September 30, 2026, about 4.0x pro forma trailing Adjusted EBITDA of $27.3B (our arithmetic: ($108.6B − $0.6B cash) ÷ $27.3B). Interest coverage is 2.53x (StockAnalysis). With equity worth about $19.8B against $108.6B of debt, small changes in EBITDA cause large swings in equity value. In Q3 Charter exchanged about $5.5B of secured notes for about $4.0B of cash and new notes, cutting debt by about $1.5B.
03
Integration, a cost plan and management turnover at once. Cox customers are moving to Spectrum pricing and packaging, the free mobile year delays revenue, and the new operating model aims to take $2B a year out of operating costs within 18 months. All of this runs under an interim CFO, after the Chief Commercial Officer left his role in October. Wolfe Research downgraded the stock to Underperform with a $118 target on September 14.
04
Losing index buyers. Removal from the NASDAQ-100, effective June 22, 2026, meant funds that track that index had to sell and stopped buying. Short interest of 15.76% of float (17.43M shares at the October 15, 2026 settlement) shows how many investors are betting against the stock.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the reported figures. First, the $6.3B franchise impairment: a pre-tax, non-cash charge. Charter's 10-Q says the sustained decline in its share price triggered an interim test of the franchise value as of July 31, 2026. It is the main reason Q3 swung to a $2.40B loss attributable to shareholders from a $1.14B profit. Second, Cox is in the reported numbers only from August 19, which flatters reported growth (+9.1% revenue, +6.9% Adjusted EBITDA); the pro forma figures (−1.5% and −4.0%) are the like-for-like view. Third, transition expenses of $76M in Q3 reduced Adjusted EBITDA. Fourth, debt moves: about $5.5B of secured notes were exchanged for about $4.0B of cash and new notes, and $1.0B of legacy Cox notes were repaid at maturity. Total principal debt still rose to $108.6B with Cox's debt included. Any trailing per-share number in this report should be read with these in mind.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At about 3x consensus FY2027 earnings and 5.8x EV/EBITDA, the market is pricing Charter as if it were in terminal decline. The combined company produced $27.3B of pro forma Adjusted EBITDA over the last twelve months. Management is now targeting $2B a year of operating-cost savings within 18 months plus more than $1B of Cox synergies, together more than a tenth of that EBITDA, and expects the plan to support significantly higher free cash flow. Pro forma capex already fell to $3.2B in Q3 from $3.6B a year earlier, and should fall further once the network upgrade finishes in 2027. Mobile service revenue grew 17.3% pro forma and makes the bundle stickier. With equity at only about 15% of enterprise value, a small improvement in EBITDA would have a large effect on the share price. The median target of $145 implies +19.6%, and the most bullish target of $380 implies about 3.1x. Cox Enterprises, a long-term family owner, took most of its payment in Charter equity rather than cash.

Bear Case

The low multiple reflects real risk. Pro forma internet losses widened to 233K in Q3 from 171K a year earlier, revenue per customer is falling, and the first quarter with Cox ended in a $2.4B loss after a $6.3B write-down of the franchises themselves. Principal debt of $108.6B is about 4.0x pro forma EBITDA, interest coverage is only 2.53x, and trailing free cash flow fell to $3.79B from $4.74B a quarter earlier. The company is integrating its largest acquisition and running a $2B cost program under an interim CFO, and the plan was announced with few specifics, more details to follow. Short interest is 15.76% of float, 6 of 21 rated analysts say Sell, and the lowest target is $68. If EBITDA keeps falling at 3–4% a year, leverage rises even without new borrowing, and the shares could fall much further than the business itself.

Top 3 Risks

01
Broadband subscriber losses keep getting worse. Pro forma internet customers fell 233K in Q3 2026 against 171K a year earlier; legacy Cox alone lost 77K. Internet is 43% of revenue. If the new operating model does not slow the losses within a few quarters, the market will assume they are permanent.
02
Leverage while EBITDA is shrinking. Pro forma Adjusted EBITDA fell 4.0% in Q3 2026. With $108.6B of principal debt, each 1% drop in EBITDA hits equity value several times over, and the Altman Z-score, a bankruptcy-risk score where readings under 1.8 signal distress, is 0.54 (StockAnalysis).
03
Integration, cost cuts and leadership at the same time. Moving Cox customers to Spectrum pricing, giving away a free year of mobile, cutting $2B of yearly costs and running finance under an interim CFO all overlap through 2027. Cost cuts that reach customer service could make the subscriber losses worse rather than better.
Industry Commentary
Cable broadband vs. fiber and fixed wireless
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For cable, broadband used to be a near-monopoly product with steady price increases. The numbers now show the opposite. Legacy Charter's customers as a share of the homes and businesses its network passes fell from 55.7% to 53.4% in the year to June 30, 2026; in Q3 the combined company lost 233,000 internet customers on a pro forma basis, and monthly residential revenue per customer fell 1.6%. Fiber builders and fixed-wireless providers, which sell home internet over their cell networks, compete for the same homes. Charter's answer, in its Q3 release, is to rework how it sells internet and to lean harder on the mobile and video products only a cable company bundles with it. The coming quarters will show whether bundling can hold on to customers against rivals who compete on price.

Consolidation: Charter + Cox
EXECUTION TEST

Merging two cable operators is a bet on scale: spreading programming, technology and product costs over more customers. The deal closed effective August 19, 2026. Cox Enterprises took about 33.6M common units of Charter's operating partnership, exchangeable into Charter shares, 60M convertible preferred units with a $6.0B face amount (Q3 10-Q) and about $4B in cash. Charter's total principal debt, Cox's included, was $108.6B at September 30. Charter expects more than $1B of synergies from the deal, now alongside a separate $2B savings program. The deal gives Charter scale. It does not answer the competitive question in Cox's markets, which face the same fiber and wireless pressure as Charter's.

The valuation debate — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

CHTR trades at 11.4x trailing earnings, a figure inflated by the Q3 write-down, about 3x consensus FY2027 earnings and 5.8x EV/EBITDA. The bull framing: this is a leveraged cash-flow business priced as if its cash flows are about to disappear. If internet losses stabilize, the $2B savings and the Cox synergies arrive and capex falls after 2027, debt paydown and buybacks shift value back to shareholders. The bear framing: a low multiple on a business with about 4x leverage and falling EBITDA is simply what shrinking equity looks like, and the franchise write-down is the accounts catching up with the share price. Quarterly internet net adds will decide which view is right. Charter reports that number every quarter, and in the first quarter with Cox it was worse than a year earlier.
