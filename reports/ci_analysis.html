01 Company Overview

The Cigna Group is a $282 billion-revenue health services company that, unusually for the managed-care sector, earns the large majority of its money outside of insurance risk. It reports two segments: Evernorth Health Services — the pharmacy benefit manager Express Scripts, the specialty pharmacy Accredo, and a set of behavioral, home-delivery and care-services businesses — and Cigna Healthcare, the U.S. commercial employer and international health insurance operation. Full-year 2025 revenue was $274.9 billion, up 11% YoY, with Evernorth contributing $234.95 billion of adjusted revenue (+16%) against Cigna Healthcare's $47.16 billion (−11%, reflecting the divestiture of the Medicare business).

By market value Cigna is the world's #351 most valuable public company, but by revenue it is one of the largest companies in the United States — a gap that is the entire valuation story. The stock trades at P/E 11.5 trailing and 8.8 forward while the S&P 500 sits near 28x, because the market assigns a structural discount to pharmacy benefit management earnings that face open political and regulatory attack. In July 2026 Brian Evanko succeeded David Cordani as CEO, completing a transition announced in March 2026; Cordani, CEO for nearly 17 years, moved to executive chair.

Evernorth is built around Express Scripts, the largest PBM (pharmacy benefit manager) in the United States, which processed 2.22 billion equivalent prescription claims in 2025 (+4.8%) — ahead of CVS Caremark's 1.90 billion and Optum Rx's 1.659 billion. Cigna reported 123.6 million pharmacy customers at year-end 2025 (+4%), though that fell to 118.2 million by Q2 2026 on client transitions. Economics are thin-margin and volume-driven: Evernorth generated $7.221 billion of pre-tax adjusted income on $234.95 billion of revenue in FY2025 — roughly a 3.1% pre-tax margin — with income up only 3% against 16% revenue growth, the clearest evidence that PBM revenue growth is not translating into profit growth.

The segment splits into two very different engines. Pharmacy Benefit Services — traditional claims administration and rebate negotiation — delivered $609 million of pre-tax adjusted earnings in Q2 2026, down year over year. Specialty & Care Services — Accredo specialty pharmacy, biosimilar and generic substitution, behavioral health and care delivery — delivered $1.1 billion, up 22%, helped by generic Revlimid penetration exceeding 80%. The mix shift is the strategic point: the part of Evernorth under political attack is shrinking in profit terms, and the part that dispenses and manages complex drugs is compounding.

In October 2025 Evernorth announced a rebate-free "Signature" pharmacy benefit model that replaces post-purchase manufacturer rebates with upfront negotiated discounts, guaranteeing members the lowest of the negotiated rate, copay, cash price or direct-to-consumer price. Management projects roughly 30% lower monthly brand-drug cost for high-deductible members and targets approximately 4% margins on the model. It launches for Cigna Healthcare fully insured plans in 2027 and becomes the standard for all Evernorth clients in 2028 — deliberately ahead of Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements that bite in 2028. On the Q2 2026 call management reported "significant early interest from health plans and employers."

Revenue Mix
Cigna Healthcare is a U.S. commercial employer and international health insurance business — and, critically, almost nothing else. On March 19, 2025 Cigna completed the sale of its Medicare Advantage, standalone Medicare Part D and related businesses to Health Care Services Corporation (HCSC). That divestiture is why segment revenue fell 11% in FY2025 and why total medical customers declined 5% to 18.1 million — and it is also the single best thing that happened to Cigna's risk profile. The 2024–2026 Medicare Advantage cost surge that battered Humana, UnitedHealth and CVS/Aetna simply does not flow through Cigna's income statement. Membership has since resumed growing: 18.4 million medical customers as of Q2 2026, with U.S. employer membership up year to date and stop-loss tracking well.

The key insurer metric here is the MCR (medical care ratio), the share of premium paid out in claims. FY2025 MCR was 84.4%, up from 83.2% in 2024, and Q4 2025 spiked to 88.0%. Q2 2026 came in at 84.5% — again above the 83.2% of Q2 2025, but management described it as "slightly favorable to expectations," with outpatient surgical spend lower than assumed and cost trend "elevated but stable, high single digits." Segment pre-tax adjusted income was $4.153 billion in FY2025 (−2%), and $1.3 billion in Q2 2026 (+17% YoY) — evidence that 2026 pricing caught up to trend.

Cigna is further narrowing the risk pool: it will exit the Individual and Family Plans (ACA exchange) business effective January 1, 2027. Combined with the Medicare exit, this leaves a business concentrated on employer group insurance and administrative-services-only arrangements, where the company bears less claims risk and earns fees. FY2026 guidance calls for Cigna Healthcare pre-tax income of at least $4.55 billion, raised from $4.5 billion at the start of the year.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
#1 PBM SCALE (2.22B CLAIMS) ACCREDO SPECIALTY PHARMACY 188.4M CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIPS EMPLOYER SWITCHING COSTS BIOSIMILAR/GENERIC SOURCING NO MEDICARE ADVANTAGE EXPOSURE STOP-LOSS UNDERWRITING DATA

Cigna's moat is purchasing scale converted into negotiating leverage. With 2.22 billion equivalent prescription claims flowing through Express Scripts — more than any other PBM, and roughly 31% of a market where the top three control 80% — Cigna extracts drug pricing that a standalone employer or regional plan cannot replicate. That scale is self-reinforcing: many smaller PBMs outsource claims adjudication and fulfillment to one of the Big Three, and roughly half of Prime Therapeutics' network spend runs through Express Scripts, so Cigna earns economics even on volume it does not nominally own. Switching a 50,000-life employer's pharmacy benefit is a multi-quarter, disruption-heavy project, which is why PBM contracts are sticky and why management could say on the Q2 2026 call that new 2027 business wins exceed the prior two years combined.

The second, less-discussed moat is Accredo and the specialty/biosimilar franchise, where Cigna does the physical work of dispensing and managing complex, high-cost therapies. This is where the profit growth actually is: Specialty & Care Services pre-tax earnings rose 22% to $1.1 billion in Q2 2026, and generic Revlimid penetration above 80% shows Cigna's ability to steer volume to cheaper equivalents — a capability that is more valuable, not less, if rebates disappear. The third element is the negative moat that turned into a positive: by selling Medicare Advantage to HCSC in March 2025, Cigna removed the one line of business where government reimbursement rates, risk-adjustment audits and senior utilization have destroyed peer earnings since 2024. Cigna's Beta of 0.32 is a partial statistical reflection of that de-risked, fee-heavy mix.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. PBM business-model risk — the central debate. Roughly 83% of Cigna's revenue and 63% of FY2025 segment pre-tax income comes from a business whose core economic mechanism — retaining a spread on manufacturer rebates — is the explicit target of federal and state legislative activity and of the Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements effective 2028. Cigna's own answer is to pre-empt it: the Signature rebate-free model targets roughly 4% margins on a transparent fee basis. But that is a deliberate admission that the old economics are ending, and 4% on a fee stream is a different, smaller, and as-yet-unproven business than the one that generated $7.2 billion of Evernorth pre-tax income. Pharmacy Benefit Services pre-tax earnings already declined to $609 million in Q2 2026.

2. Medical cost trend inflation in the remaining risk book. The MCR moved from 83.2% (FY2024) to 84.4% (FY2025) to 84.5% (Q2 2026), with Q4 2025 at 88.0%. Management calls trend "elevated but stable, high single digits." Cigna Healthcare earns roughly $4.5 billion of pre-tax income on ~$47 billion of revenue; every additional point of MCR is worth hundreds of millions. Because Cigna is now a commercial-only insurer, it cannot offset a bad employer year with Medicare — the concentration cuts both ways.

3. GLP-1 drug costs flow through both segments — with opposite signs. On the Evernorth side, GLP-1 volume is revenue and, through Accredo, profit. On the Cigna Healthcare side, GLP-1 coverage is claims cost inside the MCR. Q2 2026 commentary was that "GLP-1 prescription growth moderated" and utilization growth "slowed from elevated levels," with weight-loss coverage declining slightly — a "modest headwind offset by specialty strength." That is a benign outcome this quarter, but the two-sided exposure means Cigna has limited ability to hedge a step-change in GLP-1 pricing or utilization.

4. Earnings quality and one-off noise. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $22.18 against adjusted EPS of $29.84 — a $7.66 gap. An enterprise-wide "strategic optimization program" running through 2028 had already absorbed $450 million of pre-tax cost by June 30, 2026. In 2024, a non-cash after-tax investment loss of $2.7 billion ($9.53 per share) plus a $182 million dividend-receivable impairment cut GAAP earnings roughly in half. Persistent adjustments of this size make the headline GAAP P/E less informative and require investors to trust management's definition of "core."

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

One-off items at Cigna are large, recurring and material to how the headline P/E should be read. FY2025 after-tax special items were: a strategic optimization program charge of $565 million, integration and transaction-related costs of $247 million, a net loss on sale of businesses of $404 million (principally the HCSC Medicare transaction), partly offset by a $13 million benefit from litigation. Together these are the bulk of the $7.66 per-share gap between FY2025 GAAP EPS of $22.18 and adjusted EPS of $29.84.

FY2024 was distorted far more severely by a non-cash after-tax investment loss of $2.7 billion ($9.53 per share) — the write-down of Cigna's equity stake in a value-based primary care investment — plus a $182 million ($0.49 per share) impairment of a dividend receivable. Those two items are why FY2024 GAAP EPS collapsed to $12.12 and why FY2025's 83% GAAP EPS "growth" is an artifact of the prior-year base rather than genuine operating improvement. Investors should compare adjusted EPS ($29.84 FY2025) year over year, not GAAP.

The strategic optimization program is ongoing and expected to continue through 2028, with $450 million of pre-tax cost recorded through June 30, 2026. Charges this persistent sit uncomfortably in the "non-recurring" bucket — a restructuring running four calendar years is closer to a cost of doing business. Assume further quarterly adjustments and treat the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge as a permanent feature of the reporting.

RSI measures how much of recent price movement has been up versus down, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 suggests the stock has run hot and may be due for a pause; below 30 suggests it has been beaten down and may be due for a bounce. Neither is a signal on its own.

Five-year trajectory. From a September 2021 close of $200.16 to $277.45 on August 19, 2026, CI returned +38.6% on price alone — roughly a 6.8% compound annual price return before dividends. That headline masks a violent round trip. The stock nearly doubled from the September 2021 low to a March 2024 peak of $363.19, then gave back a third of its value to an October 2025 trough of $244.41, and has spent the ten months since chopping in a $265–$295 band. An investor who bought at the 2024 high is down 24% two and a half years later; an investor who bought the October 2025 low is up 14%. The path, not the endpoint, is the story.

The technical picture is neutral-to-heavy. At $277.45 the stock sits below its 10-month (200-day equivalent) moving average and essentially on top of its 3-month average — a market with no trend. Six-period monthly RSI has oscillated in a compressed 35–65 range through 2026 without reaching either threshold, the signature of a stock that is being accumulated by value buyers and distributed by growth holders in roughly equal measure. There is no oversold washout to buy and no overbought extension to fade.

Relative performance. This is where the analysis gets uncomfortable for bulls. Year to date CI has returned +2.4% and over one year +3.6%. Over the same one-year window the SPDR S&P 500 ETF returned +20.9% — CI underperformed the index by roughly 17 percentage points. Against its own sector the gap is worse: year to date Humana +52.1%, UnitedHealth +25.0%, CVS Health +23.0%, Elevance +14.3%, versus CI at +2.4%. Cigna is last among the five large managed-care names.

The irony is precise: Cigna sold the Medicare Advantage business that damaged its peers, and its peers have massively outperformed it while recovering from that damage. The market spent 2026 paying up for MA recovery stories and refusing to pay for the company that avoided the problem — because Cigna's remaining earnings base is the one under regulatory attack. Investors buying CI here are explicitly betting that the PBM discount is too large, not that the operations are better. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

CI generates $9.1 billion of free cash flow — 12.4% of its $73.3 billion market cap — at a 13.4% ROIC and 16.8% ROE, yet trades at 8.75x forward earnings against 14.7x for peers and 28x for the S&P 500. FY2026 guidance was raised to at least $30.45 adjusted EPS, 2027 new business wins exceed the prior two years combined, and Specialty & Care Services earnings grew 22% in Q2 2026. Management is not waiting to be regulated: the Signature rebate-free model launches in 2027, two years ahead of the 2028 statutory deadline, and every analyst target on the street — including the lowest at $290 — sits above the current price.

Bear Case

The multiple is low because the earnings may not be durable. Pharmacy Benefit Services pre-tax earnings already declined to $609 million in Q2 2026, and Evernorth's FY2025 pre-tax income grew just 3% on 16% revenue growth. The Signature model explicitly targets ~4% margins on a transparent fee — an admission that rebate-spread economics are ending, with no proof yet that the replacement earns as much. Meanwhile MCR has risen every comparable period (83.2% → 84.4% → 84.5%), the commercial-only book has no diversification left after exiting Medicare and, from January 2027, the exchanges, and H1 2026 buybacks collapsed to $250 million from $3.6 billion in FY2025 against a 42.8% debt-to-capitalization ratio. Two analysts downgraded within five days of a beat.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
PBM regulatory and legislative action. Federal and state pressure on rebate retention, spread pricing and PBM-payer vertical integration targets the mechanism behind ~83% of Cigna's revenue and the majority of its profit. Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements take effect in 2028. If legislation goes further than transparency — to mandatory rebate pass-through, delinking of PBM compensation from list price, or forced divestiture of PBM from insurer — Cigna's earnings base could be permanently reset, and it has less non-PBM diversification than CVS or UnitedHealth to absorb the hit.
02
Commercial medical cost trend outrunning pricing. MCR at 84.5% in Q2 2026 is 1.3 points above the year-ago 83.2%, and Q4 2025 printed 88.0%. Management describes trend as "elevated but stable, high single digits" and has declined to guide 2027. Because Cigna is now a commercial-only insurer with no Medicare or exchange offset, a single mispriced January renewal cycle would hit the full ~$47 billion premium base with no cushion.
03
Transition execution on the Signature model — and the capital-return slowdown. Replacing rebate economics with a ~4% fee margin across a 118-million-member pharmacy book, in 2027 for fully insured and 2028 for everyone, is a business-model transplant with no precedent at this scale. If clients accept the transparency but not the fee, or if competitors undercut on price to take share during the changeover, Evernorth margins compress permanently. The 42.8% debt-to-capitalization ratio and the drop in H1 2026 buybacks to $250 million from a $3.6 billion FY2025 pace reduce management's ability to support the share price through the transition.
  Industry Commentary
PBM Regulatory & Political Risk
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The dominant overhang, and the reason a company earning 13.4% ROIC with $9.1B of free cash flow trades at 8.75x forward earnings. Three PBMs — Express Scripts (~31% of claims), CVS Caremark (~26%) and Optum Rx (~23%) — control 80% of U.S. equivalent prescription claims, and all three are owned by, or own, a major insurer. That concentration is the target. Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements land in 2028. Cigna's response — the rebate-free Signature model announced October 2025, launching for fully insured members in 2027 and universally in 2028 at a ~4% target margin — is a pre-emptive capitulation on the old economics. The market has not yet decided whether that is leadership or the first markdown of a larger repricing. Evidence for the bear reading is already visible: Pharmacy Benefit Services pre-tax earnings fell to $609 million in Q2 2026.
Medical Cost Trend Inflation
ONGOING HEADWIND
Utilization has run above pre-2023 baselines across the industry, and Cigna's MCR tracks it: 83.2% (FY2024) → 84.4% (FY2025) → 84.5% (Q2 2026), with a 88.0% Q4 2025 spike. Management's language — "elevated but stable, high single digits" — signals a trend that has plateaued at a higher level rather than reverting. Cigna's specific position is unusual: by selling Medicare Advantage to HCSC in March 2025 it removed the segment where cost trend did the most damage to Humana, UnitedHealth and CVS/Aetna, and its Q2 2026 Cigna Healthcare pre-tax earnings grew 17% while peers were repairing MA margins. The trade-off is concentration — from January 2027, with the exchange exit complete, Cigna's entire underwriting risk sits in one employer-group book.
GLP-1 Spending & Specialty Drug Economics
EMERGING TAILWIND
Bull framing: GLP-1 and specialty drugs flow through Evernorth as revenue and, via Accredo, as high-value dispensing and management fees — and they are exactly the therapies where a PBM's clinical management is hardest to disintermediate. Specialty & Care Services pre-tax earnings grew 22% to $1.1 billion in Q2 2026, with generic Revlimid penetration above 80% demonstrating Cigna's ability to convert branded spend into cheaper equivalents while keeping the margin. Management reported GLP-1 prescription growth "moderated" and utilization growth "slowed from elevated levels" — the benign outcome.

Bear framing: the same drugs are claims cost inside Cigna Healthcare's MCR, and weight-loss coverage is already being trimmed. The specialty growth that offsets PBM decline is itself a function of drug-price inflation the political system is trying to end. If net drug prices fall materially, Evernorth's pass-through revenue base shrinks alongside the rebate pool — the two halves of the bull case are less independent than they appear. The resolution point for both debates is the same: 2028.
