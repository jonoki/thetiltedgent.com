01 Company Overview

The Cigna Group is a $286 billion-revenue (last twelve months) health services company that, unusually for the managed-care sector, earns the large majority of its money outside of insurance risk. It reports two segments: Evernorth Health Services — the pharmacy benefit manager Express Scripts, the specialty pharmacy Accredo, and a set of behavioral, home-delivery and care-services businesses — and Cigna Healthcare, the U.S. commercial employer and international health insurance operation. Full-year 2025 revenue was $274.9 billion, up 11% YoY, with Evernorth contributing $234.95 billion of adjusted revenue (+16%) against Cigna Healthcare's $47.16 billion (−11%, reflecting the divestiture of the Medicare business).

By market value Cigna ranks #162 in the S&P 500, but by revenue it is one of the largest companies in the United States — a gap that is the entire valuation story. The stock trades at P/E 13.0 trailing and 9.0 forward while the S&P 500 sits near 24x, because investors pay less for pharmacy benefit management earnings that face open political and regulatory attack. In July 2026 Brian Evanko succeeded David Cordani as CEO, completing a transition announced in March 2026; Cordani, CEO for nearly 17 years, moved to executive chair. At its Investor Day on September 30, 2026, management set a long-term target of 10% to 14% a year growth in adjusted earnings per share through 2030, and on November 5 it raised its 2026 outlook for the third time this year.

Evernorth is built around Express Scripts, the largest PBM (pharmacy benefit manager) in the United States, which processed 2.22 billion equivalent prescription claims in 2025 (+4.8%) — ahead of CVS Caremark's 1.90 billion and Optum Rx's 1.659 billion. Cigna reported 123.6 million pharmacy customers at year-end 2025 (+4%), though that fell to 117.7 million by September 30, 2026 on client transitions. Economics are thin-margin and volume-driven: Evernorth generated $7.221 billion of pre-tax adjusted income on $234.95 billion of revenue in FY2025 — roughly a 3.1% pre-tax margin — with income up only 3% against 16% revenue growth, the clearest evidence that PBM revenue growth is not translating into profit growth.

The segment splits into two very different engines. Pharmacy Benefit Services — traditional claims administration and rebate negotiation — delivered $765 million of pre-tax adjusted earnings in Q3 2026, down 22% on revenue up 9% (Healthcare Dive reports the company is spending to roll out its new pharmacy benefit model). Specialty & Care Services — Accredo specialty pharmacy, biosimilar and generic substitution, behavioral health and care delivery — delivered $1.06 billion in Q3 2026, up 15% on revenue up 1%, driven by adoption of biosimilars and specialty generics (lower-cost copies of complex drugs); CEO Brian Evanko said on the Q3 call that the penetration rate for Revlimid, a widely used blood-cancer drug, is now around 90% (Healthcare Dive), meaning the share moved to cheaper generic versions. The mix shift is the strategic point: the part of Evernorth under political attack is shrinking in profit terms, and the part that dispenses and manages complex drugs is growing.

In October 2025 Evernorth announced a rebate-free "Signature" pharmacy benefit model that replaces post-purchase manufacturer rebates with upfront negotiated discounts, guaranteeing members the lowest of the negotiated rate, copay, cash price or direct-to-consumer price. Management projects roughly 30% lower monthly brand-drug cost for high-deductible members and targets approximately 4% margins on the model. It launches for Cigna Healthcare fully insured plans in 2027 and becomes the standard for all Evernorth clients in 2028 — deliberately ahead of Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements that bite in 2028. On the Q2 2026 call management reported "significant early interest from health plans and employers."

Cigna Healthcare is a U.S. commercial employer and international health insurance business — and, critically, almost nothing else. On March 19, 2025 Cigna completed the sale of its Medicare Advantage, standalone Medicare Part D and related businesses to Health Care Services Corporation (HCSC). That divestiture is why segment revenue fell 11% in FY2025 and why total medical customers declined 5% to 18.1 million — and it is also the single best thing that happened to Cigna's risk profile. The 2024–2026 Medicare Advantage cost surge that battered Humana, UnitedHealth and CVS/Aetna simply does not flow through Cigna's income statement. Membership has since resumed growing: 18.5 million medical customers as of September 30, 2026 (18.4 million at Q2), with U.S. employer membership up year to date and early indicators on stop-loss (insurance that protects self-funded employers from very large claims) tracking well (Q2 call).

The key insurer metric here is the MCR (medical care ratio), the share of premium paid out in claims. FY2025 MCR was 84.4%, up from 83.2% in 2024, and Q4 2025 spiked to 88.0%. Q3 2026 came in at 85.0% (84.8% a year earlier). Q2 2026 was 84.5% — above the 83.2% of Q2 2025, but management described it as "slightly favorable to expectations," with outpatient surgical spend lower than assumed and cost trends "elevated but stable," running in the high single digits. Segment pre-tax adjusted income was $4.153 billion in FY2025 (−2%), and $1.3 billion in Q2 2026 (+17% YoY) and $1.193 billion in Q3 2026 (+15%) — evidence that 2026 pricing caught up to trend.

Cigna is further narrowing the risk pool: it will exit the Individual and Family Plans (ACA exchange) business effective January 1, 2027. Combined with the Medicare exit, this leaves a business concentrated on employer group insurance and administrative-services-only arrangements, where the company bears less claims risk and earns fees. FY2026 guidance calls for Cigna Healthcare pre-tax income of at least $4.6 billion, raised on November 5 from $4.55 billion (and from $4.5 billion at the start of the year).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
#1 PBM SCALE (2.22B CLAIMS) ACCREDO SPECIALTY PHARMACY 177.6M CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIPS EMPLOYER SWITCHING COSTS BIOSIMILAR/GENERIC SOURCING NO MEDICARE ADVANTAGE EXPOSURE STOP-LOSS UNDERWRITING DATA

Cigna's moat is purchasing scale converted into negotiating leverage. With 2.22 billion equivalent prescription claims flowing through Express Scripts — more than any other PBM, and roughly 31% of a market where the top three control 80% — Cigna extracts drug pricing that a standalone employer or regional plan cannot replicate. That scale is self-reinforcing: many smaller PBMs outsource claims adjudication and fulfillment to one of the Big Three, and roughly half of Prime Therapeutics' network spend runs through Express Scripts, so Cigna earns economics even on volume it does not nominally own. Switching a 50,000-life employer's pharmacy benefit is a multi-quarter, disruption-heavy project, which is why PBM contracts are sticky and why management could say on the Q2 2026 call that new 2027 pharmacy benefit business already secured is above the prior two selling seasons combined.

The second, less-discussed moat is Accredo and the specialty/biosimilar franchise, where Cigna does the physical work of dispensing and managing complex, high-cost therapies. This is where the profit growth actually is: Specialty & Care Services pre-tax earnings rose 15% to $1.06 billion in Q3 2026 (22% to $1.1 billion in Q2), and a Revlimid penetration rate of around 90% (Q3 call, via Healthcare Dive) shows Cigna's ability to steer volume to cheaper equivalents — a capability that is more valuable, not less, if rebates disappear. The third element is the negative moat that turned into a positive: by selling Medicare Advantage to HCSC in March 2025, Cigna removed the one line of business where government reimbursement rates, risk-adjustment audits and senior utilization have destroyed peer earnings since 2024. Cigna's Beta of 0.25 partly reflects that lower-risk, fee-heavy mix.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. PBM business-model risk — the central debate. Roughly 83% of Cigna's revenue and 63% of FY2025 segment pre-tax income comes from a business whose core economic mechanism — retaining a spread on manufacturer rebates — is the explicit target of federal and state legislative activity and of the Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements effective 2028. Cigna's own answer is to pre-empt it: the Signature rebate-free model targets roughly 4% margins on a transparent fee basis. On the Q2 2026 call management said it expects those margins to be similar to what its legacy pharmacy benefit products delivered and that it will spend at similar levels on the transition in 2027 as in 2026, but the model is unproven at this scale. Pharmacy Benefit Services pre-tax earnings already fell 22% to $765 million in Q3 2026.

2. Medical cost trend inflation in the remaining risk book. The MCR moved from 83.2% (FY2024) to 84.4% (FY2025) to 84.5% (Q2 2026) and 85.0% (Q3 2026, against 84.8% a year earlier), with Q4 2025 at 88.0%. Management calls cost trends "elevated but stable," running in the high single digits. Cigna Healthcare expects at least $4.6 billion of pre-tax income in 2026, on revenue of ~$47 billion in FY2025; every additional point of MCR is worth hundreds of millions. Because Cigna is now a commercial-only insurer, it cannot offset a bad employer year with Medicare — the concentration cuts both ways.

3. GLP-1 drug costs flow through both segments — with opposite signs. On the Evernorth side, GLP-1 volume is revenue and, through Accredo, profit. On the Cigna Healthcare side, GLP-1 coverage is claims cost inside the MCR. On the Q2 2026 call CFO Ann Dennison described "moderating GLP-1 growth as coverage levels slightly declined and utilization growth lowered from elevated levels," and CEO Brian Evanko called the slight fall in employers covering weight-loss GLP-1s "a modest headwind to our 2026 results," more than offset by strength in Specialty. That was a benign outcome in Q2, but the two-sided exposure means Cigna has limited ability to hedge a step-change in GLP-1 pricing or utilization. For 2027, Healthcare Dive reports that management flagged shrinking employer coverage of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs as one headwind for Express Scripts.

4. Earnings quality and one-off noise. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $22.18 against adjusted EPS of $29.84 — a $7.66 gap. An enterprise-wide "strategic optimization program" running through 2028 cost $535 million pre-tax in the first nine months of 2026 ($85 million of it in Q3), after a charge in FY2025. In 2024, a non-cash after-tax investment loss of $2.7 billion ($9.53 per share) plus a $182 million dividend-receivable impairment cut GAAP earnings roughly in half. Persistent adjustments of this size make the headline GAAP P/E less informative and require investors to trust management's definition of "core."

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

One-off items at Cigna are large, recurring and material to how the headline P/E should be read. FY2025 after-tax special items were: a strategic optimization program charge of $565 million, integration and transaction-related costs of $247 million, a net loss on sale of businesses of $404 million (principally the HCSC Medicare transaction), partly offset by a $13 million benefit from litigation. Together these are the bulk of the $7.66 per-share gap between FY2025 GAAP EPS of $22.18 and adjusted EPS of $29.84.

FY2024 was distorted far more severely by a non-cash after-tax investment loss of $2.7 billion ($9.53 per share) — the write-down of Cigna's equity stake in a value-based primary care investment — plus a $182 million ($0.49 per share) impairment of a dividend receivable. Those two items are why FY2024 GAAP EPS collapsed to $12.12 and why FY2025's 83% GAAP EPS "growth" is an artifact of the prior-year base rather than genuine operating improvement. Investors should compare adjusted EPS ($29.84 FY2025) year over year, not GAAP.

The strategic optimization program is ongoing and expected to continue through 2028, with $535 million of pre-tax cost recorded in the first nine months of 2026 ($85 million of it in Q3). Charges this persistent sit uncomfortably in the "non-recurring" bucket — a restructuring running four calendar years is closer to a cost of doing business. Assume further quarterly adjustments and treat the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge as a permanent feature of the reporting.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

CI generates $8.3 billion of free cash flow — 10.7% of its $77.4 billion market cap — at a 12.8% ROIC and 15.7% ROE, yet trades at 9.0x forward earnings against 14.8x for peers and 18.9x for the S&P 500. FY2026 guidance was raised on November 5 to at least $30.60 adjusted EPS (the third raise this year), management said on the Q2 call that new 2027 pharmacy benefit business already secured is above the prior two selling seasons combined, and Specialty & Care Services earnings grew 15% in Q3 2026. Management is not waiting to be regulated: the Signature rebate-free model launches in 2027, ahead of the statutory deadline in mid-2028, and the average analyst target of $342.96 sits 16.8% above the current price.

Bear Case

The share price is low relative to earnings because those earnings may not last. Pharmacy Benefit Services pre-tax earnings fell 22% to $765 million in Q3 2026, and Evernorth's FY2025 pre-tax income grew just 3% on 16% revenue growth. The Signature model replaces rebates with a transparent fee; management expects margins around 4%, similar to its legacy products, but there is no proof yet that it will earn as much at scale. Meanwhile MCR is higher than a year earlier in both of the last two quarters (84.5% vs 83.2% in Q2; 85.0% vs 84.8% in Q3), the commercial-only book has no diversification left after exiting Medicare and, from January 2027, the exchanges, and 2026 buybacks through November 4 were only about $500 million, against $3.6 billion in FY2025, with debt at 41.8% of capital. Evercore ISI rates the stock Hold with a $290 target, below the current price.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
PBM regulatory and legislative action. Federal and state pressure on rebate retention, spread pricing and PBM-payer vertical integration targets the mechanism behind ~83% of Cigna's revenue and the majority of its profit. Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements take effect in 2028. If legislation goes further than transparency — to mandatory rebate pass-through, delinking of PBM compensation from list price, or forced divestiture of PBM from insurer — Cigna's earnings base could be permanently reset, and it has less non-PBM diversification than CVS or UnitedHealth to absorb the hit.
02
Commercial medical cost trend outrunning pricing. MCR at 85.0% in Q3 2026 is 0.2 points above the year-ago 84.8% (Q2 2026: 84.5%, 1.3 points above the year-ago 83.2%), and Q4 2025 printed 88.0%. Management described cost trends on the Q2 call as "elevated but stable," in the high single digits, and has not yet given formal 2027 guidance. Because Cigna is now a commercial-only insurer with no Medicare or exchange offset, one January renewal season priced too low would hit a segment with ~$47 billion of annual revenue, with no cushion.
03
Transition execution on the Signature model — and the capital-return slowdown. Replacing rebate economics with a ~4% fee margin across a 118-million-member pharmacy book, in 2027 for fully insured and 2028 for everyone, is a business-model transplant with no precedent at this scale. If clients accept the transparency but not the fee, or if competitors undercut on price to take share during the changeover, Evernorth's margins shrink for good. The 41.8% debt-to-capitalization ratio and the drop in 2026 buybacks to about $500 million (through November 4) from a $3.6 billion FY2025 pace reduce management's ability to support the share price through the transition.
  Industry Commentary
PBM Regulatory & Political Risk
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The main worry, and the reason a company earning 12.8% ROIC with $8.3B of free cash flow trades at 9.0x forward earnings. Three PBMs — Express Scripts (~31% of claims), CVS Caremark (~26%) and Optum Rx (~23%) — control 80% of U.S. equivalent prescription claims, and all three are owned by, or own, a major insurer. That concentration is the target. Consolidated Appropriations Act transparency requirements land in 2028, and a sweeping Department of Labor rule finalized in late October adds more; on the Q3 call CEO Brian Evanko said it is "very unlikely that this rule will result in lower prescription drug spending" and that Cigna will comply (Healthcare Dive, November 5). Cigna's response — the rebate-free Signature model announced October 2025, launching for fully insured members in 2027 and universally in 2028 at a ~4% target margin — gives up the old economics before regulators force it to. The market has not yet decided whether that is leadership or the first step down to lower profits. Evidence for the bear reading is already visible: Pharmacy Benefit Services pre-tax earnings fell 22% to $765 million in Q3 2026.
Medical Cost Trend Inflation
ONGOING HEADWIND
Utilization has run above pre-2023 baselines across the industry, and Cigna's MCR tracks it: 83.2% (FY2024) → 84.4% (FY2025) → 84.5% (Q2 2026) → 85.0% (Q3 2026), with an 88.0% Q4 2025 spike. Management's language on the Q2 call — "elevated but stable," in the high single digits — signals a trend that has plateaued at a higher level rather than reverting. Cigna's specific position is unusual: by selling Medicare Advantage to HCSC in March 2025 it removed the segment where cost trend did the most damage to Humana, UnitedHealth and CVS/Aetna, and its Q3 2026 Cigna Healthcare pre-tax earnings grew 15% (17% in Q2) while peers were repairing MA margins. The trade-off is concentration — from January 2027, with the exchange exit complete, Cigna's entire underwriting risk sits in one employer-group book.
GLP-1 Spending & Specialty Drug Economics
EMERGING TAILWIND
Bull framing: GLP-1 and specialty drugs flow through Evernorth as revenue and, via Accredo, as high-value dispensing and management fees — and they are exactly the therapies where a PBM's clinical management is hardest to replace. Specialty & Care Services pre-tax earnings grew 15% to $1.06 billion in Q3 2026 (22% in Q2), with a Revlimid penetration rate around 90% (Q3 call, via Healthcare Dive) demonstrating Cigna's ability to convert branded spend into cheaper equivalents while keeping the margin. On the Q2 call management described "moderating GLP-1 growth" with utilization growth "lowered from elevated levels" — the benign outcome.

Bear framing: the same drugs are claims cost inside Cigna Healthcare's MCR, and weight-loss coverage is already being trimmed. The specialty growth that offsets PBM decline is itself a function of drug-price inflation the political system is trying to end. If net drug prices fall materially, Evernorth's pass-through revenue base shrinks alongside the rebate pool — the two halves of the bull case are less independent than they appear. The resolution point for both debates is the same: 2028.
