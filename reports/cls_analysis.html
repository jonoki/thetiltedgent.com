01 Company Overview

Celestica is a contract manufacturer turned AI-infrastructure supplier: it designs and builds the network switches, servers and storage hardware that hyperscale cloud operators put in their data centres, alongside a slower industrial, aerospace/defence and healthcare manufacturing business. It ranks #33 among Canadian companies by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap country ranking, at the October 28, 2026 close). Its home index is the S&P/TSX Composite; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500 because the priced line is the NYSE one.

The "AI picks and shovels" label checks out against the filings, and the numbers are more extreme than the phrase suggests. In Q3 2026 (quarter ended September 30, 2026) revenue rose 83% to $5,829.7M; the CCS segment grew 102% to $4,872.3M and is now 84% of revenue, up from 76% a year earlier. FY2025 revenue was $12.39B (+28.5%) with diluted EPS of $7.16; trailing-twelve-month revenue is $18.23B (+61.6%). With the Q3 report management raised its FY2026 outlook to $21.0B of revenue and $11.55 adjusted EPS (from $20.5B and $11.30) and gave a first 2027 outlook of $40.0B of revenue (+90%) and $21.50 adjusted EPS. "Celestica's strong momentum continued in the third quarter," CEO Rob Mionis said in the release, adding that "the demand environment continues to be very robust"; the company says its customers' demand forecasts for 2028 are already well above the 2027 outlook.

The price of that growth is concentration. Three CCS customers were 66% of Q3 2026 revenue (35%, 17% and 14%), and the top ten were 79% of FY2025 revenue, up from 64% in 2023. This is a poker table with three players who own most of the chips — the question is not whether Celestica plays well, it is whether those three keep buying in.

The growth is being paid for partly with new shares. In August Celestica sold 11.1 million new shares at $310 each (about $3.45B gross, $3,391.3M net; offering completed August 7, 2026), which lifted cash to $3.96B at September 30 and the share count to 126.1M, about 9.6% more shares than a year earlier (115.0M). The money arrives as the company plans $1.5–2.0B of capital spending in 2027 and cut its 2026 free-cash-flow outlook for what it calls targeted working-capital investments to support 2027's growth.

Celestica files 10-K / 10-Q under U.S. GAAP, and its NYSE line has a full five-year monthly history — the chart in Section 07 uses 60 month-end closes plus the October 28, 2026 close.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Own-design switch platforms (HPS) Qualified on 800G / 1.6T programs Hyperscaler engineering integration Component supply secured into 2027 Asian manufacturing footprint ROIC 42%

The moat is design-in. A hyperscaler that qualifies a Celestica-designed switch for a network generation does not swap the supplier mid-generation — the requalification cost, the firmware work and the deployment risk are too high. That is why the ten active 1.6T programs management cited on the Q2 call matter more than any single quarter: each is a multi-year revenue stream once it ramps. Management also said on that call that it had secured component supply for the rest of 2026 and for 2027 (as reported by The Motley Fool Canada) — in a market short of parts, having them is itself a competitive position. The company says customers' 2028 demand forecasts are well above its $40.0B 2027 outlook, and TheStreet's report of the October 27 call says those forecasts are based on programs Celestica has already won. The vulnerability is that design-in protects Celestica within a generation, not across them: at every generation change the customer can put the business out to bid again, bring design in-house, or go direct to an Asian ODM.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Three customers are 66% of revenue. Losing the largest customer's business (35% of Q3 2026 revenue) would take out more than a third of the top line; no amount of execution elsewhere replaces that inside a year. The customers' own capex budgets — not Celestica's competitiveness — set the ceiling.
02
Thin gross margins. Adjusted gross margin was 11.2% in Q3 2026, down from 11.7% a year earlier. A buyer that is a third of your revenue has pricing leverage, and an 11–12% gross margin leaves little room if it uses it. Operating margin gains so far come from scale, not price.
03
Working capital and cash conversion. TTM free cash flow is $568.4M on $1,245.8M of net income — a 3.1% FCF margin against a 6.8% net margin. Inventory reached $4.51B at September 30, up $2.32B since December 31, 2025; Q3 capex was $274.1M, and 2027 capex is planned at $1.5–2.0B. The 2026 free-cash-flow outlook was cut to $460M for what the company calls targeted working-capital investments to support 2027's growth, and TheStreet reports management expects to use cash in 2027. Growth this fast absorbs cash; a sudden demand pause would leave the inventory on Celestica's balance sheet. The cushion is the $3.96B of cash raised largely through the August share sale.
04
Each new generation is up for bid again. The same design-in that protects a program within a generation opens it up at the next one. The 800G-to-1.6T transition is happening now; share won or lost there sets the 2027–2028 revenue base.

Notable One-Off Items

The big one is the total-return swap (TRS) — a derivative Celestica holds on its own shares, revalued through earnings every quarter. With the stock rising, it added a $0.90-per-share pre-tax gain in Q2 2026, which is most of the gap between GAAP EPS of $3.17 and adjusted EPS of $2.54 that quarter. In Q3, with the stock roughly flat from June to September, it swung to a small loss: $3.9M, or $0.03 per share pre-tax; over the first nine months the swap is still an $83.2M gain. The TTM GAAP EPS of $10.57 carries those gains, so the GAAP trailing P/E understates the multiple on core earnings; if the stock falls, the same line turns into a loss. Management's $11.55 FY2026 EPS outlook, the $21.50 2027 outlook and the consensus figures are on the adjusted basis, which excludes the TRS. The August share sale adds a second effect: per-share figures from Q3 on are spread over about 11 million more shares.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $9.82 to the October 28, 2026 settled close of $299.80, CLS rose 30.5x — a +2,953% price return (no dividends paid). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Year-over-year revenue growth has risen in each of the last seven reported quarters, from +19.9% to +82.5%, and the growth is concentrated in the higher-margin segment (CCS at 8.9% versus ATS at 6.6%), so operating margin rises mechanically — 8.5% adjusted in Q3 2026, a company high. ROIC is 42%. Celestica's own 2027 outlook is $40.0B of revenue and $21.50 of adjusted EPS, and it says customers' 2028 forecasts are higher still (based, per TheStreet's report of the call, on programs Celestica has already won); on that EPS the stock trades at 13.9x. The balance sheet now holds net cash of about $3.14B, and the stock is 26.8% below its April close despite a higher outlook. If the 2027 numbers are even roughly right, today's price is the pullback.

Bear Case

Three customers are 66% of revenue and the top ten were 79% last year — and rising. Celestica's growth is its customers' capex budgets with an 11–12% gross margin on top. Free cash flow is $568.4M TTM, a 1.5% yield, and the 2026 outlook for it was just cut to $460M to put more into working capital for 2027's growth, with inventory already at $4.51B, while capex rises to $1.5–2.0B next year. Shareholders were diluted by about 9.6% in August to pay for it. GAAP EPS is flattered by swap gains on Celestica's own stock, which reverse on the way down. Twenty-one of twenty-one StockAnalysis analysts rate it a buy, so there is almost no one left to upgrade. The January–March 2025 drawdown (−36% in two months) and the October 2026 slide (−36.5% in three weeks of closes) are what this stock does when the AI-capex story wobbles.

Top 3 Risks

01
Customer concentration. 35% / 17% / 14% of Q3 2026 revenue from three CCS customers; top ten 79% of FY2025. One hyperscaler pausing, putting its business out to bid again at the 1.6T transition or bringing design in-house would take a double-digit share of revenue out in one step.
02
AI capex cyclicality. Consensus assumes revenue growth of +69.9% in 2026 and +90.9% in 2027. Hardware suppliers sit at the end of the bullwhip: a slowdown in hyperscaler spending growth, not even a decline, shows up as a much larger swing in orders.
03
Cash conversion and inventory. A 3.1% FCF margin against a 6.8% net margin, and inventory up $2.32B in nine months to $4.51B. It is the right bet in a shortage and the wrong one in a pause.
Industry Commentary
The 800G → 1.6T networking cycle
EMERGING TAILWIND

AI clusters are bottlenecked as much by the network between accelerators as by the accelerators themselves, so every GPU generation pulls through a faster switch generation. Celestica's Networking end market (formerly Communications) — $3,263.2M in Q3 2026, 56% of revenue — is that switching business. The ten active 1.6T programs management cited on the Q2 call, ramping from the second half of 2026, are part of the bridge from the 2026 outlook ($21.0B) to the 2027 outlook ($40.0B). The picks-and-shovels framing fits: Celestica is paid per box shipped, whoever wins the model race.

Component scarcity and the balance sheet
ONGOING CONSTRAINT

On the Q2 call the CEO said demand was running ahead of global component supply. Suppliers that secured parts early win share; Celestica said on that call it had secured supply for the rest of 2026 and for 2027, and inventory reached $4.51B at September 30, up $2.32B since the end of 2025. That is why FCF ($568.4M TTM) lags net income ($1,245.8M) so badly. In a shortage, inventory is an advantage; in a slowdown, it is a write-down. The FY2026 FCF outlook, cut to $460M on $21.0B of revenue, is the number that says which one it is.

Hyperscaler buying power
STRUCTURAL RISK

The customers are few and very large, and they design much of their own infrastructure. Celestica's top-ten share went 64% → 73% → 79% from 2023 to 2025. That concentration is the source of the growth and the cap on the margin: GAAP gross margin has stayed in a 10.3–13.0% band for seven quarters (Q1 2025 to Q3 2026; 11.0% in Q3 2026) even as quarterly revenue more than doubled. The own-design HPS model is Celestica's answer — it sells engineering, not just assembly — and HPS at about 40% of revenue is the best evidence that the answer is working. The next generation change is the test of whether it holds.
