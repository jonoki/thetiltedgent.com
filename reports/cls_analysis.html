01 Company Overview

Celestica is a contract manufacturer turned AI-infrastructure supplier: it designs and builds the network switches, servers and storage hardware that hyperscale cloud operators put in their data centres, alongside a slower industrial, aerospace/defence and healthcare manufacturing business. It ranks #27 in Canada by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap country ranking) and #590 globally. Its home index is the S&P/TSX Composite; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500 because the priced line is the NYSE one.

The premise for this report — "AI picks and shovels" — checks out against the filings, and the numbers are more extreme than the phrase suggests. In Q2 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026) revenue rose 62% to $4,698.6M; the CCS segment grew 84% to $3,810.3M and is now 81% of revenue, up from 72% a year earlier. FY2025 revenue was $12.39B (+28.5%) with diluted EPS of $7.16; trailing-twelve-month revenue is $15.60B (+47%). Management's FY2026 outlook is $20.5B of revenue and $11.30 adjusted EPS, and it has signalled growth of more than 65% into 2027.

The price of that growth is concentration. Three CCS customers were 63% of Q2 2026 revenue (32%, 17% and 14%), and the top ten were 79% of FY2025 revenue, up from 64% in 2023. Celestica does not name them in the figures fetched here. This is a poker table with three players who own most of the chips — the question is not whether Celestica plays well, it is whether those three keep buying in.

Two premises from the brief for this report did not survive the filings: Celestica files 10-K / 10-Q under U.S. GAAP (not 40-F / 6-K), and its NYSE line has a full five-year monthly history (not a short one) — the chart in Section 07 uses all 60 months.

The margin mix shift, in one line of arithmetic

CCS earned an 8.7% segment margin in Q2 2026; ATS earned 6.3%. CCS's share of revenue went from 71.7% in Q2 2025 ($2,074.3M of $2,893.4M) to 81.1% in Q2 2026. Weighting the two margins by revenue: (3,810.3 × 8.7% + 888.3 × 6.3%) ÷ 4,698.6 = 8.25% — which ties to the reported 8.2% adjusted operating margin, a record. The growth is coming from the higher-margin segment, so group margin rises even if neither segment improves. The catch: gross margin (11.5% adjusted in Q2, down 20bps) is not expanding — the operating-margin gain is scale leverage on overhead, not pricing power on each box.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Own-design switch platforms (HPS) Qualified on 800G / 1.6T programs Hyperscaler engineering integration Component supply secured into 2027 Asian manufacturing footprint ROIC 42%

The moat is design-in. A hyperscaler that qualifies a Celestica-designed switch for a network generation does not swap the supplier mid-generation — the requalification cost, the firmware work and the deployment risk are too high. That is why ten active 1.6T programs matter more than any single quarter: each is a multi-year revenue stream once it ramps. Management also says it is "appropriately hedged for 2026 and 2027" on component supply through non-cancellable, non-refundable orders — in a supply-constrained market, having the parts is itself a competitive position. The vulnerability is that design-in protects Celestica within a generation, not across them: at every generation change the customer can re-tender, bring design in-house, or go direct to an Asian ODM.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Three customers are 63% of revenue. Losing a single program at the largest customer (32%) would take out roughly a third of the top line; no amount of execution elsewhere replaces that inside a year. The customers' own capex budgets — not Celestica's competitiveness — set the ceiling.
02
Thin gross margins. Adjusted gross margin was 11.5% in Q2 2026 and guided to "the mid-11s". A buyer that is a third of your revenue has pricing leverage, and an 11–12% gross margin leaves little room if it uses it. Operating margin gains so far come from scale, not price.
03
Working capital and cash conversion. TTM free cash flow is $518.7M on $1,116M of net income — a 3.3% FCF margin against a 7.2% net margin. Inventory rose $1.5B YoY and Q2 capex was $264M. Growth this fast absorbs cash; a sudden demand pause would leave the inventory on Celestica's balance sheet.
04
Generation-change re-tender risk. The same design-in that protects a program within a generation opens it up at the next one. The 800G-to-1.6T transition is happening now; share won or lost there sets the 2027–2028 revenue base.

Notable One-Off Items

The big one is the total-return swap (TRS) — a derivative Celestica holds on its own shares, marked to market through earnings. With the stock rising, it added a $0.90-per-share pre-tax gain in Q2 2026, which is most of the gap between GAAP EPS of $3.17 and adjusted EPS of $2.54. The TTM GAAP EPS of $9.62 carries these gains, so the GAAP trailing P/E understates the multiple on core earnings; if the stock falls, the same line reverses into a loss. Management's $11.30 FY2026 EPS outlook and the consensus figures are on the adjusted basis, which excludes the TRS.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a stock can stay overbought for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Year-over-year revenue growth has risen in each of the last six reported quarters, from +19.9% to +62.4%, and the growth is concentrated in the higher-margin segment (CCS at 8.7% versus ATS at 6.3%), so operating margin rises mechanically — a record 8.2% adjusted in Q2 2026. ROIC is 42%. Ten 1.6T programs are ramping into 2027, when consensus expects $35.22B of revenue and $19.59 of EPS; on that EPS the stock trades at 17.6x. Leverage is modest (net debt about $0.44B), component supply is locked through 2027, and the stock is 16% below its April close despite two outlook raises. If the 2027 numbers are even roughly right, today's price is the pullback.

Bear Case

Three customers are 63% of revenue and the top ten were 79% last year — and rising. Celestica's growth is its customers' capex budgets with an 11–12% gross margin on top. Free cash flow is $518.7M TTM, a 1.3% yield, because the growth is being funded with $1.5B of extra inventory held under non-cancellable orders. GAAP EPS is flattered by swap gains on Celestica's own stock, which reverse on the way down. Twenty-one of twenty-one analysts rate it a buy, so there is no one left to upgrade. The January–March 2025 drawdown (−36% in two months) is what this stock does when the AI-capex story wobbles.

Top 3 Risks

01
Customer concentration. 32% / 17% / 14% of Q2 2026 revenue from three CCS customers; top ten 79% of FY2025. One hyperscaler pausing, re-tendering at the 1.6T transition or insourcing design would take a double-digit share of revenue out in one step.
02
AI capex cyclicality. Consensus assumes +66% in 2026 and +71% in 2027. Hardware suppliers sit at the end of the bullwhip: a slowdown in hyperscaler spending growth, not even a decline, shows up as a much larger swing in orders.
03
Cash conversion and inventory. A 3.3% FCF margin against a 7.2% net margin, and $1.5B more inventory than a year ago, bought on non-cancellable terms. It is the right bet in a shortage and the wrong one in a pause.
Industry Commentary
The 800G → 1.6T networking cycle
EMERGING TAILWIND

AI clusters are bottlenecked as much by the network between accelerators as by the accelerators themselves, so every GPU generation pulls through a faster switch generation. Celestica's Communications end market — $2,653.2M in Q2 2026, 56% of revenue — is that switching business. Management's ten active 1.6T programs, ramping from the second half of 2026, are the bridge from the 2026 outlook ($20.5B) to the 2027 consensus ($35.22B). The picks-and-shovels framing fits: Celestica is paid per box shipped, whoever wins the model race.

Component scarcity and the balance sheet
ONGOING CONSTRAINT

"Demand outpaces global component supply" — the CEO's words on the Q2 call. Suppliers that secured parts early win share; Celestica says it is hedged for 2026 and 2027 through non-cancellable, non-refundable orders, and inventory rose $1.5B year over year. That is why FCF ($518.7M TTM) lags net income ($1,116M) so badly. In a shortage, inventory is a moat; in a slowdown, it is a write-down. The FY2026 FCF outlook of about $600M on $20.5B of revenue is the number that says which one it is.

Hyperscaler buying power
STRUCTURAL RISK

The customers are few and very large, and they design much of their own infrastructure. Celestica's top-ten share went 64% → 73% → 79% from 2023 to 2025. That concentration is the source of the growth and the cap on the margin: GAAP gross margin has stayed in a 10.3–13.0% band for six quarters even as revenue grew 77% (Q1 2025 to Q2 2026). The own-design HPS model is Celestica's answer — it sells engineering, not just assembly — and HPS reaching 41% of revenue is the best evidence that the answer is working. The next generation change is the test of whether it holds.
