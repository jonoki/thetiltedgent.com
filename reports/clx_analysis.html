01 Company Overview

Clorox is the company behind the bleach under the sink, the trash bags in the drawer, the cat litter in the garage and the charcoal by the grill — Clorox, Pine-Sol, Glad, Fresh Step, Kingsford, Brita, Burt's Bees and, since April 1, 2026, Purell. It was founded in the Bay Area in 1913 as the Electro-Alkaline Company, went public in 1928, spent just over a decade inside Procter & Gamble until the Supreme Court sided with the FTC and forced a divestment completed on January 1, 1969, and has been an S&P 500 member since March 31, 1969. Fiscal 2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) revenue was $6.72B, down 5.4% from $7.10B, with diluted EPS of $4.81 (down 26%) and adjusted EPS of $5.53 (down 28%).

This is the stock people buy so they don't have to think about it — and it has been anything but restful. From a $174.36 month-end close in December 2021 the shares have fallen to $84.88, a 51% decline, through a 2023 cyberattack that caused product shortages, a five-year digital transformation costing about $580M whose US ERP go-live distorted two fiscal years of shipments, a $2.25B cash acquisition of GOJO, and on May 28, 2026 the announcement that Chair and CEO Linda Rendle will step down for health reasons once a successor is found. The dividend — $5.00 a year at the current $1.25 quarterly rate — now yields 5.89% and exceeds GAAP earnings per share. The central question is simple to state: is Clorox a staples franchise going through a messy but temporary transition, or a brand portfolio losing ground to value-seeking shoppers? Organic sales growth in fiscal 2027 — guided to +3.5–4.5%, most of it from lapping the ERP drawdown — is the first clean read.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Household-name brand portfolio Purell in hand sanitiser Multi-channel retail distribution Advertising scale (~10% of sales) 49 years of dividend growth

The moat is trust at the shelf. People pay up for bleach they believe will kill what it says it kills and for a trash bag that won't split on the way to the curb — and retailers give space to the brands shoppers ask for. Clorox reaches them through mass retailers, grocers, warehouse clubs, dollar stores, pet and drug stores, e-commerce and a direct sales force. The weakness is that trust is paid for continuously: management plans advertising at about 10% of sales in fiscal 2027, and when volume falls the brand premium becomes the thing under test. A 5.4% fall in revenue with gross margin down 290bps is what that test looks like when it goes badly.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Volume, not price, is the problem. Q4 Household volume fell 16 points and Lifestyle 14 points. Much of that is the ERP comparison; how much is a consumer trading down is the open question. The fiscal 2027 outlook itself cites higher-than-normal inflation and negative mix as the reasons gross margin stays near 42%.
02
A thin balance sheet under a fat dividend. Shareholders' equity is so small that debt-to-equity reads 22.2x and ROE 164% (StockAnalysis). Total debt is $5.60B against $143M of cash, the current ratio is 0.66, and long-term debt rose to $3.98B from $2.48B to fund GOJO and the Glad buyout. The $5.00 dividend is 104% of GAAP EPS.
03
Leadership in transition. The CEO who ran the ERP programme, the GOJO deal and the cyberattack recovery is leaving; no successor or interim CEO has been named. A new chief executive inherits a low-growth business and the option to reset expectations — including the portfolio and capital allocation.
04
Operational shocks have a history here. An August 2023 cyberattack (Cybersecurity Dive) caused revenue loss and product shortages (Wikipedia), and the ERP cut-over swung two years of shipments. Each was survivable; together they have given retailers and shoppers repeated reasons to try an alternative.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported figures. First, the ERP transition: retailers bought about 3.5 points of extra volume in Q4 FY25 ahead of the cut-over and drew about 7.5 points down in FY26, cutting FY26 adjusted EPS by about 90 cents; FY27 gets more than 3.5 points back from lapping it. Second, GOJO: Q4 gross margin carried an inventory step-up, and the deal adds about 9.5 points to FY27 sales. Third, GAAP vs adjusted: FY26 diluted EPS was $4.81 against $5.53 adjusted; the FY27 guide is $5.41–$5.71 diluted against $5.70–$6.00 adjusted. The long-run EPS series is lumpy too — FY23's $1.20 and FY24's $2.25 sit far below FY22 and FY25.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Much of what went wrong in fiscal 2026 has a named, temporary cause. The ERP drawdown cost about 90 cents of adjusted EPS and reverses in fiscal 2027; the GOJO step-up was an acquisition-accounting charge; the cyberattack is three years back. Purell adds a brand with nearly $800M of sales and at least $50M of targeted synergies. At $84.88 you pay 14.7x forward earnings and collect a 5.89% yield from a company with 49 straight years of dividend increases, a 0.54 beta and a consensus target of $100.76. A new CEO gets a clean slate. If organic growth returns and gross margin holds at 42%, the stock is priced for a decline that does not come.

Bear Case

Strip out the ERP swing and Clorox is barely growing — International, the one clean segment, grew 1% organically in Q4, and fiscal 2027's organic guide is mostly a comparison effect. Household volume fell 16 points in Q4. Gross margin is guided flat at about 42% even with cost savings. The dividend is 104% of GAAP EPS and more than FY26 free cash flow, on a balance sheet with near-zero equity and $5.6B of debt after a debt-funded acquisition. Four sell ratings to one buy, an 8.9% short float and the leader leaving: a high yield on a shrinking earnings base is how dividend cuts usually start.

Top 3 Risks

01
Brand erosion to cheaper alternatives. Volume declines in Household and Lifestyle point at the brand premium. If fiscal 2027 organic growth comes in below the +3.5% bottom of the guide, the ERP explanation is spent.
02
Dividend coverage. $5.00 of dividends against $4.81 of GAAP EPS, and $405M of FY26 free cash flow against about $0.6B of dividends (our estimate). Fiscal 2027's guided $5.41–$5.71 GAAP EPS would restore coverage — just — if delivered. A miss, or a new CEO who prioritises deleveraging, puts the streak in play.
03
Leadership and integration together. A CEO search coincides with the first full year of integrating a $2.25B acquisition and with inflation the company calls higher than normal. Execution risk is concentrated in exactly the year guidance depends on it.
Industry Commentary
The value-seeking shopper vs the brand premium
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Branded household products earn their margins by persuading shoppers that the name on the bottle is worth paying for. Clorox's fiscal 2027 gross-margin outlook of about 42% reflects the opposite pressure: higher-than-normal inflation and negative mix more than offsetting cost savings. Q4 volume was down 16 points in Household and 14 in Lifestyle — largely the ERP comparison, but not provably all of it. The bill for defending the brands is advertising at about 10% of sales. The one clean read, International, grew 1% organic in Q4. None of this is unique to Clorox, but Clorox is the smallest of the peers compared here and has the least room to absorb it.

Operational noise: cyberattack, ERP, acquisition
ONGOING HEADWIND

Three years of Clorox results have been dominated by events rather than demand: the 2023 cyberattack, the roughly $580M five-year digital transformation whose US ERP go-live pulled shipments into Q4 FY25 and out of FY26, and now GOJO, which lifts reported growth by about 9.5 points in fiscal 2027. Each has an explanation; together they make the underlying trend hard to see — and the share price says investors have stopped giving the benefit of the doubt. Fiscal 2027 is the first year in several with a chance to be read cleanly.

The valuation and the yield — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $84.88 Clorox trades at 17.65x trailing GAAP earnings, 14.66x forward consensus and a 5.89% dividend yield. The bull framing: a household-name staples business with half a century of dividend growth is priced as if the brands are impaired, while the causes of the earnings drop are largely one-off and reversing; a new CEO and the Purell synergies give two routes to a re-rating toward the $100.76 average target. The bear framing: the yield is high because the market doubts it. Free cash flow of $405M did not cover the dividend in fiscal 2026, equity is near zero and debt has risen. The way to adjudicate is not the P/E but two numbers in the next two quarters: organic volume in Household, and gross margin against the 42% guide.
