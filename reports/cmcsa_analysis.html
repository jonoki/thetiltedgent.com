01 Company Overview
Comcast runs one of the two biggest U.S. cable broadband networks (Xfinity, Comcast Business); the other, Charter, became the world's largest cable operator when it closed its purchase of Cox on Aug 20, 2026 (as reported by Light Reading). Comcast is also a fast-growing mobile virtual network operator (MVNO — it rents Verizon's wireless network and sells it under the Xfinity Mobile brand), and, through NBCUniversal and Sky, a global media company that owns NBC, Telemundo, Universal Pictures, the Universal theme parks and the Peacock streaming service. In FY2025 it reported $123.7B of revenue, flat YoY, $37.4B of adjusted EBITDA (−1.8%) and $19.2B of free cash flow (+53%). On Jan 2, 2026 it spun off its cable networks (USA, CNBC, MSNBC, E!, Syfy, Oxygen, Golf Channel) as Versant (NASDAQ: VSNT), and on Jun 29, 2026 it announced a far bigger move: a tax-free spin-off of all of NBCUniversal and Sky, now expected to complete in mid-2027, leaving "Comcast" as a pure connectivity company (home internet, mobile, TV and business networks).
The central debate is "cheap or broken?" At $22.69 the stock trades at 7.2x trailing and 6.5x forward earnings and yields 5.8%, and it still produced $4.2B of free cash flow in Q3 alone. Against that, the business the market actually trades on — home broadband — keeps shrinking: Comcast had 28.34M domestic residential broadband customers on Sep 30, 2026, 554K fewer than a year earlier, and lost 144K in Q3 against 91K a year before, to fibre overbuilders (companies laying new fibre past homes cable already serves) and fixed-wireless access (FWA — 5G home internet from T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T). Domestic broadband revenue fell 4.7% in Q3. The buyback is paused until the NBCUniversal split, so the cash return today is the dividend alone. This report leads with broadband customer losses, then Peacock's turn to profit, Theme Parks after Epic Universe, and what the Versant and NBCU separations mean for the numbers.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

28.3M Residential Broadband Customers Broadband + Mobile Bundle ~45% of Base on Gigabit-Plus 10.6M Wireless Lines (MVNO on Verizon) 56%-Margin Business Services Universal IP → Parks Flywheel ~$17B TTM FCF
Comcast's core advantage is a physical one: a hybrid fibre-coax network whose 28.3M residential broadband customers are nearly 45% on gig-plus speed tiers (up nearly 10 points in a year, per the Q3 release), financed decades ago and throwing off $19.2B of free cash flow in FY2025 and about $17.1B over the last twelve months (TTM; Q3 2026 was $4.2B, −14.8%). Bundling Xfinity Mobile — bought wholesale from Verizon, sold at a discount to the big three — turns that network into a "convergence" product meant to keep customers from leaving on both sides; 1.6M line adds over the last twelve months show the bundle selling. On the media side, owning Universal's film library, the NBC broadcast reach and the theme parks creates a self-reinforcing IP loop that only Disney can match.

Key Vulnerabilities

Broadband share loss to FWA and fibre: Comcast lost 554K domestic residential broadband customers in the twelve months to Sep 30, 2026, 144K of them in Q3. Fixed-wireless from T-Mobile, Verizon and AT&T targets exactly the price-sensitive customers cable used to own, while fibre overbuilders attack the premium tier; in September the CFO said new fibre now overlaps 4–5% of Comcast's footprint a year, against 2–3% a couple of years ago (as reported by Broadband Breakfast). Comcast's answer (five-year price locks, simplified plans, free mobile lines that convert to paid ones, $2.4B of Connectivity capex in Q3, +3.6%) protects customers at the cost of margin.

Video and linear-TV decay: $26.4B of FY2025 revenue is still video, shrinking (−274K video customers in Q3 alone; Q3 video revenue −8.7%) and low-margin after programming costs. Versant removed the worst of the cable-network exposure, but NBC and Sky remain tied to a linear advertising market in structural decline.

Execution risk on two spin-offs in 18 months: The NBCU/Sky separation means dis-synergies, a paused buyback, a leadership handover (Angelakis returning as CEO, Cavanagh leaving for NBCU), a debt re-allocation across two new balance sheets that management wants rated investment grade, and a redesign of how the company is run, with a charge of more than $1B in Q4 2026 — all while the core business is shrinking. CFO Jason Armstrong on the Q3 call: "First, we are fundamentally redesigning our organization." Break-ups often lift a stock as investors start valuing each piece on its own, but both pieces here face long-term declines.

Consumer-discretionary exposure: Parks (Q3 2026 EBITDA −17%), theatrical and advertising are all cyclical. A U.S. consumer slowdown hits the growth segments exactly when the stable segment is losing subscribers.

Notable One-Off Items

Hulu sale gain (Q2 2025): FY2025 net income of $20.0B (+23.5%) includes a $9.4B pre-tax / $7.1B after-tax gain on selling Comcast's Hulu stake to Disney. That gain rolled out of the TTM window after Q2 2026, which is why GAAP TTM EPS is $3.16 against FY2025's $5.39, and why nine-month 2026 net income ($9.15B) compares against $17.83B. Use adjusted EPS for the underlying trend: $2.90 for the first nine months of 2026 against $3.46, down 16%.

Versant spin-off (Jan 2, 2026): Comcast shareholders received 1 Versant share per 25 CMCSA shares. FY2025 segment figures include the cable networks inside Media; 2026 quarters do not. Management's "pro forma" comparisons also remove Sky Germany, sold May 31, 2026.

FIFA World Cup (Q2–Q3 2026): Added about $440M of incremental Media revenue in Q2 and $223M in Q3 (the releases) — non-recurring until 2030.

Buyback paused (from Q3 2026): Comcast suspended repurchases from the third quarter and expects the pause to last through the NBCU separation (Q3 release). Repurchases, including shares bought for employee plans, totalled $2.5B in the first nine months of 2026 (10-Q).

Restructuring charge (Q4 2026): CFO Jason Armstrong said on the Q3 call that the redesign brings "a significant charge in the fourth quarter", which he put "at over $1 billion"; The Philadelphia Inquirer reported that job cuts are expected, some already under way.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case — "Cheap"

At 6.5x forward earnings and a ~21% FCF yield, the market is treating Comcast as a business in run-off. But it still beat on profit in Q3, wireless added 1.6M lines in a year into a base that is only 7% of the lines in its footprint, Business Services earns 56% margins, Peacock has turned from a $552M loss in Q4 2025 into two straight profitable quarters, and Universal has its biggest film ever. The dividend alone yields 5.8%, and management expects broadband losses to narrow for the full year. The spin of NBCU/Sky — a company with Universal, Peacock, the parks and the NBC sports calendar — is the catalyst that could finally close a conglomerate discount (the market valuing the whole at less than its parts).

Bear Case — "Broken"

The single-digit P/E is a value trap (cheap because it keeps getting worse) if broadband keeps shrinking: Q3 losses (144K) were worse than a year earlier and worse than analysts expected, domestic broadband revenue fell 4.7% and Residential EBITDA 5.8%, and the CFO says fibre is overlapping Comcast's footprint faster than before. The buyback is paused, so the cash return is the dividend alone, on a payout that must be split across two balance sheets with no policy yet disclosed. Parks EBITDA fell 17% and management does not expect growth before the second half of 2027, World Cup ad dollars don't recur, Studios are lumpy, a charge of more than $1B lands in Q4, and Comcast will hold up to 19.9% of an asset it no longer controls. KeyBanc rates the stock Sell.

  Industry Commentary
Fixed Wireless and Fibre Are Ending Cable's Broadband Monopoly
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
For twenty years cable's hybrid fibre-coax plant was the only real high-speed option in most U.S. neighborhoods, and Comcast priced accordingly. That ended around 2022 when T-Mobile and Verizon began selling 5G fixed-wireless home internet at $50/month with no contract, and when AT&T, Frontier and a wave of private-equity-backed overbuilders started laying fibre past cable homes. The industry result is that U.S. cable operators keep losing broadband customers — Comcast −554K residential in the twelve months to Sep 30, 2026, −144K in Q3 alone, and Charter −172K internet customers in its own Q2 2026 (as reported by Advanced Television). In September Comcast's CFO said new fibre now overlaps 4–5% of its footprint a year, against 2–3% a couple of years ago, and called some $30–40 gigabit prices irrational. Comcast's response is to compete on price certainty (five-year locks, simplified tiers), bundle mobile so customers are less likely to leave, and keep spending on the network ($2.4B of Connectivity capex in Q3) upgrading to DOCSIS 4.0 (the next cable standard, enabling multi-gig symmetrical speeds) — all of which protects subscribers at the expense of margin: Residential's adjusted EBITDA margin fell 0.9 points to 35.9% in Q3. The bull argument is that FWA is capacity-constrained and will saturate; the bear argument is that fibre isn't.
Big Media Is Unbundling Itself — Spins, Splits and Sales
ONGOING HEADWIND
Comcast's twin separations are part of an industry-wide retreat from the "own everything" model. Cable-network portfolios are being isolated (Versant; Warner Bros. Discovery's own split), studios are changing hands (Paramount Skydance), and Disney has consolidated Hulu. The logic is that declining linear assets drag on the multiple of growing ones, so separate them and let each find its natural owner. For Comcast, this means NBCUniversal/Sky will trade on Peacock's profitability, the parks and the studio slate — a Disney-like profile — while "RemainCo" Comcast becomes a pure telecom judged on broadband net adds and free cash flow. The headwind is that both halves inherit secular declines (linear TV; cable broadband), and the transition year brings a paused buyback, one-time separation costs ($77M of spin transaction costs in the first nine months of 2026, $51M for Versant and $26M for the NBCU spin, plus the Q4 restructuring charge) and the market's tendency to sell the parent until the pieces are visible.
Streaming Finally Makes Money — and Sports Is Why
EMERGING TAILWIND
What is happening: After a decade of subsidized growth, the streaming industry has pivoted to profitability — price rises, ad tiers, password-sharing crackdowns and, above all, live sports that viewers won't cancel around. Peacock's first profitable quarter ($189M in Q2 2026, on the NBA playoffs and the World Cup), a second in Q3 ($261M) and its August price increase are textbook examples. NBC's new NBA package, Premier League, Big Ten and the 2028 Olympics give NBCU one of the deepest sports calendars in the business.

Bull implication: If Peacock scales toward $1B+ of annual EBITDA on 48M subscribers, the spun-off NBCU gets valued as a growth media company rather than a linear-TV run-off, and Comcast shareholders own it outright.

Bear implication: Sports rights inflate faster than subscription revenue, the World Cup bump (~$440M of revenue in Q2, $223M in Q3) won't repeat until 2030, and the August price hike will be tested by cancellations the moment the big sports seasons end — paid subscribers were 48M at both the end of Q2 and Q3. A profitable streamer at 48M subs is still a distant #4 behind Netflix, Disney and Amazon.
