01 Company Overview

CME Group operates the world's largest derivatives marketplace — the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, Chicago Board of Trade, NYMEX and COMEX — plus the clearing house that stands between every buyer and seller in those markets. It is the venue of record for U.S. interest-rate futures (SOFR, Fed Funds, the full Treasury curve), for E-mini S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 futures, for WTI crude and Henry Hub natural gas, and for the CBOT grain complex that sets global food benchmarks. FY2025 revenue was $6.52 billion, up 6% year over year — a fourth consecutive year of record annual revenue, adjusted operating income, adjusted net income and adjusted EPS — on ADV of 28.1 million contracts per day.

The economics are unusual even among exchanges: CME converted 65.5% of FY2025 revenue into GAAP operating income and 69.4% into adjusted operating income, with essentially no cost of goods sold. It ranks #111 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation. Critically, CME does not need markets to go up — it needs them to move. Revenue is a function of volume and volatility, not direction, which is why the stock often trades less like a typical financial company and more like a bet on how unsettled the world's markets are.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Capital efficiencies — $100B/day Open interest lock-in Exclusive S&P 500 / NDX licences CBOT / NYMEX / COMEX benchmarks Vertically integrated clearing house Liquidity network effects Asset-light — 69.4% adj. op margin

The moat is the clearing house, and specifically the margin it lets you avoid posting. Because CME clears rates, equity index, FX, energy, metals and agricultural futures inside a single clearing organisation, a trader with offsetting or correlated positions across those products posts one net margin requirement rather than several gross ones. CME's Q3 2026 release reports a new high of $100 billion a day in average capital efficiencies for customers (reduced margin requirements from clearing across products; it was more than $95 billion a day in Q2). That number is the moat expressed in dollars. A competitor listing an economically identical contract cleared somewhere else cannot offer that offset — so even if the rival's contract is cheaper per trade, moving a position there increases the trader's total capital requirement. The rival must be cheap enough to compensate for the extra margin, which is a very high bar.

Open interest is the second lock. Futures positions are not fungible across venues: a position opened on CME must be closed on CME. Every contract of open interest is therefore a customer who must return. On the Q3 call Terry Duffy said quarter-end open interest reached 128 million contracts, the highest end-of-September level in CME's history (Investing.com transcript) — the health metric that matters most, because it measures committed risk rather than day-trading churn. Liquidity then compounds the effect: deep books attract flow, flow deepens books, and the resulting bid-ask advantage is worth more to a large hedger than any listed fee schedule. This is why challengers to established futures benchmarks have historically failed even when backed by the banks that are the exchange's own customers.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The FMX / LCH challenge in rates. FMX Futures Exchange — backed by BGC Group and a consortium of major banks and market makers — launched U.S. SOFR and Treasury futures with clearing at LCH, explicitly to attack CME's rate franchise by offering netting against LCH's dominant interest-rate swap book. Trade press has reported FMX's CEO conceding that the venue misses much of the daily volume BrokerTec captures, and industry reporting has documented FMX volumes slumping as traders reverted to CME during the 2025 tariff volatility episode. So far the challenge has not visibly dented CME's share, but it has been enough to force a competitive response and to cap pricing power in the largest product line — a structurally negative development even if FMX never wins meaningful share.

2. Volume, not direction — but volume is cyclical. CME earns nothing from markets rising and nothing from markets falling; it earns from markets moving. That is a genuine diversifier, but it makes revenue a direct function of macro uncertainty. Q2 2026 revenue grew just 0.8% year over year despite ADV of 29.8 million contracts, as rate and energy activity softened (UBS cited weaker-than-expected futures volumes and slightly softer pricing in June); in Q3 revenue grew 13% on ADV of 29.4 million (+16%) as uncertainty picked up, though revenue per contract still fell 3.3%. The swing between the two quarters is the point. A durably calm Fed and a compressed volatility regime is the single most reliable way to produce a bad year for this business, and there is nothing management can do about it.

3. Market-data pricing and regulatory scrutiny. Market data was 14.0% of Q3 2026 revenue ($243 million of $1.737 billion, our calculation) and grew 20%, well ahead of the 12.2% growth in clearing and transaction fees. Much of that growth comes from price increases and licence audits levied on customers who have no alternative source for CME prices. That is precisely the fact pattern that attracts regulatory attention to exchange data fees, in the U.S. and in Europe. The line is currently carrying the growth story; a pricing constraint would remove it.

4. Leadership transition plus an infrastructure re-platforming, at the same time. Lynne Fitzpatrick takes over as CEO on March 1, 2027, while CME also migrates its markets onto a private Google Cloud region and co-location facility in Aurora, Illinois. CME has committed to at least 18 months' advance notice before markets actually move. A latency regression or an outage during that migration would be materially damaging to a franchise whose entire value proposition is that it is the most reliable place to transact. Executing the largest technology change in the company's history under a new CEO is a real, if manageable, risk.

Notable One-Off Items

Investment gains are distorting GAAP results in both directions and should be stripped out. The cleanest illustration is Q4 2025: GAAP diluted EPS of $3.24 exceeded adjusted EPS of $2.77 by 47 cents, because non-operating investment gains flowed through GAAP net income but were excluded from the adjusted figure. In Q2 and Q3 2026 the relationship inverted — GAAP EPS $2.88 versus adjusted $2.99, then $2.89 versus $3.02. For the full year 2025, GAAP net income of $4.072 billion and adjusted net income of $4.088 billion were within $16 million of each other, so the annual distortion largely nets out; the quarterly noise does not.

Specific FY2025 items CME identified: $31.8 million of strategic transaction costs associated with the divestment of OSTTRA — the post-trade services joint venture CME held alongside S&P Global — and $20.9 million of deferred tax adjustments and tax audit settlements. Neither is large relative to a $6.5 billion revenue base, but the OSTTRA-related gains and costs are the main reason FY2025 GAAP and adjusted operating income diverged ($4.229B vs $4.525B, a $296 million gap). There is no litigation reserve, impairment or restructuring charge of consequence in the recent record. Investors should model the adjusted series and treat quarterly GAAP EPS beats or misses driven by investment marks as noise.

CME's signature capital-return mechanism: after covering the year's needs, it pays out excess cash as a single variable dividend on top of the regular quarterly. The series has risen every year — $3.25 (2022), $4.50 (2023), $5.25 (2024), $5.80 (2025) and $6.15 declared for 2025 results, paid March 26, 2026. BGC-backed FMX, clearing at LCH, continues to contest U.S. SOFR and Treasury futures. CME and Google Cloud are building a dedicated private Google Cloud region and co-location facility in Aurora, Illinois, adjacent to CME's existing data centre campus, with disaster recovery in Dallas. CME has committed to at least 18 months' advance notice before markets actually move; cash markets stay in current facilities during the transition. Execution risk is real; the strategic payoff is a modern, AI-capable platform and a deeper Google relationship.

That understates the shareholder outcome, because CME pays a large annual variable dividend ($3.25, $4.50, $5.25, $5.80 and $6.15 declared over the period) on top of its regular quarterly dividend; those payments are not in this price return. Over the last twelve months (Oct 24, 2025 to Oct 26, 2026, price only) CME rose 14.3% against 13.0% for the SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY), which tracks the S&P 500.

The path was anything but smooth. The stock fell 29% from its March 2022 close of $237.86 to a December 2022 trough of $168.16 as the zero-rate era ended and the market questioned whether an exchange could grow into higher rates. It then compounded steadily for three years to an all-time monthly closing high of $319.50 in February 2026, before losing 30.9% to $220.83 by the end of June 2026 — a two-quarter drawdown driven by the surprise CEO succession announcement, weaker-than-expected June volumes, and a cascade of sell-side target cuts. The recovery came in three steps: July closed at $267.79 (+21.3%) on the Q2 beat, August at $285.50 (+6.6%), then September gave back 8.2% to $261.98. October has added 17.6% to $308.06 as of the 26th, on the Q3 results and a round of target increases. That is 88% of the drop from the February closing high to the June low recovered, and higher than any month-end close since February. The shares now sit above both the 50-day average ($278.14) and the 200-day ($279.61); at the previous edition's August 19 close they were above the 50-day but below the 200-day.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a regulated near-monopoly on the world's most important risk-transfer benchmarks at 25.3× trailing and 23.8× forward earnings — in line with the exchange peer group (25.8×) and the S&P 500 (25.7×) on trailing earnings, at a premium on forward earnings (21.9× and 20.1×). The clearing moat is quantified and enormous: a record $100 billion a day of capital efficiencies that no unbundled competitor can replicate, plus a record end-of-September open interest of 128 million contracts. Market data is compounding at 20% with 34 consecutive quarters of growth and no volume sensitivity. The asset-light model converts 69.4% of revenue to adjusted operating profit and $4.33 billion to free cash flow, funding a 3.68% yield including a variable dividend that has risen every year from $3.25 to $6.15. And earnings are driven by volatility, not direction — at a 0.27 beta, this is one of the few businesses that gets paid when everything else is going wrong.

Bear Case

Growth depends on volume, which comes and goes. Q2 2026 revenue rose just 0.8% year over year, then Q3 rose 13%, and revenue per contract still fell 3.3% to $0.679 — Q3's transaction-fee growth came from more contracts at lower fees. A PEG of 3.48 versus 1.36 for peers says you are paying a growth multiple for a utility. FMX may never take real share, but its existence permanently caps rate-futures pricing, and perpetual futures and crypto-native venues are attacking the retail end that CME needs for contract growth. A payout ratio of about 93% leaves little for reinvestment or defensive price cuts. After the rally the average analyst target is only 2.9% above the price, and Bank of America and Goldman Sachs both rate the stock Sell with targets below it.

Top 3 Risks
01
A low-volatility regime. CME's revenue is a direct function of how much the world argues about the future. A Fed on a settled, well-telegraphed path, calm equity markets and range-bound energy would compress ADV across the two largest product lines simultaneously — and there is no management action that offsets it. Q2 2026's +0.8% revenue growth on softer rates and energy activity is a preview of what that regime does to the P&L; Q3's +13% shows the other side.
02
Structural erosion of the clearing moat. FMX clearing at LCH is the credible version of this threat, because LCH's dominance in interest-rate swaps means it can offer swap-versus-futures netting that CME cannot match. If enough of the dealer consortium that backs FMX genuinely commits flow, the liquidity network effect that has protected CME for decades can invert. It has not happened yet — traders reverted to CME under stress in 2025 — but the fee ceiling is already in place.
03
Execution risk on the Google Cloud migration under a new CEO. Re-platforming the matching engine for the world's largest derivatives market onto a private cloud region in Aurora, while a new chief executive takes over on March 1, 2027, concentrates two significant risks in the same window. Latency regression, an outage, or a botched cutover would hand competitors the one argument they currently lack — that CME is not the most reliable place to transact.
  Industry Commentary
Interest-Rate Volatility Is the Primary Volume Driver — and It Cuts Both Ways
VOLUME-DRIVEN
Roughly half of CME's contract volume — 15.121 million of 29.4 million daily contracts in Q3 2026 — sits in interest rate futures and options, and that volume is a function of disagreement about the Fed. When the policy path is contested, hedgers and speculators reposition constantly; when it is settled, they do not. The mechanism showed in two quarters: UBS cut its price target 16% on July 6 on softer interest-rate and energy activity, citing weaker-than-expected futures volumes and slightly softer pricing, and total revenue growth slowed to 0.8% year over year in Q2 2026. Then in Q3 total revenue grew 13% on a record third-quarter ADV of 29.4 million (+16%), with double-digit volume growth in all six asset classes, and fourth-quarter volume is tracking 17% ahead of last year's record fourth quarter (per Investing.com's transcript of the call). Rate volatility is not a trend CME manages; it is weather the company is exposed to, and the exposure is roughly half the franchise.
The Clearing Moat Is Under Its First Credible Attack in a Generation
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
CME's defence has always been margin efficiency: because everything clears in one place, offsetting positions net down to a single requirement, saving customers a record $100 billion a day in capital efficiencies in Q3 2026 (more than $95 billion in Q2). FMX Futures Exchange — backed by BGC Group and a consortium of banks and market makers, clearing at LCH — is the first challenger with an answer, because LCH dominates interest-rate swap clearing and can therefore offer swap-versus-futures netting CME cannot. So far the attack has underperformed: trade press has reported FMX's CEO acknowledging that the venue misses volume BrokerTec captures, and reported FMX volumes slumped during the 2025 tariff episode as traders reverted to CME's deeper books. But the damage is already done to pricing power — CME cannot raise rate-futures fees while a funded alternative exists, and in 2025 it announced a second BrokerTec order book for cash Treasuries designed to connect cash and futures trading. Separately, perpetual futures and crypto-native venues are probing the retail flank; Duffy's Q2-call response, that customers representing 94% of volume have no interest in perpetuals, is defensible on institutional flow but says nothing about where the next generation of retail traders will go.
Retail Participation in Futures — the Contract-Count Tailwind, and How to Frame It
EMERGING TAILWIND
The Micro E-mini complex — one-tenth the size of a standard E-mini — plus event contracts and small-notional crypto futures have brought a retail cohort into markets that were institutional-only a decade ago. This matters mechanically: CME earns per contract, and micro products generate many contracts per unit of notional risk. Equity index ADV rising from 7.4M in FY2025 to 7.714M in Q3 2026 (8.633M in Q2) is partly this effect. The bull framing: retail is a structurally growing, price-insensitive, high-RPC flow that diversifies CME away from a dealer community that is simultaneously its largest customer and, through FMX, its would-be competitor; combined with a 3.68% yield, 69.4% adjusted operating margins and a 0.27 beta, that supports the Morgan Stanley $359 case. The bear framing: retail flow is the least sticky in the market and the most likely to migrate to whichever venue is cheapest and most novel — offshore perpetuals, prediction markets, tokenised products — and CME's decision to sit out perpetual futures entirely is a bet that institutions define the market. If that bet is wrong, the growth engine of the contract-count story leaves, and Bank of America's $227 target would look generous for a business growing revenue at 8.9% on a PEG of 3.48.
