01 Company Overview

Cummins designs and builds engines, powertrain components, aftertreatment systems, generator sets and, increasingly, the electrolysers and electric drivetrains that are supposed to replace them. It reports through five segments — Engine, Distribution, Components, Power Systems and Accelera — and its products end up under the hood of Class 8 trucks, inside RAM heavy-duty pickups, behind data-centre walls as standby power, and in mining and marine applications. FY2025 revenue was $33.67B, down 1.3% from $34.10B in FY2024; the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $34.71B, up 2.9%, and the most recent quarter was a record $9.46B, up 9.4% YoY. At $77.20B the company ranks roughly #322 globally by market capitalisation.

The debate this page is built around is that Cummins is now two companies wearing one ticker. The legacy company — Engine and Components — is a late-cycle North American truck business whose next two years are hostage to a federal emissions rule and to whether a pre-buy shows up. The re-rating company is Power Systems, where data-centre standby gensets have pushed EBITDA margin to 26.8% in the first half of 2026 and lead times out to the second half of 2028. And bolted underneath is Accelera, which lost money every quarter of the period examined here. The stock has already paid for the good news: it closed at $713.21 in June 2026 and sits 21.4% below that today. What follows is an attempt to separate which of those three businesses the $560.75 price is actually discounting.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED-BASE AFTERMARKET EMISSIONS CERTIFICATION IP OEM-AGNOSTIC SUPPLY LARGE-BORE CAPACITY SCARCITY OWNED DISTRIBUTION NETWORK MULTI-GW HYPERSCALER CONTRACTS 107-YEAR BRAND IN UPTIME

The real moat is the installed base plus the certification barrier, and the two reinforce each other. Cummins is one of very few engine makers that sells to truck OEMs it does not own — Paccar, Daimler and others fit Cummins engines alongside their own — which means its volume is not hostage to one brand's market share. Every engine sold seeds decades of parts and service revenue through the company-owned Distribution segment, which is why Distribution is the largest segment by gross sales at $6.442B in the first half and why its margin held at 13.9% while Engine's fell 350 basis points. On top of that sits the certification barrier: getting an engine family through EPA and CARB (the California Air Resources Board, whose standards several states adopt) is a multi-year, capital-intensive process, and the model-year 2027 transition is exactly the kind of event that punishes sub-scale competitors more than it punishes Cummins. The newest and least appreciated piece of the moat is physical: large-bore genset capacity is scarce, lead times run into the second half of 2028, and a customer who wants gigawatts of standby power in 2027 cannot simply conjure a second supplier.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Regulatory dependence cuts both ways. Tighter emissions rules raise Cummins' content per truck and its competitive advantage — but they also determine the shape of the demand curve. The EPA has confirmed the model-year 2027 start date stands while simultaneously proposing to change the surrounding requirements (warranty periods, useful-life timing, nonconformance penalties, DEF derates). Cummins responded with a deliberately phased launch. Both the upside content story and the pre-buy story now depend on a rule that has been reopened but not finalised.
02
The pre-buy may not arrive. The historical pattern — fleets buying ahead of an emissions deadline to avoid new, costlier, less proven engines — is the reason many models carry a strong 2026 for Engine and Components. Industry commentary as of August 2026 is that the anticipated pre-buy "has not materialized to the level many predicted", blamed on weak freight markets and poor fleet profitability. Management itself said any pre-buy is "more driven by just market improvement" than regulatory urgency. If that is right, 2026 Engine strength is cyclical rather than borrowed — better for 2027, worse for the pre-buy narrative.
03
Concentration in a single demand theme. Power Systems is carrying the margin story, and inside Power Systems the marginal dollar is increasingly data-centre standby power tied to hyperscaler capital spending. That is one customer set, one capital cycle, and one thesis. A slowdown in AI-driven data-centre construction would hit the segment that is currently masking a 350-basis-point margin decline in Engine.
04
Prior regulatory settlement and its consent decree. Cummins announced on December 22, 2023 an agreement in principle to settle regulatory proceedings over its emissions certification and compliance process for pick-up truck applications — the RAM 2500/3500 engine matter. The company's Q2 2026 filing refers to potential adverse consequences from prior regulatory agreements effective April 2024, indicating an operative consent decree. The dollar amount of the settlement and whether all payments are complete were not verified from the sources fetched and are deliberately not stated here.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF SEPTEMBER 4, 2026

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the market has been buying much harder than selling and the move is described as "overbought"; below 30 the reverse, "oversold". It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a stock can stay overbought for a long time in a genuine re-rating.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $239.84 to the September 4, 2026 close of $560.75, CMI returned +133.8% on price alone, before the dividend — which has been raised for 17 consecutive years and now runs at $8.80 annualised. That headline number hides two completely different regimes. From October 2021 through late 2023 the stock went nowhere: it closed October 2021 at $239.84 and December 2023 at $239.57, a 26-month round trip of exactly nothing, with a trough of $189.19 in April 2022. Everything happened afterwards. From the December 2023 close to the June 2026 peak monthly close of $713.21, the stock rose 197.7% — and the great majority of that is the Power Systems re-rating, not the truck cycle.

The current setup is a 21.4% drawdown from that June peak, with monthly closes of $713.21 (Jun), $634.20 (Jul), $564.40 (Aug) and $560.75 (Sep 4). Three consecutive down months into a raised guidance print is unusual and is the most interesting technical fact on this page: the Q2 beat on August 4 did not arrest the decline. The 6-period monthly RSI reflects that — it ran hot through the first half of 2026 and has rolled over sharply. Note also that the September point is a partial-month close (September 4), not a completed month end; the moving averages and the final RSI reading incorporate it as though it were, which slightly understates the smoothing at the right-hand edge.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Power Systems is a capacity-constrained business with multi-gigawatt contracted hyperscaler demand and lead times into the second half of 2028, growing 19% with EBITDA margin expanding to 26.8% — and the company is now investing to relieve that constraint. Underneath it sits a $6.4B distribution annuity and 15.66% ROIC with $3.37B of free cash flow. Buy it at a 17.05 forward P/E, 21% below the June peak, with the EPA 2027 deadline confirmed rather than delayed, and you are paying a mid-cycle industrial multiple for a business whose fastest-growing, highest-margin segment is sold out into 2028.

Bear Case

Half the company by gross sales — Engine and Components — went backwards on profit in the first half: $1.383B of EBITDA against $1.637B, with Engine's margin down 350 basis points on a 1.5% sales gain. The pre-buy that was supposed to carry 2026 has not shown up. Accelera burns roughly a quarter of a billion dollars a year against $350–400M of revenue for optionality with no dated path to profit. The 28.66 trailing P/E is being paid on the strength of one demand theme — data-centre standby power — whose customers are three or four hyperscalers with the ability to slow their capital plans at will. Three straight down months through a record quarter is the market already asking this question.

Top 3 Risks

01
Data-centre capex concentration. Power Systems is 19% of gross segment sales but roughly a third of segment EBITDA, and the marginal dollar is hyperscaler standby power. Any deceleration in AI-driven data-centre construction removes the segment that is currently offsetting a deteriorating Engine business.
02
A messy MY2027 transition with no pre-buy cushion. The EPA kept the 2027 start date but is rewriting warranty, useful-life and derate provisions; Cummins has phased its own launch across three quarters of 2027. If the pre-buy stays absent and the ramp slips, Engine and Components face weak volume and elevated launch cost simultaneously.
03
Accelera as an open-ended cash drain. The segment lost $346M of EBITDA in the first half against $246M of revenue, and the reconciliation to the $260–290M full-year guidance is not clear from the sources fetched. Until Cummins puts a date on breakeven, this is a recurring charge against the free cash flow that funds the dividend and buyback.
  Industry Commentary
Data-centre power is a physical shortage, not a demand forecast
EMERGING TAILWIND

The distinguishing feature of this cycle is that the constraint sits on the supply side. Cummins is not forecasting genset demand — it is rationing it. Rumsey stated flatly that "our growth in 2026 will continue to be constrained by capacity", lead times run into the second half of 2028, and the company has signed a multiyear hyperscaler agreement covering several gigawatts of future backup power. That is why Power Systems is the only segment whose margin expanded: 24.5% in Q2 versus 22.8%, and 26.8% across the half versus 23.1%. A capacity-constrained supplier has pricing power that a demand-constrained one does not. The counterpoint is that capacity investment — the May 21, 2026 large-engine programme, inside $1.35–1.45B of 2026 capex — arrives in a market where the constraint may have eased.

EPA 2027: reopened, not repealed — and the pre-buy is missing
ONGOING HEADWIND

The near-term truck story hinges on a rule whose deadline is fixed and whose contents are not. The 0.035 g/bhp-hr NOx standard for model year 2027 stands; EPA rejected the industry's request to delay it while signalling a revised rule that changes the extended warranty requirement (currently 450,000 miles, up from the original 100,000), useful-life timing, nonconformance penalties and DEF derates. For Components that means the content-per-truck uplift may be smaller than originally modelled. For Engine it means a launch executed into regulatory ambiguity — hence the phased X15/X10 ramp. Meanwhile the classic pre-buy is not materialising: July 2026 Class 8 orders were 22,100 units, +68% YoY but −30% month over month seasonally adjusted, and the market read is that weak freight and poor fleet profitability are overwhelming the regulatory incentive. Management raised the 2026 North American heavy-duty forecast to 240,000–250,000 units on cyclical improvement rather than regulatory urgency — a healthier reason to be strong, but one that removes the 2027 air-pocket hedge from the bear case as well.

What the $560.75 price is actually paying for
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Strip the company down and there are three cash-flow streams with three different multiples embedded in one 28.66 trailing P/E. The Distribution annuity ($6.442B, 13.9% EBITDA, +8.3%) deserves a stable mid-teens multiple. Power Systems ($4.211B, 26.8% EBITDA, +19.0%, sold out to 2028) deserves considerably more. Engine and Components ($11.18B combined, EBITDA down 15.5% year over year on flat sales) deserve a cyclical trough multiple on trough earnings — which is not the same as a low one. And Accelera deserves a negative number: −$346M of first-half EBITDA on $246M of revenue is not optionality priced at zero, it is optionality priced at a cost. Bull implication: if you believe the 2027 truck ramp is smooth and gensets stay sold out, the forward P/E of 17.05 on 24.7% consensus EPS growth is the entry point, and the $757.02 average target is defensible. Bear implication: if Power Systems' growth normalises toward the company average while Engine margin keeps compressing and Accelera keeps burning, then you are paying 28.66× for a diversified industrial with a 7.82% net margin, and the correct comparison is the $530 low target — 5.5% below today — not the $894 high.
