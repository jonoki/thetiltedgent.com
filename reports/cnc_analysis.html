01 Company Overview

Centene is the largest managed-care organisation in the United States by government-programme membership: roughly 25.9 million at-risk members across Medicaid, the ACA Marketplace (individual exchange plans sold under the Ambetter brand), Medicare Advantage and standalone Medicare Part D drug plans. FY2025 revenue was $176.15B, up 19.7% from $147.15B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $180.30B, up 12.9%. It ranks roughly #796 globally by market capitalisation at $32.3B — a company selling $180B of premium a year valued at 0.18x sales.

That last ratio is the whole story. On July 1, 2025 Centene withdrew its 2025 earnings guidance after independent actuarial data from Wakely, covering 22 of its 29 Marketplace states (~72% of Marketplace membership), showed market-wide morbidity far above plan — a preliminary $1.8 billion reduction to expected net risk-adjustment revenue transfer, about $2.75 of adjusted EPS. The stock fell roughly 40% in a single session. In October 2025 the company took a $6.7 billion non-cash goodwill impairment ($13.69 per share), producing a FY2025 GAAP loss of $(13.53) per share against adjusted EPS of $2.08. Since then the HBR has come down hard and management has twice raised 2026 guidance. The central debate on this page: is the 2025 de-rating a repricing of a permanently lower-margin business, or a cyclical mispricing cycle that a two-year rate catch-up mechanically repairs? The page lays out what would have to be true in each direction and does not pick.

Centene contracts with state Medicaid agencies to manage care for low-income and disabled populations at a fixed per-member-per-month capitation rate. The economics are simple and brutal: the state sets the rate roughly a year in advance, the members' actual cost (their acuity — how sick they are on average) is discovered after the fact, and the gap is the margin. Post-pandemic redeterminations removed millions of members from Medicaid rolls, and the members who left were disproportionately healthy, so the remaining book got sicker faster than states raised rates. That is the acuity mismatch, and it is why Medicaid HBR ran at 93.0% in Q4 2025. It was 93.9% in Q2 2026 — still the highest-cost segment, and the one metric that most directly controls whether the recovery is real. Management told investors on the Q2 2026 call it now expects full-year Medicaid membership down 8–9% versus a prior 6% forecast, and that it is deliberately holding back roughly 50 basis points of rate benefit to absorb potential acuity shifts from OBBBA implementation. Shrinking membership while rates catch up is the strategy, not an accident.

Individual exchange plans. The ACA's risk-adjustment programme moves money between insurers in each state: plans that enrol healthier-than-average members pay in, plans that enrol sicker-than-average members receive. Crucially, the transfer depends on the whole market's average morbidity, not just your own book — so an insurer can price its own members correctly and still lose badly if the market around it turns out healthier than assumed. That is precisely what the Wakely data revealed in July 2025: market morbidity came in materially different from plan, producing a preliminary $1.8B hit to expected net risk-adjustment revenue and about $2.75 of adjusted EPS. Centene immediately began refiling 2026 Marketplace rates to a higher baseline morbidity assumption across states representing a substantial majority of its Marketplace membership.

The 2026 outcome has gone the other way. Membership fell by 2.3 million year over year — repricing this hard always costs volume — but segment HBR of 79.2% is by far the best in the portfolio, and the company booked $180 million of favourable development on the final 2025 CMS risk-adjustment reconciliation. Guided full-year Marketplace pre-tax margin was raised to 4.5–5%. The bear objection is specific: a true-up on a prior year is not a run-rate, and management itself flagged that roughly $0.50 of Q2 EPS came from non-recurring settlement items.

Key structural risk: enhanced ACA premium subsidies and their expiry schedule drive exchange enrolment and the morbidity mix of who stays. The 2.3M member decline is consistent with both repricing and a shrinking subsidised pool; the split between the two was not disaggregated on the sources fetched.

Standalone prescription-drug plans for Medicare beneficiaries — the only segment adding members at scale, up 957,000 year over year. Management raised expected FY2026 pre-tax margin from an original ~2% to greater than 3%, attributing it to specialty drug trend running below original expectations. Centene is a marginal player in Medicare Advantage — under a million members against the scale of UnitedHealth and Humana — and it is treating the business as a fix-it rather than a growth engine. Management cited headwinds from "artificial cut point increases" in the CMS Star Ratings system (quality scores that determine bonus payments and rebate dollars; a plan that slips below 4 stars loses the bonus entirely, so small methodology changes translate directly into revenue), while stating confidence in break-even or better results in 2027 through portfolio optimisation — pruning unprofitable counties and plan designs — and operational execution. Membership down 47K YoY is consistent with that pruning. For the equity, MA is small enough that it is a 2027 option rather than a 2026 driver, but a Stars disappointment is the kind of headline that re-opens the credibility question.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
STATE CONTRACT INCUMBENCY SCALE IN GOVERNMENT PROGRAMMES 25.9M AT-RISK LIVES SG&A RATIO 7.0% MULTI-STATE DIVERSIFICATION REGULATORY COMPLEXITY BARRIER $27.1B CASH & INVESTMENTS

The real advantage is unglamorous: administrative cost per member. Centene runs an SG&A ratio of 7.0% (Q2 2026, versus 7.1% a year earlier and 7.4% for full-year 2025). In a business where the medical loss ratio is 90%+ and often regulated to a floor, the fixed-cost line is one of the few levers a manager actually controls, and spreading it over 25.9 million lives across dozens of state contracts is genuinely hard to replicate. State Medicaid procurements also favour incumbents with operating history and provider networks already in place — switching costs sit with the state, not the member. What the moat emphatically does not do is protect the revenue line: rates are set by regulators and the risk-adjustment transfer is set by the market, so a wide administrative moat coexists with almost no pricing power.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Rate-setting lag is structural, not cyclical. Medicaid capitation rates are set by 30-odd separate state agencies on annual cycles, using data that is already a year old when filed. When the acuity of the remaining book moves faster than that cycle — as it did through the post-redetermination period — the insurer absorbs the gap for at least four quarters with no contractual remedy. This is not a bug that gets fixed; it is the shape of the business, and it can recur whenever enrolment mix shifts sharply.
▸
ACA risk adjustment depends on other insurers' books. The July 2025 event was not a pricing error in Centene's own membership — it was a mis-forecast of market-wide morbidity across 22 states, which is information the company does not possess until a third-party actuary publishes it. A single Wakely data release wiped out roughly 40% of the equity in one session. The same informational asymmetry exists every year.
▸
Political and legislative exposure is direct, not indirect. Essentially all of Centene's revenue comes from government programmes. Changes to Medicaid eligibility and funding, to enhanced ACA premium subsidies, or to Medicare Advantage benchmark and Star Ratings methodology alter the revenue base by legislative act. Management is already reserving against OBBBA-driven acuity shifts by holding back 50bp of rate benefit — an explicit acknowledgement that policy risk is being priced into their own plan.
▸
The adjusted-to-GAAP gap is wide and management-defined. FY2025 adjusted EPS was $2.08 against a GAAP loss of $(13.53). FY2026 guidance is >$4.80 adjusted against >$3.11 GAAP — a $1.69 per-share wedge, roughly 35% of the adjusted number. A $6.7B goodwill write-off and a $513M Magellan impairment are genuinely non-cash, but a company that has now impaired a fifth of its own book value has limited standing to insist that only the adjusted figure counts.

RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has risen persistently and is described as "overbought"; below 30 it has fallen persistently and is "oversold". It is a description of momentum, not a forecast — and on a stock that lost half its value in one month, it says more about the violence of the move than about what comes next.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The instruction here is to set out what would have to be true in each direction rather than to call the outcome, and the two cases are unusually clean because they disagree about one testable thing: whether the 2026 margin recovery survives the removal of one-off items.

What would have to be true for the recovery to hold

Medicaid HBR keeps falling from 93.9% as state rate updates land against a book management is deliberately shrinking 8–9%; Marketplace holds near a 79.2% HBR and a 4.5–5% pre-tax margin on the repriced 2026 cohort without needing another prior-year true-up; PDP margin stays above 3% on contained specialty drug trend; and Q3 2026 (Oct 27) prints adjusted EPS consistent with >$4.80 full-year without settlement items. If those four hold, the current price is 13.6x a guidance floor management has already raised twice, on a business generating $8.95B of free cash flow with SG&A at 7.0% — and the 2025 de-rating was a cycle, not a repricing.

What would have to be true for the de-rating to be permanent

The 2026 numbers are substantially borrowed from 2025: $180M of favourable risk-adjustment development and roughly $0.50 per quarter of settlement items are prior-period recoveries, not run-rate. Strip them and the earnings power is materially lower. Medicaid HBR at 93.9% has not yet reached a level that generates normal segment margin; Marketplace bought its 79.2% by shedding 2.3 million members, which competitors can attack by pricing back in; PDP margin depends on drug trend staying benign; and MA still needs a Stars outcome to reach break-even in 2027. On this reading, government-programme margins are structurally lower post-OBBBA, and a $32.3B market cap on $180B of revenue is the market correctly refusing to capitalise premium it does not believe converts to profit.

Top 3 Risks

01
Earnings quality, not earnings level. The company itself flagged ~$0.50 of Q2 2026 EPS as non-recurring, and the market sold the stock 8.15% on a large beat. The first quarter in which the one-offs are absent — Q3 2026, October 27 — is where this risk resolves in one direction or the other.
02
A repeat of the risk-adjustment shock. The July 2025 event was informational: a third-party actuary published market-wide morbidity data that invalidated the company's assumption, and ~40% of the equity disappeared in a session. Nothing about the structure of ACA risk adjustment has changed. Beta of 1.11 does not describe this risk.
03
Legislative and rate-setting risk on essentially 100% of revenue. Medicaid eligibility and funding, enhanced ACA subsidies, MA benchmarks and Star Ratings methodology are all set by government. Management is already holding back 50bp of rate benefit against OBBBA acuity shifts — a self-declared reserve against a policy outcome it cannot control.
Industry Commentary
The Medicaid acuity overhang
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Post-pandemic Medicaid redeterminations removed millions of members from state rolls across the industry, and the members who left skewed healthy. The result is a smaller, sicker book priced on rates filed before the mix shifted. Centene's Medicaid HBR was 93.0% in Q4 2025 and 93.9% in Q2 2026 — the segment covering 12.1 million members, 46.7% of at-risk lives, has not yet recovered. The July 2025 disclosure named the specific cost drivers: behavioural health, home health and high-cost drugs, with New York and Florida called out. The mechanism that fixes this is state rate updates arriving over 2026–27, which is why management is simultaneously guiding membership down 8–9% and holding back 50bp of rate benefit. It is a deliberate trade of volume for margin, and it takes years, not quarters.

ACA exchange economics after the 2025 repricing
ONGOING HEADWIND

Centene repriced its 2026 Marketplace book to a much higher baseline morbidity assumption after the Wakely shock and got what repricing gets you: a 79.2% HBR, a guided 4.5–5% pre-tax margin, and 2.3 million fewer members — a 37% reduction in commercial membership year over year. The open question for the whole exchange market is whether that margin is defensible. Risk adjustment is zero-sum within each state: if every carrier prices up together the market shrinks and margins hold; if one carrier prices back in for share, morbidity re-sorts and the transfer calculations move again. The 2027 filing season, this coming autumn and winter, is where that gets decided. Note also that the size and composition of the subsidised enrolment pool is itself a policy variable — the drivers of the 2.3M decline were not disaggregated on the sources fetched.

Bull versus bear: the same numbers, two readings
EMERGING TAILWIND — CONTESTED

Consolidated HBR improved 340 basis points year over year in Q2 2026, to 89.6% from 93.0%. SG&A held at 7.0%. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance has moved from ">$3.00" (February 2026) to ">$3.40" to ">$4.80" (July 2026) — a 60% increase in six months. The bull reads that as a rate cycle turning on schedule and a management team that set conservative milestones and cleared them. The bear reads the same sequence as a low bar, twice reset, cleared with the help of $180M of prior-year risk-adjustment development and ~$0.50 per quarter of settlements — and points out that the market agreed, selling the stock 8.15% on the raise. Analysts are split down the middle to match: 9 Buy, 11 Hold, 1 Sell, with targets from $56 to $85. The resolution is not a matter of opinion — it is Q3 2026, reported October 27, read on Medicaid HBR and on adjusted EPS ex-one-offs. Everything on this page reduces to those two lines.
