01 Company Overview

Centene is the largest managed-care organisation in the United States by government-programme membership: 25.7 million at-risk members at September 30, 2026 across Medicaid, the ACA Marketplace (individual exchange plans sold under the Ambetter brand), Medicare Advantage and standalone Medicare Part D drug plans. FY2025 total revenues were $194.8B, up 19.4% from $163.1B in FY2024, and the trailing twelve months through September 2026 come to $203.9B, up 9.7% (both from Centene's earnings releases; total revenues include premium tax revenue, $20.2B of the FY2025 figure). At the October 28 close the company was worth $33.2B — a business taking in over $200B a year valued at 0.16x sales.

That last ratio is the whole story. On July 1, 2025 Centene withdrew its 2025 earnings guidance after independent actuarial data from Wakely, covering 22 of its 29 Marketplace states (~72% of Marketplace membership), showed market-wide morbidity far above plan — a preliminary $1.8 billion reduction to expected net risk-adjustment revenue transfer, about $2.75 of adjusted EPS. The stock fell roughly 40% in a single session. In October 2025 the company took a $6.7 billion non-cash goodwill impairment ($13.69 per share), producing a FY2025 GAAP loss of $(13.53) per share against adjusted EPS of $2.08. Since then the HBR has come down and management has raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance from more than $3.00 (February) to more than $5.20 (October 27, 2026). The Q3 2026 print added a sobering number of its own: CFO Drew Asher said 2026 earnings include "$0.50 that we would not expect to recur in 2027", and called "approximately $4.70 of adjusted EPS" the jump-off point for 2027, before next year's tailwinds and headwinds (Q3 2026 earnings call, October 27). The central debate on this page: is the 2025 de-rating a repricing of a permanently lower-margin business, or a pricing mistake that a two-year catch-up in state rates repairs? The page lays out what would have to be true in each direction and does not pick.

Centene contracts with state Medicaid agencies to manage care for low-income and disabled populations at a fixed per-member-per-month capitation rate. The economics are simple and brutal: the state sets the rate roughly a year in advance, the members' actual cost (their acuity — how sick they are on average) is discovered after the fact, and the gap is the margin. Post-pandemic redeterminations removed millions of members from Medicaid rolls, and the members who left were disproportionately healthy, so the remaining book got sicker faster than states raised rates. That is the acuity mismatch, and it is why Medicaid HBR ran at 93.0% in Q4 2025. It was 93.6% in Q3 2026, against 93.4% a year earlier — still the highest-cost segment, and the one metric that most directly controls whether the recovery is real. On the Q3 call CEO Sarah London said the company was "keeping pace with our trajectory to achieve a full-year Medicaid HBR of 93.5," and that "just over 40% of our Medicaid revenue rerates as of January 1," where "there are more positive signals than negative probably for the first time in a long while." CFO Drew Asher said the company is "holding our full-year view of membership down 8% to 9%." On the Q2 call management had said it was holding back roughly 50 basis points (0.5 percentage points) of rate benefit to cover higher Medicaid membership losses in the second half of 2026 and the sicker mix they leave behind; London said some of those losses may be an early start on the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA), the federal budget law signed on July 4, 2025, which requires states to add Medicaid work requirements and to recheck the eligibility of adults covered by the ACA's Medicaid expansion every six months, starting by December 31, 2026 (KFF summary of the law). London named the risk plainly: "the biggest thing that is a concern is a dislocation between rate and acuity." Shrinking membership while rates catch up is the strategy, not an accident.

Individual exchange plans. The ACA's risk-adjustment programme moves money between insurers in each state: plans that enrol healthier-than-average members pay in, plans that enrol sicker-than-average members receive. Crucially, the transfer depends on the whole market's average morbidity, not just your own book — so an insurer can price its own members correctly and still lose badly if the market around it turns out healthier than assumed. That is precisely what the Wakely data revealed in July 2025: market morbidity came in materially different from plan, producing a preliminary $1.8B hit to expected net risk-adjustment revenue and about $2.75 of adjusted EPS. Centene immediately began refiling 2026 Marketplace rates to a higher baseline morbidity assumption across states representing a substantial majority of its Marketplace membership.

The 2026 outcome has gone the other way. Commercial membership fell by 2.4 million year over year to 3.9 million at September 30, 2026 (Marketplace alone: 3.4 million, from 5.8 million) — repricing this hard costs volume — but margins recovered. Segment HBR was 79.2% in Q2, helped by $180 million of favourable development on the final 2025 CMS risk-adjustment reconciliation, and 89.0% in Q3 against 89.9% a year earlier; the Q3 release credits "improved pricing and risk transfer" in Marketplace. On the Q3 call London said "we now expect Marketplace will produce a full-year pretax margin at the top of our previous range of 4.5% to 5.0%." The bear objection is specific: a true-up on a prior year is not a run-rate, and management itself counts $0.50 of 2026 EPS as not expected to recur in 2027.

Key structural risk: enhanced ACA premium subsidies drive exchange enrolment and the morbidity mix of who stays. On the Q3 call London said: "our expectations for market overall are still some degree slight contraction".

Standalone prescription-drug plans for Medicare beneficiaries — the only segment adding members at scale, up 975,000 year over year to 8.9 million. Management raised expected FY2026 pre-tax margin from an original ~2% to greater than 3% at Q2, and on the Q3 call London said "we now expect PDP to generate pretax margins in the mid- to high 3s." Asher said Medicare trend (the growth in medical and drug costs) "was lower than what we had planned for," with PDP "representing the majority of the outperformance" in the quarter. For 2027 he set the bar lower: "probably somewhere in 2% to 3% range would be the opening expectation for PDP for the year." PDP is low premium per member and therefore a small revenue contributor relative to its head-count, but it is currently a clean positive: growing, margin-accretive versus plan, and structurally less exposed to the state-rate and market-morbidity mechanics that broke Medicaid and Marketplace. The vulnerability is the mirror image — PDP margin is a bet on drug cost trend, and specialty drug trend is the one line item in US healthcare that has historically surprised to the upside, not the downside.

Watch item: a mid-to-high-3% PDP pre-tax margin driven by favourable drug trend is a genuine but fragile source of the 2026 beat, and management's own 2027 opening view of 2–3% already assumes part of it goes away.

Centene is a marginal player in Medicare Advantage — under a million members against the scale of UnitedHealth and Humana — and it is treating the business as a fix-it rather than a growth engine. On the Q2 call management cited headwinds from "artificial cut point increases" in the CMS Star Ratings system (quality scores that determine bonus payments and rebate dollars; a plan that slips below 4 stars loses the bonus entirely, so small methodology changes translate directly into revenue). On the Q3 call London said the Star Ratings CMS released in October, which apply to the 2028 plan year, "were in line with our expectations given known program headwinds," and Asher described "Medicare Advantage moving from breakeven in 2026 to better than breakeven in 2027." Membership down 59K year over year is consistent with pruning unprofitable counties and plans. The Medicare segment's reported HBR (93.8% in Q3, against 94.3% a year earlier) covers the PDP drug plans as well as Medicare Advantage: on the Q2 call Asher put Medicare Advantage at "a little over 40% of segment revenue," and on the Q3 call he credited the segment's result to "both Medicare Advantage and PDP." For the equity, MA is small enough that it is a 2027 option rather than a 2026 driver.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
STATE CONTRACT INCUMBENCY SCALE IN GOVERNMENT PROGRAMMES 25.7M AT-RISK LIVES ADJ. SG&A RATIO 6.5% MULTI-STATE DIVERSIFICATION REGULATORY COMPLEXITY BARRIER $39.9B CASH & INVESTMENTS

The real advantage is unglamorous: administrative cost per member. Centene runs an SG&A ratio of 7.2% in Q3 2026, or 6.5% adjusted for severance and other cost-cutting charges, against 7.0% on both bases a year earlier (Q3 2026 release). In a business where the medical loss ratio is 90%+ and often regulated to a floor, the overhead line is one of the few levers a manager actually controls, and spreading it over 25.7 million lives across dozens of state contracts is genuinely hard to replicate. State Medicaid procurements also favour incumbents with operating history and provider networks already in place — switching costs sit with the state, not the member. What the moat emphatically does not do is protect the revenue line: rates are set by regulators and the risk-adjustment transfer is set by the market, so a wide administrative moat coexists with almost no pricing power.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Rate-setting lag is structural, not cyclical. Medicaid capitation rates are set state by state, by each state's Medicaid agency, on annual cycles, using data that is already a year old when filed. When the acuity of the remaining book moves faster than that cycle — as it did through the post-redetermination period — the insurer absorbs the gap for at least four quarters with no contractual remedy. This is not a bug that gets fixed; it is the shape of the business, and it can recur whenever enrolment mix shifts sharply.
▸
ACA risk adjustment depends on other insurers' books. The July 2025 event was not a pricing error in Centene's own membership — it was a mis-forecast of market-wide morbidity across 22 states, which is information the company does not possess until a third-party actuary publishes it. A single Wakely data release wiped out roughly 40% of the equity in one session. The same informational asymmetry exists every year.
▸
Political and legislative exposure is direct, not indirect. Essentially all of Centene's revenue comes from government programmes. Changes to Medicaid eligibility and funding, to enhanced ACA premium subsidies, or to Medicare Advantage benchmark and Star Ratings methodology alter the revenue base by legislative act. Management said on the Q2 call it was holding back 50bp of rate benefit against the cost of higher Medicaid membership losses, some of which London tied to an early start on OBBBA — policy risk showing up in the company's own plan.
▸
The adjusted-to-GAAP gap is wide and management-defined. FY2025 adjusted EPS was $2.08 against a GAAP loss of $(13.53). FY2026 guidance is >$5.20 adjusted against >$3.46 GAAP — a $1.74 per-share wedge, roughly a third of the adjusted number. In Q3 2026 alone the gap was $0.72 a share, mostly $276M of severance from cost cutting and contract exits. A $6.7B goodwill write-off and a $513M Magellan impairment are genuinely non-cash, but a company that has written off $7.2B in two quarters has limited standing to insist that only the adjusted figure counts.

RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has risen persistently and is described as "overbought"; below 30 it has fallen persistently and is "oversold". It is a description of momentum, not a forecast — and on a stock that lost half its value in one month, it says more about the violence of the move than about what comes next.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The instruction here is to set out what would have to be true in each direction rather than to call the outcome, and the two cases are unusually clean because they disagree about one testable thing: whether the 2026 margin recovery survives the removal of one-off items. Management has now put its own number on that: about $4.70 of adjusted EPS as the 2027 starting point, after taking out $0.50 of 2026 items it does not expect to recur.

What would have to be true for the recovery to hold

Medicaid HBR falls from the 93.5% management expects for 2026 as the January 1 rate resets — just over 40% of Medicaid revenue — land against a book it is deliberately shrinking 8–9%; Marketplace holds a pre-tax margin near 5% on the repriced cohort without another prior-year true-up; Medicare Advantage moves from break-even to better than break-even in 2027, as management guides; and PDP holds its 2–3% opening margin. If those hold, 2027 earnings build on the ~$4.70 base rather than shrinking from it, and at $67.05 the stock is 14.3x that base, on a business that has cut debt to about $15B and generated $3.94B of free cash flow over the last four quarters — and the 2025 de-rating was a cycle, not a repricing.

What would have to be true for the de-rating to be permanent

The 2026 numbers are partly borrowed from 2025: $180M of favourable risk-adjustment development, the Q2 settlement items and the $0.50 of 2026 earnings management itself does not expect to recur. Q3 showed consolidated HBR flat at 92.7% and Medicaid at 93.6%, worse than 93.4% a year earlier, so the core segment has not yet reached a level that generates normal margin; Marketplace bought its margin by shedding 2.4 million commercial members, which competitors can attack by pricing back in; PDP margin is guided down for 2027; and London's own concern is "a dislocation between rate and acuity" as Medicaid eligibility rules tighten. On this reading, government-programme margins are structurally lower post-OBBBA, and a $33.2B market cap on $204B of total revenues is the market correctly refusing to capitalise premium it does not believe converts to profit.

01
Earnings quality, not earnings level. The company tied ~$0.50 of Q2 2026 EPS to settlements of 2025 items, and the shares closed 0.3% lower on the day of a large beat. After Q3 management counts $0.50 of full-year 2026 earnings as unlikely to recur and starts 2027 from ~$4.70, below this year's >$5.20 floor. A repeat of the risk-adjustment shock. The July 2025 event was informational: a third-party actuary published market-wide morbidity data that invalidated the company's assumption, and ~40% of the equity disappeared in a session. Nothing about the structure of ACA risk adjustment has changed. Beta of 1.13 does not describe this risk.

Management said in July it was holding back 50bp of rate benefit against the cost of higher Medicaid membership losses, some of which London tied to an early start on OBBBA — a self-declared reserve against outcomes it cannot control — and just over 40% of Medicaid revenue rerates on January 1, 2027.
Industry Commentary
The Medicaid acuity overhang
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Post-pandemic Medicaid redeterminations removed millions of members from state rolls across the industry, and the members who left skewed healthy. The result is a smaller, sicker book priced on rates filed before the mix shifted. Centene's Medicaid HBR was 93.0% in Q4 2025, 93.9% in Q2 2026 and 93.6% in Q3 2026 — the segment covering 11.9 million members, 46.3% of at-risk lives, has not yet recovered. The July 2025 disclosure named the specific cost drivers: behavioural health, home health and high-cost drugs, with New York and Florida called out. The mechanism that fixes this is state rate updates arriving over 2026–27 — just over 40% of Centene's Medicaid revenue rerates on January 1, 2027 — which is why management is simultaneously guiding membership down 8–9% and, as of the Q2 call, holding back 50bp of rate benefit. It is a deliberate trade of volume for margin, and it takes years, not quarters.

ACA exchange economics after the 2025 repricing
ONGOING HEADWIND

Centene repriced its 2026 Marketplace book to a much higher baseline morbidity assumption after the Wakely shock and got what repricing gets you: a full-year Marketplace pre-tax margin at the top of its 4.5–5% range, and 2.4 million fewer commercial members — a 38% reduction year over year to 3.9 million at September 30, 2026. The open question for the whole exchange market is whether that margin is defensible. Risk adjustment is zero-sum within each state: if every carrier prices up together the market shrinks and margins hold; if one carrier prices back in for share, morbidity re-sorts and the transfer calculations move again. Enrolment for 2027, this coming winter, is where that gets decided; London expects the overall market to contract slightly. Note also that the size and composition of the subsidised enrolment pool is itself a policy variable.

Bull versus bear: the same numbers, two readings
EMERGING TAILWIND — CONTESTED

Consolidated HBR improved 340 basis points year over year in Q2 2026, to 89.6% from 93.0%, and was flat at 92.7% in Q3. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance has moved from ">$3.00" (February 2026) to ">$4.80" (July) to ">$5.20" (October 27, 2026) — a 73% increase in under nine months. The bull reads that as a rate cycle turning on schedule and a management team that set conservative milestones and cleared them. The bear reads the same sequence as a low bar, repeatedly reset, cleared with the help of $180M of prior-year risk-adjustment development and settlements — and points to management's own ~$4.70 starting point for 2027, about 10% below the 2026 floor. Analysts are split to match: 8 Buy, 12 Hold, 1 Sell on StockAnalysis, with targets from $56 to $85. The resolution is not a matter of opinion — it is the January 1 Medicaid rate resets and the first 2027 guidance, read on Medicaid HBR and on adjusted EPS without one-offs. Everything on this page reduces to those two lines.
