01 Company Overview

CN is one of Canada's two Class I freight railroads — the other is CPKC — running rail, intermodal and trucking services across Canada and into the United States. At a $74.4B market value (our calculation: 599.7M shares × the November 6 close) it is a little smaller than CPKC ($80.2B on the same basis). FY2025 revenue was C$17,304M, up 2%, on revenue ton-miles (RTMs) up just 1% to 238.2 billion; diluted EPS was C$7.57, up 8%. The newest quarter, Q3 2026 (July–September), brought revenue of C$4,771M, up 15% and diluted EPS of C$2.00, up 9% (adjusted C$2.16, up 18%).

A railroad is judged first on its operating ratio (OR) and its volumes. FY2025 was a cost story: volume barely grew, yet the OR improved 1.5 points to 61.9% (adjusted 61.7%). 2026 has been a volume story with rising costs. In Q2 2026 RTMs rose 5%, revenue 11%, and the OR worsened 0.8 points to 62.5% on a 60% jump in fuel expense. Q3 2026 repeated the pattern: RTMs rose 4%, revenue 15% (14% at constant currency, meaning with exchange-rate changes removed), but the reported OR worsened 2.3 points to 63.7%. Fuel expense rose 58% (+C$244M), while fuel surcharges billed to customers rose C$343M, more than half of the C$606M revenue gain. CN also booked a C$118M asset impairment for dispatching and back-office IT software it stopped developing. Excluding the impairment and merger-advisory costs, the adjusted OR was 61.0%, 0.4 points better than a year earlier. On October 30 management kept 2026 guidance — low single-digit RTM growth and mid-to-high single-digit adjusted EPS growth — and said adjusted EPS growth is now tracking toward the upper end of that range. It also now expects the 2026/2027 grain crops to be above their five-year averages, where in January it had assumed average.

The usual framing — a Canadian rail duopoly with CPKC, with tariffs and cross-border trade as the live risk — holds up only in part. The two-carrier structure is real. The tariff risk is real but narrow in the numbers so far. CN's Q3 MD&A ties lower forest-products volumes to higher cross-border tariffs and duties (forest-products RTMs −4% in Q3 and for the nine months), and forest products (−5%) and metals & minerals (−4%) were the two revenue declines in FY2025. But in Q3 2026 every commodity group grew revenue, forest products +10% and metals & minerals +18%. Chief commercial officer Janet Drysdale said on October 30 that CN has overcome last year's C$350M of tariff headwinds (Canadian Press, via BNN Bloomberg). Fuel cost, not tariffs, was the bigger 2026 swing factor in the reported numbers, on both the cost line and the surcharge line. The Q3 MD&A also flags two 2026 trade developments: on July 1 the US told its partners in the USMCA (the North American free-trade agreement) that it would not support renewing the deal in its current form, and in August Canada–US trade talks were suspended without agreement. At home, CN is a member of the S&P/TSX Composite; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500.

FX exposure: CN reports in Canadian dollars; the NYSE line trades in US dollars. Its network and workforce span both countries (23,822 employees at September 30, 2026), so a stronger Canadian dollar lowers the C$ value of its US-dollar revenue. The average rate was C$1.399 per US$ in Q3 2026 versus C$1.378 a year earlier, and C$1.385 versus C$1.400 for the nine months. CN's guidance assumes a Canadian dollar worth US$0.71 for the rest of 2026; the Bank of Canada rate for November 6, 2026 (US$1 = C$1.4113) implies US$0.7086.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Irreplaceable right-of-way Two Class I carriers in Canada Bulk franchise (grain, energy) Revenue per RTM up in all 7 groups in Q3 Low-cost long-haul vs truck 30 consecutive dividend raises

A railroad's moat is physical. Nobody will assemble a new transcontinental right-of-way through Canada, so the competitive set for most of CN's bulk traffic is one other railroad and, at shorter distances, the truck. In Q3 2026 revenue per RTM rose in all seven commodity groups (+6% to +14%), including +12% in intermodal on falling volume; CN's MD&A attributes the increase mainly to higher fuel surcharges, freight rate increases and a weaker Canadian dollar. The vulnerability is that a moat protects price, not volume — CN cannot make lumber mills ship more when US duties make Canadian lumber less competitive, and it cannot make grain crops bigger. CN's volume is set by what Canada produces and exports, and by trade policy.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Trade policy sets part of the volume. The MD&A lists "trade restrictions, trade barriers, or the imposition of tariffs" among its key risks and ties lower forest-products volumes to higher cross-border tariffs and duties (RTMs −4% in Q3). Forest products and metals & minerals — 22.1% of Q3 2026 revenue — were the two groups that shrank in FY2025, though both grew revenue in Q3 2026. The MD&A updates this risk for the US position on renewing the USMCA and for the August breakdown of Canada–US trade talks.
02
Costs moved faster than volume in 2026. Q3 2026 operating expenses rose 19% against revenue up 15%. Fuel alone rose 58% (+C$244M) and an IT-software write-down added C$118M. Fuel surcharge revenue rose C$343M, more than the C$244M rise in fuel expense, so the fuel bill was passed on to customers; the adjusted OR (61.0%) did improve 0.4 points, but the reported OR went from 61.4% to 63.7%.
03
The US rail map may be redrawn. Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern are pursuing a merger under US Surface Transportation Board review. CN spent C$48M in the first nine months of 2026 on "analysis and advocacy" for that review (C$14M in Q3). A combined UP–NS would be a far larger competitor, and a far larger partner at the points where CN hands US traffic to other railroads. CN signed two memoranda in July 2026: one with UP for new rights over UP's network on Canada–Mexico moves, which the MD&A says is not conditional on the merger (some of the rights need STB approval), and one with the merger applicants securing competitive access including Kansas City, which depends on STB approval and the merger closing.
04
Crop, fuel and labour cycles. Grain & fertilizers was the best-growing group in 2025, and its revenue is up 15% for the first nine months of 2026 (RTMs +12%) on higher Canadian and US grain exports, after 2025/2026 crops that beat their five-year averages; CN now expects an above-average 2026/2027 crop, but a normal one would remove the lift. Two labour agreements (about 6,000 train and engine employees and about 2,800 track workers) expire on December 31, 2026, with bargaining scheduled through December.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items move the reported figures. First, a C$118M pre-tax impairment (C$87M after tax, C$0.14 a share) in Q3 2026, for dispatching and back-office IT software components CN stopped developing; it accounts for C$0.14 of the C$0.16 gap between Q3 diluted EPS (C$2.00) and adjusted (C$2.16), and US rail-merger advisory costs are the other C$0.02. Third, rail-consolidation advisory costs on the UP–NS merger review: C$14M in Q3 and C$48M for the nine months, after C$15M in FY2025. Fourth, workforce-reduction costs of C$34M in FY2025. Buybacks also flatter per-share growth: CN repurchased about 15 million shares for C$2B in 2025, and about 3.9 million for C$676M in Q3 2026 (C$1,999M for the nine months); StockAnalysis shows the share count down 2.68% year on year, and CN had 599.7 million shares outstanding on October 29, 2026.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $126.57 to the November 6, 2026 settled close of $124.06, the NYSE line lost 2.0% on price over just under five years. Dividends would add to that. The path was not flat: a $134.14 peak in March 2022, a three-year range between roughly $106 and $132 into 2024, then a slide to a $93.45 low in July 2025 — the bottom of the window — and a 2026 recovery. Part of the price move for a US holder is currency, because the underlying earnings are in Canadian dollars.

The 6-period monthly RSI hit 26.3 in April 2025, the most oversold reading in the window, and sat at or near 70 from May to August 2026 (69.6 to 76.8) — overbought territory. September's 4.7% drop from the August close brought it back to 58.3, October's 4.0% gain lifted it to 64.5, and it stands at 65.0 at the November 6 close. The 10-month moving average is about $118.35, below the current price, so the long-term trend is still up.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 22.0x trailing earnings for one of two Class I railroads in Canada, with a 61.0% adjusted operating ratio in Q3, a 26.4% trailing net margin, 30 consecutive dividend increases and a buyback behind a 2.7% drop in the share count over a year. Volume has turned: RTMs rose 4% in Q3 2026 and for the nine months, after +1% in all of 2025, and adjusted EPS grew 18% in Q3, with management saying full-year adjusted EPS growth is tracking toward the upper end of its mid-to-high single-digit guidance. It now expects an above-average grain crop for 2026/2027. If fuel costs stabilise, each extra dollar of revenue should leave more profit behind. Analysts' average target of $135.74 is 9.4% above the price.

Bear Case

Revenue rose 15% in Q3 and the reported operating ratio still worsened 2.3 points to 63.7%: operating expenses rose 19%, with fuel up 58% and a C$118M software write-down. Even the adjusted ratio improved only 0.4 points on 15% more revenue. The volume leans on grain and energy, both cyclical, and CN's assumptions put oil at US$80–110 a barrel for the rest of 2026. Tariffs are visible in forest products (RTMs −4%). Two labour agreements expire on December 31, the CFO steps down in February, and a UP–NS merger would put a much larger railroad at the points where CN hands US traffic to other railroads. A stock that is down 2.0% on price over the window is not obviously cheap at 21.3x estimated 2026 earnings.

Industry Commentary
Tariffs and the cross-border freight base
LIVE RISK

For a Canadian railroad, trade policy is a volume input. CN's Q3 2026 MD&A lists "trade restrictions, trade barriers, or the imposition of tariffs" among the risks to its outlook, and ties lower forest-products volumes to higher cross-border tariffs and duties. The reported numbers show the damage is concentrated: FY2025 forest products −5% and metals & minerals −4%, while grain (+7%) and intermodal (+4%) grew. In Q3 2026 every group grew revenue, and chief commercial officer Janet Drysdale said CN has overcome last year's C$350M of tariff headwinds (Canadian Press, via BNN Bloomberg). The honest reading: tariffs have hit specific commodity lanes, not the whole network — so far.

US rail consolidation: the duopoly's neighbour is changing
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

The duopoly framing is about Canada: CN and CPKC are the country's two Class I railroads. The live structural question in 2026 is south of the border. Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern are seeking US Surface Transportation Board approval to combine, and CN has spent C$15M in FY2025 and C$48M in the first nine months of 2026 on "analysis and advocacy" for that review, which it excludes from adjusted results. A combined UP–NS would be much larger than CN (UP alone is $172.43B by market value versus CN's $74.40B, our calculation at the November 6 close). Whether that helps or hurts CN depends on the conditions the STB imposes on how railroads hand traffic to one another and reach each other's customers.

Volume is back; the operating ratio has not followed yet
EMERGING TAILWIND

For most of 2024–2025 CN's story was efficiency on flat volume: RTMs +1% in FY2025, OR down 1.5 points to 61.9%. 2026 turned the volume side — RTMs +4% for the nine months, guidance raised in July to low single-digit growth and kept in October — while fuel (+58% in Q3) and wage costs held the reported OR back (63.7% in Q3). The bull framing: the adjusted OR already improved 0.4 points in Q3 (61.0%) despite fuel, fuel surcharge revenue rose more than fuel expense in Q3 (+C$343M against +C$244M), and once costs settle, higher volume on a largely fixed network turns into profit. The bear framing: the volume came from big crops and energy, both cyclical, while the cost increases — general wage increases, higher incentive pay, and labour contracts reopening on December 31 — are sticky. Q4's operating ratio, due in early 2027, is the next data point.
