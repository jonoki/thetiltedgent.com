01 Company Overview

CN is one of Canada's two Class I freight railroads — the other is CPKC — running rail, intermodal and trucking services across Canada and into the United States. At a $71.9B market value it is the #15 company in Canada by market cap (companiesmarketcap's country ranking, three places behind CPKC at #12) and #351 in the world. FY2025 revenue was C$17,304M, up 2%, on revenue ton-miles (RTMs) up just 1% to 238.2 billion; diluted EPS was C$7.57, up 8%.

A railroad is judged first on its operating ratio and its volumes, and in 2026 those two moved in opposite directions. FY2025 was a cost story: volume barely grew, yet the OR improved 1.5 points to 61.9% (adjusted 61.7%), and Q4 2025's adjusted OR of 60.1% was 2.5 points better year on year. Q2 2026 (Apr–Jun 2026) was the reverse — a volume story. RTMs rose 5%, revenue rose 11% to C$4,753M, but the OR worsened 0.8 points to 62.5% because fuel expense rose 60% (+C$246M) and wages and incentive pay went up. On July 24 management raised 2026 guidance from "flattish" RTMs to low single-digit RTM growth, and from EPS growth "slightly exceeding" volume to mid-to-high single-digit adjusted EPS growth.

The premise supplied for this report — a Canadian rail duopoly with CPKC, with tariffs and cross-border trade as the live risk — holds up only in part. The two-carrier structure is real, but no fetched source gave a market-share split, so none is stated. The tariff risk is real but narrow in the numbers so far. CN's own Q2 MD&A names US anti-dumping and countervailing duties and Section 232 tariffs as the reason for weaker lumber shipments, and forest products (−5%) and metals & minerals (−4%) were the two revenue declines in FY2025. But in Q2 2026 both groups grew revenue again (+7% and +6%), and the fetched filings did not say how much of CN's traffic crosses the border. Fuel cost, not tariffs, was the bigger 2026 swing factor in the reported numbers. At home, CN is a constituent of the S&P/TSX Composite; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500.

FX exposure: CN reports in Canadian dollars; the NYSE line trades in US dollars. Its network and workforce span both countries (17,300 employees in Canada and 6,525 in the US at June 30, 2026), so a stronger Canadian dollar lowers the C$ value of its US-dollar revenue. The average rate was C$1.384 per US$ in Q2 2026 versus C$1.385 a year earlier, and C$1.378 versus C$1.411 for the first half. CN's 2026 plan assumed a Canadian dollar worth US$0.715; the Bank of Canada rate for September 21, 2026 (US$1 = C$1.4021) implies US$0.7132.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Irreplaceable right-of-way Two Class I carriers in Canada Bulk franchise (grain, energy) Pricing power (rev/RTM up in 6 of 7 groups) Low-cost long-haul vs truck 30 consecutive dividend raises

A railroad's moat is physical. Nobody will assemble a new transcontinental right-of-way through Canada, so the competitive set for most of CN's bulk traffic is one other railroad and, at shorter distances, the truck. Q2 2026 shows the pricing side of that: revenue per RTM rose in six of seven commodity groups (coal was flat), including +9% in intermodal on falling volume. The vulnerability is that a moat protects price, not volume — CN cannot make lumber mills ship more when US duties make Canadian lumber less competitive, and it cannot make grain crops bigger. CN's volume is set by what Canada produces and exports, and by trade policy.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Trade policy sets part of the volume. The MD&A lists "trade restrictions, trade barriers, or the imposition of tariffs" among its key risks and already blames US anti-dumping and countervailing duties and Section 232 tariffs for weaker Canadian lumber shipments. Forest products and metals & minerals — 21.5% of Q2 2026 revenue — were the two groups that shrank in FY2025.
02
Costs moved faster than volume in 2026. Q2 2026 operating expenses rose 13% against revenue up 11%. Fuel alone rose 60% (+C$246M). Fuel surcharges recover part of that over time, but the OR still went from 61.7% to 62.5%. The FY2025 OR gains came from cost control on flat volume; 2026 has so far given some of them back.
03
The US rail map may be redrawn. Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern are pursuing a merger under US Surface Transportation Board review. CN spent C$34M in H1 2026 on "analysis and advocacy" for that review. A combined UP–NS would be a far larger competitor and interchange partner for CN's US traffic.
04
Crop and commodity cycles. Grain & fertilizers was the best-growing group in 2025 and Q2 2026 because the 2025/2026 crops beat their five-year averages. CN's own assumption for 2026/2027 is an average crop.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $115.65 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $117.51, the NYSE line gained 1.6% on price over five years. Dividends would add to that; a total-return figure was not computed. The path was not flat: a $134.14 peak in March 2022, a three-year range between roughly $106 and $132 into 2024, then a slide to a $93.45 low in July 2025 — the bottom of the window — and a 2026 recovery. Part of the price move for a US holder is currency, because the underlying earnings are in Canadian dollars.

The 6-period monthly RSI hit 26.3 in April 2025, the most oversold reading in the window, and sat at or near 70 from May to August 2026 (69.6 to 76.8) — overbought territory. September's 5.8% drop from the August close brought it back to 55.9. The 10-month moving average is about $112.94, below the current price, so the long-term trend is still up; the short-term move is a pullback from overbought levels.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 21.1x trailing earnings — five points below the S&P 500's 26.1 — for one of two Class I railroads in Canada, with a 62% operating ratio, a 26.9% net margin, 30 consecutive dividend increases and a buyback that retired 2.3% of the shares in a year. Volume has turned: RTMs rose 5% in Q2 2026 after +1% in all of 2025, and management raised guidance to low single-digit RTM growth and mid-to-high single-digit adjusted EPS growth. Pricing held in six of seven commodity groups. If fuel costs stabilise and surcharges catch up, the OR returns toward FY2025's 61.9% on a higher volume base — the operating leverage that makes railroads compound. Analysts' average target of $139.33 is 18.6% above the price.

Bear Case

The best quarter for volume in years still produced a worse operating ratio: opex rose 13% against revenue up 11%. The volume came from an above-average grain crop and energy, and CN's own assumption is an average crop next year. Trade policy is already visible in CN's disclosures — US duties and Section 232 tariffs on Canadian lumber — and forest products and metals, 21.5% of revenue, both shrank in 2025. A UP–NS merger would put a much larger railroad on the other side of CN's US interchanges. Meanwhile a stock that has returned 1.6% on price in five years, and was overbought as recently as August, is not obviously cheap at 20x forward earnings.

Top 3 Risks

01
Tariffs and cross-border trade. CN names tariffs and trade barriers as a key risk and already attributes weaker lumber volumes to US duties. The fetched filings do not quantify the share of traffic that crosses the border, which is the missing number for sizing this risk.
02
Operating-ratio slippage. Q2 2026 OR 62.5% (+0.8 pts), H1 63.5% (+0.9 pts). Fuel +60% and wage increases outran an 11% revenue gain. If this persists into Q3, the FY2025 efficiency gains unwind while volume is growing.
03
US rail consolidation. The proposed UP–NS combination under STB review would change the competitive and interchange landscape for CN's US network. CN is already spending on advocacy (C$34M in H1 2026).
Industry Commentary
Tariffs and the cross-border freight base
LIVE RISK

For a Canadian railroad, trade policy is a volume input. CN's Q2 2026 MD&A lists "trade restrictions, trade barriers, or the imposition of tariffs or other changes to international trade arrangements" among the risks to its outlook, and cites "higher U.S. anti-dumping and countervailing duties as well as section 232 tariffs affecting Canadian lumber shipments to the U.S." as a reason for weaker forest-products volumes. The reported numbers show the damage is concentrated: FY2025 forest products −5% and metals & minerals −4%, while grain (+7%) and intermodal (+4%) grew. In Q2 2026 even those two groups grew revenue on price. The honest reading: tariffs have hit specific commodity lanes, not the whole network — so far. The number that would settle it, the share of CN revenue that crosses the Canada–US border, was not in the sources fetched.

US rail consolidation: the duopoly's neighbour is changing
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

The duopoly premise is about Canada: CN and CPKC are the country's two Class I railroads. The live structural question in 2026 is south of the border. Union Pacific and Norfolk Southern are seeking US Surface Transportation Board approval to combine, and CN has spent C$15M in FY2025 and C$34M in H1 2026 on "analysis and advocacy" for that review, which it excludes from adjusted results. A combined UP–NS would be much larger than CN (UP alone is $160.18B by market value versus CN's $71.08B at companiesmarketcap). Whether that helps or hurts CN depends on the conditions the STB imposes on interchange and access — not something the sources fetched resolve.

Volume is back; the operating ratio has not followed yet
EMERGING TAILWIND

For most of 2024–2025 CN's story was efficiency on flat volume: RTMs +1% in FY2025, OR down 1.5 points to 61.9%. Q2 2026 turned the volume side — RTMs +5%, guidance raised to low single-digit growth — but fuel (+60%) and wage costs kept the OR from improving. The bull framing: fuel surcharges recover fuel with a lag, record fuel efficiency (0.836 gallons per 1,000 GTMs) limits the damage, and once costs settle, higher volume on a fixed network drops through to margin. The bear framing: the volume came from a big crop and energy, both cyclical, while the cost increases — general wage increases, higher incentive pay — are sticky. Q3's operating ratio on October 30 is the next data point.
