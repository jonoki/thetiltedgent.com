01 Company Overview

Coinbase runs what its Q3 2026 presentation calls the "#1 platform for crypto stored globally": a retail brokerage and wallet, an institutional exchange, prime brokerage and custody, a derivatives exchange (its international exchange, combined with Deribit in October 2026), prediction markets, the USDC stablecoin partnership with Circle, staking ("blockchain rewards"), and the Base network. FY2025 net revenue was $6.88B, up 9.4% from $6.29B in FY2024, on total revenue of $7.18B and $5.2 trillion of trading volume (+156% YoY). Its market value is $36.49B (264.76M shares at the November 2, 2026 close). It joined the S&P 500 on May 19, 2025 (per S&P Dow Jones Indices) and is not in the Nasdaq-100.

The central debate is not whether Coinbase is a good business — it is which business you are buying. Transaction revenue rises and falls with how much people trade crypto: $590M in Q3 2026, down 1.5% from Q2 and 44% from $1,046M a year earlier. Subscription & services revenue was $570M, 49% of net revenue; it comes mostly from interest and fees and moves with interest rates and crypto prices rather than with trading. The reported profit swings with something else again. Coinbase holds crypto as an investment and books every change in its value through the income statement, so a $715.0M gain turned Q3's $61.7M of operating income into a GAAP net profit of $718.7M, after net losses in the three quarters before. Over the last four quarters the GAAP result is still a net loss of $701.6M. Section 04 takes that apart.

This is the line the market now values most, and it deserves the lead. Stablecoin revenue was $307M in Q3 2026, up 5% on Q2 — what Coinbase earns from its Circle partnership on the interest from USDC reserves, plus incentives on USDC held in Coinbase products. Average USDC held in Coinbase products hit a new all-time high of $22B, about 30% of USDC's average market value in the quarter (up from $15B a year earlier). Coinbase One, the paid consumer subscription bundling zero-fee trades and a higher USDC yield, reached a new all-time high in paid subscribers in Q3 2026 (the company gives no count). The margin profile is the point: stablecoin and custody revenue cost almost nothing extra to serve, which is the main reason gross margin is about 85%. Retail trading on the Coinbase app and Advanced Trade — the highest-fee revenue in the company and the most volatile. Read those facts together: Coinbase is winning share in a shrinking market. The newest product is prediction markets, contracts that pay out on the outcome of an event: they passed $200M of annualised revenue in Q3, with daily traders up more than 60% on Q2. Retail fees are under steady pressure from zero-fee brokers, offshore venues and Coinbase's own Coinbase One bundle, which trades per-order fees for a monthly subscription, shrinking this line to feed the one above it. That is a sensible strategy and a real drag on this line at the same time.

Institutional transaction revenue is small — $92M of a $1,160.5M quarter, down 8% on Q2 — because large clients pay a fraction of retail fees. Its importance is elsewhere: Coinbase is a custodian, holding the crypto, for US spot crypto ETFs, whose Q3 inflows, the presentation says, offset nearly all their outflows since last year, and that custody work helps put $377B of assets on the platform, 12.5% of the whole crypto market's value, at the end of Q3 2026. The core advantage is regulatory, not technological. Any competent engineering team can build a trading engine; very few can get a US-regulated company approved to hold an ETF's underlying assets, hold state money-transmitter licences across the country, and be the counterparty a pension fund's compliance department will sign off on. That is why Coinbase, whose retail fees are visibly higher than offshore venues', took its share of global crypto trading volume to 10.7% in Q3 2026, from 5.6% a year earlier. The point was made again on the day Coinbase reported: on Oct 29 Binance, an offshore exchange, announced that hackers had taken about $6.3B, which CNBC called the largest crypto theft on record and the FBI blamed on North Korea. The same standing that wins ETF custody work makes Coinbase the natural home for USDC, and USDC is what turns a cyclical brokerage into a $307M-a-quarter interest business with a gross margin of about 85%. Trust brings in customer balances, and the balances earn the half of revenue that does not depend on anyone trading.

Key Vulnerabilities

Interest-rate sensitivity dressed as recurring revenue. Stablecoin revenue — $307M in Q3 2026 and $1.35B in FY2025 — is at heart interest earned on USDC reserves. Investors value it like a growing subscription; it behaves more like the interest a bank earns on $22B of deposits, without the branches and insured accounts that keep a bank's deposits in place. A run of Fed rate cuts shrinks the most highly valued revenue line in the company without anything going wrong in the business.
Trading fees under pressure. Transaction revenue was 44% lower than a year earlier in a quarter when Coinbase's share of trading rose for a fourth straight quarter. Zero-fee competition, Coinbase One's own fee waivers and cheaper institutional pricing all push the average fee down. Winning a bigger slice of a shrinking, cheaper pie is not the same as growing.
The value of its own crypto makes GAAP earnings hard to read. Coinbase holds crypto as an investment and books every change in its market value through the income statement. Q3 2026 included a $715.0M gain on those holdings, which turned $61.7M of operating income into a $718.7M net profit; Q4 2025 included $718.2M of losses and turned $273.8M of operating income into a $666.7M net loss. The four-quarter net loss of $701.6M against $200.6M of operating income comes from this mechanism, and it cuts both ways, inflating results when crypto rises.
Custody assets rise and fall with the market. Assets on platform fell from $516B in Q3 2025 to $246B in Q2 2026, then recovered to $377B in Q3. Custody and ETF fees shrink exactly when trading revenue shrinks: the two "diversified" revenue streams share one driver, the price and popularity of crypto.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers and should be read out before drawing conclusions. First, the value of the crypto it holds. Coinbase carries crypto held for investment at market value and books the changes through earnings. Q3 2026 booked a $715.0M gain, lifting $61.7M of operating income to a $718.7M net profit; Q4 2025 booked $718.2M of losses, turning $273.8M of operating income into a $666.7M net loss; Q1 and Q2 2026 added losses of $482.4M and $209.5M. Quarterly diluted GAAP EPS over the last five quarters runs $1.50, −$2.49, −$1.49, −$1.36, $2.51 — a series that says almost nothing about the operating business. Second, the share count. The Q3 presentation counts 10.2M Class A shares repurchased for more than $2.0B, with $2.0B of authorisation left, and says buybacks have offset more than 85% of the shares issued as stock-based pay since Q4 2024; 264.76M shares were outstanding on Oct 22, 2026. Third, the adjusted figures. Management's preferred measures — adjusted EBITDA of $332.9M in Q3 2026 (the 15th straight positive quarter) and adjusted net income of $77.7M against the $718.7M GAAP profit — are the closest thing to a clean operating read, but adjusted EBITDA leaves out the $233.0M of stock-based pay, and both leave out the crypto gains and losses (adjusted net income also leaves out gains and losses on other investments), so they can flatter in the other direction. The honest summary: the operating business is thinly profitable and level with Q2; the swings in reported profit are mostly accounting.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY  ·  ⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOVEMBER 2, 2026
RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the asset has risen so fast that buyers may be exhausted ("overbought"); below 30 it has fallen so fast that sellers may be exhausted ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — on a 3.4-beta asset like this one it spends a lot of time at the extremes.

Five years of COIN is a lesson in what a 3.4 beta costs you (beta measures how far a stock swings relative to the market). From the November 2021 month-end close of $315.00 to the November 2, 2026 close of $137.83, the stock is down 56.2% in price, with no dividend to soften it. The ride inside that window was wilder: the stock fell 88.8% to $35.39 by December 2022, rose 967% to $377.76 by July 2025, then fell 61% to $146.19 by June 2026. It bounced to $188.12 at the end of August, then lost 23.0% in October to $143.54, and the November 2 close is 63.5% below the July 2025 month-end high. An investor's outcome in this name has depended almost entirely on entry date and almost not at all on the analysis in Sections 02 through 04.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Half the revenue no longer depends on anyone trading. Subscription & services reached $570M, 49% of net revenue, on a record $22B of average USDC and $377B of assets on platform — high-margin, repeat income built on a regulatory position competitors cannot easily copy, and the Binance hack is a fresh advertisement for it. Gross margin is about 85%, free cash flow is +$1.41B, adjusted EBITDA has been positive for 15 straight quarters, trading share rose for a fourth quarter to 10.7%, prediction markets passed $200M of annualised revenue, and management cut its 2026 expense range again. Costs are largely fixed ($4.15–4.30B of adjusted expenses this year), so if crypto trading recovers most of the extra revenue becomes profit, and at $137.83 the stock is 59.8% below its 52-week high.

Bear Case

Revenue is shrinking: total revenue fell 35% YoY in Q3 2026, and consensus expects 2026 revenue of $5.21B, down 24.3% (stockanalysis.com). The "diversification" is partly a rate bet — $307M a quarter of stablecoin revenue is interest income that rate cuts shrink. Assets on platform fell from $516B to $246B in three quarters before recovering, so custody income moves with the market too. The Q3 profit came from a $715M gain on crypto holdings, management calls Q4 "broadly flat" with Q3, and you pay 55.8x 2027 consensus earnings for a business with negative trailing EPS, a 3.41 beta and 10.8% of its float sold short — while the same crypto exposure can be bought directly.

Top 3 Risks

01
Rate-driven compression of the stablecoin line. The highest-quality, highest-multiple revenue stream in the company is interest on USDC reserves. A run of rate cuts shrinks $307M a quarter with no misstep by management and nothing it can do about it.
02
Correlated, not diversified, revenue. Transaction revenue, custody fees and staking rewards all scale with crypto prices and flows. Assets on platform halved between Q3 2025 and Q2 2026 while trading revenue fell — the shift in mix changes what investors are willing to pay, but not what the business is exposed to.
03
Valuation with no earnings floor. Trailing P/E is not meaningful and the forward P/E of 55.8 rests on 2027 consensus EPS of $2.47 after an expected 2026 loss. Analyst targets span $110 to $330 — there is no agreed anchor. On a 3.41-beta stock with 10.8% of its float sold short, position size matters more than the thesis.
Industry Commentary
The Crypto Volume Recession
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

FY2025 was a record: $5.2 trillion of trading volume, up 156%. FY2026 is the hangover. Total revenue has fallen year on year in every quarter of 2026, by 35% in Q3, and assets on platform went from $516B a year ago to $246B in Q2 before recovering to $377B. The uncomfortable detail is that Coinbase is executing well inside this — its share of crypto trading went from 5.6% to 10.7% over the year — and revenue fell anyway. When the market shrinks faster than your slice grows, good execution does not show up in the totals, and consensus has 2026 revenue down 24.3% to $5.21B before a recovery to $6.58B in 2027 (stockanalysis.com).

Stablecoins Go Mainstream — And Invite Competition
ONGOING HEADWIND

The same regulatory clarity that made USDC a legitimate corporate treasury instrument — and took Coinbase's stablecoin revenue to $1.35B in FY2025 and $307M in Q3 2026 — also invites banks and payment networks into a business whose whole model is to hold dollars, earn the short-term interest rate on them and share the interest. Coinbase's defence is distribution: about 30% of USDC sits on its platform (a record $22B on average in Q3), and Coinbase One pays members a higher USDC yield to keep balances there. That is a real moat, but it is a moat around an interest margin. The margin shrinks when rates fall and when new entrants pass more of the interest to holders — and both are plausible from here.

The Regulated-Custodian Franchise — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND

Frame the whole page through one number: 12.5% of the total crypto market's value sits on Coinbase's platform ($377B at the end of Q3 2026). The bull reading: that is an irreplaceable position. Every institution that wants crypto exposure through a regulated US entity — ETF issuers, corporate treasuries, wealth platforms — routes through infrastructure Coinbase already owns, and the revenue it generates is fee-based, high-margin and grows with adoption rather than with speculation. Coinbase One, staking and custody all compound off that base. The bear reading: 12.5% of a market's value is a percentage of a number that can halve. The $270B fall in assets on platform between Q3 2025 and Q2 2026 was not a competitive loss — it was the market moving. You are buying a superb toll booth on a road whose traffic you cannot forecast, at 55.8x 2027 consensus earnings with a 3.41 beta. Both readings are correct; which one you act on depends entirely on your view of crypto itself, which is the honest conclusion of this report.
