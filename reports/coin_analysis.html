01 Company Overview

Coinbase runs the largest regulated crypto-asset platform in the United States: a retail brokerage and wallet, an institutional exchange, prime brokerage and custody business, the USDC stablecoin partnership with Circle, staking ("blockchain rewards"), and the Base layer-2 network. FY2025 net revenue was $6.88B, up 9.4% from $6.29B in FY2024, on total revenue of $7.18B and $5.2 trillion of trading volume (+156% YoY). At a $45.45B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #547 globally. It is a member of the S&P 500 (added May 2025 — index membership per the report brief, not re-verified on a page fetched this session); it is not a Nasdaq-100 constituent, verified against the current 102-name NASDAQ-100 list on stockanalysis.com.

The central debate is not whether Coinbase is a good business — it is which business you are buying. Transaction revenue is a levered bet on crypto trading volume and has fallen 22% YoY to $599M in Q2 2026. Subscription & services revenue is now $555M, 48% of net revenue, and behaves like a spread-and-fee annuity rather than a cyclical. That mix shift is what the market is being asked to pay for — and in the last four quarters it has not been enough: Coinbase has printed a GAAP net loss in three of them, −$987.8M on a trailing-twelve-month basis, largely because the crypto it holds on its own balance sheet is marked to fair value through the income statement. Section 04 takes that apart.

BRAND / TRUST PREMIUM

The core advantage is regulatory, not technological. Any competent engineering team can build a matching engine; almost none can get a US-regulated entity approved to custody an ETF's underlying assets, hold state money-transmitter licences across the country, and be the counterparty a pension fund's compliance department will sign off on. That is why Coinbase, whose retail fees are visibly higher than offshore venues', still took its crypto trading volume share to an all-time-high 10.3% in Q2 2026. The second-order effect is the flywheel that actually matters: the same regulatory standing that wins ETF custody mandates is what makes Coinbase the natural home for USDC, and USDC is what turns a cyclical brokerage into a $292M-a-quarter spread business with 85.8% gross margin. The moat converts trust into float, and float into the half of the P&L that does not depend on anyone trading.

Key Vulnerabilities

Interest-rate sensitivity dressed as recurring revenue. Stablecoin revenue — $292M in Q2 2026 and $1.35B in FY2025 — is fundamentally interest earned on USDC reserves. The market is capitalising it at a growth multiple; it is closer to a bank's net interest income on a $20B deposit base with no deposit franchise protecting it. A sustained easing cycle compresses the highest-multiple line in the company without any operational failure at all.
Take-rate compression in the trading business. Transaction revenue fell 22% YoY in a quarter when Coinbase's share hit an all-time high. Zero-fee competition, Coinbase One's own fee waivers, and institutional pricing all push the blended take rate down. Winning a bigger slice of a shrinking, cheaper pie is not the same as growing.
Balance-sheet marks make GAAP earnings uninterpretable. Coinbase holds crypto for investment and marks it to fair value through the income statement. Q2 2026 included a $209.5M gain on those holdings and still produced a $359M net loss; Q4 2025 included $718M of unrealised crypto losses and turned a positive operating quarter into a $667M net loss. The TTM headline loss of $987.8M against $445.9M of positive operating income is entirely an artefact of this mechanism — and it cuts both ways, inflating results in up-quarters.
Custody assets are pro-cyclical, not sticky. Assets on platform dropped from $294B to $246B in a single quarter on Bitcoin ETF outflows. The custody and ETF-servicing annuity shrinks exactly when trading revenue shrinks — the two "diversified" revenue streams share the same underlying driver, which is the price and popularity of crypto.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers and should be read out before drawing conclusions. First, crypto fair-value marks. Coinbase carries crypto assets held for investment at fair value through earnings. Q4 2025 absorbed $718M of unrealised losses, flipping $193M of positive operating income into a $667M net loss; Q2 2026 contained a $209.5M gain on the same holdings and still produced a $359M net loss. Quarterly GAAP EPS over the last six quarters runs $0.24, $5.14, $1.50, −$2.49, −$1.49, −$1.36 — a series with essentially no signal about the operating business. Second, the Q2 2025 outlier. That quarter's $1.429B of net income on $1.397B of revenue is a net margin above 100%, which is arithmetically only possible through non-operating gains; treat any "year-ago comparison" against Q2 2025 net income as meaningless. Third, the adjusted bridge. Management's own preferred metrics — adjusted EBITDA of $208M in Q2 2026 (the 14th consecutive positive quarter) and an adjusted net loss of $105M against the $359M GAAP loss — are the closest thing to a clean operating read, but they exclude stock-based compensation and the marks, so they flatter in the other direction. The honest summary: the operating business is modestly profitable and shrinking; the reported loss is mostly accounting.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the asset has risen so fast that buyers may be exhausted ("overbought"); below 30 it has fallen so fast that sellers may be exhausted ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Half the revenue no longer depends on anyone trading. Subscription & services reached $555M, 48% of net revenue, on $20B of average USDC and $246B of assets on platform — a high-margin annuity built on a regulatory position competitors cannot replicate. Gross margin is 85.8%, free cash flow is +$1.71B, adjusted EBITDA has been positive for 14 straight quarters, and trading share hit an all-time-high 10.3%. If crypto volumes normalise at all, the operating leverage on a $4.2–4.45B annual expense base is enormous, and at 2.4x the June low the stock is still 57% below its 52-week high.

Bear Case

Revenue is shrinking 9.2% on a TTM basis and fell 17.3% YoY in the most recent quarter; consensus expects 2026 revenue down 23%. The "diversification" is a rate bet — $292M a quarter of stablecoin revenue is interest income that easing compresses mechanically. Custody assets fell 16% in one quarter on ETF outflows, proving the annuity is pro-cyclical. You pay 142.5x forward earnings and a 11.9 PEG for a business with negative trailing EPS, a 3.39 beta and 8.7% short interest — and the underlying exposure is available directly, without the equity risk premium.

Top 3 Risks

01
Rate-driven compression of the stablecoin line. The highest-quality, highest-multiple revenue stream in the company is interest on USDC reserves. A sustained easing cycle shrinks $292M a quarter with no operational misstep and no management remedy.
02
Correlated, not diversified, revenue. Transaction revenue, custody fees and staking rewards all scale with crypto prices and flows. Q2 2026 showed all three deteriorating together — the mix shift changes the multiple the market is willing to pay, but it does not change what the business is exposed to.
03
Valuation with no earnings floor. Trailing P/E is not meaningful, forward P/E is 142.5 and PEG is 11.9. Analyst targets span $95 to $330 — the market has no agreed anchor. On a 3.39-beta stock with 8.7% short float, position sizing matters more than the thesis.
Industry Commentary
The Crypto Volume Recession
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

FY2025 was a record: $5.2 trillion of trading volume, up 156%. FY2026 is the hangover. Q1 2026 revenue fell 30.9% YoY, Q2 fell 17.3%, and Q3 guidance implied a slow start with only ~$130M of transaction revenue through July 26. Assets on platform dropped from $294B to $246B in one quarter on Bitcoin ETF outflows. The uncomfortable detail is that Coinbase is executing well inside this — share went from 9.1% to an all-time-high 10.3% — and revenue fell anyway. When the pie shrinks faster than your slice grows, operational excellence does not show up in the numbers, and consensus has 2026 revenue down 23% to $5.30B before a recovery to $6.77B in 2027.

Stablecoins Go Mainstream — And Invite Competition
ONGOING HEADWIND

The same regulatory clarity that made USDC a legitimate corporate treasury instrument — and took Coinbase's stablecoin revenue to $1.35B in FY2025 and $292M in Q2 2026 — also invites banks and payment networks into a business whose entire economics are "hold dollars, earn the short rate, share the spread." Coinbase's defence is distribution: more than 30% of USDC in circulation sits on its platform, USDC is 79% of stablecoin transaction volume year-to-date, and Coinbase One pays members enhanced USDC yield to keep balances there. That is a real moat, but it is a moat around a spread. Spreads compress when rates fall and when new entrants pass more of the yield to holders — and both are plausible from here.

The Regulated-Custodian Franchise — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND

Frame the whole page through one number: 11.2% of the total crypto market capitalisation sits on Coinbase's platform. The bull reading: that is an irreplaceable position. Every institution that wants crypto exposure through a regulated US entity — ETF issuers, corporate treasuries, wealth platforms — routes through infrastructure Coinbase already owns, and the revenue it generates is fee-based, high-margin and grows with adoption rather than with speculation. Coinbase One, staking and custody all compound off that base. The bear reading: 11.2% of a market cap is a percentage of a number that halves. The $48B decline in assets on platform in a single quarter was not a competitive loss — it was the market moving. You are buying a superb toll booth on a road whose traffic you cannot forecast, at 142.5x forward earnings with a 3.39 beta. Both readings are correct; which one you act on depends entirely on your view of crypto itself, which is the honest conclusion of this report.
