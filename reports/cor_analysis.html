01 Company Overview

Start with the margin, not the revenue, or you will misread this company entirely. Cencora's fiscal 2026 (year ended September 30, 2026) gross margin is 4.09% — 409 basis points (stockanalysis.com basis), and its adjusted operating margin is 1.43% (company release). Net margin is 1.03%. Against that, revenue of $337.4B (+5.0%) is one of the largest revenue lines in American business — but it buys a market capitalisation of only $65.98B, or 0.20× sales. Cencora is the second-largest of the three companies that move essentially every prescription drug in the United States, #189 in the S&P 500 by market value, and it does about $6.6M of revenue per employee (more than 51,000 employees at September 30, 2025, per the 10-K) — a number that tells you this is a logistics utility, not a manufacturer.

The central debate on this page is therefore not "will revenue grow." Revenue growth is the least informative number Cencora publishes. A distributor earns a small negotiated spread plus fee-for-service compensation on branded drugs, and a materially better margin on generics. So the earnings engine is generic mix, generic sourcing economics and manufacturer list-price increases — not volume. The September 2026 quarter shows how far the two can part: revenue rose 5.5% to $88.4B while adjusted operating income rose 22.1% to $1.25B. In the U.S. segment the release credits higher gross profit on drug sales and from OneOncology, bought in February, and it says sales of GLP-1 drugs lifted revenue while carrying lower gross profit margins. Everything else — customer concentration, the Walgreens relationship, opioid liabilities, the push into specialty drugs and cancer-practice management (OneOncology bought in February 2026, American Oncology Network agreed in October 2026) and the planned exit from animal health — is a question of whether Cencora can add margin dollars faster than the mix erodes them.

HOW A DISTRIBUTOR ACTUALLY MAKES MONEY

Cencora does not buy drugs low and sell them high in any meaningful sense. On a branded drug it takes title, warehouses it, and delivers it, earning a negotiated discount off the manufacturer's list price plus explicit fee-for-service compensation — distribution service agreements that pay a fixed percentage of list for handling, data and inventory services. Crucially, a portion of that compensation is tied to the drug's price, so when manufacturers raise list prices, distributor gross profit rises on the same physical volume. This is the single most under-appreciated driver of the sector's earnings, and it is entirely outside Cencora's control.

On a generic drug the economics invert. There is no list price to index to, but there are dozens of interchangeable manufacturers competing for shelf space, so the distributor's scale purchasing power translates into a real buying advantage and a materially wider percentage margin. Generics are a small fraction of revenue dollars and a large fraction of gross profit dollars. The offsetting force is generic deflation — prices in the category grind downward year after year, shrinking the base the margin is earned on.

Put those together and you get the rule that governs this stock: generic mix and manufacturer price increases drive earnings; revenue growth barely does. A quarter in which volumes surge on low-margin branded products — which is what GLP-1 growth is — will show excellent revenue and mediocre profit. A quarter with strong generic launches and healthy branded price inflation will show unremarkable revenue and excellent profit. The September 2026 quarter delivered 5.4% U.S. revenue growth and 22.0% U.S. operating income growth, with the OneOncology purchase among the reasons the release gives for the profit growth.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
THREE-PLAYER OLIGOPOLY SCALE PURCHASING NEGATIVE WORKING CAPITAL GENERIC SOURCING JV COLD-CHAIN & SPECIALTY DEA/STATE LICENSING SWITCHING COSTS

The moat is structural, not clever. McKesson, Cencora and Cardinal Health held an estimated 95% of the U.S. drug distribution channel in 2018 (Drug Channels) and booked roughly $995B of company-wide revenue between them in their latest fiscal years. Nobody enters this market: it requires a national network of temperature-controlled distribution centres, DEA and 50-state licensing, controlled-substance monitoring systems, and the willingness to run a business at a 1% operating margin. The economics only work at scale, and scale here is a purchasing weapon — the larger buyer sources generics cheaper, and generics are where the margin lives. Cencora also has a generics purchasing arrangement with Walgreens, which enlarges the buying pool beyond its own volumes.

The second, less-discussed advantage is the balance sheet mechanics. Distributors collect from customers faster than they pay manufacturers, which means the business runs on negative working capital — growth funds itself. That is how a company with just $3.68B of stockholders' equity (September 30, 2026) supports a $337.4B revenue base and generates $3.88B of free cash flow in fiscal 2026. It is also why the ROE reads at an absurd 129.01% and the debt-to-equity at 3.06 — the equity base is small next to the profit and the debt, so both ratios are artefacts of capital structure rather than statements about profitability or risk. ROIC of 28.62% (stockanalysis.com) is the more honest number, and it is genuinely good.

Three companies, one business model, and a striking amount of dispersion in a year. Cardinal Health is up 33.5% and McKesson 29.1% in price so far in 2026, while Cencora is up 2.4% — despite delivering adjusted EPS above the top of its guidance range. The bull argument for COR is essentially that it is cheap next to its peers: same oligopoly, same structural economics, strong return on invested capital, and the lowest forward P/E. The bear argument is that the discount is deserved because Walgreens and Boots are about a quarter of Cencora's revenue and its fiscal 2027 guide of 8% to 10% adjusted EPS growth sits below the 10% to 14% long-term target the company reaffirmed on the same day. Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Customer concentration is extreme, and it is the single most important fact about this company. In the fiscal 2025 Form 10-K, Walgreens and Boots together were approximately 25% of revenue, Evernorth Health Services approximately 13%, and the top ten customers, including governmental agencies, approximately 66%. Two customers were therefore about 38% of the top line. The U.S. distribution and generics purchasing agreements with Walgreens have a stated term to 2029 and the Boots supply agreement to 2031. Losing or repricing either relationship would not dent this business — it would reshape it.
▸
Walgreens has a new owner. Sycamore Partners, a private equity firm, acquired Walgreens Boots Alliance on August 28, 2025 (fiscal 2025 10-K). Walgreens Boots Alliance ceased to control more than 5% of Cencora's stock in June 2025 (January 2026 proxy statement), so the equity alignment that once made this the most stable customer relationship in the industry has faded; what remains is a commercial contract with an expiry date and a counterparty whose owners and priorities have changed.
▸
Earnings depend on prices Cencora does not control. Branded fee-for-service compensation is partly indexed to manufacturer list-price increases; generic margins erode with ongoing generic deflation. The fourth-quarter release cites a decline in manufacturer prices on certain brand drugs as a drag on revenue. A policy environment that suppresses U.S. drug list prices compresses distributor gross profit as a direct mechanical consequence, not as a second-order effect.
▸
Disintermediation risk in exactly the fastest-growing category. The release says GLP-1 sales lifted revenue while carrying lower gross profit margins. If manufacturers of those drugs route more volume directly to patients, outside the traditional wholesaler-to-pharmacy channel, the revenue line and the profit line could move apart in the unhelpful direction.
▸
Opioid liabilities are still real cash. The three distributors agreed to pay up to $21B over 18 years under the national settlements reached in 2021. Cencora's own accrued liability was $4.3B at September 30, 2025, to be paid over 13 years (fiscal 2025 10-K), and those payments continue to run through operating cash flow. The direction of travel has recently been favourable — fiscal 2026 included a $102.0M reduction of the opioid liability on dismissal of litigation — but the liability is a multi-year cash drag on a business whose entire equity base is $3.68B.

Notable One-Off Items

There are several, they are large relative to a $3.48B net income line, and they run in both directions. This year they roughly cancelled: GAAP EPS of $17.95 is $0.02 below adjusted EPS of $17.97. Quarter by quarter they do not: fourth-quarter GAAP EPS was $2.71 against $4.66 adjusted.

+$1.1B gain on the OneOncology acquisition (fiscal 2026). When Cencora acquired the remaining stake in OneOncology (February 2, 2026), accounting rules required it to mark its existing minority holding to fair value and book the difference as a gain. It is non-cash and non-recurring.

Other gains in fiscal 2026 (release): a $387.5M credit from the LIFO inventory-costing method, $72.5M of antitrust litigation settlement gains, a $102.0M reduction of the opioid liability on dismissal of litigation and an $86.8M credit from a derivative lawsuit settlement. These are reserve releases and settlements, not operating profit.

Charges in fiscal 2026 (release): $533.0M of deal and integration expenses, $180.8M of restructuring and a $249.5M impairment on the U.S. Consulting Services business, divested in April 2026. In the fourth quarter alone, deal and integration expenses were $177.4M, restructuring $65.1M and acquisition-related amortisation $116.0M, and the tax line carried a $146.8M unfavourable valuation adjustment on Swiss deferred tax assets, partly offset by an $87.1M benefit tied to the pending MWI Animal Health divestiture.

−$723.9M goodwill impairment on PharmaLex (quarter ended September 30, 2025), which produced a GAAP net loss of $339.7M (−$1.75 per share) in that quarter and explains why fiscal 2025 GAAP EPS was only $7.96.

Net effect: fiscal 2026 adjusted diluted EPS was $17.97, up 12.3% from $16.00, and adjusted net income was $3.49B, up 11.7%. The adjusted number is the one that describes the business. The 12.3% is the real growth rate.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $115.75 to the November 9, 2026 close of $345.78, COR returned +198.7% in price alone — a compound annual rate of about 24.8% over 4.94 years, before dividends. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A three-company oligopoly in a non-cyclical, volume-growing market, generating 28.62% ROIC and $3.88B of free cash flow on a $3.68B equity base, trading at 17.60× forward earnings — 19% below McKesson and 15% below Cardinal Health. Fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS came in above the top of its guidance range at $17.97 (+12.3%), the September quarter's adjusted EPS grew 21.4%, and the company is guiding fiscal 2027 to $19.40–$19.75 with a 10% to 14% long-term EPS growth target reaffirmed. The mix is moving toward specialty and cancer-practice management, guided to 18% to 21% operating income growth next year. Litigation and opioid costs were a net credit of $148.9M in fiscal 2026, the dividend just rose 10%, and the stock is the 2026 laggard of a sector that has worked.

Bear Case

You are buying 103 basis points of net margin with about 38% of revenue concentrated in two customers, one of which — Walgreens and Boots, about 25% of revenue in fiscal 2025 — has a new private-equity owner and a distribution contract expiring in 2029. Earnings depend on manufacturer list-price increases and generic sourcing spreads, neither of which Cencora sets, in a policy environment explicitly aimed at lowering U.S. drug prices. GLP-1 drugs lifted revenue at lower gross profit margins (release), and more of that volume could move to manufacturers' direct-to-patient channels. Debt is $11.27B against $3.68B of equity, with a $2.4B acquisition pending and multi-year opioid instalments still to pay. And the fiscal 2027 guide of 8% to 10% adjusted EPS growth is below the company's own 10% to 14% long-term target, with the MWI Animal Health merger an assumed $0.35 per share drag, according to the CFO on the November 4 call.

Top 3 Risks

01
Walgreens contract renewal and Walgreens' own condition. Roughly a quarter of revenue sits with one customer group, under an agreement with a stated term to 2029 and a new private owner. Any renegotiation on worse terms compresses gross profit on a base where a 10 basis point move is worth ~$337M — about 10% of net income.
02
Drug-pricing policy compressing the spread. Distributor gross profit is mechanically linked to branded list-price inflation and to generic sourcing economics. Reform aimed at lowering U.S. list prices reduces the fee base directly, and there is no volume response that offsets it — you cannot sell more units to compensate for a smaller percentage of a smaller number.
03
Channel disintermediation in GLP-1 and specialty. The categories driving growth are the ones manufacturers have the strongest incentive and capability to route directly to patients and providers. Cencora's answer is to own the provider (OneOncology, and AON if the deal closes), which is sound — but it is early, and carries policy risk of its own through physician reimbursement.
  Industry Commentary
The Oligopoly Is the Industry
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
McKesson, Cencora and Cardinal Health booked roughly $995B of company-wide revenue between them in their latest fiscal years, and Drug Channels estimated their combined share of the U.S. drug distribution channel at 95% in 2018. This is not a market with competitive dynamics in the usual sense — it is a regulated-utility structure in private hands. The consequence cuts both ways: no new entrant can threaten the position, and no participant can raise price, because the customers on the other side of the table are themselves concentrated — Cencora's top ten customers were about 66% of its fiscal 2025 revenue. Margins sit at 1% because two concentrated groups are negotiating with each other and neither can win. That equilibrium has held for two decades and is the deepest reason to own these businesses — and the reason none of them will ever be valued like a software company.
GLP-1: More Revenue, Lower Margin
ONGOING HEADWIND
The GLP-1 class shows the distributor model plainly. The fourth-quarter release says increased sales of GLP-1 drugs lifted revenue and that these products carry lower gross profit margins. So revenue grows while each dollar of it earns less. Any investor watching the top line here is watching the wrong variable. McKesson (+8.2% trailing revenue growth) and Cardinal (+10.0%) are both growing revenue faster than Cencora, and neither is converting it at a meaningfully better net rate (net margins of 1.12% and 0.71% against Cencora's 1.03%).
Vertical Integration Into the Provider
EMERGING TAILWIND
All three distributors are buying into physician groups (Pharmaceutical Commerce, November 5, 2026): if you cannot widen the spread, buy the customer. Cencora owns OneOncology (acquired February 2, 2026) and has agreed to buy American Oncology Network, a physician-led network of more than 350 oncology providers. The prize is the buy-and-bill channel, where practices buy the drug and bill the insurer. Bull framing: the company guides its specialty segment to 18% to 21% operating income growth in fiscal 2027, against 2% to 4% for its distribution segment; if this scales, the consolidated gross margin genuinely improves rather than merely being relabelled. Bear framing: Cencora's last diversification away from pure distribution — PharmaLex and U.S. Consulting Services — produced a $723.9M impairment in the September 2025 quarter and a $249.5M impairment in fiscal 2026; U.S. Consulting Services has been sold, and the other PharmaLex parts are split between the specialty segment and the businesses under review. Owning physician practices exchanges customer-concentration risk for reimbursement-policy risk, and the accounting benefit (consolidating high-margin service revenue) arrives years before the strategic benefit can be verified.
