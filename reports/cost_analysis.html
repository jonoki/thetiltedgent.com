01 Company Overview

Costco Wholesale operates 931 membership-only warehouse clubs worldwide (639 in the US/Puerto Rico, 115 in Canada, 177 across Mexico, Japan, the UK, Korea, Taiwan, Australia, China and Europe), selling a deliberately limited assortment (~4,000 SKUs) at razor-thin markups and monetizing loyalty through membership fees. It is the world's largest warehouse club operator by a wide margin — roughly 64% of the US club channel — and the third-largest retailer globally behind Walmart and Amazon. In fiscal 2025 (ended August 31, 2025), total revenue reached $275.2B, up 8.1% YoY, with net income of $8.1B (+10.0%); through 36 weeks of FY2026, net sales are running +9.6% with adjusted comparable sales of +6.6%.

Executive-Member Flywheel

Costco's moat is a self-reinforcing loop that is easy to describe and nearly impossible to copy at scale: 82.9M paid members pre-pay for the right to shop, which lets Costco cap merchandise markups at roughly 11% (14–15% for Kirkland) and still earn its profit from fees. Ultra-low prices drive volume; volume gives Costco the largest per-SKU purchasing power in retail (~4,000 SKUs vs. 100,000+ at Walmart supercenters, concentrating billions of dollars on each item); that buying power funds even lower prices. Kirkland Signature — an estimated $85–90B+ brand — disciplines national-brand suppliers and deepens the price gap. Bare-bones warehouses (concrete floors, pallet displays, ~$6.5B FY26 capex mostly for growth), minimal advertising, and industry-leading wages/retention keep SG&A near 9% of sales, less than half of typical grocers. The result: a retailer earning ~3% net margins that compounds ROE near 30%.

Key Vulnerabilities

Valuation premium. At ~48× trailing and ~43× forward earnings — roughly double Walmart's forward multiple and triple the broad discount-retail group — COST is priced like a software compounder while growing EPS ~10–14%. The stock fell ~22% from its February 2025 peak to its January 2026 low of $844 on essentially no bad news: multiple compression alone is the single largest risk to holders.

A rearmed Sam's Club. Walmart is investing aggressively in Sam's Club — "Just Go Out" checkout-free tech, club remodels, an accelerated ~30-clubs/yr opening cadence, and a $50 entry membership undercutting Costco's $65. Sam's has matched Costco's ~6% US comps recently; sustained share gains would pressure Costco's fee-increase cadence and treasure-hunt exclusivity.

E-commerce & convenience disruption. Digitally-enabled sales are growing ~21% but remain ~8–10% of revenue; the model still depends on members driving to a warehouse. Amazon same-day grocery, Walmart's delivery density, and instant-commerce players chip at the "stock-up trip" behavior the club format monetizes, especially with younger households.

Thin-margin sensitivity. With a ~12.9% gross margin and ~3% net margin by design, small external shocks — tariffs on the ~⅓ of US merchandise that is imported, gasoline price swings, wage inflation (Costco raised top-of-scale pay above $30/hr in 2025), or FX (a quarter of sales are non-USD) — move EPS disproportionately. Costco absorbs rather than passes on costs to defend price perception, so shocks land on the P&L first.

Notable One-Off Items

Costco's P&L is unusually clean — no goodwill write-downs, restructurings, or acquisition noise. Items to adjust for: (1) Membership fee increase timing — the Sep 2024 hike ($65/$130) is recognized ratably, so FY2025–FY2026 membership fee growth (+10–13%) is temporarily boosted by roughly 2–3 points vs. the organic member-growth run-rate; this tailwind fades by early FY2027. (2) Special dividends — none since the $15/share (~$6.7B) payout declared Dec 2023; the $19.9B cash pile (up 34% since fiscal year-end) has analysts handicapping another. (3) Gasoline & FX — record gas gallon volumes flattered FY26 reported comps (unadjusted +9.8% vs. +6.6% adjusted in Q3); always use ex-gas/ex-FX comps. (4) Litigation — a July 2026 class action alleging heavy metals in Kirkland protein powder is a headline risk but immaterial financially at this stage.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The best subscription business in retail keeps compounding: 82.9M paid members renewing at 92.2%, fees (~51% of operating income) growing 12%+, comps running +6–9% against soft retail, and a ~300-warehouse global runway. Fee-hike optionality, a probable special dividend from $19.9B of cash, and untouched pricing power mean low-teens EPS growth is highly repeatable — and premium multiples historically revert upward for COST after resets like Jan 2026's $844.

Bear Case

You're paying ~48× trailing / ~43× forward earnings and a ~4.0 PEG for a 3%-margin grocer growing EPS ~12%. The 2025 de-rating (−22% on zero bad news) showed what multiple compression alone can do; a comp slowdown toward +4%, a resurgent $50-entry Sam's Club, or tariff-driven margin pressure could compress the multiple toward 35× — implying $700s even with earnings intact.

01
Valuation / multiple compression. At ~43× forward earnings vs. a ~28× index, COST prices in flawless execution; history (May–Dec 2022, Feb 2025–Jan 2026) shows 20%+ drawdowns arrive without any fundamental deterioration.
02
Competitive escalation from Sam's Club. Walmart's checkout-free tech, remodels, accelerated openings, and $50 entry fee directly target Costco's value perception; sustained comp parity would erode the case for future fee increases.
03
Thin-margin shock exposure. Tariffs on ~⅓ of US merchandise, fuel price swings, FX (~25%+ of sales non-USD), and wage inflation hit a 3%-net-margin P&L hard — and Costco's philosophy is to eat costs before repricing members.
  Industry Commentary — What Matters Right Now
Tariffs & Global Sourcing Squeeze
ONGOING HEADWIND
Roughly one-third of Costco's US merchandise is imported, with meaningful exposure to China, Mexico, and Canada. Through FY2026 management has mitigated via re-sourcing (shifting Kirkland production closer to end markets), pulling forward buys, leaning on suppliers, and selectively eating cost — including CEO Vachris's member-friendly pledge to roll back prices where tariff costs reversed. The tell in the numbers: gross margin has held near 12.9% while comps ran high-single-digit, meaning tariffs so far are a rounding error, not a crisis. But each new tariff round hits a P&L with only ~300bps of net margin to absorb it — this stays a watch item into FY2027.
Consumer Trade-Down & the High-Income Member Wave
EMERGING TAILWIND
Warehouse clubs are the structural winner of a value-seeking consumer: the US club channel grew ~12.6% in 2024–25 versus low-single-digit broader retail, and Costco's sign-ups skew increasingly toward $125k+ household incomes — trade-down at the top, not the bottom. Executive membership penetration (~47% of paid members, 73.6% of sales) keeps mixing higher, e-commerce comps are compounding ~21%, and gold bars/gas/pharmacy keep pulling trip frequency. As long as grocery prices stay elevated, the $65 membership is the easiest ROI in a household budget — which is exactly why paid members grew from 81.4M to 82.9M in just three quarters.
Membership-Fee Economics: The Bull/Bear Fulcrum
DECIDING FACTOR
Everything about the COST debate reduces to one question: how durable and how expandable is the fee annuity? Bulls: $5.3B+ of ~100%-margin fees renewing at 92.2%, a fee hike every ~6 years (next likely ~2029–2031, or earlier), member growth of ~6%/yr, and 300 more warehouses — that's a bond-plus-growth profile deserving 40×+. Bears: the annuity is already fully priced at ~43× forward; Sam's Club at $50 and Walmart+ at $98 cap fee-raising power, and when the flywheel is this well understood, the marginal buyer is paying peak multiple for decelerating incremental returns. The Jan 2026 $844 low and May 2026 $1,093 high mark the market's live bid/ask on that exact argument.
