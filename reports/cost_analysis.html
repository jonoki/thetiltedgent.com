01 Company Overview

Costco Wholesale runs 939 membership-only warehouse clubs (647 in the US and Puerto Rico, 115 in Canada and 177 across Mexico, Japan, the UK, Korea, Australia, Taiwan, China, Spain, France, Sweden, Iceland and New Zealand). Each carries fewer than 4,000 active SKUs, which its annual report calls significantly less than other broadline retailers, sold at thin markups; most of the profit comes from the membership fee. In fiscal 2026 (the 52 weeks to August 30, 2026), total revenue reached $303.2B, up 10.1% YoY, and net income $9.23B (+13.9%), or $20.76 per share. Adjusted comparable sales rose 6.6% for the year and 6.7% in the fourth quarter.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Membership Lock-In (92.3% Renewal)
Scale Purchasing Power
Kirkland Signature Private Label
Thin-Markup Price Trust
Treasure-Hunt Merchandising
Warehouse Cost Discipline
Executive-Member Flywheel

Costco's moat is a self-reinforcing loop that is easy to describe and hard to copy at scale: 84.1M paid members pre-pay for the right to shop, which lets Costco run a merchandise margin of about 11% of net sales (FY2026: $297.2B of net sales less $264.3B of merchandise costs) and still earn its profit from fees. Low prices drive volume; fewer than 4,000 SKUs per warehouse concentrate that volume on each item, and the buying power that follows funds lower prices again. Kirkland Signature puts pressure on national-brand suppliers and, per the 10-K, is priced below national brands while generally earning higher margins. No-frills warehouses and little advertising keep SG&A near 9% of net sales (9.1% in FY2026; 8.94% in Q4). The result: a retailer with a 3.0% net margin that earns a 28% ROE.

Key Vulnerabilities

Valuation premium. At 44.5× trailing and 40.6× forward earnings, COST is priced for years of steady compounding, while EPS grew 14.0% in FY2026 and analysts expect about 10.5% in FY2027 (from $20.61, the reported $20.76 less the $0.15 refund; 9.7% from the reported figure). From its February 13, 2025 closing peak of $1,076.86 the stock fell 21.1% to $850.00 on December 22, 2025, while the business kept growing: multiple compression alone is the largest risk to holders.

A rearmed Sam's Club. Walmart keeps investing in Sam's Club, which raised its own fees on May 1, 2026 to $60 (Club) and $120 (Plus). Its base fee still sits below Costco's $65, and sustained share gains there would limit how far Costco can push its next increase.

E-commerce & convenience disruption. Digitally enabled sales topped $33B in FY2026, up more than 20%, or roughly 11% of net sales (our calculation), and management says sales through third-party delivery apps are mostly incremental. The model still depends on members driving to a warehouse, and same-day delivery from Amazon and Walmart competes for the stock-up trip the club format is built on.

Thin-margin sensitivity. With an 11% merchandise margin and a 3% net margin by design, small shocks move EPS a lot: tariffs, gasoline price swings, wage costs, or currency (27% of FY2025 net sales came from outside the US). Costco's habit is to hand windfalls back in price, as it did with most of the Q4 tariff refunds, so the cushion stays thin in both directions.

Notable One-Off Items

Costco's P&L is unusually clean: no goodwill write-downs, restructurings or acquisition noise. Items to adjust for: (1) IEEPA tariff refunds: Q4 includes a non-recurring benefit of $0.15 per diluted share from refunds of tariffs levied under the IEEPA ($174M of refunds plus $10M of interest), net of the part reinvested in lower prices. Millerchip said this was a little more than a third of the refunds Costco expects, that a similar amount had already arrived in Q1 FY2027, and that most of it will keep going into member prices. (2) Membership fee increase timing: the Sep 2024 increase ($65/$130) was recognized over two years and has now fully lapped; Q4 was the last quarter with a year-over-year benefit, so fee growth steps down toward member growth (Q4: +6.8% excluding the increase and currency). (3) Special dividends: none since the $15/share special (ex-date Dec 27, 2023); cash and short-term investments stand at $21.3B, up 39% from $15.3B a year earlier. (4) Gasoline & FX: record gas volumes and higher pump prices lifted Q4 reported comps to +9.4% against +6.7% adjusted; the adjusted figure is the one to watch.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The best subscription business in retail keeps compounding: 84.1M paid members renewing at 92.3%, Executive members up 9.4% to half the base, adjusted comps holding at 6–7%, digital sales up more than 20%, and 33 openings planned for FY2027. With the 2024 increase lapped, the next fee increase is optionality on top; $21.3B of cash and short-term investments leaves room for another special dividend; and low-double-digit EPS growth looks repeatable. After the 2025 reset to $850, the stock set a new all-time high within five months.

Bear Case

You're paying 44.5× trailing / 40.6× forward earnings and a ~3.9 PEG for a 3%-margin retailer that analysts expect to grow EPS about 10% in FY2027 (10.5% from the refund-adjusted $20.61, 9.7% from the reported $20.76). The fee tailwind ended in Q4, the Q4 beat leaned on a $0.15 one-off, and most post-print target changes were cuts. The 2025 de-rating (−21.1% while sales kept growing) showed what multiple compression alone can do; a comp slowdown, a stronger Sam's Club or tariff costs could take the multiple toward 35×, about $800 on FY2027 consensus EPS.

Valuation / multiple compression. At 40.6× forward earnings vs. a ~28× index (est.), COST prices in near-flawless execution; history (Apr–May 2022, Feb–Dec 2025) shows 20%+ drawdowns can arrive without any fundamental break.

Competitive escalation from Sam's Club. Walmart keeps investing in Sam's Club; even after its 2026 increase to $60/$120, the Sam's base fee undercuts Costco's $65, and sustained comp parity would weaken the case for Costco's next increase.

  Industry Commentary — What Matters Right Now
Tariffs & the IEEPA Refunds
WATCH ITEM
Tariffs levied under the IEEPA are now coming back as refunds: Costco received $184M in Q4 ($174M plus $10M of interest), a little more than a third of what it expects, and a similar amount already in Q1 FY2027. Management put most of it into price cuts on produce, meat, beverages, home furnishings and hardware, keeping a net $0.15 per share in Q4. The tell in the numbers: the merchandise margin on net sales held at about 11.1% in both FY2025 and FY2026, and in Q4 the CFO put the gross margin rate 0.11 points lower as reported but 0.20 points higher excluding gas inflation. Tariffs that remain still land on a P&L with about 3% of net margin to absorb them, so this stays a watch item into FY2027.
Value-Seeking Shoppers & Younger Members
EMERGING TAILWIND
Warehouse clubs keep winning the value-seeking shopper. In FY2026 Costco's paid members grew 3.8% to 84.1M and Executive members 9.4% to 42.3M, and new sign-ups grew even against a strong FY2025. Management says members under 40 have grown nearly 60% since COVID and are now more than a quarter of the base, and that shoppers using third-party delivery apps skew significantly younger. Digitally enabled sales topped $33B, and gas volumes hit a record in Q4. As long as grocery prices stay high, the $65 membership is an easy return in a household budget.
Membership-Fee Economics: The Bull/Bear Fulcrum
DECIDING FACTOR
Everything about the COST debate reduces to one question: how durable and how expandable is the fee annuity? Bulls: $5.9B of nearly pure-profit fees renewing at 92.3%, Executive penetration rising, paid members up 3.8% last year and 33 openings next year: a bond-plus-growth profile worth 40×+. Bears: with the 2024 increase lapped, fee growth falls back to member growth (Q4: +6.8% excluding the increase and currency) until the next hike; Sam's Club at $60 limits how far Costco can push; and at ~41× forward the flywheel is fully priced. The Dec 2025 $850.00 close and the May 2026 $1,094.32 close mark the market's live bid and ask on that exact argument.
