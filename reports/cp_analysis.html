01 Company Overview

Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC) runs roughly 20,000 miles of railway from Vancouver and Atlantic Canada through the US Midwest to the Gulf Coast and the Mexican port of Lázaro Cárdenas. It was created on April 14, 2023, when Canadian Pacific took control of Kansas City Southern in a US$31 billion transaction after Surface Transportation Board approval on March 15, 2023. CPKC describes itself as "the first and only single-line transnational railway linking Canada, the United States and México." Trailing-twelve-month revenue is C$16.01B (the sum of the last four reported quarters) and FY2025 revenue was C$15.08B with diluted EPS of C$4.51. At a $78.0B market value it is the third-largest of the five big North American railroads, behind Union Pacific and CSX and ahead of Norfolk Southern and the other Canadian transcontinental, CN (our calculation, Section 07).

The investment case is a merger that has to keep paying. KCS gave Canadian Pacific a single line into Mexico. Three years in, the combined network is producing growth: Q3 2026 freight revenue rose 15% to C$4.14B, with grain up 29% and intermodal (containers and trailers moved by rail) up 22%; on the Q2 call in July chief marketing officer John Brooks put merger synergies at US$1.4–1.5B by the end of 2026. The cost side is harder: the operating ratio got worse in Q3 — 64.8% reported (up 1.3 percentage points) and 61.9% core adjusted (up 1.2 points) — and CPKC said higher fuel prices alone added 1.7 points to the reported ratio and 1.9 points to the core adjusted one. Core adjusted EPS of C$1.28 came in below the roughly C$1.31 consensus (Investing.com), and the stock fell 1.2% on the first session after the results. And the live risk sits on the map: a railroad whose unique asset is a Canada–US–Mexico single line depends on trade between those three countries staying open, and on July 1, 2026 the US declined to renew USMCA for a further 16 years, putting the treaty into annual review. The home-market index is the S&P/TSX Composite; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Only single-line Canada–US–Mexico railway Irreplicable right-of-way (~20,000 miles) Lázaro Cárdenas & Gulf port access Class I duopoly economics in Canada PSR operating discipline Rail's cost edge over trucks on long haul

A railroad's moat is land. Nobody will build another continuous right-of-way from Canadian ports to the Gulf of Mexico. Without CPKC, freight moving Canada→Mexico has to change railroads at least once, and each handoff adds days and a second price negotiation. That structural advantage helps explain the Q3 growth in grain, metals and intermodal. The weakness of this moat is that it is a trade moat: it is worth most when freight crosses borders. A domestic railroad loses volume in a trade war. CPKC loses the thing that makes it different.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
USMCA is now on an annual leash. Canada and Mexico backed a 16-year renewal. On July 1, 2026 the US said it "did not agree to renew the USMCA in its current form" (USTR statement). The three countries now hold annual reviews (Article 34.7) until they agree to extend it or it expires on July 1, 2036, and any party can withdraw on six months' written notice (Article 34.6), per the Congressional Research Service's September 29, 2026 report. Nothing has been withdrawn. But the premium for CPKC's unique route now depends on a trade framework that has to be re-endorsed every year.
02
Costs have not fallen as revenue grew. A 15% revenue quarter produced an OR 1.3 percentage points worse (1.2 on the core adjusted measure). The company says higher fuel prices alone added 1.7 points to the reported ratio and 1.9 to the core adjusted one, which is partly temporary. Still, a merger case built on network efficiency needs the OR to improve at some point. The CFO said on the Q3 call that he feels "very comfortable we'll have a sub-60 OR in Q4 given the volume environment"; until it shows, the growth is revenue growth, not margin growth.
03
Mexico is a jurisdiction, not just a market. CPKC's Mexican subsidiary faces a 2014 tax assessment of Ps. 6,710 million (about C$528M) at September 30, 2026 including penalties and interest. The company filed an ICSID arbitration claim under the CPTPP on March 24, 2026, which ICSID registered on April 27, 2026, and the Mexican unit is pursuing an Amparo appeal (company release). In Q3 it also settled the 2020–2021 tax years with Mexico's tax authority (SAT) for a C$20M payment and a C$17M tax expense, while 2016–2019 remain in dispute. The dispute shows the regulatory exposure that comes with owning a Mexican concession.
04
Labour and industry consolidation. About 300 IBEW Signals & Communications employees in Canada struck from May 31, 2026 until the union agreed to binding arbitration on August 21. They returned to work on August 24. Separately, the proposed Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger would create a US transcontinental competitor. Asked on the Q2 call whether UP's agreements with CN put the deal on a better competitive footing, Creel called them "a few steps forward in a positive direction" but said "I don't think it changes the math", and on the Q3 call, asked about conditions, he said: "We don't want a condition. We don't want a concession." CPKC booked C$17M of advisory costs tied to the STB review in Q3 (company release).

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the figures. First, reported vs core EPS: Q3 2026 reported diluted EPS of C$1.04 (+3%) sits against core adjusted EPS of C$1.28 (+16%). The release lists the items that separate them: C$96M of amortization from the KCS purchase accounting, a C$94M tax charge from the Canada Revenue Agency's reassessment of prior years, C$12M of KCS acquisition-related costs, a C$17M tax expense on the Mexican tax settlement and C$17M of advisory costs tied to the STB review of the UP–NS merger. Reported net income was C$916M against C$917M a year earlier. Second, buybacks: CPKC repurchased 11.5M shares for C$1.487B in Q3 and 28.1M for C$3.431B in the first nine months, and the current NCIB (normal course issuer bid, a program to buy back its own shares) allows up to 44.9M shares through February 1, 2027. Third, financing: CPKC issued C$1.8 billion of senior unsecured notes in four tranches (C$500M, C$550M, C$300M and C$450M, due 2030 to 2056) on October 6, 2026, and had US$1,535 million of commercial paper outstanding at September 30 against an undrawn US$2.2 billion revolving credit facility (company release).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $77.40 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $89.68, the NYSE line rose 15.9% on price, about 3.0% a year. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

CPKC owns a route nobody else can build. That route is now producing growth: Q3 freight revenue up 15%, grain up 29%, intermodal up 22%, land-bridge revenue expected by the CEO to exit 2026 north of $600 million (from about $100M in 2023), and US$1.4–1.5B of merger synergies expected by the end of 2026 (Q2 call). The OR deterioration comes mainly from fuel prices, which can reverse, while employees at period end were 19,996 against 20,151 a year earlier as volume grew, and the CFO said on the Q3 call he feels "very comfortable we'll have a sub-60 OR in Q4 given the volume environment". Management says it is on track for its full-year guidance (on the Q2 call Creel described it as "another year of double-digit earnings growth in 2026"), with nine-month buybacks of C$3.43B. On FY2026 consensus the stock is about 24.6x forward. If UP–NS merges, shippers who want an alternative may choose CPKC's independent network.

Bear Case

You are paying 29.0x trailing US$ EPS for earnings that sit 4.2% below FY2025's, ROIC of 6.4% on a US$31B acquisition, and an operating ratio that got worse in a 15% revenue quarter, with core adjusted EPS slightly under consensus. The whole premium depends on trade across three borders, and the US has just put USMCA into annual review instead of renewing it. The Mexican unit is in an arbitration over a tax claim of about C$528M. Every US$ holder is also exposed to the Canadian dollar.

Top 3 Risks

01
USMCA and tariff exposure. The US declined to renew on July 1, 2026, and the agreement now faces annual review until 2036. Automotive, grain to Mexico, and land-bridge intermodal are all cross-border, and grain and intermodal were the two fastest-growing lines in Q3.
02
Margin delivery. Q3 OR was 64.8% reported and 61.9% core, 1.3 and 1.2 percentage points worse, with fuel the main cause. The merger case needs revenue synergies to turn into a lower OR. If Q4 2026 does not show the improvement the CFO described, the forward multiple has little support.
03
Mexico jurisdiction and currency. There is a Ps. 6,710M (about C$528M) tax assessment under ICSID arbitration, plus the peso and C$ translation of a railway that reports in C$ and trades in US$.
Industry Commentary
North American trade on an annual review
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Canada and Mexico backed renewing USMCA for another 16 years. On July 1, 2026 the United States said it would not (USTR statement). The agreement does not end: it continues to July 1, 2036, with annual joint reviews (Article 34.7), and any party can still withdraw on six months' notice (Article 34.6), per the Congressional Research Service. For most railroads that is background. For CPKC it goes to the heart of the investment case, because the company's defining asset is a single line connecting three countries. The Q3 numbers show how the network is being used: grain +29%, metals, minerals & consumer products +16%, intermodal +22%, automotive +9%. USMCA was not mentioned on the Q3 call (Motley Fool transcript). The risk is that a railroad cannot move its track if trade flows move.

Rail consolidation: UP–NS and the next map
ONGOING HEADWIND

Union Pacific's proposed merger with Norfolk Southern would create the first US coast-to-coast railroad. On the Q2 call Creel called CPKC's own deal "additive": it joined networks that did not overlap. He described UP–NS as a substitution that leaves shippers with fewer options. Asked about UP's agreements with CN (UP access around Chicago on CN's EJ&E line, CN a route to Mexico through the Memphis gateway), he called them "a few steps forward in a positive direction" but said "I don't think it changes the math", and that customers want to "protect competitive options." On the Q3 call, asked about conditions, he said: "We don't want a condition. We don't want a concession." There are two ways this could go. A merged UP–NS could compete harder for CPKC's Midwest and Gulf interchange traffic. Or regulator-imposed access conditions could give CPKC new gateways.

Precision railroading meets record volumes
EMERGING TAILWIND

CPKC's Q3 gross ton-miles (train weight times distance, a workload measure) rose 7% to 107,042 million and revenue ton-miles rose 6%, while employees at period end were 19,996 against 20,151 a year earlier. The company reported a record 2025–26 crop year of 30.66 million metric tons of Canadian grain. CFO Nadeem Velani said the company is on track "to deliver full year CapEx of approximately $2.65 billion"; nine-month capex was C$2,035M against C$2,314M a year earlier. The bull reading: this is the classic PSR sequence. Volume comes first, and the OR follows once fuel and one-off cost pressures ease. The bear reading: a 1.3-point worse OR in a 15% revenue quarter suggests the easy efficiency gains are finished, and the merged network is running closer to capacity than the growth targets assume. The Q4 OR, which the CFO expects to be below 60% on the company's measure, is the next test of which reading is right.
