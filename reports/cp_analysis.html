01 Company Overview

Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC) runs roughly 20,000 miles of railway from Vancouver and Atlantic Canada through the US Midwest to the Gulf Coast and the Mexican port of Lázaro Cárdenas. It was created on April 14, 2023, when Canadian Pacific took control of Kansas City Southern in a US$31 billion transaction after Surface Transportation Board approval on March 15, 2023. The premise supplied for this report checks out on the company's own wording: CPKC describes itself as "the first and only single-line transnational railway linking Canada, the United States and México." Trailing-twelve-month revenue is C$15.45B (+3.6%) and FY2025 revenue was C$15.08B with diluted EPS of C$4.51. At a $76.8B market value it ranks #12 by market cap in Canada and #322 globally (companiesmarketcap) — ahead of the other Canadian transcontinental, CN (#15, $71.08B).

The investment case is a merger that has to keep paying. KCS gave Canadian Pacific a single line into Mexico. Three years in, the combined network is producing record freight: Q2 2026 freight revenue rose 13% to C$4.09B, grain to Mexico from Canadian territory rose 24%, and management reiterated a target of $1.4–1.5B in synergy revenues by year-end 2026. The cost side is harder: the operating ratio got worse in Q2 — 64.6% reported, 61.6% core adjusted, both up 90 bps — as fuel, casualty and stock-compensation costs rose. And the live risk sits on the map: a railroad whose unique asset is a Canada–US–Mexico single line depends on trade between those three countries staying open, and on July 1, 2026 the US declined to renew USMCA for a further 16 years, putting the treaty into annual review. The home-market index is the S&P/TSX Composite; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Only single-line Canada–US–Mexico railway Irreplicable right-of-way (~20,000 miles) Lázaro Cárdenas & Gulf port access Class I duopoly economics in Canada PSR operating discipline Rail's cost edge over trucks on long haul

A railroad's moat is land. Nobody will build another continuous right-of-way from Canadian ports to the Gulf of Mexico. Without CPKC, freight moving Canada→Mexico has to change railroads at least once, and each handoff adds days and a second price negotiation. That structural advantage explains the Q2 growth in grain, autos and intermodal. The weakness of this moat is that it is a trade moat: it is worth most when freight crosses borders. A domestic railroad loses volume in a trade war. CPKC loses the thing that makes it different.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
USMCA is now on an annual leash. Canada and Mexico backed a 16-year renewal. On July 1, 2026 the US said it "did not agree to renew the USMCA in its current form" (USTR statement). The three countries now hold annual reviews until the agreement expires on July 1, 2036, unless a party gives six months' notice to withdraw (Article 34.7, per White & Case's July 2026 alert). Nothing has been withdrawn. But the premium for CPKC's unique route now depends on a trade framework that has to be re-endorsed every year.
02
Operating leverage has not arrived. A 13% revenue quarter produced a 90 bps worse OR. Management's explanation (fuel, casualty, stock comp) is plausible and partly temporary. Still, a merger case built on network efficiency needs the OR to improve at some point. Until it does, the growth is revenue growth, not margin growth.
03
Mexico is a jurisdiction, not just a market. CPKC's Mexican subsidiary faces a 2014 tax assessment of Ps. 6.667 billion (C$540M) including penalties and interest. The company filed an ICSID arbitration claim under the CPTPP on March 24, 2026 and holds an injunction blocking enforcement. Management expects to win, but the dispute shows the regulatory exposure that comes with owning a Mexican concession.
04
Labour and industry consolidation. About 300 IBEW Signals & Communications employees in Canada struck from May 31, 2026 until the union agreed to binding arbitration on August 21. They returned to work on August 24. Separately, the proposed Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger would create a US transcontinental competitor. Creel told the Q2 call that UP's concessions to CN are "a few steps forward" but do not change the competitive maths.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

CPKC owns a route nobody else can build. That route is now producing growth: Q2 freight revenue up 13%, grain up 24%, autos up 22%, land-bridge revenue heading from $100M to $600M in three years, and a $1.4–1.5B synergy-revenue target for year-end 2026. The OR deterioration comes from fuel, casualty and stock compensation, which can reverse, while headcount fell as volume grew. Management guides to double-digit EPS growth with capex down 15% and buybacks shrinking the share count by 3.6% a year. On FY2026 consensus the stock is about 23.5x forward. If UP–NS merges, shippers who want an alternative may choose CPKC's independent network.

Bear Case

You are paying 28.3x trailing US$ EPS for reported earnings that fell 4.2%, ROIC of 6.4% on a US$31B acquisition, and an operating ratio that got worse in a 13% revenue quarter. The whole premium depends on trade across three borders, and the US has just put USMCA into annual review instead of renewing it. The Mexican unit is in an arbitration over a C$540M tax claim. Every US$ holder is also exposed to the Canadian dollar. The synergy story can't be checked from outside: the original target and the amount realised to date are not disclosed in the materials fetched.

Top 3 Risks

01
USMCA and tariff exposure. The US declined to renew on July 1, 2026, and the agreement now faces annual review until 2036. Automotive, grain to Mexico, and land-bridge intermodal, the three fastest-growing lines, are all cross-border.
02
Margin delivery. Q2 OR was 64.6% reported and 61.6% core, both 90 bps worse. The merger case needs revenue synergies to turn into a lower OR. If H2 2026 shows no improvement, the forward multiple has little support.
03
Mexico jurisdiction and currency. There is a Ps. 6.667B (C$540M) tax assessment under ICSID arbitration, plus the peso and C$ translation of a railway that reports in C$ and trades in US$.
Industry Commentary
North American trade on an annual review
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Canada and Mexico backed renewing USMCA for another 16 years. On July 1, 2026 the United States said it would not (USTR statement). The agreement does not end: it continues to July 1, 2036, with annual joint reviews and a six-month withdrawal option (Article 34.7, per White & Case's July 2026 alert). For most railroads that is background. For CPKC it goes to the heart of the investment case, because the company's defining asset is a single line connecting three countries. The Q2 numbers show how the network is being used: grain from Canadian territory to Mexico +24%, US grain to Mexico +14%, automotive +22%. Management did not discuss USMCA in the Q2 release. The risk is that a railroad cannot move its track if trade flows move.

Rail consolidation: UP–NS and the next map
ONGOING HEADWIND

Union Pacific's proposed merger with Norfolk Southern would create the first US coast-to-coast railroad. On the Q2 call Creel called CPKC's own deal "additive": it joined networks that did not overlap. He called UP–NS "substitutive": it removes competition. He acknowledged UP's concessions to CN on EJ&E access and the Memphis gateway as "a few steps forward in a positive direction" but said they don't change the merger maths, and that customers want "competitive options." There are two ways this could go. A merged UP–NS could compete harder for CPKC's Midwest and Gulf interchange traffic. Or regulator-imposed access conditions could give CPKC new gateways.

Precision railroading meets record volumes
EMERGING TAILWIND

CPKC reported record Q2 freight revenue excluding fuel and all-time record gross ton-miles, a record 2025–26 grain crop year of 30.66 million metric tons, and a new August grain record. Headcount is down about 500 year to date, and 2026 capex of $2.65B is about 15% lower. The bull reading: this is the classic PSR sequence. Volume comes first, and the OR follows once fuel and one-off cost pressures ease. The bear reading: a 90 bps worse OR in a record quarter suggests the easy efficiency gains are finished, and the merged network is running closer to capacity than the growth targets assume.
