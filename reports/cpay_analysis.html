01 Company Overview

For most of its public life this company was called FLEETCOR and sold fuel cards to trucking fleets: a plastic card that lets a driver fill up anywhere while the fleet owner sees every gallon. On March 25, 2024 it became Corpay and started trading as CPAY on the NYSE — a name chosen for a portfolio that had drifted toward corporate payments. Same company, same share count history, new name. The S&P 500 membership carried over: FLEETCOR joined the index on June 20, 2018.

Today it is a toll booth on business spending. Corpay takes a small cut when fleets buy fuel and pay tolls, when companies wire money across borders or pay suppliers by virtual card, and when airlines and railroads house crews in hotels. TTM revenue is $5.02B, up 20.4%, on an 80.5% gross margin and a 49.0% operating margin — software-like economics on a payments business. Q2 2026 revenue rose 21% to $1,338.8M (10% organic) and adjusted EPS rose 36% to $7.00; management raised the full-year guide to $27.15–$27.55 adjusted EPS. The debate is not whether the machine makes money. It is whether investors should pay a full multiple for a company whose fuel-card business a federal court found charged hidden and unauthorised fees — the FTC matter Corpay agreed to settle for $100M on September 18, 2026 — and whose growth increasingly comes from acquisitions in cross-border FX.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Closed-loop fuel-card networks Embedded in fleet & AP workflows 93% retention 80.5% gross margin Cross-border FX scale Serial acquirer / integrator

The moat is inertia priced in basis points. A fleet manager who has issued cards to 200 drivers, set fuel-type and location controls and wired the data into accounting does not switch to save a fraction of a cent per gallon. A finance team that pays foreign suppliers through one platform does not rebuild that plumbing for a slightly tighter FX spread. Small take rates on large, sticky flows produce the 49% operating margin. The weakness in that model is that fees nobody notices are fees nobody agreed to — which is exactly what the FTC litigated.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Fee practices on the record. The FTC says a federal district court found in 2023 that FleetCor charged hidden or unauthorised fees and misrepresented fuel-card savings, and that an appeals court upheld the judgment in 2026. Corpay agreed to pay $100M without admitting wrongdoing, subject to final FTC approval after public comment. The cash is small next to $1.64B of FCF; the question is whether fee practices elsewhere in the portfolio attract the same scrutiny.
02
Macro does part of the work. Management attributed roughly $30M of Q2's revenue upside to macro conditions — fuel prices and FX — and only about $15M to the underlying business. Revenue that rises with diesel and currency volatility falls with them too.
03
Leverage and acquisition dependence. $10.74B of debt against $3.18B of cash, debt-to-equity of 2.75 and a 2.55x leverage ratio. Corporate Payments' 42% reported growth against 16% organic shows how much of the story is bought. Buying growth works until an integration stumbles or rates stay high.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At 14.5x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS you buy a business guiding to 28% adjusted EPS growth, 10% organic revenue growth, 93% retention and a 57% adjusted EBITDA margin. Corporate Payments is growing 16% organically and will soon be the largest segment. Management is selling the non-core pieces and using the proceeds to shrink the share count. The FTC case, a 2019 overhang, now has a $100M price tag — about 6% of one year's free cash flow. Remove the discount and Scotiabank's "cleaner multiple" argument does the rest.

Bear Case

A federal court found the fuel-card business charged customers fees they did not agree to, and an appeals court upheld it. Margins built on fees customers don't notice deserve a discount, not a re-rating. Reported growth of 21% is 10% organic, and management itself credited twice as much of the Q2 beat to fuel and FX as to the business. On GAAP earnings the stock trades at 24.2x, with 2.75x debt-to-equity, and the stock sits at RSI 68.6 near an all-time high with 13 of 16 analysts already at Buy.

Top 3 Risks

01
Regulatory and fee-practice risk. The FTC settlement awaits final approval, and the underlying finding was about fee disclosure — the same revenue mechanism that runs across the company.
02
Macro sensitivity. Fuel prices, FX volatility and interest rates (via float income) all feed revenue; the 180bp float drag on Corporate Payments organic growth shows the rate channel working in reverse.
03
Leverage and M&A execution. $10.74B of debt, a 2.55x leverage ratio and a strategy that buys growth and sells what doesn't fit. Each integration and divestiture is a chance to miss.
Industry Commentary
From fuel cards to B2B payments
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

The rename tells you where management thinks the growth is. Business-to-business payments — supplier invoices, cross-border transfers — are still heavily paper and bank-wire; every flow moved onto a card or platform gives the processor a fee or an FX spread. Corpay's Corporate Payments segment grew 16% organically in Q2 2026 while Vehicle grew 8% and Lodging 2%. The mix shift is what the market is being asked to pay for.

Fee transparency under scrutiny
ONGOING HEADWIND

The FTC's case against FleetCor ran from 2019 to a 2023 district-court finding, a 2026 appeals decision and a $100M settlement. For a business whose economics depend on small, unnoticed charges on large flows, disclosure rules are a direct input to revenue, not a compliance footnote.
