01 Company Overview

Before a new drug gets anywhere near a human, somebody has to show a regulator it is safe enough to try. For decades a large share of that work has run through Charles River: it breeds the research models (mostly rats and mice) that labs use, runs the non-clinical safety studies that drug developers submit to regulators, and sells the microbial testing kits manufacturers use to check that their products are sterile. FY2025 revenue was $4.02B, down 0.9% (organic −1.6%), with non-GAAP EPS of $10.28 but a GAAP loss of $(2.91) per share after impairments.

The story of the last eighteen months is a business that was told it might be obsolete and then was not. On April 10, 2025 the FDA announced a plan to phase out animal-testing requirements for monoclonal antibodies and other drugs in favour of "more effective, human-relevant methods." Investing.com reported the stock plummeted 28% that day, and April 2025 closed at $118.62, the lowest month-end in our five-year series. By May 6, 2025 the company had signed a cooperation agreement with the activist investor Elliott Investment Management. What followed was a reshaping: the sale of its contract drug manufacturing (CDMO) and Cell Solutions businesses to GI Partners and of some European discovery sites to IQVIA (both May 2026), a CEO handover to chief operating officer Birgit Girshick on May 5, 2026, and an Investor Day on September 24 that put 2030 targets on the plan. Then the numbers moved. Organic revenue, up 0.1% in Q2 2026, rose 4.7% in Q3, which the CFO called "our strongest growth in three years," and the company raised its guidance. The stock had already run to a $376.10 close on October 29. It fell 11.0% over the two sessions after the November 4 results, then rebounded 7.8% on November 6 to close at $337.52, 184.5% above the April 2025 month-end and still 24.8% below its October 2021 month-end high of $448.68. The central debate: is the non-animal-methods shift an evolution Charles River can sell into, or a slow erosion of its core safety-testing volume that a biotech-funding recovery is currently hiding?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Discovery-to-safety integration Research-model supply chain $2.03B DSA backlog Endosafe testing installed base Regulatory data track record AI/data ties (Lilly TuneLab)

The moat is trust inside a regulated workflow. A drug developer that submits safety data to a regulator is paying for studies the agency will accept, and changing providers in the middle of a programme adds risk and delay. Charles River sells the models, the studies and the quality-control kits in the same relationship, which is why the CEO said on the Q2 call that it is "uniquely positioned to be able to integrate traditional in vivo, in vitro, and new innovative capabilities into one comprehensive solution for the biopharmaceutical industry." (In vivo means studies in living animals; in vitro, tests in lab dishes.) The weakness is that the moat protects the workflow as it exists today. If regulators change what counts as acceptable evidence, the workflow changes with them.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Regulation is moving away from animal studies. The FDA's April 10, 2025 plan aims to replace animal testing for monoclonal antibodies and other drugs with "more effective, human-relevant methods." Management's position is that new approach methodologies (NAMs) will be integrated with traditional studies rather than replace them, and the CEO said on the Q3 call that virtual control groups, one such method, are not "a huge revenue upside." The pace of that shift is set in Washington, not in Wilmington.
02
Demand depends on biotech funding. Small and mid-sized biotech clients spend when capital markets are open. Management said in November that the biotech funding environment "has improved meaningfully since the middle of last year," while new company starts have "yet to meaningfully improve." The current upswing is a cycle, and cycles turn.
03
The balance sheet carries real debt. Total debt of $2.85B including leases (StockAnalysis) against $200.4M of cash (debt-to-equity 0.87), after $467.3M of cash spent on acquisitions and $324.1M on buying back its own shares in the first nine months of 2026. The CFO said "gross and net leverage improved sequentially to 2.3x" on the company's own measure. That is better, but the cushion is thin if bookings roll over.
04
GAAP earnings are a mess. Q4 2025 carried $211.0M of intangible impairments and a $165.0M goodwill impairment. Q1 2026 took a $118.0M loss on assets held for sale and Q2 2026 a $63.7M loss on divestitures. Q3 2026 went the other way: a $245.6M reversal of acquired tax reserves, worth $5.06 per share, took GAAP EPS to $6.86. Trailing GAAP EPS is +$0.91 against +$10.56 on the company's non-GAAP basis, and would be −$4.15 without the reversal (our arithmetic), so any valuation rests on management's adjustments.

Notable One-Off Items

Almost every GAAP line is distorted. Q4 2025 carried $211.0M of intangible-asset impairments (Biologics Solutions and Cell Solutions) and a $165.0M goodwill impairment in Biologics Solutions, which produced a $(5.62) GAAP loss per share against $2.39 non-GAAP. Q1 2026 booked a $118.0M loss on assets held for sale tied to the CDMO and Cell Solutions divestitures ($1.53 per share). Q2 2026 added a $63.7M loss on divestitures ($1.40 per share). The planned divestitures represented about 7% of 2025 revenue. Q3 2026 went the other way: a $245.6M reversal of acquired tax reserves after a newly enacted tax law ($5.06 per share), split between a $201M income-tax benefit and a $45M benefit in operating costs, which produced GAAP EPS of $6.86 against $3.09 non-GAAP. FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance, cut in August to $3.05–$3.35, is now $8.15–$8.35 including about $5.03 from the reversal, while non-GAAP guidance rose to $11.35–$11.55 (from $11.15–$11.45). When the two numbers swing this far apart, read the adjustments before trusting either.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 6, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — an expensive stock can stay overbought for a long time.

Over the five years from the November 2021 close of $365.87 to the November 6, 2026 settled close of $337.52, CRL lost 7.7% of its price. It pays no dividend, so that is also the total return. The endpoints hide a round trip. The stock's October 2021 month-end of $448.68 falls just before this window. From there it fell 73.6% to $118.62 in April 2025, the month the FDA announced its animal-testing plan, and it has since gained 184.5%, including +69.2% in 2026 so far from the $199.48 December 2025 close.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how fast that recovery has been. It hit 90.6 at the October 2026 month-end, the highest reading in the five-year series, and stands at 78.3 on the November 6 close, still above the 70 overbought line. The 10-month moving average is about $243.81, and the price sits roughly 38% above it. On the daily chart the picture is calmer: the 14-day RSI is 53.7, and the close is 10.5% above the 50-day average of $305.60 and 50.3% above the 200-day average of $224.56. At the other extreme, the lowest monthly RSI reading in the window was 6.1 in June 2022, and the April 2025 FDA month registered 21.2. The trend has clearly turned up. The open question is whether one quarter of 4.7% organic growth has earned a move this stretched.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The recovery has reached the numbers. Organic revenue grew 4.7% in Q3, DSA grew 5.9% organically, net bookings rose 32% from a year earlier and backlog reached $2.03B. Guidance went up again, non-GAAP EPS of $11.35–$11.55 implies 10–12% growth, and the Investor Day set 2030 targets of 5–7% organic growth, about a 24% non-GAAP operating margin and low-double-digit EPS growth, backed by over $300M of cumulative savings. The portfolio is cleaner after selling the CDMO and Cell Solutions, and what remains in Manufacturing earns a 38.6% non-GAAP margin heading towards 40%. Analysts raised every target shown in the post-print table. If NAMs turns out to be an added service layer rather than a substitute, as management argues, and the Lilly TuneLab link suggests the company has a seat in AI-driven discovery, then 29.4x forward earnings is a fair price for a recovering franchise.

Bear Case

The stock has risen 184.5% from its April 2025 low, sits at 78.3 on monthly RSI, and trades only 3.3% below its own average analyst target. The net book-to-bill slipped from 1.19x to 1.09x, and the shares fell 11.0% over the two sessions after a beat and a raise. One analyst's target is $145. The larger question has not gone away: the FDA has said it wants to replace animal testing, and DSA is 61% of revenue. Management says pricing power has not come back and will not help until at least the second half of 2027, and that new biotech company starts have yet to improve. GAAP EPS of $0.91 is positive only because of a $5.06 per share tax-reserve reversal, net debt is about 2.9x EBITDA, and the TTM free-cash-flow yield is about 2.5%. On this view the stock has priced in the whole recovery before most of it has arrived, and the 2030 targets now have to be delivered, not just announced.

Top 3 Risks

01
Regulatory substitution of animal studies. The April 10, 2025 FDA plan knocked the stock down 28% in a day, as reported by Investing.com. Each further step (guidance, pilot programmes, acceptance of non-animal data in filings) tests management's claim that NAMs will be integrated with traditional studies rather than replace them.
02
Bookings normalisation. The net book-to-bill slipped from 1.19x to 1.09x in one quarter, though the CFO calls Q3 "seasonally usually a very weak quarter." If DSA bookings slip back towards 1.0 while the stock is priced for faster growth, what investors pay for each dollar of earnings has room to fall, as it did after the March 2024 high.
03
Debt in a cyclical business. $2.85B of debt including leases against $200M of cash, with $467.3M spent on acquisitions and $324.1M on buying back its own shares in the first nine months of 2026. A funding downturn would hit revenue and balance-sheet flexibility together.
Industry Commentary
New approach methodologies vs. the animal-study model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The FDA's April 10, 2025 announcement said the agency is "replacing animal testing in the development of monoclonal antibody therapies and other drugs with more effective, human-relevant methods," with the stated aims of improving drug safety, speeding evaluation and lowering R&D costs. For a company whose largest segment runs regulated safety studies, that is an existential question on paper. Management's answer has been consistent: NAMs is "the right ethical and scientific direction," but it arrives as "an integrated approach" in which in vivo, in vitro and newer methods sit together, and Charles River intends to sell all three. On the Q3 call the CEO said one of those newer methods, virtual control groups, is not "a huge revenue upside," earning about what a regular control group does, so that method protects existing work rather than adding a new revenue line. The bookings data so far supports management. The regulatory timeline is not in its hands.

The biotech funding cycle
CYCLICAL DRIVER

Preclinical spending follows the money that biotech companies raise. On the Q2 call management put trailing-twelve-month biotech funding at roughly $100 billion, near pandemic peaks (as reported by Investing.com). At the September conference the CFO called demand "pretty broad-based," with large biotech, smaller biotech and pharma all improving across North America and Europe. On the Q3 call the CEO said "the biotech funding environment has improved meaningfully since the middle of last year," while naming "some soft spots that we still would like to fill in, such as early company formation."

Portfolio reset under activist oversight
EMERGING TAILWIND

Since the May 2025 cooperation agreement with Elliott, Charles River has sold its CDMO, Cell Solutions and some European discovery sites (about 7% of 2025 revenue), bought K.F. Cambodia and PathoQuest, replaced its CEO with an internal successor, and bought back $300.0M of stock in the first half of 2026. On pricing the CEO was blunt on the Q3 call: "What we have not yet seen really is a return to pricing power, particularly not in the DSA segment." The bull framing: a smaller, higher-margin company is re-emerging just as its main market turns. The bear framing: the rise from $118.62 to $337.52 has already paid for most of that, and the 2030 targets now have to be delivered.
