01 Company Overview

Salesforce is the largest independent enterprise application software company in the world — a cloud platform for sales, service, marketing, commerce, integration, analytics and, since 2024, autonomous AI agents. Fiscal 2026 (ended January 31, 2026) revenue was $41.53B, up 9.6% YoY, the first sub-10% growth year in the company's history as a public company; trailing-twelve-month revenue through July 2026 is $43.94B, up 11.2%, helped by the Informatica acquisition. Market capitalisation is $212.42B after the post-earnings re-rating of late August — still below the $323.89B peak at the end of 2024, a decline of 34%. The #126 global and #75 US rankings carried from the August 24 snapshot were not re-verified at the higher market cap.

The company's problem is not profitability. Operating margin has gone from 2.07% (FY2022) to 21.47% (FY2026) on a GAAP basis and 34.1% on a non-GAAP basis in Q2 FY2027, and FCF is $15.15B on a trailing basis. The problem is growth and the durability of the pricing model. Salesforce sells seats. Agentic AI is explicitly designed to reduce the number of humans who need seats. That tension — not the P&L — held the multiple at 15.0x into late August. The August 26 print and the Anthropic partnership have since re-rated it to a 17.7x forward P/E, and that re-rating is the 23% move: the earnings base barely changed, what the market will pay for it did.

Salesforce's moat is customer data that has been in one place for fifteen years. A large enterprise CRM instance is not a piece of software, it is a decade of custom objects, workflow rules, Apex code, integrations, permission models and audit history. Ripping it out is a multi-year, eight-figure programme with revenue risk attached, which is why Salesforce holds roughly 27% of tracked CRM installations — more than Microsoft Dynamics, SAP, Oracle and Odoo combined on the same measure — and why the company can carry a 77.3% trailing gross margin and a 34.1% non-GAAP operating margin while growing at 11% constant currency. The Informatica acquisition is a deliberate deepening of that moat: by owning data cataloguing, quality, lineage and master data management, Salesforce is trying to make itself the governed data layer that any AI agent — its own or a competitor's — has to query. Total remaining performance obligation of $66.3B, up 11%, against $43.9B of trailing revenue is the moat expressed as contract: 1.5 years of revenue is already signed. The forward-looking half — cRPO — is $33.5B and grew 14% in constant currency, with Q3 guided to roughly the same 14%. That is the single number the August 26 re-rating was actually about: the honest leading indicator held, and the market had been pricing it to break.

The seat-based pricing model is structurally exposed. Salesforce charges per user per month. Agentforce's stated purpose is to let software do work that people currently do. If a 5,000-seat service organisation deploys agents and needs 3,500 humans, the renewal is 30% smaller even if the customer is delighted. Salesforce's answer is consumption pricing on work units and Flex Credits, but that transition converts predictable, high-visibility subscription revenue into variable usage revenue — a lower-quality, harder-to-forecast revenue stream that the market has historically valued at a lower multiple. Agentforce ARR of $1.5B — roughly 3% of guided FY2027 revenue — is not yet large enough to offset a seat contraction across a $44B base.

Growth has decelerated to the point where the growth premium is gone. FY2023 revenue grew 18.3%, FY2024 11.2%, FY2025 8.7%, FY2026 9.6%. The apps segment — 63% of revenue — grew 7.6% last quarter. FY2027 guidance was raised at Q2 to $46.1–46.4B (+11–12%), but it still carries 3-plus points from Informatica, implying organic growth around 8%; Q2's own organic rate, stripping Informatica out, was roughly 6.4%. At that rate Salesforce is a mature software company. It traded at 15.0x forward earnings on August 24 and 17.7x on September 1 — the re-rating happened without the organic growth rate changing, which is either the market forgiving the growth or the market paying for the Anthropic option.

Competitive encirclement from both ends. Microsoft can bundle Dynamics 365 and Copilot into an Office/Azure agreement at effectively marginal price; at $3.62T market cap and 17.8% revenue growth it has no need to earn a return on CRM specifically. From below, HubSpot ($3.45B revenue, +21.1%) keeps moving upmarket, and AI-native entrants are building CRM-shaped products where the agent — not the record — is the primary interface, with no legacy seat revenue to protect. Salesforce is defending the middle.

Balance sheet flexibility has been consumed. Total debt is $42.38B against $11.40B of cash, debt-to-equity is 1.10 and the current ratio is 0.84 — both marginally better than a quarter ago, neither comfortable. This is the direct consequence of returning $27.5B to shareholders in a single quarter, including a $25B accelerated share repurchase. The buyback was almost certainly the right capital allocation at the prices that prevailed, and with the stock 23% higher it looks better still — but the ASR reaches final settlement in October 2026, it removes the option to do it again at scale, and it means a genuine demand shock would now hit a levered balance sheet rather than a fortress one. Two further acquisitions, Contentful and Fin, are pending and expected to close in Q3 FY2027.

Notable One-Off Items

Q2 FY2027 contains four distortions large enough to change the picture, all disclosed in the quarter's statements. One of them is the largest single distortion this page has carried:

1. $2,613M of gains on strategic investments is doing most of the work in GAAP EPS. This is the number to internalise before reading anything else on the page. GAAP diluted EPS of $4.29 was up 119% YoY; on the 821M diluted shares in the quarter, $2.61B of mark-to-market gains on Salesforce Ventures holdings is roughly $3.18 per share pre-tax. These gains are non-operating, are excluded from non-GAAP, and can reverse in a single quarter. They also flow through into the trailing figures this page quotes: trailing EPS of $10.79, the 21.99% net margin and the 19.38% ROE are all flattered by them. Trailing operating margin is 21.52% and that is the honest number.

2. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is 13.6 points and reconciles exactly. GAAP operating margin 20.5%; non-GAAP 34.1%. The bridge is stock-based compensation of $904M (8.0% of revenue), amortisation of purchased intangibles of $522M (4.6%) and restructuring charges of $115M (1.0%). Stock-based compensation is a real cost to shareholders — it is dilution — and at 8% of revenue it is the largest single reason the headline non-GAAP margin overstates economic profitability. Restructuring is still running, three years after the programme began.

3. Informatica is inorganic revenue, and the gap widened. $456M of total revenue and $440M of subscription revenue in the quarter came from an acquisition that closed November 18, 2025 and has no comparable in the year-ago base. Strip it out and Q2 total revenue growth falls from 10.8% to roughly 6.4% — where the equivalent Q1 calculation gave about 8.5%. The raised FY2027 guide still carries 3-plus points of Informatica, and Q3 is guided to carry "slightly above 4 points". Two more acquisitions, Contentful and Fin, are expected to close in Q3 and account for $200M of the $200M headline guidance raise — meaning the organic component of the raise was about $100M, against a $100M FX headwind.

4. The $25B accelerated share repurchase still distorts every per-share metric. Diluted share count in Q2 was 821M against 838M guided for the full year; shares outstanding are 823M. The ASR reaches final settlement in October 2026, which is when the true retired share count is known. Non-GAAP EPS of $5.90 was up 103% YoY — a number that cannot be read as an operating result, because a materially smaller denominator is doing a large part of it. Also note the cash line: operating cash flow was $1.3B and free cash flow $1.1B in the quarter, and FY2027 free cash flow growth is guided to just 4–5%, well below any EPS growth rate quoted anywhere on this page.

Five-year trajectory. From a September 2021 monthly close of $266.61 to $258.11 on September 1, 2026, Salesforce has delivered a total price return of −3.2% over five years, before the small dividend stream that began in 2024. Nine days ago that same figure was −21.6%: eighteen points of a five-year return arrived in one session. The number conceals two complete cycles. The stock ran to $294.59 by October 2021 at the peak of the software multiple, collapsed 55.8% to $130.34 by December 2022 as rates repriced every long-duration cash flow, then more than doubled to an all-time monthly closing high of $337.76 in January 2025 on the margin-expansion story. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The August 26 print settled the question this page was built around, and settled it favourably. cRPO — the honest forward indicator — grew 14% in constant currency, not the 10% the bear case needed, and Q3 is guided to roughly 14% again. Guidance was raised. Agentforce ARR passed $1.5B (+240%) and combined Agentforce + Data 360 ARR reached ~$3.9B (+210%), now about 8% of guided revenue rather than 3% — consumption-priced revenue, growing at triple digits, in the exact place the seat-erosion risk would show up. The Anthropic partnership answers the strategic objection directly: Salesforce does not need to fund a frontier model, it needs to be the governed data and workflow layer one plugs into, and Informatica plus Contentful are it buying exactly that. You are still paying only 17.7x forward earnings for a 77.3% gross margin, 34.1% non-GAAP operating margin, $15.15B of free cash flow ($18.41 per share, a 7.1% FCF yield) and $66.3B of contracted backlog. The bull case never required reacceleration. It required growth not to break, and Q2 showed it did not.

Bear Case

The multiple moved 23%; the business moved very little. The 63%-of-revenue Agentforce Apps segment grew 7.6% — that is the core franchise, and it is still barely growing. Strip Informatica out and Q2 organic growth was roughly 6.4%, slower than the ~8.5% organic rate implied by Q1, and professional services — the leading indicator for bookings — went from +1.5% to −3.8%. Read the earnings quality before the earnings: GAAP EPS of $4.29 (+119%) contains $2,613M of gains on strategic investments, about $3.18 a share pre-tax, and those gains flow into the trailing P/E, the 21.99% net margin and the 19.38% ROE that now make the stock look cheap and healthy. Agentforce ARR of $1.5B is still only ~3% of revenue. Meanwhile the valuation cushion is gone: PEG crossed 1.0, the stock is 4.1% below its 52-week high, the consensus target of $268.61 implies just 4.1% upside, and Guggenheim held its target at $228 — 11.7% below spot — after reading the same quarter. Debt is $42.38B against $11.40B of cash, the $25B ASR settles in October and cannot be repeated, and two more acquisitions are pending at a 10.96% ROIC. The market spent nine days repricing a company whose organic growth rate got slower.

Top 3 Risks
01
Seat-based pricing erosion. Every successful Agentforce deployment is an argument for fewer human licences at the next renewal. Consumption pricing on work units and Flex Credits is the intended replacement, but it converts high-visibility subscription revenue into variable usage revenue that is harder to forecast and historically carries a lower multiple. At $1.5B of Agentforce ARR against a $43.9B revenue base — even at $3.9B including Data 360 — the replacement is not yet able to absorb a seat contraction. The apps segment growing 7.6% while the data segment grows 20.3% is that substitution already visible in the mix.
02
Organic growth is single-digit and got slower, not faster. FY2023 +18.3% → FY2024 +11.2% → FY2025 +8.7% → FY2026 +9.6%, with the raised FY2027 guide of 11–12% still including 3-plus points of Informatica and Q3 guided to carry over 4 points. Q2's organic rate was roughly 6.4% against ~8.5% in Q1, and the apps segment grew 7.6%. Management has publicly recommitted to organic reacceleration in H2 FY2027 and the price now assumes it. If it fails to appear, the risk is not just to numbers but to guidance credibility — and a 17.7x multiple has considerably further to fall than a 15.0x one did.
03
Leverage plus M&A, no longer a temptation but a habit. $42.38B of debt, a 0.84 current ratio and $11.40B of cash leave far less room than Salesforce has historically enjoyed, and the $25B ASR reaches final settlement in October. Informatica is tracking ahead of plan, but Contentful and Fin are now pending as well, together supplying the entire $200M FY2027 guidance raise. Salesforce's twenty-year record of large acquisitions is reflected in a 10.96% ROIC — barely above a plausible cost of capital, and slightly lower than last quarter. Buying the growth that organic execution is not delivering is the fastest route back to a lower multiple; the market is watching M&A discipline as closely as it is watching Agentforce.
  Industry Commentary
Agentic AI vs. the Seat
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Enterprise software has priced itself per named user for two decades. Agentic AI attacks that unit directly: the product's value proposition is that fewer people do the work. That was the fear that took the stock to $156.66 in June, and the August 26 print is the first real evidence against it: cRPO grew 14% in constant currency, not 10%, and combined Agentforce + Data 360 ARR reached ~$3.9B (+210%) — roughly 8% of guided revenue, up from 3%. Salesforce's counter is to move the meter, and the meter is moving: 3.2 billion Agentic Work Units in Q2, up 97% quarter-over-quarter, are consumption units rather than seats. The Anthropic "Claudeforce" partnership extends the same logic — own the governed data and workflow layer, rent the reasoning. The peer market-cap comparison this card previously carried (ServiceNow −28.5%, HubSpot −49.1%, SAP −20.7%, Microsoft −4.0%) was not re-sourced and is stale; Salesforce's own twelve-month change is now +0.9%, from −27.3% nine days ago. What has not changed is the arithmetic underneath: Agentforce ARR alone is still ~3% of revenue and the seat-based apps segment still grew 7.6%.
IT Budget Scrutiny & Seat Rationalisation
ONGOING HEADWIND
Q2 reported growth of 9.9% (Americas), 13.8% (Europe) and 9.1% (Asia Pacific) — constant-currency rates were not disclosed by region — again shows no regional idiosyncrasy, which tells you the slowdown is not a macro shock but a structural change in how enterprises buy. Buyers are consolidating vendors, auditing unused licences and pushing renewals toward shorter terms. The clearest evidence is the professional-services line, which went from +1.5% to −3.8% year-over-year: implementation demand typically leads subscription bookings, and a line that is now shrinking argues against an imminent booking inflection in exactly the half-year management has promised one. It shows up again in the gap between headline EPS growth (+103% non-GAAP, +119% GAAP) and free cash flow growth (guided +4–5% for FY2027): the earnings line is being driven by a shrinking share count and $2.6B of investment gains, while the cash line reflects what customers are actually buying.
Growth-to-Profitability: The Trade Is Done
EMERGING TAILWIND — WITH A CATCH
Software's decade-long repricing from growth to profitability is complete at Salesforce: operating margin went 2.07% (FY2022) → 5.93% → 17.21% → 20.23% → 21.47% (FY2026) GAAP, and 34.8% non-GAAP, following the activist pressure of 2022–23. The company now returns capital like a mature industrial — $27.5B in a single quarter, a dividend initiated after 25 years, a 20.4% payout ratio with room to grow. Bull implication: the re-rating on August 27 was precisely the market starting to pay something for Agentforce, and at 17.7x forward earnings with a 7.1% FCF yield it is not paying much yet; combined AI ARR of ~$3.9B growing 210% is the evidence the last edition said was missing, and the shrinking share count still means EPS rises on flat revenue. Bear implication: margin expansion is a one-time lever and it has been pulled — Q2 non-GAAP margin was 34.1%, below Q1's 34.8%, and full-year guidance of 34.3% sits between them. Payout ratio is 16.3%, so the dividend has room, but with margins near a ceiling, leverage at 1.10x D/E, the ASR settling in October and PEG through 1.0, there is no remaining lever except growth — which is precisely the variable that got slower in Q2. From here the stock has to be re-rated on cRPO and Agentforce dollars, and it has already been re-rated once on the promise of them.
