01 Company Overview

Salesforce is the largest independent enterprise application software company in the world — a cloud platform for sales, service, marketing, commerce, integration, analytics and, since 2024, autonomous AI agents. Fiscal 2026 (ended January 31, 2026) revenue was $41.53B, up 9.6% YoY, the first sub-10% growth year in the company's history as a public company; trailing-twelve-month revenue through April 2026 is $42.83B, up 11.0%, re-accelerated by the Informatica acquisition. At a $171.22B market capitalisation Salesforce ranks #126 globally and #75 in the United States — down from a $323.89B market cap at the end of 2024, a two-year decline of 47%.

The company's problem is not profitability. Operating margin has gone from 2.07% (FY2022) to 21.47% (FY2026) on a GAAP basis and 34.8% on a non-GAAP basis, and FCF is $14.66B on a trailing basis. The problem is growth and the durability of the pricing model. Salesforce sells seats. Agentic AI is explicitly designed to reduce the number of humans who need seats. That tension — not the P&L — is what has compressed the multiple to a 15.0x forward P/E.

Salesforce's moat is customer data that has been in one place for fifteen years. A large enterprise CRM instance is not a piece of software, it is a decade of custom objects, workflow rules, Apex code, integrations, permission models and audit history. Ripping it out is a multi-year, eight-figure programme with revenue risk attached, which is why Salesforce holds roughly 27% of tracked CRM installations — more than Microsoft Dynamics, SAP, Oracle and Odoo combined on the same measure — and why the company can carry a 77.6% gross margin and a 34.8% non-GAAP operating margin while growing at only 12% constant currency. The Informatica acquisition is a deliberate deepening of that moat: by owning data cataloguing, quality, lineage and master data management, Salesforce is trying to make itself the governed data layer that any AI agent — its own or a competitor's — has to query. Total remaining performance obligation of $67.9B against $42.8B of trailing revenue is the moat expressed as contract: 1.6 years of revenue is already signed.

The seat-based pricing model is structurally exposed. Salesforce charges per user per month. Agentforce's stated purpose is to let software do work that people currently do. If a 5,000-seat service organisation deploys agents and needs 3,500 humans, the renewal is 30% smaller even if the customer is delighted. Salesforce's answer is consumption pricing on work units and Flex Credits, but that transition converts predictable, high-visibility subscription revenue into variable usage revenue — a lower-quality, harder-to-forecast revenue stream that the market has historically valued at a lower multiple. Agentforce ARR of $1.2B is not yet remotely large enough to offset a seat contraction across a $42B base.

Growth has decelerated to the point where the growth premium is gone. FY2023 revenue grew 18.3%, FY2024 11.2%, FY2025 8.7%, FY2026 9.6%. The apps segment — 62% of revenue — grew 7% in constant currency last quarter. FY2027 guidance of $45.9–46.2B includes roughly 3 points from Informatica, implying organic growth around 8%. At 8% organic, Salesforce is a mature software company, and mature software companies trade at 15x forward earnings, which is exactly where it trades.

Competitive encirclement from both ends. Microsoft can bundle Dynamics 365 and Copilot into an Office/Azure agreement at effectively marginal price; at $3.62T market cap and 17.8% revenue growth it has no need to earn a return on CRM specifically. From below, HubSpot ($3.45B revenue, +21.1%) keeps moving upmarket, and AI-native entrants are building CRM-shaped products where the agent — not the record — is the primary interface, with no legacy seat revenue to protect. Salesforce is defending the middle.

Balance sheet flexibility has been consumed. Total debt is $42.55B against $11.84B of cash, debt-to-equity is 1.24 and the current ratio is 0.79. This is the direct consequence of returning $27.5B to shareholders in a single quarter, including a $25B accelerated share repurchase. The buyback was almost certainly the right capital allocation at these prices, but it removes the option to do it again at scale, and it means a genuine demand shock would now hit a levered balance sheet rather than a fortress one.

Notable One-Off Items

Q1 FY2027 contains four distortions large enough to change the picture, all disclosed in the quarter's reconciliation:

1. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is 13.7 points and reconciles exactly. GAAP operating margin 21.1%; non-GAAP 34.8%. The bridge is stock-based compensation of $870M (7.8% of revenue), amortisation of purchased intangibles of $561M (5.0%) and restructuring charges of $96M (0.9%). Stock-based compensation is a real cost to shareholders — it is dilution — and at nearly 8% of revenue it is the largest single reason the headline non-GAAP margin overstates economic profitability. Use the GAAP figure, or subtract SBC from the non-GAAP figure, but do not use 34.8% as if it were cash margin.

2. $558M of net gains on strategic investments flattered GAAP EPS. These are mark-to-market gains on Salesforce Ventures holdings, excluded from non-GAAP. On roughly 871M diluted shares that is about $0.64 per share pre-tax — meaningful against GAAP EPS of $2.42. They are non-operating, non-recurring and can reverse. GAAP EPS growth of 52% should be discounted accordingly.

3. Informatica is inorganic revenue. $444M of total revenue and $428M of subscription revenue in the quarter came from an acquisition that closed November 18, 2025, and therefore has no comparable in the year-ago base. Strip it out and Q1 total revenue growth falls from 13% to roughly 8.5%. Management itself flags that the FY2027 guide includes about 3 points of Informatica contribution.

4. The $25B accelerated share repurchase distorts every per-share metric. Salesforce returned $27.5B in one quarter ($27.1B buybacks, $365M dividends), retiring 103M shares upfront on the ASR. Diluted share count in the quarter was 871M; shares outstanding are now 819M. Market cap fell 27.3% year-over-year while the share price fell about 17.7% — the difference is share count. EPS growth of 34.8% is substantially a denominator story, and comparisons of EPS across the next four quarters will be distorted in the same direction.

Restructuring charges of $96M continuing into FY2027 also indicate that the headcount programme begun in 2023 has not fully concluded.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 15.0x forward earnings and 0.93x PEG for a business with a 77.6% gross margin, a 34.8% non-GAAP operating margin, $14.66B of free cash flow ($17.90 per share — an 8.6% FCF yield) and $67.9B of contracted backlog. cRPO — the honest forward indicator — is still growing 13% in constant currency, which is not the profile of a business being disrupted; it is the profile of one being priced as if it were. Management is converting the balance sheet into shareholder value at scale, retiring 103M shares in a single quarter and shrinking the count roughly 12% year-over-year, so even flat revenue produces rising EPS. And the Agentforce thesis has an underappreciated symmetry: Data 360 + Agentforce ARR is already ~$3.4B growing over 200%, and it is consumption-priced — if agents do displace seats, Salesforce owns the meter they run on, and Informatica makes it the governed data layer any agent must query. The bull case does not require growth to reaccelerate. It requires growth not to break.

Bear Case

Salesforce sells seats, and it is selling the technology that eliminates seats. The 62%-of-revenue Agentforce Apps segment grew 7% in constant currency — that is the core franchise, and it is barely growing. Strip Informatica out and total organic growth is roughly 8.5%, meaning the headline 13% is bought. Agentforce ARR of $1.2B is under 3% of revenue against 28.6 trillion tokens and 3.8 billion work units — enormous usage that is not converting into dollars, which is precisely the pattern that precedes a pricing-model reset rather than a growth reacceleration. Meanwhile the balance-sheet flexibility that funded the defence is spent: $42.55B of debt against $11.84B of cash, D/E of 1.24, current ratio 0.79, and a $25B ASR that cannot be repeated. Note also what the market did with the good news: record margins, record EPS and the largest buyback in company history were followed by a 17.8% decline in the next month to a five-year low. When a stock ignores a great quarter, the market is not disputing the quarter — it is disputing the model. Citigroup's Tyler Radke raised his target on August 18 to a level still below the current price.

Top 3 Risks
01
Seat-based pricing erosion. Every successful Agentforce deployment is an argument for fewer human licences at the next renewal. Consumption pricing on work units and Flex Credits is the intended replacement, but it converts high-visibility subscription revenue into variable usage revenue that is harder to forecast and historically carries a lower multiple. At $1.2B of Agentforce ARR against a $42.8B revenue base, the replacement is nowhere near able to absorb a seat contraction.
02
Organic growth is already single-digit and decelerating. FY2023 +18.3% → FY2024 +11.2% → FY2025 +8.7% → FY2026 +9.6%, with the FY2027 guide of ~11% including roughly 3 points of acquired Informatica revenue. The apps segment grew 7% CC last quarter. Management has publicly committed to organic reacceleration in H2 FY2027; if that fails to appear, the risk is not just to numbers but to guidance credibility, and low-growth software with credibility problems trades below 15x.
03
Leverage plus M&A temptation. $42.55B of debt, a 0.79 current ratio and $11.84B of cash leave far less room than Salesforce has historically enjoyed. The Informatica deal is tracking ahead of plan, but Salesforce's twenty-year record of large acquisitions is reflected in an 11.07% ROIC — barely above a plausible cost of capital. Another sizeable acquisition, funded with debt at this leverage, would be the fastest route to a lower multiple; the market is watching M&A discipline as closely as it is watching Agentforce.
  Industry Commentary
Agentic AI vs. the Seat
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Enterprise software has priced itself per named user for two decades. Agentic AI attacks that unit directly: the product's value proposition is that fewer people do the work. The evidence that the market believes this is in the tape — over twelve months, market caps fell 27.3% (CRM), 28.5% (ServiceNow), 49.1% (HubSpot) and 20.7% (SAP), while Microsoft, which sells the AI layer rather than the seats, fell 4.0%. The de-rating is not correlated with growth rate: HubSpot grew 21.1% and fell hardest. It is correlated with exposure to per-seat billing. Salesforce's counter is to move the meter — 3.8 billion Agentic Work Units (+111% QoQ) and 28.6 trillion tokens (+152% QoQ) are consumption units, not seats — but $1.2B of Agentforce ARR is under 3% of guided revenue. The transition arithmetic is not yet close to working.
IT Budget Scrutiny & Seat Rationalisation
ONGOING HEADWIND
Constant-currency growth of 11% (Americas), 12% (Europe) and 12% (Asia Pacific) is remarkably uniform — which tells you the slowdown is not a regional macro shock but a structural change in how enterprises buy. Buyers are consolidating vendors, auditing unused licences and pushing renewals toward shorter terms. The evidence sits in the professional-services line, up just 1.5% YoY: implementation demand typically leads subscription bookings, and its flatness argues against an imminent booking inflection. It also shows up in the gap between EPS growth (+34.8%) and free cash flow growth (guided +4–5% for FY2027): the earnings line is being driven by financial engineering, while the cash line reflects what customers are actually buying.
Growth-to-Profitability: The Trade Is Done
EMERGING TAILWIND — WITH A CATCH
Software's decade-long repricing from growth to profitability is complete at Salesforce: operating margin went 2.07% (FY2022) → 5.93% → 17.21% → 20.23% → 21.47% (FY2026) GAAP, and 34.8% non-GAAP, following the activist pressure of 2022–23. The company now returns capital like a mature industrial — $27.5B in a single quarter, a dividend initiated after 25 years, a 20.4% payout ratio with room to grow. Bull implication: at 15.0x forward earnings, 0.93 PEG and an 8.6% FCF yield, the market is paying nothing for Agentforce; any evidence of monetisation is upside, and the shrinking share count means EPS rises on flat revenue. Bear implication: margin expansion is a one-time lever and it has been pulled. Non-GAAP margin guidance for FY2027 is 34.3% — lower than the 34.8% just reported. With margins near a ceiling, leverage at 1.24x D/E and the ASR spent, there is no remaining lever except growth — which is precisely the variable in question. From here the stock has to be re-rated on cRPO and Agentforce dollars, or not at all.
