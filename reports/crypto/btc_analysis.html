01 Overview

Bitcoin is a digital currency that runs on a public network without a central operator. A shared ledger (the blockchain) records every transfer, and independently run computers (nodes) check each block against the same rules. The first block (the "genesis block") carries a timestamp of January 3, 2009, 18:15 UTC. Bitcoin.org describes the design as first published "in 2009 in a cryptography mailing list by Satoshi Nakamoto", and states that "Nobody owns the Bitcoin network much like no one owns the technology behind email." The original paper is hosted at bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf.

Consensus: proof of work. Miners bundle transactions into blocks and compete to find a valid block hash. The network adjusts the difficulty every 2,016 blocks so that blocks arrive every 10 minutes on average (Bitcoin Core nPowTargetSpacing = 10 * 60). Over the 12 months to September 22, 2026, 52,244 blocks were mined, an average of 10.06 minutes per block (our calculation from mempool.space block heights).

Supply and issuance. New bitcoin enter circulation only as the block subsidy. In Bitcoin Core's code (GetBlockSubsidy), the subsidy starts at 50 BTC and is halved every 210,000 blocks ("approximately every 4 years", as the code comment puts it). The MAX_MONEY constant is 21,000,000 BTC. Because each subsidy is rounded down to whole satoshis, the schedule adds up to 20,999,999.9769 BTC (our calculation). Bitcoin.org's FAQ: "The number of new bitcoins created each year is automatically halved over time until bitcoin issuance halts completely with a total of 21 million bitcoins in existence."

03 Network Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Strengths:

Largest crypto asset by market cap ($1.73T) Hash rate ~925 EH/s (7-day avg) Fixed, published issuance schedule Blocks produced since Jan 2009 Listed ETFs in US & Canada Open-source, many independent node operators

Bitcoin's main strength is network effect plus cost to attack. The 7-day average hash rate to September 22, 2026 was about 925 EH/s (exahashes per second; blockchain.com estimate). Rewriting recent blocks would require more computing power than that. Liquidity is concentrated here as well: thirteen US spot ETFs held about 1.21M BTC at their latest disclosures on or before September 22, 2026 (see 05). The rules are stable by design. The subsidy schedule in today's code (50 BTC, halved every 210,000 blocks) reproduces the issued supply exactly, and upgrades such as SegWit (2017) and Taproot (2021) were backward-compatible soft forks.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Security budget depends on the subsidy. Fees were 0.60% of miner revenue over the last 30 days. The subsidy halves at block 1,050,000 (around 2028, estimate). Unless fees or the bitcoin price rise, the amount paid to secure the network falls with each halving.
02
Price volatility. Annualised volatility over 60 months was 56.3%, against 15.8% for the S&P 500 (our calculation). The largest fall in daily closes over the five years was −76.6% (November 8, 2021 → November 21, 2022). The price on the banner date is 30.9% below the October 6, 2025 record close.
03
Custody is concentrating. One company (Strategy) reports 846,000 BTC, 4.2% of supply. One ETF (IBIT) reported 734,261 BTC at June 30, 2026, 3.7% of supply. Most wrapped BTC used elsewhere is held by a few custodians. A failure at one of them would not change Bitcoin's rules, but it would affect a large number of holders.
04
Rule changes are slow. Merges into Bitcoin Core go through five keyholders, and any consensus change needs broad node adoption. That protects the fixed supply. It also means responding to a new technical threat, such as future quantum computers capable of breaking today's signature schemes, would need a coordinated soft fork that has no fixed timetable.
05
History of software bugs. In August 2010 a transaction in block 74,638 created about 184 billion BTC through a value-overflow bug (CVE-2010-5139), and the chain was patched and reorganised. In March 2013 incompatible software versions split the chain (BIP 50). Both show that the software is the rulebook: a bug in it is a bug in the rules.

Self-custody vs exchange custody. Holding bitcoin directly means controlling the private keys, usually in a hardware or software wallet. No third party can freeze or lose the coins, but a lost key cannot be recovered. Holding on an exchange means the exchange controls the keys, and the customer has a claim on the exchange. Mt. Gox shows what that can mean in practice: the former exchange's creditors are still being repaid under court-supervised rehabilitation, with an October 31, 2026 deadline. An ETF holds coins through a named custodian and trades in a brokerage account, and its fee is charged on assets every year.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Supply is fixed and fully scheduled: 95.7% is already issued, annual issuance is 0.82% and falls to about 0.4% after the 2028 halving. Access has widened. Thirteen US spot ETFs held about 1.21M BTC at their latest disclosures, Canadian ETFs are listed on the TSX, and 2025–26 US policy moved toward clearer rules (the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve order; the SEC–CFTC interpretation). The network has produced blocks for more than 17 years, and at ~925 EH/s it is costly to attack. Correlation with equities of 0.51 over 60 months means it has not simply tracked the stock market.

Bear Case

Over the matched 60-month window, bitcoin returned slightly less than the S&P 500 price index (10.7% vs 11.2% a year) with 3.6 times the volatility, and a 76.6% peak-to-trough fall in 2022. Fees are 0.60% of miner revenue, so the network's security budget depends almost entirely on the subsidy, which halves again around 2028. Holdings are concentrating in a few vehicles: one company holds 4.2% of supply and one ETF 3.7%. US market-structure legislation (the CLARITY Act) failed a Senate procedural vote 49–50 on September 15, 2026, so the division of oversight between regulators is still not set in statute.

Top 3 Risks

01
Deep drawdowns. −76.6% on daily closes in 2021–22 and −53.1% from the October 2025 record to the June 2026 month-end. Annualised volatility is 56.3%. In the first episode, month-end closes stayed below half of the November 2021 peak close from May 2022 to September 2023.
02
Security budget after the subsidy. Miners earned about $40.2M a day over the last 30 days, and 0.60% of that came from fees. Each halving cuts the subsidy by half. If fees do not rise, the security of the network depends increasingly on the price.
03
Custodial concentration. About 11.3% of supply sits with thirteen US ETFs and ten listed companies, and the largest wrapped tokens rely on single custodians. A failure or forced sale at a large custodian or treasury company would not change the protocol, but it could move the price and put holders' claims at risk.

The SEC approved spot bitcoin ETP listings on January 10, 2024. At their latest disclosures on or before September 22, 2026, thirteen US spot ETFs held about 1.21M BTC, 6.0% of supply. The largest single fund, IBIT, held 734,261 BTC at June 30, 2026. Morgan Stanley's trust listed on April 8, 2026 with a 0.14% fee, the lowest in the table in 05. Rules in the US, 2025–2026
IN PROGRESS

The March 2025 executive order set up a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve funded with forfeited coins and stated no quantity. In March 2026 the SEC, joined by the CFTC, published an interpretation dividing crypto assets into five categories, and listed bitcoin as an example of a digital commodity; the Chairman said "most crypto assets are not themselves securities." The CFTC has treated bitcoin as a commodity since 2015. In August 2026 the SEC proposed offering exemptions for non-security crypto assets. The market-structure bill (CLARITY Act) is the main unresolved item: it passed the House in July 2025 but failed a Senate cloture vote on September 15, 2026.
