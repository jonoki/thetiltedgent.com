01 Overview

What it is. Ethereum is a public network that records transfers of its own currency, ether (ETH), and also runs programs that anyone can publish to it. Ethereum.org puts it this way: "Ethereum is a decentralized blockchain network and software development platform, powered by the cryptocurrency ether (ETH)." The programs are called smart contracts. Every action on the network, from a simple payment to a trade on a lending or exchange contract, pays a fee in ETH, which ethereum.org calls the "transaction fee (or gas fee)". A shared ledger (the blockchain) holds the record, and independently run computers (nodes) check each new block against the same rules.

How it was created. The founding document is a whitepaper titled "A Next-Generation Smart Contract and Decentralized Application Platform". Ethereum.org's history page dates it to November 27, 2013: "The introductory paper, published in 2013 by Vitalik Buterin, the founder of Ethereum, before the project's launch in 2015." The copy of the paper on ethereum.org carries a different year: "This whitepaper was published in 2014, before Ethereum launched." The paper opens by building on Bitcoin: "Satoshi Nakamoto's development of Bitcoin in 2009 has often been hailed as a radical development in money and currency". A public sale of ether began on July 22, 2014; per ethereum.org, "Ether officially went on sale for 42 days. You could buy it with BTC." The network went live as "Frontier" on July 30, 2015. The genesis block's own data carries no date (block 0's timestamp field is zero). The first mined block, block 1, is timestamped July 30, 2015, 15:26:28 UTC, and its extra-data field reads "Geth/v1.0.0/linux/go1.4.2": version 1.0.0 of the Go client, Geth.

The stated purpose. The whitepaper: "The intent of Ethereum is to create an alternative protocol for building decentralized applications, providing a different set of tradeoffs that we believe will be very useful for a large class of decentralized applications, with particular emphasis on situations where rapid development time, security for small and rarely used applications, and the ability of different applications to very efficiently interact, are important." Its method is "a blockchain with a built-in Turing-complete programming language, allowing anyone to write smart contracts and decentralized applications where they can create their own arbitrary rules for ownership, transaction formats and state transition functions."

Consensus: proof of stake. Ethereum ran on proof of work (mining, like Bitcoin) until The Merge. In the block data, block 15,537,393 (September 15, 2022, 06:42:42 UTC) is the last block with a mining difficulty, and block 15,537,394 (06:42:59 UTC) is the first with difficulty zero. Ethereum.org: "Staking is the act of depositing ETH to activate a validator, a participant in Ethereum's consensus protocol." A validator "requires at least 32 ETH and can hold up to 2048 ETH". Validators that break the rules are penalised: "provable misbehavior, such as signing two conflicting blocks, results in slashing: part of the validator's stake is destroyed and the validator is forcibly removed from the network."

Supply, issuance and the fee burn. ETH has no maximum supply. Ethereum.org: "Unlike Bitcoin's fixed 21 million cap, ETH has dynamic supply mechanics". New ETH is paid to validators, and the amount depends on how much ETH is staked. Ethereum.org's issuance page estimates about 1,700 ETH a day "based on about 14 million total ETH staked", against about 13,000 ETH a day paid to miners before The Merge. Since the London upgrade (August 5, 2021, block 12,965,000, EIP-1559), the base part of every transaction fee is burned. When fees are high the burn can exceed issuance and the supply shrinks; when fees are low the supply grows. Over the 12 months to September 22, 2026 the supply grew (cards below).

Client software
7 + 6 clients
Ethereum has no single reference implementation. Ethereum.org lists seven execution clients (Geth, Nethermind, Besu, Erigon, ethrex, Reth, and EthereumJS in beta) and six consensus clients (Lighthouse, Lodestar, Nimbus, Prysm, Teku, Grandine). Upgrade process
Named hard forks
Protocol changes ship as coordinated upgrades that every client implements, roughly once or twice a year recently: Dencun (March 2024), Pectra (May 2025), Fusaka (December 2025). The next, Glamsterdam, was "in public testing" per the EF's September 9, 2026 post. The EF Protocol team's September 7, 2026 post plans on "Shipping Glamsterdam in December 2026", then the Hegotá upgrade, with full post-quantum readiness targeted for December 2029.

03 Network Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Strengths:

43.0M ETH staked (35.25% of supply) 56.5% of DeFi value locked (DefiLlama) $147B of dollar stablecoins on-chain 13 client implementations Blocks produced since Jul 2015 Listed ETFs in US & Canada

Ethereum's main strength is where the applications and the dollars already are. On September 22, 2026 it held 56.5% of the value DefiLlama tracks in on-chain applications and $147.26B of dollar stablecoins. Layer-2 networks connect to it through bridge contracts on Ethereum: $3.56B sat in Arbitrum's and $3.10B in Base's (DefiLlama). Its security rests on 43.0M staked ETH spread across 902,665 validators. Software risk is spread across 13 independently written clients in several programming languages, so a bug in one client does not have to halt the chain. Ten US spot ETFs held about 5.28M ETH at June 30, 2026 (see 05).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
No fixed supply, and the burn is currently small. Supply grew 0.82% over the last 12 months because the fee burn offset only 2.3% of new issuance. The supply shrinks only when fees are high, so ETH holders depend on continued demand for block space.
02
Price volatility. Annualised volatility over 60 months was 75.2%, against 56.3% for bitcoin and 15.8% for the S&P 500 (our calculation). The largest fall in daily closes over the five years was −79.4% (November 8, 2021 → June 18, 2022). The banner-date price is 43.0% below the record daily close of August 22, 2025.
03
Stake and holdings are concentrated. Lido accounts for 22.7% of staked ETH. One company, BitMine, reports 5,983,940 ETH (4.9% of supply), of which 5,067,309 ETH is staked. Large pools and custodians decide how a big share of validators is run.
04
Application risk sits on top of the chain. Smart contracts can have bugs. In the 2016 DAO attack, per ethereum.org, "an insecure contract was drained of over 3.6 million ETH"; the network responded with a hard fork at block 1,920,000 that moved the funds to a withdrawal contract. Bridges and layer-2 networks add their own contracts and operators.
05
An ambitious upgrade schedule. The EF Protocol team plans five more hard forks after Glamsterdam to reach full post-quantum readiness by December 2029, "an average cadence of 7.2 months per fork", which it calls "quite aggressive". Each upgrade has to be implemented in every client and adopted by node operators.

Self-custody, exchange custody and staking. Holding ether directly means controlling the private keys, usually in a hardware or software wallet. No third party can freeze the coins, but a lost key cannot be recovered. Holding on an exchange means the exchange controls the keys, and the customer has a claim on the exchange. ETH can also be staked: directly (32 ETH or more per validator), through a pool or liquid-staking token such as Lido's, or through a fund that stakes. Staking earns rewards (an APR of 2.59% on validatorqueue.com on September 22) but adds slashing risk and, when many validators queue to exit, delays in getting ETH back. An ETF holds coins through a named custodian and trades in a brokerage account, and its fee is charged on assets every year; funds that stake also keep part of the staking rewards.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Ethereum is where most on-chain finance sits: 56.5% of the value DefiLlama tracks in applications, $147B of dollar stablecoins, and layer-2 networks such as Arbitrum connect to it through bridge contracts on Ethereum. Its security stake keeps growing: 43.0M ETH (35.25% of supply) was staked on September 22, 2026, with 1.73M ETH more in the entry queue. In the US, the SEC's March 2026 interpretation lists ether as a digital commodity and says protocol staking does not involve the offer of a security, and spot ETFs now stake. The ten US ETFs and eight companies named in 05 together disclosed about 9.7% of supply. Development continues on a published roadmap, with Glamsterdam planned for December 2026.

Bear Case

Over the matched 60 months ether lost 28.2% while bitcoin gained 66.5% and the S&P 500 69.9%, with 75.2% annualised volatility and a 79.4% peak-to-trough fall in 2022. Supply grows when fees are low: over the last 12 months the burn offset only 2.3% of new issuance and supply rose 0.82%. Ownership and stake are concentrated: one company reports 4.9% of supply, and Lido accounts for 22.7% of staked ETH. US market-structure legislation failed a Senate procedural vote 49–50 on September 15, 2026, so the split of oversight between regulators is still not set in statute.

Top 3 Risks

01
Deep drawdowns. −79.4% on daily closes in 2021–22 and −67.6% from the August 2025 record close to the June 2026 low close. Annualised volatility is 75.2%, higher than bitcoin's 56.3%. Month-end closes were below half of the November 2021 month-end high in 30 of the 57 month-ends after it.
02
Fee demand and supply growth. Network fees were 55,556 ETH over 12 months and the burn was 23,366 ETH, against about 1.01M ETH issued. If on-chain fee demand stays low, supply keeps growing at about 0.8% a year.
03
Concentration in a few stakers and holders. Lido (22.7% of stake), BitMine (4.9% of supply, mostly staked) and ten US ETFs (4.3%) hold large blocks. A failure, a forced sale or a client bug at a large staking operator would not change the protocol's rules, but it could move the price and lead to slashing penalties on the affected stake.
Sector Commentary
ETFs that stake
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

The SEC approved spot ether ETP listings on May 23, 2024, with staking explicitly outside the order. Since then the wrappers have changed. Grayscale's two trusts began staking on October 6, 2025 and were renamed "Staking" ETFs; the 21Shares trust had 86.42% of its ether staked at June 30, 2026. The SEC's March 2026 interpretation states that protocol staking, as described there, does not involve the offer and sale of a security. The largest fund, ETHA (2.70M ETH at June 30, 2026, down from 3.47M at December 31, 2025), discloses no staking; BlackRock's separate iShares Staked Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHB), listed March 12, 2026, had 86.9% of its 288,417 ETH staked at June 30, 2026. Staking funds keep part of the rewards: Grayscale's fees take 6% (Mini) and 23% (ETHE) of gross staking rewards, and the 21Shares sponsor takes 25%. In Canada, CSA staff guidance from 2023 sets expectations for funds that stake, including using a third-party validator.

Company treasuries
CONCENTRATION

The eight companies named above disclose about 6.50M ETH, and one of them dominates: BitMine reports 5,983,940 ETH as of September 20, 2026, which it describes as "4.9% of the ETH supply". At the banner-date price of $2,752.63 that stake is worth about $16.5B (5,983,940 × $2,752.63, our calculation). 5,067,309 of those ETH are staked. Others have sold or pledged: GameSquare sold 1,209 ETH in July 2026, Forum Markets (formerly ETHZilla) has pledged all 12,441 of its ETH as loan collateral, and FG Nexus's June 30, 2026 10-Q states "the Company no longer holds any cryptocurrency assets".

Rules in the US, 2024–2026
IN PROGRESS

The CFTC's KuCoin complaint of March 2024 described ether as a commodity; that case ended with a consent order in March 2026. In March 2026 the SEC, joined by the CFTC, published an interpretation dividing crypto assets into five categories and naming Ether (ETH) as an example of a digital commodity. In August 2026 the SEC proposed offering exemptions for non-security crypto assets sold under an investment contract. The market-structure bill (CLARITY Act) is the main unresolved item: it passed the House in July 2025 but failed a Senate cloture vote on September 15, 2026.
