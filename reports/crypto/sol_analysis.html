01 Overview
In brief — why it matters
  1. What it is used for today. Trading, lending, staking and dollar stablecoins. On November 1, 2026 applications on Solana held $5.41B, third behind Ethereum ($43.70B) and Base ($5.89B) on DefiLlama, and $15.43B of dollar stablecoins sat on the chain, up 5.7% in a year. Users sent about 119 million transactions a day (Trillium, epoch 1073, our calculation).
  2. The adoption story. First block March 16, 2020. After the SEC approved generic listing standards for spot commodity funds on September 17, 2025, Bitwise's BSOL began trading on October 28, 2025; eight US spot SOL trusts held 11.54M SOL at June 30, 2026. On March 17, 2026 the SEC and CFTC named Solana (SOL) among their "Examples of digital commodities". On October 21, 2026 the Alpenglow consensus upgrade went live on mainnet; Anza says it cut the time for a transaction to become irreversible from 12.8 seconds to about 150 milliseconds.
  3. What moves the price. Supply: 23.97M new SOL in 12 months (3.8%), on a schedule falling 15% a year toward 1.5%. Flows: US spot trusts hold 1.8% of supply and eight companies 2.9%; the largest, Forward Industries, reported 8.50M SOL on September 30, 2026. Risk appetite: beta 2.96 and correlation 0.40 to the S&P 500, beta 1.64 to bitcoin, over 60 months (our calculation).
  4. Competition. Ethereum holds 53.1% of the value DefiLlama tracks in applications, Solana 6.6%. Base ($5.89B) is ahead of Solana, with Tron ($5.36B) and BNB Smart Chain ($5.23B) close behind.
  5. The open question. Whether usage grows enough to matter against issuance: $234.4M of fees in 12 months against about $2.26B of new supply.

What it is. Solana is a public blockchain: a shared ledger, checked by independently run computers (validators), that records transfers of its currency, SOL, and runs programs anyone can publish. Solana.com calls it "the leading high performance network powering internet capital markets, payments, and crypto applications." Transactions pay fees in SOL, and SOL is what validators stake to secure the network.

How it was created. The founding document is the whitepaper "Solana: A new architecture for a high performance blockchain" (v0.8.13) by Anatoly Yakovenko. Solana.com's own posts name Yakovenko "Solana Labs co-founder and CEO" (2021) and Raj Gokal "co-founder of Solana" (2022). The first block (slot 0) is timestamped March 16, 2020, 14:29:00 UTC in the chain's own data.

The stated purpose. The whitepaper's abstract: "This paper proposes a new blockchain architecture based on Proof of History (PoH) - a proof for verifying order and passage of time between events." It claims "throughput up to 710k transactions per second is possible with todays hardware."

How it works. Proof of history was designed as a cryptographic clock that orders transactions (whitepaper). Validators vote on blocks with a weight equal to the SOL delegated to them (proof of stake); since October 21, 2026 that voting runs on Alpenglow, which replaced the consensus protocol based on proof of history and TowerBFT (SIMD-0326). There is no maximum supply: new SOL is paid to stakers at 8% a year initially, falling 15% a year to a long-term 1.5% (solana.com). Half of each base fee is burned.

Founding and design detail

An SEC complaint of 2023 (section 03) says Solana Labs was "founded in 2018". The Alpenglow proposal describes the design it replaces as "based on Proof-of-History and TowerBFT". Solana.com's 2020 review: "before the end of Q1 in March, the Mainnet Beta network was officially initialized with the help of the worldwide validator community." The whitepaper defines proof of history as "a sequence of computation that can provide a way to cryptographically verify passage of time between two events". Fees (solana.com docs): each signature pays a base fee of "5,000 lamports", "split 50% burned / 50% to the validator"; priority fees go "100% to the validator".

How the supply figures were built

Total supply. Supply changes only through reward issuance and fee burns. The previous edition's figure, from the network's getSupply call less the rewards its EpochRewards account records, was 634,529,659 SOL at the start of epoch 1040 (05:09 UTC, September 22, 2026), net of fee burns. Adding Trillium's inflation rewards for epochs 1040 to 1073 (2,330,475 SOL) and subtracting the burned half of base fees over the same epochs (35,128 SOL, equal to Trillium's validator signature fees) gives 636,825,006 SOL at the end of epoch 1073 (05:05 UTC, November 1, 2026), which rounds to 636.83M SOL. Circulating supply of 590,150,254 SOL is CoinGecko's market cap divided by its price at 00:00 UTC on November 2, 2026.

Issuance. Sum of Trillium's per-epoch inflation rewards (validator plus delegator) for epochs 873 to 1073, which ran from November 1, 2025 (03:10 UTC) to November 1, 2026 (05:05 UTC): 23,973,727 SOL. 3.8% = 23,973,727 ÷ 636,825,006. Staked SOL is Trillium's "total_active_stake" for epoch 1073 (433,649,347 SOL) and epoch 873 (413,806,752 SOL). The inflation parameters (initial 0.08, taper 0.15, terminal 0.015) are from the network's getInflationGovernor call and solana.com's staking page. The protocol rate was 3.58% a year in epoch 1073 (Trillium's inflation_rate); realised issuance over the year was higher because the rate fell during it.

Epochs. An epoch is 432,000 slots (getEpochSchedule). Epoch 1073 ran from October 31, 03:24 UTC to November 1, 2026, 05:05 UTC: 25 hours 42 minutes, 212 ms per slot on average (Trillium); epoch 873, a year earlier, lasted 48 hours 4 minutes. Delegation, per the Anza docs: "Any number of Stake accounts can delegate to a single Vote account"; stake warms up and cools down at a limited rate per epoch. Frankendancer, per the Firedancer repository, "uses the Firedancer networking stack and block production components"; "execution and consensus" use "the Agave validator code". Jito maintains its own fork of the validator (jito-foundation/jito-solana).

03 Network Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Strengths:

433.6M SOL staked (68.1%) #3 chain by application value (DefiLlama) $15.4B dollar stablecoins Two independent client codebases ~119M user transactions a day Listed ETFs in US & Canada

Solana's strength is throughput at low cost on one chain: about 119 million transactions a day, at under a cent each on average (our calculation).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A record of halts. Official post-mortems cover six mainnet outages from September 2021 to February 2024; the longest, in February 2023, ran about 20 hours until block production resumed (04).
02
Stake concentration. 18 validators hold more than a third of active stake (Trillium, epoch 1073, our calculation); clients built on Firedancer ran 12.5% in epoch 1039.
03
Issuance dwarfs fees. 3.8% of supply issued in 12 months, against fees worth about 10% of that. Holders who do not stake are diluted.
04
Extreme volatility. 113.9% annualised over 60 months, twice bitcoin's 53.7%.

Formulas and data notes

Monthly return rt = Pt/Pt−1 − 1 on 61 month-end closes (Oct 31, 2021 … Oct 31, 2026), giving 60 returns. SOL, BTC and ETH use the Yahoo close on the last UTC day of each month; the S&P 500 uses the Yahoo ^GSPC close on the last trading day (price index, no dividends). Volatility = stdev(r) × √12. Sharpe = mean(e) / stdev(e) × √12, where e = r − rf is the monthly excess return and rf = the 3-month Treasury par yield at the prior month-end ÷ 12 (U.S. Treasury daily par yield curve; average 3.94% a year). β = cov(rSOL, rX) / var(rX); ρ = cov / (σSOLσX), sample (n−1) moments. 5-yr annualised = (Pend/Pstart)1/5 − 1; on month-ends Oct 2021 → Oct 2026 it is −14.0% a year. Max drawdown = min over t of (Pt / maxs≤t Ps − 1) on daily closes from Nov 1, 2021.

Market cap uses CoinGecko's circulating supply at 00:00 UTC on November 2, 2026 (market cap ÷ price in the same snapshot). User transactions a day = 127,297,497 ÷ (1,542 minutes ÷ 1,440). "18 validators" = the smallest number of validators whose epoch-1073 active stake sums to more than one-third of the total (Trillium per-validator data). Staker yield = Trillium's average delegator total APY for epoch 1073. Fee per transaction = $367,710 ÷ about 119M transactions. Fees-to-issuance = $234.4M ÷ (23.97M × $94.45). Client shares (epoch 1039) group Trillium's client labels as its API documentation defines them: Jito-labelled = Jito_Labs 28.5% + Jito_BAM 34.1%; Harmonic 22.0%; Firedancer-based = Frankendancer 6.6% + Firedancer 3.5% + Harmonic_Major ("Harmonic client running on Firedancer"), FD_Harmonic and FireBAM 2.4%; others = Rakurai, Agave, Raiku and unlabelled.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What would improve the picture

Usage that turns into fees, narrowing the gap between what users pay and what issuance costs holders. Faster disinflation (SIMD-0550) would cut new supply sooner. Alpenglow, on mainnet since October 21, 2026, running without a halt and a larger Firedancer share would answer the two old criticisms: halts and one dominant codebase.

What would worsen it

Another multi-hour halt under the new consensus protocol. Application value that keeps falling while issuance stays near 4% a year. Three chains within 9% of Solana's application value. With a beta near 3, a broad risk-off move hits SOL harder than bitcoin or stocks.

Top 3 Risks

01
Deep drawdowns. −96.3% in 2021–22; −76.3% from the January 2025 record to the June 2026 low.
02
Reliability under a new consensus protocol. Six halts from 2021 to 2024; Alpenglow, live since October 21, 2026, changes how blocks are finalised.
03
Concentration. 18 validators hold over a third of stake; Forward Industries reports 8.50M SOL (1.3% of supply); BSOL holds 70% of US trust SOL.
