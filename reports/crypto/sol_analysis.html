01 Overview
In brief — why it matters
  1. What it is used for today. Trading, lending, staking and dollar stablecoins. On September 22, 2026 applications on Solana held $6.46B, second only to Ethereum ($54.47B) on DefiLlama, and $17.11B of dollar stablecoins sat on the chain, up 33.9% in a year. Users sent about 178 million non-vote transactions a day (Trillium, epoch 1039, our calculation).
  2. The adoption story. First block March 16, 2020. After the SEC approved generic listing standards for spot commodity funds on September 17, 2025, Bitwise's BSOL began trading on October 28, 2025; eight US spot SOL trusts held 11.54M SOL at June 30, 2026. On March 17, 2026 the SEC and CFTC named Solana (SOL) among their "Examples of digital commodities".
  3. What moves the price. Supply: 24.36M new SOL in 12 months (3.8%), on a schedule falling 15% a year toward 1.5%. Flows: US spot trusts hold 1.8% of supply and eight companies 3.0%; the largest, Forward Industries, reported about 8.16M SOL on September 21, 2026. Risk appetite: beta 2.85 and correlation 0.39 to the S&P 500, beta 1.55 to bitcoin, over 60 months (our calculation).
  4. Competition. Ethereum holds 56.5% of the value DefiLlama tracks in applications, Solana 6.7%, with Base ($6.25B), BNB Smart Chain ($5.91B) and Tron ($5.70B) close behind.
  5. The open question. Whether usage grows enough to matter against issuance: $238.6M of fees in 12 months against about $2.89B of new supply.

What it is. Solana is a public blockchain: a shared ledger, checked by independently run computers (validators), that records transfers of its currency, SOL, and runs programs anyone can publish. Solana.com calls it "the leading high performance network powering internet capital markets, payments, and crypto applications." Transactions pay fees in SOL, and SOL is what validators stake to secure the network.

How it was created. The founding document is the whitepaper "Solana: A new architecture for a high performance blockchain" (v0.8.13) by Anatoly Yakovenko. Solana.com's own posts name Yakovenko "Solana Labs co-founder and CEO" (2021) and Raj Gokal "co-founder of Solana" (2022). The first block (slot 0) is timestamped March 16, 2020, 14:29:00 UTC in the chain's own data.

The stated purpose. The whitepaper's abstract: "This paper proposes a new blockchain architecture based on Proof of History (PoH) - a proof for verifying order and passage of time between events." It claims "throughput up to 710k transactions per second is possible with todays hardware."

How it works. Proof of history works as a cryptographic clock that orders transactions (whitepaper). On top of it, validators vote on blocks with a weight equal to the SOL delegated to them (proof of stake). There is no maximum supply: new SOL is paid to stakers at 8% a year initially, falling 15% a year to a long-term 1.5% (solana.com). Half of each base fee is burned.

Founding and design detail

An SEC complaint of 2023 (section 03) says Solana Labs was "founded in 2018". The Alpenglow proposal describes the current design as "based on Proof-of-History and TowerBFT". Solana.com's 2020 review: "before the end of Q1 in March, the Mainnet Beta network was officially initialized with the help of the worldwide validator community." The whitepaper defines proof of history as "a sequence of computation that can provide a way to cryptographically verify passage of time between two events". Fees (solana.com docs): each signature pays a base fee of "5,000 lamports", "split 50% burned / 50% to the validator"; priority fees go "100% to the validator".

How the supply figures were built

Total supply. Supply changes only through reward issuance and fee burns. The network's getSupply call (slot 449,936,572, epoch 1041) less the rewards its EpochRewards account records for the start of epoch 1041 gives 634,529,659 SOL: the supply from the start of epoch 1040 (05:09 UTC, September 22, 2026) net of fee burns, which run at about 1,300 SOL an epoch (the burned half of base fees). At the close of September 22 that rounds to 634.53M SOL. Circulating supply of 587,507,292 SOL is CoinGecko's market cap divided by its price at 00:00 UTC on September 23, 2026.

Issuance. Sum of Trillium's per-epoch inflation rewards (validator plus delegator) for epochs 853 to 1039, which ran from September 22, 2025 (10:28 UTC) to September 22, 2026 (05:09 UTC): 24,362,585 SOL. 3.8% = 24,362,585 ÷ 634,529,659. Staked SOL is Trillium's "total_active_stake" for epoch 1039 (439,905,519 SOL) and epoch 853 (410,622,510 SOL). The inflation parameters (initial 0.08, taper 0.15, terminal 0.015) are from the network's getInflationGovernor call and solana.com's staking page. The protocol rate was 3.64% a year in epoch 1039 (our calculation: getInflationRate returns 3.634% for epoch 1041, and the rate only steps down between epochs); realised issuance over the year was higher because the rate fell during it.

Epochs. An epoch is 432,000 slots (getEpochSchedule). Epoch 1039 ran from September 20, 21:06 UTC to September 22, 2026, 05:09 UTC: 32 hours 3 minutes, 265 ms per slot on average (Trillium); epoch 853, a year earlier, lasted 47 hours 38 minutes. Delegation, per the Anza docs: "Any number of Stake accounts can delegate to a single Vote account"; stake warms up and cools down at a limited rate per epoch. Frankendancer, per the Firedancer repository, "uses the Firedancer networking stack and block production components"; "execution and consensus" use "the Agave validator code". Jito maintains its own fork of the validator (jito-foundation/jito-solana).

03 Network Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Strengths:

439.9M SOL staked (69.3%) #2 chain by application value (DefiLlama) $17.1B dollar stablecoins Two independent client codebases ~178M user transactions a day Listed ETFs in US & Canada

Solana's strength is throughput at low cost on one chain: about 395 million transactions a day including votes, at under a cent each on average (our calculation).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A record of halts. Official post-mortems cover six mainnet outages from September 2021 to February 2024; the longest, in February 2023, ran about 20 hours until block production resumed (04).
02
Stake concentration. 18 validators hold more than a third of active stake (Trillium, epoch 1039, our calculation); clients built on Firedancer run 12.5%.
03
Issuance dwarfs fees. 3.8% of supply issued in 12 months, against fees worth about 8% of that. Holders who do not stake are diluted.
04
Extreme volatility. 115.3% annualised over 60 months, twice bitcoin's 56.3%.

Formulas and data notes

Monthly return rt = Pt/Pt−1 − 1 on 61 month-end closes (Aug 31, 2021 … Aug 31, 2026), giving 60 returns. SOL, BTC and ETH use the Yahoo close on the last UTC day of each month; the S&P 500 uses the Yahoo ^GSPC close on the last trading day (price index, no dividends). Volatility = stdev(r) × √12. Sharpe = mean(e) / stdev(e) × √12, where e = r − rf is the monthly excess return and rf = the 3-month Treasury par yield at the prior month-end ÷ 12 (U.S. Treasury daily par yield curve; average 3.80% a year). β = cov(rSOL, rX) / var(rX); ρ = cov / (σSOLσX), sample (n−1) moments. 5-yr annualised = (Pend/Pstart)1/5 − 1; on month-ends Aug 2021 → Aug 2026 it is −1.0% a year. Max drawdown = min over t of (Pt / maxs≤t Ps − 1) on daily closes from Sep 22, 2021.

Market cap uses CoinGecko's circulating supply at 00:00 UTC on September 23, 2026 (market cap ÷ price in the same snapshot). User transactions a day = 237,342,873 ÷ (1,923 minutes ÷ 1,440). "18 validators" = the smallest number of validators whose epoch-1039 active stake sums to more than one-third of the total (Trillium per-validator data). Staker yield = Trillium's average delegator total APY for epoch 1039. Fee per transaction = $1,097,157 ÷ about 395M transactions. Fees-to-issuance = $238.6M ÷ (24.36M × $118.51). Client shares group Trillium's client labels as its API documentation defines them: Jito-labelled = Jito_Labs 28.5% + Jito_BAM 34.1%; Harmonic 22.0%; Firedancer-based = Frankendancer 6.6% + Firedancer 3.5% + Harmonic_Major ("Harmonic client running on Firedancer"), FD_Harmonic and FireBAM 2.4%; others = Rakurai, Agave, Raiku and unlabelled.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What would improve the picture

Usage that turns into fees, narrowing the gap between what users pay and what issuance costs holders. Faster disinflation (SIMD-0550) would cut new supply sooner. Alpenglow on mainnet and a larger Firedancer share would answer the two old criticisms: halts and one dominant codebase.

What would worsen it

Another multi-hour halt during the consensus change. Application value that keeps falling while issuance stays near 4% a year. Three chains within 12% of Solana's application value. With a beta near 3, a broad risk-off move hits SOL harder than bitcoin or stocks.

Deep drawdowns. −96.3% in 2021–22; −76.3% from the January 2025 record to the June 2026 low.

Concentration. 18 validators hold over a third of stake; Forward Industries reports about 8.16M SOL (1.3% of supply); BSOL holds 70% of US trust SOL.
