01 Overview
In brief — why it matters
  • Used for. Payments and trading on the built-in exchange: 2,356,179 transactions on September 22, 2026 (ledger data); 40.4% payments in XRPScan's 30-day count. RLUSD, Ripple's stablecoin: $1.10B on the ledger; all other applications: $45.7M (DefiLlama).
  • Adoption. June 2012: the ledger launches and 80 billion XRP go to the company now called Ripple. July 13, 2023: a court rules Ripple's exchange sales of XRP were not offers of investment contracts. Nov–Dec 2025: five US spot XRP trusts list; they held 906.8M XRP at June 30, 2026.
  • What moves the price. No XRP is issued. Ripple held 37.66B XRP at June 30, 2026, 32.6B in escrow releasing up to 1B a month; ETF flows add demand. Over 60 months: beta 2.18 and correlation 0.24 to the S&P 500, correlation 0.53 with bitcoin (our calculation).
  • Competition. Dollar stablecoins on any chain, including Ripple's own RLUSD, which has more on Ethereum ($1.34B) than on the XRP Ledger. Other chains hold 99.95% of the value DefiLlama tracks in on-chain applications.
  • Open question. Whether ledger use turns into lasting demand for XRP while Ripple controls about 38% of the supply.

What it is. XRP is the native currency of the XRP Ledger: "a decentralized blockchain that uses its own digital currency to process and record financial transactions" (xrpl.org). Accounts send XRP and other tokens, trade them on an exchange built into the ledger, and pay a tiny fee in XRP that the ledger destroys.

How it was created. "In 2011, three engineers—David Schwartz, Jed McCaleb, and Arthur Britto—began developing the XRP Ledger" (xrpl.org). "The XRP Ledger first launched in June 2012" with 100 billion XRP. In September 2012, with Chris Larsen, they started NewCoin, "quickly renamed OpenCoin and now named Ripple", and 80 billion XRP went to it. The founding paper is The Ripple Protocol Consensus Algorithm (Ripple Labs Inc, 2014) by David Schwartz, Noah Youngs and Arthur Britto.

The stated purpose. A better version of Bitcoin, "more sustainable and built specifically for payments" (xrpl.org).

Consensus. No mining or staking: new ledger versions close "about every 3 to 5 seconds" as validators agree. Each server trusts a "Unique Node List, or UNL"; the XRP Ledger Foundation and Ripple publish the default lists.

Supply. Fixed: "no more than the original 100 billion can be created" (xrpl.org). Fees are "irrevocably destroyed": 99,985,611,280 XRP remained at the end of September 22, 2026.

03 Network Strengths & Vulnerabilities
A ledger every ~4 seconds Built-in exchange since 2012 Fixed supply, no issuance Named a digital commodity (SEC/CFTC) Listed ETFs in US & Canada
The strengths in figures

The ledger is fast and cheap: a new version every 3.86 seconds on September 22, and a standard fee of 0.00001 XRP. No issuance means no dilution. US regulators now list XRP as a "digital commodity", and eight spot ETFs in the US and Canada give brokerage access.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One company holds a large share. Ripple held 37.66B XRP (37.7%) at June 30, 2026, with up to 1B a month released from escrow.
02
A small trusted validator set. Two organisations publish the default validator lists, one of them Ripple. The network stopped for "approximately 10 minutes" on November 25, 2024 (xrpl.org) and for 64 minutes between ledgers on February 4, 2025.
03
Price volatility. 143.1% annualised over 60 months (68.3% without November 2024).

Litigation history, 2023–2025
  • July 13, 2023, summary judgment (Judge Analisa Torres, ECF 874). "XRP, as a digital token, is not in and of itself a 'contract, transaction[,] or scheme' that embodies the Howey requirements of an investment contract." Institutional Sales "constituted the unregistered offer and sale of investment contracts"; "Ripple's Programmatic Sales of XRP did not constitute the offer and sale of investment contracts"; the Other Distributions did not either. Opinion.
  • October 19, 2023. The SEC dismissed its claims against Bradley Garlinghouse and Christian Larsen by stipulation (per the August 7, 2024 remedies order).
  • August 7, 2024, remedies. The SEC had sought $876,308,712 in disgorgement, $198,150,940 in prejudgment interest and an $876,308,712 penalty; the court imposed a $125,035,150 penalty and an injunction (ECF 973, 974). September 4, 2024: enforcement of the penalty stayed pending appeal, with 111% of it deposited in an interest-bearing account (ECF 977).
  • 2024–2025, appeals and settlement attempt. SEC appeal October 2, 2024; Ripple cross-appeal October 10, 2024 (2d Cir. 24-2648, 24-2705). On May 8, 2025 the parties asked the court for an indicative ruling that it would dissolve the injunction and release the escrowed penalty, with $50 million to the SEC (SEC LR-26306; ECF 983). The court declined on May 15, 2025 (ECF 984) and again on June 26, 2025 (ECF 989): "the parties do not have the authority to agree not to be bound by a court's final judgment". The appeals were dismissed on August 7, 2025 and the mandate issued August 22, 2025 (ECF 993). On August 8, 2025 the SEC granted Ripple a waiver of the Rule 506(d) disqualification (Rel. 33-11383), noting that "the Final Judgment's injunction against Ripple remains in effect". On August 18, 2025 Ripple's counsel told the court that the amount agreed with the SEC, "including the full court-ordered penalty plus statutory interest", had been released (ECF 992). Formulas and data notes

Monthly return rt = Pt/Pt−1 − 1 on 61 month-end closes (Aug 31, 2021 … Aug 31, 2026), giving 60 returns. XRP and BTC use the Yahoo XRP-USD and BTC-USD close on the last UTC day of each month; the S&P 500 uses the Yahoo ^GSPC close on the last trading day (price index, no dividends). Volatility = stdev(r) × √12. Sharpe = mean(e) / stdev(e) × √12, where e = r − rf and rf = the 3-month Treasury par yield at the prior month-end ÷ 12 (U.S. Treasury daily par yield curve; average 3.80% a year over the window). 52-week range: the banner price is 58.4% above the low close and 48.3% below the high close. Transactions = the number of transactions in every ledger that closed on the UTC day (JSON-RPC ledger method, ledgers 107,147,280 – 107,169,637). 08 Summary & Key Risks

What would change the picture: how fast Ripple's XRP reaches the market, whether ETF holdings (about 1% of supply) keep growing, and whether ledger activity starts to need XRP.

Bull Case

The SEC case is closed and US regulators list XRP as a digital commodity; spot ETFs offer brokerage access. Ripple is building regulated infrastructure around the ledger: a New York-chartered stablecoin and a conditionally approved trust bank. If that business settles on the XRP Ledger, demand can rise while supply cannot.

Bear Case
