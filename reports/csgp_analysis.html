01 Company Overview

CoStar Group owns the data and the storefronts of real estate. On the commercial side it sells CoStar, the subscription database of property inventory, leases, sales comps and ownership that brokers, lenders and landlords treat as the industry's standard reference, and runs LoopNet, the commercial listings marketplace. On the residential side it runs Apartments.com, Homes.com and, since its 2025 takeover, Australia's Domain. In August 2026 it added Zonda, which supplies new-home construction data and software for home builders, and in October it agreed to buy Vexcel, which flies aircraft to photograph buildings and land from above. FY2025 revenue was $3.25B, up 18.7%, and trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 is $3.67B, up 20.2%. The gross margin is 78.3%. The operating margin is 5.8%.

The gap between those two margins explains the stock. In FY2021 CoStar turned 22.2% of revenue into operating profit. By FY2025 that was −1.7%. Revenue kept growing; the profit went into building Homes.com, a national home-listing portal set up to take on the incumbents with heavy marketing and a large sales force. Third Point, the activist fund, later put CoStar's residential spend at roughly $5 billion over five years (as reported by RealEstateNews). The market went along with that bet for a long time. It stopped in 2026. The stock closed 2025 at $67.24. In late January Third Point wrote to the board demanding a pullback from Homes.com. On February 24 CoStar guided to first-quarter adjusted EBITDA of $95–115M, far below a quarter of the $740–800M full-year guide it had given on January 7 and affirmed that day. Then the Q2 release on July 28 cut the full-year revenue guide. The Q3 release on October 27 showed both sides of the retreat at once. Profit kept climbing: residential adjusted EBITDA reached a record $49M, up from $12M in Q2, and company-wide adjusted EBITDA doubled to $229M. But net new bookings, the annual value of new subscriptions signed in the quarter, fell to $37M from $84M a year earlier, and management blamed a tough economy and too few salespeople. The stock fell 13.96% the next day. The settled close on October 29, 2026 was $28.14, 58.2% lower than where the year started. The question now is whether CoStar can keep the profit gains while rebuilding the sales force it cut.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Proprietary commercial property database Everyday workflow for CRE professionals Subscription revenue model Two-sided marketplaces (LoopNet, Apartments.com) 78.3% gross margin Low debt

The commercial moat is accumulated data. A rival would have to rebuild building-level research across the country before a broker would switch, and a broker who leaves loses the comps (records of comparable deals) used to price every deal. That moat has held. The Commercial segment grew revenue 6.7% and earned $180M of adjusted EBITDA in a single quarter. What got hurt was the residential business. In for-sale homes, CoStar is the challenger, and a challenger pays for traffic and agents. The 2026 sell-off was a verdict on the price of that attempt.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Homes.com is still a cost centre. CoStar has said it does not expect Homes.com to reach positive adjusted EBITDA until 2030 (per RealEstateNews). Residential as a whole reached $49M of adjusted EBITDA in Q3, about 11% of its $460M of revenue. If spending cuts start to erode traffic, the company has spent the money without getting the market position it was paying for.
02
Bookings fell as the cuts landed. Net new bookings were $37M in Q3 against $84M a year earlier, after $69M in Q2. The full-year revenue guide was cut in July to $3.715–3.755B from $3.78–3.82B; in October it went to $3.75–3.77B only because Zonda was added, and without Zonda it is about $35M lower than in July (Florance, Q3 call). A leaner sales force sells less. The cost savings show up straight away, while the lost bookings reach revenue later.
03
Big-platform competition. The stock closed 4.6% lower on June 11, 2026, the day Google expanded its Local Services Ads for home listings to all 50 states (Online Marketplaces; Yahoo Finance daily closes), and an FTC order announced August 24, 2026 requires Redfin to restart its rental advertising business within six months of the order being finalised (FTC), which would put a second competitor back into Apartments.com's market.
04
The loudest activist has left. Third Point attacked the board in January, D.E. Shaw followed in February, and in April Third Point sold its entire stake and dropped any proxy fight (Reuters; RealEstateNews). With the fund that led the push for the Homes.com retreat gone, holding management to the cuts falls more to the board and its Capital Allocation Committee.
05
Buying again, with less cash. After $668M of buybacks and the Zonda purchase, cash was $500M at September 30. The roughly $730M Vexcel deal, expected to close in Q1 2027, is to be paid from cash on hand and the company's revolving credit line (company release). CoStar already owes more ($994M of long-term debt) than it holds in cash; Vexcel would widen that gap.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value, and a stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $86.05 to the October 29, 2026 settled close of $28.14, CSGP lost 67.3% of its price. Over the same dates the SPY S&P 500 fund rose 67.5% and the QQQ Nasdaq-100 fund 85.3%, also on price alone. CSGP pays no dividend, so nothing offsets that. For most of the window the stock moved sideways: every month-end from October 2021 to September 2025 sat between $60.41 and $96.60. The collapse came after mid-2025. From the July 2025 month-end of $95.19 the stock has fallen 70.4%, and it is down 58.2% since December 31, 2025. The timing is the point. The market paid up while Homes.com spending was at its heaviest, and lost patience only when the costs, the activists and the guidance cuts arrived in the same year.

The 6-period monthly RSI has been below 30 at every point from January 2026 through October 29, bottoming at 10.9 in June 2026, the lowest reading in the series. It stands at 21.6 now. The 10-month moving average of the chart points is about $35.80, 27% above the current price. The trend is still down. The only hint of a floor: the last five chart points (the June to September month-ends plus October 29) have all sat between $27.40 and $32.07. On daily closes the stock is 7.3% below its 50-day average of $30.37 and 23.6% below its 200-day average of $36.84 (Yahoo daily closes, our calculation).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The stock is priced as if residential will keep losing money, and the evidence is moving the other way. Residential swung from −$69M to +$49M of adjusted EBITDA in a year. Company-wide adjusted EBITDA doubled to $229M in Q3, and the full-year EBITDA guide has been raised twice, to $820–840M. At $28.14 the enterprise value is about 14.4x the midpoint of that guide (our calculation). For that price you get a growing commercial data franchise, light debt, and $668M of buybacks in nine months. The CEO bought $2.49M of stock at $29.89 in August. If the cost cuts hold, bookings recover from Q3 as the CFO expects, and residential margins keep widening, the market is pricing a failed bet that is in fact being wound down.

Bear Case

The EBITDA improvement came from spending less, and spending less shows up in bookings. Net new bookings fell to $37M in Q3 from $84M a year earlier, the revenue guide without Zonda has been cut twice, and the Q4 revenue guide implies growth slowing to about 10%. Apartments.com, the biggest residential business, slowed to 6% growth while rivals priced aggressively, by Florance's account. By the company's own account Homes.com is not expected to make money until 2030. Google is moving further into home search and Redfin is being pushed back into rentals. The trailing P/E is 65 on GAAP earnings of $0.43. The activist who forced the retreat has sold out, and the company is spending again on acquisitions (Zonda, then Vexcel) with cash that is down to $500M. A business that spent roughly $5 billion (Third Point's figure) to reach a challenger position may simply be worth less than the market once thought.

Top 3 Risks

01
Cutting spend means cutting growth. A leaner sales force is already showing up in net new bookings, which fell from $84M to $37M year over year in Q3, mostly in residential (CFO, Q3 call), and the Ten-X restructuring in Other Commercial revenue, down 8%. If revenue growth slows faster than costs fall, profit margins stop widening.
02
Platform competition in residential. Google's June 2026 expansion into home-search ads and Redfin's required return to rental advertising both land in the markets where CoStar is spending to win share.
03
Execution on a back-loaded guide. The raised $820–840M adjusted EBITDA guide needs a Q4 of about $285M, well above Q3's $229M (see Section 06), with the Vexcel deal due to close in Q1 2027. A miss would undercut the one number investors are currently giving CoStar credit for.
Industry Commentary
The challenger's bill in residential portals
ONGOING HEADWIND

Home-listing portals are a scale business. Agents advertise where buyers are, and buyers go where the listings are. A challenger has to pay for both sides before either pays it back. CoStar tried to do this with Homes.com, funded by a commercial data business with gross margins near 80%. Its operating margin fell from 22.2% to −1.7% between FY2021 and FY2025 while gross margin barely moved. The cost of the push was in marketing and sales, not in the product. The 2026 reset (a 35% cut to Homes.com spending, a 2030 profitability target and residential turning EBITDA-positive, with a record $49M in Q3) is what it looks like when a challenger stops spending to win and settles for defending what it has. Q3 showed the cost of that switch: with Homes.com's inside sales staff down from 655 to 267, residential bookings fell, and management now plans to expand its field sales team, the salespeople who meet agents in person.

AI as a distribution channel
EMERGING TAILWIND

Portals worry that AI assistants will answer "find me an apartment" without sending anyone to a portal. CoStar's position is that its listings become the data those assistants draw on. Apartments.com's own AI tools logged over half a million sessions averaging 20 minutes in Q2. In early September 2026, StockAnalysis's news feed carried a headline reading "OpenAI Features Apartments.com in GPT-6 Astra Apartment-Hunting Demo." Whether that turns into paid leads or just gives away traffic is unresolved.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

At $28.14 the market pays about 14.4x the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance (our calculation). The bull framing: ~15x is a normal price for a subscription data business, so the market is now valuing the commercial franchise alone and giving residential no credit, or even negative credit, just as residential turns profitable. The bear framing: the forward EBITDA is flattered by cost cuts that are already slowing bookings, and a lower multiple on a lower growth rate is a reasonable price, not a bargain. Q3's bookings, less than half of last year's, leaned toward the bear reading; the CFO's call that Q3 is the low point is the claim the next two quarters will test.
