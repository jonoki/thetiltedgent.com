01 Company Overview
CSX is one of the two Class I (the largest, federally defined tier of) freight railroads east of the Mississippi, running roughly 20,000 route miles across 26 states, the District of Columbia and into Canada from its Jacksonville headquarters. It moves chemicals, grain, autos, metals, forest products, fertilizer, coal and intermodal containers, and owns a trucking subsidiary (Quality Carriers) for chemical tank loads. FY2025 revenue was $14.09B, down ~3% YoY — the third consecutive annual decline from the 2022 peak of $14.9B — but the trailing twelve months to June 2026 have swung back to $14.51B (+2.5%) on a record Q2 2026 quarter of $3.94B (+10%). By market cap ($91.5B) it ranks roughly #266 globally.
The central debate is simple to state and hard to price: is CSX an operating-ratio recovery story, or a merger story? The stock has re-rated ~53% in twelve months as a new CEO (Steve Angel, ex-Linde — not Joe Hinrichs, who was removed in September 2025) pulled the operating ratio from 64.1% back to 61.7% and raised full-year guidance. At the same time, Union Pacific's $85B acquisition of Norfolk Southern sits in front of the Surface Transportation Board (STB), and Berkshire Hathaway has publicly said BNSF will not bid for CSX. The bull case needs the operating turnaround to keep compounding on its own; the bear case is that the market already paid a merger premium for a railroad nobody is currently buying.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Eastern Rail Duopoly ~20,000 Route Miles — Unreplicable Port Access: Baltimore / Hampton Roads / Savannah Double-Stack Howard Street Tunnel Fuel Surcharge Pass-Through Rail's 3–4x Fuel Efficiency vs Truck Regulated Common-Carrier Pricing Power
A railroad's moat is its right-of-way. Nobody is going to lay 20,000 miles of new track through the eastern United States, so for any shipper east of Chicago and the Mississippi the choice is CSX, Norfolk Southern, or a truck. That duopoly shows up as a 47% gross margin and a 35.7% operating margin on the trailing twelve months, and in the ability to push revenue per unit up 4% in a quarter when volumes were also up 6%. The fuel surcharge mechanism let CSX absorb a $177M YoY jump in fuel expense (locomotive diesel prices +74%) in Q2 2026 and still expand margin 240 basis points. The Howard Street Tunnel clearance project removes the last double-stack bottleneck between Baltimore and the Midwest, which is a moat-deepening asset that was 100 years in the making.

Key Vulnerabilities

Becoming the odd one out: If the STB approves UP–NS, the resulting transcontinental railroad can quote single-line service from Los Angeles to Atlanta while CSX must interchange. CSX filed a motion for summary denial (Aug 6, 2026) alongside BNSF and shipper groups, and CEO Angel has called the STB's evaluation criteria "pretty onerous" — but Berkshire Hathaway has said BNSF will not pursue a CSX merger, which removes the most obvious defensive partner.

Service still below 2023 levels: Train velocity improved to 18.0 mph (+3%) in Q2 2026, but car dwell worsened to 11.0 hours (+6%) and trip-plan compliance slipped to 71% carload / 88% intermodal. Volume growth of 6% is straining a network that only just finished two rebuilds; a second congestion episode like 2022 would hand share back to trucks.

Coal and commodity exposure: Coal is 13% of revenue and export met coal is now the larger half of it. Seaborne met-coal pricing, Indian steel demand and Appalachian mine economics are outside CSX's control; a 2023-style price collapse can take $300M+ off annual coal revenue quickly.

Regulatory and labor overhang: The STB's growing appetite for reciprocal-switching rules and rate-reasonableness cases, plus a unionized workforce (national bargaining round due 2025–26), constrain both pricing and the "productivity" lever that the operating-ratio story depends on.

Notable One-Off Items

Rebuild-depressed comparisons (FY2025): The Blue Ridge Subdivision rebuild after Hurricane Helene (~60 miles of track, ~$450M) and the Howard Street Tunnel closure ran through most of 2025. Then-CFO Sean Pelkey quantified the drag at roughly $1M/day of missed revenue in Q1 2025 (~$100M/quarter) plus ~$10M/month of detour costs. FY2025 capex of $2.9B versus ~$1.8B in 2021 inflated by these projects; FY2025 FCF of $1.7B is therefore a trough, not a run-rate. The flip side: Q2 2026's +240bps margin expansion and 80%+ FCF growth guidance are partly the absence of those costs, not pure productivity.

Fuel: Locomotive fuel expense rose $177M YoY in Q2 2026 (price +74%, gallons −4%). Fuel surcharges recover most of this with a lag, which inflates revenue per unit growth (intermodal RPU +16%) without adding much profit. Strip fuel out and underlying pricing is closer to the +4% seen in merchandise.

Buyback slowdown: H1 2026 repurchases were $506M (12M shares) versus $1,152M (38M shares) in H1 2025 — capital was diverted to the rebuilds and to deleveraging. Share-count tailwind to EPS is fading from ~2% to ~1%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Angel is a proven operator with a merger-integration résumé, and the operating ratio has already moved from 64.1% to 61.7% in three quarters with the rebuild costs still rolling off. Consensus EPS of $2.01 (2026) and $2.27 (2027) implies the stock is at 22x forward — for a duopoly asset with 47% gross margins, 80%+ FCF growth this year and a double-stack Baltimore gateway that just opened. If the STB blocks UP–NS, CSX keeps its eastern duopoly intact; if it approves it, CSX becomes the only independent eastern railroad and the logical partner for BNSF or CN whenever Berkshire changes its mind.

Bear Case

The stock rose 53% in a year on a revenue base growing 2.5%. A 28x trailing multiple and a 1.88 PEG price in a sub-60% operating ratio that CSX last achieved before 2022 — while service metrics (dwell 11.0 hrs, 71% carload trip-plan compliance) are still worse than a year ago. Half the margin gain is fuel-surcharge lag and rebuild costs lapping. Berkshire has said no; a UP–NS approval leaves CSX quoting interline service against a transcontinental rival. Coal, 13% of revenue, is hostage to seaborne met-coal prices. And a $0.56 dividend does not cushion a 1.21-beta stock if the operating story wobbles.

  Industry Commentary
The Transcontinental Question: UP–NS at the STB
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The facts that are verifiable: Union Pacific agreed on Jul 28–29, 2025 to acquire Norfolk Southern for ~$85B ($320/share: one UP share plus $88.82 cash). Shareholders of both approved (>99%) in November 2025. The STB rejected the first application as incomplete (Jan 16, 2026), accepted an amended application but held it in abeyance pending supplemental information (May 28, 2026), and on Aug 19, 2026 set a procedural schedule: parties-of-record deadline Sep 4, 2026; final briefs May 28, 2027; decision within 90 days of the close of record — i.e. late 2027, versus the companies' original early-2027 target. CSX, BNSF and shipper groups filed motions for summary denial on Aug 6, 2026, and a "Stop the Rail Merger Coalition" including BNSF formed in April 2026. The 2001 STB major-merger rules require an applicant to show the deal enhances competition, a standard no Class I combination has yet been tested against. What is not verifiable: any bid for CSX. Berkshire Hathaway has said BNSF will not pursue one, and Angel's public line is "you've got to wait for the right timing," with a pointed reference to UP's 1997–98 post-Southern Pacific meltdown.
Operating Ratio Recovery vs Service Reality
ONGOING HEADWIND
Operating ratio (operating expense ÷ revenue — lower is better, and the single number rail investors trade on) went from sub-60% before September 2022 to 62.7% in FY2024, 66.3% in FY2025, and 64.1% in Q2 2025. It has since recovered to 61.7% in Q2 2026 and 62.8% for H1 2026, and guidance implies roughly 62–63% for the full year. The Class I peer set spent 2024–25 in the high-50s to low-60s, so CSX is closing the gap rather than leading. The tension: volume grew 6% in Q2 2026 and the network absorbed it with velocity up 3%, but dwell rose 6% to 11.0 hours and trip-plan compliance fell 4 points (carload) and 2 points (intermodal). Safety improved sharply (FRA injury rate 0.83 vs 1.03; train-accident rate 2.72 vs 3.88). A railroad that is getting faster but less reliable is one that is running hot; the next leg of margin has to come from precision, not just volume leverage.
Baltimore, Met Coal and the Intermodal Reset
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two physical assets came back online within a week of each other in fall 2025: the Howard Street Tunnel (double-stack clearance under downtown Baltimore, the last such chokepoint on the East Coast) and the ~60-mile Blue Ridge Subdivision (~$450M rebuild after Helene, the CSX artery from the Carolinas into Tennessee). The first full quarters without detours show intermodal revenue +26% and export coal tonnage +12%. Export met coal now exceeds domestic tonnage, tying CSX more tightly to Indian and Brazilian steel demand and to seaborne pricing — a tailwind while Asian steel output holds, a trapdoor if it does not.

Bull implication: CSX has a two-year window in which UP and NS are distracted by an integration (or a failed one) and CSX is running the cleanest eastern network with a new Baltimore gateway; that is when share is taken. Bear implication: the same two years feature a $85B rival deal, a union bargaining round, fuel volatility and a stock already priced for the operating ratio to keep falling. The market has paid for the turnaround in advance; from here CSX has to deliver it quarter by quarter, with no acquirer waiting to underwrite a miss.
