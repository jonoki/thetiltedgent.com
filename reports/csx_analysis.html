01 Company Overview
CSX is one of the two Class I (the largest, federally defined tier of) freight railroads east of the Mississippi, running roughly 20,000 route miles across 26 states, the District of Columbia and into Canada from its Jacksonville headquarters. It moves chemicals, grain, autos, metals, forest products, fertilizer, coal and intermodal containers, and owns a trucking subsidiary (Quality Carriers) for chemical tank loads. FY2025 revenue was $14.09B, down ~3% YoY — the third consecutive annual decline from the 2022 peak of $14.9B — but the trailing twelve months to September 2026 have swung back to $14.94B on a record Q3 2026 quarter of $4.02B (+12%). By market cap ($93.6B) it ranks #127 in the S&P 500.
The central debate is simple to state and hard to price: is CSX an operating-ratio recovery story, or a merger story? The stock is up ~43% in twelve months as a new CEO (Steve Angel, ex-Linde, who replaced Joe Hinrichs in September 2025) cut the operating ratio — operating expenses as a share of revenue, lower is better — to 60.9% in Q3 2026, from 65.1% a year earlier excluding a goodwill write-down, and raised full-year revenue guidance again in October. At the same time, Union Pacific's ~$85B acquisition of Norfolk Southern is moving through the Surface Transportation Board (STB), the federal rail regulator, which on Sep 18, 2026 turned down CSX's request to reject the application without a full review; Berkshire Hathaway has publicly said BNSF will not bid for CSX. The bull case needs the operating turnaround to keep compounding on its own; the bear case is that the market has already paid up for a turnaround that the service numbers have not yet confirmed.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Eastern Rail Duopoly ~20,000 Route Miles — Unreplicable Port Access: Baltimore / Hampton Roads / Savannah Double-Stack Howard Street Tunnel Fuel Surcharge Pass-Through Rail's 3–4x Fuel Efficiency vs Truck Regulated Common-Carrier Pricing Power
A railroad's moat is its right-of-way. Nobody is going to lay 20,000 miles of new track through the eastern United States, so for any shipper east of Chicago and the Mississippi the choice is CSX, Norfolk Southern, or a truck. That duopoly shows up as a 48% gross margin (stockanalysis.com, trailing twelve months) and a 36.4% operating margin over the twelve months to September 2026 (our calculation from CSX's filings), and in the ability to push revenue per unit up 7% in a quarter when volume was also up 5%. The fuel surcharge mechanism let CSX absorb a $170M YoY jump in fuel expense (its locomotive fuel price was up 67% a gallon) in Q3 2026 and still lift its operating margin to 39.1%, 420 basis points above Q3 2025 excluding that quarter's goodwill charge (880 basis points on the reported figure). The Howard Street Tunnel expansion gave CSX double-stack clearance through Baltimore in September 2025.

Key Vulnerabilities

Becoming the odd one out: If the STB approves UP–NS, the resulting transcontinental railroad can quote single-line service from Los Angeles to Atlanta while CSX must hand freight to another railroad at an interchange. CSX, BNSF and five shipper groups asked the Board to reject the application without a full review; on Sep 18, 2026 the Board denied those motions, saying more evidence would help it decide and that the ruling was not a judgment on the merits. Opening comments are due Nov 18, 2026. Berkshire Hathaway has said BNSF will not pursue a CSX merger, which removes the most obvious defensive partner.

Service slipping as volume grows: In Q3 2026 train velocity fell 2% to 18.6 mph, dwell (hours a car sits in a terminal) rose to 10.6 from 9.5, and trip-plan compliance fell to 73% carload / 83% intermodal from 83% / 93% a year earlier. Volume growth of 5% is straining a network that only finished two rebuilds in September 2025; a second congestion episode like 2022 would hand share back to trucks.

Coal and commodity exposure: Coal is 13% of revenue, and export coal was just over half of its tonnage in the first nine months of 2026 (32.0M of 62.0M tons). Seaborne met-coal pricing, Indian steel demand and Appalachian mine economics are outside CSX's control, and a 2023-style price fall can cut coal revenue quickly.

Regulatory and labor overhang: The STB has tried to give shippers more leverage over railroads: its 2024 reciprocal-switching rule (letting a poorly served shipper reach a second railroad) was struck down by a federal appeals court in July 2025, but rate-reasonableness cases remain. That, plus a unionized workforce, constrains both pricing and the "productivity" lever that the operating-ratio story depends on.

Notable One-Off Items

Rebuild-depressed comparisons (FY2025): The Blue Ridge Subdivision rebuild after Hurricane Helene (~60 miles of track) and the $450M Howard Street Tunnel expansion kept two key routes out of service for much of 2025, until both reopened in late September 2025. FY2025 capex of $2.90B was inflated by these projects, and FY2025 free cash flow of $1.79B is therefore a trough, not a run-rate. The flip side: 2026's margin expansion and 80%+ FCF growth guidance are partly the absence of those costs, not pure productivity.

Goodwill charge in the base: Q3 2025 included a $164M goodwill impairment (a write-down of the value of a past acquisition), so the reported comparisons (operating income +44%, EPS +54%) flatter the quarter; against Q3 2025's adjusted figures ($1.25B operating income, $0.44 EPS) the gains were +25% and +30%.

Fuel: Fuel expense rose $170M YoY in Q3 2026 to $451M as CSX's locomotive fuel price rose 67% to $4.24 a gallon. Fuel surcharges recover most of this with a lag, which inflates revenue-per-unit growth (intermodal RPU +13%) without adding much profit.

Buybacks: Repurchases were $1.06B in the first nine months of 2026 (cash-flow statement) against $1.28B a year earlier; Q3 diluted shares were 0.7% lower than a year earlier, so the share-count boost to EPS is small.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Angel is a proven operator with a merger-integration résumé, and the operating margin reached 39.1% in Q3 2026 (a 60.9% operating ratio) with the rebuild costs behind it. Consensus EPS of $2.00 (2026) and $2.29 (2027) puts the stock at about 22x 2027 earnings — for a duopoly asset with 48% gross margins, free cash flow guided up more than 80% this year and a double-stack Baltimore gateway that opened in September 2025. If the STB blocks UP–NS, CSX keeps its eastern duopoly intact; if it approves it, CSX becomes the only independent eastern railroad and the logical partner for BNSF or CN whenever Berkshire changes its mind.

Bear Case

The stock rose 43% in a year while revenue grew 8% in the first nine months of 2026, part of it fuel surcharge. A 26x trailing multiple and a 1.76 PEG price in more margin gains while service metrics (dwell 10.6 hours vs 9.5, carload trip-plan compliance 73% vs 83%) are worse than a year ago. Part of the margin gain is rebuild costs and last year's $164M goodwill charge dropping out of the comparison. Berkshire has said no; a UP–NS approval leaves CSX handing freight to other railroads against a transcontinental rival. Coal, 13% of revenue, is hostage to seaborne met-coal prices. And a $0.56 dividend does not cushion a 1.20-beta stock if the operating story wobbles.

  Industry Commentary
The Transcontinental Question: UP–NS at the STB
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The facts that are verifiable: Union Pacific agreed on Jul 28, 2025 to acquire Norfolk Southern for one UP share plus $88.82 in cash per NS share (an enterprise value of about $85B at announcement). Shareholders of both companies approved on Nov 14, 2025. Either company may owe the other a $2.5B termination fee. The 2001 STB major-merger rules require an applicant to show the deal enhances competition, a standard no Class I combination has yet been tested against. What is not verifiable: any bid for CSX. Berkshire Hathaway has said BNSF will not pursue one.
Operating Ratio Recovery vs Service Reality
ONGOING HEADWIND
Operating ratio (operating expense ÷ revenue — lower is better, and the single number rail investors trade on) was 67.9% for FY2025 (66.8% excluding the $164M goodwill charge) and 69.7% in Q3 2025 (65.1% excluding it). It has since recovered to 60.9% in Q3 2026 and 62.1% for the first nine months. Guidance calls for more than 350 basis points of operating-margin expansion this year; the first nine months ran at a 37.9% margin, against 32.2% a year earlier. The tension: volume grew 5% in Q3 2026, but velocity fell 2% to 18.6 mph, dwell rose to 10.6 hours from 9.5 and trip-plan compliance fell 10 points for both carload and intermodal. Safety improved (FRA injury frequency 0.97 vs 1.17; train-accident rate 2.77 vs 2.91). On the call Angel said: "As we move into 2027, we will be focused on ensuring we have sufficient resources across the network to support a growing business." A railroad that is moving more freight but less reliably is one that is running hot; the next leg of margin has to come from precision, not just volume.
Baltimore, Met Coal and the Intermodal Reset
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two physical assets came back online within days of each other in late September 2025: the Howard Street Tunnel (a $450M expansion giving double-stack clearance under downtown Baltimore) and the ~60-mile Blue Ridge Subdivision (rebuilt over nearly a year after Hurricane Helene, the CSX artery from the Carolinas into Tennessee). In Q3 2026 intermodal revenue rose 20% and export coal tonnage 7%. Export coal ran ahead of domestic tonnage over the first nine months of 2026, tying CSX more tightly to Indian and Brazilian steel demand and to seaborne pricing — a tailwind while Asian steel output holds, a trapdoor if it does not.

Bull implication: CSX has a two-year window in which UP and NS are distracted by an integration (or a failed one) and CSX is running the cleanest eastern network with a new Baltimore gateway; that is when share is taken. Bear implication: the same two years feature an $85B rival deal, labor contracts, fuel volatility and a stock already priced for the operating ratio to keep falling. The market has paid for the turnaround in advance; from here CSX has to deliver it quarter by quarter, with no acquirer waiting to underwrite a miss.
