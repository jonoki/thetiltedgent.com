01 Company Overview

Cognizant is one of the large global IT services firms, selling consulting, systems integration, application development, engineering and outsourced operations to large companies — 75.2% of Q3 2026 revenue came from North America and 18.7% from Europe. FY2025 revenue was $21.11B, up 6.95% from $19.74B in FY2024, the fastest growth since 2021, with GAAP EPS of $4.56; over the twelve months to September 2026 revenue was $21.89B. The company ranks #326 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation at a $29.1B market value — about what it books in a single year: $28.7B of contract value signed over the last twelve months.

That comparison is the whole report. Cognizant is an effort-priced business: 359,900 people whose billable hours are the product. Generative AI attacks precisely that pricing model — if a model writes the code, the client needs fewer billed hours for the same outcome. In 2026 the market took that argument seriously and took the stock from an $83.00 close on December 31, 2025 to a $38.73 close on June 30, 2026 — down 53% in six months — with removal from the Nasdaq-100 index on June 22, 2026 adding forced selling by the funds that track it. The reported numbers held up better. Q3 2026, reported on October 29, grew revenue 4.8% in constant currency (stripping out exchange-rate moves) with a 16.3% adjusted operating margin, the seventh straight quarter of year-over-year margin expansion. But bookings fell 7% after a 6% fall in Q2, and management trimmed its full-year growth range to 4.0–4.4% in constant currency, citing weaker discretionary spending by health care and consumer-facing clients. The stock closed October 30 at $65.11, up 68% from the June 30 close. The central debate: is AI a deflation shock that permanently shrinks the revenue earned per client outcome, or a mix shock that Cognizant can charge for through what CFO Jatin Dalal calls "skills plus inference" pricing — people plus AI model usage? Bookings and the share of work sold on outcomes, not the revenue line, show the answer first.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Multi-year contracted backlog TriZetto payer platform lock-in Global delivery scale (359,900) Regulated-industry domain depth Switching cost on day-to-day operations Security & compliance clearances Balance-sheet capacity (D/E 0.11)

The real moat is not technology — it is embeddedness. A large share of Cognizant's revenue is "run" work: keeping claims systems, trading platforms and manufacturing ERP environments alive. Replacing that vendor means moving hundreds of people to a new employer, re-documenting undocumented systems and accepting operational risk in a regulated environment, which is why these contracts renew even when the client is unhappy with pricing. The $28.7B TTM bookings number against $21.9B of TTM revenue quantifies the same point: the company signs more contract value each year than it bills. The vulnerability is that embeddedness protects the relationship, not the price — and price is what AI attacks.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The pricing model is the product. Revenue is a function of billable hours × rate. If clients demand that AI's productivity gain be passed through, revenue falls even as delivery improves. Management's answer is to charge for outcomes and AI usage: at the June 5, 2026 AI Forum the CFO described selling "skills plus inference," and on the Q3 call the CEO cited "outcome-based pricing, AI-infused rate cards, and platform monetization." Fixed-price and transaction-based work reached nearly 58% of revenue in Q3, up 1.4 points from a year earlier, per the CEO. A pricing model announced is not a pricing model proven.
02
Growth from within is thin. Of Q3's 4.6% revenue growth, acquisitions added about 2.1 points and resale of other companies' products about 1.9 points, per the release. Financial Services (+12.0%) is the one large segment growing on its own; Health Sciences and Products & Resources, 52% of revenue, shrank. If the Financial Services cycle matures, group growth leans almost entirely on deals.
03
Bookings have fallen two quarters running. Q3 bookings fell 7% after a 6% fall in Q2, and trailing-twelve-month growth slowed to 4% from 5%. Revenue follows bookings with a lag of several quarters, so the trailing growth rate is the number to watch. Management says the pipeline has more new business in it and expects smaller deals to return when clients' discretionary budgets loosen; that is a forecast, not yet a result.
04
Index removal removed a buyer. Nasdaq dropped CTSH from the Nasdaq-100 in its June 2026 quarterly rebalance, effective before the open on June 22, 2026. Funds that track the index had to sell their shares whatever the price. That one-off selling is over, but those funds will not buy again unless the stock re-enters the index.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, Project Leap, the restructuring programme launched in Q2 2026: $51M of charges in Q3 and $135M in the first nine months, mostly severance. The company now expects $180–250M in total, running into Q1 2027, and about $200M of savings in 2026, which it is spending on AI and other investments. Adjusted figures exclude it, and it is most of the gap between Q3's 15.4% GAAP and 16.3% adjusted operating margin. Second, one-off tax and benefit items: Q3 2025 GAAP EPS of $0.56 carried a one-time, non-cash $0.80 tax charge, which flatters this year's GAAP comparison, and Q2 2026 included an $81M benefit from releasing an Indian provident-fund liability that is no longer required. Third, buybacks: Cognizant repurchased 4.9M shares for $258M in Q3, after 22.5M for $1.153B in Q2, with $2.08B of authorisation left; diluted shares fell to 450M in Q3 from 487M a year earlier, so per-share growth runs ahead of profit growth.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 30, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the October 29, 2021 close of $78.09 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $65.11, CTSH lost 16.6% of its price. Adding back the $5.91 per share of dividends paid over the period (20 payments, not reinvested) brings the return to about −9% — a lost half-decade, while SPY, the S&P 500 fund, rose 64.6% on price over the same dates. The shape matters more than the endpoints: this was not a slow bleed. It was a 2022 sell-off from the March 2022 peak, three years of range-bound drift between about $60 and $83, and then a violent 2026 in which the stock lost more than half its value in six months and has since regained about 60% of the loss.

The 6-period monthly RSI tells the story cleanly. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 11.9x trailing earnings and about 10.4x FY2027 consensus earnings for a business with $28.7B of trailing bookings against $21.9B of revenue — a 1.3x book-to-bill — constant-currency growth guided to 4.0–4.4% for 2026, adjusted operating margin up year over year for seven straight quarters to 16.3%, an ~8.1% free-cash-flow yield, borrowings matched by cash, and buybacks that cut the diluted share count by nearly 8% in a year. The 2026 collapse came from index-removal selling and a thesis about the future, not from a run of missed quarters. If outcome-based and "skills plus inference" pricing works — the $1B context-engineering pipeline target for year-end is a test — AI becomes a shift toward higher-value work rather than a revenue cut, and a business growing mid-single digits with 16% margins does not belong at about 10x forward earnings.

Bear Case

The bear case does not dispute the reported numbers; it disputes what they will be worth. Revenue is billable hours × rate, and generative AI attacks the number of hours directly. Bookings have now fallen two quarters in a row (−6% in Q2, −7% in Q3), management cut the top of its 2026 growth range, and almost all of Q3's growth came from acquisitions (about 2.1 points) and resale of other companies' products (about 1.9 points). Health Sciences and Products & Resources, more than half of revenue, are shrinking. A 6.5% short position says a meaningful part of the market thinks the P/E is not cheap but right for a business whose revenue pool is about to shrink. Cheap on trailing earnings is exactly what a structurally impaired business looks like on the way down, and the Nasdaq-100 index funds that sold in June are not coming back.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI-driven price deflation on the staffing delivery model. This is the thesis that took the stock from $83.00 to $38.73 in six months, and it remains unresolved: management has an answer — outcome-based pricing, AI-infused rate cards and platform fees — but has not yet shown it in revenue at scale.
02

Revenue follows bookings with a lag, so a third down quarter would weigh on 2027, where the StockAnalysis consensus already expects only 4.4% revenue growth. The Q4 print and the first 2027 outlook are the next read.
03
Growth that leans on deals and one segment. Financial Services (+12.0%) and acquisitions (about 2.1 points of group growth) carry a company whose Health Sciences and Products & Resources segments shrank in Q3, and management says discretionary spending by health care and consumer-facing clients weakened going into Q4.
Industry Commentary
Generative AI vs. the effort-priced services model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The IT services industry has priced its work as billable hours × rate for thirty years. In 2026 the market priced in the view that AI shrinks the hours: CTSH fell 53% from its December close to its June close without a large earnings miss. Cognizant's own numbers show both sides. On the Q3 call the CEO said more than 50% of its software development was AI-assisted and revenue per associate (excluding trainees) rose more than 3%; the CFO said service lines that benefit from AI make up about 40% of the business and are growing at roughly 2 to 3 times the rate of total organic services revenue. Fixed-price and transaction-based work is nearly 58% of revenue. Rising revenue per person is what productive AI adoption looks like — and also what passing the productivity gain back to clients looks like. The two are indistinguishable in one quarter and fully distinguishable over four.

Sector-wide demand softness and index mechanics
ONGOING HEADWIND

On top of the AI argument came a purely technical event: Nasdaq removed CTSH from the Nasdaq-100 in its June 2026 quarterly rebalance, effective before the open on June 22, 2026, so funds that track the index had to sell whatever the price. The selling was one-off; the lost buyer is lasting unless the company re-enters the index. The pressure going into Q4 is demand. Management says macro uncertainty is weighing on discretionary spending, particularly in health care and consumer-facing sectors; in Health Sciences, the CFO said, regulatory and funding changes continue to hold spending back, and in Products & Resources retail, consumer goods and travel clients face supply-chain disruption and high oil prices. Financial Services and technology clients are the exceptions.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

CTSH trades at 11.9x trailing GAAP earnings and about 10.4x FY2027 consensus earnings, with an ~8.1% free-cash-flow yield, a 2.03% dividend and borrowings matched by cash. The bull framing: index removal and a sector-wide scare pulled the price away from a business that is still growing, widening its margins, signing $28.7B of contract value a year and buying back nearly 8% of its shares in twelve months. The bear framing: the discount is the market pricing what the business will be worth in ten years, not this year's earnings. A low multiple on a business whose unit of sale is a billable hour, in the years the cost of producing that hour is collapsing, is not an anomaly to be exploited — it is a forecast. The way to judge it is not the P/E; it is several quarters of trailing bookings growth and of the share of revenue sold on outcomes rather than hours. The first has weakened for two quarters; the second is rising slowly.
