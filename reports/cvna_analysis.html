01 Company Overview

Carvana buys used cars, reconditions them, photographs them in a 360-degree studio, and sells them online with delivery or vending-machine pickup — and it finances a large share of those sales itself. In FY2025 it did $20.32B of revenue, up 48.6%, and on a trailing-twelve-month basis it is now at $25.06B, up 54.0%, with $1.57B of net income attributable to shareholders. It is the second-largest used-vehicle retailer in the United States by revenue behind CarMax's $28.20B — but its equity is worth $83.11B versus CarMax's $8.98B and Lithia Motors' $8.33B. The market is not paying for a car dealer. It is paying for a compounding logistics-and-credit machine, and that gap is the whole argument.

The central debate is GPU durability. Carvana sold 197,325 retail units in Q2 2026, up 38%, and revenue grew 52% — but total gross profit per unit fell roughly $455 year over year, gross margin compressed from 22.0% to 18.8%, and adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 12.4% to 10.4%. Volume is buying growth; unit economics are giving some of it back. Underneath that sits an earnings-quality question the company does not make easy to answer — how much of gross profit is the gain on selling finance receivables rather than the gain on selling cars — and a control structure in which roughly 40% of consolidated profit does not belong to public shareholders at all. The bull case is that a 5.5%-share operator in a fragmented, 40-million-unit-a-year market has a decade of runway. The bear case is that the multiple is priced off a GPU that is already rolling over.

Recon Capacity

Carvana bought ADESA's U.S. physical auction business in 2022 — the deal that, combined with the rate shock, nearly killed it. Strategically it was the right asset for the wrong balance sheet: ADESA's sites supply wholesale volume and provide land and buildings that can be converted into reconditioning capacity. Reconditioning throughput is the hard ceiling on unit growth: you cannot retail a car you have not reconditioned, and adding capacity is a construction project, not a software release. The Q2 2026 disclosures fetched for this report reference ADESA integration benefits but do not quantify site conversions, megasite counts or reconditioning capacity, so no figure is asserted here. What is visible is inventory: $3,263M at June 2026 versus $2,408M at FY2025 year-end (+35.5%), roughly in line with unit growth — the company is stocking ahead of demand, not liquidating into it.

Watch item: the single most useful disclosure Carvana could add is reconditioning units of capacity by site. Without it, "we have runway" is an assertion investors cannot audit.

When Carvana sells a car it usually also writes the loan, then sells that loan — into securitization trusts or to forward-flow buyers — and books the profit immediately as gain on sale. This is real cash profit, but it is front-loaded and it is rate-sensitive: the gain is the spread between the yield on the loan and the cost of the bonds sold against it. Widen ABS spreads or lift benchmark rates without lifting the customer's APR, and the gain compresses instantly, with no change whatsoever in how many cars were sold. The critical disclosure — how many dollars of gross profit came from finance receivables, and what share of total gross profit that is — could not be verified from the sources fetched for this report, so it is not stated here. What is verified is the direction and the magnitude of the move: other GPU fell $192 per unit year over year, which is 42% of the entire $455 GPU decline, and management explicitly cited "lower interest rates to customers" against "higher benchmark rates." An investor who cannot size this line cannot fully underwrite the earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
NATIONAL INVENTORY POOL RECONDITIONING SCALE ADESA SITE FOOTPRINT IN-HOUSE ORIGINATION SECURITIZATION SHELF LOGISTICS NETWORK BRAND / VENDING MACHINE

The moat is a national pool of inventory attached to a national logistics network and a captive lender. A traditional dealer sells you what is on its lot, financed by whoever will take the paper. Carvana shows every car it owns to every customer in the country, moves it on its own trucks, and writes and sells the loan itself. That produces three compounding effects: selection improves conversion, scale lowers cost per unit delivered, and capturing the loan means a second profit pool on the same transaction. The operating leverage is visible — total operating expense per retail unit fell from roughly $3,867 to $3,568 (derived: gross profit less GAAP operating income, divided by retail units; the company separately disclosed SG&A per unit down $157, including a $272 overhead reduction offset by higher operational and advertising spend). Overhead per car falling while volume grows 38% is the clean part of this story.

What it is not is a moat around price. Used cars are a commodity, the customer shops on payment, and every dollar of GPU is contestable. The evidence is in the numbers already: gross margin fell 320 basis points in a quarter when volume grew 38%. That is a business buying share.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Gain-on-sale earnings quality. A material but unquantified (in sources fetched) share of gross profit is the immediate gain from selling finance receivables. This profit is recognised up front and depends on the spread between customer APR and securitization funding cost. Other GPU already fell $192 per unit year over year — 42% of the whole GPU decline — while units grew 38%. If credit spreads widen or delinquencies rise, this line moves first and moves hard, and the operating story stays intact while the earnings do not.
▸
Dual-class control and the Up-C leak. Public Class A holders do not control the company, and they do not own all of its profit. Q2 2026 consolidated net income was $513M; $310M — 60.4% — was attributable to Carvana Co. The balance sheet carries $1,110M of minority interest against $5,138M of total equity. Headline "net income" and "EPS" describe different pies, and the P/E on the fetched EPS of $1.81 is the honest one. The specific DriveTime and Garcia-family related-party agreements, and the exact voting-power split, were not verified from sources fetched.
▸
Balance-sheet memory. Total debt peaked at $8,820M at FY2022 with equity at −$1,053M. It is now $5,697M against $5,138M of equity and roughly 1.0x net debt to trailing adjusted EBITDA. That is a genuine repair. But it was achieved partly by a distressed exchange that PIK'd interest rather than paying it, and the business still consumes working capital in inventory ($3,263M and rising) and in receivables it must fund before it can sell them. A frozen securitization market is the specific scenario that hurts.
▸
Beta 3.50. This is not a footnote, it is a description of the instrument. A stock that fell 98% and then rose more than seventy-fold does not have a stable discount rate. At 41x trailing earnings with 9.23% of the float short, both the fundamental and the positioning inputs are unstable.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A structurally advantaged operator with low-single-digit share of a fragmented 36–40 million-unit market, growing units 38% while overhead per unit falls, is early in its run. The balance sheet is repaired — debt down from $8.82B to $5.70B, equity from −$1,053M to $5,138M, net leverage about 1.0x — and the August 2026 term loan retired the last distressed coupon, saving roughly $45M a year. FY2026 adjusted EBITDA is guided to $2.7–3.0B against $2.24B in 2025, ROIC is 29.7%, and free cash flow is $929M. Nothing in the used-car market forces the share gains to stop.

Bear Case

You are paying 41x trailing earnings — inflated by an unexplained $857M Q4 2025 quarter — for a business whose gross margin fell 320bp and whose adjusted EBITDA margin fell 200bp in the same quarter that units grew 38%. Total GPU declined $455, and 42% of that came from the finance line, the profit that is booked up front and moves first when credit spreads widen. Only 60.4% of consolidated income belongs to public shareholders. Beta is 3.50, 9.23% of the float is short, and the stock is up 2.93% over a year in which everything went right operationally. The good news is in the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Finance-margin reversal. Other GPU fell $192 per unit while units grew 38%, and the absolute dollars of finance gross profit are not disclosed in the sources fetched. A widening in auto-ABS spreads or a deterioration in subprime credit hits this line immediately and invisibly to the unit narrative.
02
Multiple compression on stable operations. At 41x trailing and 36.5x forward, with a consensus target only 11% above spot and a beta of 3.50, the stock can lose a third of its value on a rate move or a single quarter of flat GPU without any change in the business.
03
Capacity and working capital. Unit growth is gated by reconditioning throughput, which is not disclosed, and funded by inventory that grew 35.5% in six months to $3,263M. Growth here consumes cash before it produces it; a funding-market disruption is the specific 2022 failure mode, and it has been made less likely, not impossible.
Industry Commentary
Rates, spreads and the price of a booked profit
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Carvana's finance profit is a spread business dressed as a retail line item. Management said explicitly in Q2 2026 that other GPU fell $192 because it passed lower interest rates to customers against higher benchmark rates — the two halves of the spread moved the wrong way at once. This is the mechanism that makes the stock a rates instrument rather than a consumer-discretionary one, and it explains a beta of 3.50 better than the equity story does. It also cuts the other way: a rally in auto-ABS and a fall in benchmark rates would restore that $192 without Carvana selling one extra car.

A shrinking industry that Carvana is growing inside
ONGOING HEADWIND

The comparison set is stalling. CarMax revenue is down 1.7% with net income down 60.2% to $222.5M; Lithia is up 2.1% with net income down 19.3%. Against that, CVNA is up 54.0%. Share is not being handed over — it is being taken from operators with fixed lot costs, local inventory and a financing arm they cannot scale. The headwind is that a flat industry means every unit of Carvana growth is somebody else's loss, and incumbents defending margin are why GPU is contestable. AutoNation and Sonic's Echo Park figures were not fetched and are not stated.

Balance-sheet normalisation is done — what it means for the debate
EMERGING TAILWIND

The August 2026 term loan at SOFR + 225bp replaced notes that carried a 9.00%/11.00%/13.00% cash-or-PIK coupon. That is the moment Carvana stopped being a credit story. Quarterly interest expense has already fallen from $143M to $101M year over year, with roughly $45M a year more to come. Bull implication: every dollar of that saving drops to pre-tax income, and a company at 1.0x net leverage with $929M of free cash flow can fund reconditioning capacity internally instead of asking the market for it. Bear implication: the credit repair is precisely what the last three years of share-price appreciation already paid for.
