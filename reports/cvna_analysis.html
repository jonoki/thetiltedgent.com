01 Company Overview

Carvana buys used cars, reconditions them, photographs them in a 360-degree studio, and sells them online with delivery or vending-machine pickup — and it finances a large share of those sales itself. In FY2025 it did $20.32B of revenue, up 48.6%, and over the twelve months to September 2026 it did $27.45B, up 50.3%, with $1.71B of net income attributable to shareholders. That revenue is now close to CarMax's $29.46B — but Carvana's shares are worth $76.20B versus CarMax's $7.89B and Lithia Motors' $6.48B. The market is not paying for a car dealer. It is paying for a fast-growing logistics-and-credit machine, and that gap is the whole argument.

The central debate is GPU durability. Carvana sold 217,385 retail units in Q3 2026, up 39%, and revenue grew 42% — but total gross profit per unit fell $365 year over year to $6,997, gross margin slipped from 20.3% to 18.9%, and adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 11.3% to 10.0%. Q2 showed the same pattern: volume is buying growth, and unit economics give some of it back. Underneath sits an earnings-quality question — how much of gross profit is the gain on selling the car loans Carvana writes rather than the gain on selling cars — and a control structure in which roughly 40% of consolidated profit does not belong to public shareholders at all. The bull case is that a company with a small share of a fragmented market has a decade of runway: the Q3 shareholder letter, signed by founder and CEO Ernie Garcia and CFO Mark Jenkins, says "We have line of sight to our goal of selling 3 million cars per year and achieving 13.5% Adjusted EBITDA margin," against a Q3 pace of about 870,000 a year. The bear case is that the price leans on a per-car profit that keeps slipping.

GPU is the metric. Total gross profit per unit = total gross profit ÷ retail units sold. For Q3 2026 the company reports $6,997 ($1,521M of gross profit on 217,385 units), against $7,362 a year earlier on 155,941 units — a $365 decline. The parts moved in different directions: retail GPU rose $100 to $3,556, while wholesale GPU fell $158 to $708 and other (finance) GPU fell $307 to $2,733. The shareholder letter puts the total decline down mainly to Carvana's "decision to give back to customers in the form of lower rates on their loans relative to benchmark rates" and to lower wholesale GPU as retail sales grew faster than wholesale marketplace profit, partly offset by wider spreads between retail and wholesale car prices; it adds that elevated fuel prices and rapidly rising benchmark interest rates weighed on the quarter. Read the loan-rate part carefully: Carvana is handing finance margin to buyers to move cars. Revenue per retail unit was about $37.0k, up 2% (this includes wholesale and finance revenue, not just the sticker price). Gross margin fell from 20.3% to 18.9%.

Scale check: 217,385 retail units in Q3 2026 is 61,444 more than a year earlier; at that pace Carvana sells about 870,000 cars a year (Q3 × 4, our arithmetic), against management's stated goal of 3 million.

Carvana bought ADESA's U.S. physical auction business in 2022 — the deal that, combined with the rate shock, nearly killed it. Strategically it was the right asset for the wrong balance sheet: ADESA's sites supply wholesale volume and provide land and buildings that can be converted into reconditioning capacity. Reconditioning throughput is the hard ceiling on unit growth: you cannot retail a car you have not reconditioned, and adding capacity is a construction project, not a software release. What is visible is inventory: $3,456M at September 2026 versus $2,408M at FY2025 year-end (+43.5%) — the company is building stock, not selling it down. In Q3 Carvana integrated retail production at two more ADESA locations, bringing the total to 21, and the shareholder letter says it is building reconditioning capacity ahead of need.

Watch item: the single most useful disclosure Carvana could add is reconditioning units of capacity by site. Without it, "we have runway" is an assertion investors cannot audit.

When Carvana sells a car it usually also writes the loan, then sells that loan — into securitization trusts or to forward-flow buyers — and books the profit immediately as gain on sale. This is real cash profit, but it is front-loaded and it is rate-sensitive: the gain is the spread between the yield on the loan and the cost of the bonds sold against it. Raise the extra yield investors demand on bonds backed by car loans (ABS spreads) or lift benchmark rates without lifting the customer's APR, and the gain compresses instantly, with no change whatsoever in how many cars were sold. The direction and the size of the move are clear: other GPU fell $307 per unit year over year in Q3 2026, more than the whole $365 net decline, because retail GPU rose. The shareholder letter ties the decline to lower rates on customers' loans relative to benchmark rates, at a time when benchmark rates were rising, and pitches the same thing to customers as "lower interest rates on their loans." The "other" line carried $594M of gross profit in Q3, 39% of the total, and $381M of it was gains on selling loans ($331M a year earlier, Q3 2026 Form 10-Q), so it is the line to watch.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
NATIONAL INVENTORY POOL RECONDITIONING SCALE ADESA SITE FOOTPRINT IN-HOUSE ORIGINATION SECURITIZATION SHELF LOGISTICS NETWORK BRAND / VENDING MACHINE

The moat is a national pool of inventory attached to a national logistics network and a captive lender. A traditional dealer sells you what is on its lot, financed by whoever will take the paper. Carvana shows every car it owns to every customer in the country, moves it on its own trucks, and writes and sells the loan itself. That produces three effects that build on each other: selection improves conversion, scale lowers cost per unit delivered, and capturing the loan means a second profit pool on the same transaction. Scale is showing up in costs: SG&A per retail unit fell from $3,816 to $3,703 year over year in Q3 2026 (company-reported). Overhead per car falling while volume grows 39% is the clean part of this story.

What it is not is a moat around price. Used cars are a commodity, the customer shops on payment, and every dollar of GPU is contestable. The evidence is in the numbers: gross margin fell 1.4 percentage points (20.3% to 18.9%) in a quarter when volume grew 39%. That is a business buying share.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Gain-on-sale earnings quality. A material share of gross profit is the immediate gain from selling finance receivables: $381M of Q3 2026's $1,521M (Form 10-Q). This profit is recognised up front and depends on the spread between customer APR and securitization funding cost. Other GPU fell $307 per unit year over year in Q3 2026 — more than the whole $365 decline in total GPU — while units grew 39%. If credit spreads widen or delinquencies rise, this line moves first and moves hard, and the operating story stays intact while the earnings do not.
▸
Dual-class control and the Up-C leak. Public Class A holders do not control the company, and they do not own all of its profit. Q3 2026 consolidated net income was $483M; $289M — 59.8% — was attributable to Carvana Co. The balance sheet carries $1,301M of non-controlling interests against $5,653M of total equity. Headline "net income" and "EPS" describe different pies, and the P/E on the trailing EPS of $1.99 is the honest one.
▸
Balance-sheet memory. Total debt peaked at $8,820M at FY2022 with equity at −$1,053M. It is now $5.70B including leases (stockanalysis.com), or $5,228M of borrowings in the Q3 release, against $5,653M of equity and $3,049M of cash — net borrowings of about $2.2B, under one times the adjusted EBITDA guided for 2026. That is a genuine repair. But it was achieved partly by a distressed exchange that paid interest in extra notes instead of cash (PIK), and the business still ties up cash in inventory ($3,456M and rising) and in receivables it must fund before it can sell them. A frozen securitization market is the specific scenario that hurts.
▸
Beta 3.55. The stock moves about three and a half times as much as the market. A stock that fell 98% and then rose more than seventy-fold has no settled view of what it is worth. At 34x trailing earnings with 9.48% of the float short (Oct 15 settlement), both the earnings and the bets against the stock can swing fast.

RSI compares the size of recent gains with the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has been rising unusually persistently ("overbought"); below 30 it has been falling unusually persistently ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value — a stock can stay overbought for a year.

This is the round trip. On a split-adjusted basis the stock closed October 2021 at $60.64, bottomed at $0.95 in December 2022 — a 98.4% drawdown on month-end closes — and closed October 30, 2026 at $68.29. The five-year price return is therefore a modest +12.6%; the return from the December 2022 month-end low is +7,088%. Both numbers are true, and the distance between them is the entire lesson. Buying the company in October 2021 and buying the identical company fourteen months later were not the same trade. Note also the last year: +11.9%, about the same as the S&P 500, in a year when units grew 39% and the 2030 notes from the 2023 debt exchange were refinanced at a lower rate. The operating recovery was largely in the price already.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A structurally advantaged operator with a small share of a fragmented used-car market, growing units 39% while overhead per unit falls, is early in its run. The balance sheet is repaired — total debt down from $8.82B to $5.70B, equity from −$1,053M to $5,653M, $3.05B of cash — and the August 2026 term loan refinanced the 2030 notes from the 2023 exchange at a lower rate. Q3 delivered record units, record adjusted EBITDA of $800M and a full-year expectation at the higher end of $2.7–3.0B against $2.24B in 2025; ROIC is 31.1% and trailing free cash flow $1.11B. Management says it has line of sight to 3 million cars a year at a 13.5% adjusted EBITDA margin. Nothing in the used-car market forces the share gains to stop.

Bear Case

You are paying 34x trailing earnings — inflated by a Q4 2025 quarter that carried a $618M net non-cash benefit — for a business whose total GPU fell $365 year over year in Q3, after falling in Q2 too, and whose adjusted EBITDA margin slipped to 10.0% from 11.3%. The finance line, the profit that is booked up front and moves first when credit spreads widen, fell $307 per unit as Carvana gave buyers cheaper loans. Q3 EPS missed the Nasdaq consensus by 17%. Only 59.8% of consolidated income belongs to public shareholders. Beta is 3.55, 9.48% of the float is short, and the stock has only kept pace with the S&P 500 over a year in which units grew 39%. The good news is in the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Finance-margin reversal. Other GPU fell $307 per unit in Q3 2026 while units grew 39%. A rise in the extra yield investors demand on car-loan bonds or a deterioration in subprime credit hits this line immediately and invisibly to the unit narrative.
02
A lower valuation on steady results. At 34x trailing and 30x forward (2027 EPS), with a consensus target 18% above the price and a beta of 3.55, the stock can lose a third of its value on a rate move or a single quarter of flat GPU without any change in the business.
03
Capacity and working capital. Unit growth is gated by reconditioning throughput, which is not disclosed, and funded by inventory that grew 43.5% in nine months to $3,456M. Growth here consumes cash before it produces it; a funding-market disruption is the specific 2022 failure mode, and it has been made less likely, not impossible.
Industry Commentary
Rates, spreads and the price of a booked profit
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Carvana's finance profit is a spread business dressed as a retail line item. In Q3 2026 other GPU fell $307, and management again tied it to lower customer loan rates relative to benchmarks while benchmark rates rose (Q3 shareholder letter) — the two halves of the spread moved the wrong way at once. The letter says Carvana has lowered the rates on its customers' loans by more than one percentage point relative to benchmark rates. This is why the stock trades as much on interest rates as on car sales, and it explains a beta of 3.55 better than the equity story does. It also cuts the other way: lower yields on car-loan bonds and a fall in benchmark rates would restore that $307 without Carvana selling one extra car.

A slow industry that Carvana is growing inside
ONGOING HEADWIND

The comparison set is barely growing. CarMax's trailing revenue is up 4.2%, with net income of $292.5M on $29.46B (a 1.0% margin); Lithia's is up 1.6%, with net income down 12% to $788M. Against that, CVNA is up 50.3%. Share is not being handed over — it is being taken from operators with fixed lot costs, local inventory and a financing arm they cannot scale. The headwind is that a slow industry means every unit of Carvana growth is somebody else's loss, and incumbents defending margin are why GPU is contestable.

Balance-sheet normalisation is done — what it means for the debate
EMERGING TAILWIND

The August 2026 term loan at SOFR plus 2.25 percentage points (SOFR is a benchmark lending rate) replaced notes issued in the 2023 exchange with a 9.00%/11.00%/13.00% cash-or-PIK coupon (PIK: interest paid in extra notes rather than cash), paying 9% in cash by then. The 2031 notes from the same exchange, $2,269M at 9%, are still outstanding (Q3 2026 Form 10-Q). Quarterly net interest expense fell from $125M to $98M year over year in Q3 2026, a quarter that had the 2030 notes outstanding until late August; the company put the refinancing's saving at roughly $45M a year. Bull implication: every dollar of that saving drops to pre-tax income, and a company with about $2.2B of net borrowings and $1.11B of trailing free cash flow can fund reconditioning capacity internally instead of asking the market for it. Bear implication: the credit repair is precisely what the last three years of share-price appreciation already paid for. From here the stock has to be underwritten on GPU and units alone — and total GPU fell again in Q3.
