01 Company Overview

Dominion Energy is a $59.3 billion regulated electric utility holding company that, following a multi-year portfolio simplification, now derives essentially all of its earnings from state-regulated electric and gas utilities in Virginia, North Carolina and South Carolina. As of December 31, 2025 it operated roughly 30.7 GW of generating capacity, 10,800 miles of transmission line and 80,400 miles of distribution line, serving about 3.6 million electric customers and 0.5 million gas customers. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $16.51 billion, up 14.2% year over year, with net income of $2.95 billion and diluted EPS of $3.45; TTM revenue through June 30, 2026 reached $18.12 billion (+19.0%). It is the 11th-largest utility in the world by market capitalization and roughly the 427th-largest public company globally.

The dominant fact about this equity today is not its fundamentals. On May 18, 2026, NextEra Energy agreed to acquire Dominion in a stock-plus-cash combination — 0.8138 NextEra shares per Dominion share plus a $360 million aggregate special cash payment — with joint regulatory applications filed July 15, 2026 and closing targeted for the second half of 2027. From announcement onward, D has traded primarily as a deal-spread instrument: its price is a function of NextEra's share price, the market's estimate of completion probability, the time to close, and the dividend stream collected in the interim. Fundamental valuation still matters, but chiefly as the downside anchor if the deal breaks.

Dominion's moat is the classic regulated-utility construct, sharpened by an accident of geography. It holds exclusive franchises to serve defined territories; capital deployed into those territories earns an authorised return set by the Virginia State Corporation Commission and the South Carolina PSC; and 80,400 miles of distribution line plus 10,800 miles of transmission line are not replicable by any entrant at any price. What differentiates Dominion from an ordinary regulated electric is that its franchise happens to sit on top of the world's densest data-center cluster. A 53.8 GW contracting pipeline against a 30.7 GW existing fleet is a rate-base growth runway that most utilities would need decades of load growth to manufacture, and Virginia's large-load tariff structures are explicitly designed to keep the associated capital risk with the hyperscaler rather than the utility. Layered on top since May 2026 is an unusual, temporary form of downside protection: a signed agreement with NextEra puts a negotiated exchange ratio under the stock for as long as the market believes the deal closes.

Regulatory Concentration in a Single Commission. Roughly 84% of operating earnings come from one segment answering primarily to the Virginia State Corporation Commission. A single adverse ruling on authorised ROE, on CVOW cost recovery, or on large-load tariff design moves the whole company. The Q2 2026 $95 million disallowance charge is a small but directional signal that Virginia will not automatically pass every offshore-wind overrun to ratepayers.

Offshore Wind Execution. CVOW has moved from ~$10.7bn to $11.7bn and slipped from early-2027 to end-2027 completion, with each additional quarter beyond July 2027 potentially costing $150–200 million. Marine construction, weather windows, blade-damage incidents, federal permitting posture (a BOEM stop-work order cost $228 million in one stroke) and tariff policy are all outside management's control. The remaining ~19% of the build is the part where the residual risk is concentrated.

Balance Sheet and Cash Generation. Free cash flow is −$6.85 billion on TTM operating cash flow of $5.39 billion; total debt is $53.93 billion against $298 million of cash, debt-to-equity of 1.60x and a current ratio of 0.81. The dividend payout ratio is 93.3%. Dominion is structurally dependent on continuous access to debt and equity markets; a ratings action during the merger review period would be expensive.

Loss of Independent Optionality. With a definitive agreement signed, Dominion's board has effectively capped the equity's upside at the exchange ratio. If Northern Virginia load growth proves even better than the 53.8 GW pipeline implies, that upside now accrues to NextEra holders — Dominion shareholders participate only through the 0.8138 NEE shares they will receive. Conversely, a deal break returns the stock to a fundamental multiple that, at 23.4x trailing, is unlikely to be defended on standalone merits.

Notable One-Off Items

Q2 2026 carried approximately $704 million of pre-tax adjusting items, principally impairments of nonregulated renewable natural gas facilities and charges on solar generation assets. That is the entire explanation for GAAP EPS of $0.37 against operating EPS of $0.79, and for GAAP net income falling to $340 million from $760 million a year earlier despite operating earnings rising to $712 million from $649 million. Separately, the quarter included a $95 million charge for Virginia Power's share of CVOW costs not expected to be recovered — small in dollars, meaningful as precedent.

Historical distortions worth naming. Dominion cut its dividend by roughly a third in 2020 in connection with the sale of its gas transmission and storage business; the payout was rebased and the annual rate has been held flat at $2.67 since 2022, with the trailing payout ratio now 93.3%. The subsequent strategic business review culminated in the sale of the gas distribution utilities to Enbridge, completing the pivot to a regulated-electric pure play — the reason FY21 EPS of $4.12 (boosted by discontinued-operations gains) is not comparable to today's continuing-operations base, and why FY22 EPS collapsed to $1.33. Anyone modelling multi-year EPS trend lines from the 2021–2023 period is modelling three different companies. These pre-2026 corporate-history items are contextual; the live sources used for this snapshot confirmed the 2020-era rebasing of the dividend rate but did not re-verify transaction dates or proceeds.

Five-year trajectory. From a September 2021 monthly mark of $58.90 to the August 20, 2026 close of $67.47, D delivered a price return of roughly +14.6% over five years — about 2.8% annualised. That is a poor price outcome, but a misleading one in isolation: with the dividend rebased to $2.67 and held flat since 2022, dividends contributed roughly $13 per share over the period, taking total return to something on the order of +36% (≈6.4% annualised), still well short of the S&P 500 over the same window. The shape matters more than the endpoint. The stock peaked near $69.74 in March 2022, then lost roughly half its value into an October 2023 trough of $35.36 as rate hikes crushed bond-proxy valuations and the market lost confidence in the balance sheet and the offshore wind budget. The recovery from that trough — $35.36 to $67.47, +90.8% — was driven first by the completed business review and the pivot to regulated electric, then by the Northern Virginia data-center thesis, and finally, in May 2026, by NextEra's bid.

The merger inflection. The May 2026 announcement is visible but not dramatic in the monthly series: April's $63.86 mark rose to $66.94 at end-May and peaked at $69.17 in July before easing to $67.47. That muted reaction is itself the story. NextEra offered stock, not cash, so Dominion holders took on NEE price risk rather than banking a fixed premium; and with closing 14+ months out and six separate regulatory approvals outstanding, the market has priced in a discount. At NEE's $85.03 close, the exchange ratio is worth $69.20 plus roughly $0.41 of cash per share — an implied $69.61 against D's $67.47, or a gross spread of about 3.2%. Add the interim dividend stream and the arithmetic is a mid-single-digit annualised return for taking regulatory and deal-break risk.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A signed agreement with a strategically motivated, investment-grade acquirer puts a negotiated floor under the stock, and the ~3.2% gross spread to the $69.61 implied deal value plus roughly $3.34 of dividends over a ~15-month wait is a mid-single-digit annualised return with utility-grade volatility (beta 0.63). Underneath the deal, the asset is genuinely scarce: 53.8 GW of data-center capacity in contracting against a 30.7 GW fleet, in the largest data-center market on earth, under a Virginia tariff regime designed to place the risk on hyperscalers. If the deal breaks, the buyer that bid once may bid again, and the underlying franchise is the reason.

Bear Case

You are paid roughly 3% of spread to underwrite six regulatory approvals across three state commissions, FERC, the NRC and HSR, over 14-plus months, in an all-stock deal where the consideration floats with NextEra's share price and there is no cash collar disclosed. If the Virginia SCC blocks or heavily conditions the transaction, the stock reverts to standalone fundamentals that are unattractive: 23.4x trailing earnings, 4.22% ROIC, 8.28% ROE, 1.60x debt-to-equity, a 93.3% payout ratio, −$6.85 billion free cash flow, and an offshore wind project that has already moved from ~$10.7bn to $11.7bn with 19% of the build left to go.

Top 3 Risks
01
Deal-break / regulatory denial. The Virginia SCC alone governs ~84% of Dominion's operating earnings and has already shown, via the $95 million CVOW disallowance, that it will impose costs on shareholders. A denial or an approval loaded with conditions NextEra will not accept removes the exchange-ratio floor overnight; the downside reference is not the current price but something closer to the pre-announcement April 2026 level of $63.86 or below.
02
Offshore wind cost and schedule. CVOW is $11.7 billion, 81% complete, and has slipped to end-2027. Management has quantified the slippage cost at $150–200 million per additional quarter past July 2027. Marine construction risk, weather windows, blade damage, federal permitting posture and tariffs are all outside management control, and the Q2 charge establishes that overruns are not automatically recoverable.
03
Balance sheet and funding dependence. $53.93 billion of debt against $298 million of cash, a 0.81 current ratio, 0.32 quick ratio, and −$6.85 billion of free cash flow mean Dominion must continuously access capital markets to fund its build. A credit downgrade during the merger review period — or a rate backup that reprices the whole bond-proxy complex — raises the cost of a plan that already earns only 4.22% on invested capital.

CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The NextEra–Dominion combination is the defining regulated-utility transaction of this cycle: an all-stock deal at 0.8138x plus $360 million of cash, announced May 18, 2026, filed July 15, 2026, targeted to close in H2 2027, and creating what the parties call the world's largest regulated electric utility by market cap. NextEra brings roughly 80 GW of capacity and an expected $45 billion of annual capex to a Dominion franchise that needs capital more than it needs anything else. The approval stack is long — Virginia SCC, North Carolina Utilities Commission, South Carolina PSC, FERC, NRC and HSR, plus both shareholder votes — and the parties have pre-loaded the political case with $2.25 billion of customer bill credits over two years post-close, $10 million a year of charitable giving for five years, 18-month job protections and 24-month pay-and-benefit guarantees. For an investor this reduces to one number: at NEE's $85.03, the consideration is worth about $69.61 against a $67.47 market price. That ~3.2% gap, not the P/E, is the valuation.
Data-Center Load in Northern Virginia
EMERGING TAILWIND
Dominion's disclosed data-center contracting pipeline reached 53.8 GW in July 2026, up 11% from December 2025 — against an existing fleet of about 30.7 GW. Even heavily discounted for attrition and duplicate requests, that is the largest single-utility load-growth opportunity in the United States, and it is the reason Dominion Energy Virginia's operating EPS contribution rose $0.12 year over year in Q2 2026 while every other segment went sideways or backwards. The critical structural detail is contract design: Virginia's large-load tariff framework imposes minimum-demand commitments and exit obligations on hyperscalers so that the utility builds against contracted revenue rather than speculative forecasts. That is what turns a load boom into rate base instead of into stranded assets — and it is also, not coincidentally, what made Dominion worth $67 billion of NextEra stock.
Offshore Wind Execution — the bull/bear hinge
ONGOING HEADWIND
CVOW is the swing factor that cuts both ways, and it frames the entire bull/bear debate. Bull framing: at 81% complete with over 450 MW already operational, the majority of the capital is sunk and the majority of the execution risk is behind; delivering 2.6 GW of contracted carbon-free capacity into the country's tightest load pocket is exactly the asset NextEra is paying for, and completion removes the last major overhang before close. Bear framing: the budget has climbed from ~$10.7bn (early 2025) to $11.5bn (Feb 2026) to $11.7bn (Aug 2026), completion has slipped from early-2027 to end-2027, a single federal stop-work order cost $228 million, tariffs and PJM network-upgrade reassignments added to the latest increase, and the Q2 2026 $95 million disallowance proves Virginia will make shareholders eat part of it. Each further quarter of delay past July 2027 costs $150–200 million — and every dollar of that lands before the merger closes, when Dominion shareholders, not NextEra's, still own it.
