01 Company Overview

Dominion Energy is a $60.3 billion regulated electric utility holding company that, following a multi-year portfolio simplification, now derives essentially all of its earnings from state-regulated electric and gas utilities in Virginia, North Carolina and South Carolina. As of December 31, 2025 it operated roughly 30.7 GW of generating capacity, 10,800 miles of transmission line and 80,400 miles of distribution line, serving about 3.6 million electric customers and 0.5 million gas customers. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $16.51 billion, up 14.2% year over year, with net income of $2.95 billion and diluted EPS of $3.45; TTM revenue through September 30, 2026 reached $19.21 billion, and third-quarter revenue of $5.62 billion was up 24.1% from $4.53 billion a year earlier. Third-quarter operating earnings (the company's own measure, which leaves out one-off gains and charges) were $1.28 per share against $1.06, and management now expects full-year operating EPS in the top half of its $3.45–$3.69 range. It ranks #200 in the S&P 500 by market value.

The dominant fact about this equity today is not its fundamentals. On May 18, 2026, NextEra Energy announced an agreement, dated May 15, to acquire Dominion in a stock-plus-cash combination — 0.8138 NextEra shares per Dominion share plus a $360 million aggregate special cash payment — with joint regulatory applications filed July 15, 2026, shareholder votes at both companies on September 3, 2026 that approved it (671.3 million of the 682.1 million votes cast at Dominion's meeting, or 98.4%, were in favor, per its 8-K) and closing targeted for the second half of 2027. Virginia's State Corporation Commission, the state regulator, starts evidentiary hearings on November 17, 2026 and must rule by January 11, 2027. On October 19 its staff recommended against the deal as proposed, saying it should be approved only with dozens of binding conditions, and Governor Abigail Spanberger asked for a six-year rate freeze and larger bill credits. From announcement onward, D has traded mainly on the deal: its price depends on NextEra's share price, the market's estimate of whether the deal closes, the time to close, and the dividends collected while waiting. The business's own value still matters, but chiefly as the floor if the deal breaks.

The regulated electric utility serving 2.8 million customers in Virginia and North Carolina, and the single reason to own this company. Its service territory contains Northern Virginia's "Data Center Alley" — the largest concentration of data centers on earth. On the Q3 2026 call Dominion put the data center capacity in various stages of contracting at more than 54 GW, including more than 12 GW already under electric service agreements, up more than 6 GW (13%) since the end of 2025 — about 1.8× the company's entire current generating fleet of 30.7 GW. Virginia has responded with a dedicated large-load rate class and long-dated contract structures designed to make the largest data-center customers commit to minimum payments and exit fees rather than spreading the cost onto household customers — the mechanism that turns a load boom into rate base (the investment regulators let a utility earn a return on) rather than into plants nobody pays for. Dominion's moat is the classic regulated-utility construct, sharpened by an accident of geography. It holds exclusive franchises to serve defined territories; capital deployed into those territories earns an authorised return set by the Virginia State Corporation Commission and the South Carolina PSC; and 80,400 miles of distribution line plus 10,800 miles of transmission line are not replicable by any entrant at any price. What differentiates Dominion from an ordinary regulated electric is that its franchise happens to sit on top of the world's densest data-center cluster. A more-than-54 GW contracting pipeline against a 30.7 GW existing fleet is a rate-base growth runway that most utilities would need decades of load growth to manufacture, and Virginia's large-load tariff structures are explicitly designed to keep the associated capital risk with the data-center customer rather than the utility. Layered on top since May 2026 is an unusual, temporary form of downside protection: a signed agreement with NextEra puts a negotiated exchange ratio under the stock for as long as the market believes the deal closes.

Regulatory Concentration in a Single Commission. About three-quarters of segment operating earnings (75% over the first nine months of 2026) come from one segment answering primarily to the Virginia State Corporation Commission. A single adverse ruling on authorised ROE, on CVOW cost recovery, or on large-load tariff design moves the whole company. The $95 million net charge booked in 2026, primarily for offshore wind costs not expected to be recovered from customers, is a small but telling sign that not every overrun gets passed on to them.

Offshore Wind Execution. CVOW has moved from ~$10.7bn to $11.65bn and slipped from early-2027 to end-2027 completion; in February management put each additional quarter of turbine installation beyond July 2027 at $150–200 million. Marine construction, weather windows, blade-damage incidents, federal permitting posture (a BOEM stop-work order cost $228 million in one stroke) and tariff policy are all outside management's control. The remaining ~15% of the build is the part where the residual risk is concentrated.

Balance Sheet and Cash Generation. Free cash flow is −$6.30 billion on TTM operating cash flow of $5.68 billion; total debt is $55.60 billion against $435 million of cash, debt-to-equity of 1.62x and a current ratio of 0.88. The dividend payout ratio is 88.6%. Dominion is structurally dependent on continuous access to debt and equity markets; a ratings action during the merger review period would be expensive.

Upside Now Tied to NextEra. With a definitive agreement signed, Dominion holders' upside now comes through the exchange ratio. If Northern Virginia load growth proves even better than the more-than-54 GW pipeline implies, that upside now accrues to NextEra holders — Dominion shareholders participate only through the 0.8138 NEE shares they will receive. Conversely, a broken deal would leave the stock priced on its own earnings, and 22.8 times trailing earnings would be hard to defend on those alone.

Notable One-Off Items

The first nine months of 2026 carried a net pre-tax adjustment of −$661 million between operating and GAAP earnings (GAAP EPS $2.37 against operating EPS $3.02, per the Q3 release). The main items: $719 million of nonregulated impairments and other charges (an $820 million charge on renewable natural gas facilities and $79 million on solar assets, partly offset by a $195 million benefit from revised retirement obligations at Millstone, a nuclear plant in Connecticut), $123 million of hedging losses, $39 million of merger costs and $119 million of severe-weather costs in Dominion Energy Virginia, offset by $438 million of net gains on the nuclear decommissioning trust. The period also included a $95 million net charge, primarily for Virginia Power's share of CVOW costs not expected to be recovered from customers — small in dollars, meaningful as precedent. Q3 on its own was clean: GAAP EPS $1.30 against operating EPS $1.28.

Historical distortions worth naming. Dominion cut its dividend by roughly a third in 2020 in connection with the sale of its gas transmission and storage business; the payout was rebased and the annual rate has been held flat at $2.67 since 2022, with the trailing payout ratio now 88.6%. The subsequent strategic business review culminated in the sale of the gas distribution utilities to Enbridge, completing the pivot to a regulated-electric pure play — the reason FY21 EPS of $4.12 (boosted by discontinued-operations gains) is not comparable to today's continuing-operations base, and why FY22 EPS collapsed to $1.33. Anyone modelling multi-year EPS trend lines from the 2021–2023 period is modelling three different companies.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A signed agreement with a strategically motivated, investment-grade acquirer ties the stock to a negotiated exchange ratio, and the ~3.7% gross spread to the $71.17 implied deal value plus $0.6675 a quarter of dividends while waiting for an H2 2027 close is the return on offer, with utility-grade volatility (beta 0.59). Underneath the deal, the asset is genuinely scarce: more than 54 GW of data-center capacity in contracting against a 30.7 GW fleet, in the largest data-center market on earth, under a Virginia tariff regime designed to place the risk on the big data-center customers. If the deal breaks, the buyer that bid once may bid again, and the underlying franchise is the reason.

Bear Case

You are paid about 3.7% of spread to take on six regulatory approvals across three state commissions, FERC, the NRC and HSR, over the 8 to 14 months to an H2 2027 close, in a mostly-stock deal where the value floats with NextEra's share price, and Virginia's commission staff and governor have already come out against the deal as proposed. If the Virginia SCC blocks or heavily conditions the transaction, the stock reverts to standalone fundamentals that are unattractive: 22.8x trailing earnings, 4.39% ROIC, 8.82% ROE, 1.62x debt-to-equity, a 88.6% payout ratio, −$6.30 billion free cash flow, and an offshore wind project that has already moved from ~$10.7bn to $11.65bn with about 15% of the build left to go.

Top 3 Risks
01
Deal-break / regulatory denial. The Virginia SCC alone governs about three-quarters of Dominion's segment operating earnings; its staff recommended against the deal as proposed on October 19, and the governor wants a six-year rate freeze. A denial or an approval loaded with conditions NextEra will not accept removes the exchange-ratio floor overnight; the downside reference is not the current price but something closer to the pre-announcement April 2026 level of $64.50 or below.
02
Offshore wind cost and schedule. CVOW has an $11.65 billion budget, is about 85% complete, and is still targeted for end-2027 after slipping from early 2027. In February management put the cost at $150–200 million for each additional quarter of turbine installation past July 2027. Marine construction risk, weather windows, blade damage, federal permitting posture and tariffs are all outside management control, and the Q2 charge establishes that overruns are not automatically recoverable.
03
Balance sheet and funding dependence. $55.60 billion of debt against $435 million of cash, a 0.88 current ratio, 0.38 quick ratio, and −$6.30 billion of free cash flow mean Dominion must continuously access capital markets to fund its build. A credit downgrade during the merger review period — or a rise in interest rates that cuts the price of bond-like utility stocks — raises the cost of a plan that already earns only 4.39% on invested capital.
  Industry Commentary
Utility M&A and Consolidation — the deal is the security
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The NextEra–Dominion combination is the defining regulated-utility transaction of this cycle: a stock deal at 0.8138x plus $360 million of cash, announced May 18, 2026, filed July 15, 2026, targeted to close in H2 2027, and creating what the parties call the world's largest regulated electric utility by market cap. NextEra brings roughly 80 GW of capacity and an expected $45 billion of annual capex to a Dominion franchise that needs capital more than it needs anything else. The approval stack is long — Virginia SCC, North Carolina Utilities Commission, South Carolina PSC, FERC, NRC and HSR, with the shareholder votes already passed (both on September 3, 2026) — and the parties have pre-loaded the political case with $2.25 billion of customer bill credits over two years post-close, $10 million a year of charitable giving for five years, 18-month job protections and 24-month pay-and-benefit guarantees, then extended Virginia's residential credits to four years on September 14. On October 19 the commission's staff and the governor said that was not yet enough. For an investor this reduces to one number: at NEE's $86.95, the consideration is worth about $71.17 against a $68.60 market price. That ~3.7% gap, not the P/E, is the valuation.
Data-Center Load in Northern Virginia
EMERGING TAILWIND
Dominion's disclosed data-center contracting pipeline reached more than 54 GW on the Q3 2026 call, up more than 6 GW (13%) since the end of 2025 — against an existing fleet of about 30.7 GW. Even heavily discounted for attrition and duplicate requests, that is a very large load-growth opportunity for one utility. It has not yet driven the quarterly numbers: the release credits Dominion Energy Virginia's $0.19 rise in Q3 2026 operating EPS ($0.98 vs. $0.79) mainly to the 2025 Biennial Review, returns earned on riders and weather. The critical structural detail is contract design: Virginia's large-load tariff framework imposes minimum-demand commitments and exit obligations on the largest data-center customers so that the utility builds against contracted revenue rather than speculative forecasts. That is what turns a load boom into rate base instead of into stranded assets — and it is also, not coincidentally, what made Dominion worth $67 billion of NextEra stock.
Offshore Wind Execution — the bull/bear hinge
ONGOING HEADWIND
CVOW is the swing factor that cuts both ways, and it frames the entire bull/bear debate. Bull framing: at about 85% complete with 44 turbines (over 640 MW) installed, the majority of the capital is sunk and the majority of the execution risk is behind; delivering 2.6 GW of contracted carbon-free capacity into the country's tightest load pocket is exactly the asset NextEra is paying for, and completion removes the last major overhang before close. Bear framing: the budget has climbed from ~$10.7bn (early 2025) to $11.5bn (Feb 2026) to $11.65bn (July 2026), unchanged on the Q3 call, completion has slipped from early-2027 to end-2027, a single federal stop-work order cost $228 million, tariffs and PJM network-upgrade reassignments added to the latest increase, and the $95 million net charge in 2026 shows shareholders are bearing part of it. In February management put each further quarter of turbine installation past July 2027 at $150–200 million, and the July update has since moved completion to end-2027, the same half in which the merger is targeted to close.
