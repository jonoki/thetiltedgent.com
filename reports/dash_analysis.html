01 Company Overview

DoorDash operates a local-commerce platform that connects merchants, consumers and independent couriers ("Dashers"), spanning restaurant delivery, grocery, convenience and general retail, plus merchant software and an advertising business. FY2025 revenue was $13.72B, up 27.9% from $10.72B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $15.89B, up 33.6% — growth inflated by the October 2025 completion of the Deliveroo acquisition. The company ranks roughly #243 globally by market capitalisation at $100.41B.

The reason this name is worth a careful look is narrower than "growth stock." Food delivery was for a decade the canonical business that grew fast and lost money — DoorDash itself lost $1.37B in FY2022 on $6.58B of revenue. It has since printed $935M of GAAP net income in FY2025. The whole question on this page is whether that reflects genuinely inverted unit economics or an accounting-and-scale artefact. Section 04 and the prose below take that apart.

White-label delivery (Drive), merchant-branded ordering (Storefront), reservations and CRM (SevenRooms, acquired for $1,152M, completed June 13, 2025), and advertising. DoorDash does not disclose advertising revenue, so no figure is given here — but this is analytically the most important small line on the page. Every advertising dollar therefore falls to contribution profit at a rate the delivery business can never match. Following the Symbiosys acquisition the company nearly doubled its number of advertising partners between June and December 2025. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
DELIVERY DENSITY DASHER SUPPLY LIQUIDITY DASHPASS HABIT LOCK-IN SUBURBAN-FIRST FOOTPRINT MERCHANT SOFTWARE STACK ROUTING & DATA SCALE ADVERTISING FLYWHEEL

The moat here is density, not technology. In any given zip code the platform with the most orders per square mile can batch deliveries more efficiently, keep Dashers busier per hour of pay, and quote shorter delivery times — which wins more consumers, which raises density again. That loop is local, which is why market share is fought street by street and why DoorDash's historical strength in suburban U.S. markets, where the competition arrived later, is worth more than a national share number would suggest. Sitting on top of density are two hardening layers: DashPass (the paid subscription that waives delivery fees and reduces service fees, converting price-sensitive one-off users into habitual repeat buyers) and the merchant software stack — Storefront, Drive and now SevenRooms — which raises the cost of a restaurant leaving the ecosystem entirely. The contribution-profit disclosure supports the density argument: Q2 2026 contribution profit was $1,641M, or 5.0% of GOV (contribution profit = revenue less the direct variable costs of fulfilling an order — Dasher pay, payment processing, support — before overhead; it is the cleanest read on whether an incremental order makes money). Five cents of contribution on every dollar of gross order value, at 970 million orders a quarter, is a real machine. What it is not is a wide moat against a well-capitalised competitor in a single dense city.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Driver classification law. Dashers are independent contractors (driver classification = the legal question of whether gig couriers are self-employed contractors or employees entitled to minimum wage, overtime, payroll taxes and benefits). Reclassification would reset the cost base of the entire delivery layer, not trim it. California's Proposition 22, which preserved contractor status with limited benefits, was upheld by the California Supreme Court in July 2024 — but that is one state, and city- and country-level rules continue to move independently. DoorDash recorded $98M of legal, tax and regulatory expense in Q2 2026 alone, which it adds back to adjusted EBITDA; a line item that appears every quarter is arguably a cost of doing business, not a one-off.
▸
City-level fee caps. Several U.S. municipalities have capped the commission platforms may charge restaurants. Caps compress the take rate directly in exactly the densest, highest-GOV markets, and the platform's usual response — pushing costs onto the consumer as fees — invites the next round of regulation. This is a slow, grinding, politically durable risk rather than a single event.
▸
Competition from Uber Eats and Instacart. Uber can subsidise Eats from a $55.23B revenue base and a $9.58B profit pool, which means it can afford to be irrational in a given city for longer than DoorDash can. Instacart is the incumbent in exactly the grocery vertical DoorDash needs to make work, and is already profitable there ($472M net income on $3.99B revenue). DoorDash is attacking Instacart's core while defending its own.
▸
Adjusted-versus-GAAP disclosure risk. The company leads with adjusted EBITDA, which excludes stock-based compensation. At $1,051M of SBC in FY2025 on $13.72B of revenue — 7.7% — the exclusion is not a rounding convention, it is the difference between a $2.7B business and a $935M one. If the market ever re-anchors on GAAP, the multiple compresses without a single operating metric changing.

Notable One-Off Items

There are material non-recurring distortions in this period and they all run the same direction — they flatter the top line and depress GAAP earnings.

Deliveroo, $3,724M, completed October 2, 2025. This is a structural change, not a bolt-on. It added roughly ten percentage points to reported order growth (Q2 2026: +27% total orders versus +17% organic) and roughly thirteen to GOV growth (+36% versus +23%). It brought a large chunk of the $2.9B of new goodwill recognised in 2025, along with identified intangibles — developed technology ($216M), merchant, customer, rider and new-vertical relationships, and trade names. Those intangibles amortise, which is why depreciation and amortisation ran $295M in Q2 2026 alone, with FY2026 D&A guided at $1.1–1.2B. Management guided Deliveroo's FY2026 adjusted EBITDA contribution to roughly $200M, down from a Q4 2025 run rate above $45M per quarter, because it is deliberately reinvesting.

SevenRooms, $1,152M, completed June 13, 2025. Restaurant reservation, waitlist and CRM software. Smaller, but strategically it is the merchant-lock-in play, and it contributes further intangible amortisation.

Symbiosys (advertising technology) was also acquired, and the company credits it with nearly doubling advertising partners between June and December 2025. Terms were not verified from the sources fetched.

Legal, tax and regulatory expense of $98M in Q2 2026 and transaction-related costs of $13M are both added back to adjusted EBITDA. The transaction costs are genuinely one-off. The legal and regulatory line has appeared repeatedly, and for a business whose central open question is driver classification, adding it back every quarter is a choice worth noticing.

Buyback: 6.8M shares repurchased year-to-date 2026 for $1,049M — an average of roughly $154 per share, well below the current price. Weighted-average diluted shares were 439M in Q2 2026 against 433.30M shares outstanding now, so the buyback is running at roughly the scale needed to offset SBC dilution rather than to shrink the count meaningfully.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The stack, in the company's own order. Start with Total Orders: 970M in Q2 2026, up 27%, up 17% organically. Multiply by average order value and you get Marketplace GOV (Gross Order Value — the full retail value of everything transacted on the platform): $33.1B, up 36%, up 23% organically. GOV divided into orders gives an implied AOV of about $34, against roughly $32 a year earlier — the grocery and retail push is doing what it is supposed to do to basket size. Revenue is the take rate on that GOV (the share of gross order value DoorDash keeps as its own revenue, after paying merchants): $4,454M ÷ $33.1B = 13.5%, matching the company's stated net revenue margin, and up from 13.2% for FY2025. Then adjusted EBITDA of $914M, up 40% — growing well ahead of both orders and GOV, which is exactly the operating-leverage signature the bull case requires.

And then GAAP net income: $200M, down 30% year over year. This is the disclosure most coverage glosses, and it is the most important thing on this page. In the same quarter that adjusted EBITDA rose 40%, actual accounting profit fell by nearly a third. The reconciliation explains it: from $200M of net income, DoorDash adds back D&A of $295M, legal/tax/regulatory of $98M, transaction costs of $13M, taxes of $8M — and stock-based compensation. SBC (stock-based compensation — shares and options granted to employees; a real transfer of ownership from existing shareholders to staff, which costs no cash but dilutes every holder) ran $580M in the first half of 2026, was $1,051M in FY2025 — 7.7% of revenue — and is guided to $1.2–1.3B for FY2026, roughly 7% of the $17.82B consensus revenue forecast. Put differently: FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was $2.7B and GAAP net income was $935M; SBC alone accounts for about 60% of that $1.77B gap. Anyone valuing this business on adjusted EBITDA is valuing it before paying its employees in full.

Thread 1 — cohort economics: what is and is not disclosed. The bull case rests on a specific mechanism: each annual cohort of new customers costs marketing money once, then orders more frequently every year afterward, so a maturing customer base should throw off rising profit without rising acquisition spend. DoorDash confirms the direction. Its Q2 2026 letter states that as consumer cohorts age, "DashPass penetration increases, order rates increase, and Adjusted Gross Profit per MAU increases." That is a real disclosure and it is consistent with the reported numbers. But note carefully what is not disclosed: no cohort retention table, no per-cohort order-frequency curve, no customer acquisition cost, no payback period, no gross-profit-per-cohort-year figures. Investors are given the shape of the curve in words and asked to accept its steepness on trust. The one place the argument is checkable is new verticals, where management put a date on it — 2H 2026 for positive unit economics. That deadline is the test.

Thread 2 — DashPass. The subscription (DashPass — a paid membership that waives delivery fees and reduces service fees on qualifying orders) is the mechanism that converts a price-comparison shopper into a habit. Combined membership across DashPass, Wolt+ and Deliveroo Plus exceeded 35 million at the end of FY2025. More striking is the acceleration: the company said that in the twelve months through Q2 2026 it added more U.S. paid DashPass members than in the previous twenty-four months combined. And DashPass is where the vertical expansion actually lands — members placed roughly 75% of U.S. grocery and retail orders in Q2 2026. The subscription, frequency and new-vertical stories are one story.

Thread 3 — beyond restaurants, quantified. Over 30% of U.S. MAUs and nearly 30% of global MAUs engaged with grocery and retail in December 2025. Bigger baskets lift AOV (implied AOV rose from roughly $32 to roughly $34 year over year), which lifts GOV per order and therefore revenue per order at a constant take rate. But the margin profile differs: more items per order means more Dasher shop time, more substitutions, more refunds. The company's own admission that unit economics here are not yet positive — with a fix promised for 2H 2026 — is the honest version of what "expanding into grocery" costs.

Thread 4 — advertising, the real margin path. Advertising revenue is not separately disclosed, so no number appears here. Structurally, though, it is the most credible route to durable GAAP profitability: ad dollars carry almost no incremental cost, unlike a delivery, where every dollar of revenue drags Dasher pay behind it. Following the Symbiosys acquisition the advertising partner count nearly doubled between June and December 2025. Alongside it, merchant software is compounding — digital ordering revenue grew over 40% YoY across 150,000+ merchants, and SevenRooms signed venues grew over 100% YoY. These lines are small; they are also the only ones whose growth translates almost fully into profit.

Valuation — and which earnings base. Be explicit, because the answer changes by a factor of four depending on the denominator. (i) On trailing GAAP earnings of $1.90 per share, DASH trades at 122×. That is the honest, fully-loaded number: it charges the company for stock compensation and for the amortisation of what it paid for Deliveroo and SevenRooms. (ii) On 2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $5.79, it trades at 40×; the reported forward P/E of 36.11× implies a slightly later or higher earnings base still. The $3.89 gap between $1.90 and $5.79 is where all the argument lives. (iii) On trailing free cash flow of $2.60B, it trades at about 39× (EV/FCF ≈ 38×) — but FCF adds SBC back as non-cash, so it flatters an owner who cares about dilution. (iv) On adjusted EBITDA, annualising the Q2 run rate and Q3 guidance gives roughly $3.6–3.8B for FY2026 (est.), or about 26–27× EV/adjusted EBITDA against the $98.37B enterprise value; the reported EV/EBITDA of 68.5× uses the GAAP measure instead. The defensible base is somewhere between (i) and (iii): GAAP earnings are real but are temporarily depressed by acquisition amortisation that will run off, while free cash flow is real but ignores about $1.2B of annual dilution. Charge the business for SBC and credit it for the non-cash intangible amortisation, and the multiple lands well above the 40× headline and well below 122×. At any point in that band, the stock is priced for the unit economics to keep inverting — not merely to have inverted once.

Bull Case

The economics have genuinely inverted and the inversion is compounding: contribution profit of $1,641M is 5.0% of GOV, adjusted EBITDA grew 40% against 27% order growth, and free cash flow more than doubled to $742M in a single quarter. Cohorts mature into higher frequency, DashPass locks in that frequency at record signup rates, and advertising and merchant software — near-pure-margin lines growing 40–100% — layer profit on top of a delivery business that already covers its variable costs. A net-cash balance sheet ($5.35B cash against $3.31B debt) funds it all with no financing risk.

Bear Case

GAAP net income fell 30% in the same quarter adjusted EBITDA rose 40%, and the difference is largely $1.2–1.3B of annual stock-based compensation plus $1.1–1.2B of acquisition amortisation. Headline growth is inflated by Deliveroo (27% orders becomes 17% organic) and will step down mechanically from Q4 2026. New-vertical unit economics are still negative on the company's own admission. At 122× trailing GAAP earnings with a 1.77 beta, driver-classification law that could reset the cost base, and Uber able to subsidise from a $9.58B profit pool, the margin for error on a still-thin 5.29% net margin is very small.

Top 3 Risks

01
Driver classification. Reclassifying Dashers as employees would add minimum wage, overtime, payroll taxes and benefits to the largest variable cost in the model — it would not shave the cost base, it would reset it. Prop 22 survived in California (upheld July 2024), but city and international rules move separately, and $98M of legal/tax/regulatory expense in a single quarter shows the cost of contesting them is already live.
02
The adjusted-to-GAAP gap closing the wrong way. The market currently pays roughly 40× an adjusted number and tolerates 122× the GAAP one. If SBC stays near 7% of revenue while revenue growth normalises toward the high teens after Deliveroo annualises, the multiple has to compress — no operating metric needs to miss for that to happen.
03
New verticals failing to cross over in 2H 2026. Management has staked a dated claim on grocery and retail unit economics turning positive. That vertical is where the AOV growth, the DashPass attach and much of the incremental TAM sit. A slip would undercut the cohort-maturation argument at exactly the point where it is testable, and would leave the company competing with an already-profitable Instacart on unprofitable orders.
  Industry Commentary
Labour Law Is the Sector's Cost Base
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every on-demand delivery platform runs on contractor labour, and the entire industry's margin structure is a bet that this arrangement holds. California's Proposition 22 was upheld by the state Supreme Court in July 2024, which removed the largest single-jurisdiction threat — but the question is now fragmented across cities and countries rather than settled. DoorDash's $98M of legal, tax and regulatory expense in Q2 2026 alone, added back to adjusted EBITDA every quarter, is the visible running cost of that fragmentation. This is the one risk that could change the sign of the industry's economics rather than the magnitude.
Consolidation Has Replaced the Subsidy War
ONGOING HEADWIND
The 2018–2021 phase of buying share with discounts is over; the 2025–2026 phase is buying share outright. DoorDash spent roughly $4.9B on Deliveroo ($3,724M, closed Oct 2, 2025) and SevenRooms ($1,152M, closed June 13, 2025), booking about $2.9B of new goodwill in 2025 and committing to $1.1–1.2B of annual D&A. That is cheaper than a price war and structurally worse for GAAP earnings — it converts a cash cost into a decade of amortisation that adjusted EBITDA never sees. Expect more of it, and expect the adjusted-versus-GAAP gap in this sector to widen, not narrow.
Retail Media Is the Margin, Delivery Is the Traffic
EMERGING TAILWIND
The lesson of Amazon and now Instacart is that a marketplace's profits come from advertising against its own demand, not from fulfilling orders. DoorDash is running the same play — the Symbiosys acquisition nearly doubled its advertising partners between June and December 2025, and DashPass gives it a 35M-member logged-in audience with revealed purchase intent. Bull framing: if advertising and merchant software scale, DoorDash re-rates from a thin-margin logistics business toward a media-and-software multiple, and today's 40× forward earnings looks reasonable. Bear framing: the company does not disclose advertising revenue at all, so there is currently no way to verify the ramp; until it breaks the line out, the margin story is an assertion, and the fully-loaded 122× trailing GAAP multiple is the only number you can actually check.
