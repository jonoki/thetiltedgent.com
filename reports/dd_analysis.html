01 Company Overview

The DuPont on the ticker today has been rebuilt twice in seven years. In 2019 DowDuPont split three ways — Dow went one way, Corteva (agriculture) another, and what was left kept the DD symbol and did a 1-for-3 reverse split on June 1, 2019. On November 1, 2025 that DuPont spun off its electronics business as Qnity Electronics (NYSE: Q), handing holders one Qnity share for every two DD shares. On April 1, 2026 it sold its aramids business to Arclin. And on June 24, 2026 it did another 1-for-3 reverse split, cutting the share count from roughly 405 million to 135 million. What remains is a two-segment company with FY2025 revenue of $6.85B — Tyvek, water filtration systems, medical-device components, and building and industrial materials. It is now classified by S&P as an Industrial Conglomerate, having moved out of Materials effective July 31, 2026.

That history is the first thing to understand, because it breaks almost every long-run number on a data terminal. The trailing GAAP EPS of $0.39 is swamped by losses booked in discontinued operations as the electronics and aramids businesses left. The "52-week change" of −44.6% shown on quote pages compares today's price with a pre-spin price that still included Qnity. The business underneath is steadier than either number suggests. Q2 2026 sales rose 4% organically to $1.819B, adjusted EPS rose 48% to $1.88, and management raised full-year guidance to $7.17–$7.32 adjusted EPS. At $129.79 the stock trades at roughly 17.9x the $7.26 consensus 2026 estimate. So the question is simple. Is a smaller, simpler DuPont a steady mid-single-digit compounder worth a market multiple? Or does the PFAS liability, which never left with any of the spin-offs, keep it at a discount for good?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brand-name materials (Tyvek) Specified-in medical components Water-filtration technology 35.1% gross margin Two-segment focus Moderate leverage (D/E 0.23)

DuPont's edge is specification. A membrane written into a water plant's design, or a component validated in a regulated medical device, is costly for the customer to switch. Changing it means requalifying the product, and in healthcare that can mean regulatory paperwork. That shows in a 35.1% gross margin and a 30.1% operating-EBITDA margin in Healthcare & Water. The weakness is that the moat protects the product lines, not the balance sheet. Legacy liabilities from two centuries of chemistry came along through every restructuring.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
PFAS liability is shared, open-ended and still being settled. On September 10, 2026, Chemours, DuPont and Corteva agreed a $455M settlement with North Carolina and 11 local entities, paid over 15 years (net present value ~$355M across the three companies). DuPont puts the pre-tax present value of its own share at ~$126M, 44% of it reimbursable by Qnity, and says it is materially covered by existing accruals. ESG Dive reports a further $135M reserve fund set up by DuPont and Corteva in case Chemours cannot pay; state officials put the total at $590M. The Q2 release lists the three-way arrangement to share future PFAS costs as a risk factor. How much DuPont pays in future is not a number anyone can model with confidence.
02
GAAP earnings are nearly invisible. TTM GAAP EPS is $0.39, and FY2025 was −$5.58. The dividend payout ratio on GAAP earnings is 616%. The adjusted figures ($1.88 in Q2) exclude the separation and discontinued-operations charges. That is standard practice, but it means investors have to trust management's adjustments. The Altman Z-score shown on StockAnalysis is 0.96. The Piotroski F-score is a healthier 8.
03
Slower growth after the spin. Qnity was the semiconductor exposure. What remains is guided to "slightly above 4%" organic growth for 2026. Consensus has revenue rising 4.63% in 2026 and 3.94% in 2027. That is a solid multi-industrial profile, not a growth story, and the multiple has to reflect it.
04
Construction and industrial cycle. Diversified Industrials is 52.9% of sales, and Building Technologies grew only low-single digits in Q2 2026. Healthcare & Water also flagged Middle East weakness in water. A construction downturn would hit the larger segment first.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, discontinued operations. Qnity (spun off November 2025) and aramids (sold April 2026) leave through that line, so FY2025 GAAP net income was −$779M while operating income was $865M. Second, restatement. StockAnalysis's annual revenue falls from $13.0B in FY2022 to $6.6B in FY2023. That likely reflects later years being restated to continuing operations while FY2021–22 were not (my reading, not confirmed on a filing), so multi-year revenue trends before FY2023 are not like-for-like. Third, the June 2026 reverse split tripled all per-share figures. Pre-split EPS, dividends and prices have to be multiplied by 3 to compare. The quarterly dividend of $0.60 is the post-split equivalent of $0.20. In capital returns, DuPont said it intends to buy back $250M of shares in Q3 2026. TTM repurchases were $809M.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

How to read this chart. Every point before June 2026 has been adjusted twice. It is multiplied by 3 for the June 24, 2026 reverse split, and scaled by Yahoo's factor for the Qnity spin-off (Yahoo records the spin as a "239:100" adjustment dated November 3, 2025). Note too that digrin's "Real price" column is itself spin-adjusted before November 2025 (it shows $34.15 for October 2025), so it is not a truly unadjusted series.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

For about 17.9x 2026 consensus earnings, you get a simpler company. Two segments, 4% organic growth, a gross margin that has risen from 33.4% to 35.1%, a 30% EBITDA-margin healthcare and water business, and a guidance raise already in hand (adjusted EPS $7.17–$7.32). The balance sheet is moderate (D/E 0.23, current ratio 2.43). Q2 cash conversion was 127%, and a $250M quarterly buyback is under way. The North Carolina settlement puts a price on one piece of the PFAS overhang. Every one of 17 analysts' targets sits above the price, with a floor 20% higher. If the GICS move to Industrials leads the market to value DuPont like a multi-industrial rather than a chemical company, the multiple can follow.

Bear Case

Take away the adjustments and there is very little GAAP profit: $0.39 of TTM EPS, a −$5.58 FY2025, and a 616% payout ratio on reported earnings. The PFAS liability is shared three ways under a cost-sharing arrangement the company itself lists as a risk. North Carolina is one state, and more claims are possible. The growth engine (Qnity's semiconductors) is gone, and consensus sees revenue growth under 4% in 2027. Diversified Industrials, more than half of sales, rides the construction cycle. The chart has rolled over from a 91 RSI peak to below both moving averages. A 30% gap between price and the average target is what a legal overhang looks like when the models do not price it.

Top 3 Risks

01
PFAS litigation and cost-sharing. The September 10, 2026 North Carolina settlement ($455M over 15 years; DuPont's pre-tax share ~$126M before Qnity's 44% reimbursement) shows the liability is real and still being paid out. How future costs split among DuPont, Corteva and Chemours is a named risk in DuPont's own filings.
02
Reliance on adjusted earnings. The valuation case uses adjusted EPS ($7.17–$7.32 guided) because GAAP EPS ($0.39 TTM) is distorted by separations. If separation and legal charges keep recurring, the gap between the two stops being a one-off.
03
Cyclical exposure in the larger segment. Diversified Industrials (52.9% of Q2 sales) depends on construction and industrial demand. Building Technologies grew only low-single digits in Q2 2026, and Middle East water weakness was already flagged.
Industry Commentary
PFAS: the liability that never spun off
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Each restructuring (Dow and Corteva in 2019, Qnity in 2025, aramids in 2026) moved businesses out of DuPont. The PFAS exposure stayed shared among DuPont, Corteva and Chemours. The North Carolina agreement of September 10, 2026 is the latest example. Officials there called it the largest state recovery for environmental damage in the state's history, and it covers both the Fayetteville Works facility and statewide firefighting-foam contamination. The settlement is payable over 15 years, which spreads the cash cost. It does not settle any other state's claims.

From chemicals to multi-industrial
RE-CLASSIFICATION

The July 31, 2026 GICS change to Industrials follows the portfolio. With electronics gone to Qnity and aramids sold to Arclin, DuPont is a maker of engineered components and materials for healthcare, water, construction, aerospace and autos. Classification affects which index funds and sector managers own the stock, and which peer multiples analysts use. Industrial conglomerates and specialty chemicals do not trade on the same multiples. The size of that gap was not sourced for this report.

Healthcare and water as the quality core
EMERGING TAILWIND

Healthcare & Water Technologies is 47% of sales and earns a 30.1% operating-EBITDA margin. Management expects "continued strength across healthcare, industrial water, and aerospace end-markets" in the second half. The bull framing: as this segment's share grows, the group margin and the multiple rise with it. The bear framing: water already showed regional weakness in Q2, and the segment margin fell 30 bps even as sales grew. The next two quarters of segment margin will show which framing holds.
