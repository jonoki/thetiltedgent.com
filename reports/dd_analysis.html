01 Company Overview

The DuPont on the ticker today has been rebuilt twice in seven years. In 2019 DowDuPont split three ways — Dow went one way, Corteva (agriculture) another, and what was left kept the DD symbol and did a 1-for-3 reverse split on June 1, 2019. On November 1, 2025 that DuPont spun off its electronics business as Qnity Electronics (NYSE: Q), handing holders one Qnity share for every two DD shares. On April 1, 2026 it sold its aramids business to Arclin. And on June 24, 2026 it did another 1-for-3 reverse split, cutting the share count from roughly 405 million to 135 million. What remains is a two-segment company with FY2025 revenue of $6.85B — Tyvek, water filtration systems, medical-device components, and building and industrial materials. It is now classified by S&P as an Industrial Conglomerate, having moved out of Materials effective July 31, 2026.

That history is the first thing to understand, because it breaks almost every long-run number on a data terminal. The trailing GAAP EPS of $2.21 is still held down by losses booked in discontinued operations as the electronics and aramids businesses left. The business underneath is steadier than that number suggests. In Q3 2026 (reported November 3) sales rose 4% to $1.868B, 5% organically (excluding currency, acquisitions and divestitures), and adjusted EPS rose 62% to $2.09, ahead of the $1.86 Nasdaq consensus. Management raised full-year guidance again, to $7.47–$7.52 adjusted EPS from $7.17–$7.32 in August. The shares rose 8.6% on results day. At $150.73 the stock trades at about 20.1x the guidance midpoint. So the question is simple. Is a smaller, simpler DuPont, growing sales in the mid-single digits, worth the price investors pay for the average large company? Or does the PFAS liability, which never left with any of the spin-offs, keep it at a discount for good?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brand-name materials (Tyvek) Specified-in medical components Water-filtration technology 35.5% gross margin Two-segment focus Moderate leverage (D/E 0.23)

DuPont's edge is specification. A membrane written into a water plant's design, or a component validated in a regulated medical device, is costly for the customer to switch. Changing it means requalifying the product, and in healthcare that can mean regulatory paperwork. That shows in a 35.5% gross margin over the last twelve months and a 30.4% operating-EBITDA margin in Healthcare & Water in Q3 2026. The weakness is that the moat protects the product lines, not the balance sheet. Legacy liabilities from two centuries of chemistry came along through every restructuring.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
PFAS liability is shared, open-ended and still being settled. In a deal announced on September 10, 2026, Chemours, DuPont and Corteva agreed a $455M settlement with North Carolina and 11 local entities, paid over 15 years (net present value ~$355M across the three companies). DuPont puts the pre-tax present value of its own share at ~$126M, 44% of it reimbursable by Qnity, and says it is materially covered by existing accruals. DuPont and Corteva must also put up $135M of financial assurance, 71% from DuPont (Q3 10-Q), which ESG Dive describes as a reserve in case Chemours cannot pay; North Carolina officials put the total, with that reserve, at $590M (WUNC). The first payment, $103M of which $36M came from DuPont, was made on October 9, 2026 (10-Q). DuPont booked a $127M charge for the North Carolina matters in discontinued operations in the first nine months of 2026 (Q3 10-Q), and the Q3 release still lists the three-way arrangement to share future PFAS costs as a risk factor. How much DuPont pays in future is not a number anyone can model with confidence.
02
GAAP earnings are nearly invisible. TTM GAAP EPS is $2.21, against about $7.50 of adjusted EPS guided for 2026, and FY2025 was −$5.58. The dividend takes about 109% of GAAP earnings. In Q3 2026 alone, GAAP EPS from continuing operations was $1.24 and adjusted EPS $2.09. Most of the gap is the amortization of intangible assets from past acquisitions ($0.38 a share) and tax items ($0.31), with separation and restructuring costs making up most of the rest (Q3 release reconciliation). That is standard practice, but it means investors have to trust management's adjustments. The Altman Z-score shown on StockAnalysis is 0.68 (a balance-sheet stress score where low readings flag risk). The Piotroski F-score, a 0–9 checklist of financial health, is a healthier 8.
03
Slower growth after the spin. Qnity was the semiconductor exposure. What remains now expects about 4.5% organic growth for 2026, per the Q3 release. Consensus has revenue rising 4.80% in 2026 and 3.95% in 2027 (StockAnalysis). That is a solid multi-industrial profile, not a growth story, and the price investors pay per dollar of earnings has to reflect it.
04
Construction and industrial cycle. Diversified Industrials is 53.7% of sales. Building Technologies picked up to mid-single-digit organic growth in Q3 2026, but it is the part of DuPont most tied to construction. Water also flagged disruption in the Middle East. A construction downturn would hit the larger segment first.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, discontinued operations. Qnity (spun off November 2025) and aramids (sold April 2026) leave through that line, so FY2025 GAAP net income was −$779M while operating income was $865M. Second, restatement. StockAnalysis's annual revenue falls from $13.0B in FY2022 to $6.6B in FY2023. From FY2023 the figures cover only the businesses DuPont still owns (FY2024 $6.72B, FY2025 $6.85B), while FY2021–22 still include businesses since separated, so multi-year revenue trends before FY2023 are not like-for-like. Third, the June 2026 reverse split tripled all per-share figures. Pre-split EPS, dividends and prices have to be multiplied by 3 to compare. The quarterly dividend of $0.60 is the post-split equivalent of $0.20. In capital returns, DuPont completed a $250M accelerated share purchase and about $100M of open-market buybacks in Q3 2026, and intends to buy back $250M more in Q4. Cash spent on share purchases, including forward contracts for them, totalled $611M in the first nine months (10-Q). Q3 also carried $7M of pre-tax legal costs for Corian Quartz personal-injury cases, nearly offset by a $6M gain from lowering the estimated extra payment owed to the sellers of Donatelle, a business DuPont bought (release footnote).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

For about 20x this year's guided earnings (18.7x the 2027 consensus), you get a simpler company that beat its own forecast again in Q3. Two segments, 5% organic growth in Q3, margins up in both, a gross margin that has risen from 33.4% to 35.5%, a 30% EBITDA-margin healthcare and water business, and a fresh guidance raise (adjusted EPS $7.47–$7.52). The balance sheet is moderate (D/E 0.23, current ratio 2.36). Q3 transaction-adjusted cash conversion was 121%, and $250M of buybacks a quarter continues into Q4. The North Carolina settlement puts a price on one piece of the PFAS overhang. Fourteen of 18 analysts rate it a Buy, and the average target is 11% above the price. If the GICS move to Industrials leads the market to value DuPont like a multi-industrial rather than a chemical company, investors may pay more for each dollar of its earnings.

Bear Case

Take away the adjustments and GAAP profit is still thin: $2.21 of TTM EPS against about $7.50 adjusted, a −$5.58 FY2025, and a dividend that takes about 109% of reported earnings. The PFAS liability is shared three ways under a cost-sharing arrangement the company itself lists as a risk, and it is still producing charges ($127M for North Carolina so far this year). North Carolina is one state, and more claims are possible. The growth engine (Qnity's semiconductors) is gone, and consensus sees revenue growth under 4% in 2027. Diversified Industrials, more than half of sales, rides the construction cycle. After a 16% rise in six weeks the monthly RSI is back above 70, the average target is only 11% away, and JPMorgan's $145 target sits below the price.

01
PFAS litigation and cost-sharing. The September 10, 2026 North Carolina settlement ($455M over 15 years; DuPont's pre-tax share ~$126M before Qnity's 44% reimbursement) shows the liability is real and still being paid out. Industry Commentary
PFAS: the liability that never spun off
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Each restructuring (Dow and Corteva in 2019, Qnity in 2025, aramids in 2026) moved businesses out of DuPont. The PFAS exposure stayed shared among DuPont, Corteva and Chemours. The North Carolina agreement of September 10, 2026 is the latest example. North Carolina officials called it the largest environmental damage payment in state history (WUNC). It covers the Fayetteville Works claims and the state's separate claims over firefighting foam. The settlement is payable over 15 years, which spreads the cash cost. It does not settle any other state's claims.

From chemicals to multi-industrial
RE-CLASSIFICATION

The July 31, 2026 GICS change to Industrials follows the portfolio. With electronics gone to Qnity and aramids sold to Arclin, DuPont is a maker of engineered components and materials for healthcare, water, construction, aerospace and autos. Classification affects which index funds and sector managers own the stock, and which peer multiples analysts use. Industrial conglomerates and specialty chemicals do not trade on the same multiples.

Healthcare and water as the quality core
EMERGING TAILWIND

Healthcare & Water Technologies is 46% of sales and earned a 30.4% operating-EBITDA margin in Q3 2026, up 80 bps. In August management expected "continued strength across healthcare, industrial water, and aerospace end-markets" in the second half, and in November the CFO again pointed to those three markets. The bull framing: as this segment's share grows, the group margin rises, and so may the price investors pay per dollar of earnings. The bear framing: water still reports disruption in the Middle East, and in Q3 the faster sales and margin gains came from the more cyclical Diversified Industrials. The next few quarters of segment growth will show which framing holds.
