01 Company Overview

Datadog sells observability — software that collects the logs, metrics and traces a company's computer systems emit and turns them into a single picture of what those systems are actually doing, so engineers can find out why something is slow or broken before customers do. The platform now spans infrastructure monitoring, application performance monitoring (APM), log management, digital-experience monitoring, cloud security and, most recently, monitoring for AI models and agents. Datadog reports as a single operating segment and does not break out revenue by product.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $3.427 billion, up 27.7% year over year; the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 30, 2026 is $3.967 billion, up 31.5%, and management guided fiscal 2026 to $4.45–4.47 billion (about +30%). Growth has re-accelerated: the second quarter of 2026 grew 36% to $1.12 billion, the fastest rate since 2022. Datadog served about 33,400 customers at June 30, 2026 and ranks roughly #301 globally by market capitalisation.

02 Core Businesses

The business model detail that drives everything: Datadog is consumption-based, not seat-based. A seat-based software company charges per named user, so revenue only moves when headcount moves. Datadog charges for volume — hosts monitored, gigabytes of logs ingested and indexed, spans traced, containers observed, custom metrics stored. That has two consequences. When a customer's own infrastructure grows — more servers, more traffic, more AI inference — Datadog's revenue from that customer rises automatically, with no new sales cycle. When the customer optimises — deletes idle instances, samples logs more aggressively, moves to cheaper storage tiers — revenue falls automatically, also with no sales conversation. Consumption pricing is the reason this stock trades on two operating metrics rather than on bookings.

Metric one: net revenue retention (NRR) — what last year's cohort of customers spends this year, expressed as a percentage, including expansion, contraction and churn but excluding brand-new customers. For a consumption business NRR is the cleanest read on whether existing usage is compounding or being optimised away, because expansion happens silently through the meter. Datadog's trailing-twelve-month NRR was about 120% in the second and third quarters of 2025, "in the low 120%" in the first quarter of 2026 and "in the low 120s" again in the second quarter of 2026 — a modest but real improvement off the level the company printed after the 2022–23 cloud-optimisation wave, when enterprises spent a year deliberately cutting cloud bills and Datadog's disclosed retention stepped down hard. A falling NRR is the single clearest early warning in this business.

Metric two: the large-customer counts. At June 30, 2026 Datadog had about 4,720 customers with annual recurring revenue (ARR) of $100,000 or more, up 23% from about 3,850 a year earlier, and 603 customers above $1 million of ARR (against 462 at September 30, 2025). Those $100K+ accounts generated about 91% of total ARR, up from 89% a year earlier. The mix bar below shows that disclosed concentration — it is the only revenue split Datadog actually publishes.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
CONSUMPTION METER LAND-AND-EXPAND UNIFIED TELEMETRY AGENT INSTALL BASE MULTI-PRODUCT ATTACH 33,400 CUSTOMERS AI-NATIVE DESIGN WINS

Datadog's moat is the combination of a meter that grows on its own and a switching cost that grows with every product added. Instrumenting an estate — deploying agents, wiring dashboards, writing alert rules, training on-call engineers, embedding trace IDs in application code — takes quarters, and it has to be redone from scratch to move vendors. The multi-product numbers show that cost compounding in real time: at June 30, 2026, 85% of customers used two or more products, 58% used four or more (from 52%), 37% used six or more (from 29%), 22% used eight or more (from 14%) and 13% used ten or more (from 7%). Every step up that ladder makes the platform harder to unpick and is the mechanic that justifies the multiple. The commercial proof is the large-account count — 4,720 customers above $100K of ARR, 603 above $1M — and a net revenue retention rate in the low 120s%, meaning the average surviving customer cohort spends roughly a fifth more each year without a new sale.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. AI-native customer concentration — handle this carefully. Datadog's fastest-growing cohort is companies whose own product is AI. On the Q2 2026 call management said 31 AI customers now spend more than $1 million a year and 8 spend more than $10 million, up from 22 and 5 respectively one quarter earlier — extraordinary sequential growth. The same call disclosed the other side: "We have seen a usage reduction, which is incorporated in our Q3 and full year 2026 guidance… we chose to fully de-risk forward expectations for this specific account." A single account moved the guide. Note honestly what is not available: Datadog did not put a percentage of ARR on the AI-native cohort in the Q2 2026 release, supplemental or call, so the precise concentration figure this brief asked for is not disclosed and is deliberately not estimated here. The nearest disclosed anchor is from the Q1 2026 call — over 6,500 customers send data from one or more AI integrations, about 20% of the customer base, and management said that group represents about 80% of ARR. That is a very broad definition (any customer touching an AI integration, not AI-native start-ups) and should not be read as the AI-native revenue share. What is certain is that the cohort is large enough that one customer's optimisation forced a guidance reset.

2. The consumption meter runs in reverse. Because nothing is contracted per seat, a customer can cut Datadog spend materially without cancelling anything — sample logs, shorten retention, shut idle hosts. That is exactly what the 2022–23 cloud-optimisation period produced across the industry, and it is visible in this stock's own history: DDOG fell from a $178.11 close in December 2021 to $67.38 in April 2023, a 62% decline, as growth decelerated from 63% in FY2022 to 27% in FY2023. The mechanism has not changed.

3. Hyperscaler native monitoring and Splunk under Cisco. AWS CloudWatch, Azure Monitor and Google Cloud Operations ship with the cloud, are billed on the same invoice, and are good enough for a meaningful slice of workloads at materially lower cost. They do not do cross-cloud correlation well, which is Datadog's answer, but they cap pricing power at the low end and they improve every year. Splunk inside Cisco is a better-capitalised competitor in log management and security than Splunk standalone was, and Dynatrace, Elastic, New Relic and open-source Grafana/OpenTelemetry stacks all pull at different parts of the platform.

4. The gap between adjusted and GAAP profitability. In Q2 2026, non-GAAP operating income was $257.0 million (23% margin) while GAAP operating income was $5.5 million (0% margin). The bridge is overwhelmingly stock-based compensation (SBC) of $220.3 million, or about 19.7% of revenue (Q1 2026: $196.8 million, 19.5%). At 483.8× trailing GAAP earnings, a fifth of revenue paid in shares is not a rounding item — it is most of the reported profit.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Growth is re-accelerating, not decaying: 36% in Q2 2026 against 27.7% for FY2025, with the full-year guide raised twice this year to $4.45–4.47 billion. The land-and-expand machine is visibly deepening — 58% of customers on four or more products (from 52%), 22% on eight or more (from 14%), 13% on ten or more (from 7%) — and net revenue retention in the low 120s% means the installed base alone adds roughly a fifth to revenue each year before a single new logo. Free cash flow of $1.18 billion on a 29.8% margin and $3.7 billion of net cash mean the company funds its own optionality. If AI workloads are a decade-long build-out rather than a two-year one, Datadog meters the whole thing.

Bear Case

The August 6 session is the whole argument: a 36% growth quarter produced a 19% share-price fall because one customer's usage reduction had to be de-risked out of guidance. Consumption revenue that arrives without a sales cycle can leave without a cancellation, and the AI-native cohort — 31 customers above $1 million, 8 above $10 million, growing sequentially at extraordinary rates — is funded by a capital cycle Datadog does not control. Meanwhile the price is 483.8× trailing GAAP earnings and 93.7× forward, GAAP operating margin is 0.48%, and stock compensation at 19.7% of revenue is larger than every dollar of GAAP operating profit the company has ever earned in a year. Goldman Sachs' $158 target is 33% below the current price and is not an outlier view within the panel.

01
AI-native concentration, with the size undisclosed. Management has confirmed the cohort is large enough that a single account's optimisation forced a guidance reset, but has not published an AI-native percentage of ARR. Investors are being asked to underwrite a concentration they cannot measure — and the cohort's own funding environment, not Datadog's execution, is the variable that matters.
02
The consumption meter is symmetric. The 2022–23 optimisation wave took the stock down 62% and cut revenue growth from 63% to 27% without a single major logo loss. Nothing structural prevents a repeat; a broad enterprise cost-cutting cycle would show up in NRR within two quarters and in the multiple within two days.
03
Valuation leaves no room for a GAAP disappointment. At 483.8× trailing earnings and a PEG of 3.34, the stock is priced for sustained 30%-plus growth and for the market to keep excluding $220 million a quarter of stock compensation. Either assumption breaking is enough; both breaking together is the $158 scenario.
  Industry Commentary
AI infrastructure spend has become the swing factor in observability
EMERGING TAILWIND
Observability revenue is a derivative of how much compute is running, and AI has changed the level and the shape of that compute. Datadog's own disclosures track it directly: LLM Observability spans "nearly tripled quarter-over-quarter" and MCP Server tool calls "quadrupled quarter-over-quarter" (Q1 2026 call); by Q2 2026 over 750 AI customers were on the platform, all ten of the top ten AI leaders among them, with 31 spending above $1 million a year and 8 above $10 million — versus 22 and 5 respectively one quarter earlier. That is the mechanism behind Q2 revenue growth of 36%, up from 27.7% for FY2025. The market's May 2026 re-rating (+87.1% in a month) was the recognition of this shift, not a valuation drift.
Consumption models re-rate violently in both directions
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Because there is no seat count anchoring revenue, a consumption business gets no warning from renewal calendars. The 2022–23 precedent is unambiguous — growth from 62.8% (FY2022) to 27.1% (FY2023) and a 62% peak-to-trough share-price decline — and August 2026 showed the same physics at single-customer scale: $283.17 on August 5, $229.29 on August 6, on a quarter that grew 36% and a guide that went up. Management's response was to "fully de-risk" the account, which is the right disclosure practice and also an admission that the meter can move faster than the sales organisation can see.
Hyperscaler bundling and Splunk-under-Cisco cap the pricing ceiling
ONGOING HEADWIND
AWS CloudWatch, Azure Monitor and Google Cloud Operations are bundled, invoiced with the cloud spend, and genuinely adequate for single-cloud workloads that do not need cross-service correlation. They are the reason Datadog's argument has to be "one platform across everything" rather than "better monitoring". Splunk inside Cisco is a materially better-funded competitor in logs and security than Splunk standalone, and open-source OpenTelemetry plus Grafana gives sophisticated engineering teams a credible do-it-yourself route. Datadog's 79.5% gross margin has held through all of this, which is evidence the pressure has been on growth of the low end rather than on realised price — but it is a ceiling, not a non-issue.
What each side is actually betting on
THE DECISION
The bull is betting that AI-native spend is the leading edge of a durable enterprise build-out — that today's 31 seven-figure AI accounts become hundreds of ordinary enterprise accounts as AI moves into normal software, that NRR in the low 120s% holds or improves, and that the multi-product ladder (13% of customers now on ten or more products, from 7%) converts the platform into an unremovable system of record. The bear is betting that a meaningful slice of the recent acceleration is a funding-cycle artefact — revenue from companies spending venture and hyperscaler capital rather than operating cash flow — and that when that cycle turns, the meter falls as fast as it rose, into a 483.8× trailing multiple with 19.7% of revenue going out as stock. Both sides agree on the operating facts; the disagreement is entirely about the durability of consumption. The $158-to-$330 target range is that disagreement priced.
