01 Company Overview

Datadog sells observability — software that collects the logs, metrics and traces a company's computer systems emit and turns them into a single picture of what those systems are actually doing, so engineers can find out why something is slow or broken before customers do. The platform now spans infrastructure monitoring, application performance monitoring (APM), log management, digital-experience monitoring, cloud security and, most recently, monitoring for AI models and agents. Datadog reports as a single operating segment and does not break out revenue by product.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $3.427 billion, up 27.7% year over year; the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 30, 2026 is $4.278 billion, up 33.2%, and management guided fiscal 2026 to $4.515–4.525 billion (about +32%). The third quarter of 2026 grew 35% to $1.197 billion, a touch below the second quarter's 36%. Datadog served about 33,500 customers at September 30, 2026 and sits in both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq-100.

02 Core Businesses

The business model detail that drives everything: Datadog is consumption-based, not seat-based. A seat-based software company charges per named user, so revenue only moves when headcount moves. Datadog charges for volume — hosts monitored, gigabytes of logs ingested and indexed, spans traced, containers observed, custom metrics stored. That has two consequences. When a customer's own infrastructure grows — more servers, more traffic, more AI inference — Datadog's revenue from that customer rises automatically, with no new sales cycle. When the customer optimises — deletes idle instances, samples logs more aggressively, moves to cheaper storage tiers — revenue falls automatically, also with no sales conversation. Consumption pricing is the reason this stock trades on two operating metrics rather than on bookings.

Metric one: net revenue retention (NRR) — what last year's cohort of customers spends this year, expressed as a percentage, including expansion, contraction and churn but excluding brand-new customers. For a consumption business NRR is the cleanest read on whether existing usage is growing or being optimised away, because expansion happens silently through the meter. Datadog's trailing-twelve-month NRR was about 120% in the second and third quarters of 2025, "in the low 120%" in the first quarter of 2026, in the low 120s in the second and "in the low 120s percent" again in the third quarter of 2026, with gross revenue retention (what customers keep before any expansion) "stable in the mid- to high 90s". That is a modest but real improvement off the level the company reported after the 2022–23 cloud-optimisation wave, when enterprises spent a year deliberately cutting cloud bills and Datadog's disclosed retention stepped down hard. A falling NRR is the single clearest early warning in this business.

Metric two: the large-customer counts. At September 30, 2026 Datadog had about 4,860 customers with annual recurring revenue (ARR) of $100,000 or more, up 20% from about 4,060 a year earlier. Those $100K+ accounts generated about 91% of total ARR, up from 89% a year earlier (10-Q). At December 31, 2025, 603 customers stood above $1 million of ARR, up from 462 a year earlier (10-K). 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
CONSUMPTION METER LAND-AND-EXPAND UNIFIED TELEMETRY AGENT INSTALL BASE MULTI-PRODUCT USE 33,500 CUSTOMERS AI-NATIVE DESIGN WINS

Datadog's moat is the combination of a meter that grows on its own and a switching cost that grows with every product added. Instrumenting an estate — deploying agents, wiring dashboards, writing alert rules, training on-call engineers, embedding trace IDs in application code — takes quarters, and it has to be redone from scratch to move vendors. The multi-product numbers show that cost building in real time: at September 30, 2026, 61% of customers used four or more products (from 54% a year earlier), 41% used six or more (from 31%) and 16% used ten or more (from 8%). Every step up that ladder makes the platform harder to unpick. The commercial proof is the large-account count — about 4,860 customers above $100K of ARR, 603 above $1M at the end of 2025 — and a net revenue retention rate in the low 120s%, meaning the average surviving customer cohort spends roughly a fifth more each year without a new sale. On the Q3 call management also said that, according to industry analysts, Datadog is the market-share leader in global observability with a share still in the teens.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. AI-native customer concentration. Datadog's fastest-growing cohort is companies whose own product is AI: over 850 AI customers at Q3 2026, with 39 spending more than $1 million a year, up from 31 one quarter earlier. The third quarter showed the other side. CFO David Obstler said on the November 4 call: "our largest customer reduced their usage in the third quarter, while their usage remained above their minimum commitment." The customer renewed, and Datadog built its fourth-quarter guide on that account paying only the contract minimum, which is why the guide calls for roughly flat revenue from the third quarter. Excluding that customer, management said the Q4 guide implies growth in the low to mid-30s percent, against 25–26% overall. Early renewals are a second near-term drag: management said several large customers, including other AI customers, renewed well ahead of schedule at larger commitments and better prices, and that this creates a short-term revenue headwind. Datadog last gave the AI customers' share of revenue for Q3 2025, when it was 12% (11% a quarter earlier, about 6% a year earlier), and has given no percentage on the four earnings calls since. The widest measure it still discloses is much broader: about one-third of all customers, including three-quarters of $100K+ customers, use at least one of its AI products. That should not be read as the AI-native revenue share. What is certain is that the cohort is large enough that one customer's usage set the shape of the guide for two quarters running.

2. The consumption meter runs in reverse. Because nothing is contracted per seat, a customer can cut Datadog spend materially without cancelling anything — sample logs, shorten retention, shut idle hosts. That is exactly what the 2022–23 cloud-optimisation period produced across the industry, and it is visible in this stock's own history: DDOG fell from a $178.11 close in December 2021 to $67.38 in April 2023, a 62% decline, as growth decelerated from 63% in FY2022 to 27% in FY2023. The mechanism has not changed.

3. The big cloud providers' own monitoring tools, and Splunk under Cisco. AWS CloudWatch, Azure Monitor and Google Cloud Operations ship with the cloud, are billed on the same invoice, and are good enough for a meaningful slice of workloads at materially lower cost. They do not do cross-cloud correlation well, which is Datadog's answer, but they cap pricing power at the low end and they improve every year. Splunk inside Cisco is a better-capitalised competitor in log management and security than Splunk standalone was, and Dynatrace, Elastic, New Relic and open-source Grafana/OpenTelemetry stacks all pull at different parts of the platform.

4. The gap between adjusted and GAAP profitability. In Q3 2026, non-GAAP operating income was $334.3 million (28% margin) while GAAP operating income was $74.7 million (6% margin). Most of the gap is stock-based compensation (SBC) of $237.2 million, or about 19.8% of revenue (Q2 2026: $220.3 million, 19.7%). At 248.7× trailing GAAP earnings, a fifth of revenue paid in shares is not a rounding item: in Q3 it was more than three times GAAP operating income.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Growth is holding: 35% in Q3 2026 against 27.7% for FY2025, with the full-year guide raised for the third time this year to $4.515–4.525 billion. Revenue from non-AI customers has accelerated for five straight quarters to the high-20s percent, so the growth is not only AI. Adoption across products is deepening — 61% of customers on four or more products (from 54%), 41% on six or more (from 31%), 16% on ten or more (from 8%) — and net revenue retention in the low 120s% means the installed base alone adds roughly a fifth to revenue each year before a single new customer. Contracted future revenue (remaining performance obligations) reached $3.85 billion, up 38%. Free cash flow of $1.11 billion on a 25.9% margin and about $3.9 billion of net cash mean the company funds its own growth. And the Q4 guide counts the largest customer only at its minimum: any spend above that is upside. If AI workloads are a decade-long build-out rather than a two-year one, Datadog meters the whole thing.

Bear Case

Two earnings reports running, a strong quarter has been overshadowed by one customer: a 19% fall on August 6 and 14.42% on November 4, as the largest account cut usage and Datadog's Q4 guide assumed only its minimum commitment. Consumption revenue that arrives without a sales cycle can leave without a cancellation, and the AI-native cohort — 39 customers above $1 million a year — is funded by a capital cycle Datadog does not control. Early renewals at better prices add a second, near-term drag on revenue from the same group. The Q4 guide calls for no sequential growth. Meanwhile the price is 248.7× trailing GAAP earnings and 65.6× forward, GAAP operating margin is 2.3% over twelve months, and a single quarter's stock compensation ($237 million) is more than twice the last twelve months' GAAP operating profit ($97 million). Goldman Sachs, the only Sell in StockAnalysis's count of 48, targets $171, 4.5% below the current price.

Nothing in the business model prevents a repeat: a broad round of corporate cost-cutting would show up in net revenue retention and in the share price.

  Industry Commentary
AI infrastructure spend has become the swing factor in observability
EMERGING TAILWIND
Observability revenue follows how much computing is running, and AI has changed the level and the shape of that computing. Datadog's own disclosures track it directly: on the Q3 2026 call management said spans sent to agent observability were up by a factor of 9 this year, GPU devices monitored by a factor of 8, and MCP tool calls by a factor of 50, with over 850 AI customers on the platform, all 15 of the top 15 AI leaders among them, and 39 spending above $1 million a year (31 a quarter earlier). The same call showed the wider business doing the heavy lifting too: revenue from non-AI customers grew in the high-20s percent, accelerating for a fifth straight quarter. The shares' 87.1% gain in May 2026 and their fall since September show how far the stock can swing on this story.
Consumption models swing hard in both directions
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Because there is no seat count anchoring revenue, a consumption business gets no warning from renewal calendars. The 2022–23 precedent is unambiguous — growth from 62.8% (FY2022) to 27.1% (FY2023) and a 62% fall in the share price between month-end closes — and 2026 has shown the same physics at single-customer scale, twice: $283.17 on August 5 to $229.29 on August 6, on a quarter that grew 36% and a guide that went up, then $220.52 on November 3 to $188.72 on November 4, on a quarter that grew 35% and a guide that went up again. Management's response has been to count the account conservatively, first by taking the usage reduction out of guidance and now by assuming only its minimum commitment, which is sensible disclosure and also an admission that the meter can move faster than the sales organisation can see.
Cloud providers' bundled tools and Splunk under Cisco limit what Datadog can charge
ONGOING HEADWIND
AWS CloudWatch, Azure Monitor and Google Cloud Operations are bundled, invoiced with the cloud spend, and genuinely adequate for single-cloud workloads that do not need cross-service correlation. They are the reason Datadog's argument has to be "one platform across everything" rather than "better monitoring". Splunk inside Cisco is a materially better-funded competitor in logs and security than Splunk standalone, and open-source OpenTelemetry plus Grafana gives sophisticated engineering teams a credible do-it-yourself route. Datadog's 79.6% gross margin has held through all of this, which suggests the pressure has fallen on growth among smaller accounts rather than on prices, though it still limits what Datadog can charge.
What each side is actually betting on
THE DECISION
The bull is betting that AI-native spend is the leading edge of a durable enterprise build-out — that today's 39 seven-figure AI accounts become hundreds of ordinary enterprise accounts as AI moves into normal software, that NRR in the low 120s% holds or improves, and that the multi-product ladder (16% of customers now on ten or more products, from 8%) makes the platform very hard to remove. The bear is betting that a meaningful slice of the recent acceleration comes from companies spending venture capital and money from the big cloud providers rather than cash from their own operations — and that when that cycle turns, the meter falls as fast as it rose, into a 248.7× trailing multiple with 19.8% of revenue going out as stock. Both sides agree on the operating facts; the disagreement is about how long consumption lasts. The $171-to-$330 target range is that disagreement priced.
