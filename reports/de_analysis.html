01 Company Overview

Deere & Company designs, manufactures and finances agricultural, turf, construction and forestry equipment under the John Deere brand, and is the world's largest agricultural machinery maker by revenue. Fiscal 2025 (ended November 2, 2025) total net sales and revenues were $45.684 billion, down 12% YoY, with net income of $5.027 billion and diluted EPS of $18.50 — the second consecutive down year after the FY2023 peak of $61.2 billion in revenue and $34.63 in EPS. That two-year, roughly 25% revenue decline is not a franchise problem; it is the agricultural equipment cycle doing what it always does. Fiscal 2026 is where the cycle is supposed to turn, and the third quarter (ended August 2, 2026) was the first to say so in the numbers: net sales and revenues of $12.608 billion, up 5%, net income of $1.379 billion and diluted EPS of $5.10 (up from $4.75), with nine-month revenue up 7% to $35.589 billion. Management raised the floor of its fiscal 2026 net income guidance to $4.75–5.00 billion and repeated that 2026 will mark the bottom of the current ag equipment cycle. At $684.73 the company is worth about $184.6 billion (our calculation: 269.63 million shares at the September 21 close).

The company is unusual among industrials in that it runs a genuine captive bank. John Deere Financial holds a multi-billion-dollar receivable and lease portfolio that funds dealers and farmers, generating $890 million of FY2025 net income (up 28% in a year when every equipment segment fell). It also means the consolidated balance sheet carries $64.58 billion of total debt — a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.30 that looks alarming until you separate the finance book from the industrial one. See Section 04.

The heart of the franchise: large row-crop tractors (7R/8R/9R series), combine harvesters (S7/X9), planters, sprayers, cotton pickers and the precision-ag hardware and software layered on top of them. These are $500,000-to-$1,000,000+ capital goods sold to commercial row-crop farmers in the US Corn Belt, Brazil's Mato Grosso and the European plains — which makes this the most cyclical segment Deere owns and, at peak, the highest-margin. In FY2023 this segment carried operating margins in the low-to-mid 20s; FY2025 operating profit fell 41% to $2.671 billion on a 17% sales decline, and margin compressed to 15.4%. Q2 FY2026 (quarter ended May 3, 2026) was the low point so far: net sales $4.503B, down 14%, operating profit $706M, down 39%, margin 15.7% vs 22.0% a year earlier — the classic cyclical pattern where operating leverage runs violently in reverse. Q3 FY2026 was a smaller decline on an easier comparison: net sales $3.998B, down 6%, operating profit $527M, down 9%, margin 13.2% vs 13.6%. The outlook got slightly worse, not better: management now guides P&PA net sales down ~10% for FY2026 (previously down 5–10%), with about +2.5% currency and +1.0% price, against US & Canada large ag down 15–20% and South America tractors and combines now down 15–20% (previously ~15%). The gap between "industry down 15–20%" and "Deere down ~10%" is still the share and price story management points at. The forward signal is the early order programme: on the Q3 call Deanna Kovar said combined planter and sprayer orders were up mid-single digits on last year, and that late-model used inventory in North America continues to improve. Precision ag — See & Spray targeted herbicide application, ExactEmerge planting, machine autonomy, and the JDLink/Operations Center data layer — sits inside this segment and is the structural growth argument on top of the cycle: it converts a one-time iron sale into recurring, higher-margin technology revenue. Deere has publicly committed to a recurring-revenue ambition, and on the Q3 call said customers were running See & Spray on significantly more acres than a year earlier with herbicide savings above 50% — but no verified precision-ag revenue, subscription ARR or engaged-acre figure was obtainable from live sources for this build — treat any specific number you see elsewhere as unconfirmed.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
DEALER NETWORK DENSITY CAPTIVE FINANCE 189-YEAR BRAND PARTS & SERVICE ANNUITY PRECISION-AG DATA LOCK-IN SCALE VS FRAGMENTED RIVALS USED-EQUIPMENT RESIDUALS

Deere's moat is not the tractor — it is everything around the tractor. A row-crop farmer buying a $700,000 combine is buying an eight-to-twelve-hour parts guarantee during a four-week harvest window, and the density of the John Deere dealer network is the thing no competitor can replicate on any reasonable timeframe. That network is subsidized and stabilized by John Deere Financial, which floorplans dealer inventory and retail-finances the end customer, meaning Deere controls both ends of the transaction and can flex credit terms to defend share when the cycle turns. The brand carries real pricing power: even in a fiscal year when P&PA volumes collapsed 17%, Deere still guided positive price realization (+1.0% in P&PA, +1.5% in SA&T, +3.0% in C&F for FY2026, as updated in August). Layered on top is the newest and most contested moat source — the precision-ag data layer. Every connected machine feeds the John Deere Operations Center; agronomic history, prescription maps and machine telematics accumulate per-farm and raise switching costs each season. This is the asset that turns a cyclical iron business into something with recurring revenue characteristics, and it is exactly the asset regulators have now started to pry open. Finally, the aftermarket: parts and service revenue is far less cyclical than whole-goods and provides a margin floor through the trough — one reason Deere's gross margin only fell from 30.63% at the FY2023 peak to 25.18% TTM despite a ~22% revenue decline from the FY2023 peak ($61.2B) to TTM ($47.93B).

Key Vulnerabilities
Right-to-Repair and the FTC Settlement. In roughly July 2026 Deere reached a settlement giving farmers access to diagnostic and repair tools — the culmination of years of litigation and state-level right-to-repair pressure. This is a direct assault on the highest-margin part of the annuity: authorized-dealer service. The near-term financial impact is unlikely to be large, but structurally it erodes the argument that Deere's software layer is an unbreachable lock. The settlement's specific financial terms were not obtainable from live sources for this build.
Pure Cyclicality and Mid-Cycle Ambiguity. Revenue went $61.2B (FY23) → $51.5B (FY24) → $45.7B (FY25), and has turned up in FY2026 (nine months +7%). EPS went $34.63 → $25.62 → $18.50 → a consensus $18.09 for FY2026 — the recovery is in the top line of two segments, not yet in earnings per share. The entire bull case rests on management's repeated assertion that 2026 will mark the bottom of the current ag equipment cycle. If mid-cycle EPS is $22 rather than $28, today's $684.73 is not cheap on any framing.
Trade Policy and Retaliation Risk on Ag Exports. Deere sits downstream of US farm income, and US farm income sits downstream of export demand — principally soybeans and corn to China. Retaliatory tariffs on US agricultural exports depress crop prices, depress farm income, and depress equipment demand with a one-to-two-season lag. Separately, input tariffs hit Deere's own cost base: the company recorded tariff recoveries of $272 million in Q2 and $110 million in Q3 FY2026 ($382 million over nine months) for refunds of IEEPA-imposed duties after a February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidated them, and on the Q3 call still put direct tariff expense at roughly $1.1 billion for the year excluding those refunds.
Dealer Inventory and Used-Equipment Overhang. The mechanism that ends an ag downturn is dealers working down new and used inventory to normal turn rates. Elevated used large-tractor and combine inventory suppresses new-equipment pricing and residual values, which in turn hits John Deere Financial's lease book. On the Q3 FY2026 call management said North American new inventories remain tight and late-model used inventory continues to improve — the right direction, but a description rather than a ratio. Specific dealer inventory-to-sales ratios were not disclosed in any source obtainable for this build.
Labour. On August 23, 2026 UAW members at Deere rejected the company's offer to extend their collective agreement through 2029; the union cited job security and says 1,600 workers remain laid off. The current contract runs to 2027, so there is no near-term strike deadline, but the 2027 negotiation now starts from a rejected offer — and it lands in the year the bull case needs production to ramp.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items materially distort the reported numbers, plus one framing issue.

1. Tariff refund recoveries ($272 million in Q2, $110 million in Q3 FY2026). Deere recorded recoveries for refunds of duties imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, following a February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating those tariffs — $382 million over the first nine months. The Q2 credit sat inside that quarter's $1.773 billion of net income and made the comparison against Q2 FY2025's $1.804 billion flatter than it looks; the Q3 credit is a smaller share of Q3's $1.379 billion. Neither repeats, and any clean read of fiscal 2026 profitability must strip them out.

2. Employee-separation programs. Deere ran restructuring/employee-separation programs carrying $157 million of charges referenced in the fiscal 2024 period, part of a multi-year cost-structure reset as the company sized down for the downturn. Comparable FY2025 restructuring charges were not separately broken out in the release obtainable for this build.

3. Brazil financial services deconsolidation. In February 2025, Banco Bradesco S.A. acquired 50% of Banco John Deere S.A. The investment moved to equity-method accounting, reducing Deere's incremental Brazilian credit risk but breaking comparability in the Financial Services segment's Brazil contribution across FY2024/FY2025/FY2026. Currency translation in Brazil and Argentina also runs through segment results — management explicitly credits +2.5% currency in the FY2026 P&PA guide (August update), which is a meaningful share of a "down only 5–10%" outlook.

4. The mid-cycle framing (not a one-off, but essential). For a cyclical, headline P/E on trough EPS is close to useless. The right question is what Deere earns across a full cycle. Reported diluted EPS over the last five fiscal years: $18.99 (FY21), $23.28 (FY22), $34.63 (FY23), $25.62 (FY24), $18.50 (FY25), with $17.99 TTM and consensus of $18.09 (FY26E) and $22.71 (FY27E). A simple five-year average of FY21–FY25 is $24.20, which puts the stock at roughly 28× a crude mid-cycle EPS rather than 38× trailing — up from 24× at the previous edition, entirely because the price moved. That is the number the bulls are underwriting, and reasonable people disagree about whether the correct mid-cycle figure is $22 or $28 — a range that spans a 27% swing in fair value at any given multiple.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The ag cycle bottoms in FY2026 exactly as management guided, and Deere exits it structurally stronger. Two of three equipment segments have inflected for two quarters running — Small Ag & Turf grew 12% with an 18.4% operating margin in Q3 FY2026 and Construction & Forestry grew 18% with operating profit up 84% — and management has now raised FY2026 net income guidance twice, from $4.0–4.75B to $4.5–5.0B to $4.75–5.00B. The leading indicators turned: early orders for planters and sprayers are up mid-single digits and late-model used inventory is improving. Deere is guiding its own large-ag segment down ~10% against an industry down 15–20%, which is share gain plus positive price realization in the worst part of the cycle. Consensus has FY2027 EPS at $22.71 (+25.55%), and on a crude five-year mid-cycle EPS of $24.20 the stock trades at ~28×, not 38×. Layer on precision ag converting iron sales into recurring revenue, rising net cash farm income, a short interest under 2% of float, and two post-print upgrades to Buy.

Bear Case

You are paying 38.06× trailing earnings and 30.15× the FY2027 consensus for a business whose TTM revenue is still 22% below its FY2023 peak and whose flagship segment's margin fell again to 13.2% in Q3. The P&PA guide actually got worse at the print (down ~10%, from down 5–10%) and South America large ag was cut to down 15–20%. "2026 is the bottom" is an assertion, not a fact, and cyclical bottoms are routinely called a year early. ROIC of 5.69% is below the peer median of 7.50%, TTM free cash flow fell to $3.23B, and $382M of non-repeating tariff refunds sit inside this year's earnings. The UAW has rejected a contract extension ahead of 2027 talks, the FTC right-to-repair settlement chips at the service annuity, and the price has already reached the $688 average target; Bernstein cut to $580 on the last day of this edition's window and one analyst now rates it Sell.

Top 3 Risks
01
The bottom slips to FY2027 or FY2028. Every element of the bull case is timing-dependent. If crop prices stay depressed, dealer inventories clear slower than modelled, or a retaliatory trade action hits soybean and corn exports, FY2027 EPS lands nearer $18 than $22.71 and the stock re-rates toward the $500 low target — roughly 27% below spot. The 63% gap between the Street's low and high targets is an honest reflection of how wide this distribution is — and after a 16% run in 34 days, more of that distribution now sits below the price than above it.
02
Earnings quality in the current guidance. The FY2026 guidance of $4.75–5.00B is supported by $382M of one-time tariff refunds, ~+2.5% currency in P&PA and ~+1.5% in C&F, and price realization in a volume-declining market. Strip those out and the underlying volume recovery is far thinner than the headline suggests. Currency in particular reverses, and Brazil/Argentina exposure makes it volatile in both directions.
03
Financial Services credit quality in a prolonged trough. John Deere Financial contributed $890M of FY2025 net income (~$870M guided for FY2026) against $64.58B of consolidated debt. A longer downturn pressures farmer credit, raises delinquencies and writedowns, and depresses used-equipment residual values that underpin the lease book — a second-order hit that historically shows up late in an ag downturn, well after equipment volumes have already troughed.
  Industry Commentary
The Agricultural Equipment Down-Cycle — and Where It Bottoms
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

This is the whole story, and everything else is a footnote to it. Industry-wide, Deere now assumes (August 2026 outlook) US & Canada large ag down 15–20% and South America tractors and combines down 15–20% in FY2026, with Europe flat and Asia flat. Deere's own numbers trace the arc: revenue $61.2B (FY23) → $51.5B (FY24) → $45.7B (FY25), then up 7% over the first nine months of FY26; EPS $34.63 → $25.62 → $18.50 → $18.09E. That is a ~48% peak-to-trough earnings decline over three years, with revenue turning before earnings. The peers confirm it is the industry, not the company: CNH revenue fell 8.78% in FY2025 with net income down 62.2% to $311M and an Altman Z-Score of 1.76; CNH management explicitly described a market "at the trough of the agriculture cycle." AGCO's TTM revenue grew just 1.7%. Deere's counter-evidence is real — Small Ag & Turf +16% and +12%, Construction & Forestry +29% and +18% in Q2 and Q3 FY2026, with the company guiding P&PA down ~10% against an industry down 15–20% — but the segment that matters most is still contracting, and it is the one carrying the highest incremental margins. (Peer figures in this paragraph are as of the previous edition.)

Farm Income and Trade Policy — the Demand Function
ONGOING HEADWIND

Combine orders are a derivative of farm cash flow, which is a derivative of crop prices, which are a derivative of global supply and trade policy. USDA ERS forecasts 2026 net farm income at $153.4 billion, down $1.2 billion (-0.7%) nominal and -2.6% inflation-adjusted versus 2025. But the more relevant measure for capital equipment is net cash farm income, forecast at $158.5 billion, up $4.6 billion (+3.0%), with average net cash income per farm business projected up 18.7% to $135,000. Both measures remain above the 2005–24 inflation-adjusted average. That divergence — accrual income drifting down while cash income and per-farm cash income rise — is the strongest single data point in the bull case, because farmers write cheques out of cash, not accruals. The offsetting risk is trade policy in both directions: Deere booked $382 million of refund recoveries in Q2 and Q3 FY2026 after the February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidated IEEPA duties (a cost-side relief that will not repeat), while retaliatory tariffs on US soybean and corn exports remain the live threat to the crop-price base that funds the whole cycle.

Precision Agriculture and Autonomy — the Margin Story on Top of the Cycle
EMERGING TAILWIND

The structural argument for owning a cyclical at 38× trough earnings is that the next cycle is more profitable than the last. Deere's version of that argument is precision ag: See & Spray computer-vision herbicide targeting that cuts chemical use per acre, ExactEmerge high-speed planting, machine autonomy that addresses a genuine and worsening farm labour shortage, and the John Deere Operations Center as the data spine tying it together. The commercial ambition is to convert one-time iron sales into recurring subscription revenue at software-like incremental margins — which, if achieved at scale, both raises mid-cycle margins and dampens cyclical amplitude. The evidence so far is indirect but not nothing: Deere held positive price realization (+1.0% P&PA, +1.5% SA&T, +3.0% C&F guided for FY2026) through the worst of the downturn, gross margin fell only from 30.63% to 25.18% TTM on a ~22% revenue decline, and on the Q3 call management reported See & Spray running on significantly more acres with herbicide savings above 50%. The bull-versus-bear frame: bulls argue precision ag structurally lifts mid-cycle EPS above the $24.20 five-year average and justifies a permanently higher multiple, with the $730–$813 post-print bull cluster implying roughly 32–36× the $22.71 FY2027 consensus. Bears argue the technology is a defensive necessity that competitors will match, that the roughly-July-2026 FTC right-to-repair settlement has already begun prying open the closed ecosystem the whole thesis depends on, and that Deere has not disclosed — and this analysis could not verify from any live source — a single hard figure for precision-ag revenue, subscription ARR, engaged acres or attach rate. Until those numbers exist publicly, this remains a narrative, and narratives do not survive a guidance miss.
