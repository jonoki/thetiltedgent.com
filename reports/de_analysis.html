01 Company Overview

Deere & Company designs, manufactures and finances agricultural, turf, construction and forestry equipment under the John Deere brand, and is the world's largest agricultural machinery maker by revenue. Fiscal 2025 (ended November 2, 2025) total net sales and revenues were $45.684 billion, down 12% YoY, with net income of $5.027 billion and diluted EPS of $18.50 — the second consecutive down year after the FY2023 peak of $61.2 billion in revenue and $34.63 in EPS. That two-year, roughly 25% revenue decline is not a franchise problem; it is the agricultural equipment cycle doing what it always does. Deere ranks #138 globally and #78 in the United States by market capitalization, and at $158.9 billion is worth more than CNH Industrial, AGCO and every other pure-play ag equipment maker combined several times over.

The company is unusual among industrials in that it runs a genuine captive bank. John Deere Financial holds a multi-billion-dollar receivable and lease portfolio that funds dealers and farmers, generating $890 million of FY2025 net income (up 28% in a year when every equipment segment fell). It also means the consolidated balance sheet carries $64.53 billion of total debt against $27.46 billion of equity — a debt-to-equity ratio of 2.35 that looks alarming until you separate the finance book from the industrial one. See Section 04.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
DEALER NETWORK DENSITY CAPTIVE FINANCE 189-YEAR BRAND PARTS & SERVICE ANNUITY PRECISION-AG DATA LOCK-IN SCALE VS FRAGMENTED RIVALS USED-EQUIPMENT RESIDUALS

Deere's moat is not the tractor — it is everything around the tractor. A row-crop farmer buying a $700,000 combine is buying an eight-to-twelve-hour parts guarantee during a four-week harvest window, and the density of the John Deere dealer network is the thing no competitor can replicate on any reasonable timeframe. That network is subsidized and stabilized by John Deere Financial, which floorplans dealer inventory and retail-finances the end customer, meaning Deere controls both ends of the transaction and can flex credit terms to defend share when the cycle turns. The brand carries real pricing power: even in a fiscal year when P&PA volumes collapsed 17%, Deere still guided positive price realization (+1.0% in P&PA, +1.5% in SA&T, +2.5% in C&F for FY2026). Layered on top is the newest and most contested moat source — the precision-ag data layer. Every connected machine feeds the John Deere Operations Center; agronomic history, prescription maps and machine telematics accumulate per-farm and raise switching costs each season. This is the asset that turns a cyclical iron business into something with recurring revenue characteristics, and it is exactly the asset regulators have now started to pry open. Finally, the aftermarket: parts and service revenue is far less cyclical than whole-goods and provides a margin floor through the trough — one reason Deere's gross margin only fell from 30.63% at the FY2023 peak to 25.96% TTM despite a ~23% revenue decline over the same span.

Key Vulnerabilities
Right-to-Repair and the FTC Settlement. In roughly July 2026 Deere reached a settlement giving farmers access to diagnostic and repair tools — the culmination of years of litigation and state-level right-to-repair pressure. This is a direct assault on the highest-margin part of the annuity: authorized-dealer service. The near-term financial impact is unlikely to be large, but structurally it erodes the argument that Deere's software layer is an unbreachable lock. The settlement's specific financial terms were not obtainable from live sources for this build.
Pure Cyclicality and Mid-Cycle Ambiguity. Revenue went $61.2B (FY23) → $51.5B (FY24) → $45.7B (FY25), and consensus has FY2026 revenue at $41.42B, down another 9.23%. EPS went $34.63 → $25.62 → $18.50 → a consensus $18.04 for FY2026. The entire bull case rests on management's assertion that "2026 will mark the bottom of the large ag cycle." If mid-cycle EPS is $22 rather than $28, today's $588.72 is not cheap on any framing.
Trade Policy and Retaliation Risk on Ag Exports. Deere sits downstream of US farm income, and US farm income sits downstream of export demand — principally soybeans and corn to China. Retaliatory tariffs on US agricultural exports depress crop prices, depress farm income, and depress equipment demand with a one-to-two-season lag. Separately, input tariffs hit Deere's own cost base: the company recorded a $272 million recovery in Q2 FY2026 for refunds of IEEPA-imposed duties after a February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidated them — a reminder of how large the gross tariff exposure was in the first place.
Dealer Inventory and Used-Equipment Overhang. The mechanism that ends an ag downturn is dealers working down new and used inventory to normal turn rates. Elevated used large-tractor and combine inventory suppresses new-equipment pricing and residual values, which in turn hits John Deere Financial's lease book. Specific dealer inventory-to-sales ratios were not disclosed in the earnings releases obtainable for this build — this is a metric to check directly on the Q3 FY2026 call.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items materially distort the reported numbers, plus one framing issue.

1. $272 million tariff refund recovery (Q2 FY2026). Deere recorded a $272 million recovery for refunds of duties imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, following a February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidating those tariffs. This is a non-recurring credit inside the quarter's $1.773 billion of net income — roughly 15% of it on a pre-tax-equivalent basis. Any clean read of underlying Q2 FY2026 profitability must strip it out, and it makes the year-over-year comparison against Q2 FY2025's $1.804 billion flatter than it looks.

2. Employee-separation programs. Deere ran restructuring/employee-separation programs carrying $157 million of charges referenced in the fiscal 2024 period, part of a multi-year cost-structure reset as the company sized down for the downturn. Comparable FY2025 restructuring charges were not separately broken out in the release obtainable for this build.

3. Brazil financial services deconsolidation. In February 2025, Banco Bradesco S.A. acquired 50% of Banco John Deere S.A. The investment moved to equity-method accounting, reducing Deere's incremental Brazilian credit risk but breaking comparability in the Financial Services segment's Brazil contribution across FY2024/FY2025/FY2026. Currency translation in Brazil and Argentina also runs through segment results — management explicitly credits +3.0% currency in the FY2026 P&PA guide, which is a meaningful share of a "down only 5–10%" outlook.

4. The mid-cycle framing (not a one-off, but essential). For a cyclical, headline P/E on trough EPS is close to useless. The right question is what Deere earns across a full cycle. Reported diluted EPS over the last five fiscal years: $18.99 (FY21), $23.28 (FY22), $34.63 (FY23), $25.62 (FY24), $18.50 (FY25), with $17.65 TTM and consensus of $18.04 (FY26E) and $22.52 (FY27E). A simple five-year average of FY21–FY25 is $24.20, which puts the stock at roughly 24× a crude mid-cycle EPS rather than 34× trailing. That is the number the bulls are underwriting, and reasonable people disagree about whether the correct mid-cycle figure is $22 or $28 — a range that spans a 27% swing in fair value at any given multiple.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been up versus down. Above 70 suggests the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 suggests it has been beaten down and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. DE closed September 2021 at $314.69 and August 18, 2026 at $588.72 — a price return of +87.1%, or roughly +13.3% annualized before dividends. That is a strong absolute outcome, but the path was anything but linear, and it hides two complete cycles inside it. The stock made a post-pandemic run into the March 2022 ag boom ($392.37), gave it all back to $283.87 in June 2022 during the rate shock, then ripped to $419.39 by November 2022 on record FY2022 results. From there it went essentially nowhere for two years — $419 in November 2022 to $362 in July 2024 — as the market de-rated peak earnings ahead of the fundamentals rolling over. The re-rating came in two bursts: November 2024 ($455.34) and then the extraordinary January–February 2026 move from $463.01 to $626.25, a 35% gain in two months. The last six months have been a wide, violent chop between $540 and $634 with no net progress — a market that cannot decide whether the trough is in.

Where it sits now. At $588.72 the stock is 12.7% below its 52-week high of $674.19 and 36.0% above its 52-week low of $433.00. The August close sits inside the April ($588.34)-to-July ($592.67) congestion and below the June spike ($634.33). On the monthly series charted below, price ($588.72) has slipped below its 3-month moving average of $605.24 while holding comfortably above its 10-month moving average of $558.14 — short-term momentum has rolled over, but the longer-term uptrend established off the December 2025 base is intact. That is a consolidation, not a breakdown, heading into the Q3 FY2026 print. The 6-period monthly RSI has come off the deeply overbought readings registered around the February 2026 spike and now sits in neutral territory, which is neither a warning nor an invitation.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The ag cycle bottoms in FY2026 exactly as management guided, and Deere exits it structurally stronger. Two of three equipment segments have already inflected — Small Ag & Turf grew 16% with a 20.6% operating margin in Q2 FY2026 and Construction & Forestry grew 29% with margin up 190bps — while management raised FY2026 net income guidance from $4.0–4.75B to $4.5–5.0B. Deere is guiding its own large-ag segment down 5–10% against an industry down 15–20%, which is share gain plus positive price realization in the worst part of the cycle. Consensus has FY2027 EPS at $22.52 (+24.88%), and on a crude five-year mid-cycle EPS of $24.20 the stock trades at ~24×, not 34×. Layer on precision ag converting iron sales into recurring revenue, rising net cash farm income (+3.0% in 2026), a 2.33% short interest that signals no crowded bearish position, and $3.77B of free cash flow that has grown while earnings fell.

Bear Case

You are paying 33.99× trailing and 30.56× forward earnings for a business whose revenue has fallen from $61.2B to a consensus $41.4B and whose flagship segment just posted a 39% operating profit decline with margins down 630bps to 15.7%. "2026 is the bottom" is an assertion, not a fact, and cyclical bottoms are routinely called a year early — US & Canada large ag is still guided down 15–20% and South America down ~15%. ROIC of 5.93% is below the peer median of 7.50%. Roughly 15% of Q2's net income came from a non-repeating $272M tariff refund, and a meaningful slice of the P&PA guide is +3.0% currency, not volume. The FTC right-to-repair settlement chips at the service annuity, retaliatory tariffs on US ag exports threaten the farm-income base, and 11 of 24 analysts are on Hold with a target cluster only 4% above spot. JPMorgan's most recent target of $570 is below the current price.

Top 3 Risks
01
The bottom slips to FY2027 or FY2028. Every element of the bull case is timing-dependent. If crop prices stay depressed, dealer inventories clear slower than modelled, or a retaliatory trade action hits soybean and corn exports, FY2027 EPS lands nearer $18 than $22.52 and the stock re-rates toward the $500 low target — roughly 15% below spot. The 62% spread between the Street's low and high targets is an honest reflection of how wide this distribution is.
02
Earnings quality in the current guidance. The FY2026 guidance raise to $4.5–5.0B is supported by a $272M one-time tariff refund, ~+3.0% currency in P&PA and ~+2.0% in C&F, and price realization in a volume-declining market. Strip those out and the underlying volume recovery is far thinner than the headline suggests. Currency in particular reverses, and Brazil/Argentina exposure makes it volatile in both directions.
03
Financial Services credit quality in a prolonged trough. John Deere Financial contributed $890M of FY2025 net income against $64.53B of consolidated debt. A longer downturn pressures farmer credit, raises delinquencies and writedowns, and depresses used-equipment residual values that underpin the lease book — a second-order hit that historically shows up late in an ag downturn, well after equipment volumes have already troughed.
  Industry Commentary
The Agricultural Equipment Down-Cycle — and Where It Bottoms
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

This is the whole story, and everything else is a footnote to it. Industry-wide, Deere assumes US & Canada large ag down 15–20% and South America tractors and combines down ~15% in FY2026, with Europe flat to +5% and Asia flat. Deere's own numbers trace the arc precisely: revenue $61.2B (FY23) → $51.5B (FY24) → $45.7B (FY25) → consensus $41.4B (FY26E), and EPS $34.63 → $25.62 → $18.50 → $18.04E. That is a 49% peak-to-trough earnings decline over three years. The peers confirm it is the industry, not the company: CNH revenue fell 8.78% in FY2025 with net income down 62.2% to $311M and an Altman Z-Score of 1.76; CNH management explicitly described a market "at the trough of the agriculture cycle." AGCO's TTM revenue grew just 1.7%. Deere's counter-evidence is real — Small Ag & Turf +16% and Construction & Forestry +29% in Q2 FY2026, with the company guiding P&PA down only 5–10% against an industry down 15–20% — but the segment that matters most is still contracting, and it is the one carrying the highest incremental margins.

Farm Income and Trade Policy — the Demand Function
ONGOING HEADWIND

Combine orders are a derivative of farm cash flow, which is a derivative of crop prices, which are a derivative of global supply and trade policy. USDA ERS forecasts 2026 net farm income at $153.4 billion, down $1.2 billion (-0.7%) nominal and -2.6% inflation-adjusted versus 2025. But the more relevant measure for capital equipment is net cash farm income, forecast at $158.5 billion, up $4.6 billion (+3.0%), with average net cash income per farm business projected up 18.7% to $135,000. Both measures remain above the 2005–24 inflation-adjusted average. That divergence — accrual income drifting down while cash income and per-farm cash income rise — is the strongest single data point in the bull case, because farmers write cheques out of cash, not accruals. The offsetting risk is trade policy in both directions: Deere booked a $272 million refund recovery after the February 2026 Supreme Court decision invalidated IEEPA duties (a cost-side relief that will not repeat), while retaliatory tariffs on US soybean and corn exports remain the live threat to the crop-price base that funds the whole cycle.

Precision Agriculture and Autonomy — the Margin Story on Top of the Cycle
EMERGING TAILWIND

The structural argument for owning a cyclical at 34× trough earnings is that the next cycle is more profitable than the last. Deere's version of that argument is precision ag: See & Spray computer-vision herbicide targeting that cuts chemical use per acre, ExactEmerge high-speed planting, machine autonomy that addresses a genuine and worsening farm labour shortage, and the John Deere Operations Center as the data spine tying it together. The commercial ambition is to convert one-time iron sales into recurring subscription revenue at software-like incremental margins — which, if achieved at scale, both raises mid-cycle margins and dampens cyclical amplitude. The evidence so far is indirect but not nothing: Deere held positive price realization (+1.0% P&PA, +1.5% SA&T, +2.5% C&F guided for FY2026) through the worst of the downturn, and gross margin fell only from 30.63% to 25.96% on a ~23% revenue decline. The bull-versus-bear frame: bulls argue precision ag structurally lifts mid-cycle EPS above the $24.20 five-year average and justifies a permanently higher multiple, with the $680–$812 target band implying roughly 30–36× the $22.52 FY2027 consensus. Bears argue the technology is a defensive necessity that competitors will match, that the roughly-July-2026 FTC right-to-repair settlement has already begun prying open the closed ecosystem the whole thesis depends on, and that Deere has not disclosed — and this analysis could not verify from any live source — a single hard figure for precision-ag revenue, subscription ARR, engaged acres or attach rate. Until those numbers exist publicly, this remains a narrative, and narratives do not survive a guidance miss.
