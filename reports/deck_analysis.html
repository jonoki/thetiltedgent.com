01 Company Overview

Deckers Outdoor — trading as Deckers Brands — is a two-brand company wearing a portfolio's clothes. UGG, the casual-lifestyle footwear and apparel brand, and HOKA, the performance running and trail brand, together made up 97.3% of fiscal 2026 revenue; Teva and the rest are rounding. Fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) revenue was a record $5.472B, up 9.8%, with diluted EPS of a record $7.02, up 11%. The balance sheet carries no outstanding borrowings and $1.210B of cash at September 30, 2026.

The latest quarter sharpened the debate. In the three months to September 30, 2026 revenue rose only 3.5% to $1.481B, HOKA grew 6.8% and sales through the company's own stores and website fell 1.8%. Management cut the full-year sales outlook to $5.700–$5.725B (from $5.86–$5.91B) and lowered both brands' growth targets; CEO Stefano Caroti described “a challenging consumer environment” in the release. Earnings went the other way: diluted EPS of $2.31 included $0.28 from refunds of tariffs paid in earlier quarters, and the EPS guide rose to $7.80–$7.95 because of them. The stock fell 4.9% the next day.

Here is the puzzle. Split-adjusted EPS went from $2.71 in fiscal 2022 to $7.02 in fiscal 2026 — up 159% — yet the stock rose only 22.0% over five years, from a $65.89 close in October 2021 to the $80.37 settled close on October 26, 2026. The difference is the multiple. The market paid 31.8x earnings at the end of fiscal 2024, when revenue was compounding at 18%; it pays 10.6x today, with growth running at 3.5% in the latest quarter. The stock peaked at a $203.09 month-end close in December 2024 and now sits 60.4% below it — a de-rating of a business that kept printing records. The debate is whether HOKA is a maturing brand being priced correctly, or a still-growing one being priced like a fad, and the answer shows up in HOKA's growth rate and in the US market before it shows up anywhere else.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Two brands above $2.5B each HOKA performance credibility UGG category ownership 57%+ gross margin International runway No borrowings, $1.2B cash $1.1B annual free cash flow

Footwear has no switching costs and no network effects; the only moat is pricing power held in consumers' heads. Deckers' gross margin — 58.9% over the last twelve months, and 57.4% in the September quarter before the tariff refunds (our calculation from the release's 60.5% less the CFO's 310-basis-point refund effect), for a company that sells more than half its product through other people's stores — is the quantitative evidence that it has some. The second, quieter moat is financial: a net-cash balance sheet and a business that converts about 20% of revenue into free cash flow can keep investing in marketing through a soft patch that forces weaker brands to discount. The vulnerability is that brand heat is not contractual. Nothing obliges a runner to buy the next HOKA, and the market has seen enough footwear brands peak to price that risk aggressively.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
HOKA's growth is decelerating. +15.9% for fiscal 2026, +7.7% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 and +6.8% in the second. Management has now cut the full-year guide from low-double-digit to high-single-digit growth. A brand valued as a growth story that becomes a mid-single-digit grower loses the valuation argument even if every other number holds.
02
The US is flat. Domestic net sales grew 0.2% in fiscal 2026, 3.2% in Q1 FY27 and fell 0.4% in Q2. International cannot carry the group forever, and the US is where HOKA faces the most competition on the running-specialty and sporting-goods shelf.
03
A promotional market. The CFO said on the October 22 call that the US market became increasingly promotional as the quarter went on and that Deckers would not chase it; comparable direct-to-consumer sales fell 6.4%. BMO Capital initiated coverage on September 8, 2026 at Sell/Underperform with a $70 target, citing, as reported by SGB Media, a weakening athletic demand backdrop and an athletic category it expects to grow more promotional. Holding prices protects the margin and costs volume.
04
Earnings flattered by one-off refunds. The raised fiscal 2027 EPS guide of $7.80–$7.95 includes about $0.52 a share from IEEPA tariff refunds received through October 8, 2026 ($87.9M plus $4.7M of interest), and the guided 58.5% gross margin and 23% operating margin include about 150 basis points from them. Without the refunds the guide implies roughly $7.28–$7.43 (our calculation), below the July guide of $7.35–$7.50, and refunds do not recur.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things shape the per-share numbers. First, tariff refunds: the September quarter included $46.0M of IEEPA tariff refunds plus $3.0M of interest, worth about 310 basis points of margin and $0.28 a share after tax; the company had received $87.9M plus $4.7M of interest by October 8, 2026, and the full-year guide counts about $0.52 a share from them. They are one-off. Second, buybacks: 10.5 million shares repurchased for $1.075B in fiscal 2026 (average $102.43), and 6.5 million for $637.5M in the first half of fiscal 2027 (average $98.77; $93.65 in the September quarter) — all above today's $80.37. Second-quarter diluted share count was 135.3 million against 147.7 million a year earlier, so EPS grows faster than net income: Q2 FY27 net income rose 16.8% while diluted EPS rose 27%. Third, portfolio pruning: the Other-brands decline reflects the Koolaburra phase-out and Sanuk sale, which depress reported wholesale and domestic growth. The 6:1 stock split of September 17, 2024 affects nothing economically; every per-share figure and chart point in this report is on the post-split basis.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $65.89 to the October 26, 2026 settled close of $80.37 (both split-adjusted), DECK gained 22.0% on price; it pays no dividend, so that is also the total return. Over the same dates the S&P 500 ETF SPY rose 66.7% and the Nasdaq-100 ETF QQQ 84.5% on price (Yahoo Finance closes, our calculation). The endpoints hide a round trip. The stock more than tripled to a $203.09 month-end high in December 2024 — 3.1x the starting level — and then gave back 60.4% of that peak. September 2026's $78.14 month-end was the lowest close on the chart since March 2023 ($74.93). Along the way earnings per share rose 159%. Nothing about this chart is a business collapsing; all of it is a multiple being repriced from "hyper-growth" to "ordinary".

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 32.0, just back above the oversold line after September's 28.9. Lower readings in the series came in the 2022 bear market (April–June 2022, as low as 6.7, when the series opens on six months of mostly falling prices), the post-peak slide of 2025 (March–June 2025, 27.1 to 29.9, and October 2025, 25.4) and September 2026. The 10-month moving average sits at about $99.45, 23.7% above the price, and the 3-month average at about $81.86: the trend is down on the longer horizons the chart measures. Monthly RSI below the oversold line has twice in this series preceded sharp rebounds (July 2022, +22.7%; November 2025 to January 2026, +46% from the October low) — and has also sat below 30 for four months running in 2025 without one. October 2026 so far is up 2.9%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 10.6x trailing and 10.4x forward earnings for a company with two brands above $2.5B each, a gross margin of about 57% even before tariff refunds, no borrowings, $1.2B of cash and a 10.2% free-cash-flow yield — and which cut its share count by about 7% in a year with $4.4B of authorisation still unused. It refused to chase a promotional US market in the September quarter and still lifted its underlying gross margin by about 120 basis points; international grew 9.0%; inventory is rising no faster than sales. At the end of fiscal 2022 the stock traded at 16.5x EPS of $2.71; today trailing EPS is $7.57 and the multiple is 10.6x. Even the cut guide is growth, not decline, and the market is pricing a business that will not grow at all.

Bear Case

The bear case does not dispute the balance sheet; it disputes the brand's trajectory. HOKA went from +15.9% for the year to +7.7% and then +6.8%, the growth guide for both brands was cut, the US shrank 0.4%, comparable direct sales fell 6.4%, and the EPS guide only rose because of about $0.52 of one-off tariff refunds — without them it would sit slightly below July's. Every target change after the print was a cut, the consensus slipped to Hold, and Goldman and BMO sit below the current price. Footwear history is full of brands that looked cheap on trailing earnings at exactly this moment. Buybacks executed at $94–$104 a share have already lost money on paper. Cheap-on-earnings is what a fading brand looks like on the way down.

Top 3 Risks

01
HOKA deceleration becomes permanent. The stock's valuation tracks HOKA's growth rate. Two quarters at +7.7% and +6.8% have already pulled the full-year guide down to high-single-digit growth; another step down would test the fiscal 2028–2030 framework's low-double-digit HOKA target.
02
A promotional US market and a shrinking direct channel. Domestic sales fell 0.4% and comparable direct-to-consumer sales 6.4% in Q2 FY27, while management chose not to match discounting. If discounting spreads to wholesale partners, who took 74% of the quarter's sales (our calculation: $1.093B of $1.481B), full-price positioning — the source of the margin — comes under pressure.
03
Earnings that lean on one-offs. About $0.52 of the $7.80–$7.95 EPS guide and roughly 150 basis points of the guided margins come from tariff refunds that will not recur, so next year's comparison starts from a flattered base. Any new tariffs are a cost the refunds do not offset.
Industry Commentary
Growth deceleration and the multiple
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Deckers' annual revenue growth went 23.75% (FY22), 15.14% (FY23), 18.21% (FY24), 16.28% (FY25), 9.76% (FY26), the TTM figure is 6.3%, and the cut fiscal 2027 guide implies 4.2% to 4.6%. The year-end P/E went 16.5x, 22.9x, 31.8x, 17.6x, 13.9x, and today 10.6x. The market has been paying for the growth rate, not the earnings level — and it has marked the multiple down one step behind each slowdown. That is the mechanism that turned a 159% rise in EPS into a 22% rise in the share price over the chart's five years. It also defines what would change the picture: not another record year, but evidence that growth has stopped falling. The October 22 print was the opposite.

Athletic footwear demand and a promotional shelf
ONGOING HEADWIND

For scale: Nike's market value is $56.31B and On Holding's $11.09B — now slightly larger than Deckers' $10.94B (October 26 closes; see Section 02). BMO's September initiation framed the risk as weakening athletic demand and an athletic category growing more promotional (as reported by SGB Media), and Deckers' own September quarter bore it out: management described a US market that grew more promotional as the quarter went on and shoppers who wait for sales events, and its comparable direct sales fell 6.4% while wholesale grew 5.5%. In the June quarter it had been the other way round. On costs the picture flipped too: the IEEPA tariffs the July guide treated as sunk are now coming back as refunds ($87.9M plus interest by October 8, 2026).

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $80.37 the stock trades at 10.6x trailing earnings, roughly 7.4x EV/EBITDA and a 10.2% free-cash-flow yield, against an analyst average target of $96.74. The bull framing: a business with no debt, record revenue and a management team that cut the share count by about 7% in a year is being priced as if HOKA has already peaked; any stabilisation in HOKA growth plus the buyback compounds per-share value quickly at this price. The bear framing: the market is not mispricing a steady compounder, it is pricing the fade curve of a fashion-exposed brand, and the multiple has been right at every step of the deceleration so far. The September quarter was the first of the two the previous edition called the test, and on growth it sided with the bears: HOKA slowed again and the guide came down. What the bulls still have is the price — the stock barely moved between editions even though the average target fell by a fifth.
