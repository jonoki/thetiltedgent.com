01 Company Overview

Deckers Outdoor — trading as Deckers Brands — is a two-brand company wearing a portfolio's clothes. UGG, the casual-lifestyle footwear and apparel brand, and HOKA, the performance running and trail brand, together made up 97.3% of fiscal 2026 revenue; Teva and the rest are rounding. Fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) revenue was a record $5.472B, up 9.8%, with diluted EPS of a record $7.02, up 11%. The balance sheet carries no outstanding borrowings and $1.603B of cash at June 30, 2026.

Here is the puzzle. Split-adjusted EPS went from $2.71 in fiscal 2022 to $7.02 in fiscal 2026 — up 159% — yet the stock rose only 33.6% over five years, from a $60.03 close in September 2021 to the $80.23 settled close on September 21, 2026. The difference is the multiple. The market paid 31.8x earnings at the end of fiscal 2024, when revenue was compounding at 18%; it pays 11.3x today, with growth running at 5.7% in the latest quarter. The stock peaked at a $203.09 month-end close in December 2024 and now sits 60.5% below it — a de-rating of a business that kept printing records. The debate is whether HOKA is a maturing brand being priced correctly, or a still-growing one being priced like a fad, and the answer shows up in HOKA's growth rate and in the US wholesale channel before it shows up anywhere else.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Two brands above $2.5B each HOKA performance credibility UGG category ownership 57.8% gross margin International runway No borrowings, $1.6B cash $1.1B annual free cash flow

Footwear has no switching costs and no network effects; the only moat is pricing power held in consumers' heads. Deckers' 57.8% trailing gross margin — for a company that sells more than half its product through other people's stores — is the quantitative evidence that it has some. The second, quieter moat is financial: a net-cash balance sheet and a business that converts about 20% of revenue into free cash flow can keep investing in marketing through a soft patch that forces weaker brands to discount. The vulnerability is that brand heat is not contractual. Nothing obliges a runner to buy the next HOKA, and the market has seen enough footwear brands peak to price that risk aggressively.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
HOKA's growth is decelerating. +15.9% for fiscal 2026, +7.7% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027. The full-year guide (low-double-digit) needs re-acceleration. A brand valued as a growth story that becomes a mid-single-digit grower loses the valuation argument even if every other number holds.
02
The US is flat. Domestic net sales grew 0.2% in fiscal 2026 and 3.2% in Q1 FY27. International cannot carry the group forever, and the US is where HOKA faces the most competition on the running-specialty and sporting-goods shelf.
03
Wholesale partners and a promotional market. BMO Capital initiated coverage on September 8, 2026 at Sell/Underperform with a $70 target, citing — as summarised by TheFly — a weakening athletic demand backdrop and peers describing a highly promotional environment. Wholesale was 65% of Q1 FY27 sales (our calculation: $666.7M of $1,019.5M) and grew only 2.2%.
04
Tariffs and margin guide-down. The fiscal 2026 release notes inventories "including the impact of incremental tariffs", and the July 23, 2026 outlook update states that it "does not assume the collection of refunds for tariffs previously paid." Guided gross margin (slightly better than 56.5%) and operating margin (slightly better than 21.5%) are both below fiscal 2026's 57.7% and 23.1%.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $60.03 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $80.23 (both split-adjusted), DECK gained 33.6% on price; it pays no dividend, so that is also the total return. The endpoints hide a round trip. The stock more than tripled to a $203.09 month-end high in December 2024 — 3.4x the starting level — and then gave back 60.5% of that peak. Today's close is the lowest point on the chart since May 2023 ($79.17). Along the way earnings per share rose 159%. Nothing about this chart is a business collapsing; all of it is a multiple being repriced from "hyper-growth" to "ordinary".

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 30.0 — on the oversold line. The only lower readings in the series came in the 2022 bear market (March–June 2022, bottoming at 23.1) and the post-peak slide of 2025 (March–June and October 2025, between 25.4 and 29.9). The 10-month moving average sits at about $101.99, 27% above the price, and the 3-month average at about $88.06: the trend is down on every horizon the chart measures. Monthly RSI at the oversold line has twice in this series preceded sharp rebounds (July 2022, +22.7%; November 2025 to January 2026, +46% from the October low) — and has also sat below 30 for four months running in 2025 without one.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 11.3x trailing and 10.7x forward earnings for a company with two brands above $2.5B each, a 57.8% gross margin, a 22.7% operating margin, no borrowings, $1.6B of cash and a 10.2% free-cash-flow yield — and which is shrinking its share count by nearly 6% a year with $4.7B of authorisation still unused. International grew 26.8% last year; inventory is falling while sales rise; management's own framework calls for low-double-digit EPS growth through fiscal 2030. At the end of fiscal 2022 the stock traded at 16.5x EPS of $2.71; today EPS is $7.09 and the multiple is 11.3x. If HOKA re-accelerates even modestly toward its low-double-digit guide, the market is pricing a no-growth business that is not one.

Bear Case

The bear case does not dispute the balance sheet; it disputes the brand's trajectory. HOKA went from +15.9% for the year to +7.7% in the latest quarter, the US grew 0.2%, wholesale grew 2.2%, Q1 operating income fell 6% and net income 6.6%, and management is guiding margins down. BMO's $70 target and three Strong Sells say a part of the Street sees a footwear brand past its peak heat in a promotional market, and footwear history is full of brands that looked cheap on trailing earnings at exactly that moment. Buybacks executed at $102–$104 a share have already lost money on paper. Cheap-on-earnings is what a fading brand looks like on the way down.

Top 3 Risks

01
HOKA deceleration becomes permanent. The stock's valuation tracks HOKA's growth rate. Q1 FY27's +7.7% against a low-double-digit full-year guide makes the next two quarters the test; a guide cut would likely move the multiple, not just the estimates.
02
Promotional wholesale environment and US stagnation. Wholesale is roughly two-thirds of sales and grew 2.2% in Q1 FY27; domestic sales grew 0.2% for fiscal 2026. If partners discount, full-price positioning — the source of the 57.8% gross margin — comes under pressure.
03
Margin compression from tariffs and investment. FY2027 guidance already steps gross margin down to slightly better than 56.5% and operating margin to slightly better than 21.5%, and assumes no tariff refunds. Q1 SG&A rose 12.7% on 5.7% sales growth. Further tariff escalation is not in the guide.
Industry Commentary
Growth deceleration and the multiple
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Deckers' annual revenue growth went 23.75% (FY22), 15.14% (FY23), 18.21% (FY24), 16.28% (FY25), 9.76% (FY26), and the TTM figure is 7.85%. The year-end P/E went 16.5x, 22.9x, 31.8x, 17.6x, 13.9x, and today 11.3x. The market has been paying for the growth rate, not the earnings level — and it has marked the multiple down one step behind each slowdown. That is the mechanism that turned a 159% rise in EPS into a 34% rise in the share price. It also defines what would change the picture: not another record year, but evidence that growth has stopped falling.

Athletic footwear demand and a promotional shelf
ONGOING HEADWIND

For scale: Nike's market value is $53.43B and On Holding's $9.13B — smaller than Deckers' $10.93B (our calculations at September 21 closes). BMO's September initiation framed the risk as weakening athletic demand and a highly promotional environment among peers (as summarised by TheFly). Deckers' own numbers show the pressure in the wholesale channel (+2.2% in Q1 FY27) rather than in its direct business (+13.0%) — consistent with retailers ordering cautiously while end-consumers keep buying. Tariffs are a second, cost-side headwind; the company's guidance explicitly excludes any refunds of tariffs already paid.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $80.23 the stock trades at 11.3x trailing earnings, roughly 7.4x EV/EBITDA and a 10.2% free-cash-flow yield, against an analyst average target of $120.41. The bull framing: a business with no debt, record revenue and a management team retiring ~6% of the share count a year is being priced as if HOKA has already peaked; any stabilisation in HOKA growth plus the buyback compounds per-share value quickly at this price. The bear framing: the market is not mispricing a steady compounder, it is pricing the fade curve of a fashion-exposed brand, and the multiple has been right at every step of the deceleration so far. The adjudicator is the next two quarters of HOKA growth and wholesale orders. Both are reported. Neither has yet given a clean signal.
