01 Company Overview

Diageo is the company behind Johnnie Walker, Guinness, Smirnoff, Baileys, Captain Morgan, Tanqueray, Crown Royal, Don Julio and Casamigos, sold in nearly 180 countries. It was formed in 1997 when Guinness merged with Grand Metropolitan, and it reports in US dollars with a fiscal year that ends on June 30. In FY2026 (the year to June 30, 2026) net sales were $19.64B, down 3.0%; on an organic basis they fell 2.0% (volume −0.4%, price/mix −1.6%). Operating profit before exceptional items was $5.68B, up 2.0% organically, but reported operating profit fell 27.2% to $3.16B after $2.53B of impairments and restructuring charges. Basic EPS was 78.1 cents per share (IFRS), or 165.3 cents before exceptional items.

The story of the last five years is a de-rating that has run longer than anyone expected. The ADS closed October 2021 at $199.67 and October 2, 2026 at $84.75 — −57.6% on price while the FTSE 100 rose 44.6% over the same dates. Three things broke at once: US spirits demand weakened, with tequila (Don Julio, Casamigos) going from growth engine to the biggest drag; Chinese white spirits collapsed under government policy (Greater China net sales −34.9% in FY2026); and the balance sheet, at 3.4x net debt to EBITDA a year ago, left no room. CEO Debra Crew stepped down in July 2025; Sir Dave Lewis, formerly group chief executive of Tesco, took over on January 1, 2026, and on February 25, 2026 the board cut the dividend: a 20-cent interim (down from 40.5 cents a year earlier) and a new policy of paying out 30–50% of earnings with a 50-cent annual floor. The ADS fell 15.7% that day. On August 6, 2026 Lewis set out a $1.2B two-year restructuring for about $1B of savings over three years, flat organic sales for FY2027 and mid-single-digit profit growth through FY2029; the ADS rose 9.0% over the two sessions from the release. The question now is whether a cheaper, leaner Diageo can win back share in North America — 37% of sales and the one region still shrinking fast.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brands chosen by name Aged scotch inventory Scale in ~180 countries Guinness (since 1759) Distribution and on-trade reach 34% of Moët Hennessy Strong free cash flow ($3.2B)

The moat is time and brand. Whisky has to age for years in cask before it can be sold, so Diageo carries $8.5B of maturing inventories (whisky, rum, tequila and baijiu) that a newcomer cannot buy at any price, with Johnnie Walker on top of it. Brands like Guinness, Baileys and Smirnoff have decades of consumer recognition and shelf space in bars that rivals must pay to dislodge. The 59.5% gross margin is the measure of that pricing power. Diageo also owns 34% of Moët Hennessy, LVMH's champagne and cognac business. What the moat does not protect against is a whole category falling out of fashion (tequila in the US) or a government changing how people drink (baijiu in China) — and FY2026 had both.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
North America is losing share, not just volume. US Spirits net sales fell 11.5% and the company says both of its tequila brands lost share. Lewis called Diageo "uncompetitive" in its largest market. A turnaround needs share gains in a market Diageo itself expects to shrink about 3% in FY2027.
02
Leverage forced a dividend cut. Net debt was $20.5B at June 30, 2026, 3.1x adjusted EBITDA, above the 2.5–3.0x target range. The board cut the dividend from 103.48 cents (FY2025) to 50 cents (FY2026) to fix it, and the plan to reach the middle of the range by June 2027 relies on the East African Breweries and Royal Challengers Bengaluru sales completing.
03
People drinking less. Lewis wrote that moderation and low- and no-alcohol "remain important trends" and that the company continues "to watch GLP-1s carefully", adding that early data shows a lower impact on spirits than on beer and wine. Mix did the damage: price/mix took 1.6 points off FY2026 organic sales, mainly from US spirits and Chinese white spirits, with downtrading within tequila.
04
Tariffs, currency and hyperinflation. Tariffs weighed on FY2026 North American profit and group gross margin. Türkiye, a growth market, required a $786M impairment largely because of hyperinflation accounting. Exchange movements, mainly in sterling, the rupee and the lira, took $502M off equity in the year.

Notable One-Off Items

FY2026 carried $2,527M of exceptional operating charges. About $1.5B were impairments: $786M on the Türkiye business and the goodwill and brands from the Mey İçki acquisition, mostly because hyperinflation accounting lifted their carrying values, and $287M on the Don Papa rum brand as rum declined in Europe. About $0.9B was restructuring, of which $752M for the new operating framework — roughly 70% of its total cost. About $450M more falls in FY2027 — the rest of the $1.1B framework cost plus about $100M for supply chain (our arithmetic from the Capital Markets Day figures) — so reported profit will stay below pre-exceptional profit for another year. Disposals also distort comparisons: Guinness Ghana, Seychelles Breweries, the Italian operations and the Sheridan's brand were sold in FY2026, and East African Breweries is held for sale. Venezuela and Türkiye are reported under hyperinflation accounting.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $199.67 to the October 2, 2026 settled close of $84.75, the ADS lost 57.6% of its price. About $16.97 per ADS of dividends went ex over the period (Yahoo Finance dividend record), which brings the loss to roughly 49% without reinvesting them. Over the same dates the FTSE 100 rose 44.6% and SPY 67.6%. The shape: a peak of $220.14 in December 2021, a slow slide through 2022, then lower highs every year from 2023 to early 2026 — the March 2026 close of $74.45 was 66% below the peak.

The 6-period monthly RSI has spent most of the five years below 50 and repeatedly near or under 30: its lowest reading was 20.5 in July 2024, with 23.6 in March 2026 after the dividend cut. It has since recovered to 43.5 — neutral, but it has not been above 55 since April 2023. The 10-month moving average, about $85.05, sits just above the price: the stock has stopped falling but has not yet turned the trend.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 12.6x forward pre-exceptional earnings for Johnnie Walker, Guinness and a portfolio that still grew in three of five regions with a 28.9% pre-exceptional margin. The dividend cut is done, net debt is falling (3.4x to 3.1x in a year, heading for about 2.75x with the asset sales), and free cash flow of $3.2B is a 6.8% yield. A CEO hired for his record running Tesco and Unilever businesses has set numbers that can be checked quarter by quarter: about $1B of savings, flat sales this year, mid-single-digit profit growth to FY2029 and about $8B of cumulative free cash flow. If North America stops losing share, the operating leverage on a 36% margin region is large, and a stock 61% below its 2021 peak has room to re-rate.

Bear Case

Diageo's two largest growth bets of the last decade — premium tequila in the US and baijiu in China — are both shrinking, and FY2027 guidance itself has North America down again. Cost savings can protect profit for two years, but they cannot create demand, and the company is spending $1.2B and cutting jobs (now with strikes at a key distillery) to get them. Moderation, GLP-1 drugs and trading down are structural, not cyclical. Leverage is still above the target range, the CFO who wrote the three-year plan is leaving, and FY2027 consensus EPS is up only 1.3%. Investors who held for the dividend have already seen it halved.

Top 3 Risks

01
North America fails to stabilise. 37% of net sales, the highest margin, organic sales −8.4% in FY2026 and guided down mid-single-digit again. The medium-term targets assume sales accelerate "as we stabilise and grow share in North America"; if they don't, the FY2027–FY2029 plan rests on cost cuts alone.
02
Execution of the restructuring. $1.1B of the $1.2B goes on a new operating framework that is already being rolled out, with about $800M of exceptional cash costs in FY2027. Strikes at Cameronbridge and a CFO handover in 2027 add friction in the year the savings must start to arrive.
03
Balance sheet and the asset sales. Ending FY2027 at the mid-point of the 2.5–3.0x leverage range depends on the East African Breweries and Royal Challengers Bengaluru sales completing. A delay, more impairments, or weaker cash flow would push leverage back up — and the dividend has already been reset once.
Industry Commentary
The US spirits hangover
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The US has turned into the spirits industry's problem market. Diageo's US Spirits net sales fell 11.5% in FY2026 and the company expects the US market to shrink about 3% in FY2027. Tequila fell 21.1% for Diageo, with both its brands losing share as competition intensified. Distributors cut orders faster than consumers cut drinking, which exaggerated the reported decline; the company now describes inventories as in line with history. Peers show the same pressure — Brown-Forman, Constellation and Becle shares all fell over the past year. Lewis's answer is less reliance on premium price points alone: "Premiumisation remains integral to our strategy but this is now more nuanced," using "the full price and format ladder" of the portfolio, including ready-to-drink cocktails, which grew 35.1% in the US.

Drinking less: moderation, GLP-1s and price
ONGOING HEADWIND

In the FY2026 Annual Report Lewis named the economy, not abstinence, as the biggest pressure: "The biggest pressure we see today is economic, with consumers being more selective about their discretionary spending." He added that moderation and low- and no-alcohol remain important trends and that "early data indicates that the impact is lower on spirits when compared to beer and wine" for GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. Diageo is positioned on both sides: Guinness 0.0 is the fastest-growing non-alcoholic beer in Great Britain, and in September it began trialling 20% ABV versions of Smirnoff, Captain Morgan and Gordon's in UK stores (The Spirits Business, September 15). Group volume fell only 0.4% in FY2026; the damage came from mix — weaker tequila and baijiu, and downtrading within tequila.

Emerging markets and beer still grow
EMERGING TAILWIND

Outside North America and China the picture is healthy: Africa +13.3%, Latin America +7.7%, India +7.1% and Europe +3.4% organically in FY2026, and the company-compiled consensus has FY2027 organic net sales growth of +2.4% in Europe and Türkiye, +5.7% in Latin America and +7.7% in Africa, against −5.2% in North America. Guinness keeps taking share in pubs in Britain and Ireland. The trade-off is currency and accounting risk: Türkiye's growth came with a $786M impairment, and the East African brewing business is being sold to Asahi to cut debt. For the bull case to work, these regions have to keep growing while North America stops shrinking — FY2026 showed the first half of that, not yet the second.
