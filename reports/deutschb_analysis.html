01 Company Overview

Deutsche Bank describes itself as "the leading bank in Germany with strong European roots and a global network". It runs four businesses: an Investment Bank (bond, currency and credit trading plus deal advice), a Private Bank (19 million Private Bank customers in Germany, per CEO Christian Sewing, plus wealth management), a Corporate Bank (payments, cash management and trade finance for companies) and Asset Management (DWS). In 2025 it earned net revenues of €32.1B, up 7%, and a record pre-tax profit of €9.7B, up 84%, and hit both 2025 targets it had set the market: a RoTE of 10.3% (target: above 10%) and a cost/income ratio of 64% (target: below 65%).

The latest print kept that going. On October 28, 2026, before the Frankfurt open, the bank reported its third consecutive record quarterly profit: profit after tax of €1.9B (+2%), net revenues of €8.3B (+4%), profit before tax of €2.6B (+8%) and diluted EPS of €0.94 (+6%). The bank's own compiled consensus (October 14) had expected EPS of €0.77, revenue of €8,208M and profit before tax of €2,390M, so EPS beat by about 21%. Part of the profit was accounting: the EU version of the hedge-accounting rules added €3.0B to the quarter's profit before tax (see Key Vulnerabilities). Nine-month profit after tax was a record €5.9B (+7%), with RoTE of 11.5% (Q3 alone 10.6%, against 10.7% a year earlier, when favourable tax effects helped, according to the release) and the CET1 ratio at 14.0%, the top of its 13.5–14.0% operating range. The Investment Bank's revenue fell 2% against a record quarter a year earlier, as the CFO had guided on September 30; the Corporate Bank had record revenue of €1,978M (+9%), and the Private Bank and DWS had record inflows (€47B in the quarter, €103B in nine months). The bank said nine-month revenues support a full-year 2026 ambition of more than €33B, up from about €33B. The plan "Scaling the Global Hausbank" (November 2025) targets a RoTE above 13% and a cost/income ratio below 60% in 2028, and raised the share of profit paid out to shareholders from 50% to 60% from 2026. Sewing said in the results release: "We are pleased to report our third consecutive record quarterly profit in 2026." In his message to staff he said the bank would set out further ambitions with the full-year results in January.

The stock tells a two-part story, and the print added a third part. It rose from €11.146 at the end of October 2021 to a five-year-high month-end close of €34.66 in August 2026, nearly doubling in 2025 alone (+99.0%). Then September cost it 8.1%: on September 23 CFO Raja Akram told a Bank of America conference that the Investment Bank's third-quarter revenue would probably be "flat to maybe slightly down", against what he called a record quarter a year earlier for fixed-income trading, and the shares fell 3.0% that day and 2.3% the next. The results then arrived on October 28: the shares rose 3.97% that day (€28.875 → €30.02) and 3.78% the next (to €31.155), then gave back 3.45% on October 30 to €30.08, a day on which the DAX fell 0.9% and ING, BNP Paribas, UniCredit and Barclays fell 2.0–3.9% (our calculation from Yahoo Finance closes). At €30.08 the market values the bank at 0.94x its tangible book value (€31.87 a share at September 30) and 9.1x trailing earnings. The debate is whether a bank earning 11–12% on tangible equity, with Sewing saying in the Q3 release that the bank has "upside to our RoTE target of above 13% in 2028", deserves to trade above book — or whether a business that still leans on trading revenue deserves the discount.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Germany's corporate house bank €716B deposit base 19M German Private Bank customers European FIC trading scale €1.97T assets under management Euro cash-management network A1 / A / A+ credit ratings

The moat is being the one global bank Germany has. A German exporter that needs dollar cash management, trade finance in Asia and a bond issue in the same year has few domestic alternatives with that reach; the deposits those clients leave with the Corporate Bank (up 13% in a year) are cheap, sticky funding. On top sits a trading franchise that, in Q2, produced the highest second-quarter revenue in the Investment Bank's history. The credit agencies' view: Moody's A1 for deposits (outlook positive since February 19, 2026), S&P A (outlook positive since December 4, 2025) and Fitch A+ (last changed May 12, 2026), per the bank's ratings page. The weakness is the return on all that franchise: group RoTE was 4.7% as recently as 2024, and the shares traded at €7.646 at the September 2022 month-end.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Trading revenue does not repeat on command. The Investment Bank was 37% of nine-month revenue. When the CFO guided Q3 Investment Bank revenue to "flat to maybe slightly down" at a conference on September 23, 2026, the stock fell 3.0% that day and 5.2% over two sessions; Q3 revenue then came in 2% lower (€2,918M) against a record quarter a year earlier. A strong quarter for trading lifts the comparison bar for the next year.
02
Commercial real estate is still costing money. Provisions for credit losses were €501M in Q3 (41 bps of average loans, annualised), after €460M in Q2 and €519M in Q1, between the Q1 and Q2 levels as the bank had guided on September 30. The release says they included further de-risking of commercial real estate and an "idiosyncratic" (one-off) credit event in the Corporate Bank in Asia; on the Q2 call the CFO described a targeted decision of around €100M to de-risk the commercial real estate portfolio.
03
Capital is adequate, not abundant. CET1 of 14.0% sits at the top of the 13.5–14.0% range. Payouts beyond the 60% ratio need the ratio to be "sustainably" above 14%, which the CFO said in September is likely to be beyond 2026. Growth in risk-weighted assets (now €365B) competes directly with buybacks for the same capital.
04
Accounting swings in reported profit. The bank applies the EU "carve out" version of hedge accounting for its deposit and mortgage interest-rate hedges. In Q3 2026 it added €3.0B to profit before tax and €2.2B to profit (Q3 2025: €343M and €120M), after reducing Q2 2026 profit before tax by €688M. Under the version of the rules issued by the IASB (the international standard-setter), which the bank uses for its SEC filings, the same third quarter shows a loss before tax of €0.4B. Reported quarterly profit therefore moves with interest-rate markets in ways that say little about the underlying business.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distorted the second quarter. First, the EU carve-out hedge accounting cut Q2 2026 profit before tax by €688M (profit by €496M); over the first half it was a €166M positive, so the quarter flatters nothing and understates the run-rate. Second, Q2 costs of €5,340M (+8%) include about €0.2B from the exit of the Indian private-banking franchise and the absence of a litigation release booked a year earlier; excluding it, costs rose 4%. Third, provisions include the planned, capital-accretive exit of certain non-performing exposures, partly offset by releases of earlier overlays. On the other side of the ledger, buybacks reduce the share count: the €1.0B programme bought 35.7M shares at an average €28.00 (February 26 – August 21, 2026) and the €500M programme 15.1M shares at €33.19 (completed in late September), together about 2.7% of share capital. The third quarter had a bigger swing: the EU carve-out added €3.0B to profit before tax and €2.2B to profit, and the release attributes the €501M of provisions partly to further commercial real estate de-risking and an "idiosyncratic event" in the Corporate Bank in Asia.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of €11.146 to the October 30, 2026 close of €30.08, DBK.DE gained 169.9% on price. Adding the €2.63 of dividends paid over the period (€0.20, €0.30, €0.45, €0.68 and €1.00, one per year from 2022 to 2026) without reinvestment lifts that to about 193%. The DAX performance index, which reinvests dividends, rose 64.6% over the same window. Almost all of the gain came in one stretch: from August 2024 to August 2025 the stock closed higher for 13 consecutive months, and calendar 2025 alone added 99.0% (€16.64 → €33.11) as the bank hit its 2025 targets.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how stretched that run became: it peaked at 98.8 in August 2025, the highest reading in the series, after a low of 26.9 at the September 2022 trough. It now reads 50.0, neutral, down from 58.2 at the September 2026 month-end. The 10-month moving average is about €30.12, level with the price: the long-term trend is flat, not broken. The stock is 9.15% down in 2026 while the DAX is up 5.42%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a bank at 0.94x tangible book and 9.1x trailing earnings that earned an 11.5% RoTE in the first nine months, beat the bank's compiled consensus for third-quarter EPS by about 21% (€0.94 against €0.77), lifted its 2026 revenue ambition to more than €33B, and pays out 60% of its profit — a 3.3% dividend plus €1.5B of buybacks completed this year. Every division earned at least 12% RoTE in the first nine months. Net interest income is guided above €14B, with hedge benefits growing into 2027 and 2028; costs are guided to around €21.3B, about 3% above 2025's €20.7B; EU capital relief could free more room. If the bank reaches its "above 13%" RoTE target in 2028 — management says it has "upside" to it, while consensus sits at 12.2% — a stock priced at about its book value has room to rise, and the nine latest broker targets average €37.56, 24.9% above the price.

Bear Case

The first half's profit beat came from the Investment Bank, the least repeatable revenue a bank has, and in Q3 its revenue fell 2% from last year's record, as management had guided. Q3 RoTE was 10.6%, flat on a year earlier and below the nine-month 11.5%; the company-compiled consensus (October 14) has the 2028 average at 12.2%, short of the target. CET1 at 14.0% is at the top of its range rather than above it, so the CFO expects extra distributions to wait until after 2026. Commercial real estate still needs de-risking, a one-off credit event in Asia added to Q3 provisions, and hedge accounting can swing quarterly profit by billions: it added €3.0B to Q3 profit before tax. In 2026 the market has rewarded deposit-rich peers such as ING (+17.7%) and BNP Paribas (+6.0%) while DBK.DE is down 9.15%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Investment Bank revenue normalising. At 37% of nine-month revenue, a flat-to-lower Investment Bank held Q3 group revenue growth to 4%. The CFO had guided Q3 flat to slightly down and revenue fell 2% against a record 2025 quarter; the fourth quarter shows whether that is one quarter or a trend.
02
Credit costs. Provisions were €1,479M in the first nine months (+13% YoY), driven by commercial real estate clean-up and the exit of non-performing exposures, and Q3 included what the bank called an "idiosyncratic event" in the Corporate Bank in Asia. A recession in Germany, where energy prices and geopolitics were the CEO's own named concerns in March, would hit the Corporate and Private Bank loan books together.
03
Capital return slower than hoped. Distributions are capped at the 60% payout until CET1 is sustainably above 14%, and it was 14.0% at September 30. If risk-weighted assets keep absorbing capital, or regulatory relief stalls in the EU process, the buyback cadence stays at one modest programme every six months.
Industry Commentary
European banks: from book-value discount to earning their capital
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Deutsche Bank's shares closed September 2022 at €7.646; they now trade at 0.94x tangible book value. Over the same years the group's net interest margin went from 1.3% in 2024 to 1.7% in Q2 2026 (1.5% in Q3), and the bank's hedging strategy spreads the rate effect over several years: management says the benefit from recent rate decisions will become "more pronounced" in 2027 and 2028 as hedges roll. Peers' shares have risen too — ING +17.7% and BNP Paribas +6.0% in 2026 through October 30 — and the question for 2027 is which banks keep their returns as rates settle. Deutsche Bank's answer is volume: deposits +8% and loans +5% in a year.

German consolidation: UniCredit and Commerzbank
COMPETITIVE SHIFT

UniCredit's offer for Commerzbank closed its additional acceptance period on July 3, 2026 with 17.60% of shares tendered, and Commerzbank said on July 8 that the transfer of those shares remained subject to regulatory approval. For Deutsche Bank the deal is an opportunity rather than a threat, by its own account. Sewing said on the Q2 call that the bank studied a combination in 2019 and therefore knows "exactly what is possible on revenue synergies and revenue dis-synergies", and that it has hired senior staff in Germany to capture clients. The CFO said in September that, asked for new client names in the space where the merger might happen, his team produced "a dozen names of clients which are new to us" within ten minutes — "a small sample" against an aspiration of hundreds.

Regulation turning from headwind to tailwind
EMERGING TAILWIND

The last stage of the Basel III rules (CRR 3 in the EU) raised capital requirements for trading books and some lending. On the Q2 call Sewing listed the European Commission's moves on CRR 3, FRTB, capital buffers and software amortisation and said they "can have a meaningful impact", while declining to quantify it: "we should not front-run this process". The bank's CET1 ratio is already 14.0%; any relief would mostly flow to shareholders, because the bank has said excess capital above a sustained 14% goes first to buybacks. The timing is political, though: the legislative package is expected in early 2027, and Sewing expects the tailwind "in the latter year of 2027 and in particular in 2028".
