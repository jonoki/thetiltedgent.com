01 Company Overview

Somewhere in America this morning a phlebotomist tied off an arm, filled three tubes, stuck a barcode on each and dropped them into a courier bag. By tonight those tubes are in a lab, and by tomorrow a doctor has a number. That loop — draw, move, test, report — is the whole business. Quest Diagnostics runs it at national scale: routine blood work, advanced and genetic testing, anatomic pathology and workplace drug testing. FY2025 revenue was $11.04B, up 11.78%, with diluted EPS of $8.75.

The company began in 1967 as Metropolitan Pathology Laboratory in New York, became MetPath, was bought by Corning Glass Works in 1982 and run as Corning Clinical Laboratories, and was spun off to Corning shareholders as Quest Diagnostics on December 31, 1996. The COVID years distort everything since: FY2021 revenue hit $10.79B and EPS $15.55 on pandemic testing, then revenue fell two years running as that volume vanished — FY2023 revenue was $9.25B. The recovery has been built the unglamorous way: buying hospital outreach labs, winning health-system partnerships and adding volume. In Q2 2026 requisition volume grew 13.1% while revenue per requisition fell 2.8% — more tubes, slightly cheaper tubes. The stock is up about 41% from the December 2025 close and sits near its all-time high. The question the price now asks: is 13% volume growth the new normal, or the peak?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Courier & logistics network Patient service center footprint Scale cost per test Payer network contracts Physician ordering integration Health-system lab partnerships Advanced test menu

The moat is logistics wearing a lab coat. Running a routine test is cheap; getting a tube from a suburban doctor's office to an automated analyser overnight, reliably, across a whole country, is not. That density — plus being in-network with the health plans and built into physicians' ordering workflows — is why two national independent labs, Quest and Labcorp, carry near-identical market values of about $27B and $26B. The weakness is that the price of a test is set largely by payers and government fee schedules, not by Quest. Scale protects the cost side; it does not protect the price.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Price is set by others. Revenue per requisition fell 2.8% in Q2 2026. Reimbursement rates from government programmes and commercial payers are a lever Quest does not hold; any fresh fee-schedule pressure lands on a business whose volume growth is already doing all the work.
02
13% volume is hard to repeat. Consensus already expects revenue growth to slow from 8.81% in 2026 to 4.70% in 2027 (StockAnalysis, estimates). A stock priced near its high is priced for the fast half.
03
Cash is lagging earnings. First-half operating cash flow rose 1.9% while Q2 adjusted EPS grew 19.1%, and TTM free cash flow of $1.35B is flat on FY2025. A persistent gap would mean working capital or integration costs are absorbing the growth.
04
Quality and reputation. A lab's product is a correct answer. Quest was among the US labs linked to cervical-smear inaccuracies in Ireland's CervicalCheck programme in 2018 (Wikipedia, citing the Irish Times). Errors are rare, but they are litigated.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF SEP 21, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $145.31 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $244.88, DGX gained 68.5% on price, before dividends. The path was anything but straight: a COVID-testing spike to $173.01 in December 2021, a 22% one-month drop in January 2022 as the market priced the end of that business, and then nearly three years stuck between roughly $122 and $157 while revenue shrank back to its pre-pandemic shape. The breakout came in mid-2024, and 2026 has been the steepest leg: from $173.53 at the end of December 2025 to $244.88, about +41%.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 80.2, the highest in the five-year series, after 76.3 in July and 78.7 in August. The 10-month moving average is about $208.77, roughly 15% below the price. Momentum is not the issue; stretch is. Three straight overbought monthly readings with the price at the consensus target is the setup where one soft quarter gets punished.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Quest has become a growth company again without stopping being a defensive one. Requisitions grew 13.1% in Q2 2026, adjusted EPS 19.1%, guidance has been raised, and beta is 0.55. Health-system partnerships like Corewell, the Fresenius kidney-care work, Alzheimer's blood testing and consumer distribution through questhealth.com and Apple Health all add volume to a fixed network, and adjusted margin held at 16.5% even as price per test fell. Consensus has the company earning $11.19 in 2026 and $12.06 in 2027 (estimates); 21.9x forward for a business with this visibility is not a heroic multiple.

Bear Case

The good news is priced. The stock is at the average target of $246.73, monthly RSI is 80.2, PEG is 2.27, and consensus expects revenue growth to roughly halve to 4.70% in 2027. Revenue per requisition is falling, so the story depends on volume staying near double digits. Free cash flow has not kept pace with earnings — TTM FCF of $1.35B is flat on FY2025 — and insiders sold $8.3M over three months with no buys. An unexplained 6.89% after-hours drop on the day of this snapshot shows how little room there is for a surprise.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume slowdown against a stretched price. Q2's 13.1% requisition growth is doing all the work while price per test falls 2.8%. Any slowdown at the October 20 print meets a stock at its target with RSI above 80.
02
Reimbursement pressure. Payers and government fee schedules set price. A lab cannot out-execute a rate cut.
03
Earnings-to-cash gap. H1 2026 operating cash flow +1.9% against double-digit EPS growth, with about $1.08 per share between reported and adjusted FY2026 EPS guidance. If the gap persists, the quality of the beat deserves a harder look.
Industry Commentary
Health systems handing over their labs
EMERGING TAILWIND

Hospitals run their own labs, and many run them below efficient scale. Quest's Co-Lab Solutions arrangement with Corewell Health and its outreach acquisitions are how an independent lab takes share from hospitals without a price war. That volume arrives in large blocks, which fits requisitions growing 13% in a quarter while revenue per requisition declines.

Consumer-ordered testing
EARLY

Quest launched consumer-initiated testing in 2018 (QuestDirect, now questhealth.com), and the Q2 2026 release describes that channel as generating robust revenue. The September 2026 Apple Health integration puts the checkout inside a phone many patients already carry. The consumer channel's revenue is not disclosed in the sources fetched, so it cannot yet be weighed against the core physician and hospital business.
