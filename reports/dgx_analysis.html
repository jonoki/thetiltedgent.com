01 Company Overview

Somewhere in America this morning a phlebotomist tied off an arm, filled three tubes, stuck a barcode on each and dropped them into a courier bag. By tonight those tubes are in a lab, and by tomorrow a doctor has a number. That loop — draw, move, test, report — is the whole business. Quest Diagnostics runs it at national scale: routine blood work, advanced and genetic testing, anatomic pathology and workplace drug testing. FY2025 revenue was $11.04B, up 11.78%, with diluted EPS of $8.75.

The company began in 1967 as Metropolitan Pathology Laboratory in New York, became MetPath, was bought by Corning Glass Works in 1982 and run as Corning Clinical Laboratories, and was spun off to Corning shareholders as Quest Diagnostics on December 31, 1996. The COVID years distort everything since: FY2021 revenue hit $10.79B and EPS $15.55 on pandemic testing, then revenue fell two years running as that volume vanished — FY2023 revenue was $9.25B. The recovery has been built the unglamorous way: buying hospital outreach labs, winning health-system partnerships and adding volume. In Q3 2026 requisition volume grew 14.5% (14.4% organic) while revenue per requisition fell 4.2% — more tubes, cheaper tubes. On the October 22 call, management said the slide in price per order came mainly from mix: the Corewell Health and Fresenius Medical Care volumes pay less per order, and volume excluding those two relationships grew 5%, up from 4.1% in Q2 (Q3 2026 earnings call). The stock is up about 40% from the December 2025 close and sits 2.4% below its 52-week high. The question the price now asks: how much of 14% volume growth is the new normal, and how much is two big partnerships being counted for the first time?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Courier & logistics network Patient service center footprint Scale cost per test Payer network contracts Physician ordering integration Health-system lab partnerships Advanced test menu

The moat is logistics wearing a lab coat. Running a routine test is cheap; getting a tube from a suburban doctor's office to an automated analyser overnight, reliably, across a whole country, is not. That density — plus being in-network with the health plans and built into physicians' ordering workflows — is why two national independent labs, Quest and Labcorp, dominate the market. The weakness is that the price of a test is set largely by payers and government fee schedules, not by Quest. Scale protects the cost side; it does not protect the price.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Price is set by others. Revenue per requisition fell 4.2% in Q3 2026. On the October 22 call, management put the 2027 effect of cuts under PAMA (the law behind Medicare's lab fee schedule) at about 1% of total company revenues, and the cumulative effect for 2027 through 2029 at under 1.3%. It said it would offset part of it but could not yet say how much (Q3 2026 earnings call).
02
14% volume is hard to repeat. Consensus expects revenue growth to slow from 9.45% in 2026 to 4.47% in 2027 (StockAnalysis, estimates). Part of 2026's jump is two partnerships (Corewell and Fresenius) that stop adding to growth once they have been in the base for a year.
03
Cash is lagging earnings. Nine-month operating cash flow rose 4.6% while Q3 adjusted EPS grew 15.4%, and TTM free cash flow of $1.375B is up only about 1.2% on FY2025's $1.359B. A persistent gap would mean working capital or integration costs are absorbing the growth.
04
Quality and reputation. A lab's product is a correct answer. Quest was among the US labs linked to cervical-smear inaccuracies in Ireland's CervicalCheck programme in 2018 (Wikipedia, citing the Irish Times). Errors are rare, but they are litigated. Data is the other exposure: on October 21, 2026 Quest learned of unauthorized access to its Quanum LSM test-ordering system and believes certain patient records were accessed; it said operations were not materially disrupted (8-K, October 22, 2026).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $145.31 to the October 26, 2026 settled close of $242.95, DGX gained 67.2% on price, before dividends. The path was anything but straight: a COVID-testing spike to $173.01 in December 2021, a 22% one-month drop in January 2022 as the market priced the end of that business, and then nearly three years stuck between roughly $122 and $157 while revenue shrank back to its pre-pandemic shape. The breakout came in mid-2024, and 2026 has been the steepest leg: from $173.53 at the end of December 2025 to $242.95, about +40%.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 74.7, after 70.3 in September, 78.7 in August (the highest in the five-year series) and 76.3 in July. The 10-month moving average is about $214.53; the price is about 13% above it. Momentum is not the issue; stretch is. Four straight monthly readings above 70 with the price 5% below the consensus target is the setup where one soft quarter gets punished — though this quarter, with guidance raised again, drew a +1.82% first session.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Quest has become a growth company again without stopping being a defensive one. Requisitions grew 14.5% in Q3 2026, adjusted EPS 15.4%, guidance has been raised again, and beta is 0.54. Health-system partnerships like Corewell, the Fresenius kidney-care work, the Northeast outreach-lab purchase, Alzheimer's blood testing and consumer distribution through questhealth.com and Apple Health all add volume to a fixed network, and adjusted margin rose to 16.7% even as price per test fell. Consensus has the company earning $11.30 in 2026 and $11.99 in 2027 (adjusted, estimates); 21.5x forward for a business with this visibility is not a heroic multiple.

Bear Case

The good news is mostly priced. The stock is 5% below the average target of $256.60, monthly RSI is 74.7, PEG is 2.25, and consensus expects revenue growth to roughly halve to 4.47% in 2027. Revenue per requisition fell 4.2%, so the story depends on volume staying strong once the Corewell and Fresenius partnerships are in the base; excluding them, volume grew 5%. Management's own estimate of the 2027 PAMA cut is about 1% of total company revenues. Free cash flow has not kept pace with earnings — TTM FCF of $1.375B is up only about 1.2% on FY2025. A 4.13% one-day drop on September 22 shows how little room there is for a surprise, and October's unauthorized access to patient records adds reputational risk.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume slowdown against a stretched price. Q3's 14.5% requisition growth is doing most of the work while price per test falls 4.2%. Organic volume excluding two partnerships grew 5%; the 2027 guide arrives with Q4 results.
02
Reimbursement pressure. Payers and government fee schedules set price. Management puts the 2027 PAMA cut at about 1% of total company revenues, and a lab cannot out-execute a rate cut.
03
Earnings-to-cash gap. Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow +4.6% against double-digit adjusted EPS growth, with about $1.04 per share between reported and adjusted FY2026 EPS guidance. If the gap persists, the quality of the beat deserves a harder look.
Industry Commentary
Health systems handing over their labs
EMERGING TAILWIND

Hospitals run their own labs, and many run them below efficient scale. Quest's Co-Lab Solutions arrangement with Corewell Health and its outreach acquisitions are how an independent lab takes share from hospitals without a price war. The Q3 release adds a definitive agreement to buy select outreach lab assets from a Northeastern health system that already uses Co-Lab Solutions. That volume arrives in large blocks, which fits requisitions growing 14.5% in a quarter while revenue per requisition declines.

Consumer-ordered testing
EARLY

The Q3 2026 release lists the consumer channel among the growth drivers, and describes the ability to buy a Quest test panel of more than 50 biomarkers through the Apple Health app, which puts the checkout inside a phone many patients already carry.
