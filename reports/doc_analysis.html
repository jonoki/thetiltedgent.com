01 Company Overview

Healthpeak is a REIT that owns, operates and develops real estate "for healthcare discovery and delivery" — in plain terms, three kinds of building: outpatient medical offices next to hospitals, life-science labs where biotech companies do research, and senior housing, now held through a separately listed subsidiary, Janus Living. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $3.06B (StockAnalysis, which includes the Q3 2026 quarter). The company that trades as DOC today was Healthpeak (ticker PEAK) until it merged with Physicians Realty Trust — which had itself used the DOC ticker. The merger closed on March 1, 2024 and the combined company kept the Healthpeak name and took the DOC symbol on March 4, 2024. Every price in this report is Healthpeak's own history, not the old Physicians Realty stock.

The story of the last five years is one number falling and another holding. The share price went from a $36.09 month-end close in December 2021 to $15.55 in October 2023 — a 57% fall. The cash flow did not follow it down: FFO as Adjusted was $1.81 a share in 2024 and $1.84 in 2025, and management now guides $1.76–$1.78 for 2026 (raised from $1.73–$1.77 on Nov 2). At $20.26, the stock trades at about 11.4x the midpoint of that guide and yields 6.0%. The GAAP P/E of 37.5 is not the valuation that matters here; depreciation across $22.4B of assets swamps accounting profit. The question is whether lab occupancy (79.8% after a third straight quarterly gain) keeps turning into rent, and whether Janus Living's acquisitions and the asset sales turn a discounted portfolio into cash at close to what it is worth on paper.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Hospital-campus locations Health-system relationships Core life-science clusters 81.7% outpatient tenant retention Scale after the $5B merger Investment-grade access to capital

In real estate the moat is location plus the tenant relationship. A medical office building attached to a hospital campus cannot be reproduced across the street. The physicians need to be next to the operating rooms, and 81.7% of outpatient tenants renewed in Q3 2026 (Q3 call). Health systems also bring repeat development work: the Northside Hospital building in Atlanta, announced in June 2026, is Healthpeak's fifth ground-up project for that system, totaling 565,000 sq ft. Lab is different. A cluster location helps, but when funding dries up the tenants are small companies that can shrink or disappear, and a well-located empty lab still earns nothing.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Lab tenants live on biotech funding. Healthpeak's Q3 release lists among its risks "changes within the life science industry, and significant regulation, funding requirements, and uncertainty faced by our lab tenants." More than a third of same-store NOI (35.6% in Q3) rests on a tenant base whose capital comes from venture and public equity markets that open and close.
02
Rates set the ceiling on value. REIT prices compete with bond yields. In CEO Brinker's words, the cost of debt "isn't much lower than the fair market cap rate of our real estate". Every new purchase is close to break-even until rates fall or rents rise.
03
Complexity and conflicts with Janus. Healthpeak externally manages a listed subsidiary it owns 69% of. The Q3 release's risk list flags "potential conflicts of interest in our relationship with Janus Living" and "economic exposure to shifts in the price of Janus Living common stock." A structure built to unlock value can also lead investors to value the whole at less than its parts.
04
Shrinking to grow. The 2026 FFO guide ($1.76–$1.78) is below 2025's $1.84 because assets are being sold faster than new income arrives. That is fine if the proceeds are recycled at better returns and a problem if they are not.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP earnings in 2026 are unusually noisy. Nine-month 2026 net income attributable to Healthpeak was $263.7M ($0.38 a share) against a $42.6M loss ($0.06 a share) a year earlier. The first-half swing came from $60.7M of gains on property sales and a $138.3M gain upon change of control (Q2 release); neither repeated in Q3, which had no sale gains and EPS of $0.02. Q3 depreciation and amortization was $298.9M against $262.3M, much of it from Janus Living's acquisitions, which is why the 2026 EPS guide was cut while the FFO guide rose. The Janus IPO also changes the share base per unit of senior-housing income: about 31% of Janus's earnings now go to outside shareholders as noncontrolling interest. Finally, Healthpeak bought back 5.9M shares at $16.81 (~$100M) in April 2026, and the board authorized a new $500M program in July; the Q3 release does not mention buybacks.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay about 11.4x 2026 guided FFO, with a 6.0% yield covered about 1.45 times by FFO, for a portfolio whose parts are being priced privately at higher values. Brookfield paid a ~5.9% cap rate for 49% of an 86-property outpatient portfolio, Janus Living went public and has since closed $1.75B of acquisitions in a single quarter, and Healthpeak's stake in it is worth about $6.87B at JAN's Nov 6 close. Leverage is 4.7x. Lab occupancy has risen for three straight quarters, to 79.8%, with 643,000 sq ft executed or under letter of intent since quarter-end, and lab same-store NOI fell only 0.7% in Q3. If lab NOI turns positive in 2027, the drag on the portfolio becomes a contributor. The share price is still 44% below its December 2021 month-end close.

Bear Case

2026 FFO is guided below 2025 ($1.76–$1.78 against $1.84), and Lab, over a third of same-store NOI, is still shrinking at −0.7%. The recovery depends on biotech funding that Healthpeak does not control, and the CEO himself said on the Q2 call that the cost of debt is close to property yields, so external growth adds little. The new lab purchases are only 41% occupied today and earn a cash yield of about 5% until they are leased up to Healthpeak's targeted 9–10%. Janus Living adds a layer of complexity and potential conflicts, and about 31% of its growth now belongs to outside shareholders. After a 35% rally from April to July, 14 of 22 analysts rate it Hold, and Cantor cut its target to $21 the day after the earnings call. The easy part of the rebound may be done.

Top 3 Risks

01
Lab recovery stalls. Occupancy at 79.8% and same-store NOI at −0.7% leave little room for error. If signed leases and letters of intent do not turn into occupied space, or tenants shrink as funding tightens, the improvement slips into 2027.
02
Interest rates. A REIT yielding 6.0% competes directly with bonds. Higher long-term yields would lower property values and make refinancing more expensive, and with debt costs already near cap rates there is little cushion.
03
Execution of a complex restructuring. A listed subsidiary, a Brookfield joint venture that Healthpeak has the right to buy back after year seven, ongoing asset sales and a buyback all compete for capital. The 2026 FFO decline is accepted in exchange for a better portfolio later. If proceeds are redeployed poorly, the lower FFO stays and the improvement does not come.
Industry Commentary
Life science: from development to acquisition
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For a decade, lab REITs grew by building. Brinker's Q2 2026 call view was that "for the next few years, it will be an acquisition game", meaning the value is in buying existing buildings from distressed or exiting owners rather than adding supply. Healthpeak's February 2026 plan fits that: sell fully stabilized outpatient medical assets into strong private demand and recycle the proceeds into higher-growth opportunities, including life-science campuses in core submarkets, as with the $600M South San Francisco purchase. The constraint is tenant demand, which depends on biotech capital markets. Healthpeak's Q3 release lists this among its risks.

Public vs private real-estate pricing
ONGOING DEBATE

Healthpeak spent 2026 testing whether private buyers would pay more for its assets than public investors. Brookfield valued an 86-building outpatient portfolio at $2.1B (about $380 per sq ft, ~5.9% trailing cash cap rate), and about $400M of seller-financing loans was repaid. Year-to-date proceeds reached $1.75B, well ahead of the $1B plan. Each such sale sets a private-market reference price for the rest of the portfolio. Selling also shrinks near-term FFO, which is why the 2026 guide sits below 2025.

Senior housing demand
EMERGING TAILWIND

Janus Living's same-store NOI grew 25.9% with a 360 bps margin expansion in Q3 2026, and its total revenue rose 78%, the strongest growth anywhere in the portfolio. The bull framing is that Healthpeak put its fastest-growing business into a listed vehicle whose share price makes its value visible. The bear framing is that Healthpeak now owns less of that growth, collects it through a management agreement and a 69% stake, and carries the conflicts that come with the structure. Senior housing was only 10.0% of Healthpeak's same-store NOI in Q3, so the effect on group numbers will be slow unless Janus keeps buying.
