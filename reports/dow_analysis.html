01 Company Overview

Dow Inc. is one of the world's largest commodity and specialty chemical producers, selling ethylene and polyethylene, industrial intermediates (glycols, amines, polyurethanes), and performance silicones and coatings materials out of an integrated global cracker and derivative network. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 2026 was $41.32B, down 1.2% against FY2025's $42.0B, and the company sits at roughly #1,150 globally by market capitalisation at $21.41B — a company that was worth $41.70B in 2021 and has lost close to half its equity value since.

The premise most readers arrive with — "commodity chemicals in a prolonged downcycle" — was accurate through the end of 2025 and is no longer the whole story as of the June 2026 quarter. Q2 2026 net sales were $12.09B, up 19.7% YoY, and Operating EBITDA was $2.3B against $703M a year earlier. Critically, that swing came almost entirely from price, not volume: local price rose 20% while volume fell 1%. The company itself has guided Q3 2026 Operating EBITDA back down to roughly $1.7B on a June polyethylene settlement of −$0.15 per pound. So the central question on this page is not "when does the trough end" but something sharper: was Q2 a durable earnings recovery, or a price spike on top of a cost structure that is still being dismantled? The dividend is where that question gets settled in cash, and Section 04 takes it apart.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
US Gulf Coast ethane cost position Integrated cracker-to-derivative chains Replacement-cost asset base Global scale & logistics Downstream silicone formulation IP Investment-grade balance sheet Customer qualification switching costs

Dow's advantage is a cost curve, not a brand. Polyethylene is fungible; what is not fungible is the delivered cash cost of producing it, and a US Gulf Coast cracker running cheap North American ethane sits far enough down the global cost curve that it earns through cycles that shut higher-cost European and Asian naphtha capacity. That is the entire logic of the current restructuring: Dow is not shrinking, it is deleting the part of its own asset base that sits on the wrong side of the curve. The three European closures — Böhlen, Schkopau, Barry — target roughly $200M of Operating EBITDA uplift, with about half of that expected by the end of 2027, at a cost of $630M–$790M in restructuring charges and around $500M of cash over four years. Set against a company that just printed $2.3B of EBITDA in a single quarter, $200M sounds modest; set against a company that earned $703M in the comparable quarter a year earlier, it is material.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Earnings are a price derivative, and Dow does not set the price. Q2 2026's entire improvement came from +20% local price against −1% volume. A single June polyethylene settlement of −$0.15/lb is enough to guide company EBITDA from $2.3B down to ~$1.7B in one quarter. No amount of cost work insulates a business whose profit swing per quarter exceeds its entire annual self-help target.
02
Global capacity overhang, particularly from the Middle East and China. The closures Dow is executing in Europe are a response to structural oversupply, not a cure for it. Capacity added elsewhere on advantaged feedstock can re-flood the market and compress the spread again regardless of what Dow removes.
03
Leverage into a volatile earnings stream. Long-term debt of $17.2B and enterprise value of $36.14B against a $21.41B equity market cap means bondholders own a large share of the enterprise. Debt/EBITDA of 3.61× on TTM earnings looks tolerable; on FY2025's trough earnings it was 4.78×. The ratio is a function of the cycle, which is the problem.
04
Off-balance-sheet and joint-venture exposure. The Sadara guarantee produced a $292M pretax charge in the first half of 2026 from a fair-value adjustment alone. Obligations that move with someone else's operating performance are not obligations management can restructure away.

The dividend against free cash flow — the live question

Here is what is actually reported, without forecasting. The quarterly dividend is $0.35 per share, declared and paid through the September 2026 cycle (ex-date August 31, 2026). That rate is half what it was: Dow paid $0.70 per quarter from early 2024 through the May 2025 payment, and the rate has been $0.35 since the August 2025 payment — a 50% cut, showing up as a −42.86% one-year dividend growth rate and a drop in dividend per share from $2.10 in FY2025 to $1.40 on a forward-annualised basis. No change to the current rate was announced with Q2 2026 results, and no increase was announced.

The cash arithmetic, as reported: Dow paid $253M of dividends in Q2 2026 and $505M in the first half. First-half free cash flow was $1.3B — so the dividend was covered roughly 2.6× in the half. On a trailing-twelve-month basis it is tighter: $1.46B of FCF against a dividend run-rate of about $1.01B annualised (722.34M shares × $1.40), or coverage near 1.4× — and the TTM FCF figure includes the Q2 price spike. Against Q3 2026's guided ~$1.7B of Operating EBITDA and a capex run-rate above $600M a quarter, the dividend remains payable out of operations but the margin of safety narrows quickly as EBITDA steps down.

What would have to be true for the payout to hold at $0.35, framed as testable conditions rather than a prediction: (1) quarterly Operating EBITDA stays at or above roughly the guided $1.7B, so that operating cash flow comfortably exceeds capex plus $253M; (2) capex continues to be managed down or held flat as the Alberta Path2Zero build runs off — management said roughly 60% of that project's capex has been deployed, so the remaining call on cash is shrinking rather than growing; (3) the "Transform to Outperform" savings land — the company raised its in-year target by about $200M to greater than $1.3B of benefits in 2026, on top of a completed $1B programme in 2025 — and stay landed rather than being competed away in price; (4) deleveraging proceeds without a capital-structure event, with long-term debt already down from $17.8B to $17.2B and no buybacks planned for 2026; and (5) no repeat of a Sadara-scale guarantee charge or working-capital reversal. If polyethylene settles materially below the current level for two or three consecutive quarters, conditions (1) and (3) fail together, because trough pricing both removes the cash and removes the pricing power that the cost savings were supposed to compound with. That is the scenario the 5.19% short interest is expressing. This page takes no view on which way it resolves.

Notable One-Off Items

The reported numbers are heavily distorted in both directions and should not be read straight. Working against earnings: $526M of pretax restructuring charges in Q2 2026 ($445M severance, $81M costs to achieve); a $292M pretax first-half charge for the fair-value adjustment to the Sadara guarantee liability; and the in-period cost of the Barry, UK siloxanes shutdown, which is the direct reason Performance Materials & Coatings EBIT fell $19M YoY. Working in favour: a $150M income tax adjustment benefit in Q2 tied to the NOVA Chemicals legal settlement (cash proceeds received March 2026), and roughly $50M from a Taiwan land sale inside Industrial Intermediates & Infrastructure. The total European closure programme carries $630M–$790M of restructuring charges and about $500M of cash spending over four years, with decommissioning and demolition potentially running through 2029 — so charges of this kind are a recurring feature of the next several quarters, not a clean one-time reset. The gap between Q2 GAAP EPS of $0.99 and operating EPS of $1.44 is the size of that distortion in per-share terms.

RSI measures how much of a period's price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 suggest the stock has risen far and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 suggests the opposite ("oversold"). It describes momentum, not value — an oversold stock can stay oversold for a very long time, as DOW's 2025 readings demonstrate.

Five years of this chart is a single story told twice. From the October 2021 close of $55.97 to the September 10, 2026 close of $29.64, the shares are down 47.0% on price — and that is before accounting for the fact that the dividend funding much of the holding-period return was halved along the way. The post-COVID chemical supercycle peaked at $67.98 in May 2022; everything since has been the long unwind of that peak, punctuated by two violent counter-trend rallies.

The capitulation ran from September 2024 ($54.63) to September 2025 ($22.93) — a 58% decline in twelve months, through the dividend cut and into a trough where the 6-period RSI printed readings of 5.8 and 7.2. Those are among the most extreme oversold readings a large-cap industrial produces. What followed was equally extreme in the other direction: from the December 2025 close of $23.38 to the March 2026 close of $41.65, the stock gained 78% in three months, with RSI pushing to 79.8. It then gave back 34% into June 2026 ($27.36) before the Q2 print stabilised it in the high twenties. The current reading of 45.2 is dead neutral — the market has no momentum view on this name right now, which is consistent with 11 of 19 analysts sitting on Hold.

Relative performance. Over the trailing year DOW is +21.53%, a genuinely strong number that reflects buying the trough rather than owning a good business — the same twelve months that produced +21.5% also contained the 78% three-month rally and a 34% drawdown. Over five years the −47.0% price return is a severe underperformance of any broad US index, and the pattern of it matters: with a beta of just 0.42, DOW has not been tracking the S&P 500 or the NASDAQ-100 at all. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The trough is behind: Q2 2026 Operating EBITDA of $2.3B against $703M a year earlier shows what this asset base earns when polyethylene spreads are merely normal rather than catastrophic. Meanwhile the cost structure is being permanently reduced — over $1.3B of in-year self-help benefits targeted in 2026 on top of a completed $1B programme, plus roughly $200M of EBITDA uplift from removing three high-cost European plants. Free cash flow of $1.3B in the first half covered the dividend 2.6×, capex is rolling off as Path2Zero passes 60% deployment, and debt is already falling. At 0.50× sales and a 4.72% yield, very little recovery is in the price.

Bear Case

Nothing structural changed — only price did. Volume fell 1% company-wide and 4% in the largest segment; the entire earnings swing was a 20% local price increase Dow does not control. Management's own Q3 guide takes EBITDA from $2.3B to ~$1.7B on a single −$0.15/lb settlement, which is a larger quarterly swing than the whole annual cost-savings target. ROIC is 3.88% — below any plausible cost of capital — and trailing EPS is −$1.82. With $17.2B of long-term debt and a dividend already halved once, a second leg down in pricing puts the remaining $0.35 back in play.

Top 3 Risks

01
Polyethylene price reversal. One settlement moved guided EBITDA by roughly $600M a quarter. Two or three consecutive quarters at trough pricing would exhaust the free cash flow cushion the dividend currently sits on, and no cost programme operates on that timescale.
02
A second dividend cut. The rate has already been halved once, in 2025, and the market did not treat that cut as balance-sheet repair — the stock fell through it. A 4.72% yield on a company with negative trailing earnings and 3.88% ROIC is the market pricing non-trivial odds of a repeat. The board's quarterly declaration is therefore a recurring event risk, not a formality.
03
Restructuring execution and its cash cost. $630M–$790M of charges and ~$500M of cash across four years, decommissioning potentially running to 2029, and a $200M EBITDA prize that is only half-delivered by end-2027. If the closures slip or the savings are competed away in price, Dow will have spent the cash and kept the cost curve.
Industry Commentary
Global capacity overhang in commodity chemicals
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The downcycle premise is structurally correct even where the 2026 prints are not. Dow's own trailing-twelve-month revenue of $41.32B is down 1.2% and the chemicals industry aggregate on stockanalysis.com shows a −5.60% profit margin with an "n/a" P/E — the whole peer set is loss-making on trailing earnings. The response across the industry is capacity rationalisation, and Dow is executing a textbook version of it: close Böhlen (German cracker, Q4 2027), close Schkopau (German chlor-alkali/vinyls, Q4 2027), close Barry (UK siloxanes, mid-2026, done), restart the lower-cost Terneuzen cracker in the Netherlands. The stated goal is to "align regional production capacity with market needs, lower exposure to merchant sales and eliminate higher-cost, energy-intensive operations." That is a coherent plan for one company's own cost curve. It does nothing about the capacity being added elsewhere.

European energy cost and the deindustrialisation trade
ONGOING HEADWIND

Everything Dow is closing is in Europe, and that is not a coincidence. A cracker's competitiveness is a function of feedstock and power cost, and the European versions of both have been structurally impaired since 2022 — the September 2022 close of $43.93, 35% below the May peak, is when the equity market first priced it. The Barry shutdown alone removed roughly 25% of European siloxane capacity and cost Performance Materials & Coatings $19M of YoY EBIT in the quarter it happened. The read-across is uncomfortable for anyone modelling a smooth recovery: Dow's earnings recovery and its European retreat are the same event, and the cash cost of the retreat (~$500M over four years) lands before the ~$200M annual benefit does.

What the bull and bear actually disagree about
EMERGING TAILWIND — CONTESTED

Both sides accept the same facts. Q2 2026 EBITDA was $2.3B; Q3 is guided to ~$1.7B; local price was +20% and volume −1%; the self-help target is >$1.3B for 2026; free cash flow was $1.3B in the first half against $505M of dividends. The disagreement is entirely about which number is the baseline. The bull reads $1.7B as the new floor — a cost structure roughly $1.3B lighter meeting a pricing environment off its lows, which annualises to roughly $6–7B of EBITDA against $36.1B of enterprise value. The bear reads $1.7B as a waypoint on the way back down, notes that the same asset base produced $703M in a quarter fifteen months ago, and points out that at 3.88% ROIC Dow is not yet earning its cost of capital even in the good quarter. Management has supplied a falsifiable test: the Q3 print on October 22, 2026, and the free cash flow it carries against a $253M quarterly dividend. This page takes no position on the outcome.
