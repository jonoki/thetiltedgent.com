01 Company Overview

Dow Inc. is one of the world's largest commodity and specialty chemical producers, selling ethylene and polyethylene, industrial intermediates (glycols, amines, polyurethanes), and performance silicones and coatings materials out of an integrated global network of crackers (plants that split natural-gas liquids or naphtha into ethylene) and the units downstream of them. Trailing-twelve-month net sales through September 2026 were $42.61B, up 4.2% on the twelve months before (our sum of Dow's reported quarters), and the company ranks #367 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation at $23.61B. The shares closed October 23, 2026 at $32.61, 41.7% below their October 2021 month-end close of $55.97.

The last edition asked whether the June 2026 quarter was a durable earnings recovery or a price spike. The September quarter gave a partial answer. Operating EBITDA fell to $1.55B from $2.31B in Q2, as management had warned, and landed below the roughly $1.7B it had guided to in July. But it was still 79% above the $868M of a year earlier, and the drivers were the same as before: local price +14% (the change in selling prices, before currency) against volume −1%, plus cost cuts. Net sales were $11.27B, up 13% YoY. Operating EPS of $0.71 beat analysts' $0.61–$0.64, and Q4 is guided to about $1.5B of Operating EBITDA. So the answer so far: earnings stepped down from the Q2 peak but settled near double their 2025 level, and the cash came in stronger than the earnings. Free cash flow of $1.13B in the quarter covered the $253M dividend about 4.5 times. Section 04 takes the dividend apart.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
US Gulf Coast ethane cost position Integrated cracker-to-derivative chains Replacement-cost asset base Global scale & logistics Downstream silicone formulation IP Investment-grade balance sheet Customer qualification switching costs

Dow's advantage is a cost curve, not a brand. Polyethylene is interchangeable; what is not is the cash cost of making it, and a US Gulf Coast cracker running cheap North American ethane sits low enough on the global cost curve to earn through cycles that shut higher-cost European and Asian naphtha capacity. That is the logic of the current restructuring: Dow is removing the part of its own asset base that sits on the wrong side of the curve. The three European shutdowns — Barry (done, mid-2026), Böhlen and Schkopau (Q4 2027) — were announced in July 2025 with a target of about $200M a year of Operating EBITDA, half of it by the end of 2027 and all of it by 2029, at a cost of $630M–$790M in charges and about $500M of cash over four years. On top of that sits Transform to Outperform: in the Q3 release the CEO said Dow is on track for the upsized $700M of growth and productivity benefits in 2026 and about $2B of total uplift by the end of 2027. Against a quarter of $1.55B of EBITDA, those are material sums; against a one-cent-a-pound polyethylene move worth about $50M a quarter, they are not a shield.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Earnings follow a price Dow does not set. Q3 2026 again ran on local price (+14%) with volume down 1%. Management's Q4 guide of about $1.5B assumes polyethylene margins improve by about 2 cents a pound; it put the upside at roughly $200M if the full October and November price increases stick. A miss of a few cents works the same way in reverse.
02
Global capacity overhang and disrupted trade. The closures Dow is executing in Europe are a response to oversupply, not a cure for it: capacity added elsewhere on cheap feedstock can push the spread back down whatever Dow removes. The Middle East conflict has also disrupted trade: it cost Dow volume in Asia Pacific and polyurethanes and left some Sadara units running at reduced rates.
03
Leverage into a volatile earnings stream. Total debt of $18.97B including leases (stockanalysis.com) and an enterprise value of about $38.1B against a $23.61B equity market cap (our calculation) mean lenders fund a large share of the business. Debt/EBITDA is 3.17× on trailing earnings (stockanalysis.com). Liquidity of about $14B and no meaningful maturities until 2029 (the CFO, on the Q3 call) buy time; they do not change that the ratio moves with the cycle.
04
Joint-venture exposure. The Sadara guarantee produced a $292M pretax charge in the first half of 2026 from a fair-value change alone. Dow has now put limits around it — a funding cap of about $1.3B and no further equity losses booked — but the guarantee itself remains an obligation tied to someone else's operations.

The dividend against free cash flow — the live question

What is reported, without forecasting: on October 15, 2026 the board declared $0.35 per share, payable December 11 to holders of record on November 30 — Dow's 461st consecutive dividend, by its own count, since 1912. That rate is half the $0.70 a quarter paid until the cut, declared on July 24, 2025; the first $0.35 dividend was paid on September 12, 2025. No change to the rate was announced with Q3 results.

The cash arithmetic, as reported: Dow paid $253M of dividends in Q3 2026 and $758M in the first nine months. Free cash flow was $1.13B in Q3 (about 4.5× the dividend) and $2.45B for the nine months (about 3.2×). On a trailing-twelve-month basis it is $2.18B against a dividend run-rate of about $1.01B a year (723.88M shares × $1.40), or coverage near 2.1×. Two qualifications: Q3 was helped by more than $400M of cash released from working capital (inventory and receivables), which does not repeat every quarter, and capex was $706M in the quarter, with the on-site workforce at the Alberta Path2Zero build set to double next year.

What would have to be true for the payout to hold at $0.35, framed as testable conditions rather than a prediction: (1) quarterly Operating EBITDA stays near the guided $1.5B, so that operating cash flow comfortably exceeds capex plus $253M; (2) capex stays manageable through the Path2Zero build — in July management said about 60% of that project's capex had been spent, and in October the CEO said phase one is slated for completion at the end of 2029; (3) the self-help lands — about $450M of Transform to Outperform benefits delivered by September against $700M for 2026 — and is not competed away in price; (4) debt keeps coming down, with about $1.4B of debt retired across the 2026 actions and no buybacks planned this year; and (5) no repeat of a Sadara-scale guarantee charge. If polyethylene settles well below current levels for two or three quarters, conditions (1) and (3) fail together, because low prices remove both the cash and the pricing power the savings were meant to build on. This page takes no view on which way it resolves.

Notable One-Off Items

The reported numbers still carry large one-offs. In Q3 2026, items Dow excludes from operating EPS came to −$0.09 a share net: Transform to Outperform costs (−$0.08), costs of the 2025 restructuring programme (−$0.03), a loss on buying back bonds early (−$0.06) and a litigation-related credit (+$0.08). That is the gap between GAAP EPS of $0.62 and operating EPS of $0.71. Earlier in the year: $526M of pretax restructuring charges in Q2 2026 ($445M severance, $81M costs to achieve), the $292M first-half Sadara guarantee charge, and a $150M tax benefit in Q2 tied to a payment from NOVA Chemicals. Restructuring and asset charges totalled $539M for the first nine months (10-Q). The European shutdown programme carries $630M–$790M of charges and about $500M of cash spending over four years, with decommissioning possibly running into 2029 — so charges like these will keep appearing for several quarters.

07 5-Year Stock Price Analysis

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY   ⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCTOBER 23, 2026
RSI measures how much of a period's price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 suggest the stock has risen far and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 suggests the opposite ("oversold"). It describes momentum, not value — an oversold stock can stay oversold for a very long time, as DOW's 2025 readings demonstrate.

Five years of this chart is a single story told twice. From the October 2021 month-end close of $55.97 to the October 23, 2026 close of $32.61, the shares are down 41.7% on price — and the dividend that made up much of a holder's return was halved along the way. The post-COVID chemical boom peaked at a $67.98 month-end close in May 2022; everything since has been the long unwind of that peak, punctuated by two violent counter-trend rallies.

The capitulation ran from September 2024 ($54.63) to September 2025 ($22.93) — a 58% decline in twelve months, through the dividend cut, with the 6-period RSI printing 7.2 in June 2025 and 5.8 in July 2025, extreme oversold readings. What followed was just as extreme the other way: from the December 2025 close of $23.38 to the March 2026 close of $41.65, the stock gained 78% in three months, with RSI reaching 79.8. It then gave back 34% into June 2026 ($27.36), bounced on the Q2 print, slid again to $27.54 at the end of September, and rose 18.4% from there to $32.61 through the Q3 print. The current reading of 54.4 is close to neutral.

Over the last year. From the October 2025 month-end close of $23.85 the shares are up 36.7% — a strong number that reflects buying near the trough, and one that contains both the 78% three-month rally and the 34% drawdown. With a beta of just 0.41, DOW has not been tracking the S&P 500; it has been tracking the polyethylene spread.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The trough is behind. Even after the expected step down from Q2, Q3 2026 Operating EBITDA of $1.55B was 79% above a year earlier, and Q4 is guided to hold about $1.5B with roughly $200M of upside if the autumn price increases stick. The cost base is being cut for good: Transform to Outperform is on track for $700M of benefits in 2026, Dow says it is on track for about $2B of total uplift by the end of 2027, and the first European closure is already lifting a segment. Cash is the proof: $1.13B of free cash flow in Q3 covered the dividend about 4.5×, and debt is down about $600M this year. At about 0.55× sales, an enterprise value about 7× trailing Operating EBITDA (our calculation) and a 4.29% yield, the shares do not price in a full recovery.

Bear Case

Nothing structural changed — only price did. Q3 EBITDA came in about $150M below the ~$1.7B management guided in July; volume fell again (−1% overall, −7% in Industrial Intermediates), and the earnings growth is a 14% local price increase Dow does not control. Part of the segment improvement is Sadara's losses no longer being booked, and Q3 cash flow was flattered by more than $400M released from working capital. Trailing GAAP EPS is still −$1.28, ROIC is 5.65%, long-term debt is $16.8B, and the Path2Zero workforce doubles next year. A dividend already halved once is not safe if polyethylene turns down again.

Top 3 Risks

01
Polyethylene price reversal. At about $50M of quarterly EBITDA per cent a pound, a ten-cent reversal would take roughly $500M a quarter off the guide. Two or three quarters at trough prices would use up the free-cash-flow cushion the dividend sits on, and no cost programme works that fast.
02
A second dividend cut. The rate was halved once, in July 2025, and the stock fell through that cut. The yield of 4.29% is about double the chemicals industry's, on a company with a trailing GAAP loss. Each quarterly declaration is a recurring event risk, not a formality.
03
Restructuring execution and its cash cost. $630M–$790M of charges and about $500M of cash over four years for the European shutdowns, decommissioning possibly running into 2029, and a $200M-a-year EBITDA target only half delivered by end-2027 — plus Transform to Outperform's job cuts still under way. If the closures slip or the savings are competed away in price, Dow will have spent the cash and kept the cost curve.
Industry Commentary
Global capacity overhang in commodity chemicals
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Dow's trailing-twelve-month net sales of $42.61B are up 4.2%, but the chemicals industry aggregate on stockanalysis.com still shows a −4.10% profit margin and an "n/a" P/E — the peer set as a whole is loss-making on trailing earnings. The industry's response is to close capacity, and Dow is running a textbook version of it: close Barry (UK siloxanes, mid-2026, done), close Böhlen (German cracker, Q4 2027) and Schkopau (German chlor-alkali/vinyls, Q4 2027), and keep running the lower-cost Terneuzen cracker in the Netherlands, restarted after being idled. Dow's stated aim when it announced the closures was to right-size European capacity, sell less on the open market and remove higher-cost, energy-intensive operations. That is a coherent plan for one company's cost curve. It does nothing about the capacity being added elsewhere.

European energy cost and the Middle East conflict
ONGOING HEADWIND

Everything Dow is closing is in Europe, and that is no coincidence: a cracker's competitiveness depends on feedstock and power costs, and European energy has been expensive since 2022 — the September 2022 close of $43.93, 35% below the May peak, is when the stock first priced it. Q3 2026 showed both sides. Higher European energy costs held back Industrial Intermediates, while the Barry closure helped lift Performance Materials & Coatings EBIT by $74M on a year earlier. Meanwhile the Middle East conflict has disrupted trade: the CFO said oil supply worries have eased but risks to energy trade flows remain elevated, and the CEO said some Middle East assets, including part of Sadara, are running at reduced rates.

What the bull and bear actually disagree about
EMERGING TAILWIND — CONTESTED

Both sides accept the same facts. Q2 2026 Operating EBITDA was $2.31B; Q3 was $1.55B; Q4 is guided to about $1.5B; Q3 local price was +14% and volume −1%; free cash flow was $2.45B in the first nine months against $758M of dividends. The disagreement is about which number is the baseline. The bull reads $1.5B as the new floor — the roughly $2B of uplift Dow expects by the end of 2027 meeting prices off their lows — which annualises to about $6B of EBITDA against an enterprise value of about $38.1B (our calculation). The bear reads it as a waypoint on the way back down, notes that the same asset base earned $868M in Q3 2025 and $703M in Q2 2025, and points out that the Q3 figure already missed management's own July guide. The next test is the Q4 print, and whether the October and November price increases show up in it. This page takes no position on the outcome.
