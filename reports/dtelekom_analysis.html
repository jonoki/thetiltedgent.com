01 Company Overview

Deutsche Telekom is Germany's former state phone monopoly, converted into a stock corporation at the start of 1995 and listed in 1996, and today one of the largest telecom groups in the world. It runs mobile and fixed-line networks in Germany and in a string of European countries, an IT-services arm (T-Systems), and — the part that dominates the numbers — a 54.2% ownership stake in T-Mobile US, America's growth leader in wireless. In 2025 it booked €119.1B of net revenue and €44.2B of adjusted EBITDA AL; 78% of revenue came from outside Germany. In the first half of 2026 the US segment supplied 65.0% of segment revenue and 66.2% of segment EBITDA AL.

That ownership structure is the whole debate. Because Deutsche Telekom controls T-Mobile US, it consolidates 100% of the American carrier's revenue and cash flow, but roughly 46% of that business belongs to other shareholders. At the October 2 closes, Deutsche Telekom's 54.3% stake (as of July 17) was worth about $95.3B against a total Deutsche Telekom market value of $144.9B — about two-thirds of the parent's price is the US stake (our calculation from T-Mobile US's share count and both closes). So the stock moves with T-Mobile US: when the American shares fell 28.9% over the twelve months to October 2, Deutsche Telekom fell 8.8% despite growing its own earnings. In 2026 a second question was added: press reports on April 22, citing two sources, said Deutsche Telekom was exploring a full merger with T-Mobile US through a new holding company, and on September 2 the activist fund Elliott was reported to have built a stake in Deutsche Telekom to oppose it. Management's answer at the August 6 results was a bigger buyback and a refusal to discuss the deal.

The latest print (Q2 2026, reported August 6): net revenue €29.93B (+4.4% reported, +3.3% organic), adjusted EBITDA AL €11.82B (+7.5%), adjusted EPS €0.58 (+12.7%) and free cash flow AL €5.03B (+3.1%). Against Deutsche Telekom's own compiled analyst consensus (as of June 21, 2026) that was revenue in line (−0.1%), EBITDA AL +1.0%, free cash flow +3.0% and adjusted net profit +5.9% ahead. Free-cash-flow guidance for 2026 was raised to around €20.0B; the EBITDA AL (around €47.5B) and adjusted EPS (around €2.20) targets were left unchanged. The stock rose 6.3% on the day, close to close.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Control of T-Mobile US Spectrum licences Germany's mobile leader by service revenue Fixed-line and fibre infrastructure A− credit rating Multi-country scale in Europe Sovereign-cloud credentials

Telecom moats are built from licences and concrete. Spectrum is auctioned in finite blocks, networks take decades and tens of billions to build, and once a household is on a contract it rarely leaves — Deutsche Telekom's German consumer contract churn was 0.9% in Q2. On top of that sits control of T-Mobile US, whose earnings growth management says is significantly outpacing its major US competitors, which supplies two-thirds of segment EBITDA AL and which pays growing dividends up to Bonn. The vulnerability is the other side of the same coin. Deutsche Telekom's growth, valuation and now its strategic agenda all run through a company it does not wholly own, in a market where competition is getting harder, and through a possible merger that would reshape who owns the parent.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The US premium has been shrinking. T-Mobile US's share price fell 28.9% in the twelve months to October 2. Höttges acknowledged in his August 6 remarks that T-Mobile "has lost much of its former valuation premium to its U.S. peers" and that "the market now appears to be looking more closely at customer and volume growth." Because the stake is about two-thirds of the parent's market value, every 10% move in T-Mobile US moves Deutsche Telekom's look-through value by roughly 6–7%.
02
Merger uncertainty. Press reports on April 22, 2026, citing two sources, said Deutsche Telekom was exploring a full merger with T-Mobile US via a new holding company; the stock fell 4.8% that day. Barclays wrote on July 1 that such a holding structure could come at the expense of Deutsche Telekom shareholders, and Bernstein estimated in September that buying out T-Mobile minorities at a 20% premium would dilute Deutsche Telekom holders by about 11%. Deutsche Telekom has not commented.
03
German broadband is stagnating. Broadband lines fell by 20,000 in Q2 and by 49,000 across 2025, while cable and alternative fibre builders compete for the same homes. Höttges told the press conference that fibre must be aligned "even more closely to demand, penetration, churn, wholesale utilization, and return on capital employed." Wholesale revenue is also down 2.6% after contractual price escalators lapsed.
04
Leadership turnover in the finance and technology seats. The technology board member left in March 2026, the CFO leaves in April 2027, and the product chief is departing at his own request. JPMorgan's Akhil Dattani wrote on September 2 that the CFO's exit came earlier than he expected and that changes at the top carry some risk given the team's record.

Notable One-Off Items

Two gaps matter when reading these figures. First, reported versus adjusted profit: H1 2026 reported net profit fell 17.7% to €4.49B while adjusted net profit rose 8.8% to €5.39B. The CFO attributed the reported decline to restructuring and integration costs at T-Mobile US (mainly UScellular) and to the absence of 2025's book gains from reversing impairments on the GD Towers and Glasfaser+ stakes. Second, consolidation: revenue, EBITDA, free cash flow and net debt include 100% of T-Mobile US, whose own buybacks (€6.1B in H1) add to group net debt even though they raise Deutsche Telekom's stake. Guidance is set at constant exchange rates of $1.13 per euro; the dollar stood at $1.136 on September 30, per the company.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of €17.40 to the October 2, 2026 close of €26.56, Deutsche Telekom gained 52.6% on price; adding back €4.01 of dividends paid over the period lifts that to about +75.7%, ahead of the DAX performance index's +65.3% over the same window. The shape is two halves. From the November 2021 low of €15.59 the stock more than doubled to a €34.75 month-end high in February 2025, as T-Mobile US's rise lifted the value of the stake. Since then it has gone sideways in a wide range: €26.89 in October 2025, back to €34.09 in February 2026, and down to €23.85 in June 2026, its lowest month-end since June 2024. Today's price is 23.6% below the February 2025 peak.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 44.5, neutral. Its low point in the series was 32.0 in June 2026, close to oversold; its high was 91.8 in November 2024, during the run-up. Both the 3-month average (about €27.02) and the 10-month average (about €28.23) sit above the current price, so the medium-term trend is still down even after the July–August rebound.

Relative performance

Against its home benchmark, Deutsche Telekom has done better over five years and worse over one. Over five years its price-plus-dividends return of about +75.7% beats the DAX performance index's +65.3%; over the last twelve months it returned about −5.4% (−8.8% on price) while the DAX gained +3.3%. The gap of the last year is almost entirely the subsidiary: T-Mobile US's shares fell 28.9% in dollars over the same twelve months (Oct 2, 2025 close $230.14 → $163.64). Deutsche Telekom fell far less than its main asset because about a third of its value is the business outside the US — the part Höttges argues is undervalued: he said the implied EV/EBITDA multiple of the business outside the US is "around 1-2 times lower" than for European peers.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 12.1x management's 2026 adjusted-EPS target for a group whose adjusted EPS grew 10.3% in the first half, that has met or beaten its guidance (FY2025 beat its own raised targets; 2026 free cash flow raised twice) and that holds an A− rating with leverage under its 2.75x ceiling. Two-thirds of the market value is a stake in T-Mobile US at a price that has fallen 28.9% in a year, so the rest of the group — Europe's 34-quarter organic EBITDA streak, the German mobile leader, T-Systems — is priced cheaply on top. Management is acting on that view with up to €5B of buybacks this year and a 3.77% dividend, and both a shelved merger (Elliott's ask) and a recovery in T-Mobile US would lift the shares.

Bear Case

The stock is a leveraged proxy for a US carrier whose valuation premium is fading: Höttges says US competition is getting fiercer, the market is looking harder at T-Mobile US's customer volumes, and Barclays and Deutsche Bank point to satellite competition. On top sits a governance overhang — a possible all-share merger that Bernstein estimates could dilute holders by about 11%, a German state that owns 28.6%, an activist on the register, and a finance and technology leadership in transition. At home, broadband lines are shrinking and fibre penetration is 17.5% after years of building. Reported EPS is falling (TTM €1.78) even as adjusted EPS rises, and €138.4B of net debt including leases leaves the group exposed to rates and the dollar. Bernstein's €28.10 target, barely above the price, is the bear view in numbers.

Top 3 Risks

01
T-Mobile US de-rating. About two-thirds of Deutsche Telekom's market value is its stake. A further fall in T-Mobile US — on weaker account growth, tougher competition or satellite rivals — hits the parent directly; in June the two fell together, and Deutsche Telekom's sharpest September fall came the session after a 5.6% drop in T-Mobile US.
02
An unfavourable merger. A holding-company merger with T-Mobile US would require paying minorities, change the parent's shareholder base and dilute the German state's stake. Until management says what it will do, Barclays and Bernstein both flag the risk that the terms would favour T-Mobile US holders over Deutsche Telekom's.
03
German fixed-line erosion. Broadband line losses, a weaker wholesale business and slow fibre take-up cap growth in the segment that is 22.7% of segment EBITDA AL, while capital spending on fibre continues. The CEO's own goal for the unit is to "monetize more".
Industry Commentary
US wireless: growth leader under pressure
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

T-Mobile US still grows faster than its rivals — Q2 2026 service revenue +8.9% and adjusted EBITDA AL +12.1% — but the market has shifted from rewarding profit growth to questioning volumes. In his August 6 remarks Höttges said that in the US "competition is getting fiercer" and that the group does "not buy growth at any cost". Deutsche Bank's Robert Grindle wrote in July that Starlink's satellite service and the Stargate AI project had dimmed Deutsche Telekom's standing among European telecoms. For a parent whose value is two-thirds US stake, US competition is the industry trend that matters most.

European telecoms: consolidation and capital returns
ONGOING DEBATE

Europe has many national operators per market, and consolidation is the recurring investment theme: Berenberg's Nick Lyall looked for its winners on September 30 and named Deutsche Telekom among eight favourites, calling AI fears overdone. Deutsche Telekom's own path is different — it already owns its US growth asset, and the question is whether to fold it in or keep returning cash. Elliott's reported position puts that choice in front of shareholders. Separately, EU member states proposed easing the deadline for replacing Huawei and ZTE equipment, according to a Reuters report Bernstein commented on September 30; per that report, Deutsche Telekom and Vodafone would benefit most.

Sovereign cloud and AI in Europe
EMERGING TAILWIND

European governments and companies want cloud and AI infrastructure run under European law. T-Systems is winning that work — Volkswagen's private cloud, the federal citizen app and a sovereign AI platform with SAP, and a sovereign cyber-defence service with Palo Alto Networks — and Höttges calls the unit "a building block of digital sovereignty in Europe." It is still small (3.5% of segment revenue, 0.8% of segment EBITDA AL) and order entry fell 13.4% in Q2, so this is an option on the future rather than a driver today. The company scheduled an AI Investor Day for October 5, 2026, the first session after this report's data date.
