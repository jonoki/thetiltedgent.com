01 Company Overview

eBay runs a global third-party marketplace: it does not own inventory, it matches buyers and sellers and clips a fee on the transaction. The unit that matters is GMV, not units or orders. FY2025 revenue was $11.10B, up 7.95% from $10.28B, and the TTM figure through September 2026 is $12.39B, up 15.7%. At $53.40B of market capitalisation the company ranks #220 in the S&P 500 — a mid-cap, not the internet blue chip it was in 2004.

The central debate on this page is whether eBay's 2025–26 re-acceleration is structural or arithmetic. Revenue growth went from +1.7% in FY2024 to +13.7% in the September 2026 quarter (the company reports +14%, or +13% FX-neutral) — but that quarter is the first to include Depop, the resale app eBay bought from Etsy for $1.4B in cash on July 30, 2026. GMV grew 15% (16% FX-neutral), while active buyers excluding Depop and Tise grew only 1%, to 136 million; counting them, buyers are 147 million, up 10%. That gap is the whole story: on its core marketplace eBay is not winning many new customers, it is monetising the ones it has harder — through advertising, through higher-value categories, and through a take rate of 13.8% (revenue ÷ GMV, our calculation). Sections 02 and 04 test how much further that can run.

The US business is carrying the company. GMV grew 27% year over year in Q3 2026 (22% excluding acquisitions) — roughly seven times the international rate — driven by eBay's "focus category" push into collectibles (trading cards, sneakers), motor parts and accessories, refurbished electronics and authenticated luxury. These categories share three useful properties: they are high average-selling-price, they are hard for a first-party retailer to stock, and they carry buyer-protection and authentication fees that lift the take rate. The margin profile is attractive because eBay holds no inventory and bears no fulfilment cost; profit grows faster than sales when fixed platform and marketing spend is spread over more volume.

Industry size: US retail e-commerce sales were $340.2B in Q2 2026, seasonally adjusted, or about $1.36T annualised (US Census Bureau, August 18, 2026). eBay's ~$50B annualised US GMV (Q3 2026 × 4) is a low-single-digit share.

This is the most important line in the business and the reason the take-rate story has legs. eBay sold $607M of advertising in Q3 2026, equal to 2.6% of GMV and about 19% of total revenue (our calculation on the $23.17B GMV and $3.206B revenue), with first-party ads (eBay's own promoted listings, $575M) growing 16%, down from +25% reported in Q2. Retail media (advertising a shop sells on its own site) is close to pure profit on each extra dollar — eBay is selling placement on inventory sellers already listed, with no incremental cost of goods and minimal incremental operating cost. Every 10 basis points (0.1% of GMV) of ad load added drops roughly $90M of near-100%-margin revenue onto the consolidated line at current volumes. That mechanism, more than buyer growth, explains how a business whose core buyer count grew 1% printed double-digit revenue growth.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Two-sided network effect 30-year supply liquidity Non-new / used inventory Zero inventory risk Authentication infrastructure 72% gross margin Retail-media flywheel

eBay's advantage is not technology and is no longer brand — it is supply that nobody else can stock. A 1987 Porsche taillight, a graded rookie card, a discontinued watch movement: these items exist in single units, held by individuals, and cannot be bought wholesale. Amazon's model is built on replenishable inventory and cannot serve them; a vertical marketplace can serve one category but not the cross-category liquidity of a thirty-year seller base. That is why eBay earns a 72.17% gross margin and a 24.50% ROIC while growing double digits: it captures a fee on liquidity it does not have to fund. The authentication programmes (sneakers, watches, trading cards, handbags) are a deliberate deepening of that moat — they convert the single biggest objection to buying an expensive used item into a paid eBay service.

Vulnerability 01
Buyer count is flat
136 million active buyers in Q3 2026 excluding Depop and Tise, up just 1% YoY and flat on the prior quarter, against 15% GMV growth (147 million, +10%, counting them). Growth is coming mostly from spend-per-buyer, monetisation and acquisitions, not from audience expansion on the core marketplace. Monetisation levers are finite; a customer base that does not grow eventually caps GMV.
Vulnerability 02
Take-rate ceiling
Take rate is already 13.8% ($3.21B revenue on $23.2B GMV, our calculation). Sellers are price-sensitive and portable — listings can move to Whatnot, Vinted, Facebook Marketplace or direct Shopify storefronts at near-zero switching cost. Every further point of take rate raises the incentive to leave, and eBay has no lock-in comparable to Amazon's fulfilment network.
Vulnerability 03
International stagnation
43% of revenue sits in a business whose GMV is growing 4%. Europe is a crowded e-commerce market, with Temu, Shein and Vinted all attacking eBay's value-and-used positioning simultaneously. International is growing at about a seventh of the US rate (27%, or 22% excluding acquisitions).
Vulnerability 04
Leverage and thin liquidity
Debt-to-equity of 1.53 with $6.73B of debt against $1.98B of cash and short-term investments (eBay counts $3.3B including long-term investments), and a current ratio of just 0.78. The business is cash-generative enough to carry it ($749M of free cash flow in Q3 2026 alone), but the buyback programme is being run on a balance sheet with little slack, which limits options if GMV rolls over.

Notable one-off items. The five-year earnings history is badly distorted and should not be read naively. FY2021 net income was $13.61B on $10.42B of revenue — a mathematical impossibility for an operating business, and the result of one-off gains on businesses sold in 2021 (eBay Classifieds to Adevinta in June; eBay Korea and part of the Adevinta stake in November), not trading profit. FY2022 swung to a $1.27B net loss as the value of the stakes it had kept from those sales fell. Operating income over the same span went $2.96B → $2.35B → $1.94B → $2.26B → $2.43B — a far more honest picture: eBay's core profit dipped and, at $2.43B in FY2025, was still below its 2021 level while revenue grew 6.5% in total. Separately, the gap between GAAP and non-GAAP is wide: Q3 2026 GAAP EPS was $1.11 against $1.51 non-GAAP, so non-GAAP is 36% higher; non-GAAP leaves out items such as stock-based compensation and restructuring. Trailing twelve-month operating income through September 2026 was $2.67B. FY2025 free cash flow of $1.43B was also unusually depressed relative to the $2.41B TTM figure, so the "+69% FCF growth" headline is partly a base effect.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

The five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $76.72 to the October 30, 2026 close of $121.64, eBay returned +58.6% on price alone — roughly 9.7% a year, plus a dividend that has run near 1%. That headline conceals a violent round trip. The stock lost 52.0% between October 2021 and the September 2022 low of $36.81, then spent all of 2023 dead flat in a $39–$50 band, then more than tripled from the 2023 base. Monthly 6-period RSI was at its most oversold in June 2022 at 6.1 (17.6 at the September 2022 price low), and peaked near 90 twice, in July 2025 and July 2026. It sits at 77.7 today: above the 70 line, so the stock has run hard.

The important structural observation is that nearly all of the five-year return was earned in the last sixteen months. From the June 2025 close of $74.46 to today's $121.64 the stock is up 63.4%; from October 2021 to June 2025 it was down 2.9% over nearly four years. Investors began paying more for each dollar of eBay's earnings when revenue growth crossed from low single digits into double digits in mid-2025 — which is exactly why the Q4 guidance step-down to 7–9% FX-neutral revenue growth matters so much.

Within its own Internet Retail industry, Etsy grew revenue 10.8% over the last twelve months against eBay's 15.7%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A 24.5% ROIC, 72% gross margin marketplace with irreplaceable used-and-collectible supply is trading at 26.4× trailing / 17.7× forward (2027 consensus) earnings, with revenue growth that has risen from 1.7% in FY2024 to 15.7% over the last twelve months. The advertising line is the lever: $607M a quarter at 2.6% of GMV, at essentially 100% incremental margin, requiring zero new buyers. US GMV growing 27% (22% excluding acquisitions) shows the focus-category strategy works where it has been fully deployed, and a 3.8% annual reduction in share count compounds whatever the operating business delivers. The $153 street-high target implies 26% upside.

Bear Case

Excluding Depop and Tise, active buyers grew 1%. Everything else is monetisation of a static audience plus an acquisition, and monetisation has a ceiling: at a 13.8% take rate, further extraction pushes sellers toward Vinted, Whatnot, Facebook Marketplace or their own storefronts, where switching costs are near zero. Management's own Q4 guide has FX-neutral revenue growth of 7–9% against 13% in Q3, and 5–7% excluding acquisitions, so the deceleration is already conceded. Forty-three percent of revenue is growing GMV at 4% in a crowded e-commerce market. Non-GAAP EPS runs 36% above GAAP. Jefferies' $75 target — 12× 2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS — is the bear marker, 38% below the price.

01
The active-buyer ceiling. 136 million buyers (excluding Depop and Tise) growing 1% against 15% GMV growth is the defining tension in this business. Monetisation per buyer cannot compound indefinitely; if the audience does not start growing, GMV growth converges on it within a few years regardless of how well the focus categories execute.
02
Guided deceleration becoming a trend. Q4 2026 guidance implies FX-neutral revenue growth of 7–9% (5–7% excluding acquisitions), down from 13% in Q3. If 2027 guides lower again, the 2025–26 gain — which delivered almost all of the five-year return — is exposed, and a return to a low-growth valuation becomes the base case rather than the bear case.
03
Balance-sheet slack, restructuring and FX. A 0.78 current ratio, 1.53 debt-to-equity and $6.73B of debt against $1.98B of cash and short-term investments leave little room if GMV turns down while the buyback is running, and $130–$150M of restructuring charges land mostly in Q4. Separately, with 43% of revenue international, a meaningful dollar move swings the reported growth rate by a point or more in either direction — which is precisely why the FX-neutral figure, not the headline, is the number to track each quarter.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The static-audience problem
Marketplaces are two-sided network businesses, and the received wisdom is that they compound because each new buyer attracts sellers and vice versa. eBay is running that flywheel in reverse gear: 136 million active buyers excluding Depop and Tise, +1% YoY, supporting +15% GMV and a take rate of 13.8%. This is what a mature network looks like when growth is engineered rather than organic. It is not necessarily bad — extraction from a loyal base can fund years of EPS growth — but it means the long-run growth rate is bounded by how much more eBay can charge, not by market size. The 2.6%-of-GMV advertising load is the clearest measure of how much room is left.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Europe under triple attack
eBay's international business — 43% of revenue, $10.6B of quarterly GMV, growing 4% — is concentrated in Germany and the UK, where three distinct competitive models are attacking simultaneously: ultra-low-price cross-border (Temu, Shein) on the value end, dedicated resale apps (Vinted) on the used end, and direct-to-consumer storefronts on the brand end. The 18-point spread between US GMV growth excluding acquisitions (+22%) and international (+4%) is the cleanest available measure of that pressure. Any European regulatory action on cross-border imports would cut both ways — relieving competitive pressure while raising eBay's own compliance burden on international listings.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Retail media as the real business
The structural shift across e-commerce over the last five years is that transaction platforms have discovered advertising is a better business than transactions. eBay is mid-way through that transition: $607M of ad revenue in Q3 2026, 2.6% of GMV, first-party growing 16%, and about 19% of total revenue. Bull framing: if ad load reaches the levels larger marketplaces have demonstrated, the incremental revenue arrives at near-100% margin and the forward multiple of 17.7× is too low for the resulting earnings mix. Bear framing: ad load is a tax on sellers and on buyer experience — pushing it materially higher on a buyer base that is not growing risks trading tomorrow's GMV for today's revenue line, and ad growth has already slowed from 25% to 16%. Which of those two readings is right is, ultimately, the entire investment case.
