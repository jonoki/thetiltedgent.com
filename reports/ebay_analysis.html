01 Company Overview

eBay runs a global third-party marketplace: it does not own inventory, it matches buyers and sellers and clips a fee on the transaction. The unit that matters is GMV, not units or orders. FY2025 revenue was $11.10B, up 7.95% from $10.28B, and the TTM figure through June 2026 is $12.01B, up 14.7%. At $46.74B of market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #524 globally — a reminder that eBay is now a mid-cap, not the internet blue chip it was in 2004.

The central debate on this page is whether eBay's 2025–26 re-acceleration is structural or arithmetic. Revenue growth went from +1.7% in FY2024 to +14.8% in the June 2026 quarter — a genuine inflection for a business that spent four years flat. But GMV grew 15% in Q2 2026 while active buyers grew only 2%, to 136 million. That gap is the whole story: eBay is not winning new customers, it is monetising the ones it has harder — through advertising, through higher-value categories, and through a take rate that has climbed to 14.0%. Sections 02 and 04 test how much further that can run.

GMV grew 24% year over year in Q2 2026 — roughly four times the international rate — driven by eBay's "focus category" push into collectibles (trading cards, sneakers), motor parts and accessories, refurbished electronics and authenticated luxury. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Two-sided network effect 30-year supply liquidity Non-new / used inventory Zero inventory risk Authentication infrastructure 72% gross margin Retail-media flywheel

eBay's advantage is not technology and is no longer brand — it is supply that nobody else can stock. A 1987 Porsche taillight, a graded rookie card, a discontinued watch movement: these items exist in single units, held by individuals, and cannot be bought wholesale. Amazon's model is built on replenishable inventory and cannot serve them; a vertical marketplace can serve one category but not the cross-category liquidity of a thirty-year seller base. That is why eBay earns a 72.05% gross margin and a 28.48% ROIC while growing high single digits: it captures a fee on liquidity it does not have to fund. The authentication programmes (sneakers, watches, trading cards, handbags) are a deliberate deepening of that moat — they convert the single biggest objection to buying an expensive used item into a paid eBay service.

Vulnerability 01
Buyer count is flat
136 million active buyers in Q2 2026, up just 2% YoY against 15% GMV growth. Growth is coming almost entirely from spend-per-buyer and monetisation, not from audience expansion. Monetisation levers are finite; a customer base that does not grow eventually caps GMV, and there is no disclosed evidence eBay has re-started buyer acquisition at scale.
Vulnerability 02
Take-rate ceiling
Take rate is already 14.0% ($3.13B revenue on $22.4B GMV). Sellers are price-sensitive and portable — listings can move to Whatnot, Vinted, Facebook Marketplace or direct Shopify storefronts at near-zero switching cost. Every further point of take rate raises the incentive to leave, and eBay has no lock-in comparable to Amazon's fulfilment network.
Vulnerability 03
International stagnation
44% of revenue is growing at 6%. Europe is the most contested e-commerce market in the world, with Temu, Shein and Vinted all attacking eBay's value-and-used positioning simultaneously. If international decelerates to flat, consolidated growth halves even with the US holding 24%.
Vulnerability 04
Leverage and thin liquidity
Debt-to-equity of 1.53 with $7.14B of total debt against $3.31B of cash, and a current ratio of just 1.02. The business is cash-generative enough to carry it ($3.04B operating cash flow), but the buyback programme is being run on a balance sheet with very little slack, which limits optionality if GMV rolls over.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

The five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $76.72 to the September 10, 2026 close of $105.03, eBay returned +36.9% on price alone — roughly 6.5% annualised, plus a dividend that has run near 1.2%. That headline conceals a violent round trip. The stock lost 52.0% between October 2021 and the September 2022 low of $36.81, then spent all of 2023 dead flat in a $39–$50 band, then more than doubled from the 2023 base. Monthly 6-period RSI bottomed at 17.6 in September 2022 — a genuine washout reading — and peaked near 90 twice, in July 2025 and July 2026. It sits at 61.4 today: constructive, not stretched.

The important structural observation is that nearly all of the five-year return was earned in the last fourteen months. From the June 2025 close of $74.46 to today's $105.03 the stock is up 41.1%; from October 2021 to June 2025 it was down 2.9% over nearly four years. This is a re-rating story that began precisely when revenue growth crossed from low single digits into double digits in mid-2025 — which is exactly why the Q3 guidance step-down to 8–10% FX-neutral matters so much.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A 28.5% ROIC, 72% gross margin marketplace with irreplaceable used-and-collectible supply is trading at 21.7× trailing / 16.4× forward earnings — a discount to the market despite revenue growth that has quadrupled to 14.7%. The advertising line is the lever: $596M a quarter at 2.7% of GMV, growing 25%, at essentially 100% incremental margin, requiring zero new buyers. US GMV growing 24% shows the focus-category strategy works where it has been fully deployed, and a 4.4% annual reduction in share count compounds whatever the operating business delivers. The $145 street-high target implies 38% upside.

Bear Case

Active buyers grew 2%. Everything else is monetisation of a static audience, and monetisation has a ceiling: at a 14.0% take rate, further extraction pushes sellers toward Vinted, Whatnot, Facebook Marketplace or their own storefronts, where switching costs are near zero. Management's own Q3 guide cuts FX-neutral revenue growth from 14% to 8–10%, so the deceleration is already conceded. Forty-four percent of revenue is growing at 6% in the most contested e-commerce market on earth. Strip the buyback and the 32% GAAP-to-non-GAAP adjustment and the underlying earnings growth is modest. Jefferies' $75 target — 12× 2026 EPS — is simply the pre-2025 multiple reapplied.

01
The active-buyer ceiling. 136 million buyers growing 2% against 15% GMV growth is the defining tension in this business. Monetisation per buyer cannot compound indefinitely; if the audience does not start growing, GMV growth converges on it within a few years regardless of how well the focus categories execute.
02
Guided deceleration becoming a trend. Q3 2026 guidance implies FX-neutral revenue growth of 8–10%, down from 14% in Q2. If Q4 guides lower again, the entire 2025–26 re-rating — which delivered almost all of the five-year return — is exposed, and the Jefferies-style reversion to a low-growth multiple becomes the base case rather than the bear case.
03
Balance-sheet slack and FX. A 1.02 current ratio, 1.53 debt-to-equity and $7.14B of debt against $3.31B of cash leave little room if GMV turns down while the buyback is running. Separately, with 44% of revenue international, a meaningful dollar move swings the reported growth rate by a point or more in either direction — which is precisely why the FX-neutral figure, not the headline, is the number to track each quarter.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The static-audience problem
Marketplaces are two-sided network businesses, and the received wisdom is that they compound because each new buyer attracts sellers and vice versa. eBay is running that flywheel in reverse-gear: 136 million active buyers, +2% YoY, supporting +15% GMV and a take rate that has reached 14.0%. This is what a mature network looks like when growth is engineered rather than organic. It is not necessarily bad — extraction from a loyal base can fund years of EPS growth — but it means the terminal growth rate is bounded by monetisation headroom, not by market size. The 2.7%-of-GMV advertising load is the clearest measure of how much headroom is left.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Europe under triple attack
eBay's international business — 44% of revenue, $10.7B of quarterly GMV, growing 6% — is concentrated in Germany and the UK, where three distinct competitive models are attacking simultaneously: ultra-low-price cross-border (Temu, Shein) on the value end, dedicated resale apps (Vinted) on the used end, and direct-to-consumer storefronts on the brand end. The 18-point spread between US GMV growth (+24%) and international (+6%) is the cleanest available measure of that pressure, and it is not closing. Any European regulatory action on cross-border imports would cut both ways — relieving competitive pressure while raising eBay's own compliance burden on international listings.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Retail media as the real business
The structural shift across e-commerce over the last five years is that transaction platforms have discovered advertising is a better business than transactions. eBay is mid-way through that transition: $596M of ad revenue in Q2 2026, 2.7% of GMV, first-party growing 25% / 24% FX-neutral, and roughly 19% of total revenue. Bull framing: if ad load reaches the levels larger marketplaces have demonstrated, the incremental revenue arrives at near-100% margin and the forward multiple of 16.4× is far too low for the resulting earnings mix. Bear framing: ad load is a tax on sellers and on buyer experience — pushing it materially higher on a buyer base that is already not growing risks trading tomorrow's GMV for today's revenue line. Which of those two readings is right is, ultimately, the entire investment case.
