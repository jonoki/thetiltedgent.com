01 Company Overview

Ecolab sells cleaning, sanitation, food-safety and water-treatment chemistry — but the chemistry is the smaller half of what the customer actually buys. It arrives bundled with a field technician who walks the plant, a dosing system that meters the product, and a measurement layer that tells the customer how much water, energy and labour the programme saved. FY2025 revenue was $16,081M, up 2.16% on FY2024's $15,741M; the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 2026 is $17,372M, up 9.3% on the twelve months to September 2025 ($15,890M, our sum of the four quarterly figures on stockanalysis.com), with the acceleration coming from acquisitions. The company is an S&P 500 member with a market capitalisation of $84.98B, and has paid a dividend for 89 consecutive years.

The debate on this page is not whether the business is good. It plainly is: gross margin has climbed from 38.25% in FY2022 to 44.04% on a trailing basis, and Q3 2026 organic growth of 7% was made of 5% price and 2% volume — a mix almost no industrial can print without losing customers. The debate is the price. ECL trades at 41.2× trailing and 33.9× forward earnings on mid-to-high single-digit organic growth, with a PEG of 2.50. And the company has levered up $4.75B to buy a data-centre liquid-cooling manufacturer (closed July 2, 2026), which changes both the growth story and the balance sheet. Whether margin expansion and a water-scarcity secular narrative carry a multiple in the high 30s to low 40s — and what happens to that 5% pricing if raw materials reflate — is the whole question.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
ON-SITE SERVICE TECHNICIANS INSTALLED DOSING EQUIPMENT FOOD-SAFETY AUDIT RISK PROCESS-SPECIFIC FORMULATION 48,000-PERSON FIELD FORCE DIGITAL MONITORING LAYER 89-YEAR DIVIDEND RECORD

The moat is the service relationship, not the molecule. Most of what Ecolab sells could be reverse-engineered by a competent formulator; almost none of it could be delivered by one. What the customer actually contracts for is a technician who knows that specific cooling tower, that specific dishmachine, that specific fermentation suite — who sets the dose, tunes it as feedwater chemistry drifts, and produces the documentation the customer's own auditors want. Switching means re-specifying the programme, swapping installed dispensing hardware, retraining staff, and absorbing food-safety or process-upset risk during the changeover, all to save a few percent on a line item that is a rounding error against the customer's labour and energy bill. That asymmetry is why the pricing shows up where it does.

And the pricing is the proof, not the claim. Q3 2026 organic growth of 7% decomposes into 5% price and 2% volume, which the release says came despite Middle East disruptions. A business without switching costs cannot raise price 4–5% while volumes still grow and keep the customer. The margin record says the same thing over a full cycle: gross margin bottomed at 38.25% in FY2022, the peak of the post-pandemic raw-material and energy shock, and has since expanded to 44.51% in FY2025 and 44.04% trailing — +579 basis points off the trough. Operating margin over the same window went 13.03% → 18.47%, with a 20% target for 2027 that Beck restated on the February 2026 call. That is the signature of a company that priced through an input-cost spike, then did not give the price back when inputs normalised. Ecolab's internal framing for this — the "eROI" value-selling model, which quantifies the customer's water, energy and labour savings so the conversation is about total value delivered rather than price per gallon — is the mechanism management credits. The observable output is the price line.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The pricing gift can be taken back. The +579 basis points of gross-margin expansion since FY2022 is partly genuine mix and productivity and partly the arithmetic of holding price while input costs fell. If raw materials reflate, Ecolab's history says it can re-price — but with a lag, and 5% pricing is already an unusually high ask. The Q3 release says pricing offset higher commodity costs and that organic gross margin held at 44.9% against 44.8% a year earlier, which is the first sign that costs are moving up again. A reflation that outruns the price lag compresses the very margin line the multiple is built on.
▸
About 15% of the portfolio needs work. On the February 10, 2026 call (Q4 2025 results), Beck said 85% of Ecolab's businesses "are doing great" and spoke of "the 15% of the portfolio that needs work", naming the paper and basic businesses, which he described as in a down part of the cycle. So the 7% headline growth blends a strong core with a remaining 15% that is being repaired rather than grown.
▸
The balance sheet just changed character. Short- plus long-term debt was $13,192M at September 30, 2026, against $8,236M at December 31, 2025, with cash down to $722M from $1,956M a year earlier (Q3 release). Debt-to-equity is 1.33 on stockanalysis.com's definition, and management guided pro-forma net debt/EBITDA to roughly 3× at CoolIT close, returning to 2× by the end of year two. Net interest expense was $124.8M in Q3, up $70M, mainly from the acquisition financing. A company whose appeal is an 89-year dividend record and predictable compounding has taken on cycle risk to buy an AI-adjacent hardware business.
▸
Hardware dilutes the service moat's margin. The acquisitions driving reported growth — Ovivo Electronics, now CoolIT — sell engineered equipment, not metered consumables. Global Water's operating margin is 18.0%, the lowest of the four segments, even with sales up 20%; the release says acquisitions cut Ecolab's adjusted gross margin by 70 basis points in Q3. Equipment revenue is lumpier, carries lower gross margin, and does not come with the same switching cost. The more of it Ecolab buys, the less the razor/razorblade description fits the consolidated business.

Middle East disruption. The Q3 release says volume grew 2% despite Middle East disruptions; in Q2 management had put the drag at about one point of volume growth. The Q3 release does not give a number for it.

Segment names. The Q2 and Q3 2026 releases report four segments including "Global Life Sciences," while the Q1 2026 10-Q named the fourth segment "Global Healthcare and Life Sciences." In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $222.22 to the October 30, 2026 close of $302.98, ECL returned +36.3% in price over five years — a compound annual rate of about 6.4%, plus roughly one point a year of dividend. That is a modest outcome for a business whose gross margin expanded nearly 600 basis points over the same window, and it tells you where the value went: into a big fall in the multiple and then a recovery, not into a straight line. The path had three acts. Act one was the 2022 input-cost shock: the stock fell 35.0% from October 2021 to the September 2022 close of $144.42, as raw-material and energy inflation crushed gross margin to 38.25% and the market questioned whether the pricing model would hold. Act two was the repricing of that question — a 113.5% advance from $144.42 to the February 2026 high monthly close of $308.35 as margin recovered every single year. Act three is the last eight months: the stock fell to $256.00 by May after the CoolIT announcement, then recovered to $302.98, 1.7% below the February peak.

Relative performance. ECL's beta is 0.88 and its 52-week price change is +17.8% (October 30, 2025 close $257.10 to $302.98) — against nine-month adjusted EPS growth of 9% ($5.97 versus $5.46) in the Q3 release. A stock that rises faster than its earnings is, mechanically, a stock whose multiple is expanding: trailing P/E has moved from 37.5× at the September 4 edition to 41.2× now. That is the market answering the question this page opened with, in the bulls' favour for now.

Inflection points

Oct 2021
$222.22 — start of the window
Pre-shock. Gross margin was on its way down to the FY2022 trough; the market had not yet tested whether Ecolab could price through an input-cost spike.
Starting point of the 5-year window
Sep 2022
$144.42 — cycle low, −35.0% from Oct 2021

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the price/volume split, because it is the whole argument. Q3 2026 organic sales grew 7%, made of 5% price and 2% volume, and management guided Q4 organic growth to 6–7%. So the honest characterisation of Ecolab's growth today is: a low-single-digit volume business with an extraordinary ability to raise price. That is not a criticism. It is precisely what a razor/razorblade model with a service moat should look like, and it is far more valuable than the reverse. But it does mean about 70% of the growth comes from charging existing customers more, every year, and having them stay, rather than from taking share or selling more units.

The margin expansion is real and it is the bull case's entire load-bearing wall. Gross margin: 38.25% (FY2022) → 40.39% → 43.50% → 44.51% (FY2025) → 44.04% trailing. Operating margin: 13.03% → 14.27% → 16.96% → 18.35% → 18.47% trailing, against a 20% target for 2027. On roughly $17.9B of 2026 consensus revenue, each additional point of operating margin is about $179M of operating income — so closing the remaining 153 basis points to the 2027 target is worth roughly $274M, or about 8.5% of trailing operating income, without a single extra dollar of sales. That is the mechanism by which 5–7% organic growth becomes the 8–9% adjusted EPS growth guided for 2026, and operating margin has widened in each of the three years since FY2022.

What happens if raw materials reflate? This is the sharpest question on the page, and there is a clean historical answer sitting in the data: FY2022. In that year input costs spiked, gross margin fell 268 basis points to 38.25%, EPS declined 2.56%, and the stock fell 35% from its October 2021 level. Ecolab did re-price — it just did so with a lag of roughly four to six quarters, and shareholders wore the gap. The Q3 2026 release says pricing offset higher commodity costs, which means input costs are already rising again. The difference this time is that Ecolab is entering the cycle with pricing already at 5%, not at zero. There is less headroom in the price line before customers push back, and the paper and basic businesses that management says need work are the obvious place for that pushback to show up first. A reflation of the FY2022 magnitude would not break the business; on the evidence it would cost roughly two years of earnings growth and a third of the share price. At 41.2× trailing, that is a lot of downside to carry for a mid-to-high single-digit grower.

The data-centre story — what is actually disclosed, versus the narrative. Be precise here, because the narrative has run well ahead of the disclosure. What Ecolab has actually put a number on: Global High-Tech grew 63% organically in Q3 2026 (29% in Q2, when it was "approaching $1.5 billion annualised sales"); Ecolab Digital grew 34% to $129M; the CoolIT purchase is said to double the Global High-Tech market opportunity from $5B to $10B; and the target is $4B of High-Tech sales by 2030 at 25% operating margins. What is not disclosed: any standalone data-centre revenue line, any split between microelectronics fabs and data centres inside High-Tech, any customer concentration, any backlog. High-Tech is not a reportable segment — it sits inside Global Water, whose organic growth was 9% in Q3 and whose operating margin, 18.0%, is the lowest of the four segments. So: at the Q2 run rate of ~$1.5B annualised, High-Tech was roughly 8–9% of company sales. Growing it to $4B by 2030 requires a ~28% compound rate for four years, and Ecolab just paid 29× forward EBITDA to buy the hardware needed to try. The opportunity is genuine; the disclosure is thin; and the market has already been asked to fund the ambition with $4.75B of debt.

Valuation — pinning it down. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $7.35, ECL trades at 41.2×. (ii) On the 2026 adjusted guidance midpoint of $8.15, at 37.2×; on 2026 consensus of $8.16, 37.1×. (iii) On 2027 consensus EPS of $9.41, at 32.2× — and note that 2027 is the first year the 20% operating-margin target is supposed to land, while CoolIT is still dilutive until 2028. (iv) On trailing free cash flow of $1.80B against a $84.98B market cap, roughly 47× — and on enterprise value of $98.19B, about 55× FCF. That last figure is the uncomfortable one: this is a capital-intensive business where capex runs about $1.23B against $3.03B of operating cash flow, so FCF conversion is only 0.86× net income. EV/EBITDA of 22.94 (stockanalysis.com's 23.29 recomputed at the Oct 30 close) is the cleanest cross-cycle read. The defensible conclusion is that ECL is priced for the 2027 margin target to be hit and for High-Tech to compound at something close to the 2030 plan. Hit both and 32.2× 2027 earnings on an accelerating business is defensible. Miss either — a raw-material reflation that eats the margin path, or a High-Tech ramp that stalls after a $4.75B outlay — and there is no growth rate underneath to catch the multiple. The PEG of 2.50 is the compressed version of that sentence.

Bull Case

The moat is verified by the price line, not asserted: 5% pricing on 2% volume in Q3, with gross margin up 579 basis points from the FY2022 trough and operating margin up 544 to 18.47% against a 20% target for 2027. Life Sciences turned 17% organic growth into 49% operating-income growth and 500 basis points of margin expansion — proof that profit grows faster than sales when volume shows up. Global High-Tech grew 63% organically in Q3 toward a $4B/2030 target with a $10B addressable opportunity after CoolIT, and Ecolab Digital is growing 34%. Add a 0.88 beta, 1.74% short interest, 12.14% ROIC, an 89-year dividend record and a Street where the low price target is within 1% of the price, and you have a steady, defensive business buying itself a new line of growth.

Bear Case

You are paying 41.2× trailing and 37× guided earnings, with a PEG of 2.50, for 7% organic growth of which five-sevenths is price. Management itself says about 15% of the portfolio needs work. FY2022 is the live precedent for what an input-cost reflation does: margin down, EPS down, stock down 35% — and the Q3 release says commodity costs are higher. The $4.75B CoolIT deal at 29× forward EBITDA took short- plus long-term debt from $8.2B to $13.2B and is dilutive until 2028; the stock fell 13.7% from the February close in the month it was announced and has not reclaimed that close. Free cash flow is only 0.86× net income, so this business converts less cash than its margin profile suggests. And the stock is up 17.8% in a year against nine-month adjusted EPS growth of 9% — the price has already run ahead of earnings.

  Industry Commentary
Input Costs Are Turning Again
ONGOING HEADWIND
Specialty chemistry passes raw-material costs on to customers, but with a lag, and the lag is where shareholder money is made or lost. Ecolab's Q3 2026 release says pricing offset higher commodity costs — the sign that the input cycle has turned. The FY2022 precedent is the reference: gross margin fell to 38.25%, EPS declined 2.56%, and the stock lost 35% from its October 2021 level before the price increases caught up. The structural difference now is that Ecolab enters with pricing already running at 5%, versus starting from near zero in 2021. That is a stronger starting position and a thinner remaining cushion at the same time. Watch the organic gross-margin line — 44.9% in Q3 2026 against 44.8% a year earlier — as the earliest indicator.
AI Infrastructure Is Buying Water Chemistry
EMERGING TAILWIND
Data centres consume water in two distinct ways — evaporative cooling and, increasingly, closed-loop direct-to-chip liquid cooling — and both require treated fluid, corrosion control, biological control and monitoring. The numbers from the Q3 release: Global High-Tech +63% organic; Ecolab Digital +34% to $129M; the CoolIT acquisition doubling the stated market opportunity from $5B to $10B; a $4B-by-2030 sales target at 25% operating margins. The gaps: High-Tech is not a reportable segment, no standalone data-centre revenue is disclosed, and the segment it sits inside has the lowest operating margin of the four, at 18.0%. The tailwind is real; the disclosure is not yet good enough to underwrite the 2030 number.
Consolidation Has Removed the Public Comparables
STRUCTURAL SHIFT
The competitive set has gone dark. Solenis absorbed Diversey in 2023 and is private-equity owned; Veolia Water Technologies is a division inside a much larger utility group; Kurita and Rentokil are listed outside the US. There is now no clean listed pure-play against which to mark Ecolab's margins, growth or multiple — which is precisely why the Industry Avg column in Section 04 is left blank here rather than estimated. Bull framing: scarcity value. If you want listed exposure to industrial water treatment with a service moat and a data-centre option, ECL is close to the only way to buy it, and scarcity supports a premium multiple indefinitely. Bear framing: the absence of comparables is also the absence of discipline. A 41.2× trailing multiple on a mid-single-digit organic grower is easier to sustain when nothing sits beside it to make the comparison uncomfortable — and premiums built on scarcity rather than on returns tend to vanish abruptly when the operating story wobbles.
