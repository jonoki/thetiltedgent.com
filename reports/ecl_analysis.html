01 Company Overview

Ecolab sells cleaning, sanitation, food-safety and water-treatment chemistry — but the chemistry is the smaller half of what the customer actually buys. It arrives bundled with a field technician who walks the plant, a dosing system that meters the product, and a measurement layer that tells the customer how much water, energy and labour the programme saved. FY2025 revenue was $16,081M, up 2.16% on FY2024's $15,741M; the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $16,843M, up 7.11%, with the acceleration coming from acquisitions. The company ranks #316 globally by market capitalisation at $78.29B, and has paid a dividend for 89 consecutive years.

The debate on this page is not whether the business is good. It plainly is: gross margin has climbed from 38.25% in FY2022 to 44.21% on a trailing basis, and Q2 2026 organic growth of 5% was made of 4% price and 1% volume — a mix almost no industrial can print without losing customers. The debate is the price. ECL trades at 37.5× trailing and 32.3× forward earnings on mid-single-digit organic growth, with a PEG of 2.41. And the company has just levered up $4.75B to buy a data-centre liquid-cooling manufacturer, which changes both the growth story and the balance sheet. Whether margin expansion and a water-scarcity secular narrative carry a low-to-mid-30s multiple — and what happens to that 4% pricing if raw materials reflate — is the whole question.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
ON-SITE SERVICE TECHNICIANS INSTALLED DOSING EQUIPMENT FOOD-SAFETY AUDIT RISK PROCESS-SPECIFIC FORMULATION 48,000-PERSON FIELD FORCE DIGITAL MONITORING LAYER 89-YEAR DIVIDEND RECORD

The moat is the service relationship, not the molecule. Most of what Ecolab sells could be reverse-engineered by a competent formulator; almost none of it could be delivered by one. What the customer actually contracts for is a technician who knows that specific cooling tower, that specific dishmachine, that specific fermentation suite — who sets the dose, tunes it as feedwater chemistry drifts, and produces the documentation the customer's own auditors want. Switching means re-specifying the programme, swapping installed dispensing hardware, retraining staff, and absorbing food-safety or process-upset risk during the changeover, all to save a few percent on a line item that is a rounding error against the customer's labour and energy bill. That asymmetry is why the pricing shows up where it does.

And the pricing is the proof, not the claim. Q2 2026 organic growth of 5% decomposes into 4% price and 1% volume — and management guided pricing to 5–6% in the second half. A business without switching costs cannot raise price 4–6% while volumes are roughly flat and keep the customer. The margin record says the same thing over a full cycle: gross margin bottomed at 38.25% in FY2022, the peak of the post-pandemic raw-material and energy shock, and has since expanded to 44.51% in FY2025 and 44.21% trailing — +596 basis points off the trough. Operating margin over the same window went 13.03% → 18.44%, with a stated 20% target for 2027. That is the signature of a company that priced through an input-cost spike, then did not give the price back when inputs normalised. Ecolab's internal framing for this — the "eROI" value-selling model, which quantifies the customer's water, energy and labour savings so the conversation is about total value delivered rather than price per gallon — is the mechanism management credits; no eROI-specific figures were disclosed in any source fetched, so it is described here as a selling model and not scored. The observable output is the price line.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The pricing gift can be taken back. The +596bp gross-margin expansion since FY2022 is partly genuine mix and productivity and partly the arithmetic of holding price while input costs fell. If raw materials reflate, Ecolab's history says it can re-price — but with a lag, and the guided 5–6% second-half pricing is already an unusually high ask. Management explicitly framed Q2 as "accelerating pricing offset higher commodity costs," which is an admission that costs are moving up again. A reflation that outruns the price lag compresses the very margin line the multiple is built on.
▸
Roughly 15% of sales is shrinking. Management disclosed on the Q2 call that underperforming businesses representing about 15% of sales are in low-single-digit decline. That is a large drag hiding inside a 5% organic number, and it means the healthy 85% is growing meaningfully faster than the headline — which is flattering — while also meaning a sixth of the portfolio is being managed rather than grown.
▸
The balance sheet just changed character. Total debt was $8,187M at March 31, 2026. The trailing statistics now show $13.92B of total debt against $5.14B of cash, debt-to-equity of 1.38, and management guided pro-forma net debt/EBITDA to roughly 3× at CoolIT close, returning to 2× by the end of year two. A company whose appeal is a 89-year dividend record and predictable compounding has taken on cycle risk to buy an AI-adjacent hardware business.
▸
Hardware dilutes the service moat's margin. The acquisitions driving reported growth — Ovivo Electronics, now CoolIT — sell engineered equipment, not metered consumables. Global Water's operating margin fell 50 basis points to 15.7% even as its reported sales rose 12%. Equipment revenue is lumpier, carries lower gross margin, and does not come with the same switching cost. The more of it Ecolab buys, the less the razor/razorblade description fits the consolidated business.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $222.22 to the September 4, 2026 close of $279.28, ECL returned +25.7% in price over five years — a compound annual rate of about 4.7%, plus roughly one point a year of dividend. That is a modest outcome for a business whose gross margin expanded 600 basis points over the same window, and it tells you where the value went: into multiple compression and then re-expansion, not into a straight line. The path had three acts. Act one was the 2022 input-cost shock: the stock fell 35.0% from October 2021 to the September 2022 close of $144.42, as raw-material and energy inflation crushed gross margin to 38.25% and the market questioned whether the pricing model would hold. Act two was the repricing of that question — a 113.5% advance from $144.42 to the February 2026 high monthly close of $308.35 as margin recovered every single year. Act three is the last seven months: −9.4% from the February peak, with the whole drawdown dating from the CoolIT announcement.

Relative performance. A precise total-return comparison against the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ-100 over the identical five-year window could not be verified from the sources fetched and is deliberately not stated. What can be said from verified data on this page: ECL's beta is 0.89 and its 52-week price change is +2.21% — over a period in which the company grew adjusted EPS at a double-digit rate and raised guidance twice. A stock that goes nowhere for a year while earnings compound is, mechanically, a stock whose multiple is contracting. That is the market quietly answering the question this page opened with, and it argues the low-30s forward multiple is being defended rather than expanded.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the price/volume split, because it is the whole argument. Q2 2026 organic sales grew 5%, made of 4% price and 1% volume — and management guided pricing to 5–6% in the second half against a total organic guide of 6–7%, which implies volume stays roughly flat to slightly positive. Strip the roughly 1-point Middle East volume headwind management called out, and underlying volume is nearer 2%. So the honest characterisation of Ecolab's growth today is: a low-single-digit volume business with an extraordinary ability to raise price. That is not a criticism. It is precisely what a razor/razorblade model with a service moat should look like, and it is far more valuable than the reverse. But it does mean the growth is not coming from taking share or from expanding units — it is coming from charging existing customers more, every year, and having them stay.

The margin expansion is real and it is the bull case's entire load-bearing wall. Gross margin: 38.25% (FY2022) → 40.39% → 43.50% → 44.51% (FY2025) → 44.21% trailing. Operating margin: 13.03% → 14.27% → 16.96% → 18.35% → 18.44% trailing, against a stated 20% target for 2027. On roughly $17.9B of 2026 consensus revenue, each additional point of operating margin is about $179M of operating income — so closing the remaining 156 basis points to the 2027 target is worth roughly $280M, or about 9% of trailing operating income, without a single extra dollar of sales. That is the mechanism by which 5% organic growth becomes 7–10% EPS growth, and it has worked for four consecutive years.

What happens if raw materials reflate? This is the sharpest question on the page, and there is a clean historical answer sitting in the data: FY2022. In that year input costs spiked, gross margin fell 268 basis points to 38.25%, EPS declined 2.56%, and the stock fell 35% from its October 2021 level. Ecolab did re-price — it just did so with a lag of roughly four to six quarters, and shareholders wore the gap. Management's Q2 2026 language, "accelerating pricing offset higher commodity costs," says input costs are already rising again. The difference this time is that Ecolab is entering the cycle with pricing already at 4% and guided to 5–6%, not at zero. There is less headroom in the price line before customers push back, and the 15% of the portfolio management says is in low-single-digit decline is the part where that pushback would show up first. A reflation of the FY2022 magnitude would not break the business; on the evidence it would cost roughly two years of earnings growth and a third of the share price. At 37.5× trailing, that is a lot of downside to carry for a mid-single-digit grower.

The data-centre story — what is actually disclosed, versus the narrative. Be precise here, because the narrative has run well ahead of the disclosure. What Ecolab has actually put a number on: Global High-Tech grew 29% organically in Q2 2026 and is "approaching $1.5 billion annualised sales"; Ecolab Digital grew 27%; the CoolIT purchase is said to double the Global High-Tech market opportunity from $5B to $10B; and the target is $4B of High-Tech sales by 2030 at 25% operating margins. What is not disclosed: any standalone data-centre revenue line, any split between microelectronics fabs and data centres inside High-Tech, any customer concentration, any backlog. High-Tech is not a reportable segment — it sits inside Global Water, whose overall organic growth was 4% and whose operating margin fell 50 basis points. So: at ~$1.5B annualised, High-Tech is roughly 8–9% of company sales. Growing it to $4B by 2030 requires a ~28% compound rate for four years, and Ecolab just paid 29× forward EBITDA to buy the hardware needed to try. The opportunity is genuine; the disclosure is thin; and the market has already been asked to fund the ambition with $4.75B of debt.

Valuation — pinning it down. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $7.45, ECL trades at 37.5×. (ii) On the 2026 adjusted guidance midpoint of $8.15, at 34.3×; on 2026 consensus of $8.17, 34.2×. (iii) On 2027 consensus EPS of $9.42, at 29.6× — and note that 2027 is the first year the 20% operating-margin target is supposed to land, while CoolIT is still dilutive until 2028. (iv) On trailing free cash flow of $1.87B against a $78.29B market cap, roughly 42× — and on enterprise value of $87.07B, about 46× FCF. That last figure is the uncomfortable one: this is a capital-intensive business where capex runs about $1.19B against $3.06B of operating cash flow, so FCF conversion is only 0.88× net income. EV/EBITDA of 21.05 is the cleanest cross-cycle read. The defensible conclusion is that ECL is priced for the 2027 margin target to be hit and for High-Tech to compound at something close to the 2030 plan. Hit both and 29.6× 2027 earnings on an accelerating business is defensible. Miss either — a raw-material reflation that eats the margin path, or a High-Tech ramp that stalls after a $4.75B outlay — and there is no growth rate underneath to catch the multiple. The PEG of 2.41 is the compressed version of that sentence.

Bull Case

The moat is verified by the price line, not asserted: 4% pricing on 1% volume, guided to 5–6%, with gross margin up 596 basis points from the FY2022 trough and operating margin up 541 to 18.44% against a 20% target for 2027. Life Sciences turned 15% organic growth into 53% operating-income growth and 550bps of margin expansion — proof of the operating leverage. Global High-Tech is compounding at 29% toward a $4B/2030 target with a $10B addressable opportunity after CoolIT, and Ecolab Digital is growing 27%. Add a 0.89 beta, 1.24% short interest, 13.38% ROIC, an 89-year dividend record and a Street where the lowest price target sits above spot, and you have a defensive compounder buying itself a growth vertical.

Bear Case

You are paying 37.5× trailing and 34× guided earnings, with a PEG of 2.41, for 5% organic growth of which four-fifths is price. Roughly 15% of the portfolio is in low-single-digit decline. FY2022 is the live precedent for what an input-cost reflation does: margin down, EPS down, stock down 35% — and management has already flagged "higher commodity costs." The $4.75B CoolIT deal at 29× forward EBITDA took total debt from $8.2B to $13.9B and is dilutive until 2028; the stock fell 13.7% from the February close in the month it was announced and has not reclaimed that high. Free cash flow is only 0.88× net income, so this compounder converts less cash than its margin profile suggests. And the stock is up 2.21% in a year while earnings grew double digits — the multiple is already contracting.

  Industry Commentary
Input Costs Are Turning Again
ONGOING HEADWIND
Specialty chemistry is a passthrough business with a lag, and the lag is where shareholder money is made or lost. Ecolab's own Q2 2026 framing — "accelerating pricing offset higher commodity costs" — is the tell that the input cycle has turned. The FY2022 precedent is the reference: gross margin fell to 38.25%, EPS declined 2.56%, and the stock lost 35% from its October 2021 level before the price increases caught up. The structural difference now is that Ecolab enters with pricing already running at 4% and guided to 5–6%, versus starting from near zero in 2021. That is a stronger starting position and a thinner remaining cushion at the same time. Watch the organic gross-margin line — 44.9% in Q2 2026, up only 10 basis points year over year — as the earliest indicator.
AI Infrastructure Is Buying Water Chemistry
EMERGING TAILWIND
Data centres consume water in two distinct ways — evaporative cooling and, increasingly, closed-loop direct-to-chip liquid cooling — and both require treated fluid, corrosion control, biological control and monitoring. Ecolab's claim is that it is "the only company with integrated solutions" spanning water production, powering and cooling for this infrastructure. The verified numbers: Global High-Tech +29% organic, approaching $1.5B annualised; Ecolab Digital +27%; the CoolIT acquisition doubling the stated market opportunity from $5B to $10B; a $4B-by-2030 sales target at 25% operating margins. The verified gaps: High-Tech is not a reportable segment, no standalone data-centre revenue is disclosed, and the segment it sits inside saw operating margin fall 50 basis points. The tailwind is real; the disclosure is not yet good enough to underwrite the 2030 number.
Consolidation Has Removed the Public Comparables
STRUCTURAL SHIFT
The competitive set has gone dark. Solenis absorbed Diversey in 2023 and is private-equity owned; Veolia Water Technologies is a division inside a much larger utility group; Kurita and Rentokil are listed outside the US. There is now no clean listed pure-play against which to mark Ecolab's margins, growth or multiple — which is precisely why the Industry Avg column in Section 04 is left blank here rather than estimated. Bull framing: scarcity value. If you want listed exposure to industrial water treatment with a service moat and a data-centre option, ECL is close to the only way to buy it, and scarcity supports a premium multiple indefinitely. Bear framing: the absence of comparables is also the absence of discipline. A 37.5× trailing multiple on a mid-single-digit organic grower is easier to sustain when nothing sits beside it to make the comparison uncomfortable — and premiums built on scarcity rather than on returns tend to re-rate abruptly when the operating story wobbles.
