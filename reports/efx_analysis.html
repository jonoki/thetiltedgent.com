01 Company Overview

Equifax is one of the three US credit bureaus — alongside Experian and TransUnion — and, less famously, the owner of The Work Number, a database of employment and income records that lenders, landlords and government agencies use to verify what a person earns. It reports three segments: Workforce Solutions, U.S. Information Solutions (USIS) and International. FY2025 revenue was $6.08B, up 6.9%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $5.32; trailing-twelve-month revenue has since reached $6.59B, up 10.9% on the same four quarters a year earlier.

The business is still doing fine; for a month the stock priced a rule that has not been written. Q3 2026 revenue rose 10% to $1.693B and adjusted EPS of $2.26 (up 11%) came in above the top of the company's own range, though management trimmed full-year revenue guidance to $6.646–6.676B (from $6.71–6.78B) because US mortgage activity weakened late in the quarter. Before the print the shares had fallen to $137.24 at the September month-end, down 27.4% in the month and the lowest month-end close on the chart. The reason is that Equifax's fastest-growing line, US mortgage, sits inside a pricing structure a single regulator can rewrite. On September 3–4, 2026, Federal Housing Finance Agency (FHFA) Director Bill Pulte directed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to approve all lenders to use VantageScore, said the bureaus "have been overcharging Americans for far too long," and wrote that the agency is "seriously considering bi-merge" — mortgage files built from two bureaus instead of three. On October 1 Bloomberg reported that the FHFA plans to make that move; the announcement expected at the Mortgage Bankers Association's convention the week of October 12 did not come. On results day EFX rose 11.3%, and it closed October 21 at $175.57 — down 19.1% this year, 23.6% over twelve months and 42.8% below the August 2024 month-end high of $307.13. Equifax puts the revenue at stake if Fannie and Freddie loans move to two bureaus at about $80 million a year, roughly 1.2% of its 2026 revenue guidance. As of this report's date, lenders must still deliver tri-merge reports to Fannie and Freddie. The market is pricing a threat, not a rule.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

The Work Number: 217M records Three-bureau oligopoly Tri-merge mortgage requirement Co-owner of VantageScore Employer-contributed data network Regulated data & compliance scale Workforce margin (51% adj. EBITDA)

Equifax's moat has two layers, and they are not equally safe. The first is data nobody else has: The Work Number is built from payroll feeds that employers contribute because Equifax handles their verification paperwork, and every new employer makes the database more valuable to the next lender. That layer is private and contractual. The second is a rule: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac require a report from all three bureaus on every conforming mortgage. That layer is public and political — and 2025–26 has shown how quickly it can be questioned. The FHFA reversed a planned move to bi-merge in July 2025, reaffirmed tri-merge in November 2025, and on September 3, 2026 said it is "seriously considering bi-merge" again; on October 1 Bloomberg reported a plan to make the move. A moat that depends on a regulator's preference is a moat with a drawbridge someone else controls.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Bi-merge. Moving GSE mortgages from three bureau reports to two would, mechanically, cut the number of paid pulls per mortgage by a third across the industry — and each bureau would then have to compete to be one of the two. FHFA has not announced a decision; Bloomberg reported on October 1 that it plans to, and Equifax sizes the revenue at stake at about $80 million a year. The share price is treating that as a live probability.
02
FICO cutting out the middleman. Since October 1, 2025, FICO's Mortgage Direct License Program lets tri-merge resellers calculate and deliver FICO scores directly, bypassing the bureaus' resale markup. EFX fell 17.7% in October 2025. Management's 2026 guidance, as of July, assumed Equifax keeps selling FICO scores on all mortgage credit transactions this year — an assumption, not a contract.
03
Leverage is high. Total debt $5.21B against $192.9M of cash at September 30 and debt-to-equity of 1.13 — before the $750M Círculo de Crédito purchase closes. Free cash flow of $1.10B over the last twelve months covers it today; it leaves less room if mortgage economics are cut.
04
Mortgage rates. CFO John Gamble said 30-year mortgage rates reached about 7.5% in September, against 6.6% when guidance was set in July; management expects the much weaker US mortgage market of late Q3 to last through the rest of 2026 — the reason it cut full-year revenue guidance. Even without a policy change, the highest-growth line is rate-sensitive.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 18x consensus 2027 earnings for a company growing revenue about 10% with a 51%-margin verification franchise nobody else can replicate, a $150M AI cost programme, $1.1B of free cash flow and a Mexican acquisition that diversifies away from US mortgage policy. Q3 adjusted EPS beat the top of the company's own range even as the mortgage market weakened, and Equifax sizes the revenue at stake from bi-merge at about $80 million a year — roughly 1.2% of 2026 revenue guidance. FHFA has threatened bi-merge before and reversed itself in July 2025; the announcement expected in October did not come, and tri-merge remains required. If the agency again stops short, the rest of the discount the stock picked up in September disappears.

Bear Case

The growth is borrowed. In Q2, USIS mortgage revenue rose 40% while hard mortgage inquiries rose only 1%, largely because FICO raised prices, and FICO has already started selling around the bureaus. Now the regulator that sets the rules for most US mortgages says the bureaus overcharge, is "seriously considering" cutting one of them from every file, and per Bloomberg plans to. Management has already cut 2026 revenue guidance on a weaker mortgage market and expects the weakness to last through year-end. Gross margin has slid from 60.5% to 55.2% in four years, ROIC is 9.5%, debt-to-equity is 1.13, and a $750M acquisition is still to be paid for. At 28.4x trailing GAAP earnings, the stock is not obviously cheap if the mortgage profit pool shrinks.

Top 3 Risks

01
FHFA adopts bi-merge. Fewer paid bureau pulls per mortgage, and price competition between the three for the two slots. Undated, unannounced — and the stock's biggest single swing factor.
02
Loss of FICO resale economics. FICO's direct licensing and the FHFA's push for VantageScore (which Equifax co-owns but sells at $1 in mortgage through end-2028) both erode the score revenue that currently drives USIS growth.
03
Balance-sheet squeeze in a down-cycle. $5.21B of debt and a $750M acquisition pending. Manageable on $1.1B of free cash flow — less so if mortgage volumes and pricing fall together.
Industry Commentary
Washington writes the mortgage credit rules
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The timeline reads like a hand of poker played in public: October 2022, FHFA proposes bi-merge; February 2024, it pencils in Q4 2025; July 8, 2025, Director Pulte announces that Fannie and Freddie will keep the tri-merge requirement; July 15, 2025, lenders get a choice between Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0; November 2025, bi-merge is still off; January 2026, Pulte says the bureaus are "inviting a lot of scrutiny" over pricing, after the Mortgage Bankers Association complained of 40–50% increases in credit reporting costs; September 3, 2026, bi-merge is back under serious consideration; October 1, Bloomberg reports a plan to adopt it; mid-October, the expected announcement does not come. Nothing has changed in the rulebook yet. What has changed is the credibility of the rulebook as a moat.

The FICO squeeze
ONGOING HEADWIND

For years the bureaus resold FICO's mortgage score with a markup. FICO raised its own price, which flattered bureau revenue, and then in October 2025 launched direct licensing to resellers. Equifax's response is VantageScore — jointly owned by the three bureaus — priced at $1 in mortgage, now through end-2028, to win adoption. By its October 8 count, nearly 2,000 mortgage lenders and resellers were taking a free VantageScore 4.0 alongside a paid legacy score. The trade-off is plain: every mortgage that switches from FICO to a $1 VantageScore lowers Equifax's reported revenue even as it weakens FICO. The company's own guidance strips FICO royalties out to show underlying growth of 7.2–8.4%.

Verification is the part regulators are not attacking
EMERGING TAILWIND

Workforce Solutions is 41.0% of revenue at a 51.4% adjusted EBITDA margin, and the FHFA's posts were about credit reports, not income verification. In Q3 its non-mortgage verification revenue rose 13%, led by hiring checks up over 20%, while its mortgage revenue was about flat. The bull framing: the market is pricing Equifax as a mortgage bureau when its most profitable engine is a proprietary payroll database. The bear framing: verification demand is itself tied to mortgage and lending volumes, and a political climate hostile to data-broker pricing does not have to stop at credit files.
