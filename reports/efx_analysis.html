01 Company Overview

Equifax is one of the three US credit bureaus — alongside Experian and TransUnion — and, less famously, the owner of The Work Number, a database of employment and income records that lenders, landlords and government agencies use to verify what a person earns. It reports three segments: Workforce Solutions, U.S. Information Solutions (USIS) and International. FY2025 revenue was $6.08B, up 6.9%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $5.32; trailing-twelve-month revenue has since reached $6.44B, up 10.35%.

The business is doing fine. The stock is not. Q2 2026 revenue grew 11%, guidance was raised to $6.71–6.78B, and management doubled its AI cost-saving target. Yet the shares closed September 21 at $158.62 — down 26.9% this year, 38.4% over twelve months and 48% below the August 2024 month-end high of $307.13. The reason is that Equifax's most profitable-looking growth engine, US mortgage, sits inside a pricing structure a single regulator can rewrite. On September 3–4, 2026, Federal Housing Finance Agency Director Bill Pulte directed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to approve all lenders to use VantageScore, said the bureaus "have been overcharging Americans for far too long," and wrote that the agency is "seriously considering bi-merge" — mortgage files built from two bureaus instead of three. EFX fell 6.4% that day and has not bounced. The important detail for anyone reading the headlines: no final decision has been made. As of the fetched timeline, lenders must still deliver tri-merge reports to Fannie and Freddie. The market is pricing a threat, not a rule.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

The Work Number: 217M active records Three-bureau oligopoly Tri-merge mortgage requirement Co-owner of VantageScore Employer-contributed data network Regulated data & compliance scale Workforce margin (52% adj. EBITDA)

Equifax's moat has two layers, and they are not equally safe. The first is data nobody else has: The Work Number is built from payroll feeds that employers contribute because Equifax handles their verification paperwork, and every new employer makes the database more valuable to the next lender. That layer is private and contractual. The second is a rule: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac require a report from all three bureaus on every conforming mortgage. That layer is public and political — and 2025–26 has shown how quickly it can be questioned. The FHFA reversed a planned move to bi-merge in July 2025, reaffirmed tri-merge in November 2025, and on September 3, 2026 said it is "seriously considering bi-merge" again. A moat that depends on a regulator's preference is a moat with a drawbridge someone else controls.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Bi-merge. Moving GSE mortgages from three bureau reports to two would, mechanically, cut the number of paid pulls per mortgage by a third across the industry — and each bureau would then have to compete to be one of the two. FHFA has not decided; it has said it is "seriously considering" it. The share price is already treating that as a live probability.
02
FICO cutting out the middleman. Since October 1, 2025, FICO's Mortgage Direct License Program lets tri-merge resellers calculate and deliver FICO scores directly, bypassing the bureaus' resale markup. EFX fell 17.7% in October 2025. Management's guidance assumes Equifax keeps selling FICO scores on all mortgage credit transactions in 2026 — an assumption, not a contract.
03
Leverage and liquidity are tight. Total debt $5.47B against $170.1M of cash, debt-to-equity 1.21 and a current ratio of 0.60 — before the $750M Círculo de Crédito purchase closes. Free cash flow of $1.11B covers it comfortably today; it leaves less room if mortgage economics are cut.
04
Mortgage rates. Management said 30-year fixed rates rose to about 6.6% versus the ~6.3% assumed in guidance and industry volumes ran below expectations in Q2. Even without a policy change, the highest-growth line is rate-sensitive.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 18x consensus 2026 adjusted earnings for a company growing revenue 10–11% with a 52%-margin verification franchise nobody else can replicate, a $150M AI cost programme, $1.1B of free cash flow and a Mexican acquisition that diversifies away from US mortgage policy. FHFA has threatened bi-merge before — and reversed itself in July 2025. The threat is currently a post, not a rule; tri-merge remains required. Every one of the 24 covering analysts has a target above the price, the highest ($245) set after the selloff. If the agency again stops short, the discount the stock picked up in September disappears.

Bear Case

The growth is borrowed. Mortgage revenue rose 40% on 1% more inquiries because FICO raised prices, and FICO has already started selling around the bureaus. Now the regulator that sets the rules for most US mortgages says the bureaus overcharge and is "seriously considering" cutting one of them from every file. Gross margin has slid from 60.5% to 55.5% in four years, ROIC is 8.9%, debt-to-equity is 1.21 with a 0.60 current ratio, and a $750M acquisition is still to be paid for. At 27.9x trailing GAAP earnings, the stock is not obviously cheap if the mortgage profit pool shrinks.

Top 3 Risks

01
FHFA adopts bi-merge. Fewer paid bureau pulls per mortgage, and price competition between the three for the two slots. Undated, unannounced — and the stock's biggest single swing factor.
02
Loss of FICO resale economics. FICO's direct licensing and the FHFA's push for VantageScore (which Equifax co-owns but sells at $1 in mortgage through 2027) both erode the score revenue that currently drives USIS growth.
03
Balance-sheet squeeze in a down-cycle. $5.47B of debt, a 0.60 current ratio and a $750M acquisition pending. Manageable on $1.1B of free cash flow — less so if mortgage volumes and pricing fall together.

CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The fetched timeline reads like a hand of poker played in public: October 2022, FHFA proposes bi-merge; February 2024, it pencils in Q4 2025; July 8, 2025, Director Pulte announces Fannie and Freddie "will maintain the tri-merge requirement"; July 15, 2025, lenders get a choice between Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0; November 2025, bi-merge "remains cancelled"; January 2026, Pulte says the bureaus are "inviting a lot of scrutiny" over pricing, after the Mortgage Bankers Association complained of 40–50% increases in credit reporting costs; September 3, 2026, bi-merge is back under serious consideration. Nothing has changed in the rulebook. What has changed is the credibility of the rulebook as a moat.

The FICO squeeze
ONGOING HEADWIND

For years the bureaus resold FICO's mortgage score with a markup. FICO raised its own price, which flattered bureau revenue, and then in October 2025 launched direct licensing to resellers. Equifax's response is VantageScore — jointly owned by the three bureaus — priced at $1 in mortgage through end-2027 to win adoption. Q2 volumes nearly tripled to 2.2 million. The trade-off is plain: every mortgage that switches from FICO to a $1 VantageScore lowers Equifax's reported revenue even as it weakens FICO. The company's own guidance strips FICO royalties out to show underlying growth of 7.2–8.4%.

Verification is the part regulators are not attacking
EMERGING TAILWIND

Workforce Solutions is 41.5% of revenue at a 52.1% adjusted EBITDA margin, and the FHFA's posts were about credit reports, not income verification. Contract signings of about $300M of annual value in four months and a government pipeline roughly double last year's suggest demand is intact. The bull framing: the market is pricing Equifax as a mortgage bureau when its most profitable engine is a proprietary payroll database. The bear framing: verification demand is itself tied to mortgage and lending volumes, and a political climate hostile to data-broker pricing does not have to stop at credit files. The Q3 report on October 20 will show which segment is carrying the company.
