01 Company Overview

The Estée Lauder Companies manufactures and sells prestige beauty — skin care, makeup, fragrance and hair care — through a portfolio that includes Estée Lauder, La Mer, Clinique, M·A·C, Jo Malone London and TOM FORD Beauty, distributed in roughly 150 countries and territories through about 1,600 freestanding stores plus department stores, specialty retail, travel retail and e-commerce. Fiscal 2026, which ended June 30, 2026, produced net sales of $15,049M, up 5.0% from $14,326M — the first growth year after three consecutive declines — with organic growth (stripping out currency moves, acquisitions and disposals) of +3%. The first quarter of fiscal 2027, reported on November 2, 2026, kept it going: net sales of $3,715M, up 7% (6% organic), and adjusted EPS of $0.72 against $0.32 a year earlier. At a $38.19B market capitalisation the company ranks #277 in the S&P 500, a startling number for a business that was a $100B-plus company inside the last five years.

Be precise about the calendar. Everything on this page labelled "FY" refers to a fiscal year ending 30 June. FY2026 = July 2025 through June 2026. FY2026 (reported August 19, 2026) is the latest complete year; the first quarter of FY2027 — July to September 2026, reported November 2, 2026 — is the latest quarter, and the page labels which one each figure comes from. Calendar-year framings of this company's results will not line up with what follows.

The central debate is whether the FY2026 recovery is a genuine turn for the better or just a bounce off a very weak year. The bull reading is straightforward: organic sales returned to growth, adjusted operating margin expanded from 8.0% to 11.2% in FY2026 and reached 12.1% in the first quarter of FY2027, adjusted diluted EPS rose 66% to $2.51 in FY2026, Mainland China grew 12% for the year and 15% in the latest quarter in reported dollars, and management has raised its FY2027 outlook twice (in August and again in November). The bear reading is equally available: GAAP net income was $182M on $15.05B of FY2026 sales — a 1.2% net margin, and $368M (2.4%) over the twelve months to September; FY2026's $907M gap between adjusted and reported operating income was $823M of restructuring and other charges plus an $84M securities class-action settlement, and the restructuring bill was guided in August to land above the original $1.5–1.7B envelope; and the stock still sits 71.5% below its December 2021 monthly close of $370.20. Sections 04 and 07 take both readings apart.

Travel retail — the duty-free and airport channel, historically stocked by daigou-style resellers buying in Hainan and Korea for onward sale into mainland China — is the mechanism that took this stock from $370 to $60. When Chinese outbound travel collapsed and the reseller channel was squeezed, retailers stopped reordering and worked down inventory they had already bought: destocking, which hits a manufacturer's shipments far harder than end demand, because the retailer's purchases fall to zero while its sales merely slow. That is why ELC's reported revenue fell 10.3% in FY2023 and a further 8.2% in FY2025 while the underlying consumer was still buying.

FY2026 is the first year the channel stopped subtracting. The company reported value share gains in Mainland China in both Q4 and the full year, led by fragrance, skin care and makeup, with China net sales +12% reported and +9% organic for the year and +12% reported / +7% organic in Q4. Travel retail itself was mixed rather than healed: growth in Korea and in Hainan travel retail, offset by a decline in mainland China travel retail caused by duty-free retailer transitions at the Beijing and Shanghai airports — an operational, contract-level disruption rather than a demand one. Management's FY2027 assumptions explicitly bake in increased travel retail shipments against a lower prior-year base in the first half. That is a real tailwind and it is also an easy comparison with a weak year; both things are true.

The first quarter of FY2027 is the first evidence for that assumption. Mainland China net sales rose 15% to $614M (9% organic), and Asia/Pacific rose 9% to $952M (10% organic), which the release puts down primarily to the Asia travel retail business: sales from the Beijing and Shanghai airports grew on replenishment orders once the retailer changes had settled, and Hainan travel retail grew on online sales. Together the two regions were 42.2% of the quarter's sales.

Honest framing: the channel has stopped shrinking, and one quarter of growth against an easy comparison is not yet proof that it keeps growing. FY2027 first-half growth flatters it.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
PRESTIGE BRAND PORTFOLIO 75.5% GROSS MARGIN LA MER PRICING POWER ~150 COUNTRIES ~1,600 OWN STORES $278M R&D / ~1,000 R&D STAFF FAMILY CONTROL — 82% OF VOTES

The moat is brand equity expressed as gross margin. A company that keeps 75.5 cents of every revenue dollar after the cost of goods is not selling chemistry, it is selling a name, a counter and a ritual — and that spread has survived a 19% peak-to-trough revenue decline and a near-total dislocation of its most profitable channel. Gross margin actually expanded 150 basis points (1.5 percentage points) in FY2026, during the worst of the restructuring, which tells you the pricing held while the volume did not; it reached 75.7% in the first quarter of FY2027. Add distribution that would take a challenger a decade and several billion dollars to replicate — roughly 1,600 freestanding stores plus counters in essentially every prestige department store on earth — and the durability of the franchise is not really in question.

What is in question is whether the moat is around the right castle. The gross margin protects the brand; it does not protect the channel, and the channel is what broke.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Geographic concentration in the most volatile market in beauty. Asia/Pacific and Mainland China together are 45.2% of net sales ($6,804M of $15,049M), and the Americas — the world's largest prestige market — grew just 1% in FY2026 to $4,463M (5% in the first quarter of FY2027). Management has named "accelerating growth in North America" as an FY2027 priority precisely because the current geographic balance leaves the group hostage to Chinese consumer sentiment and to duty-free channel mechanics it does not control. The Beijing and Shanghai airport retailer changes that dented FY2026 travel retail turned into replenishment orders in the first quarter of FY2027 — a reminder that a handful of concession contracts can move a segment in either direction.
02
Cheaper challengers are pushing makeup prices down. Makeup is 28.4% of revenue and grew 2%. e.l.f. Beauty does $1.76B of revenue and carries a $6.58B market capitalisation — roughly a sixth of ELC's market cap on about a ninth of its revenue — because the market believes mass-priced, social-native colour cosmetics are taking the share prestige used to own. Nothing in ELC's disclosures through the first quarter of FY2027 contradicts that. A prestige portfolio can defend a serum at $350; defending a lipstick at $30 against a $6 alternative with better TikTok distribution is a different problem.
03
Leverage and a payout that exceeds reported earnings. Total debt is $9.20B against $3.20B of cash (including lease liabilities; borrowings alone are $7.25B), with debt-to-equity at 2.28 and a current ratio of 1.25. The $1.40 annual dividend against $1.01 of trailing GAAP EPS is a 139% payout ratio — covered on the $3.41 FY2027 consensus EPS (41%) and on $1.52B of trailing free cash flow, but not yet by trailing accounting profit. Restructuring cash payments run through FY2027, and operating cash flow is guided to $1.4–1.5B (raised on November 2 from $1.3–1.4B), still below FY2026's $1.77B.
04
Governance: 82% of the votes, a minority of the economics. The Lauder family controls approximately 82% of voting power through Class B shares carrying ten votes each. In practice outside shareholders cannot force a strategic change, a portfolio divestiture, a board reconstitution or a sale. That structure has been in place since the 1995 IPO and is not a new risk — but it is the reason an activist campaign, the usual circuit-breaker after an 84% fall from peak to trough, has never been a realistic path here.

Notable One-Off Items — and there are a lot of them. This is not a clean set of accounts and should not be read as one.

(i) The restructuring gap. FY2026 reported operating income was $780M; adjusted operating income was $1,687M, up 47%. The $907M difference is $823M of restructuring and other charges plus an $84M settlement of a securities class action (the release's reconciliation). Reported operating margin was 5.2%; adjusted was 11.2%. In the first quarter of FY2027 the gap shrank to $41M: $408M reported (11.0% margin) against $449M adjusted (12.1%), with restructuring and other charges of $40M against $89M a year earlier. Margin headlines still quote the adjusted number, but the two are now converging.

(ii) The Profit Recovery and Growth Plan has grown. At the August 19 print, cumulative restructuring charges through June 30, 2026 reached $1.4B ($0.8B in FY2026 alone, $0.3B in Q4), and the company now expects total cumulative charges slightly above the original $1.5–1.7B range. Net position reductions came in at 10,000 — the high end of the 9,000–10,000 guide — with expected annual gross benefits of $1.2B, also the high end of the $1.0–1.2B guide. Restructuring approvals concluded as of June 30, 2026; implementation is expected to be substantially complete in FY2027. So the charge is bigger than promised and the benefit is bigger than promised. On November 2 the FY2027 guide for restructuring and other charges came down to $0.44–0.50 per share, from $0.50–0.58.

(iii) Q4 FY2026 was a reported loss. The company reported a Q4 operating loss of $(39)M and diluted net loss per share of $(0.32), against adjusted operating income of $267M (+95%) and adjusted EPS of $0.39. Data sites differ here: stockanalysis.com shows Q4 FY2026 operating income of +$254M (read November 4, 2026) where the company's own release states $(39)M as reported. The company's figure is used here, and the gap is exactly the kind of thing that makes screening this name on a data site unreliable.

(iv) FY2025 was a $1,133M net loss (−$3.15 per share), so every FY2026 growth rate is measured against a genuinely damaged base. A 66% increase in adjusted EPS from $1.51 to $2.51 is real; it is also a recovery to a level the company last exceeded comfortably in FY2022, when it earned $6.55.

(v) A one-off gain is coming. On October 5, 2026 ELC completed buying the rest of Mountain Valley Springs India, owner of the Forest Essentials brand, in which it already held a stake. The FY2027 reported EPS guide of $2.85–3.18 includes a gain of $0.25–0.27 per share on that earlier stake — an accounting gain, not operating profit, so reported EPS will flatter the business this year. The company does not expect the brand's own results to be material in FY2027.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and in a stock that is still about 72% below its peak, a low reading has repeatedly been a warning rather than a bargain.

Five-year trajectory — frame this honestly. From the November 2021 month-end close of $332.07 to the November 3, 2026 close of $105.39, EL has returned −68.3% on price — a compound annual rate of about −20.8% before dividends. Against the December 2021 month-end peak of $370.20, the drawdown is −71.5%. At the April 2025 trough of $59.96 it was −83.8% from that peak. This is not a stock that dipped; it is a stock that was destroyed and has since won back about 15% of the dollars it lost ($45.43 of $310.24), and the recovery has itself been violent in both directions.

The 52-week price change is +13.0% ($93.25 on November 3, 2025 to $105.39), the 52-week range is $66.22–$121.64, and beta is 1.27. What the price path and the 1.27 beta jointly say is that EL has traded like a bet on Chinese consumer spending that swings more than the market, despite its consumer-staples label — and over the same five-year window SPY, the largest S&P 500 index fund, rose 65.6% on price (from its $455.56 November 2021 month-end close to $754.35 on November 3, 2026, Yahoo Finance) while EL fell 68.3%.

A note on the chart's March 2026 point. The series shows a −34.4% single month, from a February 2026 close of $109.47 to $71.77 in March 2026. That is the most extreme monthly move in the five years shown. It is corroborated by the 52-week intraday low of $66.22, set on April 2, 2026, and by the April 2026 close of $76.71.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What actually happened here. Estée Lauder built a prestige beauty franchise whose economics depended on two things being true at once: that the brands could hold a 75% gross margin, and that the Chinese consumer — reached partly through the duty-free travel-retail channel — would keep growing year after year. The first held. The second did not. Between FY2022 and FY2025 revenue fell from $17,737M to $14,326M, operating margin from 20.04% to 7.96%, and diluted EPS from $6.55 to −$3.15. The stock fell 83.8% from its month-end peak to its month-end trough. None of that was a brand problem, and that is precisely why the recovery case is credible — and also why it was allowed to get this bad before anyone acted.

What FY2026 and the first quarter of FY2027 proved, stated narrowly. Three things, and only three. (1) Sales stopped falling: $15,049M, +5.0% reported and +3% organic, with Q4 at +6%/+5% — so the trend improved through the year rather than fading. (2) Cost-out is landing in the margin: gross margin up 1.5 percentage points to 75.49%, adjusted operating margin from 8.0% to 11.2%, adjusted operating income +47% on +5% sales — costs grew far more slowly than sales, which is genuine, not an accident of mix. (3) China grew: +12% reported, +9% organic, with value share gains reported by the company and #1 positions across a mix of brands and platforms in the 11.11 and 6.18 events. Fragrance, at +12%, contributed roughly 40% of the group's entire FY2026 sales increase from 18.5% of the base. The first quarter of FY2027 confirmed all three: sales +7% (6% organic), adjusted operating margin 12.1% against 7.3%, China +15% (9% organic), fragrance +19%.

What it has not proved yet. That the recovery is self-sustaining rather than restructuring-funded. FY2026 GAAP net income was $182M — a 1.21% net margin — and GAAP EPS was $0.50 against adjusted EPS of $2.51. The first quarter narrowed the gap sharply ($0.64 reported against $0.72 adjusted), but one quarter is one quarter, and in August the restructuring bill was guided above its original $1.5–1.7B envelope with $1.4B already booked. Trailing ROIC of 9.04% and ROE of 9.28% are up from 5.92% and 4.75% at the last edition and still low for a brand portfolio of this quality. And FY2027 operating cash flow, even after the November raise to $1.4–1.5B, is guided below FY2026's $1.77B, because the cash cost of the programme is still being paid.

Valuation — pick your denominator carefully, because it changes the answer more than threefold. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $1.01, EL trades at 104.35×. That number is arithmetically correct and tells you little. (ii) On trailing adjusted EPS of $2.91 (FY2026's $2.51, less Q1 FY2026's $0.32, plus Q1 FY2027's $0.72; our calculation from the releases), it trades at 36.2×. (iii) On the FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.41, it trades at 30.9× — the forward P/E. (iv) On trailing free cash flow of $1.52B against a $44.13B enterprise value (market value plus net debt, stockanalysis.com), EV/FCF is about 29.0×, a 4.0% FCF yield on the $38.19B market value. (v) On sales, P/S is 2.50× and EV/Sales 2.89×. The defensible range is (iii) to (iv): roughly 29–31× forward earnings and cash flow. For context, L'Oréal, the largest comparable on this page, trades at 36.7× its trailing earnings (our calculation: the €432.70 November 3 close ÷ €11.78 of EPS, stockanalysis.com). At 36.2× its trailing adjusted earnings EL is priced about level with L'Oréal on its trailing reported earnings, and at a discount only on earnings it has not yet earned. That is not a deep-value setup. It is a recovery already substantially priced.

The drawdown is not itself an argument. A stock 72% below its 2021 peak is cheap relative to its own history and not necessarily cheap relative to its own earnings — those are different claims, and only the first one is true here. FY2022's $6.55 of EPS came from a $17.7B revenue base with a 20% operating margin in a world where Chinese travel retail looked set to keep growing. Management's own FY2027 guide, even after the November raise, is 4.0–5.5% organic growth and a 12.9–13.7% adjusted operating margin. Even fully delivered, that is a materially smaller, slower and lower-margin company than the one the 2021 price was paying for. Anchoring on $370 is the single most expensive mistake available in this name.

Bull Case

The brand equity was never damaged — gross margin expanded to 75.5% through the worst of it and reached 75.7% in the latest quarter — and the channel that broke has stopped subtracting: China grew 12% reported in FY2026 and 15% in the first quarter of FY2027, and Asia travel retail drove Asia/Pacific up 9% in that quarter. The restructuring delivered at the high end of its range ($1.2B of gross annual benefit, 10,000 positions), adjusted operating margin has moved from 8.0% to 11.2% for FY2026 and 12.1% in the first quarter, with the year guided to 12.9–13.7%, and fragrance grew 19% in the quarter. If FY2027 lands at the $3.41 consensus, the current $105.39 is 30.9× a still-recovering earnings base with several more years of margin recovery toward the 20% it once ran at.

Bear Case

Trailing GAAP net margin is 2.41% and ROIC 9.04%, and FY2026's $907M gap between adjusted and reported operating income was mostly restructuring charges ($823M, plus an $84M legal settlement), on a restructuring bill guided in August above its original $1.5–1.7B range. FY2027 operating cash flow is guided to $1.4–1.5B, below FY2026's $1.77B. Makeup — 28.4% of revenue — grew 2% for the year and 2% in the first quarter, and faces lasting price pressure from mass-market challengers. 45.2% of FY2026 sales sat in Asia/Pacific and China, where a handful of duty-free concession changes moved a quarter. Debt-to-equity is 2.28 and the dividend is 139% of trailing reported earnings. At 30.9× forward earnings, with 14 of 30 analysts at Hold and an average target only 8% above the November 3 close, the recovery is already in the price — the 4.1% fall on a beat-and-raise day, and the March 2026 −34.4% month, show how quickly this stock reprices.

Top 3 Risks

01
China and travel retail relapse. Asia/Pacific plus Mainland China is 45.2% of net sales, and the FY2027 guide explicitly assumes increased travel-retail shipments against a lower prior-year base. If Chinese prestige demand softens, or if the duty-free retailer transitions at Beijing and Shanghai airports extend rather than resolve, the single largest assumed source of FY2027 growth reverses. This is the risk that produced the 83.8% peak-to-trough fall once already.
02
The adjusted-to-reported gap reopens. $1.4B of restructuring charges booked by June 2026, the total guided in August above $1.5–1.7B, and implementation still running through FY2027. The first quarter narrowed the gap to $41M of operating income, but while trailing reported EPS stays far below adjusted, the 104.35× trailing multiple stays real and the 30.91× forward multiple stays a forecast. The market is paying for the second; a quarter of fresh charges or a missed margin step would pull attention back to the first.
03
Structural share loss in makeup, plus no governance remedy. Makeup is 28.4% of revenue growing at 2% against challengers with better social distribution and a fraction of the price point. If that category's prices are being pushed down for good rather than for a cycle, roughly a quarter of the revenue base has a permanently lower growth ceiling — and with the Lauder family holding ~82% of the voting power, outside shareholders have no mechanism to force a portfolio response.
  Industry Commentary
Asia Travel Retail Is Still the Swing Factor
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Prestige beauty's most profitable channel for a decade was duty-free — airports, Hainan, Korea — stocked heavily by resellers serving mainland Chinese demand. When that flow broke, the damage to manufacturers was amplified by destocking: retailers holding inventory stop ordering entirely, so shipments fall far more than consumption does. ELC's revenue fell 10.3% in FY2023 and 8.2% in FY2025 on that mechanism. FY2026 was the first year it stopped subtracting — but the company's own description was mixed: growth in Korea and Hainan, decline in mainland China travel retail on duty-free retailer transitions at the Beijing and Shanghai airports. In the first quarter of FY2027 those airports grew on replenishment orders and Hainan grew on online sales, driving Asia/Pacific up 9%. The FY2027 guide leans on this channel recovering into an easy comparison, and the first quarter did. It is the single assumption with the most leverage on the outcome, and it is the one ELC controls least.
Prestige Is Being Squeezed From Below
ONGOING HEADWIND
The clearest evidence sits in the relative market values. e.l.f. Beauty does $1.76B of revenue and is worth $6.58B; ELC does $15.28B and is worth $38.19B (November 3 closes). On a revenue multiple the challenger is valued at roughly 3.7× sales against ELC's 2.5× — the market pays more per dollar of mass-market beauty revenue than per dollar of prestige. Meanwhile Coty, the other legacy prestige operator, carries $5.81B of revenue and a $2.90B market cap. The category where this bites is makeup, 28.4% of ELC's FY2026 revenue and its slowest-growing large category at +2% (and +2% again in the first quarter of FY2027). Skin care and fragrance have defended their price points; colour cosmetics, where the product is more replicable and the purchase more impulsive, have not. This is not a China problem and travel-retail normalisation will not solve it.
Fragrance Is the Category That Works
EMERGING TAILWIND
Fragrance grew 12% in FY2026 to $2,779M, adding roughly $288M — about 40% of the group's entire $723M revenue increase — from 18.5% of the base, kept a 10% growth rate in Q4 and accelerated to 19% in the first quarter of FY2027. It is high-margin, it is harder to counterfeit or commoditise than colour cosmetics, and ELC's Jo Malone / Le Labo / TOM FORD / KILIAN stable is genuinely well positioned in the niche-luxury tier that has driven the category globally. Bull framing: if fragrance keeps growing at double digits and makeup merely holds, the group hits its 4.0–5.5% organic guide without needing a China miracle, and the margin path to 12.9–13.7% carries the earnings. Bear framing: at 18.5% of FY2026 sales, fragrance cannot for long offset a 28.4% category growing at 2%. The Americas turned up in the first quarter (+5%); makeup has not yet.
