01 Company Overview

The Estée Lauder Companies manufactures and sells prestige beauty — skin care, makeup, fragrance and hair care — through a portfolio that includes Estée Lauder, La Mer, Clinique, M·A·C, Jo Malone London and TOM FORD Beauty, distributed in roughly 150 countries and territories through about 1,600 freestanding stores plus department stores, specialty retail, travel retail and e-commerce. Fiscal 2026, which ended June 30, 2026, produced net sales of $15,049M, up 5.0% from $14,326M — the first growth year after three consecutive declines — with organic growth of +3%. At a $34.96B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #718 globally, a startling number for a business that was a $100B-plus company inside the last five years.

Be precise about the calendar. Everything on this page labelled "FY" refers to a fiscal year ending 30 June. FY2026 = July 2025 through June 2026. The FY2026 fourth-quarter and full-year print has already landed — it was reported August 19, 2026 — so the numbers below are a complete fiscal year, not a stub. Calendar-year framings of this company's results will not line up with what follows.

The central debate is whether the FY2026 recovery is a genuine inflection or a low-base bounce. The bull reading is straightforward: organic sales returned to growth, adjusted operating margin expanded from 8.0% to 11.2%, adjusted diluted EPS rose 66% to $2.51, Mainland China grew 12% in reported dollars, and management raised its FY2027 margin outlook. The bear reading is equally available from the same filing: GAAP net income was $182M on $15.05B of sales — a 1.2% net margin — the gap between $1,687M of adjusted operating income and $780M of reported operating income is $907M of restructuring and other charges, the restructuring bill is now guided above the original $1.5–1.7B envelope, and the stock still sits 73.9% below its December 2021 monthly close of $370.20. Sections 04 and 07 take both readings apart.

Asia/Pacific FY2026

Travel retail — the duty-free and airport channel, historically stocked by daigou-style resellers buying in Hainan and Korea for onward sale into mainland China — is the mechanism that took this stock from $370 to $60. When Chinese outbound travel collapsed and the reseller channel was squeezed, retailers stopped reordering and worked down inventory they had already bought: destocking, which hits a manufacturer's shipments far harder than end demand, because the retailer's purchases fall to zero while its sales merely slow. That is why ELC's reported revenue fell 10.3% in FY2023 and a further 8.2% in FY2025 while the underlying consumer was still buying.

FY2026 is the first year the channel stopped subtracting. The company reported value share gains in Mainland China in both Q4 and the full year, led by fragrance, skin care and makeup, with China net sales +12% reported and +9% organic for the year and +12% reported / +7% organic in Q4. Travel retail itself was mixed rather than healed: growth in Korea and in Hainan travel retail, offset by a decline in mainland China travel retail caused by duty-free retailer transitions at the Beijing and Shanghai airports — an operational, contract-level disruption rather than a demand one. Management's FY2027 assumptions explicitly bake in increased travel retail shipments against a lower prior-year base in the first half. That is a real tailwind and it is also a comp effect; both things are true.

Honest framing: the channel has stopped shrinking. It has not yet been shown to compound. FY2027 first-half growth will flatter it.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
PRESTIGE BRAND PORTFOLIO 75.5% GROSS MARGIN LA MER PRICING POWER ~150 COUNTRIES ~1,600 OWN STORES $278M R&D / ~1,000 SCIENTISTS FAMILY CONTROL — 82% OF VOTES

The moat is brand equity expressed as gross margin. A company that keeps 75.5 cents of every revenue dollar after the cost of goods is not selling chemistry, it is selling a name, a counter and a ritual — and that spread has survived a 15% peak-to-trough revenue decline and a near-total dislocation of its most profitable channel. Gross margin actually expanded 150 basis points in FY2026, during the worst of the restructuring, which tells you the pricing architecture held while the volume did not. Add distribution that would take a challenger a decade and several billion dollars to replicate — roughly 1,600 freestanding stores plus counters in essentially every prestige department store on earth — and the durability of the franchise is not really in question.

What is in question is whether the moat is around the right castle. The gross margin protects the brand; it does not protect the channel, and the channel is what broke.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Geographic concentration in the most volatile market in beauty. Asia/Pacific and Mainland China together are 45.2% of net sales ($6,804M of $15,049M), and the Americas — the world's largest prestige market — grew just 1% in FY2026 to $4,463M. Management has named "accelerating growth in North America" as an FY2027 priority precisely because the current geographic balance leaves the group hostage to Chinese consumer sentiment and to duty-free channel mechanics it does not control. The Beijing and Shanghai airport retailer transitions that dented FY2026 travel retail are a reminder that a handful of concession contracts can move a segment.
02
Makeup is being repriced by challengers. Makeup is 28.4% of revenue and grew 2%. e.l.f. Beauty does $1.76B of revenue and carries a $5.70B market capitalisation — roughly a sixth of ELC's market cap on an eighth of its revenue — because the market believes mass-priced, social-native colour cosmetics are taking the share prestige used to own. Nothing in ELC's FY2026 disclosure contradicts that. A prestige portfolio can defend a serum at $350; defending a lipstick at $30 against a $6 alternative with better TikTok distribution is a different problem.
03
Leverage and a payout that exceeds reported earnings. Total debt is $9.25B against $3.50B of cash, with debt-to-equity at 2.43 and a current ratio of 1.22. The $1.40 annual dividend against $0.50 of GAAP EPS is a 280% payout ratio — covered on adjusted EPS of $2.51 (56%) and on $1.32B of free cash flow, but not covered by accounting profit. That is a live constraint while restructuring cash payments run through FY2027, when operating cash flow is guided down to $1.3–1.4B from $1.77B.
04
Governance: 82% of the votes, a minority of the economics. The Lauder family controls approximately 82% of voting power through Class B shares carrying ten votes each. In practice outside shareholders cannot force a strategic change, a portfolio divestiture, a board reconstitution or a sale. That structure has been in place since the 1995 IPO and is not a new risk — but it is the reason an activist campaign, the usual circuit-breaker for a 74% drawdown, has never been a realistic path here.

Notable One-Off Items — and there are a lot of them. This is not a clean set of accounts and should not be read as one.

(i) The restructuring gap. FY2026 reported operating income was $780M; adjusted operating income was $1,687M, up 47%. The $907M difference is restructuring and other charges. Reported operating margin is 5.2%; adjusted is 11.2%. Every "margin expansion" headline you will read about this company is quoting the second number.

(ii) The Profit Recovery and Growth Plan has grown. Cumulative restructuring charges through June 30, 2026 reached $1.4B ($0.8B in FY2026 alone, $0.3B in Q4), and the company now expects total cumulative charges slightly above the original $1.5–1.7B range. Net position reductions came in at 10,000 — the high end of the 9,000–10,000 guide — with expected annual gross benefits of $1.2B, also the high end of the $1.0–1.2B guide. Restructuring approvals concluded as of June 30, 2026; implementation is expected to be substantially complete in FY2027. So the charge is bigger than promised and the benefit is bigger than promised, and only one of those two has actually been delivered in the numbers so far.

(iii) Q4 FY2026 was a reported loss. The company reported a Q4 operating loss of $(39)M and diluted net loss per share of $(0.32), against adjusted operating income of $267M (+95%) and adjusted EPS of $0.39. Note a reconciliation discrepancy worth flagging: stockanalysis.com shows Q4 FY2026 operating income of +$254M where the company's own release states $(39)M as-reported. The company's figure is used here; the difference is presumably charge classification, and it is exactly the kind of thing that makes screening on this name unreliable.

(iv) FY2025 was a $1,133M net loss (−$3.15 per share), so every FY2026 growth rate is measured against a genuinely damaged base. A 66% increase in adjusted EPS from $1.51 to $2.51 is real; it is also a recovery to a level the company last exceeded comfortably in FY2022, when it earned $6.55.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and in a stock that has fallen 74% from its peak, a low reading has repeatedly been a warning rather than a bargain.

Five-year trajectory — frame this honestly. From the October 2021 monthly close of $324.33 to the September 10, 2026 close of $96.47, EL has returned −70.3% on price — a compound annual rate of about −21.5% before dividends. Against the December 2021 monthly-close peak of $370.20, the drawdown is −73.9%. At the April 2025 trough of $59.96 it was −83.8% from that peak. This is not a stock that dipped; it is a stock that was destroyed and has recovered about a quarter of the ground, and the recovery has itself been violent in both directions.

What can be said with sourced numbers: the 52-week price change is +10.51%, the 52-week range is $66.22–$121.64, and beta is 1.27. A five-year total-return comparison against the S&P 500 and against a consumer-staples index could not be verified from the sources fetched within this build, so no specific index return is stated. What the price path and the 1.27 beta jointly say is that EL has behaved like a high-beta China-consumption proxy wearing a consumer-staples sector label — and over this five-year window, the S&P 500 rose while EL lost roughly seven-tenths of its value, so the relative underperformance is severe by any reasonable index comparison even without a precise figure.

A note on the chart's March 2026 point. The series shows a −34.4% single month, from a February 2026 close of $109.47 to $71.77 in March 2026. That is the most extreme monthly move in the five years shown. It is corroborated by the 52-week low of $66.22, which must have printed in that window, and by the April 2026 close of $76.71. The cause of that move was not verified from any source fetched for this report, and no explanation is offered here rather than invented.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What actually happened here. Estée Lauder built a prestige beauty franchise whose economics depended on two things being true at once: that the brands could hold a 75% gross margin, and that the Chinese consumer — reached partly through the duty-free travel-retail channel — would keep compounding. The first held. The second did not. Between FY2022 and FY2025 revenue fell from $17,737M to $14,326M, operating margin from 20.04% to 7.96%, and diluted EPS from $6.55 to −$3.15. The stock fell 83.8% from peak to trough. None of that was a brand problem, and that is precisely why the recovery case is credible — and also why it was allowed to get this bad before anyone acted.

What FY2026 proved, stated narrowly. Three things, and only three. (1) Sales stopped falling: $15,049M, +5.0% reported and +3% organic, with Q4 at +6%/+5% — so the trend improved through the year rather than fading. (2) Cost-out is landing in the margin: gross margin up 150bp to 75.49%, adjusted operating margin from 8.0% to 11.2%, adjusted operating income +47% on +5% sales — that is genuine operating leverage, not a mix accident. (3) China grew: +12% reported, +9% organic, with claimed value share gains and a #1 position in the 11.11 and 6.18 events. Fragrance, at +12%, contributed roughly 40% of the group's entire sales increase from 18.5% of the base.

What FY2026 did not prove. That the recovery is self-sustaining rather than restructuring-funded. GAAP net income was $182M — a 1.21% net margin — and GAAP EPS was $0.50 against adjusted EPS of $2.51. The $907M gap between reported and adjusted operating income is restructuring charges, and the restructuring bill is now guided above its original $1.5–1.7B envelope with $1.4B already booked. ROIC of 5.92% and ROE of 4.75% are both below any plausible cost of capital. And FY2027 operating cash flow is guided down, from $1.77B to $1.3–1.4B, because the cash cost of the programme is still being paid. A company genuinely past its trouble does not usually guide cash flow down.

Valuation — pick your denominator carefully, because it changes the answer by 6×. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $0.50, EL trades at 192.94×. That number is arithmetically correct and analytically useless. (ii) On FY2026 adjusted EPS of $2.51, it trades at 38.4×. (iii) On the FY2027 consensus EPS of $3.32, it trades at 29.0× — the reported forward P/E. (iv) On free cash flow of $1.32B against a $40.70B enterprise value, EV/FCF is about 30.8×, a 3.8% FCF yield on the equity. (v) On sales, P/S is 2.32× and EV/Sales 2.70×. The defensible range is (iii) to (iv): roughly 29–31× forward earnings and cash flow. For context, L'Oréal — the best-run comparable, growing 3.5% with no restructuring overhang — trades at 32.4×. EL is therefore being priced at a slight discount to the sector's best operator, on earnings it has not yet earned, in the middle of a restructuring whose bill keeps rising. That is not a deep-value setup. It is a recovery already substantially priced.

The drawdown is not itself an argument. A stock 74% below its 2021 peak is cheap relative to its own history and not necessarily cheap relative to its own earnings — those are different claims, and only the first one is true here. FY2022's $6.55 of EPS came from a $17.7B revenue base with a 20% operating margin in a world where Chinese travel retail was structurally growing. Management's own FY2027 guide is 3–5% organic growth and a 12.7–13.5% adjusted operating margin. Even fully delivered, that is a materially smaller, slower and lower-margin company than the one the 2021 price was paying for. Anchoring on $370 is the single most expensive mistake available in this name.

Bull Case

The brand equity was never damaged — gross margin actually expanded to 75.5% through the worst of it — and the channel that broke has stopped subtracting: China grew 12% reported and 9% organic in FY2026 with claimed share gains, and travel retail returns to growth in FY2027 first-half against a low base. The restructuring is delivering at the high end of its range ($1.2B of gross annual benefit, 10,000 positions), adjusted operating margin has already moved from 8.0% to 11.2% and is guided to 12.7–13.5%, and fragrance is compounding at 12% with makeup guided to return to growth. If FY2027 hits the guide, the company earns something near $3.32 and the current $96.47 is 29× a still-recovering earnings base with several more years of margin recovery toward the 20% it once ran at.

Bear Case

GAAP net margin is 1.21%, ROIC is 5.92%, and the $907M gap between adjusted and reported operating income is a restructuring bill now guided above its original $1.5–1.7B range. FY2027 operating cash flow is guided down to $1.3–1.4B. Makeup — 28.4% of revenue — grew 2% and faces structural repricing from mass-market challengers. 45.2% of sales sit in Asia/Pacific and China, where a handful of duty-free concession transitions moved a quarter. Debt-to-equity is 2.43 and the dividend is 280% of reported earnings. At 29× forward earnings against L'Oréal's 32.4×, with 15 of 29 analysts at Hold and an average target only 11% above spot, the recovery is already in the price — and the March 2026 −34.4% month is a reminder of how quickly this stock reprices.

Top 3 Risks

01
China and travel retail relapse. Asia/Pacific plus Mainland China is 45.2% of net sales, and the FY2027 guide explicitly assumes increased travel-retail shipments against a lower prior-year base. If Chinese prestige demand softens, or if the duty-free retailer transitions at Beijing and Shanghai airports extend rather than resolve, the single largest assumed source of FY2027 growth reverses. This is the risk that produced the 83.8% drawdown once already.
02
The adjusted-to-reported gap refuses to close. $1.4B of restructuring charges booked, total now guided above $1.5–1.7B, and implementation still running through FY2027. Every quarter that the reported number stays far below the adjusted number, the 192.94× trailing multiple stays real and the 29.03× forward multiple stays hypothetical. The market is currently paying for the second; a single quarter of additional charges or a missed margin step re-anchors it on the first.
03
Structural share loss in makeup, plus no governance remedy. Makeup is 28.4% of revenue growing at 2% against challengers with better social distribution and a fraction of the price point. If that category is being structurally repriced rather than cyclically pressured, roughly a quarter of the revenue base has a permanently lower growth ceiling — and with the Lauder family holding ~82% of the voting power, outside shareholders have no mechanism to force a portfolio response.
  Industry Commentary
Asia Travel Retail Is Still the Swing Factor
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Prestige beauty's most profitable channel for a decade was duty-free — airports, Hainan, Korea — stocked heavily by resellers serving mainland Chinese demand. When that flow broke, the damage to manufacturers was amplified by destocking: retailers holding inventory stop ordering entirely, so shipments fall far more than consumption does. ELC's revenue fell 10.3% in FY2023 and 8.2% in FY2025 on that mechanism. FY2026 was the first year it stopped subtracting — but the company's own description is mixed: growth in Korea and Hainan, decline in mainland China travel retail on duty-free retailer transitions at the Beijing and Shanghai airports. The FY2027 guide leans on this channel recovering into an easy comparison. It is the single assumption with the most leverage on the outcome, and it is the one ELC controls least.
Prestige Is Being Squeezed From Below
ONGOING HEADWIND
The clearest evidence sits in the relative market values. e.l.f. Beauty does $1.76B of revenue and is worth $5.70B; ELC does $15.05B and is worth $34.96B. On a revenue multiple the challenger is valued at roughly 3.2× sales against ELC's 2.32× — the market pays more per dollar of mass-market beauty revenue than per dollar of prestige. Meanwhile Coty, the other legacy prestige operator, carries $5.81B of revenue and a $2.35B market cap. The category where this bites is makeup, 28.4% of ELC's revenue and its slowest grower at +2%. Skin care and fragrance have defended their price points; colour cosmetics, where the product is more replicable and the purchase more impulsive, have not. This is not a China problem and travel-retail normalisation will not solve it.
Fragrance Is the Category That Works
EMERGING TAILWIND
Fragrance grew 12% in FY2026 to $2,779M, adding roughly $288M — about 40% of the group's entire $723M revenue increase — from 18.5% of the base, and kept a 10% growth rate in Q4. It is high-margin, it is harder to counterfeit or commoditise than colour cosmetics, and ELC's Jo Malone / Le Labo / TOM FORD / KILIAN stable is genuinely well positioned in the niche-luxury tier that has driven the category globally. Bull framing: if fragrance keeps compounding at low double digits and makeup merely stops shrinking, the group hits its 3–5% organic guide without needing a China miracle, and the margin path to 12.7–13.5% carries the earnings. Bear framing: at 18.5% of sales, fragrance cannot offset a 28.4% category growing at 2% and a 29.6% region growing at 1%. The arithmetic requires the Americas and makeup to both inflect, and neither has yet.
