01 Company Overview

Elevance Health is the largest Blue Cross Blue Shield licensee in the United States, operating Anthem-branded and affiliated plans across 14 states and serving 44.9 million medical members as of September 30, 2026. Beyond the insurance franchise, the company has built two large services businesses — CarelonRx, a PBM (pharmacy benefit manager), and Carelon Services, a care-delivery, behavioral-health and payment-integrity platform.

Fiscal 2025 total revenue was $199.1B, up 12.5% year over year (total operating revenue of $197.6B, +12.8%), making Elevance the 4th-largest company in the US Healthcare Plans group by trailing revenue (behind UnitedHealth, CVS and Cigna, whose totals include large pharmacy businesses; StockAnalysis.com) and it ranks #121 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization on October 22, 2026. But the top line is not the story: 2025 GAAP EPS of $25.21 and adjusted EPS of $30.29 were both down versus 2024 ($25.68 / $33.04), because the benefit expense ratio deteriorated 150 basis points to 90.0%.

The TTM picture through Q3 2026 is weaker still: revenue $201.8B (+3.6% YoY), net income $5.14B, GAAP EPS $23.45 — a 2.55% net margin on a business that earned 4.44% in 2021. After the third quarter, management guided full-year 2026 adjusted EPS to "at least $29.00" (raised from "at least $27.00" in July, "at least $26.75" in April and an initial "at least $25.50" in January) and GAAP EPS to "at least $22.00". For 2027 it targets at least 12% adjusted EPS growth, measured not from the $29.00 guide but from a lower "earnings baseline of at least $26.75", which CFO Mark Kaye said on the call was raised this quarter on third-quarter operating outperformance and a better full-year outlook for Individual ACA — at least about $29.96 a share if the target is met (our arithmetic).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
BCBS LICENSE — 14 STATES 44.9M MEDICAL MEMBERS PROVIDER DISCOUNT SCALE CAPTIVE PBM (CARELONRX) REGULATORY CAPITAL BARRIER BLUECARD NATIONAL NETWORK STICKY EMPLOYER ASO BOOK

Elevance's moat is fundamentally local and contractual, not technological. The Blue Cross Blue Shield license grants exclusive-brand rights in 14 states where Anthem plans typically hold leading or near-leading commercial share. That share converts into provider-discount leverage: the plan with the most covered lives in a market negotiates the deepest unit-cost concessions, which in turn produces the lowest premiums, which defends the share. It is a genuine flywheel, and it is why Elevance's commercial book has proven the most durable part of the portfolio — commercial fee-based membership grew 2.2% to 27.6M in the year to September 30, 2026 even as Medicare Advantage, Medicaid and employer risk-based membership shrank. The BlueCard program (6.60M members) extends that local network into a national offering without Elevance having to build one. Vertical integration adds a second layer: CarelonRx captures pharmacy margin that would otherwise leak to CVS or Cigna, and Carelon Services internalizes utilization management. Regulatory capital requirements and state licensure make it effectively impossible for a new insurer to start from scratch; the competitive set has been the same six or seven companies for two decades.

What the moat conspicuously does not do is protect margins from a cost-trend shock. Premiums are set annually (or, in Medicaid, by state fiat), medical costs are incurred continuously, and when the two diverge the insurer absorbs the entire gap. That is precisely what has happened since 2024.

KEY VULNERABILITIES
Government-program concentration and rate-setting risk. Medicaid (8.22M members, $15.0B of Q3 2026 revenue) and Medicare (2.80M members, $11.2B) together generate roughly 60% of Health Benefits operating revenue, and in both the counterparty setting the price is a government, not a market. Elevance cannot reprice mid-year when acuity runs hot; it can only litigate, lobby, and wait for the next rate cycle. Medicaid membership is down 28.5% since March 2023 — the steepest decline among the five largest Medicaid managed-care companies — while the remaining risk pool got sicker.
Retrospective regulatory exposure on risk adjustment. The $935M accrual booked in Q1 2026 following the February 27, 2026 CMS notice on historical Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment data demonstrates that years-old coding practices can be repriced by regulators at any time. Every large MA carrier faces some version of this; the size of the charge ($4.28/share) shows how little visibility investors have into the tail.
Litigation and antitrust overhang on the Blue system. The $2.67B BCBS subscriber antitrust settlement began paying out May 11, 2026, and the separate $2.8B provider-track settlement was approved in August 2025 with roughly 6,500 large providers (Mayo Clinic, Providence, AdventHealth, University of Michigan Health) opting out to sue independently. New suits from physician staffing groups alleging market allocation and reimbursement suppression were filed in February 2026. The settlements also dismantled the "second Blue bid" restriction — Elevance bid on 11 national accounts in 2025 and won nine, so the structural remedy cuts both ways, but it does erode the exclusivity that underpinned the license moat.
No technology or IP moat against integrated rivals. UnitedHealth's Optum is materially larger and further along in owning physician practices and analytics; CVS owns retail pharmacy, Caremark and Aetna; Cigna owns Evernorth. Elevance's Carelon is the smallest of the four integrated services platforms and, as Carelon Services' nine-month 2026 margin shows (4.9% vs 5.2%), it is not yet insulating group earnings the way Optum does for UNH.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Elevance's 2026 GAAP results are heavily distorted, and the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings is unusually wide — "at least $22.00" GAAP versus "at least $29.00" adjusted, a spread of $7.00 per share, or about 24% of adjusted earnings. The components:

1. CMS Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment accrual — $935M ($4.28/share over nine months). Booked in 2026 following a CMS notice dated February 27, 2026 regarding identified potential exposure on historical MA risk-adjustment data. This is by far the largest single item and is the reason Corporate & Other's nine-month operating loss widened to −$1,279M from −$292M. It is non-cash-timing-uncertain and retrospective, and it does not recur in the underwriting run-rate — but it also does not settle the question of whether further exposure exists.

2. Business optimization charges — $18M in Q3 2026, $147M over nine months. Restructuring charges, excluded from adjusted EPS.

3. Transaction and integration costs — $42M in Q3 2026, $133M over nine months. Ongoing M&A integration, principally in Carelon.

4. Litigation and settlement expense — $8M in Q3 2026, $26M over nine months (FY2025: $24M). Modest at the P&L line, but the underlying legal picture is larger: the $2.67B BCBS subscriber antitrust settlement began distributing payments on May 11, 2026 (accrued years earlier across the Blue system), and the separate $2.8B provider-track settlement was approved in August 2025 with roughly 6,500 providers opting out — meaning a long tail of individual provider suits, plus new February 2026 claims from physician staffing groups.

5. FY2025 comparability items: amortization of intangibles $628M, net losses on financial instruments $653M, transaction/integration $236M, litigation $24M. The investment-portfolio losses are a genuine economic cost that adjusted EPS excludes — worth deducting mentally when comparing the $30.29 adjusted figure to $25.21 GAAP.

6. A one-off running the other way — a $1.85 per-share net non-operating benefit in Q3 2026. It is inside both the $6.27 GAAP and the $6.99 adjusted figure: net investment income jumped to $1,037M from $625M, and CFO Mark Kaye attributed the benefit mainly to valuation adjustments within investment income. Without it, adjusted EPS would have been about $5.14 (our arithmetic). The 2027 growth target runs from a separate "earnings baseline of at least $26.75", $2.25 below the $29.00 guide; Kaye said this quarter's raise to it reflected third-quarter operating outperformance and a better Individual ACA outlook.

Bottom line on quality of earnings: most of the 2026 GAAP-to-adjusted bridge is legitimately non-recurring, but "non-recurring" charges have now appeared in every year since 2023, and this year's adjusted figure is also flattered by a one-off gain. The company's own $26.75 baseline is the better guide to durable earnings power than the $29.00 headline.

08 Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

Elevance is a 44.9-million-member franchise with irreplaceable BCBS licenses in 14 states, trading at 15.5× forward earnings and 0.48× sales with a 1.55% dividend and about $5.0B of buyback authorization. The cost-trend cycle appears to have inflected: full-year adjusted EPS guidance has gone from ≥$25.50 at the start of 2026 to ≥$29.00, the Q3 benefit expense ratio improved 70bps year over year, Medicare Advantage and Individual ACA did better, CarelonRx widened its margin to 5.31% and Carelon Services to 3.8%, and management reaffirmed ≥12% adjusted EPS growth in 2027 from a $26.75 baseline. Medicaid rate resets across 14 states are the mechanical repair for an MLR that is still above 2024 levels — and each 100bps of MLR recovery is worth roughly $1.5B+ of pre-tax income on this premium base. Days in claims payable rose 3.5 days in a year to 44.9, so the improvement is not coming from thinner reserves. A return to a 15% ROE on ~$207 of book value implies earnings power well north of the current run-rate.

BEAR CASE

The low P/E is a symptom, not a bargain. GAAP EPS has fallen from $25.68 (2024) to $23.45 (TTM), net margin from 4.44% (2021) to 2.55%, ROIC to 8.49% — below any plausible cost of capital — and consensus still models FY2026 revenue down 1.35%, producing a PEG of 4.00. The Q3 beat leaned on a $1.85 per-share investment gain; without it adjusted EPS was about $5.14. Health Benefits operating gain fell 27.5% year over year in Q3 2026 to $436M, a 1.0% margin, as one-time spending lifted the operating expense ratio 90bps. Membership keeps shrinking in the risk-bearing lines (Medicare Advantage −15.5%, Medicaid −4.9%, commercial risk −5.2% in a year), the ACA subsidy lapse worsens exchange morbidity, only 8 of 37 Medicare Advantage contracts earned four stars or more for 2027, and a $935M retrospective CMS charge shows regulators can reprice years-old economics at will. "Non-recurring" items have recurred every year since 2023, and after a 52% rally from the March close the stock trades within 1.2% of its 52-week high.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Medical cost trend fails to inflect. The benefit expense ratio rose in each of the last two reported years; Q3 2026 improved (90.6% vs 91.3%), but the nine-month ratio is still flat to slightly worse (89.0% vs 88.9%). On roughly $150B+ of annual premium, every additional 100bps of MLR erases well over $1.5B of pre-tax income — more than 20% of 2025's total segment operating gain. If behavioral health, home health and high-cost pharmacy utilization stay above priced trend into 2027 bids, the ≥12% growth target fails and the multiple compresses on lower earnings, the classic value-trap double-hit.
02
State Medicaid rates stay behind acuity. Elevance's Medicaid membership is down 28.5% since March 2023 — the largest decline among the five biggest Medicaid managed-care companies — while the retained population is materially sicker. Peers reported Q4 2025 Medicaid MLRs of 93.0% (Centene) and 93.5% (Molina), both, in Georgetown University's summary, blaming heavier use of services whose cost trend state rates did not reflect. Rate-setting is annual, political, and constrained by state budgets under post-H.R.1 pressure; Elevance has no unilateral remedy and no ability to reprice mid-year.
03
Regulatory and litigation tail risk on retrospective economics. The $935M CMS Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment accrual ($4.28/share over nine months) arrived from a single February 2026 notice on historical data. Separately, the $2.8B BCBS provider settlement left roughly 6,500 large providers — including Mayo Clinic and AdventHealth — free to litigate individually, and new physician-staffing antitrust claims were filed in February 2026. These are unquantifiable, uninsurable, and land in Corporate & Other, where they have already widened the nine-month operating loss from −$292M to −$1,279M.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
Medical Cost Trend Inflation Across Managed Care
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the single variable that explains the entire sector's de-rating, and Elevance is a pure expression of it. The company's benefit expense ratio moved from 88.5% in FY2024 → 90.0% in FY2025 (+150bps), with Q4 2025 spiking to 93.5%; Q2 2026 was 89.7% (+80bps year over year) and Q3 2026 90.6%, the first clear year-over-year improvement (−70bps). The drivers named by management and by peers are consistent across the industry: behavioral health, home health, and high-cost specialty pharmaceuticals (including GLP-1s) running above the trend assumptions embedded in premium and capitation rates. On the Q3 call management described commercial cost trend as elevated but stable and in line with pricing, and Medicaid trend as elevated but broadly stable versus Q2. Even so, Health Benefits operating gain fell to $436M in Q3 2026 from $601M, a 1.0% segment margin against 1.4%, because the company is spending on what it calls targeted one-time investments: the adjusted operating expense ratio rose 90bps to 11.3%. One signal has turned the right way: days in claims payable rose to 44.9 from 41.4 a year earlier, a thicker reserve cushion. The offset: pricing is being reset upward, guidance was raised in July and again in October, and management is underwriting ≥12% adjusted EPS growth for 2027.
Medicaid Redetermination Aftermath & State Rate Adequacy
ONGOING HEADWIND
When pandemic-era continuous Medicaid coverage ended on March 31, 2023, the five largest Medicaid managed-care organizations shed a combined 8.9 million members (−20%). Elevance lost the most in percentage terms: −28.5%, versus Centene −23.3%, CVS/Aetna −16.8%, UnitedHealth −11.9% and Molina −5.5%. The problem is not the volume, it is the composition: healthy members disenrolled disproportionately, leaving a residual pool with materially higher acuity, while state capitation rates were set on pre-redetermination experience. The result is a mismatch between rates and acuity (how sick the members are), and the evidence is in the numbers — Elevance's Q3 2026 Medicaid revenue rose 5.7% to $15.0B on membership that fell 4.9% to 8.22M, meaning revenue per member is climbing; whether it is climbing fast enough is the open question, since management still calls Medicaid cost trend elevated. Peer Q4 2025 Medicaid MLRs of 93.0% (Centene) and 93.5% (Molina) were both higher than the companies had expected. Post-H.R.1 (enacted July 4, 2025), the Big Five lost a further 912,000 members (−2.5%), and state budgets are tightening — which makes the rate catch-up slower and more contested, not faster.
ACA Subsidy Policy — Bull vs Bear, Framed
ONGOING HEADWIND
The ACA enhanced premium tax credits expired on December 31, 2025 without an extension. CBO projected 3.6–3.8 million fewer insured by 2030–2035 and gross premiums 7.6% lower annually over 2026–2035 had they been extended; the Urban Institute and Commonwealth Fund put 2026 coverage loss as high as 4.8 million, with 7.3 million fewer subsidized enrollees. Net premium increases for currently-subsidized members range from $169–$919 (below 250% of poverty) to $4,436–$8,471 (above 400%). Elevance's Individual/ACA book is 1.35M members at September 30, 2026, down 0.4% year over year, generating $2.5B of Q3 revenue (+6.6%).

The bull framing: Elevance's Individual ACA line was called out as improving in Q2 2026 and was one of the two lines behind the Q3 improvement in the benefit expense ratio; management now expects a modestly higher full-year margin there. The company has priced for a deteriorated risk pool, membership has barely moved, and any legislative restoration — full or partial, prospective or retroactive — is upside on 1.35M members. Combined with Medicaid rate resets, a 15.5× forward multiple, and a Health Benefits margin of just 1.0% in Q3, the recovery math is large: a return toward an 88.5% MLR alone would restore well over $2B of pre-tax income.

The bear framing: subsidy lapse is textbook adverse selection — the healthiest enrollees exit first and the residual pool's morbidity rises, exactly the dynamic that produced the July 2025 guidance cut. It compounds, rather than offsets, the Medicaid acuity problem, and it arrives while Medicare Advantage membership is down 15.5%, commercial risk membership down 5.2%, and total medical membership down 1.1% to 44.9M (year to September 30, 2026). A shrinking risk-bearing book with a loss ratio still above 2024 levels and a sub-cost-of-capital 8.49% ROIC is not a cheap stock; it is a business whose earnings base has to be rebuilt before the multiple means anything. The market's response to a 21% earnings beat in July 2026 — an 8.5% fall on the day — and to October's larger beat — down 1.37% on the day, up 1.2% over two sessions — is the honest scoreboard on which framing currently has the burden of proof.
