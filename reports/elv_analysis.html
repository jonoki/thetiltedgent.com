01 Company Overview

Elevance Health is the largest Blue Cross Blue Shield licensee in the United States, operating Anthem-branded and affiliated plans across 14 states and serving 44.9 million medical members as of June 30, 2026. Beyond the insurance franchise, the company has built two large services businesses — CarelonRx, a PBM (pharmacy benefit manager), and Carelon Services, a care-delivery, behavioral-health and payment-integrity platform.

Fiscal 2025 total revenue was $199.1B, up 12.6% year over year (total operating revenue of $197.6B, +12.8%), making Elevance roughly the 3rd-largest US managed-care organization by revenue and the #191 largest US-listed company by market capitalization (#288 globally). But the top line is not the story: 2025 GAAP EPS of $25.21 and adjusted EPS of $30.29 were both down versus 2024 ($25.68 / $33.04), because the benefit expense ratio deteriorated 150 basis points to 90.0%.

The TTM picture through Q2 2026 is weaker still: revenue $201.1B (+6.3% YoY), net income $4.96B, GAAP EPS $22.47 — a 2.47% net margin on a business that earned 4.44% in 2021. Management has guided full-year 2026 adjusted EPS to "at least $27.00" (raised from an initial "at least $25.50") and told investors it targets at least 12% adjusted EPS growth in 2027 off that base.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
BCBS LICENSE — 14 STATES 44.9M MEDICAL MEMBERS PROVIDER DISCOUNT SCALE CAPTIVE PBM (CARELONRX) REGULATORY CAPITAL BARRIER BLUECARD NATIONAL NETWORK STICKY EMPLOYER ASO BOOK

Elevance's moat is fundamentally local and contractual, not technological. The Blue Cross Blue Shield license grants exclusive-brand rights in 14 states where Anthem plans typically hold leading or near-leading commercial share. That share converts into provider-discount leverage: the plan with the most covered lives in a market negotiates the deepest unit-cost concessions, which in turn produces the lowest premiums, which defends the share. It is a genuine flywheel, and it is why Elevance's commercial book has proven the most durable part of the portfolio — fee-based membership actually grew 1.3% to 27.5M even as every risk-bearing line shrank. The BlueCard program (6.58M members) extends that local network into a national offering without Elevance having to build one. Vertical integration adds a second layer: CarelonRx captures pharmacy margin that would otherwise leak to CVS or Cigna, and Carelon Services internalizes utilization management. Regulatory capital requirements and state licensure make de-novo entry effectively impossible; the competitive set has been the same six or seven companies for two decades.

What the moat conspicuously does not do is protect margins from a cost-trend shock. Premiums are set annually (or, in Medicaid, by state fiat), medical costs are incurred continuously, and when the two diverge the insurer absorbs the entire gap. That is precisely what has happened since 2024.

KEY VULNERABILITIES
Government-program concentration and rate-setting risk. Medicaid (8.36M members, $14.4B quarterly revenue) and Medicare (2.79M members, $11.0B) together generate roughly 60% of Health Benefits premium revenue, and in both the counterparty setting the price is a government, not a market. Elevance cannot reprice mid-year when acuity runs hot; it can only litigate, lobby, and wait for the next rate cycle. Medicaid membership is down 28.5% since March 2023 — the steepest decline among the five largest Medicaid MCOs — while the remaining risk pool got sicker.
Retrospective regulatory exposure on risk adjustment. The $935M accrual booked in Q1 2026 following the February 27, 2026 CMS notice on historical Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment data demonstrates that years-old coding practices can be repriced by regulators at any time. Every large MA carrier faces some version of this; the size of the charge ($4.27/share) shows how little visibility investors have into the tail.
Litigation and antitrust overhang on the Blue system. The $2.67B BCBS subscriber antitrust settlement began paying out May 11, 2026, and the separate $2.8B provider-track settlement was approved in August 2025 with roughly 6,500 large providers (Mayo Clinic, Providence, AdventHealth, University of Michigan Health) opting out to sue independently. New suits from physician staffing groups alleging market allocation and reimbursement suppression were filed in February 2026. The settlements also dismantled the "second Blue bid" restriction — Elevance bid on 11 national accounts in 2025 and won nine, so the structural remedy cuts both ways, but it does erode the exclusivity that underpinned the license moat.
No technology or IP moat against integrated rivals. UnitedHealth's Optum is materially larger and further along in owning physician practices and analytics; CVS owns retail pharmacy, Caremark and Aetna; Cigna owns Evernorth. Elevance's Carelon is the smallest of the four integrated services platforms and, as Q2 2026 showed (operating margin 4.59% vs 5.38%), it is not yet insulating group earnings the way Optum does for UNH.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Elevance's 2026 GAAP results are heavily distorted, and the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings is unusually wide — "at least $20.10" GAAP versus "at least $27.00" adjusted, a spread of roughly $6.90 per share, or about 25% of adjusted earnings. The components:

1. CMS Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment accrual — $935M ($4.27/share). Booked in 2026 following a CMS notice dated February 27, 2026 regarding identified potential exposure on historical MA risk-adjustment data. This is by far the largest single item and is the reason Corporate & Other's year-to-date operating loss widened to −$1,204M from −$211M. It is non-cash-timing-uncertain and retrospective, and it does not recur in the underwriting run-rate — but it also does not settle the question of whether further exposure exists.

2. Business optimization charges — $129M year-to-date 2026. Restructuring tied to the workforce and capability investments management described as "accelerating."

3. Transaction and integration costs — $44M in Q2 2026, $91M year-to-date. Ongoing M&A integration, principally in Carelon.

4. Litigation and settlement expense — $11M in Q2 2026, $18M year-to-date (FY2025: $24M). Modest at the P&L line, but the underlying legal picture is larger: the $2.67B BCBS subscriber antitrust settlement began distributing payments on May 11, 2026 (accrued years earlier across the Blue system), and the separate $2.8B provider-track settlement was approved in August 2025 with roughly 6,500 providers opting out — meaning a long tail of individual provider suits, plus new February 2026 claims from physician staffing groups.

5. FY2025 comparability items: amortization of intangibles $628M, net losses on financial instruments $653M, transaction/integration $236M, litigation $24M. The investment-portfolio losses are a genuine economic cost that adjusted EPS excludes — worth deducting mentally when comparing the $30.29 adjusted figure to $25.21 GAAP.

Bottom line on quality of earnings: most of the 2026 GAAP-to-adjusted bridge is legitimately non-recurring, but "non-recurring" charges have now appeared in every year since 2023. An investor underwriting $27+ of durable earnings power should haircut for a normalized $1.50–$2.00 per year of items that keep recurring.

08 Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

Elevance is a 44.9-million-member franchise with irreplaceable BCBS licenses in 14 states, trading at 14.7× forward earnings and 0.43× sales with a 1.73% dividend and $5.3B of buyback authorization. The cost-trend cycle appears to have inflected: management has raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance twice in 2026 (from ≥$25.50 to ≥$27.00), Q2 beat consensus by 20%, Individual/ACA performance improved, CarelonRx expanded margin to 5.17%, and management has publicly committed to ≥12% adjusted EPS growth in 2027. Medicaid rate resets across 14 states are the mechanical repair for an MLR that is 150bps above 2024 levels — and each 100bps of MLR recovery is worth roughly $1.5B+ of pre-tax income on this premium base. A return to a 15% ROE on ~$206 of book value implies earnings power well north of the current run-rate.

BEAR CASE

The low P/E is a symptom, not a bargain. GAAP EPS has fallen from $25.68 (2024) to $22.47 (TTM), net margin from 4.44% (2021) to 2.47%, ROIC to 8.46% — below any plausible cost of capital — and consensus models FY2026 revenue down 1.45% and EPS down 10.3%, producing a PEG of 4.04. Health Benefits operating gain fell 42.6% year over year in Q2 2026 with the benefit expense ratio still rising 80bps; Carelon Services margin compressed too, so the integrated hedge is not working. Membership is shrinking across every risk-bearing line (MA −15.9%, Medicaid −4.3%, commercial risk −4.6%), the ACA subsidy lapse worsens exchange morbidity, and a $935M retrospective CMS charge shows regulators can reprice years-old economics at will. "Non-recurring" items have recurred every year since 2023.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Medical cost trend fails to inflect. The benefit expense ratio has risen in each of the last two reported years and again in Q2 2026 (89.7% vs 88.9%). On roughly $150B+ of annual premium, every additional 100bps of MLR erases well over $1.5B of pre-tax income — more than 20% of 2025's total segment operating gain. If behavioral health, home health and high-cost pharmacy utilization stay above priced trend into 2027 bids, the ≥12% growth target fails and the multiple compresses on lower earnings, the classic value-trap double-hit.
02
State Medicaid rates stay behind acuity. Elevance's Medicaid membership is down 28.5% since March 2023 — the largest decline among the five biggest Medicaid MCOs — while the retained population is materially sicker. Peers reported Q4 2025 Medicaid MLRs of 93.0% (Centene) and 93.5% (Molina), both attributing the overrun to services "not reflected in capitation rates." Rate-setting is annual, political, and constrained by state budgets under post-H.R.1 pressure; Elevance has no unilateral remedy and no ability to reprice mid-year.
03
Regulatory and litigation tail risk on retrospective economics. The $935M CMS Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment accrual ($4.27/share) arrived from a single February 2026 notice on historical data. Separately, the $2.8B BCBS provider settlement left roughly 6,500 large providers — including Mayo Clinic and AdventHealth — free to litigate individually, and new physician-staffing antitrust claims were filed in February 2026. These are unquantifiable, uninsurable, and land in Corporate & Other, where they have already widened the year-to-date operating loss from −$211M to −$1,204M.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
Medical Cost Trend Inflation Across Managed Care
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the single variable that explains the entire sector's de-rating, and Elevance is a pure expression of it. The company's benefit expense ratio moved from 88.5% in FY2024 → 90.0% in FY2025 (+150bps) → 89.7% in Q2 2026 (+80bps YoY), with Q4 2025 spiking to 93.5%. The drivers named by management and by peers are consistent across the industry: behavioral health, home health, and high-cost specialty pharmaceuticals (including GLP-1s) running above the trend assumptions embedded in premium and capitation rates. The mechanical consequence at Elevance was Health Benefits operating gain falling 42.6% to $896M in Q2 2026, taking segment operating margin from 3.75% to 2.10%. Two second-order signals deserve attention: days in claims payable fell to 45.4 from 46.6, thinning the reserve cushion, and the operating expense ratio rose 100bps to 11.1% as management spends on medical-cost management capability — so both sides of the expense line are moving against the company simultaneously. The offset: pricing is being reset upward, guidance has been raised twice, and management is underwriting ≥12% adjusted EPS growth for 2027.
Medicaid Redetermination Aftermath & State Rate Adequacy
ONGOING HEADWIND
When pandemic-era continuous Medicaid coverage ended on March 31, 2023, the five largest Medicaid managed-care organizations shed a combined 8.9 million members (−20%). Elevance lost the most in percentage terms: −28.5%, versus Centene −23.3%, CVS/Aetna −16.8%, UnitedHealth −11.9% and Molina −5.5%. The problem is not the volume, it is the composition: healthy members disenrolled disproportionately, leaving a residual pool with materially higher acuity, while state capitation rates were set on pre-redetermination experience. The industry shorthand is "the mismatch between rate and acuity," and the evidence is in the numbers — Elevance's Q2 2026 Medicaid revenue rose 3.8% to $14.4B on membership that fell 4.3% to 8.36M, meaning revenue per member is climbing but not fast enough. Peer Q4 2025 Medicaid MLRs of 93.0% (Centene) and 93.5% (Molina) both exceeded investor expectations to the downside. Post-H.R.1 (enacted July 4, 2025), the Big Five lost a further 912,000 members (−2.5%), and state budgets are tightening — which makes the rate catch-up slower and more contested, not faster.
ACA Subsidy Policy — Bull vs Bear, Framed
ONGOING HEADWIND
The ACA enhanced premium tax credits expired on December 31, 2025 without an extension. CBO projected 3.6–3.8 million fewer insured by 2030–2035 and gross premiums 7.6% lower annually over 2026–2035 had they been extended; the Urban Institute and Commonwealth Fund put 2026 coverage loss as high as 4.8 million, with 7.3 million fewer subsidized enrollees. Net premium increases for currently-subsidized members range from $169–$919 (below 250% of poverty) to $4,436–$8,471 (above 400%). Elevance's Individual/ACA book is 1.32M members, down 2.3% year over year, generating $2.7B of Q2 revenue (+16.8% — rate, not volume).

The bull framing: Elevance's Individual ACA line was explicitly called out as improving in Q2 2026, partially offsetting government cost trend. The company has already priced for a deteriorated risk pool, membership attrition is "anticipated" rather than surprising, and any legislative restoration — full or partial, prospective or retroactive — is unpriced upside on 1.3M members. Combined with Medicaid rate resets, MA star improvement (4+ star membership up 14% YoY, average score 3.86), a 14.7× forward multiple, and a 42.6%-depressed Health Benefits margin base, the recovery math is large: a return toward an 88.5% MLR alone would restore well over $2B of pre-tax income.

The bear framing: subsidy lapse is textbook adverse selection — the healthiest enrollees exit first and the residual pool's morbidity rises, exactly the dynamic that produced the July 2025 guidance cut. It compounds, rather than offsets, the Medicaid acuity problem, and it arrives while MA membership is down 15.9%, commercial risk membership down 4.6%, and total medical membership down 1.5% to 44.9M. A shrinking risk-bearing book with a rising loss ratio and a sub-cost-of-capital 8.46% ROIC is not a cheap stock; it is a business whose earnings base has to be rebuilt before the multiple means anything. The market's response to a 20% earnings beat in July 2026 — a 1.8% pre-market pop that fully faded by month-end — is the honest scoreboard on which framing currently has the burden of proof.
