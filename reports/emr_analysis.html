01 Company Overview

Emerson Electric is a global industrial technology company that, after a five-year portfolio overhaul, now sells almost exclusively into automation — measurement and analytical sensors, final control (valves, actuators, regulators), distributed control systems, industrial software, and test & measurement instrumentation. Its customers are the operators of continuous-process plants: refineries, LNG trains, chemical complexes, pharmaceutical and biologics facilities, power generators, water utilities and, increasingly, semiconductor fabs and data centers.

Fiscal 2025 (year ended September 2025) revenue was $18.02B, up 3.0% from $17.49B in FY2024; on a trailing-twelve-month basis through the June 2026 quarter revenue is $18.64B, up 4.8% YoY. Emerson is the 141st largest US public company and 277th largest in the world by market capitalization at roughly $88B, and it carries one of the longest dividend-increase streaks in the entire market at 69 consecutive years.

The most recent reported quarter — fiscal Q3 2026, ended June 30, 2026, reported August 4, 2026 — showed net sales of $4,873M (+7% reported, +6% underlying), adjusted EPS of $1.71 (+13%) against GAAP EPS of $1.28, adjusted segment EBITA margin of 28.5%, and free cash flow of $1,323M, up 36%. Management raised full-year FY2026 guidance to ~5% reported sales growth, ~3.5% underlying growth, adjusted EPS of ~$6.55 and free cash flow of ~$3.6B.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Installed Base Lock-In Safety-Certified Specification DeltaV / Ovation DCS Standard AspenTech Simulation Models $1.68B Software ACV Global Service Network 69-Year Dividend Record

Emerson's moat is switching cost compounded by consequence of failure. A distributed control system is the nervous system of a refinery or an LNG train; replacing it means re-engineering interlocks, re-validating safety cases and taking the plant down. Operators do not do this on price. The same logic runs through Final Control and Sensors: once a Fisher valve trim or a Micro Motion meter is written into a plant's P&ID and safety documentation, the incumbent captures decades of MRO and upgrade revenue at margins the original equipment sale never earned. That is why Intelligent Devices can expand adjusted EBITA margin 240 bps to 27.9% on only +5% underlying growth.

The AspenTech layer deepens this. Process-simulation models are built once, calibrated against a specific plant, and then embedded in how engineers plan turnarounds and optimize yields for years. Software ACV of $1.68B growing 9% is a recurring, high-incremental-margin annuity attached to a hardware base that is already sticky, and the combination is what has taken company gross margin from 44.3% in FY2021 to 53.2% today — a level far above what a traditional valve-and-instrument maker earns. Finally, scale: Emerson sits inside the top five suppliers who together hold roughly 40% of global process-automation revenue, and the global service-and-engineering footprint required to support that installed base is not something a new entrant can replicate.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Late-cycle capex dependence. Final Control, at 32.5% of revenue, is levered to greenfield project awards in oil & gas, LNG and chemicals. Its +3% underlying growth versus +23% at Test & Measurement shows the divergence: when energy operators defer FID on projects, orders stop before revenue does, and the backlog cushion — $8.2B, roughly 1.7 quarters of sales — only buys so much time. A book-to-bill of exactly 1.0 means backlog is currently being replaced, not built.

2. Geopolitical and Middle East concentration. Management quantified a ~$25M Q3 revenue headwind and ~$100M estimated annual impact from Strait of Hormuz disruption, and noted regional customer operating capacity running at roughly 75%. Gulf hydrocarbon projects are among the largest single sources of process-automation orders globally; a prolonged disruption is a direct, measurable revenue hit, not an abstraction.

3. Leverage taken on to build the pure-play. Total debt of $13.80B against $2.18B of cash leaves net debt around $11.6B and an enterprise value of $99.53B versus an $87.91B market cap. The National Instruments purchase and the March 2025 AspenTech minority buy-in both consumed cash, and the resulting intangible amortization is why GAAP EPS of $1.28 sits 25% below adjusted EPS of $1.71. The current ratio of 0.90 is thin for an industrial.

4. A competitive set that just got sharper. Honeywell completed its Aerospace spin-off in June 2026 and now operates as Honeywell Technologies, an automation pure-play in its own right, following the October 2025 Solstice separation. Emerson's structural argument — that a focused automation company deserves a software-like multiple — is now being made simultaneously by a competitor with $38.06B of revenue growing 11.0%, more than double Emerson's revenue base. Siemens, Schneider and ABB are all pushing the same industrial-software narrative, and ABB grew revenue 18.6% year over year.

Notable One-Off Items

These are material and they distort every historical comparison. Emerson's reported financials over the last five years are the accounting shadow of a complete portfolio rebuild, and the GAAP series should not be read as an operating trend.

The Copeland divestiture gain (FY2023). FY2023 net income was $13,219M on $15,165M of revenue — an 87% net margin and $22.88 of diluted EPS. This is not an operating result. Blackstone acquired a majority stake in Climate Technologies (renamed Copeland) in May 2023, and the transaction produced an enormous one-time gain. Emerson completed the sale of its remaining Copeland interests to Blackstone in August 2024 for approximately $3.5B. Any five-year earnings CAGR computed through FY2023 is meaningless.

National Instruments purchase accounting (FY2024 onward). The NI acquisition closed in October 2023 and brought a large step-up in intangible assets. FY2024 net income fell to $1,968M from $13,219M — almost entirely the absence of the prior-year gain, compounded by amortization and integration cost. Emerson disclosed $26M of integration-related stock compensation expense in the year-ago Q3 alone.

The AspenTech buy-in (March 2025). Emerson first took 55% of AspenTech in May 2022 by contributing OSI and its Geological Simulation Software business, then acquired all remaining outstanding shares in March 2025. This eliminates the noncontrolling interest line, changes the shape of consolidated earnings, and adds further intangible amortization. Cross-period software growth comparisons before and after this date are not clean.

The GAAP-to-adjusted gap. Q3 FY2026 GAAP EPS was $1.28 versus adjusted EPS of $1.71 — a $0.43, or 25%, spread made up of intangible amortization, restructuring and integration items. TTM GAAP EPS of $4.57 against FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of ~$6.55 reflects the same wedge. Investors paying 22.37x forward earnings are paying it on the adjusted number.

Currency and geopolitics. Underlying sales growth strips out currency and portfolio effects; the Q3 gap between +7% reported and +6% underlying was favorable currency and portfolio. Separately, Strait of Hormuz disruption cost roughly $25M of Q3 revenue with a ~$100M estimated annual impact — disclosed, quantified, and excluded from neither figure.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Emerson has finished a five-year rebuild — Copeland out, National Instruments in, AspenTech fully owned — and now runs a genuine automation pure-play with 53.2% gross margin, 28.5% adjusted segment EBITA margin, and $1.68B of software ACV growing 9%. Demand is broadening rather than narrowing: power orders +31%, T&M orders +19% with semiconductors +70%, growth verticals +27%, backlog $8.2B (+7%) and a project funnel up $1.2B sequentially to $12.4B. At 22.37x forward earnings the stock is the cheapest in its peer set (Rockwell 29.97, Honeywell 24.06), generates ~$3.6B of guided free cash flow, and pays a dividend it has raised for 69 consecutive years. If FY2027 guidance validates the ~10% EPS growth consensus expects, the multiple gap versus automation peers has room to close.

Bear Case

You are paying 34.48x trailing GAAP earnings and a 2.52 PEG for a business whose largest segment grew 3% and whose second-largest grew 2%. The growth is concentrated in the two smallest units, and 25% of "earnings" is an adjustment — GAAP EPS is $1.28 against $1.71 adjusted, the residue of $13.80B of acquisition debt and the intangibles it bought. Book-to-bill is exactly 1.0, meaning backlog is being replaced, not built. Five-year total return of 11.35% annualized has trailed both the S&P 500 (13.39%) and the industrial sector (13.74%) despite the entire transformation, and Honeywell Technologies now competes for the same pure-play-automation re-rating with a revenue base twice as large growing 11.0%. ROE of 12.80% and a current ratio of 0.90 are not the metrics of a company that should trade at a premium multiple.

Top 3 Risks
01
Late-cycle capex reversal. Final Control and Sensors together are 55% of revenue and depend on FIDs in oil & gas, LNG, chemicals and power. Final Control already decelerated to +3% underlying. A funding pause across the $12.4B project funnel would show up in orders within two quarters and revenue within four, and the $8.2B backlog covers only about 1.7 quarters of sales.
02
Multiple compression from peer competition. The premium in EMR's 34.48x trailing P/E rests on being the focused automation-plus-software asset. Honeywell Technologies became a direct pure-play competitor in June 2026, and ABB (+18.6% revenue growth), Schneider and Siemens are all pushing industrial software. If the "automation pure-play deserves a software multiple" argument gets crowded, the scarcity premium compresses independent of Emerson's own execution.
03
Balance sheet and integration risk. $13.80B of debt against $2.18B of cash, a 0.90 current ratio, a 0.62 quick ratio, and ROIC of 11.38% that must justify goodwill from two large acquisitions. Any impairment of NI or AspenTech intangibles, or a rate environment that makes refinancing costlier, would hit reported earnings and constrain the buyback that supports the 2.34% shareholder yield.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
Process Automation Capex Is Being Re-Pointed From Hydrocarbons to Electrons
EMERGING TAILWIND
The process-automation market is a ~$120.65B business growing at only ~5.13% CAGR toward $154.92B by 2031 — a mature, GDP-plus category. What has changed is the composition of the spending. Emerson's power-generation orders grew 31% in Q3 FY2026 on fleet modernization, new gas-fired capacity and nuclear, with the power funnel up $450M to $3.0B; LNG added $350M to reach $2.2B. Life sciences supplies a third leg, where continuous bioprocessing requires far denser in-line measurement than legacy batch manufacturing. The offset is real: the Strait of Hormuz disruption cost ~$25M in Q3 and an estimated ~$100M annually, with Gulf customers running near 75% capacity. Net, the mix shift toward power and data-center-adjacent generation is a genuine tailwind, but it is arriving into a market whose baseline growth rate is mid-single-digit, not double.
Industrial Software and the "AI in Operations" Pitch
EMERGING TAILWIND
The industry narrative is "a broad pivot from reactive maintenance toward predictive, software-defined control architectures that embed artificial intelligence at the edge." Emerson has spent $10B-plus positioning for exactly this: AspenTech taken to 100% in March 2025, OSI grid software contributed in 2022, and a Chief Technology and AI Officer role now sitting in the C-suite. The measurable proof point is software ACV of $1.68B growing 9% against a stated 10%+ target — respectable, but not the 15–20% that would justify a genuine software multiple. Control Systems & Software earns 32.6% adjusted EBITA margin, the best in the portfolio, so the mix math works if ACV growth accelerates. The bear observation is that every competitor tells the same story, and Emerson's ACV is growing roughly in line with, not ahead of, its own hardware-heavy segments.
Portfolio Simplification Has Swept the Multi-Industrials — and Erased Emerson's Scarcity Premium
ONGOING HEADWIND
Emerson moved first: Daniel Measurement sold in 2021, InSinkErator to Whirlpool in 2022, Climate Technologies (Copeland) majority-sold to Blackstone in May 2023 with the residual stake sold for ~$3.5B in August 2024, National Instruments bought in October 2023, AspenTech fully absorbed in March 2025. For two years that made Emerson the cleanest way to own process automation in US large-cap. That window has closed. Honeywell spun Solstice Advanced Materials in October 2025 and completed its Aerospace separation in June 2026, launching Honeywell Technologies as a pure-play automation company with $38.06B of revenue growing 11.0%.

Bull framing: the whole sector re-rating on focus benefits the company that got there first with the highest software mix and the cheapest forward multiple (22.37x vs Rockwell's 29.97x). Emerson has the completed balance sheet work behind it while peers are still absorbing separation costs.

Bear framing: scarcity was doing real work in EMR's multiple. With three or four credible automation pure-plays competing for the same fund flows, the valuation argument reverts to relative growth — where Emerson's +4.8% TTM revenue growth ranks below ABB (+18.6%), Rockwell (+11.3%) and Honeywell (+11.0%). Simplification is now table stakes, not a differentiator.
