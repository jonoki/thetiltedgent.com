01 Company Overview

Enbridge is North America's largest listed midstream company — first by market value in StockAnalysis's Oil & Gas Midstream industry list at $106.29B, ahead of Williams ($86.38B) and Enterprise Products ($82.55B). It runs four businesses: Liquids Pipelines (the Mainline and its US extensions, which carry the bulk of Canadian crude exports), Gas Transmission, Gas Distribution & Storage (regulated gas utilities in Ontario, Quebec and, since 2024, the US), and Renewable Power. FY2025 adjusted EBITDA was C$19,952M, up 7.2%, and the company met its financial guidance for the 20th consecutive year.

The usual description of Enbridge — a pipeline toll road — mostly holds, and the numbers say how much. Liquids Pipelines and Gas Transmission together produced 75.7% of FY2025 adjusted EBITDA; another 20.7% came from rate-regulated gas utilities. That is why this page leads with DCF (distributable cash flow) per share and not earnings per share: GAAP earnings are whipsawed by non-cash marks on Enbridge's currency, rate and commodity hedges — trailing EPS of C$2.88 makes the C$3.88 dividend look 135% paid out, while the same dividend is 67% of trailing DCF. The toll road is not perfectly smooth, though: Liquids Pipelines EBITDA fell 12.1% year on year in Q1 2026 (C$2,303M vs C$2,621M), was flat in Q2 and fell 4.4% in Q3 (C$2,206M vs C$2,307M). And the road is expensive to widen. Leverage ended Q3 2026 at 5.1x debt-to-EBITDA, slightly above the 4.5–5.0x target range. On September 9, 2026 Enbridge paired a US$2.55B all-cash purchase of Tallgrass Energy's crude oil business with a bought-deal equity offering (C$2.6B and 38.9M shares in the September 10 prospectus supplement; C$3.0B and 44.7M shares once the underwriters' 15% over-allotment option was fully exercised, closed September 14, per the Q3 release); a day earlier, on September 8, it announced that CEO Greg Ebel will retire at year-end. The NYSE line fell 3.85% the next day on 12.1M shares, about double a normal session.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Irreplaceable rights-of-way Mainline apportioned — demand exceeds capacity Regulated utility returns Take-or-pay gas contracts C$38B secured backlog 31 straight dividend increases 20 years of meeting guidance

A toll road earns its moat from permits that cannot be obtained again. No one is going to get a new cross-border crude line permitted from Alberta to the Great Lakes on any timeline an investor cares about, so the barrels that need to move keep paying Enbridge's toll. When the Mainline is apportioned, it is the shipper that bears the shortage, not Enbridge. The gas utilities earn a regulated return on capital, and gas transmission is contracted by capacity rather than volume. The weakness sits on the other side of the balance sheet. The toll road is funded with a lot of debt, it pays out about two-thirds of its cash, and it relies on continued access to both bond and equity markets to fund growth.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage is above the target range. Debt-to-EBITDA was 4.8x at the end of 2025, 5.0x at Q1 2026 and 5.1x at both Q2 and Q3 2026, against a stated 4.5–5.0x range. The Q3 release attributes the Q3 reading to currency: debt was translated at the quarter-end rate of 1.42 CAD/USD, while EBITDA was translated at the trailing average of 1.39. The equity raise (C$3.0B as completed) and an all-cash US$2.55B acquisition, announced together, show that growth is currently being funded partly with new shares.
02
Dilution is how growth gets funded. The offering as completed was 44.7M shares for C$3.0B (Q3 release), about 2.1% of the ~2,180M shares outstanding before it: the base deal of 38.9M shares at C$66.85 in the September 10 prospectus supplement, plus the underwriters' 15% over-allotment option, fully exercised. The 2026 plan calls for about C$10B of growth capital and about C$10B of debt issuance. Per-share DCF, not total DCF, is the number that has to grow. Guidance for 2023–2026 is only about 3% a year in DCF per share, even though EBITDA grows 7–9%.
03
Rate sensitivity. A 5.7% dividend yield with about 3% dividend growth is priced much like a long bond. Beta is 0.79, but the security is exposed to interest rates rather than equity-market swings. Rising yields raise Enbridge's refinancing cost and cut the value of its payout at the same time.
04
Leadership change during a heavy capital cycle. The CEO who set the current capital plan leaves on December 31, 2026, with a C$38B backlog, an acquisition still to close and leverage above target. Harradence comes from the utility business, and the market will look for continuity in the first guidance issued under her.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings is large and has mostly been non-cash. In Q3 2026, GAAP earnings attributable to common shareholders were C$1,288M (C$0.59/share) against C$682M (C$0.30) a year earlier, and adjusted earnings were C$1,074M (C$0.49 against C$0.46). For the nine months, GAAP was C$2.00 against C$2.34 and adjusted C$2.10 against C$2.14. In Q2 2026, GAAP earnings had fallen to C$1,396M (C$0.64/share) from C$2,177M (C$1.00) while adjusted earnings were flat at C$1.4B; Enbridge attributed that decline "primarily" to "non-cash, unrealized changes in the value of derivative financial instruments used to manage foreign exchange, interest rate and commodity price risks," plus a non-cash pre-issuance hedge loss on an exchange of medium-term notes and a non-cash crude inventory adjustment in Liquids Pipelines. The equity offering closed on September 14, 2026, so Q3's weighted share count of 2,192M (2,181M a year earlier) carries only the last two and a half weeks of September of the new shares, and Q4 will carry them in full.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $37.54 to the November 13, 2026 settled close of $47.67, the NYSE line gained 27.0% on price. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $47.67 you pay 11.7x trailing distributable cash flow for the largest midstream network in North America. Its crude mainline is apportioned, its gas pipes are contracted by capacity, and a fifth of EBITDA comes from regulated utilities. The 5.71% dividend is covered at 67% of DCF, has risen for 31 straight years, and management guides DCF per share growth to step up from about 3% to about 5% a year after 2026. The C$38B backlog is secured, Tallgrass is guided as accretive in its first full year, and the September sell-off came from financing and succession news, not from the tolls. Of the 24 analysts on StockAnalysis, 14 rate the stock Buy or Strong Buy.

Bear Case

A toll road with leverage above its own ceiling (5.1x vs 4.5–5.0x), free cash flow of C$0.4B after growth capex, and a habit of issuing shares when it buys assets is not the risk-free annuity the yield suggests. Per-share DCF has grown only about 3% a year even as EBITDA grew 7–9%. That gap is what dilution and interest costs take. Liquids Pipelines, half the company, fell 5.7% in the first nine months of 2026. The CEO leaves in seven weeks. And with StockAnalysis's panel at 9 Holds and one Strong Sell, an average target of $50.70 implies about 6% upside plus the yield.

Top 3 Risks

01
Leverage and funding. Debt-to-EBITDA is 5.1x against a 4.5–5.0x target, the 2026 plan needs about C$10B of new debt, and the company raised C$3.0B of equity in September. If leverage does not return to the range, the dividend-growth story depends on continued share issuance.
02
Rates and currency. The stock is valued on its yield, so rising long rates compress the multiple, as in 2023, when the stock hit its five-year low of $32.04. For USD holders, a weaker Canadian dollar reduces the USD value of a C$-declared dividend. At C$1.4257 per US$, each C$ is worth US$0.701.
03
Execution across a CEO change. A US$2.55B acquisition still has to close, and a C$38B backlog and the Line 5 relocation have to be delivered, all under a new CEO from January 1, 2027. Enbridge has met guidance for 20 consecutive years; the first year under Harradence tests whether that record continues.
Industry Commentary
Gas demand: LNG exports and power burn
EMERGING TAILWIND

Enbridge's recent project announcements are mostly gas projects: Bay Runner Twin (2.6 Bcf/d, 2030), the TTC Connector option into Freeport LNG, and C$9B of new projects sanctioned year to date at Q2 toward a C$10–20B target through 2027. Gas Transmission EBITDA grew 12.9% in FY2025. The CEO's Q1 2026 framing was that "North America's role in the global energy system has become increasingly critical." Over time, this is shifting the mix away from crude toward gas, which is now the growth segment. Crude remains half of EBITDA and has not grown this year.

Midstream consolidation, funded with equity
ONGOING HEADWIND

The Tallgrass crude purchase (US$2.55B, all cash) continues a pattern of large North American pipeline operators buying bolt-on systems that feed their existing networks. Pony Express runs from the Rockies to Cushing. The market reaction shows the trade-off: accretion to DCF per share in year one, as guided, against about 2% dilution from the offering, with leverage already above target. Investors marked the stock down 3.85% on the day, and the bought-deal price of C$66.85 now sets a reference point for the TSX line (the November 13 NYSE close converts to about C$67.96 at the Bank of Canada rate).

Yield stocks and the rate cycle
MACRO DRIVER

ENB's five-year chart follows interest rates closely. It fell to $32.04 in the 2023 rate shock and re-rated to $55.42 as yields came down. Kalkine's coverage of the September 10 decline cited "rising bond yields" alongside the equity raise. For a security yielding 5.71% with about 3% dividend growth, the spread over government bonds sets the price more than any quarterly EBITDA figure.
