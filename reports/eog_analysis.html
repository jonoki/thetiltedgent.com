01 Company Overview

EOG Resources is one of the largest independent crude oil and natural gas exploration & production (E&P) companies in the United States, producing 1,410.4 MBoed (thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day) in Q2 2026 across the Delaware Basin, Eagle Ford, Utica, Powder River Basin and the Dorado dry-gas play, plus international positions in Trinidad, Bahrain and the UAE. Full-year 2025 revenue was $22.65B (−3.5% YoY, on soft oil prices), but the trailing-twelve-month figure has since surged to $26.72B (+17.2%) as the $5.6B Encino Acquisition Partners deal added Utica volumes and WTI averaged $92.85/Bbl in Q2 2026.

EOG is the 160th most valuable US-listed company and roughly the 320th largest company globally by market capitalisation. Its distinguishing feature versus the E&P peer group is capital discipline codified into a hurdle rate: every well must clear a "premium" (30% direct after-tax rate of return at $40 WTI / $2.50 Henry Hub) or "double premium" (60% ATROR at the same deck) screen before it is drilled. That discipline shows up in returns — ROIC of 19.8% and ROE of 22.5% against an E&P peer median near 9–11%.

In a commodity business where nobody controls price, the only durable moat is cost per barrel and cost of capital — and EOG wins on both. The return-hurdle framework is the mechanism: a well is only funded if it clears "premium" economics (30% direct after-tax rate of return at $40 WTI / $2.50 Henry Hub) and the company preferentially drills "double premium" locations (60% ATROR at the same deck). That deck is deliberately punitive — it is roughly half the current strip — so inventory that clears it generates outsized cash at $90 oil. The operational scaffolding underneath is equally important: EOG self-sources its own frac sand and water, owns its gathering and processing, and markets its own barrels, which is why it realised $98.15/Bbl against $92.85 WTI in Q2 2026 while carrying total cash operating costs of just $10.57/Boe. The financial layer completes it. EOG carried net cash of $2.34B at year-end 2024, took on $3.5B of debt to buy Encino, and by mid-2026 was already back to net debt of $3.02B and net-debt-to-capitalisation of 8.7% — an D/E of 0.26 versus a 0.32 peer median and a current ratio of 1.85 versus 1.07. That balance sheet is what lets EOG buy assets counter-cyclically and keep drilling through a price trough while levered peers cut. The result is ROIC of 19.75% against an E&P peer median of ~9.3% — a roughly 2x spread that has persisted across the cycle.

Vulnerability 1 — Price-taker economics. EOG has no pricing power whatsoever. FY2025 revenue fell 3.5% and adjusted EPS fell 19% purely because oil averaged $65.63/Bbl; Q2 2026 net income doubled purely because WTI averaged $92.85. At a 0.28 five-year Beta the stock looks defensive, but that low reading reflects low correlation to equities, not low volatility — the real factor exposure is crude, and a return to $55–60 WTI would compress free cash flow by billions.

Vulnerability 2 — Inventory depletion and the reinvestment treadmill. Shale wells decline 60–70% in year one. EOG must spend ~$6.5B a year simply to grow 5% on oil, and the highest-return "double premium" locations are consumed first. The Encino purchase — $5.6B for 675,000 Utica net acres — is direct evidence that organic inventory alone was no longer sufficient to sustain the runway. Every subsequent year of drilling raises the question of what replaces the top decile.

Vulnerability 3 — Gas price and Dorado timing risk. Roughly 36% of Q2 2026 Boe volumes were natural gas realising $2.77/Mcf — a price at which much dry-gas drilling barely clears cost of capital. The Dorado thesis depends on LNG export capacity and power-sector demand arriving on schedule; if terminal commissioning slips or associated gas from the Permian floods the market, EOG's fastest-growing volume stream is also its lowest-margin one.

Vulnerability 4 — Integration and international execution. Encino is the largest acquisition in EOG's history and a departure from a culture built on organic exploration; the >$150M first-year synergy target and the assumption that Encino acreage clears EOG's own return hurdle are both unproven at scale. Separately, Bahrain and the UAE introduce sovereign, fiscal-regime and geopolitical risk that a pure Lower-48 operator does not carry — encouraging early well results notwithstanding.

Notable One-Off Items

Impairment (Q4 2025): EOG recorded a $689M full-year 2025 impairment, principally a write-down to fair value of natural gas assets in the Barnett Shale and Woodford Oil Window (a $646M component was booked in Q4 2025 and excluded from adjusted earnings). This is the main reason GAAP FY2025 EPS of $9.12 sits $1.04 below adjusted EPS of $10.16, and why Q4 2025 operating margin collapsed to 16.9% versus 33–41% in surrounding quarters. It is non-cash and reflects play-specific economics and capital reallocation, not an operating deterioration.

Encino acquisition accounting (2025–26): The $5.6B purchase — $4.45B of it showing in FY2025 investing cash flow — distorts nearly every year-over-year comparison. It is the reason TTM revenue (+17.2%) diverges so sharply from FY2025 standalone (−3.5%), the reason production is guided +14% for 2026, and the reason the balance sheet flipped from $2.34B net cash at YE2024 to $4.54B net debt at YE2025. Purchase accounting also steps up the depletable asset base, which inflates DD&A ($9.81/Boe in Q2 2026) on acquired volumes.

Non-GAAP vs GAAP capex and FCF: EOG reports FY2025 capital expenditures of $6,294M (non-GAAP) and free cash flow of $4,663M, while third-party data using GAAP investing outflows shows $6,594M of capex and $3,450M of FCF. The gap is definitional (asset retirement, non-cash items, acquisition-related additions), not a discrepancy — but comparisons across sources should specify which basis is being used.

Q2 2026 adjustments: Small and clean. GAAP net income of $2,724M ($5.15/share) versus adjusted net income of $2,683M ($5.07/share) — a $41M gain backed out of adjusted results, most likely mark-to-market on commodity derivatives. Nothing material distorts the quarter.

Asset sales and tax: No material divestiture gains or discrete tax items are flagged in the FY2025 or Q2 2026 releases beyond the impairment above. EOG's effective tax rate is not broken out in the summary financial tables reviewed; readers modelling the company should pull it from the 10-K and 10-Q directly rather than inferring it from pre-tax and net margins, since international operations in Trinidad carry a materially different fiscal regime from Lower-48 barrels.

Mid-cycle sanity check. Across FY2023–FY2025 EOG averaged roughly $23.1B of revenue and $11.1 of GAAP EPS on a smaller production base than it has today. Adding Encino's volumes at similar unit economics, a defensible mid-cycle earnings power is plausibly in the low-to-mid teens per share at a normalised $70–75 WTI — which would put the current $149.48 price at roughly 10–12x mid-cycle rather than the 9.86x forward multiple the peak-cycle consensus implies. That is not expensive for the highest-ROIC operator in the group, but it is a long way from the "5x free cash flow" framing that energy bulls sometimes apply at the top of a price cycle. In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten down and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. On a dividend-adjusted basis EOG went from $63.86 (Sep 2021) to $149.48 (Aug 2026) — a total return of roughly +134%, or about 18.6% annualised. That headline hides two completely different regimes. From late 2021 through late 2022 the stock nearly doubled as the post-COVID demand recovery and the invasion of Ukraine drove WTI toward $120 and EOG paid out enormous special dividends (FY2022 dividends paid: $5.15B). From early 2023 through the end of 2025 the stock did essentially nothing — three full years oscillating in a $96–$127 band as oil normalised, capital discipline stopped being a differentiator (everyone adopted it), and the market lost interest in the entire E&P complex. The break came in early 2026: from the December 2025 low of $103.22 the stock is up +44.8% year-to-date on a combination of higher crude, Encino volumes and a genuinely record quarter.

Where it sits now. At $149.48 the stock trades 98.4% of the way to its 52-week high of $151.87, well above both its 50-day ($138.44) and 200-day ($126.28) moving averages. The 6-period monthly RSI reads 72.5 — in overbought territory, and the highest since the early-2026 breakout. Technically this is a stock in a confirmed uptrend that has not paused; fundamentally it is a stock whose earnings base is built on $92.85 WTI. Both statements are true and they point in opposite directions.

Relative performance. Against the S&P 500, EOG's five-year total return of roughly +134% is a clear outperformance in aggregate — but almost all of the alpha was generated in two discrete windows (late 2021–2022 and 2026), with three years of dead money in between. That is the defining characteristic of the asset: it is not a compounder, it is a high-quality cyclical whose returns arrive in bursts tied to the crude cycle. Against its own sector the picture is more flattering and more consistent — EOG's 19.75% ROIC versus a ~9.3% large-cap E&P peer median, and a 25.7% net margin versus a 15.9% peer median, mean it converts each dollar of the same commodity price into materially more shareholder value than Devon, Diamondback or Occidental. Over the past 52 weeks EOG returned +26.97%. Investors buying here are making a crude-price call first and a company-quality call second; historically, the company-quality call has been the one that paid off across full cycles.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

EOG is the highest-return operator in US shale — 19.75% ROIC and a 25.73% net margin against peer medians near 9.3% and 15.9% — and it earns those returns with the least leverage in the group (0.26 D/E, 8.7% net-debt-to-capitalisation, $4.91B of cash). At 9.86x forward earnings and an 8.4% free-cash-flow yield with a 6.06% total shareholder yield, the market is paying a discount for a business that clears a 60% double-premium return hurdle at $40 oil. Encino adds 675,000 Utica net acres and >2 BBoe of undeveloped resource, extending the inventory runway that was the primary bear argument, while Dorado and the Utica gas window are unpriced call options on LNG export and data-centre power demand. At $92.85 WTI the company generated $2.80B of free cash flow in a single quarter.

Bear Case

You are buying a price-taker at a cyclical earnings peak. TTM EPS of $12.79 rests on $92.85 WTI; at the $65.63 EOG realised across 2025, GAAP EPS was $9.12 and free cash flow was less than half of what Q2 2026 annualises to. The 11.68x trailing multiple that looks cheap is exactly what E&P stocks look like before earnings roll over — and the eighteen hold ratings versus twelve buys say the Street knows it. The stock is 98.4% of the way to its 52-week high, 18% above its 200-day average, with an elevated monthly RSI. Meanwhile 36% of volumes are natural gas realising $2.77/Mcf, the Encino deal ended the net-cash balance sheet, the special dividend has effectively lapsed, and $6.5B of annual capex is required just to hold the base and grow oil 5%.

Top 3 Risks
01
Crude price reversion. The entire earnings inflection is a price event. A move from ~$93 WTI back to the mid-$60s that prevailed in 2025 would cut annualised pre-tax cash flow by roughly $4–5B on ~190 MMBbl of yearly oil volume (est.), taking free cash flow below the level required to fund capex, the $4.08 dividend and the buyback simultaneously.
02
Inventory quality erosion and integration risk. Double-premium locations are consumed first, and the $5.6B Encino purchase is itself evidence that organic inventory was not sufficient. If acquired Utica acreage does not clear EOG's own return hurdle after re-completion design, the company will have paid a premium price for average rock — and the ROIC spread that justifies its valuation narrows.
03
Natural gas oversupply. Gas is now 36% of Boe volumes and growing fastest, but realises $2.77/Mcf. Continued growth in Permian associated gas plus any delay in LNG export commissioning or data-centre load could keep Henry Hub sub-$3, stranding the economics of Dorado and the Utica gas window and turning EOG's volume growth into margin dilution.
  Industry Commentary
Oil Price & OPEC+ Policy
ONGOING HEADWIND
WTI averaged $92.85/Bbl in Q2 2026 — a level that transformed EOG's income statement, with net income doubling year over year and realisations reaching $98.15/Bbl. But this is a price the cartel can influence far more than any US operator can. OPEC+ retains meaningful spare capacity, and the 2025 experience — realised oil of $65.63 for the full year, an April 2025 −13.2% month, a December 2025 cycle low of $103.22 — is a recent reminder of how quickly the deck can move. US shale is now the swing respondent, not the swing producer: at $93 crude, capital discipline holds only as long as investors reward it. The headwind is not today's price; it is that today's price is not underwriteable.
US Gas Demand — LNG Exports & Power Load
EMERGING TAILWIND
EOG's gas volumes have grown 22% to 3,089 MMcf/d while realising only $2.77/Mcf against a $2.89 Henry Hub average — a market that is currently well supplied. Two structural demand sources are arriving on the other side of the ledger: Gulf Coast LNG export terminals converting domestic molecules into globally priced cargoes, and power generation for AI and data-centre buildout adding load to a US grid that had seen flat demand for two decades. EOG is positioned for both — Dorado sits roughly 100 miles from the Agua Dulce hub and Gulf Coast liquefaction, and the Utica gas window came with 330,000 net acres of firm transportation attached. The market currently ascribes very little value to this. A durable move above $4 Henry Hub changes that arithmetic materially.
Shale Inventory Depth & Consolidation
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The defining structural issue for US E&P is that the best rock has largely been drilled, and the industry's answer has been to buy it rather than find it. Coterra Energy was delisted on May 7, 2026 after merging into Devon Energy — removing a $24.7B pure-play from the peer set and leaving Devon at $55.4B. EOG's own $5.6B Encino purchase, announced May 2025, was the same trade in a different wrapper: 675,000 Utica net acres and >2 BBoe of undeveloped resource bought rather than explored, from a company whose culture had been built on organic discovery. Bull vs bear: the bull reads consolidation as rational — fewer, larger operators with better inventory, more capital discipline, and structurally higher returns; the bear reads it as an industry paying escalating prices for a finite resource base, where every acquisition is an admission that the drill bit alone can no longer sustain the runway. EOG's 19.75% ROIC versus a ~9.3% peer median says it has been the best allocator in the group; it does not say the constraint does not apply to it.
