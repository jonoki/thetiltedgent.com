01 Company Overview

Equinix operates the world's largest network of carrier-neutral colocation data centers — physical facilities where enterprises, cloud providers, and network operators place their servers and, critically, plug directly into one another. It is structured as a REIT, so the operating metric that matters is not GAAP earnings but AFFO per share.

Most recent full year (FY2025): revenue of $9.217B, up 5% as-reported (6% on a normalized and constant-currency basis), with adjusted EBITDA of $4.530B (49% margin, +11%) and AFFO of $3.761B, or $38.33 per diluted share (+9%). In 2025 Equinix surpassed 500,000 total interconnections and delivered 23,250 retail cabinets plus 90+ MW of xScale hyperscale capacity.

Most recent quarter (Q2 2026, reported July 29, 2026): revenue of $2.625B, +16% YoY on both as-reported and constant-currency bases; adjusted EBITDA of $1.396B at a record 53% margin; AFFO of $1.168B and diluted AFFO/share of $11.78, +19% YoY. GAAP diluted EPS was $4.83 (+29%), on net income of $479M. Annualized gross bookings hit $424M (+23%) and the company added a record 9,700 net interconnections, bringing the total to 522,700. Management raised both FY2026 guidance and the 2027–2029 long-term outlook.

At $106.3B of market capitalization Equinix ranks #216 globally by market value. The stock has returned +39.9% over the trailing 52 weeks, well ahead of the broad market and the real-estate sector.

This is the line that defines Equinix. Interconnection is the business of selling cross-connects — physical or virtual links between two customers inside the same facility. It generated $453M in Q2 2026, or 19.1% of recurring revenue and 17.3% of total revenue, an annualized run-rate of roughly $1.8B (calculated: $453M × 4). It carries the highest incremental margin in the portfolio because the marginal cost of a cross-connect, once the fibre and the customers are already in the building, is close to zero. It is also the source of the network effect: each of the 522,700 interconnections raises the cost of any single tenant leaving, because leaving means severing links to counterparties who did not move. Equinix added a record 9,700 net interconnections in Q2 2026 — the acceleration matters more than the absolute count, because AI workloads generate east-west traffic between model providers, data owners and enterprises that must be interconnected somewhere neutral.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
522,700 INTERCONNECTIONS CARRIER NEUTRALITY NETWORK-DENSE METROS SWITCHING COSTS SECURED POWER & LAND 1.8% QUARTERLY CHURN $15B JV CAPITAL ACCESS

Equinix's moat is not the buildings — anyone with capital can pour concrete — it is the ecosystem density inside them. A carrier-neutral facility with hundreds of networks, cloud on-ramps and enterprise tenants under one roof becomes the cheapest place for the next tenant to connect to all of them, which attracts more tenants, which deepens the advantage. The measurable expression of this is 522,700 interconnections generating $453M of quarterly revenue at near-zero incremental cost, and a customer churn rate of just 1.8% in Q2 2026. Pricing power follows: monthly recurring revenue per cabinet reached $2,538, up 6% YoY, while the company simultaneously added 4,200 billable cabinets — volume and price rising together is the signature of scarce, differentiated capacity rather than a commodity lease. The second, newer moat layer is access to capital at scale: the >$15B GIC/CPP joint venture lets Equinix control 1.5 GW of hyperscale capacity while consolidating only a 25% equity interest, an advantage private wholesale developers and even Digital Realty cannot easily replicate at that cost of capital. Finally, in 2026 the binding constraint on the industry is grid power and permitted land — Equinix's already-secured positions in Ashburn, Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Singapore and Tokyo are options that cannot be bought at today's prices.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. Hyperscaler self-build is a structural substitute. The largest AI and cloud buyers increasingly build their own campuses rather than lease, and they are the fastest-growing part of demand. Equinix's xScale JV is explicitly a response to this — it is an admission that the company must meet hyperscalers on wholesale economics or lose the workload entirely. Every gigawatt a hyperscaler self-builds is a gigawatt that never enters the colocation TAM, and self-built AI training campuses generate little interconnection revenue.

2. The capex ramp compresses near-term AFFO growth. FY2026 capex guidance was raised from an original $3.655–4.155B (February 2026) to $5.0–6.0B, with the 2027–2029 plan lifted from $3–4B per year to $5–7B per year. Trailing free cash flow is already negative $2.58B (operating cash flow $3.94B against $6.53B of capex). Development capital funds itself only if the delivered yields hold; if AI leasing demand softens after the buildings are committed, the company is left with depreciation, interest and empty megawatts.

3. Power and permitting can cap growth independent of demand. Utility interconnect queues in Northern Virginia, Dublin, Frankfurt, Amsterdam and Singapore run years long, and several of these markets have imposed moratoria or allocation regimes. Bookings can accelerate — Q2 gross bookings were +23% with presales +50% — while revenue conversion is gated by when a substation energises. This is the mechanism by which a demand boom becomes a backlog rather than a P&L.

4. Interest-rate and leverage sensitivity. Net debt of $22.4B against FY2026 guided adjusted EBITDA of ~$5.24B implies net leverage of roughly 4.0–4.3× (calculated), with interest coverage at 4.72×. REIT valuations trade off long rates; the stock's 2022 drawdown to $524 was a rate event, not an operating event. A doubled capex plan funded into a higher-for-longer curve raises both the cost and the equity-dilution risk of the build.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

xScale joint-venture fees and asset contributions are materially distorting the revenue line. Non-recurring revenue was $248M in Q2 2026 alone against $478M for the entirety of FY2025. This is xScale leasing and JV activity — including the Hampton, Georgia asset contributed to the U.S. JV in January 2026 — landing in a single quarter. Consequently recurring revenue fell to 90.6% of total in Q2'26 from 94.8% in FY2025, and headline revenue growth of 16% overstates the underlying subscription business, where MRR grew 11%. Model the recurring base, not the headline.

Currency. FY2025 revenue grew 5% as-reported versus 6% normalized and constant-currency — roughly a 100bp FX drag on a business with ~55% of revenue outside the Americas. In Q2 2026 the two bases converged at +16%, so FX was approximately neutral in the quarter. Any future dollar move re-opens that wedge in either direction; always read the constant-currency figure.

Capex and depreciation step-up. FY2026 capex guidance was raised from an original $3.655–4.155B (February 2026) to $5.0–6.0B, and the 2027–2029 plan from $3–4B/yr to $5–7B/yr. This is the "build bolder" program. Two mechanical consequences: (a) GAAP EPS and P/E will look progressively worse as new assets begin depreciating, independent of business quality; (b) AFFO/share growth is guided to 9–12% for 2027–29 — raised from 5–9%, but that raise assumes the delivered capacity leases at the assumed yields. The capex ramp is simultaneously the central growth story and the central risk.

FY2025 net income optics. FY2025 net income rose 66% and diluted EPS 62% on revenue growth of only 5%. Growth of that shape cannot come from operations alone; review the FY2025 10-K for gains on asset contributions to joint ventures, tax items and any real-estate dispositions before extrapolating the GAAP earnings trajectory. This analysis does not assign a cause because none was verifiable from the sources used.

Data-quality caveat. At least one widely-used aggregator reports Q2 2026 "EPS" of $11.78 against a $4.73 consensus — a ~150% "beat." That is an apples-to-oranges artifact: $11.78 is AFFO per share, while the consensus was struck on GAAP EPS. The actual GAAP comparison was $4.83 versus ~$4.67 expected, and revenue $2.63B versus $2.59B — a genuine but ordinary beat. Screeners that mix the two bases will misprice this stock.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Equinix owns the one asset AI cannot manufacture: 522,700 interconnections inside carrier-neutral facilities in the world's densest network metros, generating $453M a quarter at near-zero incremental cost with 1.8% churn. The operating inflection is real and measurable — revenue growth went from 5% in FY2025 to 16% in Q2 2026, adjusted EBITDA margin hit a record 53%, bookings are up 23% with presales up 50%, and MRR per cabinet is rising 6% while cabinet count grows. Management responded by raising the 2027–29 outlook to 10–13% revenue growth and 9–12% AFFO/share growth. The >$15B GIC/CPP xScale JV lets Equinix control 1.5 GW of hyperscale capacity for a 25% equity check. At ~25× forward P/AFFO with a 48% AFFO payout and an eleventh consecutive dividend raise, an investor is paying a growth multiple for scarce, power-constrained, network-effect infrastructure.

Bear Case

The market already paid for the story: the stock is up 39.9% in twelve months, 25 points ahead of its sector, and every one of 32 analysts rates it Buy with the low target below the current price. What it bought is a promise. Free cash flow is negative $2.58B, capex guidance doubled to $5–7B a year, and net leverage is already ~4.0–4.3× EBITDA on $22.4B of net debt with 4.72× interest coverage. The Q2 headline itself is flattered — $248M of non-recurring xScale revenue in one quarter versus $478M for all of FY2025 — and the underlying MRR grew 11%, not 16%. Q3 guidance implies a sequential revenue decline. Meanwhile hyperscalers self-build the workloads that generate the least interconnection revenue, power queues cap delivery independent of demand, and every 100bp on long rates hits both the discount rate and the build cost. If AI leasing converts more slowly than guided, the multiple and the AFFO growth compress together.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Capex execution risk — the buildings get built before the leases are signed. Annual capex is guided to $5–7B against operating cash flow of $3.94B, with FY2026 capex alone raised from an original ~$3.9B midpoint to ~$5.5B. Development capital is committed years before revenue arrives; if AI leasing demand normalises after the concrete is poured, Equinix carries the depreciation, the interest and the vacancy. This risk is the direct cost of the "build bolder" upside case — they are the same decision.
02
Hyperscaler self-build erodes the addressable market at its fastest-growing edge. The largest AI buyers increasingly develop their own multi-hundred-megawatt campuses. xScale is a defensive response, but it trades Equinix's high-margin retail interconnection economics for lower-margin wholesale economics on a 25% equity interest. Success in xScale is partly a story of mix shift away from the moat.
03
Rate sensitivity against a leveraged, growing balance sheet. $22.4B net debt, ~4.0–4.3× net leverage, 4.72× interest coverage, and a capital plan that requires continuous access to debt and JV equity. The 2022 drawdown to $524 — a 32% decline with zero revenue decline — is the historical template for what a rate shock does to this equity regardless of operating performance.
  Industry Commentary
AI data-center demand versus power and land constraints
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Demand is not the problem. Equinix booked $474M annualized gross bookings in Q4 2025 (+42% YoY) with roughly 60% of its largest deals AI-driven, followed by $424M (+23%) in Q2 2026 with presales up ~50%. The problem is delivery. The company delivered 23,250 retail cabinets and 90+ MW of xScale capacity in 2025 with 52 major expansion projects underway and 42,500+ expansion cabinets targeted through 2027 — every one of which depends on a utility energising a substation on schedule in markets (Northern Virginia, Dublin, Frankfurt, Amsterdam, Singapore) where interconnect queues run years and moratoria exist. This is why management doubled capex to $5–7B a year: securing land and grid capacity now is buying an option that will not be available later. For investors it means bookings growth and revenue growth can decouple for multiple quarters, and the gap shows up as backlog, not as AFFO.
Hyperscaler self-build and the shift from retail to wholesale
ONGOING HEADWIND
Synergy Research has documented for years that wholesale colocation revenue is growing far faster than retail, driven by hyperscalers leasing large blocks — and the largest of them increasingly skip leasing entirely and build. That is the structural threat to a retail-colocation business model, because a self-built training campus produces neither cabinet MRR nor cross-connect revenue for anyone. The market is also more fragmented than the headline suggests: Equinix, Digital Realty and NTT together held only ~30% of the worldwide colocation market (Synergy, 2022), the top six ~37%, Chinese operators ~13%, and roughly half sits with medium and small operators. A 2026 industry ranking puts Equinix, Digital Realty, NTT, KDDI and QTS at roughly 20–25% of global share. Equinix's structural answer — the xScale JVs — protects volume but at wholesale margins, and it is why the interconnection line, not the cabinet line, is the number to watch each quarter.
Interest rates, REIT valuation, and where the bull/bear line actually sits
BULL VS BEAR FRAMING
Equinix has spent 2026 escaping its REIT valuation anchor: +39.9% over twelve months against VNQ's +15.20% and SPY's +20.87%. The bull framing is that this is correct — a business compounding AFFO/share at a guided 9–12% with 53% EBITDA margins, 1.8% churn and irreplaceable network density should not trade on a real-estate cap rate, and at ~25× forward P/AFFO with a 48% payout it still does not price the full option value of 1.5 GW of hyperscale capacity. The bear framing is that the balance sheet never left the REIT world. $22.4B of net debt, ~4.0–4.3× net leverage, 4.72× interest coverage and negative $2.58B free cash flow mean this equity remains a leveraged bet on long rates staying benign while an enormous capital program is funded. The 2022 episode — a 32% drawdown with revenue growing 13.7% — is the historical proof that rates, not operations, set the multiple. The line between the two cases is delivery: if the next four quarters show MRR growth holding double-digit, interconnection adds staying near record, and capex converting at guided yields, the growth multiple is defensible. If MRR growth reverts toward the 7% of FY2025 while capex runs at $6B, the stock is a levered REIT trading at a growth multiple — and both halves of that sentence would correct at once.
