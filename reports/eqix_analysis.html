01 Company Overview

Equinix operates the world's largest network of carrier-neutral colocation data centers — physical facilities where enterprises, cloud providers, and network operators place their servers and, critically, plug directly into one another. It is structured as a REIT, so the operating metric that matters is not GAAP earnings but AFFO per share.

Most recent full year (FY2025): revenue of $9.217B, up 5% as-reported (6% on a normalized and constant-currency basis), with adjusted EBITDA of $4.530B (49% margin, +11%) and AFFO of $3.761B, or $38.33 per diluted share (+9%). In 2025 Equinix surpassed 500,000 total interconnections and delivered 23,250 retail cabinets plus 90+ MW of xScale hyperscale capacity.

Most recent quarter (Q3 2026, reported October 28, 2026): revenue of $2.571B, +11% YoY as reported (+12% on a normalized, constant-currency basis); adjusted EBITDA of $1.321B (+15%) at a 51% margin; AFFO of $1.156B and diluted AFFO/share of $11.64, +18% YoY (+12% excluding a one-time fee from the atNorth joint venture). GAAP diluted EPS was $4.93 (+29%), on net income of $489M. Annualized gross bookings set a record $533M (+35%), and the company added 6,900 net interconnections; on the call the CEO put the total at more than half a million. It raised full-year 2026 guidance for the third quarter in a row. CEO Adaire Fox-Martin, in the release: "Our Q3 results and raised full-year guidance reflect the differentiated position and growing importance of Equinix."

At $100.3B of market capitalization (98.99M shares on Oct 27, 2026 per the 10-Q, × the Oct 30 close) Equinix ranks #116 in the S&P 500 by market value. The stock is up 21.7% over the past year (price only), against 11.2% for SPY, an S&P 500 fund, over the same dates.

This is the line that defines Equinix. Interconnection is the business of selling cross-connects — physical or virtual links between two customers inside the same facility. It generated $465M in Q3 2026, up 10% YoY, or 19.0% of recurring revenue and 18.1% of total revenue (calculated from the release), an annualized run-rate of roughly $1.9B (calculated: $465M × 4). Fabric, the software-defined connection service, grew 24%. It carries the highest incremental margin in the portfolio because the marginal cost of a cross-connect, once the fibre and the customers are already in the building, is close to zero. It is also the source of the network effect: each of the more than 500,000 interconnections raises the cost of any single tenant leaving, because leaving means severing links to counterparties who did not move. Net adds were 6,900 in Q3 2026 (5,800 physical, 1,100 virtual, per Zacks), after a record 9,700 in Q2. The pace matters more than the absolute count, because AI workloads generate traffic between model providers, data owners and enterprises that must be interconnected somewhere neutral.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
500,000+ INTERCONNECTIONS CARRIER NEUTRALITY NETWORK-DENSE METROS SWITCHING COSTS SECURED POWER & LAND 1.7% QUARTERLY CHURN $15B JV CAPITAL ACCESS

Equinix's moat is not the buildings — anyone with capital can pour concrete — it is the ecosystem density inside them. A carrier-neutral facility with hundreds of networks, cloud on-ramps and enterprise tenants under one roof becomes the cheapest place for the next tenant to connect to all of them, which attracts more tenants, which deepens the advantage. The measurable expression of this is more than 500,000 interconnections generating $465M of quarterly revenue at near-zero extra cost, and a customer churn rate of 1.7% in Q3 2026 (management said on the call it expects 2–2.5% for the rest of the year). Pricing power follows: monthly recurring revenue per cabinet reached $2,574 (Zacks), CEO Adaire Fox-Martin said on the call that "Pricing on new deals was up 12% year-over-year in the quarter," and billable cabinets rose by 5,700. Volume and price rising together is the signature of scarce, differentiated capacity rather than a commodity lease. The second, newer moat layer is access to capital at scale: the >$15B GIC/CPP joint venture lets Equinix steer 1.5 GW of hyperscale capacity while putting up only a 25% equity stake, an advantage private wholesale developers and even Digital Realty cannot easily replicate at that cost of capital. Finally, in 2026 the binding constraint on the industry is grid power and permitted land — Equinix's already-secured positions in Ashburn, Frankfurt, London, Amsterdam, Singapore and Tokyo are options that cannot be bought at today's prices.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. Hyperscaler self-build is a structural substitute. The largest AI and cloud buyers increasingly build their own campuses rather than lease, and they are the fastest-growing part of demand. Equinix's xScale JV is explicitly a response to this — it is an admission that the company must meet hyperscalers on wholesale economics or lose the workload entirely. Every gigawatt a hyperscaler self-builds is a gigawatt that never enters the market colocation companies sell into, and self-built AI training campuses generate little interconnection revenue.

2. The capex ramp weighs on near-term AFFO growth. FY2026 total capex is guided to $5.0–6.0B (unchanged at Q3), with the 2027–2029 plan lifted at Q2 from $3–4B per year to $5–7B per year. Trailing free cash flow is negative $2.49B (calculated over the four quarters to Sep 30, 2026: operating cash flow $4.17B against $6.67B spent on property, equipment and real-estate purchases). Spending on new buildings pays for itself only if the finished buildings earn the expected return; if AI leasing demand softens after the buildings are committed, the company is left with depreciation, interest and empty megawatts.

3. Power and permitting can cap growth independent of demand. Utility interconnect queues in Northern Virginia, Dublin, Frankfurt, Amsterdam and Singapore run years long, and several of these markets have imposed moratoria or allocation regimes. Bookings can accelerate (Q3 gross bookings were a record $533M annualized, +35%) while revenue still waits on when a substation is switched on. This is the mechanism by which a demand boom becomes a backlog rather than revenue.

4. Interest-rate and leverage sensitivity. Net debt of about $21.6B (calculated: $25.2B of debt principal less $3.6B of cash and short-term investments at Sep 30, 2026) is 4.1× a year of adjusted EBITDA, the figure the CFO gave on the Q3 call; interest expense rose to $163M in the quarter from $128M a year earlier as the company issued $5.4B of senior notes this year. REIT prices move with long-term interest rates; the stock's 2022 fall to $566 came as rates rose, while revenue kept growing. A larger building plan funded while rates stay high raises both the interest bill and the chance of issuing new shares.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Joint-venture fees swing the revenue and profit lines. Non-recurring revenue was $248M in Q2 2026, mostly xScale fees, and $129M in Q3; recurring revenue went from 90.6% of the total in Q2 to 95.0% in Q3 (calculated). Q3 net income and AFFO also include a one-time fee earned on the atNorth joint venture: excluding it, AFFO/share grew 12% on a normalized, constant-currency basis rather than 18% as reported, and the fee added $0.31 to the raised full-year AFFO/share guide. Q3 also carried $54M of other income against a $28M other expense in Q2. The underlying subscription business, measured by monthly recurring revenue, grew 11%. Follow the recurring base, not the headline.

Currency. FY2025 revenue grew 5% as-reported versus 6% normalized and constant-currency — about one percentage point lost to exchange rates on a business with ~55% of revenue outside the Americas. In Q3 2026 revenue grew 11% as reported and 12% normalized and constant-currency; the raised full-year revenue guide is about 12% growth on both bases. Any dollar move re-opens that gap in either direction; always read the constant-currency figure.

Capex and depreciation step-up. FY2026 total capex is guided at $5.0–6.0B, and the 2027–2029 plan was raised at Q2 from $3–4B/yr to $5–7B/yr; Q3 capital expenditure alone was $1.558B (calculated from the releases: $4,392M for the nine months less $2,834M for the first half). Management now expects nearly 40% more cabinets delivered in 2026 than it planned at the start of the year, with a record of more than 13,000 in Q4. Two mechanical consequences: (a) GAAP EPS and P/E will look progressively worse as new assets begin depreciating, independent of business quality; (b) AFFO/share growth is guided to 9–12% for 2027–29 — raised from 5–9%, but that raise assumes the delivered capacity leases at the assumed yields. The capex ramp is simultaneously the central growth story and the central risk.

FY2025 net income optics. FY2025 net income rose 66% and diluted EPS 62% on revenue growth of only 5% (StockAnalysis growth rates; net income $1.350B and diluted EPS $13.76 per the Q4 2025 release). Growth of that shape does not come from operations alone, so the GAAP trend is a weak guide to the next year.

Which "EPS" beat? Nasdaq's earnings calendar lists Q3 2026 "EPS" of $11.64 against a $9.28 consensus, a 25% "beat." $11.64 is AFFO per share. Compared like with like, AFFO/share beat the Zacks consensus of $10.51 by 10.8%, GAAP diluted EPS was $4.93 (+29%), and revenue of $2.571B was about in line with the $2.57B consensus (Zacks). A solid beat on profit, not a 25% one; screens that mix the two bases misread this stock.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Equinix owns the one asset AI cannot manufacture: more than 500,000 interconnections inside carrier-neutral facilities in the world's densest network hubs, generating $465M a quarter at near-zero extra cost with 1.7% churn. The step-up is real and measurable: revenue is guided to grow about 12% in 2026 after 5% in FY2025, gross bookings hit a record $533M (+35%) in Q3, pricing on new deals rose 12%, and full-year guidance has been raised three quarters in a row. The 2027–29 outlook calls for 10–13% revenue growth and 9–12% AFFO/share growth. The >$15B GIC/CPP xScale JV lets Equinix steer 1.5 GW of hyperscale capacity for a 25% equity stake, and atNorth adds a Nordic platform. At ~23× P/AFFO on 2026 guidance, a 47% AFFO payout and an eleventh straight year of dividend growth, an investor is paying a growth price for scarce, power-limited infrastructure with a network effect.

Bear Case

The market already paid for much of the story: the stock is up 21.7% in twelve months, about 10 points ahead of the S&P 500, and 30 of 35 analysts rate it a Buy. What it bought is a promise. Free cash flow is negative $2.49B over the last four quarters, the building plan runs at $5–7B a year from 2027 (up from $3–4B), and net leverage is already 4.1× EBITDA on about $21.6B of net debt, with interest expense up 27% year on year in Q3. The headline numbers swing on one-time fees (xScale in Q2, atNorth in Q3); monthly recurring revenue grew 11%, and Q3 AFFO/share growth was 12% excluding the atNorth fee, not 18%. Management expects churn to rise to 2–2.5%. Meanwhile hyperscalers self-build the workloads that generate the least interconnection revenue, power queues cap delivery independent of demand, and every percentage-point rise in long-term rates raises both the return investors demand and the cost of building. If AI leasing converts more slowly than guided, the price investors pay for each dollar of AFFO and the AFFO growth would fall together.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Capex execution risk — the buildings get built before the leases are signed. Annual capex is guided to $5–7B from 2027 against trailing operating cash flow of $4.17B, with FY2026 capex at $5–6B. Development capital is committed years before revenue arrives; if AI leasing demand normalises after the concrete is poured, Equinix carries the depreciation, the interest and the vacancy. This risk is the direct cost of the upside case of building ahead of demand: they are the same decision.
02
Hyperscaler self-build erodes the addressable market at its fastest-growing edge. The largest AI buyers increasingly develop their own multi-hundred-megawatt campuses. xScale is a defensive response, but it trades Equinix's high-margin retail interconnection economics for lower-margin wholesale economics on a 25% equity interest. Success in xScale is partly a story of mix shift away from the moat.
03
Rate sensitivity against a leveraged, growing balance sheet. About $21.6B of net debt, 4.1× net leverage, $5.4B of new senior notes issued in the first nine months of 2026, and a capital plan that needs continuous access to debt and JV equity. The 2022 drawdown to $566, a 33% fall while revenue kept growing, is the historical template for what a rate shock does to this equity regardless of operating performance.
  Industry Commentary
AI data-center demand versus power and land constraints
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Demand is not the problem. Equinix booked $474M annualized gross bookings in Q4 2025 (+42% YoY) with roughly 60% of its largest deals AI-driven, then a record $533M (+35%) in Q3 2026; CEO Adaire Fox-Martin described demand on the Q3 call as "strong, it is broad-based, and it is durable." The problem is delivery. The company delivered 23,250 retail cabinets and 90+ MW of xScale capacity in 2025 and now has 48 expansion projects in progress, every one of which depends on a utility switching on a substation on schedule in markets (Northern Virginia, Dublin, Frankfurt, Amsterdam, Singapore) where interconnect queues run years and moratoria exist. This is why management raised the capex plan to $5–7B a year: securing land and grid capacity now buys an option that will not be available later. In Georgia it has agreed to fund all grid costs for its Hampton project under a 20-year commitment, so local ratepayers do not carry them (Q3 release). For investors it means bookings growth and revenue growth can decouple for multiple quarters, and the gap shows up as backlog, not as AFFO.
Hyperscaler self-build and the shift from retail to wholesale
ONGOING HEADWIND
Synergy Research reported in 2022 that wholesale colocation revenue was growing much faster than retail, driven by hyperscalers leasing large blocks — and the largest of them increasingly skip leasing entirely and build. That is the structural threat to a retail-colocation business model, because a self-built training campus produces neither cabinet MRR nor cross-connect revenue for anyone. The market is also more fragmented than the headline suggests: Equinix, Digital Realty and NTT together held only ~30% of the worldwide colocation market (Synergy, 2022), the top six ~37%, Chinese operators ~13%, and roughly half sits with medium and small operators. A June 2026 guide from the data-center adviser Encor puts Equinix, Digital Realty, NTT, KDDI and QTS at roughly 20–25% of global share in 2025. Equinix's structural answer — the xScale JVs — protects volume but at wholesale margins, and it is why the interconnection line, not the cabinet line, is the number to watch each quarter.
Interest rates, REIT valuation, and where the bull/bear line actually sits
BULL VS BEAR FRAMING
Equinix has spent 2026 being priced less like a property company: +21.7% over twelve months against SPY's +11.2%, both on price. The bull framing is that this is correct: a business growing AFFO/share at a guided 9–12% a year with adjusted EBITDA margins of about 51%, 1.7% churn and hard-to-copy network density should not be valued like an office landlord (on a property yield), and at ~23× P/AFFO with a 47% payout it still does not price the full value of 1.5 GW of hyperscale capacity. The bear framing is that the balance sheet never left the REIT world. About $21.6B of net debt, 4.1× net leverage and negative $2.49B free cash flow mean this stock remains a borrowed bet on long-term rates staying calm while a very large building program is funded. The 2022 episode, a 33% drawdown while revenue kept growing, is the historical proof that rates, not operations, set the price investors pay. The line between the two cases is delivery: if the next four quarters show MRR growth holding double-digit, interconnection adds staying near record, and capex converting at guided yields, the growth multiple is defensible. If MRR growth slows back toward mid-single digits while capex runs at $6B, the stock is a heavily borrowed REIT priced like a fast grower — and both halves of that sentence would correct at once.
