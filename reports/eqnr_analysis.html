01 Company Overview

Equinor is Norway's state-controlled energy company and, in its own words, the largest energy provider to Europe — oil, piped gas and LNG pumped mostly from the NCS. Founded by the Norwegian parliament as Statoil in 1972, listed in Oslo and New York in 2001 and renamed Equinor in 2018, it is still 67% owned by the Norwegian state. In 2025 it produced 2,137 thousand barrels of oil equivalent a day, booked $105.83B of revenue and $5.06B of net income (basic EPS $1.94). At the October 30 close the NYSE line valued the company at $115.40B.

2026 rewrote the numbers. The war that began on 28 February 2026 with US and Israeli strikes on Iran choked the Strait of Hormuz, which Tehran has at times declared closed (The Guardian, October 1, 2026). Equinor's own quarterly reports name the result: European gas prices rose on LNG supply disruption "following the closure of the Strait of Hormuz". The Q3 review says the same in plainer terms: "Tight market conditions and supply disruptions following the conflict in the Middle East drove a significant increase in European gas prices during the quarter." A company with no production in the Middle East and pipelines into Europe's biggest gas markets realised $19.99 per mmbtu for European gas in Q3, from fields whose all-in cost of supply management puts below $2. The ADS went from $29.83 at the end of February to $42.20 at the end of March (+41.5% in one month), fell back to $31.40 by June as Brent futures sank to $72.92, and closed October 30 at $48.75 — up 106.3% in 2026, with Brent futures back at $117.64. Q3 2026 adjusted operating income was $12.35B, up 99% on a year earlier. The question for this report: how much of the current cash flow is a war premium, and what is left at normal prices? Management's own answer, given at its June 16 Capital Markets Day, is a buyback framework sized for $60–80 oil and a cash breakeven of about $50 a barrel.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Pipelines into Europe's gas markets Gas cost of supply below $2/MMBtu NCS hubs: Troll, Johan Sverdrup Tie-backs below $35/bbl break-even State anchor shareholder (67%) Asset-backed trading (MMP) Net debt ratio 3.8%

The moat is geography plus steel already in the water. Norway's gas fields are wired by pipeline into Germany, the UK and the rest of northwest Europe. Before the 2022 energy crisis, Russian pipeline gas was Europe's largest source of supply; now LNG dominates the mix and, in management's words, "Global competition for LNG will set the price for gas in Europe." Equinor sells at that LNG-set price from fields whose cost of supply is below $2 per mmbtu. New tie-backs plug into hubs that already exist, and Equinor puts their break-evens below $35 a barrel. The vulnerability is the other side of the same coin: Equinor controls its costs, not the price it sells at. And the Norwegian state takes 78% of NCS profit as tax before the shareholder's 67% stake even comes into it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The war premium can leave as fast as it came. The ADS rose 41.5% in March 2026 and then gave back 25.6% between the March and June month-ends ($42.20 → $31.40); between the April and June month-ends, Brent futures fell from $114.01 to $72.92. By September, crude leaving the Gulf had recovered to the pre-war average excluding Iran (Kpler data, via The Guardian, October 1, 2026) — and Brent then climbed from $102.25 on October 2 to $117.64 on October 30. Gas is tighter for longer — Opedal says damage to Middle East LNG infrastructure tightens supply "both in the short term and heading toward 2030" — but liquids pricing has no such floor.
02
Norway's tax take caps the upside. The NCS is taxed at a 78% statutory rate. Q3 2026's effective rate was 77.6% (62.9% in Q2); E&P Norway turned $10,570M of pre-tax income into $2,329M. Tax is also paid in lumps: $6.5B went out in Q3, mostly the first two NCS instalments on 2026 earnings, and the CFO expects tax payments in the first half of 2027 to be higher than in the second half of 2026, so quarterly cash flow can swing well away from profit.
03
Power is still a cash drain. Power lost $36M in the first nine months of 2026 (adjusted), and Empire Wind's first power has slipped to the first half of 2027. Management says offshore wind turns cash-flow positive in 2027 and power self-finances from 2027 to 2030 after tax credits. Those are forecasts. The 2026 retreat from the 10–12 GW target shows how far earlier power promises fell short.
04
A 67% owner with its own priorities. Buybacks need an annual agreement with the state on its participation, and the state's share is redeemed in arrears, so cash to the state comes in lumps ($3,052M in July 2026). The state's interests — energy security for Europe, NCS employment, climate policy — line up with minority shareholders most of the time, not all of the time. Equinor is in neither the S&P 500 nor the Nasdaq-100, so the largest US index funds are not natural holders.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, reported vs adjusted: Q3 2026 net income of $2.54B ran $0.31B below adjusted net income of $2.86B; reported results carry $628M of impairments on a cross-border Norway–UK offshore asset and a $120M provision, partly offset by a $165M impairment reversal on a Norwegian asset. Basic EPS was $1.07 against adjusted EPS of $1.20. Second, tax timing: Q3 cash flow from operations after taxes paid ($8.14B) came after $6.54B of tax payments, mostly the first two NCS instalments on 2026 earnings. Third, the share count is shrinking fast: the weighted average fell to 2,374M in Q3 2026 from 2,527M a year earlier (−6.1%), and the May 12 AGM cancelled 166,058,472 shares, leaving 2,390,749,040 in issue from July 2. Per-share growth therefore runs ahead of absolute profit growth.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $25.43 to the October 30, 2026 close of $48.75, EQNR gained 91.7% on price. Over the same window SPY, the S&P 500 tracker, went from $459.25 to $755.72: +64.6%. Five years of war, energy crisis and slump, and Equinor finished ahead on price alone. The cash widens the gap: dividends with ex-dates in the window add up to $11.07 per ADS — 43.5% of the starting price, swollen by the much larger payouts from late 2022 through 2024 ($0.70–$0.90 a quarter) — against $34.52 per SPY share (7.5%). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 10.1x trailing and about 8.3x forward earnings, with a 3.20% dividend yield, a 6.0% total shareholder yield for 2026 and a 9.8% trailing free-cash-flow yield. The business sells gas into an LNG-priced European market from fields that cost under $2 per mmbtu, and Uniper has just signed for 15 years of it. The balance sheet is the strongest in years — a 3.8% net debt ratio at September 30, which the CFO expects around zero at year-end at current forward prices — and management has promised to keep it strong in good years. Q3 cash flow and volumes beat the analysts' figures, and 2026 production guidance went up. The Capital Markets Day plan sizes buybacks for $60–80 oil, not $100, so the war premium is upside to the framework rather than its foundation. BofA's September upgrade put its 2027 free-cash-flow estimate, at futures-market prices for oil and gas, more than 150% above consensus. If gas stays tight "heading toward 2030", as Opedal argues, the market is still pricing a normal cycle.

Bear Case

The stock has doubled in ten months on a war (+106.3% in 2026 to October 30), and the war is the thing the market cannot forecast. Crude flows out of the Gulf were back to the pre-war average, excluding Iran, by September. The last time Brent eased, the ADS gave back 25.6% in three months. The Street sits at Hold with an average target below the price ($43.47 on MarketBeat against $48.75). The 78% NCS tax means most of any windfall goes to Oslo, not to shareholders. The power business still loses money and has already missed one headline target. Q1 showed that cash flow can miss even when profit beats, and Q3 that EPS can miss even when cash beats. At $48.75 the stock prices in prices staying high; an outbreak of peace would test it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Commodity prices normalise. Realised liquids were $92.3 a barrel in Q3 against $64.9 a year earlier; European gas $19.99 against $11.43. The April–June 2026 slide ($40.75 → $31.40 month-end) shows the sensitivity. The buyback range from 2027 ($2–4B at $60–80 oil) is the company's own guide to what normal looks like.
02
Cash timing and tax. NCS taxes are paid in instalments a year in arrears, so a high-price year produces a heavy tax bill in the next. Q3 2026 paid $6.54B, mostly the first two instalments on 2026 earnings, and the CFO expects first-half 2027 tax payments to be higher than second-half 2026; 2027 prices may be lower. Current tax payable stood at $17.60B on September 30.
03
Execution on growth projects and power. The 2030 plan relies on 6–8 NCS tie-backs a year, 30% international growth, Bay du Nord reaching a decision in 2027, and Empire Wind and Baltyk being finished on budget. Power lost money in the first nine months of 2026, Empire Wind's first power has slipped to the first half of 2027, and the renewables capacity target has already been abandoned once.
Industry Commentary
Hormuz and Europe's gas market
DOMINANT DRIVER

At the Capital Markets Day Opedal said damage to Middle East LNG infrastructure and the Strait of Hormuz situation "tighten supply even further, both in the short term and heading toward 2030." Since the February 28 strikes, Iran has at times declared the strait closed, and Equinor's reports tie the higher European gas prices of Q1 and Q2 directly to LNG supply disruption from that closure. In the Q1 release Opedal said: "Heightened geopolitical tension continues to disrupt energy flows and commodity prices." In the Q3 review: "Tight market conditions and supply disruptions following the conflict in the Middle East drove a significant increase in European gas prices during the quarter." By late September the oil side was adapting — about 40% of the region's crude was leaving by Saudi and Emirati pipelines, against 17% before the war (Kpler, via The Guardian) — while refined products remained constrained; in October Brent rose 14% to $117.64 (Yahoo month-end and October 30 closes). For Equinor the gas side matters more: Europe's dependence on LNG, management argues, continues toward 2035 and beyond.

Back to hydrocarbons
STRATEGY SHIFT

Equinor's June 16 Capital Markets Day turned the company back toward oil and gas: more hydrocarbons, fewer renewables promises. NCS output is now planned at 1.35 million boe/d in 2030 (raised by 100,000), international output up 30%, oil and gas investment raised by $1B in 2027, and power capex held to about 10% of the total. The old renewables capacity target is gone. The pitch to shareholders changed with it: a dividend growing more than 5% a year and a buyback rule tied to oil and gas prices. CEO Opedal at the event: "Demand continues to grow and Equinor is uniquely positioned to provide reliable energy." The market's response was muted — the ADS fell 1.2% that day — because the immediate driver was Brent, not the plan.

The Norwegian model: state owner, state taxman
STRUCTURAL

Norway takes its share of the NCS twice: through a 78% petroleum tax and through its 67% stake. For an outside shareholder that means the windfall is mostly the state's: in Q3 2026 E&P Norway kept 22 cents of each pre-tax dollar. The buyback mechanism keeps the state at 67% by redeeming its shares in proportion, paid in arrears ($3,052M in July 2026). The result is a company that behaves like a utility for a nation's oil wealth, run with the capital discipline of an international major — and priced, at 10.1x trailing earnings, somewhere between the two.
