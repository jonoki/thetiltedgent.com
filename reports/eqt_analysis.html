01 Company Overview

EQT Corporation is one of the largest natural gas producers in the United States — it sold 583 Bcfe (billion cubic feet equivalent) in Q3 2026, about 6.3 Bcfe a day, behind Expand Energy's 7.54 Bcfe a day — operating almost entirely in the Appalachian Basin across Pennsylvania, West Virginia and Ohio. Following the July 2024 all-stock acquisition of Equitrans Midstream, the company describes itself as vertically integrated — it no longer just drills wells, it owns a large slice of the gathering and transmission pipe those wells flow into, plus a marketing arm that sells the gas. FY2025 total operating revenues were $8.64B, up 63.9% from $5.27B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 2026 is $9.35B. Market capitalisation of $34.12B places it #302 in the S&P 500.

It is about to get bigger. On October 26, 2026 EQT agreed to buy Range Resources, another large Appalachian gas producer, paying 0.987 EQT shares for each Range share. EQT's shareholders would own about 73% of the combined company and Range's about 27%; the companies put the combined enterprise value (shares plus net debt) above $50B, production above 9 Bcfe a day, and cost savings at $325M a year within two years. Closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027 and needs both sets of shareholders and the regulators to agree. Toby Rice called it "a must-own energy company capable of generating durable, through-cycle free cash flow" in the joint release. On the banner date Range closed at $53.89, about 0.1% above the $53.83 its holders would receive at EQT's close (0.987 × $54.54): the market was pricing Range almost exactly at the deal terms.

The central debate on this page is not "is gas going up." It is whether owning pipe and signing long-term sales contracts narrows the one number that has defined EQT's economics for a decade: the Appalachian basis differential — the discount at which gas sold in the Marcellus trades to the Henry Hub NYMEX benchmark, because there is more gas in Appalachia than there is pipe to move it out. In Q3 2026 that differential was −$0.64/Mcf against a NYMEX price of $2.96, a little narrower than −$0.68 a year earlier. Owning the pipe, signing power and LNG contracts, and curtailing production in weak months are all attempts to attack that number from different angles.

A note on why this report does not lead with EPS. Quarterly EPS follows the gas price, and the gas price swings with the weather: GAAP diluted EPS was $2.36 in Q1 2026, when NYMEX averaged $4.95 in a quarter that included Winter Storm Fern, then $0.34 in Q2 and $0.44 in Q3 at under $3. EQT also marks its hedges to market through revenue, which moves GAAP EPS away from the company's adjusted figure in some quarters (Q2 2025: $1.30 GAAP against $0.45 adjusted). The steadier scorecard for a gas producer is volumes, realised price net of basis and hedges, per-unit cash cost, free cash flow and net debt. Those are the numbers this page is built around.

The Equitrans re-merger closed July 22, 2024, undoing the 2018 spin-off and putting gathering systems, transmission lines, storage and the Mountain Valley Pipeline interest back inside the parent. The logic is that a producer who owns its own pipe pays itself the pipeline profit rather than paying a third party, and gains a way to work on its own takeaway problem. In Q3 2026 the Gathering and Transmission segments together earned $286.3M of operating income on $461.8M of revenue, more than the $219.6M Upstream earned (10-Q, Note 2); only $162.3M of the company's total revenue was "pipeline and other". With Q3 results EQT launched an open season (a call for shippers to commit to capacity) for the POWER Pipeline, a new 42-inch line from its core Marcellus acreage in Pennsylvania to the Clarington hub in Ohio. Its guidance still includes capital contributions to Mountain Valley Pipeline for MVP Southgate, which the July release said was set to complete construction by year-end 2026. It also contracted 7 Bcf of Gulf Coast storage at Leaf River, taking its Gulf Coast storage to 11 Bcf. EQT's release describes the company after the Range deal as "America's largest and only vertically integrated natural gas producer."

Caution: segment operating income is before interest and tax, and part of the pipeline segments' revenue is business with EQT's other segments that the consolidated accounts take out ($321.6M across all segments in Q3). So part of the pipeline profit is money moving inside the group rather than paid by outside customers.

The contracts are the concrete version of the "demand is coming to Appalachia" thesis. With Q3 2026 results EQT announced a ten-year LNG sales agreement with Ignitis Group, the Lithuanian electricity and gas supplier; the release gives no volume, start date or price. It adds to two deals announced in July: a five-year LNG offtake agreement for 0.5 million tonnes a year from 2028 with an unnamed Asian energy company, which EQT said would add about $45M to 2028 free cash flow, and a ten-year supply agreement with Competitive Power Ventures for 325,000 Dth/d to the Shay Energy Center in Doddridge County, West Virginia, priced against PJM power prices rather than Henry Hub. PJM is the grid that runs from the Mid-Atlantic to Chicago, and its January 2026 load forecast expects data centres to drive long-term growth in power demand (as reported by POWER Magazine), so that contract ties a slice of EQT's gas to the price of electricity there instead of to the discounted Appalachian gas price. On hedging, EQT is running light: 570 MMDth hedged from Q4 2026 through Q4 2027 at October 20. Most of it is in collars — a price floor bought, paid for by selling away gains above a cap — with floors of $3.00–$3.72 and caps of $4.51–$5.77, plus some 2027 swaps that lock in about $3.13–$3.16.

For scale: Q4 2026 hedges cover 108 MMDth against guided Q4 sales of 550–600 Bcfe; Q1 2027 only 62 MMDth. Most of the coming winter's gas is sold at whatever the market pays.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SCALE — ~6.3 BCFE/D SOLD $1.09/MCFE UNIT COST OWNED MIDSTREAM POST-EQUITRANS CONTIGUOUS CORE ACREAGE MVP + SOUTHGATE TAKEAWAY ABILITY TO CURTAIL PJM-LINKED POWER CONTRACTS RANGE DEAL: 9+ BCFE/D

The genuine advantage is cost per unit combined with the ability to not sell. A producer with $1.09/Mcfe of operating cost and a realised price of $2.72 keeps roughly 60% of each unit of revenue as cash margin before capital spending — and when prices are weak, EQT can hold back volumes in the worst months instead of selling into a crowded basin, then sell them later. It did exactly that in Q3 2026, curtailing 21 Bcfe, and guides to another 10–20 Bcfe in Q4. Owning the pipe gives it more control over when and where that gas moves. The Range deal adds scale to the same playbook: the companies say the combined business would produce more than 9 Bcfe a day and break even on unlevered free cash flow (before interest) below $2 per MMBtu.

What it is not is a moat against price. EQT sells an undifferentiated product into a market whose price it does not set. Every advantage listed above is a relative advantage — it determines who survives $2 gas, not whether $2 gas happens.

Appalachian basis is structural, not cyclical. The −$0.64/Mcf differential exists because the Marcellus and Utica can produce more gas than the pipes out of the region can carry. EQT has improved its exposure at the edges, but it cannot build its way out alone: the POWER Pipeline is still at the open-season stage, with no capacity or in-service date in the release. Buying Range adds more Appalachian gas behind the same bottleneck.

Single-basin, single-commodity concentration. Essentially all production is Appalachian natural gas, and Range is another Appalachian producer. There is no oil-basin leg, no international diversification, and only a modest NGL and oil contribution ($36.73/Bbl NGLs excluding ethane, $65.51/Bbl oil in Q3 2026 — meaningful per barrel but small in the mix). One regulatory regime, one weather pattern, one pipeline network, one price.

The demand story is signed but not delivered. The Asian LNG offtake begins in 2028 and is worth roughly $45M of free cash flow that year; the Ignitis deal has no disclosed start date. The CPV contract runs ten years but is tied to a power plant that must be built. None is generating cash today. A thesis that rests on data centres and LNG lifting Appalachian gas prices has a gap measured in years, during which the stock trades on the gas price.

Light hedging cuts both ways. With floors of $3.00–$3.72 and caps of $4.51–$5.77 over only 570 MMDth, EQT has chosen to leave most of its output unhedged. That keeps the gains in a cold winter — but it means soft gas prices flow almost directly into cash flow, and future free cash flow is far less locked in than the steady recent results suggest.

RSI compares the size of recent gains with the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 suggest the stock has run hot relative to its own recent history; below 30 suggests it has been beaten down. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and on monthly data it turns slowly, so treat it as context rather than a trigger.

From the $19.91 close on October 29, 2021 to $54.54 on October 29, 2026, EQT returned +173.9% in price terms over the five years — roughly 22.3% compounded annually, before the modest dividend — against +67.5% for SPY and +85.3% for QQQ over the same dates. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

One of the largest US gas producers, with a $1.09/Mcfe cost stack, generated $3.19B of free cash flow attributable to EQT over the last twelve months, and $283M in a Q3 when the realised price was only $2.72/Mcfe. Net debt has fallen to $5.47B from $7.69B at the start of the year (D/E 0.19), and EV/EBITDA of 6.48 is undemanding. Range adds scale: more than 9 Bcfe a day combined, $325M of expected yearly savings, and a combined free-cash-flow breakeven (before interest) the companies put below $2 per MMBtu. Meanwhile the demand side is changing shape: a PJM-linked ten-year power contract and two LNG sales deals begin to tie a slice of revenue to prices other than the discounted Appalachian one. Because most output is unhedged and costs are mostly fixed per unit, any rise in gas prices goes almost straight to cash margin, which was $1.63/Mcfe in Q3.

Bear Case

EQT sold 8.1% less gas than a year earlier, partly by choice, and realised price slipped to $2.72/Mcfe from $2.76 — the basis problem is being managed around with curtailments and marketing, not fixed, and buying Range adds more gas behind the same pipes. The deal brings roughly 231M new shares, needs two shareholder votes and regulatory clearance, and EQT's stock fell 1.4% the day it was announced. The demand contracts are real but small and late: ~$45M of 2028 free cash flow from the Asian LNG deal, undisclosed terms on Ignitis, and a power plant that still has to be built. With only 570 MMDth hedged, a mild winter passes straight through to cash flow. The current ratio of 0.71 and $78.7M of cash mean little buffer outside the credit line. And consensus expects adjusted EPS to fall 8.7% in FY2027.

01
Natural gas price and Appalachian basis. EQT takes the market price for an undifferentiated commodity sold at a regional discount of −$0.64/Mcf. At full-year guidance of 2,375–2,450 Bcfe, a sustained $0.50 move in the gas price swings roughly $1.2B of yearly revenue before hedges (our arithmetic), and the basis discount can widen on its own when regional supply outruns pipeline space.
02
Thin hedge coverage into the winter. 570 MMDth across five quarters leaves most production exposed. The collar caps at $4.51–$5.77 also mean part of any real price spike is given away. And from Q1 2027 most of the collars also sell a put at $2.50, so below that gas price the hedge pays nothing more (hedge table at October 20, Q3 release).
03
Deal risk. The Range merger needs shareholder approval at both companies and regulatory clearance, with closing expected in the first quarter of 2027. If it closes, EQT's shareholders go from owning all of EQT to about 73% of the combined company, in exchange for more gas in the same basin.
04
Single basin, single commodity. All meaningful production is Appalachian gas, exposed to one regulatory regime and one pipeline network. The pipeline segments that came with Equitrans held steadier through the soft gas price — $286.3M of operating income in Q3 2026 against $288.0M a year earlier, while Upstream fell from $370.3M to $219.6M (10-Q, Note 2) — but those pipes serve the same basin, so they do not spread the risk beyond Appalachia.
Industry Commentary
Appalachian Takeaway Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The Marcellus and Utica can physically produce more gas than the interstate pipeline network can carry to premium markets, and that imbalance shows up as the basis differential — −$0.64/Mcf for EQT in Q3 2026 against a NYMEX price of $2.96. That is a 22% haircut to the benchmark price before hedges and liquids. The release says the Q3 differential beat guidance, and Toby Rice credited the company's marketing in a quarter of falling gas prices. Q4 guidance is −$0.55 to −$0.65. Mountain Valley Pipeline Southgate, which EQT said in July it aimed to finish by year-end 2026, and the proposed POWER Pipeline to Ohio's Clarington hub are the relief on offer; neither solves the regional imbalance on its own.

Power Demand and the PJM Data-Centre Build
EMERGING TAILWIND

The single most important structural change available to Appalachian gas is that demand may finally come to the molecules rather than requiring the molecules to travel. EQT's ten-year, 325,000 Dth/d agreement with Competitive Power Ventures for the Shay Energy Center in West Virginia is priced against PJM power prices, not Henry Hub — which is the whole point. Announcing the Range deal, Toby Rice said the expanded resource base "solidifies EQT as the operator of choice for premium, long-term supply arrangements" (EQT/Range joint release, October 26). That is a claim about who the next big buyers of Appalachian gas will be — power plants and data centres nearby, plus LNG exporters — and it is the way the basis discount could narrow for good. The caveat is timing: the CPV plant has to be built before that contract delivers.

Bull vs Bear — What Actually Decides This
ONGOING HEADWIND

Both cases agree on the facts and disagree on the clock. The bull owns a low-cost producer generating $3.19B of free cash flow over twelve months, with $2.72/Mcfe realised in a soft Q3, a growing list of long-term sales contracts and an undemanding 6.48× EV/EBITDA, and now a deal that would make it bigger still; every increment of firm regional demand means a narrower discount and a fatter margin. The bear points out that the contracted demand delivers $45M in 2028, that the Range deal adds supply to the same crowded basin, and that hedge protection is thin into the winter. The answer is not an opinion about AI — it is whether power and LNG demand turn into firm, priced contracts for Appalachian gas faster than the region's supply grows. That is a question of years, while the stock trades day to day on the next few months of gas prices: it has ranged between $47.94 and $68.24 (intraday) over the past twelve months.
