01 Company Overview

EQT Corporation is the largest natural gas producer in the United States by volume, operating almost entirely in the Appalachian Basin across Pennsylvania, West Virginia and Ohio. Following the July 2024 all-stock acquisition of Equitrans Midstream, the company describes itself as vertically integrated — it no longer just drills wells, it owns a large slice of the gathering and transmission pipe those wells flow into, plus a marketing and risk-management desk that sells the molecules. FY2025 revenue was $8.18B, up 62.3% from $5.04B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure is $9.29B. Market capitalisation of $33.98B places it around #770 globally.

The central debate on this page is not "is gas going up." It is whether vertical integration structurally narrows the one number that has defined EQT's economics for a decade: the Appalachian basis differential — the discount at which gas sold at the wellhead in the Marcellus trades to the Henry Hub NYMEX benchmark, because there is more gas in Appalachia than there is pipe to move it out. In Q2 2026 that differential was −$0.67/Mcf against a NYMEX price of $2.89. Owning the pipe, signing power and LNG contracts, and curtailing production into weak months are all attempts to attack that number from different angles. Everything below is an attempt to test whether it is working.

A note on why this report does not lead with EPS. EQT carries a large derivative book and marks it to market through the income statement. In Q2 2026 the "gain on derivatives" line contributed $44.6M of reported revenue; in Q2 2025 the same line contributed $720.0M. That single non-cash swing is why reported revenue fell 3.9% year-over-year in a quarter when physical sales volumes rose 11.6%, and it is why quarterly EPS ran $2.36 in Q1 2026 and $0.34 in Q2 2026. The honest scorecard for a gas producer is volumes, realised price net of basis and hedges, per-unit cash cost, free cash flow and net debt. Those are the numbers this page is built around.

The Equitrans re-merger closed July 22, 2024, undoing the 2018 spin-off and putting gathering systems, transmission lines, storage and the Mountain Valley Pipeline interest back inside the parent. The strategic logic is that a producer who owns its own pipe pays itself the midstream margin rather than a third party, and — more importantly — captures an option on solving its own takeaway problem. The visible financial effect shows up less as external revenue (only $155.3M of "pipeline and other" in the quarter) and more as an internalised cost: transmission at $0.40/Mcfe and gathering at $0.09/Mcfe are now largely payments to itself. In Q2 2026 the company closed a $77M acquisition of Blackline Midstream (two propane storage and distribution terminals in New England) and accelerated $85M of capital contributions into Mountain Valley Pipeline Southgate, targeting completion by year-end 2026.

Caution: because EQT reports consolidated, the claim that midstream ownership is accretive cannot be checked against a segment P&L. It has to be inferred from the differential and the unit cost stack.

Two contracts announced with Q2 2026 results are the concrete version of the "demand is coming to Appalachia" thesis, and both are stated on the release rather than inferred. First, a five-year LNG offtake agreement with a large Asian integrated energy company for 0.5 million tonnes per annum beginning in 2028, which the company expects to add approximately $45M to 2028 free cash flow — real, but small against a $3.8B TTM FCF base, and two years out. Second, a ten-year gas supply agreement with Competitive Power Ventures for 325,000 Dth/d to the Shay Energy Center in Doddridge County, West Virginia, with pricing linked to PJM power prices rather than to Henry Hub. That last detail is the interesting one: it converts a slice of production from a basis-discounted commodity sale into an exposure to regional power prices, which is precisely where data-centre load is bidding. On hedging, EQT is running light — roughly 790 MMDth hedged across Q3 2026 through Q4 2027, predominantly collars with short calls averaging $4.51–$5.77/Dth and long puts at $3.00–$3.65/Dth. That is a floor-and-cap structure, not a locked-in price: downside is protected in the low $3s, upside is given away above roughly $4.50.

Estimate flag: hedged volume as a percentage of production is an approximation — 790 MMDth spans six quarters against annual guidance of 2,375–2,450 Bcfe, implying single-digit to low-teens percentage coverage depending on the period compared. Treat it as "lightly hedged," not a precise ratio.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SCALE — #1 US GAS PRODUCER $1.03/MCFE UNIT COST OWNED MIDSTREAM POST-EQUITRANS CONTIGUOUS CORE ACREAGE MVP + SOUTHGATE TAKEAWAY CURTAILMENT OPTIONALITY PJM-LINKED POWER CONTRACTS

The genuine advantage is cost per unit combined with the ability to not sell. A producer with $1.03/Mcfe of operating cost and a realised price of $2.65 has roughly 61% of its revenue left as cash margin before capital spending — and when the strip is weak, EQT can curtail volumes into the worst months instead of dumping them into a saturated basin, then sell them later. Management explicitly attributed Q2's better-than-guided differential to that curtailment strategy plus marketing optimisation. That is an operating option most smaller Appalachian producers cannot exercise, because they lack both the midstream flexibility and the balance-sheet slack to defer revenue. Owning the pipe is what turns that option from theory into a lever.

What it is not is a moat against price. EQT sells an undifferentiated molecule into a market it does not set the price of. Every advantage listed above is a relative advantage — it determines who survives a $2 strip, not whether a $2 strip happens.

Appalachian basis is structural, not cyclical. The −$0.67/Mcf differential exists because the Marcellus and Utica can produce more gas than the pipes out of the region can carry. EQT has improved its exposure at the margin, but it cannot build its way out alone — new interstate pipeline capacity out of Appalachia has been the single hardest thing to permit in US energy for a decade. Management describing the differential as "favourable to guidance despite widening basis during the quarter" is an admission that the underlying pressure is still building.

Single-basin, single-commodity concentration. Essentially all production is Appalachian natural gas. There is no Permian oil leg, no international diversification, and only a modest NGL and oil contribution ($38.49/Bbl NGLs, $70.14/Bbl oil in Q2 2026 — meaningful per barrel but small in the mix). One regulatory regime, one weather pattern, one pipeline network, one price.

The demand story is signed but not delivered. The LNG offtake begins in 2028 and is worth roughly $45M of annual free cash flow. The CPV contract runs ten years but is tied to a power plant that must be built. Neither is generating cash today. A thesis that rests on data centres and LNG rerating Appalachian gas has an execution gap measured in years, during which the stock trades on the strip.

Light hedging cuts both ways. With collars at roughly $3.00–$3.65 floors and $4.51–$5.77 caps over only ~790 MMDth, EQT has chosen to run largely unhedged exposure. That is a defensible post-deleveraging posture and it maximises upside in a tight winter — but it means a soft gas strip flows almost directly into cash flow, and the promised FCF is far less contracted than the headline stability of the metrics suggests.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The largest US gas producer with a $1.03/Mcfe cost stack is generating $3.76B of trailing free cash flow at a realised price of only $2.65/Mcfe — that is what the business looks like near the bottom of the price range, not the top. Leverage is modest (net debt $5.5B, D/E 0.20), the EV/EBITDA multiple of 5.63 is undemanding, and the company just raised production guidance by ~90 Bcfe while cutting maintenance capex. Meanwhile the demand side is changing shape: a PJM-linked ten-year power contract and a 2028 LNG offtake begin to decouple a slice of revenue from the Appalachian basis discount that has capped this equity for a decade. If the gas strip firms at all, the operating leverage on $1.62/Mcfe of cash margin is violent.

Bear Case

Realised price fell $0.16/Mcfe year-over-year and management itself noted basis widened during the quarter — the structural problem is not fixed, it is being managed around with curtailments and marketing. The demand contracts are real but small and late: ~$45M of 2028 free cash flow from LNG, and a power plant that still has to be built. With only ~790 MMDth hedged in collars capped near $4.51–$5.77, a mild winter passes straight through to cash flow with almost no protection. The current ratio of 0.67 and $112.9M of cash mean there is no buffer. And the forward P/E of 16.52 above the trailing 12.75 says consensus already expects earnings to fall from here.

Industry Commentary
Appalachian Takeaway Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The Marcellus and Utica can physically produce more gas than the interstate pipeline network can carry to premium markets, and that imbalance is expressed as the basis differential — −$0.67/Mcf for EQT in Q2 2026 against a NYMEX price of $2.89. That is a 23% haircut to the benchmark price before hedges and liquids. Management's own language — the differential came in favourable to guidance "despite widening basis during the quarter" — tells you the pressure is increasing, not easing, and that the beat came from optimisation and curtailment rather than from the market improving. Mountain Valley Pipeline Southgate, targeted for completion by year-end 2026 with $85M of accelerated contributions, is the incremental relief; it is not a solution to the regional imbalance.

Power Demand and the PJM Data-Centre Build
EMERGING TAILWIND

The single most important structural change available to Appalachian gas is that demand may finally come to the molecules rather than requiring the molecules to travel. EQT's ten-year, 325,000 Dth/d agreement with Competitive Power Ventures for the Shay Energy Center in West Virginia is priced against PJM power prices, not Henry Hub — which is the whole point. CEO Toby Rice framed it directly: "power generators and data centre developers increasingly look to secure reliable, long-term energy supply," with EQT positioned as "the partner of choice in Appalachia." Read carefully, that is a claim about where the next marginal buyer sits, and it is the mechanism by which the basis discount could structurally narrow. The caveat is timing — the plant has to be built, and the contract is one of one so far.

Bull vs Bear — What Actually Decides This
ONGOING HEADWIND

Both cases agree on the facts and disagree on the clock. The bull owns a low-cost producer throwing off $3.76B of free cash flow at a trough-ish $2.65/Mcfe realised price, with a signed pipeline of demand contracts and an undemanding 5.63× EV/EBITDA; every increment of firm regional demand compounds into a narrower basis and a fatter margin. The bear points out that the contracted demand delivers $45M in 2028 while the realised price is falling today, basis is widening today, and hedge protection runs out in the low $3s. The resolution is not an opinion about AI — it is whether PJM power demand converts into firm, priced Appalachian offtake faster than regional supply growth widens the discount. That is a two-to-three-year question being priced by a market that trades a two-to-three-month gas strip, which is why the stock swings between $47.94 and $68.24 while the business barely changes shape.
