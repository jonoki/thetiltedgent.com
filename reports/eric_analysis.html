01 Company Overview

Ericsson builds the equipment and software that mobile operators use to run their networks: the radios and antennas on cell towers, the "core" software that routes calls and data, and the services to install and run it all. It also licenses its mobile-standard patents to phone makers, and runs an enterprise unit built around Vonage, a cloud communications business. It began in 1876 as Lars Magnus Ericsson's mechanical workshop in central Stockholm. Sales over the four quarters to September 30, 2026 were SEK 230.8B (about $23.2B), and the Networks segment brought in 64.5% of them. For full-year 2025, sales fell to SEK 236.7B from SEK 247.9B in kronor, though they rose 2% organically; diluted EPS was SEK 8.51, lifted by a SEK 7.6B gain on selling the iconectiv unit.

For most of 2026 the story was rebuilt margins and too little growth. The balance sheet carries SEK 60.7B of net cash (cash and short-term investments minus borrowings), and the board is returning money through a SEK 3.00 dividend and a SEK 15B share buyback. The ADS rose from $9.65 at the end of 2025 to a $13.06 May close, then fell 29.9% to a $9.15 September close. The fall included three sharp down days: the CEO change announced on June 16 (−3.9%), a weak Q2 report on July 14 (the ADS fell 13.5% that day) and Morgan Stanley's downgrade to Underweight on September 22 (−4.2%). Per Narvinger took over as chief executive on October 1. His first report, on October 15, showed sales up 7% organically, meaning without currency effects and the iconectiv sale, and adjusted EBITA of SEK 10.1B against SEK 7.9B expected. The ADS rose 11.8% that day and closed October 16 at $10.55, still 19.2% below the May close. The central debate has shifted. The radio market is still flat (Narvinger's word in the report), and part of the quarter came from timing: project deliveries in India and Japan, plus back payments for patent licences. Rising component costs will press on gross margin in 2027. So was Q3 a turn, or a good quarter in a flat business that is now mainly a cash-return story?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base at major operators Patent portfolio (~SEK 13.5B/yr) Standards and R&D depth Net cash SEK 60.7B Managed-services lock-in

The moat is incumbency plus patents. Once an operator has built a region on one vendor's radios and core, swapping vendors means re-engineering the network, so upgrades and expansions usually go to the incumbent. The patent book, built from its work on mobile standards, earns a licensing stream (about SEK 13.5B a year on a recurring basis) that does not depend on selling hardware. What the moat does not protect is volume: when operators finish a 5G build-out, they stop buying, however entrenched the vendor.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A flat market leaves little room to grow. Narvinger's Q3 comment begins its outlook: "Looking ahead, the RAN market is flat, geopolitical uncertainty remains elevated and component costs are a headwind to gross margin next year." Dell'Oro's May 2026 estimate is a stable market. Q3's 7% organic growth leaned on timing: the report puts the Asian growth down mainly to when projects were delivered, and part of the patent income was back payments. For the first nine months reported sales were down 4% (SEK 161.5B against SEK 167.4B, SEK −9.6B of it currency). The Infront consensus Ericsson published before the print (October 8, 16 contributors) had full-year 2026 sales of SEK 226.2B, 4.4% below 2025.
02
Component costs are rising. Narvinger calls component costs "a headwind to gross margin next year." On the call he said 2027 could be a transition year, because price increases take time to pass through multi-year customer contracts. In Q3 the Networks adjusted gross margin of 50.3% was already "partly offset by increased component costs," and inventories rose to SEK 35.4B from SEK 23.5B at the end of 2025, which the report attributes partly to higher component prices. Gross margin has been the core of the recovery story.
03
Legal overhang from the Iraq investigation. The US Department of Justice is still investigating matters from Ericsson's 2019 internal Iraq investigation, and the company says the scope and duration "remain uncertain." Five civil suits under the US Anti-Terrorism Act (filed August 2022, February 2024, November 2025, March 2026 and September 2026) allege that payments by the company ultimately aided terrorist groups. In Sweden, 93 claimants are suing over alleged inadequate disclosure, and proceedings on the merits have resumed. China's market regulator (SAMR) has been investigating Ericsson's patent licensing there since 2019. Ericsson says it will vigorously defend the civil cases.
04
Currency works against it both ways. About half of 2025 sales were in US dollars and over 15% in euros, while Ericsson reports in Swedish kronor. A stronger krona cut reported sales in the first nine months of 2026 by SEK 9.6B. US holders of the ADS face the same exposure in reverse: over five years (October 15, 2021 to October 16, 2026) the ADS fell 13.4% while the Stockholm B share was flat (−0.1%), the gap being the krona's fall against the dollar.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the comparisons. First, the iconectiv sale in Q3 2025 produced a SEK 7.6B capital gain (SEK 1.72 per share). It has now dropped out of the four-quarter figures, which is why TTM EPS, net margin, ROE and ROCE all fell this quarter, and why Q3 2026 net income (SEK 5.8B) and EBITA look far below Q3 2025's. Second, restructuring: SEK 5.3B of charges in the first nine months against SEK 1.2B a year earlier, which is why Ericsson reports "adjusted" figures that exclude them. Third, patent timing: Q3 2026 patent income included back payments for previously unlicensed periods, which lifted Networks sales and gross margin; the report does not size them. Fourth, a SEK 0.3B non-cash impairment related to Red Bee Media hit segment Other in Q3. Share count matters too: Ericsson held 114.1 million B shares in treasury at September 30, 2026 and 125.8 million at October 9, against 38.0 million at the end of 2025, as the buyback runs.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay about 8.8 times the pre-Q3 consensus for 2026 EBITA for the whole business once SEK 60.7B of net cash is netted off, and that consensus was set before a quarter that beat it by SEK 2.2B. The company has a 48% adjusted gross margin, about SEK 13.5B a year of recurring patent income and SEK 26.1B of trailing free cash flow. Between the SEK 15B buyback and the SEK 3.00 dividend, roughly 7% of the market value is going back to holders over about a year. Networks grew 9% organically in Q3 with a 23.1% adjusted EBITA margin, and Cloud Software and Services has turned from a problem into a 13% EBITA-margin business. The new CEO's first move was a leaner Executive Team and a simpler structure. The ADS is still 19.2% below its May close, and every target on both US analyst panels sits below the price. On stockanalysis.com's panel the latest target change came on October 8, before the beat, so those targets could move up if Q4 confirms the trend.

Bear Case

Ericsson sells into a market that its new CEO calls flat and Dell'Oro calls stable. Q3's growth leaned on timing: Asian project deliveries and back payments for patent licences, which the report does not size. Reported sales are still down 4% for the first nine months. Management says component costs are a headwind to gross margin next year, and on the call Narvinger said 2027 could be a transition year while price increases work through multi-year contracts. Restructuring charges are running at SEK 5.3B for nine months, the Enterprise unit still loses money and is being broken up, and the Iraq-related DOJ investigation and five US civil suits remain open with no date. At 18 times trailing earnings the ADS is no longer cheap on reported profit, and the US analyst panels range from Hold to Sell, with Danske Bank cutting to Hold the day after the print.

Top 3 Risks

01
Gross-margin squeeze from component costs. The Q3 report calls component costs "a headwind to gross margin next year," and the Networks margin was already partly offset by them in Q3. Gross margin has been the core of the recovery story; the stock fell 13.5% on July 14, the day the Q2 report flagged component cost inflation.
02
A one-quarter rebound in a flat RAN market. Q3's 7% organic growth came largely from the timing of project deliveries in Japan and India and from back-dated patent income, while Europe is finishing modernisation projects and Latin America fell. If Q4 and 2027 revert to flat, buybacks and cost cuts carry per-share earnings on their own.
03
Legal and governance overhang. The DOJ Iraq-related investigation is open with an uncertain scope, five Anti-Terrorism Act suits name the company and its former CEO, Swedish shareholder claims proceed on the merits, and China's SAMR patent-licensing probe could bring remedies. All of this lands on a new CEO in his first year, during a reorganisation.
Industry Commentary
A flat radio market after the 5G build-out
STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINT

Mobile network equipment sells in waves: a new generation (4G, then 5G) triggers years of heavy spending, then operators run what they bought for as long as they can. Ericsson's Q4 2025 report said "For 2026, we expect the RAN market to be flat," Dell'Oro's May 2026 estimate is a stable market, and Narvinger's Q3 comment repeats that "the RAN market is flat." The Q3 2026 report shows the pattern regionally. Sales in Europe "declined slightly following the gradual completion of modernization projects," North America grew only slightly, and growth came from project deliveries in Japan, India and South East Asia and from network modernisation in the Middle East. In a market like this, share and margin matter more than volume, which is why Ericsson keeps cutting costs and headcount (85,417 people at September 30, down 5.0% in a year) and is now merging its two Asian market areas.

Component inflation and the margin question
NEAR-TERM HEADWIND

The Q3 print passed the first test: Ericsson had guided Networks adjusted gross margin to 48–50%, and it came in at 50.3%, with product mix and back-dated patent income outweighing higher component costs. The group adjusted gross margin was 47.7% against 48.1% a year earlier, when iconectiv was still in Enterprise. The harder test is 2027. The Q3 report calls component costs "a headwind to gross margin next year," and the company says it is pursuing "portfolio optimization, pricing and efficiency measures to help offset higher component costs." Price increases take time to reach multi-year operator contracts, which is why Narvinger told the call 2027 could be a transition year. For now the Q4 guide of 49–51% for Networks sits a point above the Q3 guide. The stock nearly tripled from its 2023 low while that margin recovered.

AI-era networks and the new CEO's opening hand
POSSIBLE TAILWIND

Ekholm's last quarterly letter said Ericsson had "expanded into attractive growth areas, positioning Ericsson to capitalize as AI increasingly moves into the physical world." The Q2 report's highlights include AI-enabled drone sensing using existing cell towers, demonstrated at a Texas stadium. In January the company said it planned to increase investment in defence during 2026, and that mission-critical and enterprise markets were expected to grow. In his first report Narvinger wrote that customers want "more intelligent and autonomous networks" and that Ericsson is "already seeing the efficiency gains from AI-enabled features in our portfolio being deployed in customer networks." In Q3 NTT DOCOMO began deploying Ericsson's new RAN Compute platform for 5G Advanced and future AI-driven network capabilities, and Queensland chose Ericsson for the first stage of a private 5G rail network. None of this is yet large enough to show in the segment numbers: Enterprise is 7.7% of sales and loss-making, and its private 5G business is moving into Networks. Narvinger has SEK 60.7B of net cash, a SEK 15B buyback under way and a flat core market. Whether he keeps returning cash or spends some of it on growth is the open question for the next twelve months.
