01 Company Overview

EssilorLuxottica makes spectacle lenses, the frames around them and, increasingly, the computer inside them. It owns lens brands such as Varilux, Stellest and Transitions, frame brands such as Ray-Ban and Oakley, licences for luxury labels such as Chanel and Prada, and retail chains including Sunglass Hut and LensCrafters, close to 20,000 stores in all. It also builds the Ray-Ban Meta and Oakley Meta AI glasses with Meta Platforms, a category that sold more than 7 million units in 2025 (company). FY2025 revenue was €28.49B, up 11.2% at constant exchange rates, and first-half 2026 revenue was €14.82B (+9.7% at constant currency, +5.7% reported). On 459.59M shares (StockAnalysis) × €142.80, the market value is €65.63B, or $73.67B at the ECB rate of 1.1225.

The puzzle is the price. The business kept growing, but the stock did not. The shares closed at €320.70 on November 12, 2025; on October 2, 2026 they closed at €142.80, down 55.5%, and back below every month-end of the past five years except September 2022. In that time revenue kept growing (Q1 2026 +10.8%, Q2 +8.7% at constant currency) and first-half adjusted operating profit rose 15% at constant currency. What changed is how much the market will pay for that growth. In February the company set a new five-year outlook that promises profit growing roughly in line with revenue, not faster. Since then analysts have cut their forecasts and targets. Kepler Cheuvreux moved to Reduce on September 17, cutting its 2030 smart-glasses estimate to 15 million units from 38 million (Bloomberg). A public fight with the founder's son over strategy and a Paris criminal probe into smart glasses misuse followed. It now trades at about 27x trailing earnings and 20x forward. The question is whether AI glasses are a growth engine or a margin problem.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Lens technology & clinical data Ray-Ban / Oakley ownership Luxury licences (Chanel, Prada…) ~20,000 stores 300,000 eye care professionals Meta partnership & manufacturing Vertical integration

The moat is control of the whole chain. The same company invents the lens, makes the frame, owns the brand the customer asks for, and often owns the shop that sells it. A pair of AI glasses has to fit a face, take a prescription and sit in a store window. EssilorLuxottica already does all three at scale, and that is what it brings to the Meta partnership. Myopia lenses add a medical layer, backed by clinical trials and an FDA authorisation that a fashion brand cannot copy quickly. The weakness is the flip side of the same strength. Integration only protects the business while the product is eyewear. If the value of glasses moves into the software, it moves towards Meta, Apple and Google.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Smart glasses may dilute margins. In FY2025 the adjusted operating margin fell to 15.7% (16.0% at constant currency) from 16.7%, and the company blamed U.S. tariffs and AI glasses. The new five-year outlook promises profit growth "broadly aligned" with revenue, which means no margin expansion. Bernstein argued in February that tech companies including Apple, Google, Samsung and Alibaba could reshape the industry (Investing.com, Feb 22, 2026).
02
One partner, one category. The AI-glasses story depends on Meta, which owns the AI, the software and the customer relationship on Meta.com, and holds a stake of its own. The June 2026 co-branded Meta Glasses line carries Meta's name first. Goldman Sachs, Bernstein, RBC and Barclays all cut targets in late September 2026.
03
Privacy and regulation. Reuters reported on September 18, 2026 that Paris prosecutors had opened a criminal investigation into suspected sexual harassment committed with smart glasses, after women were filmed in the street without consent. Prosecutors named no brand. Meta said it had built privacy into its glasses, and EssilorLuxottica declined to comment (Reuters). Australia is considering a ban on camera glasses in government workplaces (same report).
04
Governance. Delfin holds about 32% and its family shareholders disagree. A founder's son has publicly questioned the CEO's reappointment, and the CEO's board term ends in 2027. Kepler Cheuvreux said the governance uncertainty raised questions about leadership and strategic direction (Bloomberg, Sep 17, 2026).

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the headline numbers. First, acquisition accounting: amortisation of intangibles from the Essilor–Luxottica combination and the GrandVision purchase cost about €441M in H1 2026 (€455M a year earlier). That is why IFRS EPS (€5.35 TTM) sits well below adjusted earnings, and why the company and analysts quote adjusted figures. Second, U.S. tariffs: FY2025 margins absorbed the 2025 tariffs. After the U.S. Supreme Court ruled them unlawful in February 2026, H1 2026 booked a net positive effect, so part of the H1 margin gain is a reversal rather than a trend. Third, FX: constant-currency growth ran four points ahead of reported growth in H1 (9.7% against 5.7%). Other H1 adjustments were small: about €21M of restructuring, €12M of M&A fees and €7M of legal costs.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business has not broken. H1 2026 revenue grew 9.7% at constant currency, the adjusted operating margin rose to 18.6%, and free cash flow increased. Comparable-store sales accelerated to 8% in Q2. Myopia lenses grow over 20% a year with FDA authorisation in the US, and AI glasses almost doubled in Q2 with about three-quarters of the category. At €142.80 the shares trade at about 20x forward adjusted earnings, and the trailing IFRS multiple is below Hoya's. The 2.8% dividend is paid partly in cash and partly in shares, and the company has kept buying back stock through the fall. Consensus still reads Buy, with an average target of €232.61.

Bear Case

The company's own five-year outlook rules out margin expansion, and AI glasses were part of the FY2025 margin decline. Professional Solutions slowed to +4.9% in Q2. Tougher comparisons start in Q3, against an 11.7% quarter. Meta owns the software and the customer, now sells a co-branded Meta Glasses line, and holds a stake. Bernstein names Apple, Google and Samsung among the tech entrants. Privacy scrutiny is rising, with a criminal probe in Paris. The controlling holder is split, the CEO's term ends in 2027, and net debt has risen to 2.0x EBITDA. Kepler's €135 target sits below today's price, and the six targets set since September 17 average €197.50, well under the €232.61 consensus average.

Top 3 Risks

01
Smart glasses as a margin drag rather than a growth premium. If AI glasses keep growing as a lower-margin hardware line, revenue growth will not turn into faster profit growth, and the five-year outlook already says it will not. Kepler's cut from 38 million to 15 million units by 2030 shows how far category forecasts can move.
02
Dependence on Meta and the tech platforms. The category's leader runs on Meta's AI and is sold partly on Meta.com. A change in Meta's strategy, a rival platform winning share, or regulation of camera glasses would land on EssilorLuxottica's newest growth line.
03
Governance and the 2027 renewal. Delfin's 32% stake gives it the decisive vote, and the family is divided. A contested CEO renewal could change the strategy, including the weight put on AI glasses.
Industry Commentary
AI glasses: whose product is it?
OPEN QUESTION

The Meta–EssilorLuxottica range holds about 76% of the global smart-glasses market (Reuters, Sep 18, 2026). Its sales grew from a novelty to more than 7 million units in 2025. The industry question is where the value sits. If glasses become a computing platform, value moves to whoever owns the AI and the app layer: Meta, Apple, Google. If they stay a fashion and fit product, it stays with whoever owns the brands, the lens labs and the stores. EssilorLuxottica's answer is to own manufacturing and optics, through the Agordo lines and the Applied Materials waveguide work, so that it stays essential to whichever platform wins.

Myopia: eyewear turning into medtech
STRUCTURAL SUPPORT

Childhood short-sightedness is rising, and lenses that slow it are becoming a prescribed treatment rather than a style choice. EssilorLuxottica presented seven-year follow-up data on Stellest at ARVO 2026. A US trial showed myopia progression slowed by 71% on average over two years against single-vision lenses (company summary of the poster). FDA authorisation in September 2025 opened the US market. This is the part of the company the market pays a medtech multiple for, and the new outlook frames the whole group as a "medtech transformation".

Privacy regulation of camera glasses
NEW HEADWIND

Regulators are starting to treat camera glasses as a distinct privacy problem. France opened a criminal probe into harassment filmed with smart glasses, France's data protection authority reports growing business enquiries about workplace bans, and Australia is weighing a ban in government workplaces (Reuters, Sep 18, 2026). The camera-free Ray-Ban Meta Audio line launched on September 24 is one product response.
