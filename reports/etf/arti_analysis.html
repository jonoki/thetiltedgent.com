01 Fund Overview

ARTI is an actively managed basket of artificial-intelligence stocks, hedged to the Canadian dollar, that follows no index and starts from a list drawn up by a language model. Evolve, the manager, "primarily consults and uses a database generated by Gradient Boosted Investments Inc.'s ("Boosted.ai") proprietary large language model technology", which, in the prospectus's words, "determines an issuer's applicability to the theme of AI based on its likelihood to benefit from increased global adoption of AI." The model looks at public companies in developed markets. Evolve then picks the stocks itself: it "has full discretion over investment decisions", and "Boosted.ai does not make investment decisions on behalf of ARTI" (prospectus dated April 26, 2025). The result is a short, concentrated list: 28 stocks on October 1, 2026, rebalanced quarterly (product page).

The stated objective: the fund "seeks to provide exposure to securities of publicly traded issuers that the Manager has determined are expected to benefit from increased adoption of artificial intelligence (AI)" (interim MRFP, June 30, 2026). ARTI is young. Its units "effectively began operations on March 22, 2024" at a net asset value of $10.00 (MRFP), and Evolve announced on March 25, 2024 that the fund had closed its initial offering and begun trading on the TSX that day. Net assets were CAD 25.37 million on October 2, 2026 (product page), against CAD 7.70 million at the end of 2024 (MRFP). The MER was 0.95% annualised for the six months to June 30, 2026 (section 04).

The October 1, 2026 holdings file lists 28 shares and nothing else: 26 U.S. companies by the issuer's country field, plus Taiwan Semiconductor and ASML, both bought through their U.S. listings. The only unit class is "Hedged ETF Units". Evolve "will seek to hedge all or substantially all" foreign currency exposure back to the Canadian dollar with currency forward contracts (prospectus). On June 30, 2026 the contracts mainly sold U.S. dollars, euros and Taiwan dollars for Canadian dollars with BNY Capital Markets Inc., settling July 24; after them, net foreign-currency exposure was CAD 32,897, or 0.13% of net assets (semi-annual financial statements; our ratio).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Hedged to CAD Full holdings list published Traded 251 of 251 days Avg bid-ask spread 0.12% No trailing commission Quarterly rebalancing

The hedge does what it says. On June 30, 2026 the fund held CAD 24.51 million of investments priced in U.S. dollars and CAD 24.48 million of currency forwards selling U.S. dollars (CAD 21.67 million), Taiwan dollars (CAD 1.87 million) and euros (CAD 0.94 million). The financial statements put the total net foreign-currency exposure at CAD 32,897 and the effect of a 5% move in the Canadian dollar at CAD 1,645. In the twelve months to March 31, 2026 the units traded on 251 of 251 days, with an average bid-ask spread of 0.12% and average daily volume of 11,540 units (ETF Facts). The holdings are published in full, and the selection rule is set out in the prospectus. The ETF Facts note that the ETF "doesn't have a trailing commission."

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One theme, few names. Ten stocks are 65.18% of the fund and information technology 73.65% (October 1; our sums). The fund owns no financials, health care, industrials or energy, so a sell-off in large technology companies hits almost all of it at once.
02
A model in the loop. The prospectus warns that a learning language model can rely on "incomplete, inaccurate or contradictory data" or develop biases, and that the fund could then hold or miss stocks it otherwise would not. Evolve keeps the final say and "may determine to rely on a different database or selection process."
03
Small and thinly traded. CAD 25.37 million of net assets (October 2), against CAD 1.22 billion for the largest Canadian AI fund in section 05. On October 2 just 1,874 units changed hands, and the $17.11 close was 1.1% above the $16.92 NAV (our calculation); no units traded at all on October 1.
04
Costs and turnover. The 0.95% MER is above the peers in section 05, and trading costs add a 0.12% trading expense ratio (first half of 2026). Turnover of 80.09% in six months means frequent trading, and possibly taxable gains, for holders outside registered accounts.
05
The hedge works both ways. It removes gains from a rising U.S. dollar as well as losses from a falling one. USD/CAD rose from 1.3586 on March 28, 2024 to 1.4220 on October 2, 2026 (Yahoo Finance CAD=X), 4.7%, which an unhedged holder kept and ARTI did not. The forwards also depend on a bank counterparty: BNY Capital Markets Inc., rated A1+ in the financial statements.
06
A short record. Two and a half years of history and a portfolio that changes often. The 2024–2025 record was earned by a different list of stocks from today's (section 02).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising prices for the large companies spending on and selling AI computing: Microsoft, NVIDIA, Meta, Amazon, Alphabet, Taiwan Semiconductor and Broadcom are 56.03% of the fund (our sum). Markets that reward concentration over breadth, as in April–May 2026. A falling U.S. dollar against the Canadian dollar, which the hedge shields it from. Stock selection that beats the broad technology index, as in 2025 (+21.6% against 21.0% for the Nasdaq-100).

What hurts it

Sell-offs in large U.S. technology stocks, with no other sectors, bonds or cash to cushion them: −27.6% in spring 2025, −25.2% from October 2025 to March 2026 (daily closes). A rising U.S. dollar, whose gain an unhedged fund would keep. Stock selection that lags the broad technology index. Wide spreads or premiums in a thinly traded fund.

The four funds in section 05 all launched in 2024 and all sell "AI" in Canadian dollars, but they choose stocks differently. Index: AIQ follows the Indxx Artificial Intelligence & Big Data Index through a U.S.-listed fund; INAI follows a Morningstar index. Active, by a team: CIAI, run by CI Global Asset Management. Active, model-assisted: ARTI, where Evolve picks from a list ranked by Boosted.ai's language model. Currency: ARTI is hedged only; INAI offers both hedged and unhedged series; AIQ is unhedged. Size varies a lot: CIAI holds CAD 1.22 billion; the other three between CAD 25 and 54 million (figures on different dates). MERs run from 0.39% to 0.95%.

The same giants, in very different doses
WHAT DIFFERS

The biggest names recur everywhere: nine of ARTI's ten largest holdings are also in CIAI's top ten, and six are in AIQ's. What differs is how much of each. ARTI's top ten are 65.18% of the fund, CIAI's 58.50% and AIQ's 32.59% (our sums, on the dates in section 05). A concentrated fund tracks a handful of companies closely; a broader index spreads the same theme across more, smaller names. Returns follow from that choice, and so do drawdowns.
