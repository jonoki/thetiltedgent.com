01 Fund Overview

BND holds the core U.S. bond market in one fund: Treasuries, government-backed mortgage securities and investment-grade company bonds, all in U.S. dollars. In the issuer's words it "seeks to track the performance of the Bloomberg U.S. Aggregate Float Adjusted Index," is "passively managed using index sampling," and gives "broad exposure to the taxable investment-grade U.S. dollar-denominated bond market, excluding inflation-protected and tax-exempt bonds."

BND is not a stand-alone fund. It is the ETF share class of Vanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund, which also has Investor, Admiral, Institutional, Institutional Plus and Institutional Select shares (Vanguard 2026 dividend schedule). All classes own the same portfolio. On August 31, 2026 the ETF shares held USD 163.3 billion of the fund's USD 398.9 billion. The expense ratio is 0.03%, as reported in the prospectus dated April 28, 2026.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.03% USD 163.3B in the ETF shares 11,421 bonds 68.9% U.S. Government credit Monthly income 5-yr NAV −0.30% vs index −0.29%

The fund does one thing: it holds the investment-grade U.S. dollar bond market at very low cost. Its tracking is close. Over the five years to August 31, 2026 the NAV return was −0.30% a year against −0.29% for its index, and over ten years 1.39% against 1.42% (issuer). Vanguard reports a beta and R-squared of 1.00 against the index (Aug 31, 2026). The ETF shares sit inside a USD 398.9 billion fund.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest rates. Average duration is 5.7 years (Aug 31, 2026). Duration means a one-point rise in yields cuts the price by roughly 5.7%, before income. On September 23, 2026 the 10-year Treasury par yield rose from 4.96% to 5.11% (U.S. Treasury) and BND's price fell 0.84%.
02
Inflation. The index excludes inflation-protected bonds, so every coupon is fixed in nominal dollars. Rising inflation lowers the real value of the income and usually pushes yields up at the same time.
03
Mortgage cash-flow risk. Government mortgage pools are 19.2% of the fund and all ten of the largest lines. When rates fall, homeowners refinance and the fund gets money back to reinvest at lower yields. When rates rise, repayments slow and the bonds last longer than expected. The fund's principal-risk list (issuer) names both prepayment and extension risk.
04
Government concentration. Treasury/agency debt is 49.3% of the portfolio and "U.S. Government" credit 68.9%, so U.S. government borrowing costs drive most of the fund directly.
05
Credit spreads. 12.2% is rated BBB, the lowest investment-grade tier. In a recession, company bond prices can fall even if Treasuries rally. A bond cut below BBB drops out of the investment-grade index.
06
Sampling and tax. The fund holds 11,421 of 14,227 index bonds, so returns can differ slightly from the index. In taxable accounts, distributions are taxed each year: Vanguard's own five-year after-tax figure (to June 30, 2026) is −1.22% a year against 0.07% before tax.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling interest rates: with a 5.7-year duration, a one-point drop in yields adds roughly 5.7% to the price, on top of income. Stable rates: the fund then earns roughly its yield (SEC yield 4.89%, yield to maturity 5.0%). Weak economies and stock sell-offs that send money into Treasuries, which are 49.3% of the fund. Slowing inflation. Tight or narrowing credit spreads help the 25.6% in company bonds.

What hurts it

Rising yields, as in 2022, when the total return fell 17.4% from peak to trough, and as on September 23, 2026, when a 15-basis-point jump in the 10-year yield cut the price 0.84%. Inflation above expectations, since no holding is inflation-protected. Rising yields paired with falling stocks, which removes the diversification benefit (five-year correlation with the S&P 500: 0.63). Wider credit spreads, and slower mortgage prepayments when rates rise, which lengthen the fund's duration.

01
Interest-rate risk. Duration 5.7 years. The five years to August 2026 produced a −0.30% annualised NAV return and a −17.4% drawdown, almost all from rising yields.
02

Category Commentary
Core U.S. aggregate bond ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

"Aggregate" bond index funds are the fixed-income counterpart of a total-stock-market fund: one holding for the investment-grade, taxable, U.S. dollar bond market. The large ones are close substitutes. BND, AGG and SPAB all charge 0.03% (each issuer's prospectus figure) and reported SEC yields between 4.87% and 4.89% on September 21–22, 2026. What differs: index version (BND uses the float-adjusted Aggregate, AGG and SPAB the standard Aggregate); sampling depth (11,421 bonds for BND, 13,434 for AGG, 8,563 for SPAB, against 14,227 in each index (issuer pages)); structure (BND is a share class of a USD 398.9 billion Vanguard fund that also has mutual fund shares; AGG and SPAB are stand-alone ETFs). IUSB widens the net to the Bloomberg U.S. Universal Index, charges 0.06% and reported a 5.21% SEC yield.

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

With fees at a few hundredths of a percent and nearly identical indexes, returns across these funds are set by the bond market itself: the level and change of Treasury yields, mortgage prepayment speeds and credit spreads.
