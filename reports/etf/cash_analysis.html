01 Fund Overview

CASH is a savings account in the form of an exchange-traded fund. The money unitholders put in sits in interest-bearing deposit accounts at Canadian banks; the interest is passed on as a monthly distribution, and the unit price stays close to $50. In the issuer's words, "CASH seeks to maximize monthly income for unitholders while preserving capital and liquidity by investing primarily in high-interest deposit accounts with Canadian banks." The prospectus allows it to hold short-term (one year or less) government treasury bills, promissory notes and bankers' acceptances as well, and says it "will not engage in securities lending"; on September 30, 2026 it held only deposit accounts (section 02).

The fund began operations on November 1, 2021 (interim financial report; the ETF Facts give November 2 as the date the ETF started) as the Horizons High Interest Savings ETF. It took its current name on May 1, 2024, when its manager, Horizons ETFs Management (Canada) Inc., rebranded as Global X (Global X release, April 18, 2024). It is a money market fund under Canada's investment-fund rule NI 81-102 (prospectus). On October 2, 2026 it had CAD 6,613 million of net assets across its unit classes and 132,233,836 units outstanding (product page). Among the four Canadian high-interest savings ETFs compared in section 05, it had the most net assets on each date shown there.

Deposit insurance
Not covered by CDIC
The ETF Facts (August 12, 2026): "Although the ETF primarily invests in bank deposit accounts, it is not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation or any other government deposit insurer." The deposit insurance that protects a bank customer's own savings account does not extend to units of this fund; the prospectus says CASH is therefore subject to "the credit risk of the chartered banks in which they make deposits."
How the price behaves
Builds, then resets
Interest accrues every day, so the unit value drifts up through the month and drops back when the distribution goes ex. September 2026: NAV $50.01 on September 1 and $50.09 on September 28; on the September 29 ex-date ($0.08700 distribution) it was $50.00 (issuer daily NAV). Over the fund's life the daily NAV ranged from $49.96 to $50.22.
Distributions
Monthly
"Monthly (if any)" (product page). The last twelve, October 2025 to September 2026, totalled $1.01980 per unit, from $0.07100 (February–April 2026) to $0.10000 (July 2026). A separate accumulating class, CASH.L, reinvests its distributions; it began operations on May 8, 2026 (interim report).
Tax character
Interest income
Distributions were classified as net investment income in every period, plus small returns of capital in 2022 ($0.13 a unit) and 2023 ($0.06) (financial highlights, interim report June 30, 2026). The ETF Facts: in a non-registered account, "distributions from the ETF are included in your taxable income, whether you get them in cash or have them reinvested."

How the rate is set

The fund's income is the interest the banks pay on these deposits, and that rate follows the Bank of Canada. The fund's financial statements: "In general, the interest rates associated with these types of instruments are highly correlated to any movement in the Bank of Canada's benchmark overnight lending rate." A Global X article of October 31, 2023 says of the category: "Their yields are typically tied to the overnight rate set by the Bank of Canada, making them responsive to changes in interest rates." The product page shows a gross yield of 2.16%, before fees, "Effective as of July 8, 2026". The Bank of Canada's target for the overnight rate was 2.25% on October 2, 2026, unchanged since October 30, 2025 in Valet series V39079; at its September 2, 2026 decision the Bank held the target there, with the deposit rate at 2.20%. The gross yield was 0.09 points below the target; after the 0.11% MER it is about 2.05% (our arithmetic).

The 2024 liquidity rule. On October 31, 2023 the bank regulator, the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI), decided that deposits from high-interest savings ETFs stay classed as "unsecure wholesale funding by other legal entity customers": "OSFI’s LAR guideline specifies a run-off factor of 100% for these products." Banks taking such deposits must "hold sufficient high quality liquid assets, such as government bonds, to support all HISA ETF balances that can be withdrawn within 30 days", and had to move to that treatment by January 31, 2024. OSFI wrote that the banks' relationships "are with ETF funds and not with retail unitholders." Before the decision, Global X's October 31, 2023 article said the outcome "could mean that yields for HISA ETFs may remain steady or slightly decrease". CASH's monthly distribution went from $0.21500 in January 2024 to $0.19500 in February, March and April 2024, while the Bank of Canada's target stayed at 5.00% (issuer distribution table; Valet).

Costs are fixed by the fund's documents: the manager must pay "all the expenses of the ETF other than the management fee, any sales taxes on the management fee, and any brokerage expenses and commissions" (interim MRFP).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
No deposit insurance. The fund is "not covered by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation" (ETF Facts) and carries the credit risk of the banks that hold its deposits (prospectus). A bank failure would fall on unitholders without a government backstop. In OSFI's 2023 consultation summary, the regulator answered requests for full insurance of these deposits: "It is not under OSFI’s mandate to enforce insurance on any deposits."
02
Concentration in a few banks. Four institutions hold everything; National Bank alone holds 49.53% and National Bank plus Scotiabank 82.08% (September 30, 2026). The fund's annual report states that "due to the nature of its portfolio investments, the ETF did not have any material credit risk exposure" at the end of 2024 and 2025; the exposure is still to a handful of counterparties.
03
Income moves with the Bank of Canada. The monthly distribution peaked at $0.22500 (August 2023), with the policy rate at 5.00%, and fell to $0.07100 (February–April 2026) after cuts took it to 2.25% (issuer distribution table; Valet). Rate cuts pass through to income within months.
04
Withdrawal terms and regulation. The prospectus says CASH is "generally permitted to make withdrawals without prior notice to satisfy Unitholder redemptions, fund expenses and interest payments", but "may from time to time be otherwise limited by the terms of the deposit arrangements" in its ability to make timely withdrawals. Bank regulation decides how much liquidity banks must hold against these deposits (section 02).
05
Fully taxable income. Distributions are interest, taxed as income in a non-registered account (ETF Facts); there are no dividend or capital-gains components to soften it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

A higher Bank of Canada policy rate: income rose from $0.02564 to $0.22500 a month as the target went from 0.25% to 5.00% in 2022–23. A higher deposit rate from the banks relative to the policy rate. Stable credit at the banks that hold the deposits. Its low fee keeps more of the deposit rate for unitholders.

What hurts it

Rate cuts, which pass through to the distribution within months (from $0.22500 in 2023 to $0.07100 in early 2026). Liquidity rules that require banks to hold more liquid assets against these deposits, as OSFI's did from January 2024. Stress at one of the four institutions, since there is no deposit insurance. Inflation above the yield, which shrinks the real value of the savings.

Top 3 Risks

01
Bank credit without CDIC cover. All assets are deposits at four institutions, 49.53% at National Bank; the issuer states the fund is not covered by CDIC or any other government deposit insurer.
02
Falling rates. The yield follows the Bank of Canada; the 2024–25 cuts took the annualised distribution from 4.8% of the unit price ($0.20000 in May 2024, the last month before the first cut) to 2.1% ($0.08700 in September 2026).
03
Regulation and deposit terms. OSFI's liquidity rules require banks to hold liquid assets against these deposits (before the decision, Global X wrote that HISA ETF yields could stay steady or fall slightly), and the deposit agreements can limit how quickly the fund withdraws (prospectus).

High-interest savings ETFs: a bank account you buy on the exchange
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These funds put investors' cash into deposit accounts at large Canadian banks and pass the interest through each month, at a unit price that stays near $50 (CASH, CSAV, PSA and HISA all had a NAV of about $50 on October 2, 2026). The four funds in section 05 hold about CAD 16.7 billion between them (our sum of the figures shown, on different dates and bases). They differ in fee (MER 0.11% to 0.17%), in the banks they use, and in whether they also hold treasury bills: PSA had 14.86% in Canadian T-bills on June 30, 2026 and CSAV three Government of Canada T-bills on August 31, while CASH and HISA held only deposits. The rate on all of them follows the Bank of Canada: gross yields ranged from 2.16% to 2.35% around October 2, 2026 against a 2.25% policy rate.

Next to GICs and T-bill funds
WHAT DIFFERS

Global X's October 2023 article sets out the alternatives: guaranteed investment certificates (GICs) are bank liabilities and, when issued by CDIC members, "are insured by CDIC, protecting eligible deposits up to $100,000"; T-bill ETFs hold short-term government debt instead of bank deposits. High-interest savings ETFs trade on any trading day with no lock-up, but carry the banks' credit risk without deposit insurance. Since January 2024, OSFI requires banks to hold liquid assets against all such ETF balances that can be withdrawn within 30 days, noting that the banks' relationships are with the funds, not with retail unitholders.
