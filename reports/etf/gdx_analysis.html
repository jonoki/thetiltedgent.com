01 Fund Overview

GDX puts 59 of the world's larger gold and silver mining companies into one share: the big producers led by Newmont and Agnico Eagle, the royalty and streaming companies that finance mines in return for a share of their output, and a long list of mid-sized miners from Canada, Australia, Africa and Latin America. VanEck describes the fund as seeking to replicate, before fees and expenses, the MarketVector Global Gold Miners Index, "which is intended to track the overall performance of companies involved in the gold mining industry" (fund page). Until September 19, 2025 it tracked a different benchmark, the NYSE Arca Gold Miners Index (summary prospectus, May 1, 2026). The new index admits only companies that earn at least half their revenue from gold or silver mining, royalties or streaming (a quarter for current members), and caps how much any one of them can weigh (section 02).

The fund began on May 16, 2006 and is a series of VanEck ETF Trust; VanEck calls it the "Nation's First Gold Miners ETF" (fund page). Net assets were $26.21 billion on October 2, 2026, with 299,102,500 shares outstanding (VanEck fund page). The expense ratio is 0.51%: a 0.50% management fee plus 0.01% of other expenses, with no fee waiver. The adviser has agreed to cap operating expenses (with stated exclusions such as trading costs and taxes) at 0.53% of average daily net assets until at least May 1, 2027 (summary prospectus).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One commodity. Every holding depends on the price of gold or silver. The prospectus lists gold and silver mining companies risk first among its principal risks, describing the industries as "considered speculative." A fall in the metal price reaches every holding at once.
02
Amplified moves. Over five years GDX moved about 1.91 times as much as GLD, a gold-bullion fund, month to month, with a monthly volatility of 37.98% a year against 17.20% for GLD (our calculation, section 07). The fund fell 46.5% from April 18 to September 26, 2022 and 38.9% from February 27 to July 20, 2026 on daily total-return data.
03
Mining costs and operations. A miner's profit is the gap between the metal price and its costs, and costs move too: Newmont's gold all-in sustaining cost per ounce (co-product basis) rose from $1,593 in the second quarter of 2025 to $1,938 a year later, and on its by-product basis the cost rose 58% from the first quarter to $1,621, which Newmont attributed primarily to higher sustaining capital and other expense, mainly incremental costs at its Cadia mine during downtime after a seismic event in April (results release, July 23, 2026).
04
Countries and currencies. The holdings operate across 14 countries by the issuer's field, among them Burkina Faso, Côte d'Ivoire, Indonesia, Peru and Turkey. The prospectus names Canadian issuers, foreign securities, emerging-market issuers, political and economic risk, investment and repatriation restrictions, and foreign currency among its principal risks.
05
Concentration at the top. Newmont and Agnico Eagle are 22.15% and the top ten 58.84%. The fund is non-diversified, and the index allows a large member up to 20% at each rebalance.
06
Index owner in the same group, and a recent switch. The index belongs to MarketVector Indexes GmbH, a wholly owned VanEck subsidiary, which "reserves the right to update the rules in this Index Guide at any time" (Index Guide). The fund changed benchmark in September 2025, and the guide was revised three times in 2026.
07
Turnover and little income. Portfolio turnover was 50% in the most recent fiscal year (summary prospectus); the fact sheet lists high portfolio turnover among the fund's risks. The 30-day SEC yield is 0.52% and the distribution yield 0.72% (VanEck, October 2, 2026), paid once a year.

Why miners move more than gold

VanEck's own description is that gold miners "have historically shown higher volatility and greater beta to gold than the metal itself," and that they are "subject to stock market risk and operational factors" (fund page FAQ). The mechanism shows in a miner's own figures: the gap between the price a miner realizes and its cost per ounce moves by a larger percentage than the price itself. Year on year, a 33.0% higher gold price became a 43.4% wider margin per ounce even though costs rose 21.7%. It works the other way too: from the first to the second quarter of 2026 the realized price fell 9.9% and the margin fell 22.4%. The fund's record shows the same pattern, unevenly. Over the 60 months to September 2026, GDX moved in the same direction as GLD in 52; in the 35 months GLD rose, GDX gained 9.04% on average against GLD's 4.73%, and in the 25 months GLD fell, GDX lost 6.61% on average against 2.95% (our calculation). The calendar years above show that the multiple is not steady: in 2023 and 2024 gold rose and the miners lagged, and in 2022 gold was flat while the miners fell.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

A rising gold price, which widens miners' margins faster than it lifts the metal: GDX's beta to GLD was 1.91 over five years, and in 2025 its total return was 2.4 times GLD's. Rising silver prices: the index admits companies whose revenue comes from silver as well as gold. Steady or falling mining costs, which keep more of each dollar of the metal price as profit. Broad stock-market rallies matter less than for most stock funds: GDX's correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.28 and its beta 0.68 over five years.

What hurts it

A falling gold price, which narrows margins faster than it lowers the metal: from the first to the second quarter of 2026 Newmont's realized price fell 9.9% and its price-minus-cost margin 22.4%. Rising costs: Newmont's gold all-in sustaining cost rose 21.7% in the year to the second quarter of 2026. Years in which the miners lag the metal, as in 2023 and 2024. Operational setbacks, such as the seismic event Newmont reported at Cadia in April 2026. Political, tax and currency changes in the 14 countries where the companies are classified. The fund's own record: −46.5% in 2022 and −38.9% from February to July 2026.

Category Commentary
Owning the miners is not owning the metal
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Gold exposure comes in two kinds of fund. Bullion funds such as GLD hold metal in a vault, so their price follows gold less fees. Miner funds such as GDX hold company shares, so their price follows gold through each company's margin, costs, debts and management decisions, plus the mood of the stock market. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 GDX's total return (+219.2%) beat GLD's (+131.5%), but with more than twice the monthly volatility, and with long stretches in which it lagged the metal.

Same industry, different rulebooks
WHAT DIFFERS

Size: GDX holds the larger miners and GDXJ the smaller ones; they overlap 34.52% by weight (our calculation, October 2). Weighting: GDX's index weights by free-float market value within caps; SGDM's index selects and weights on revenue growth, free cash flow yield and debt, and overlaps GDX 60.35%. Assets: from $651.89 million (SGDM) to $26.21 billion (GDX). Cost: 0.39% (RING) to 0.52% (GDXJ). Over five years the four funds' total returns were close together, from +216.3% (SGDM) to +240.1% (RING); the gap to gold itself was far larger than the gaps between them.
