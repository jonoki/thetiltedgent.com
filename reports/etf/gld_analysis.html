01 Fund Overview

GLD is a share in a vault of gold bars. The SPDR Gold Trust owns allocated gold bullion held in London and New York, and each share is a fractional, undivided interest in that gold. Its objective, in the trust's own words: "for the Shares to reflect the performance of the price of gold bullion, less the Trust's expenses." There is no index to replicate and no portfolio manager making calls: the trustee "does not actively manage the gold held by the Trust" and uses no hedging (Form 10-Q for the quarter to June 30, 2026).

Launched on November 18, 2004, it is described by its sponsor as "the first US-traded gold ETF and the first US-listed ETF backed by a physical asset." On September 23, 2026 it held 1,056.84 tonnes (33,978,343.66 troy ounces) worth $145.54 billion in net assets, against 370,500,000 shares outstanding. Each share represented 0.09168599 oz of gold. The trust values that gold once a day at the LBMA Gold Price PM: 0.09168599 oz × $4,284.45 = $392.82, the published NAV per share (our check). The only recurring cost is a sponsor's fee of 0.40% a year, paid by selling a little gold each month.

Structure
Grantor trust, physical gold
The trust "does not hold any derivative instruments"; its assets are allocated gold bullion and, at times, gold receivable or cash (10-Q). Gold in the allocated account "is not traded, leased or loaned under any circumstances" (Form 10-K, fiscal 2025). GLD is not an investment company under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and is not regulated under the Commodity Exchange Act, so holders do not get those acts' protections (fact sheet, June 30, 2026).
How shares are made
Baskets of 100,000
Only Authorized Participants create or redeem shares, in baskets of 100,000 shares, against deliveries of gold. The 10-Q names eight: Goldman Sachs & Co., HSBC Securities (USA), J.P. Morgan Securities, Merrill Lynch Professional Clearing, Morgan Stanley & Co., RBC Capital Markets, UBS Securities and Virtu Americas. From April to June 2026, 185 baskets were created and 329 redeemed.
Distributions
None
The fact sheet states that "GLD does not generate any income". The trust sells gold to pay the sponsor's fee, so "the amount of gold represented by each Share will decline over time." The sponsor's page lists monthly gold sales of $0.13438 per share for September 2026. Currency hedging does not arise: the trust holds gold priced in U.S. dollars and its shares trade in U.S. dollars.
U.S. tax treatment
Collectibles rate, up to 28%
The trust is a "grantor trust" for U.S. federal income tax, so it pays no tax itself. For individual U.S. holders, long-term gains on the shares are taxed as gains on collectibles, "at a maximum rate of 28%, rather than the 20% rate applicable to most other long-term capital gains" (Form 10-K, fiscal year to September 30, 2025).

Trustee & custodians

Benchmark
LBMA Gold Price PM
Set by an electronic auction run by ICE Benchmark Administration (IBA). The auction runs twice a day, at 10:30 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. London time. The trustee values the gold at the PM price; if none is announced by 12:00 p.m. New York time, it uses the most recent LBMA Gold Price, AM or PM (10-Q). The trust has used this price since March 20, 2015; before that it used the London PM Fix (sponsor page).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

$145.5B net assets 100% allocated gold, no derivatives Gold not leased or loaned Daily bar lists from both custodians Two independent counts a year 7.5M shares traded Sep 23

The structure does one thing: it lets an investor hold gold bars through a brokerage account. Custody, insurance arrangements and storage costs sit inside the 0.40% fee. The bars are named on public lists, and an outside inspector counts them. Trading is deep. On September 23, 2026, 7,546,969 shares changed hands, about $2.97 billion at the close (our calculation). From September 30, 2021 to September 22, 2026 the market mid-price at 4:15 p.m. sat on average 0.02% away from the trust's indicative value, never more than +0.39% or −0.37% (our calculation from the sponsor's daily archive, 1,247 trading days).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One asset, no income. The share's value is the gold price times a slowly shrinking number of ounces. There are no dividends or coupons to offset a falling gold price, and the 10-K warns that "gold markets have historically experienced extended periods of flat or declining prices." Gold's peak-to-trough fall from January 29 to July 16, 2026 took GLD's close down 26.4% (section 07).
02
The highest fee in its peer group. At 0.40%, GLD costs four times GLDM (0.10%), from the same sponsor, and more than four times IAUM (0.09%). The fee is paid in gold, so it reduces ounces per share every year (section 04).
03
Custody concentrated in London. On the latest bar lists, 97.84% of the gold sits in London vaults and 92.90% with a single custodian, JPMorgan (our calculation). The trust "will not be a beneficiary" of the custodians' insurance, and holders "cannot be assured that a Custodian maintains adequate insurance or any insurance" (10-K).
04
Dependence on London market standards. In its June 2026 quarterly report the trust added a risk factor: if the LBMA's Good Delivery Rules change or stop being maintained, "creations or redemptions could be delayed, rejected or suspended, and the Shares could trade at a premium or discount to NAV that could be significant." The 10-K also flags that concerns about the integrity of the LBMA Gold Price PM could hurt investor interest in gold.
05
Timing gap between NAV and close. NAV is struck at the London afternoon auction (before noon in New York) while the shares close at 4 p.m. On days when gold moves in New York afternoon trading the two diverge. At the January 30, 2026 month-end the close was $444.95 against a NAV of $457.95, 2.8% below it (sponsor archive, our calculation).
06
Tax and holder rights. Long-term gains for U.S. individuals are taxed at the collectibles rate of up to 28%. Shareholders "have no voting rights, except in limited circumstances," and the trust is outside the 1940 Act and the Commodity Exchange Act (10-K; fact sheet).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

A rising gold price in U.S. dollars, since NAV is ounces per share times the LBMA Gold Price PM. The trust's 10-K lists what moves that price: global supply and demand (jewelry, industry, bar and coin buying, central bank purchases and sales, mine output), investors' inflation expectations, currency exchange rates, interest rates, hedge-fund and commodity-fund trading, and global or regional political, economic or financial events. Periods when stocks and gold part ways also favour it within a portfolio: GLD's five-year correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.15. Its deep trading ($2.97B on September 23) keeps the market price close to the value of the gold.

What hurts it

A falling gold price, which passes straight through: −26.4% on daily closes from January 29 to July 16, 2026, and −15.9% on month-end closes in 2022. There is no income to cushion a decline. The 0.40% fee removes about 0.4% of the ounces behind each share every year, the most in its peer group. Heavy redemptions do not change the value per share but show falling demand: tonnes dropped from 1,106.74 in April 2022 to 815.13 in March 2024. Disruption in the London market's standards or custody arrangements would affect creations, redemptions and the premium or discount to NAV.

U.S.-listed physical gold trusts
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These trusts turn gold bars in a vault into exchange-listed shares. Creations and redemptions in gold keep the share price close to the value of the metal. The five compared here held $256.06 billion of net assets on September 23, 2026 (our sum of the sponsors' figures). GLD alone is 56.8% of that. The products differ in four ways. Fee: from 0.09% (IAUM) to 0.40% (GLD). Share price: GLD's $392.88 against $42.68 for IAUM, $80.52 for IAU and $84.75 for GLDM. The lower-priced shares represent less gold each. Custody: HSBC and JPMorgan for GLD; JPMorgan for GLDM; ICBC Standard in London for SGOL, which seeks to hold only bars refined on or after January 1, 2012. Trading: on September 23, dollar volume was about $2.97B in GLD, $0.45B in IAU, $0.35B in GLDM and $0.07B in IAUM (shares × close, our calculation from sponsor pages).

Returns look alike because the gold is the same
WHAT DIFFERS

With one asset underneath, the long-run gap between these trusts is close to the gap in fees. Over five years to August 31, 2026, GLD's NAV returned 19.76% a year against 20.24% for the LBMA Gold Price PM, and GLDM's 20.11% (sponsor tables, same date). The 0.35-point gap between GLD and GLDM is close to their 0.30-point fee difference. Flows show who is buying. GLD's tonnes fell 26% from April 2022 to March 2024, then rose back above 1,000 tonnes by September 2025 (sponsor archive). Official buying is another driver the trust names: citing Gold Focus 2025, the 10-K reports central bank purchases averaging 784 tonnes a year from 2020 to 2024.
