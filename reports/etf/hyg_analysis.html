01 Fund Overview

HYG lends money to companies that rating agencies place below investment grade: bonds often called "high yield" or "junk". In the words of its summary prospectus, the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of U.S. dollar-denominated, high yield corporate bonds." Its index, the iBoxx USD Liquid High Yield Index, "is designed to provide a broad representation of the U.S. dollar-denominated liquid high yield corporate bond market" and is "a modified market-value weighted index with a cap on each issuer of 3%." The index had approximately 1,300 constituents on February 28, 2026 (prospectus).

The index rules, as listed in the prospectus of June 29, 2026, keep it to larger bonds from larger issuers. A bond qualifies only if its issuer is based in a developed market, its average rating from Fitch, Moody's and S&P is below investment grade, the issuer has at least $1 billion of bonds outstanding, the bond itself has at least $400 million outstanding, it was issued with less than 15 years to maturity, and it has at least one year left (a year and six months for new entries). On October 2, 2026 the fund held USD 17.86 billion in 1,334 holdings, with 232.3 million shares outstanding (issuer).

Default risk, in plain terms
A bond is a loan. The SEC's investor bulletin on high-yield bonds defines default risk as "the risk that a company will fail to make timely interest or principal payments and default on its bond," adding that "defaults also can occur if the company fails to meet certain terms of its debt agreement." The same bulletin explains that a high-yield bond "offers a higher rate of interest because of its higher risk of default." In this fund on October 2, 2026, 0.17% of the portfolio was rated D (in default), 0.32% CC and 7.32% CCC, the lowest-rated tiers it holds above default. The extra yield the fund's bonds paid over Treasuries of similar maturity (option-adjusted spread) was 2.76 percentage points (275.89 basis points, issuer). That extra yield is what the bonds pay for carrying default risk; losses from defaults reduce it.
Structure
Physical, sampled
The fund owns bonds directly. It uses "representative sampling" (prospectus), so it may not hold every index bond. It must keep at least 80% of its assets in index bonds and may lend up to one-third of its assets to other market participants (securities lending).
Currency
U.S. dollars only
Every bond in the October 2 holdings file is a U.S.-dollar bond, so there is no currency to hedge. Bonds of issuers based outside the United States are 11.47% of the fund (our sum of the file's location field; see section 02).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

USD 17.86B net assets 82.5M shares traded Oct 2 0.01% median bid/ask 1,334 holdings 3% issuer cap (index) Duration 3.28 years Monthly income

HYG is a large, heavily traded way to hold U.S. high-yield company bonds in one share. Its index screens for size (at least $400 million per bond, $1 billion per issuer), in line with the prospectus's description of the index as covering the "liquid high yield corporate bond market". Interest-rate sensitivity is modest: an effective duration of 3.28 years means a one-point rise in Treasury yields cuts the price by roughly 3.3%, before income (issuer, Oct 2). Tracking costs about the fee: over five years to June 30, 2026 the NAV returned 3.68% a year against 4.10% for the index, a gap of 0.42 points a year against the 0.49% expense ratio (issuer figures; the gap is our calculation).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Default and downgrade risk. 98.61% of the fund is rated below investment grade (our sum of BB through D), including 7.32% CCC, 0.32% CC and 0.17% already in default (Oct 2). The prospectus warns that high-yield bonds "are subject to a greater risk of default, illiquidity, price volatility and valuation uncertainty."
02
It moves with stocks. Over the five years to September 2026 the fund's monthly NAV returns had a correlation of 0.82 with the S&P 500 and a beta of 0.39 (our calculation, section 04). In the 23 months the S&P 500 fell, HYG fell in 18. Its worst month in the window, June 2022 (−7.15% NAV), came with a −8.25% month for the S&P 500 total return.
03
Cost against newer peers. The 0.49% expense ratio compares with 0.08% for iShares' own broader high-yield fund USHY and 0.05% for SPDR's SPHY (section 05). On $10,000 that is $49 a year against $5–8.
04
Liquidity of the bonds underneath. The ETF shares trade heavily, but the bonds trade far less, and 84.25% of the portfolio is in privately placed 144A bonds. The SEC bulletin notes that if many fund investors sell at once "the fund may need to sell assets to raise cash to pay for the redemptions," which "may force the fund to sell bonds at a loss."
05
Interest rates and calls. Duration is short but not zero: rising yields still cut prices, as in September 2026. Convexity is −0.07 (Oct 2); the fact-sheet glossary explains that negative convexity means duration shrinks when rates fall. Issuers can repay callable bonds early and the fund then reinvests at lower yields (prospectus "call risk").
06
Tax. The yield arrives as interest income. iShares' five-year figure to June 30, 2026 is 3.68% a year before tax and 1.37% after tax on distributions (highest federal rate).

Calculation note. Monthly returns rt = Vt/Vt−1 − 1 on the issuer's monthly "growth of hypothetical $10,000" NAV series (distributions reinvested, fees deducted), September 30, 2021 to September 30, 2026 (60 returns). The series reproduces the issuer's published 1-, 5- and 10-year returns to June 30, 2026 to within 0.01 point. Volatility = stdev(r) × √12 (sample standard deviation). Risk-free rate: U.S. Treasury daily par yield curve, 3-month rate on the last business day of the prior month, ÷ 12. Sharpe = mean(e) ÷ stdev(e) × √12, where et = rt − rf,t (sample standard deviation of the excess returns). Beta = cov(r, rm) ÷ var(rm) and correlation = cov(r, rm) ÷ (sd(r) · sd(rm)), where rm is the monthly return of Yahoo Finance's ^SP500TR on matched month-ends. All in USD.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

A steady or growing economy, in which few borrowers default and the fund keeps most of its yield (SEC yield 7.07%, average yield to maturity 7.99%). Narrowing credit spreads, which lift prices: the spread was 275.89 bps on October 2. Falling Treasury yields, worth roughly 3.3% in price for a one-point drop given the 3.28-year duration, though calls cap part of that gain. Rising stock markets, since the fund's correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.82 over five years.

What hurts it

Recessions, when defaults rise and investors sell riskier bonds for Treasuries, the "flight to quality" the SEC bulletin describes. Wider credit spreads and falling stocks at the same time: from December 2021 to September 2022 the total return fell 15.79% from peak to trough. Rising Treasury yields, as in September 2026, when the price fell 3.26%. Large redemptions that force bond sales in a thin market.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

High-yield bond ETFs hold the debt of companies rated below investment grade and pay out the interest monthly. The four funds here overlap heavily (63–80% by weight with HYG on October 2) and share a similar profile: durations of 3.1–3.3 years and SEC yields of 7.07–7.68%. What differs: index rules (HYG's index screens for bond and issuer size, and State Street describes JNK's index as covering bonds "with above-average liquidity"; USHY and SPHY follow ICE BofA indexes and hold more bonds, 1,904 and 1,933); cost (0.40–0.49% for the two older funds, launched in 2007, against 0.05–0.08% for the two newer ones); and trading (HYG traded 82.5 million shares on October 2 with a 0.01% median bid/ask spread).

WHAT MATTERS

Two things set returns: Treasury yields and credit spreads. Because durations are short, spreads and defaults matter more than for a Treasury or aggregate bond fund, and spreads tend to widen when the economy and stock market weaken.
