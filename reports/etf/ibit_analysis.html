01 Fund Overview

IBIT is a share in a pool of bitcoin. The iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF owns bitcoin held by a custodian, and each share is a fractional, undivided interest in that bitcoin. In the issuer's words, it "seeks to reflect the performance of the price of bitcoin." There is no portfolio manager making calls: the trust "does not engage in any activities designed to obtain a profit from, or ameliorate losses caused by, changes in the price of bitcoin" (Form 10-Q for the quarter to June 30, 2026). For bitcoin itself — how it was created, its supply schedule and who holds it — see our Bitcoin report.

The trust was seeded on January 5, 2024, its registration became effective on January 10, and the shares listed on Nasdaq on January 11, 2024 (10-Q). On October 2, 2026 it held 805,222.43 bitcoin and had $67.59 billion in net assets, against 1,418,840,000 shares outstanding (issuer holdings file and product page). Each day after 4:00 p.m. New York time the trust values its bitcoin at the CME CF Bitcoin Reference Rate – New York Variant (BRRNY). On October 2 that rate was $84,152.60, so the NAV of $47.64 corresponds to 0.00056608 BTC per share (NAV ÷ rate, our calculation); the issuer's basket figure gives the same: 22.64 BTC per 40,000 shares. The only recurring cost is a sponsor's fee of 0.25% a year, paid at least quarterly in U.S. dollars or in bitcoin.

Structure
Grantor trust, spot bitcoin
A Delaware statutory trust organised on June 8, 2023. Its assets are bitcoin and a little cash; on October 2 the holdings file showed bitcoin at 100.00% and $25,776.78 of cash. IBIT is not an investment company registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940, and the trust is not a commodity pool under the Commodity Exchange Act (issuer page).
How shares are made
Baskets of 40,000
Only Authorized Participants create or redeem shares, in baskets of 40,000, for cash or bitcoin. In-kind creations and redemptions were allowed from July 2025, after SEC orders of July 29, 2025. The 10-K for 2025 names 13 Authorized Participants, four of which (Jane Street, Virtu, JP Morgan Securities and Marex) could then deal in kind. From April to June 2026, 2,523 baskets were created and 4,684 redeemed (10-Q).
Distributions
None
The issuer lists the distribution frequency as "None." The trust sells bitcoin to pay its fee, so, in the fact sheet's words, "The amount of bitcoin represented by shares of the Trust will decrease over the life of the Trust due to sales of bitcoin necessary to pay the sponsor's fee and trust expenses." In the June 2026 quarter it sold 525 BTC to pay expenses (10-Q). Currency hedging does not arise: the benchmark and the shares are both in U.S. dollars.
U.S. tax treatment
Grantor trust: look-through
The sponsor treats the trust as a "grantor trust", so holders are taxed as if they owned their share of the bitcoin directly. The 10-K notes that IRS guidance treats bitcoin as "property" that is not currency. When the trust sells bitcoin to pay its fee, each holder recognises a small gain or loss on their share of the coins sold (Form 10-K, fiscal year 2025).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

$67.6B net assets 805,222 BTC, no derivatives Cold storage, segregated addresses In-kind creations since 2025 0.02% median bid/ask spread 48.7M shares traded Oct 2

The structure lets an investor hold bitcoin through an ordinary brokerage account, without managing keys or an exchange account. Custody, administration and listing costs sit inside the 0.25% fee. Trading is deep: on October 2, 2026, 48,699,106 shares changed hands, about $2.32 billion at the close (our calculation), and the 30-day median bid/ask spread was 0.02% (issuer). From January 11, 2024 to October 2, 2026 the closing price sat on average 0.25% away from NAV, between −1.77% (May 1, 2024) and +1.31% (December 24, 2024) (our calculation from the issuer's daily NAV and Yahoo Finance closes, 684 trading days).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One volatile asset, no income. The share's value is the bitcoin price times a slowly shrinking amount of bitcoin. Monthly NAV returns since launch had an annualised volatility of 53.0%, more than four times the S&P 500's 12.0% over the same months (section 04). From its record close on October 6, 2025 to June 30, 2026, IBIT fell 53.3%. The 10-Q warns the shares "could lose all or substantially all of their value."
02
One corporate group in the custody chain. The custodian, the prime execution agent and the trade credit lender are all subsidiaries of Coinbase Global. Coinbase's crime insurance "is shared among all of Coinbase's customers" and is not specific to the trust, and neither the trust nor the sponsor insures its bitcoin (10-K). The second custodian, Anchorage, holds none of it.
03
A once-a-day price for a 24-hour market. NAV is struck from one hour of trading on eight platforms. Bitcoin trades around the clock, including weekends, so moves outside U.S. market hours reach the share price only at the next open. The 10-K notes the index "has only featured its current list of Constituent Platforms since March 2025" and that prices elsewhere can differ materially.
04
Arbitrage depends on a few firms. When the 10-K was filed, four of 13 Authorized Participants could create or redeem in kind. The filing warns that the limited in-kind capacity could stop the arbitrage mechanism working as efficiently as it otherwise would, leaving the shares at a premium or discount to NAV.
05
Regulation still moving. The 10-Q for June 2026 records that the CLARITY Act, a federal market-structure framework for digital assets, passed the House in July 2025 and cleared the Senate Banking Committee in May 2026 but has not been enacted, and that its prospects "remain uncertain." Its risk factors also cover a future in which quantum computing could break the cryptography bitcoin relies on.
06
Holder rights and taxes. Under the trust agreement, "Shareholders generally have no voting rights" (10-K). The trust is outside the Investment Company Act of 1940. Each sale of bitcoin to pay the fee is a small taxable event for every holder.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

A rising bitcoin price in U.S. dollars, since NAV is bitcoin per share times the CF Benchmarks rate. Inflows add bitcoin to the trust but do not change the value per share. The 10-Q links bitcoin's price to political and regulatory developments, such as the GENIUS Act enacted in July 2025, the pending CLARITY Act and the March 2025 executive order creating a Strategic Bitcoin Reserve, and notes that market expectations around them may lift prices. Periods of strong risk appetite have also helped: since launch IBIT's beta to the S&P 500 was 2.06 (section 04). Deep trading ($2.32B on October 2) keeps the share price close to the value of the bitcoin.

What hurts it

A falling bitcoin price, which passes straight through: −53.3% on daily closes from October 6, 2025 to June 30, 2026, and three months of −17% or worse since November 2025. There is no income to cushion a decline. The 0.25% fee removes about a quarter of a percent of the bitcoin behind each share every year. Heavy redemptions do not change the value per share but show falling demand: the trust's bitcoin fell 6.3% in the June 2026 quarter. A failure or loss at the Coinbase entities that hold and trade the bitcoin, a disruption to the benchmark, or adverse regulation would affect creations, redemptions and the premium or discount to NAV.

Top 3 Risks

01
Bitcoin price risk, undiluted. One asset and no income. Volatility since launch was 53.0% a year (our calculation). The 2025–26 fall shows how fast a gain can reverse: from the October 2025 record the close fell 53.3% in under nine months.
02
Custody concentration. All the bitcoin sits with one custodian, Coinbase Custody, and passes through Coinbase Prime for cash trades. Insurance is shared with Coinbase's other customers, liability is capped per cold storage address, and the second custodian holds nothing.
03
Market structure and regulation. NAV depends on one benchmark built from eight platforms in a one-hour window; in-kind arbitrage relied on four Authorized Participants when the 10-K was filed; and federal market-structure legislation is unfinished. Any of these can widen the gap between share price and bitcoin value.

With one asset underneath, the long-run gap between these trusts is close to the gap in fees, plus small differences between their benchmarks.
