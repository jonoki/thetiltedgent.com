01 Fund Overview

IEF holds U.S. government debt from the middle of the maturity range. In the words of its prospectus, it "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of U.S. Treasury bonds with remaining maturities between seven and ten years." The issuer's own summary is that the fund "offers a way to gain targeted exposure to intermediate-term government debt." In practice that means mostly 10-year Treasury notes sold at auction over the last three years: 14 of its 16 holdings were first sold as 10-year notes between May 2023 and August 2026 (TreasuryDirect auction records).

The fund's price moves with intermediate-term U.S. interest rates. Its effective duration was 6.86 years on October 2, 2026 (issuer), so a one-point rise in 7- to 10-year yields cuts the price by roughly 7%, and a one-point fall adds roughly the same, before income. Net assets were USD 41.45 billion that day (the issuer's NAV of $89.018796 × 465.6 million shares outstanding, our calculation), and the expense ratio is 0.15% (summary prospectus, June 29, 2026).

Why this fund — the 7-to-10-year slice on its own
Basis: the intermediate-Treasury ETFs compared in section 05, with each issuer's own figures. IEF is the only one of the four limited to bonds with 7 to 10 years left; SPTI (State Street SPDR Portfolio Intermediate Term Treasury ETF) and VGIT (Vanguard Intermediate-Term Treasury ETF) take 3 to 10 years, and IEI (iShares 3-7 Year Treasury Bond ETF) takes 3 to 7. That gives IEF the longest duration of the group: 6.86 years against 4.25 to 4.9 years for the others. It is also heavily traded: 16.9 million shares changed hands on October 2, about USD 1.51 billion at the close (issuer volume × close, our calculation), and its 30-day median bid/ask spread was 0.01% (issuer).
Structure
Physical, sampled
The fund owns the bonds directly and uses "representative sampling" (prospectus). It must keep at least 80% of assets in index bonds and at least 90% in U.S. Treasuries, and may use up to 10% in futures, options and swaps. The October 2 holdings file shows no derivatives; a 0.36% money-market position is offset by −0.36% cash. The fund may lend up to one-third of its assets (prospectus).
Currency
U.S. dollars only
Every holding is a U.S. Treasury security denominated in dollars (holdings file, Oct 2, 2026), so there is no currency to hedge for a U.S. investor.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

USD 41.45B net assets 100% U.S. Treasuries 0.01% median bid/ask 16.9M shares traded Oct 2 Monthly income SEC yield 4.98%

The fund is a single-purpose tool: it gives exposure to intermediate-term U.S. government interest rates with no company credit risk. Its NAV return has trailed the index by 0.12 to 0.13 points a year over one, three, five and ten years, close to its 0.15% fee: five-year NAV return −1.14% a year against −1.02% for the index, and ten-year 0.51% against 0.63% (issuer, to June 30, 2026). On October 2 the market price ($89.05) sat 0.04% above NAV ($89.02) (issuer). Shares outstanding rose from 119.1 million on September 30, 2021 to 465.6 million on October 2, 2026 (issuer's daily history), close to four times as many.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest rates. Effective duration is 6.86 years (Oct 2, 2026): a one-point rise in 7- to 10-year yields cuts the price by roughly 7%. In September 2026 the 10-year Treasury par yield rose from 4.75% (Aug 31) to 5.29% (Sep 30) (U.S. Treasury), and the fund's NAV total return for the month was −3.32% (issuer).
02
Inflation. The index excludes inflation-linked securities, so every coupon is fixed in dollars for up to ten years. Higher inflation lowers the real value of that income and tends to push yields up, which cuts the price.
03
A narrow maturity band. All 16 holdings mature between May 2033 and August 2036, so the fund rides one segment of the yield curve. When 7- to 10-year yields rise faster than short or long yields, there is nothing elsewhere in the portfolio to offset it.
04
One issuer. All holdings are obligations of the U.S. Treasury, and the prospectus warns that changes in the government's financial condition or credit rating may lower their value. The Treasury also keeps adding to the supply: it sells a new 10-year note each quarter and reopens it in the other months, with a USD 39 billion reopening of the fund's newest note set for October 7, 2026 (TreasuryDirect).
05
Cost relative to peers. The 0.15% expense ratio is five times the 0.03% charged by SPTI and VGIT, which hold a wider 3-to-10-year set of Treasuries (section 05).
06
Diversification against stocks is not guaranteed. Over the 60 months to September 2026 the correlation of the fund's NAV returns with the S&P 500 was +0.54, and the fund rose in only 6 of the 23 months in which the S&P 500 total return fell (our calculation). In 2022 both fell: the fund −15.23% (issuer) and the S&P 500 total return −18.1% (our calculation from Yahoo ^SP500TR).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling 7- to 10-year yields: with a 6.86-year duration, a one-point drop adds roughly 7% to the price, on top of income, as in November 2023 (+4.47% on NAV in one month). Stable yields: the fund then earns roughly its yield (SEC yield 4.98%, yield to maturity 5.24%). Slowing inflation, recessions and sell-offs in which investors move into Treasuries. Stronger confidence in U.S. government finances.

What hurts it

Rising intermediate yields, as in 2022 (−15.23% NAV) and September 2026 (−3.32% NAV as the 10-year yield rose 54 basis points). Inflation above expectations, since no holding is inflation-protected. Rising yields paired with falling stocks, which removes the diversification benefit (five-year correlation with the S&P 500: +0.54). Heavier Treasury borrowing or doubts about U.S. finances that lift the yield investors demand.

Category Commentary
Intermediate U.S. Treasury ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These funds hold the middle of the Treasury market and give exposure to U.S. interest rates of roughly 3 to 10 years with no company credit risk. They differ in three ways. Maturity band and duration: IEF takes only 7 to 10 years (duration 6.86 years); SPTI and VGIT take 3 to 10 years (4.82 and 4.9 years); IEI takes 3 to 7 years (4.25 years). Cost: 0.15% for the two iShares funds against 0.03% for SPTI and VGIT (issuer prospectus figures). Concentration: IEF's narrow band means 16 holdings, against 82 to 110 for the others. Yields were close on the banner date: SEC yields of 4.79% to 4.98% (each issuer, October 2, 2026), the highest for IEF, the fund with the longest maturities.

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

With all four funds holding the same issuer, returns are set almost entirely by the level and change of intermediate Treasury yields. The last five years show how much the starting yield matters: IEF began the window with the 10-year yield at 1.52% and returned −2.06% a year on NAV to September 30, 2026 (our compounding of issuer data). Today it starts from a 30-day SEC yield of 4.98% and a 10-year par yield of 5.28%, 0.01 point below the September 30 reading of 5.29%, the highest since May 2002 (U.S. Treasury). At a 6.86-year duration, about 1.4 years of income at today's SEC yield offsets the price effect of a one-point rise in yields.
