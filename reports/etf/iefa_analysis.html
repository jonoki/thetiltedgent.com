01 Fund Overview

IEFA is a single share that owns stocks across the developed world outside North America: Europe, Japan, Australia, Hong Kong, Singapore, Israel and New Zealand. Its summary prospectus (November 28, 2025) states the objective: the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of large-, mid- and small-capitalization developed market equities, excluding the U.S. and Canada." That index is the MSCI EAFE IMI Index, which the prospectus describes as "a free float-adjusted, market capitalization-weighted index designed to measure large-, mid- and small-capitalization equity market performance and includes stocks from Europe, Australasia and the Far East." EAFE stands for Europe, Australasia and the Far East; IMI, "investable market index," means small companies are included as well as large and mid-sized ones.

The fund launched on October 18, 2012 and had $191.0 billion of net assets on September 24, 2026 ($190,995,729,886, issuer). The expense ratio is 0.07%, all of it management fee; under the advisory agreement BlackRock Fund Advisors pays the fund's other operating expenses, with listed exceptions such as interest, taxes and brokerage (prospectus). It does not buy every stock in the index. The prospectus describes "a representative sampling indexing strategy": a set of securities chosen so that, in aggregate, the fund has an investment profile similar to the index. It generally invests at least 80% of assets in index stocks or their depositary receipts.

Withholding tax and the index

Dividends from foreign companies are often taxed at source before they reach the fund. The benchmark is a "net" index, and the issuer explains that net indices assume reinvestment "after the deduction of the maximum withholding tax in each country applicable to non-residents," at rates "often at a higher rate than the rates to which the Fund is subject." That is one reason the fund's NAV return can sit above its index: 8.81% a year over five years to June 30, 2026, against 8.50% for the index (issuer).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.07% $191.0B net assets 2,627 holdings, 22 locations Includes small caps (IMI) 5-yr NAV 8.81%/yr vs index 8.50% Median bid/ask spread 0.01% Portfolio turnover 3%

The fund gives broad exposure to developed markets outside North America at a low fee, and its results have kept pace with the index. Over the five years to June 30, 2026 the NAV returned 8.81% a year against the index's 8.50%; over ten years, 9.78% against 9.50% (issuer). The gap in the fund's favour reflects, among other things, the index's use of maximum withholding-tax rates (section 01). Trading is liquid: the 30-day median bid/ask spread was 0.01% and average daily volume about 8.0 million shares (issuer, September 23). Turnover in the most recent fiscal year was 3% of the average portfolio value (prospectus).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Currency. The index is not currency-hedged, and the holdings file shows a single small currency-forward line (JPY/USD, −$0.41 million). Its U.S.-dollar value moves with the yen, euro, pound, Swiss franc and Australian dollar as well as with share prices. A stronger U.S. dollar lowers returns for a dollar-based investor even if local share prices are unchanged.
02
Two countries, two sectors. Japan (26.06%) and the United Kingdom (14.05%) are 40.11% of the fund; Financials and Industrials are 43.98% (our sums, issuer data). The prospectus names Japan-specific risks, including "significant public debt and deficits" and an aging population, and lists Asian and European economic risk.
03
What it leaves out. By design there are no U.S., Canadian or emerging-market companies, and no South Korean ones. Funds on the FTSE or S&P developed-market indices (VEA, SPDW) hold Canada and South Korea; over the five years in section 07 both returned more than IEFA.
04
Time-zone pricing. Most of its markets are closed at the 4 p.m. New York close, so the fund's NAV relies on "systematic fair value" (issuer). On September 24, 2026 the $97.48 close was 0.24% above the $97.25 NAV (our calculation); the NAV without fair valuation was $97.00. In 2025 the fund closed at a premium to NAV on 224 days, at NAV on 1 day and at a discount on 25 days (issuer).
05
Small companies and sampling. The 2,127 positions ranked below 500 make up 17.92% of the fund. The issuer warns that smaller companies "may be less stable" and their shares "more volatile and less liquid." Because the fund samples rather than holds every index stock, the prospectus lists the risk that its results diverge from the index.
06
Securities lending. The fund may lend up to one-third of its total assets; the prospectus warns it "may lose money because the borrower of the loaned securities fails to return the securities in a timely manner or at all," or through a fall in the value of collateral.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising share prices in Europe and Japan, which make up about 87% of the fund between them (our sums). A weaker U.S. dollar, which lifts the dollar value of yen, euro and pound holdings. Periods in which banks, insurers and industrial companies lead, since Financials and Industrials are 43.98% of the fund. Steady dividends: the issuer's 12-month trailing yield is 3.25%, and the holdings trade at 18.81 times earnings (issuer, September 23). A low 0.07% fee keeps the fund close to the index.

What hurts it

A stronger U.S. dollar, since the fund is unhedged. Bear markets outside the U.S.: 2022 cost −15.13% over the calendar year and −30.0% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −23.52% (March 31, 2020). A period in which U.S. stocks outpace the rest of the developed world, as over the last five years, when the S&P 500's total return was 37.4 points higher. Weak Japanese or British markets, given their 40.11% combined weight. Stress in European or Japanese banks.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

The funds in section 05 sit in the same category but draw the line in different places. Canada: MSCI EAFE indices (IEFA, EFA) leave it out; IDEV, VEA and SPDW include it. South Korea: IEFA, EFA and IDEV hold no South Korean companies; VEA and SPDW do (holdings files). Company size: EFA holds large and mid caps (657 holdings); IEFA and IDEV add small caps (the "IMI"); VEA's index is "All Cap" and SPDW's S&P BMI covers "the investable universe of publicly traded companies" in its countries. Cost: 0.03% (VEA, SPDW) to 0.32% (EFA). Distributions: semi-annual for the iShares funds and SPDW, quarterly for VEA.

Why overlaps sit at 77–87%
WHAT DIFFERS

The large European and Japanese companies are common to all five funds, so overlap is high but short of the near-identical 90% line. The gaps come from the country boundaries and the size cut-offs above: EFA's 657 stocks are almost all inside IEFA, and the 14.4% difference is IEFA's small-company tail and the reweighting it causes; VEA and SPDW each hold about 10.5–11% in Canadian companies and about 9% in South Korean ones (SPDW's including a Samsung Electronics GDR), none of which IEFA owns. Over the five years in section 07 those country choices showed up in returns: the funds with Canada and South Korea returned 8.7 to 9.5 points more than IEFA.
