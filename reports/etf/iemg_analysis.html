01 Fund Overview

IEMG is one share that holds thousands of companies listed in emerging markets, from chipmakers in Taiwan and South Korea to banks in China and India. In the words of its December 30, 2025 summary prospectus, the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of large-, mid- and small-capitalization emerging market equities." That index is the MSCI Emerging Markets Investable Market Index (IMI), which the prospectus lists as covering 24 markets: Brazil, Chile, China, Colombia, Czech Republic, Egypt, Greece, Hungary, India, Indonesia, Kuwait, Malaysia, Mexico, Peru, the Philippines, Poland, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, South Korea, Taiwan, Thailand, Turkey and the United Arab Emirates.

Launched October 18, 2012, the fund had $162.26 billion of net assets and 1,977,600,000 shares outstanding on September 24, 2026, up from 1,936,200,000 shares on June 30, 2026 (fact sheet), an increase of 41.4 million shares or 2.1% (our calculation). The expense ratio is 0.09%, all of it management fee. Under the advisory agreement the adviser pays the fund's other operating expenses, with listed exceptions such as brokerage, taxes and extraordinary expenses. The adviser has also "contractually agreed to waive a portion of its management fee such that the Fund's total annual fund operating expenses after the fee waiver will not exceed 0.09% through December 31, 2030" (summary prospectus).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.09% Fee cap to Dec 31, 2030 2,860 holdings, small caps included $162.3B net assets 30-day median bid/ask spread 0.01% No capital-gains payouts since Dec 2020

The fund gives access to 24 index markets through one U.S.-listed share at 0.09% a year. It has stayed close to its index: over the five years to June 30, 2026 its NAV returned 7.46% a year against the index's 7.17%, and over ten years 10.13% against 9.97% (issuer). The issuer notes that "the ETF total return may appear to diverge from the return of its benchmark," which "may be due to the use of systematic fair value"; the prospectus adds that the net index deducts withholding taxes at rates "often at a higher rate than the rates to which the Fund is subject." Trading is liquid: 17.2 million shares changed hands on September 24 and the 30-day median bid/ask spread was 0.01% (issuer). Portfolio turnover was 6% in the latest fiscal year (prospectus).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration in three markets and one industry. Taiwan, South Korea and China are 67.65% of the fund (issuer, September 23). Information technology is 41.75%, and TSMC, Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and MediaTek together are 28.30% (our sum). A downturn in the semiconductor cycle reaches a large part of the portfolio at once. The prospectus notes the fund "may become 'non-diversified'" solely because of changes in index weights.
02
Currency. 98.5% of the fund's market value is priced in currencies other than the U.S. dollar (our sum of the holdings file). A stronger dollar lowers the fund's dollar value even if local share prices are flat, and the prospectus flags "delays or limits on the repatriation of foreign currency."
03
China policy and listing risk. China is 18.33% of the fund. The prospectus warns that "Chinese companies are subject to the risk that Chinese authorities can intervene in their operations and structure," and describes the VIE structures and possible U.S. delisting of some Chinese companies.
04
Market access can be cut off. Russia shows how: "As a result of sanctions, the Fund is currently restricted from trading in Russian securities, including those in its portfolio, and the Underlying Index has removed Russian securities" (prospectus). The September 23 holdings file still carries Russia-located lines, at 0.00%.
05
Price and NAV can part. The prospectus warns that holdings may trade "in foreign markets that may be closed when the securities exchange on which the Fund's shares trade is open," which can lead to premiums or discounts to NAV. In 2025 the fund traded at a premium on 174 days and a discount on 71; the widest discount was −1.55% (April 7, 2025) and the widest premium +1.29% (April 9, 2025) (issuer).
06
Sampling, not full replication. The fund held 2,860 securities against 3,016 index constituents, so its return can differ from the index's. The prospectus: "There is no guarantee that the Fund's investment results will have a high degree of correlation to those of the Underlying Index."

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising emerging-market stocks, since the fund is almost entirely equities. A strong semiconductor and electronics cycle: TSMC, Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and MediaTek are 28.30% of the fund, and Taiwan and South Korea together are 49.32%. A weaker U.S. dollar, which raises the dollar value of the 98.5% held in other currencies. Better conditions for Chinese internet and bank stocks (China 18.33%) and for India (12.02%). Low costs keep the fund close to its index.

What hurts it

Broad emerging-market sell-offs: −32.7% at the daily trough in 2021–22 and −19.87% in calendar 2022; the worst quarter in the prospectus's 2015–2024 chart is −24.40% (March 31, 2020). A downturn in chips, given the weight in a few semiconductor makers. A stronger U.S. dollar. Policy shocks in China, sanctions or capital controls that restrict trading, as happened with Russia. Periods when U.S. stocks lead: from September 2021 to December 2024 the fund's total return was −5.8% against +43.6% for the S&P 500 (our calculation).

Top 3 Risks

01
Emerging-market equity risk in full. Five-year volatility of 17.42% a year (our calculation) and a −32.7% daily drawdown inside this window. The prospectus lists "greater market volatility; political, legal, economic, and social instability" among the risks of these markets.
02
Concentration inside the diversification. Ten lines are 34.58% of the fund, TSMC alone 13.58%; three markets are 67.65% and information technology 41.75%. The fund's return depends heavily on Asian chipmakers and Chinese internet companies.
03
Currency and market access. Nearly all holdings are priced in other currencies, and governments can limit trading or repatriation. The Russian holdings the fund can no longer trade are the example the prospectus gives.
