01 Fund Overview

IJH is a way to own the 400 companies of the S&P MidCap 400, the middle tier of the U.S. stock market, through a single share. Its July 31, 2026 summary prospectus states that the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of mid-capitalization U.S. equities." The index "measures the performance of the mid-capitalization sector of the U.S. equity market"; at March 31, 2026 its companies had market values between $1.6 billion and $27 billion at the time they joined (prospectus). The S&P 500 sits above it and the S&P SmallCap 600 below it: on September 21, 2026 two of its members, Everpure and Illumina, left to join the S&P 500.

Launched May 22, 2000, IJH is the largest of the four S&P MidCap 400 ETFs compared on this page, with $121.58 billion of net assets on September 24, 2026, 4.7 times MDY's $25.63 billion (our calculation, issuer figures of the same date). The expense ratio is 0.05%, all of it management fee (other expenses 0.00%).

Structure
Physical, representative sampling
The prospectus says "BFA uses a representative sampling indexing strategy to manage the Fund" and that it will not invest less than 80% of net assets in index components. In practice the September 23, 2026 holdings file lists all 400 index stocks, plus a small S&P MidCap 400 futures position (notional 0.20%), six small equity swaps and 0.23% in cash and a BlackRock cash fund.
Currency
USD, no hedging needed
399 of the 400 stocks carry the location "United States" in the issuer's file (99.35% of the fund); RB Global (0.44%) is located in Canada. All trade in U.S. dollars. For an investor who spends another currency, the dollar's moves pass straight through.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

The fund has stayed close to its index. Over the five years to June 30, 2026 its NAV returned 9.03% a year against the index's 9.06%; over ten years 11.60% against 11.64% (issuer). Portfolio turnover was 16% in the latest fiscal year (prospectus). On September 24 the closing price and NAV were both $72.73, and the issuer's 30-day median bid/ask spread was 0.01% (September 23).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Mid-sized companies swing more. "Mid-Capitalization Companies Risk" is a principal risk in the prospectus. Over the five years to August 2026 the fund's monthly returns had an annualised volatility of 18.20%, against 15.84% for the S&P 500 (our calculation).
02
Winners graduate. Companies that grow past the mid-cap range move to the S&P 500: Everpure and Illumina left on September 21, 2026. The fund sells them as they leave, so it does not keep its most successful members as they become large caps.
03
Sector tilt to industrials and financials. Industrials 23.90% and financials 14.99%; the prospectus lists consumer goods and services and industrial companies among its principal risks.
04
One country. 99.35% U.S. companies. A period in which U.S. stocks lag the rest of the world hits the fund in full.
05
Securities lending and derivatives. The prospectus says the fund "may lend securities representing up to one-third of the value of the Fund's total assets (including the value of any collateral received)" and lists securities lending risk. The holdings file shows small futures and swap positions alongside the stocks.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares trade at the market price, which can differ from NAV; the prospectus lists market trading and authorized-participant concentration risk. On the banner date the gap was 0.00%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. stocks broadly, since the fund is the S&P MidCap 400 less 0.05% a year. Periods when mid-sized companies do better than the largest ones, the reverse of the five years shown here. Strength in industrials, financials and technology, which together are 54.5% of the fund. A market that rewards breadth rather than a handful of mega-caps: no single holding is above 1.3%. Low costs and low turnover mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Broad U.S. bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion. Inside this window it fell 23.9% in 2022 and 24.1% from late 2024 to April 2025 (daily total return); the worst quarter in the prospectus is −29.70% (March 31, 2020). Markets led by the largest U.S. companies, which the fund does not hold. Economic slowdowns that hit industrial and regional-bank earnings. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency.

01
Full U.S. equity market risk, with more swing than large caps. Five-year volatility is 18.20% a year against 15.84% for the S&P 500, and two falls of about 24% occurred inside the window (our calculations).

Size and style can lag for years. Over the five years to September 24, 2026 the fund returned 48.5% against 92.1% for the S&P 500, and its Sharpe ratio was 0.31 against 0.61. Its index moves successful companies up to the S&P 500, as it did with Everpure and Illumina on September 21, 2026.
03
Sector concentration. Industrials are 23.90% of the fund, and the prospectus names industrials and consumer goods and services among its principal risks. An industrial downturn weighs more here than in the broad market.

"Mid-cap" is defined by the index
WHAT DIFFERS

Index providers draw the mid-cap band in different places. The S&P MidCap 400 takes companies of $1.6 billion to $27 billion at inclusion (prospectus, March 31, 2026), with a median holding of $11.6 billion. Vanguard's VO follows the Morningstar US Mid Cap Index, whose holdings have a median market cap of $51.2 billion (Vanguard, August 31, 2026); IJH and VO share one stock. The Russell Midcap Index behind IWR holds 822 companies, including 319 of IJH's 400. Funds sold under the same "mid-cap" label can therefore hold very different companies, and returns differ with them: VO's total return over the same five-year window was +44.99%.
