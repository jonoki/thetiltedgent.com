01 Fund Overview

IJH is a way to own the 400 companies in the middle of the U.S. stock market by size through a single share. Its July 31, 2026 summary prospectus states the objective: the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of mid-capitalization U.S. equities." That index is the S&P MidCap 400, which "measures the performance of the mid-capitalization sector of the U.S. equity market, as determined by S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC." Its companies are weighted by the market value of the shares available to the public (float-adjusted), had a market value "between $1.6 billion and $27 billion at the time of inclusion" as of March 31, 2026, and together made up about 5% of the value of all publicly traded U.S. stocks (prospectus).

Launched May 22, 2000, IJH is one of the series of iShares Trust, managed by BlackRock Fund Advisors. On October 2, 2026 it had 1,679,050,000 shares outstanding and its holdings file adds up to $123.20 billion (our sum of stocks, cash and money-market lines). The expense ratio is 0.05%, all of it management fee: under the advisory agreement the adviser pays the fund's other operating costs, apart from items such as interest, taxes, brokerage and extraordinary expenses (prospectus). The issuer describes the fund as "a low-cost strategy as a core building block within a portfolio."

Structure
Physical, representative sampling
The prospectus says the adviser "uses a representative sampling indexing strategy" and that the fund "may or may not hold all of the components" of the index. On October 2, 2026 it held 400 stocks directly, plus equity swaps adding $59.0 million of exposure to eight of them, and 550 December 2026 S&P MidCap 400 E-mini futures ($202.9 million of exposure); the issuer says index futures "may be used to offset cash and receivables." It may lend securities worth up to one-third of its total assets.
Currency
USD, no hedging needed
Every holding is priced in U.S. dollars. By the issuer's location field, U.S. companies are 99.34% of the fund (our sum); the one exception is RB Global, which the file lists under Canada (0.44%). For an investor who spends another currency, the U.S. dollar's moves pass straight through.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

400 stocks Top-10 share 7.97% ~$123.2B net assets 0.05% expense ratio 30-day median bid/ask 0.01% 5-yr gap to index −0.03 pts/yr Trading since May 2000

The fund spreads its money across the whole index, with no single name above 1.31%, so the failure of any one company barely registers. It is cheap to hold at 0.05% a year and heavily traded: the issuer reports an average of 7,484,271 shares a day over 30 days and a 30-day median bid/ask spread of 0.01% (October 2, 2026). Its results have stayed close to the index. Over the five years to June 30, 2026 the NAV returned 9.03% a year against the index's 9.06%, a gap of 0.03 percentage points; over ten years the gap was 0.04 points (11.60% vs 11.64%). In the third quarter of 2026 the closing price was above NAV on 43 trading days, equal to it on 4 and below it on 17 (issuer).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Mid-size companies swing more than large ones. The prospectus: "Compared to large-capitalization companies, mid-capitalization companies may be less stable and more susceptible to adverse developments." Over the last five years IJH's monthly returns were 18.27% volatile a year, against 15.64% for the S&P 500 total-return index (our calculation). The issuer's 3-year figures are closer: standard deviation 15.73% and beta 1.00 against the S&P 500 (August 31, 2026).
02
Sector tilt. Industrials (23.86%), information technology (16.38%) and financials (14.70%) are more than half the fund. The prospectus lists industrial companies and consumer goods and services companies among its principal risks, and names both as a significant portion of the index at March 31, 2026.
03
Long stretches behind large companies. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 the fund returned 8.41% a year with dividends, against 13.99% for the S&P 500 (our calculation, section 07). A broad mid-cap fund can trail the large-company market for years.
04
The index provider sets the membership. S&P Dow Jones Indices decides which companies enter and leave, using size and liquidity among its factors, and the prospectus warns that the components "are likely to change over time." Index changes force the fund to buy and sell; turnover was 16% in the fiscal year to March 31, 2026.
05
Sampling, derivatives and lending. The fund samples rather than promising to hold every index stock, uses futures and swaps for part of its exposure, and may lend up to one-third of its assets. The prospectus lists tracking error and securities lending among its principal risks.

Sharpe = mean(e) ÷ stdev(e) × √12, where et = rt − rf,t (sample standard deviation of the excess returns, not of raw returns). Beta = cov(r, rm) ÷ var(rm), where rm is the month-end return of Yahoo Finance's ^SP500TR (S&P 500 total return, USD); correlation = cov(r, rm) ÷ (stdev(r) × stdev(rm)). The S&P MidCap 400 correlation uses Yahoo's ^SP400 price index, which leaves out dividends. The issuer's standard deviation and beta use 3 years, ours 5.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising prices for middle-sized U.S. companies, since the fund is the S&P MidCap 400 less 0.05% a year. Strength in industrials, technology and financials, which are more than half the fund. Periods like the year to June 30, 2026, when the NAV returned 25.83%. A broad spread of companies means no single failure moves the fund much: the largest holding is 1.31%.

What hurts it

Broad U.S. stock declines: its beta to the S&P 500 was 1.02 over five years (our calculation), and daily falls of 23.9% (2021–2022) and 24.1% (2024–2025) happened inside this window. Long stretches in which the largest companies lead: over these five years the S&P 500's total return was 92.58% against IJH's 49.80%. Weakness in industrial companies, given their 23.86% weight.
