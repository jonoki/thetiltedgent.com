01 Fund Overview

IJR is a way to own about six hundred smaller U.S. companies through a single share. Its July 31, 2026 summary prospectus states the objective: the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of small-capitalization U.S. equities." That index is the S&P SmallCap 600, which the prospectus says "measures the performance of the small-capitalization sector of the U.S. equity market." Companies are weighted by the market value of the shares available to the public (float-adjusted), so bigger companies carry more weight. At March 31, 2026 the index made up about 2% of the market value of all publicly traded U.S. stocks, and its companies had market values between $260 million and $8 billion at the time they were added to the index (prospectus).

What sets this index apart from the other common small-company benchmark, the Russell 2000, is how members are picked. A committee at S&P Dow Jones Indices chooses the 600 companies, and a company can only be added if its GAAP earnings (profit under standard U.S. accounting rules) over the latest four quarters add up to a positive number and the latest quarter is positive too (S&P U.S. Indices Methodology). Section 08's commentary looks at what that rule changes.

Launched May 22, 2000, IJR is one of the series of iShares Trust, managed by BlackRock Fund Advisors. On October 2, 2026 it had $104.04 billion in net assets ($104,038,845,572) and 750,950,000 shares outstanding (issuer). The expense ratio is 0.06%, all of it management fee, with other expenses of 0.00% (prospectus fee table).

Physical, representative sampling
The prospectus: "BFA uses a representative sampling indexing strategy to manage the Fund," and the fund will not invest less than 80% of its net assets in the index's securities. The October 2, 2026 holdings file lists 602 stock lines, 36 equity-swap lines (contracts that pay the return of a stock; $1.37 billion of exposure, 1.26% of the fund by the issuer's notional weights) and two OmniAb warrant lines. It also shows 2,917 December 2026 Russell 2000 E-mini futures contracts, $415.8 million of exposure. The fund may lend securities worth up to one-third of its total assets.

Entry rules
Size, float, trading, profit
To be added, a company must fall within the index's market-value band, have at least 10% of its shares available to the public, trade at least 250,000 shares in each of the six months before the review, and pass the GAAP earnings test above. The trading and earnings tests apply at entry: the methodology says current members have no minimum trading requirement (S&P U.S. Indices Methodology, March 2025).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

0.06% expense ratio $104.04B net assets 603 companies Top-10 share 5.30% 5-yr gap to index −0.06 pts/yr Earnings test at entry

The fund costs 0.06% a year and is large and heavily traded: the issuer reports an average of 3,446,663 shares a day over 30 days and a median bid/ask spread of 0.01% (October 2, 2026). Its results have stayed close to the index. Over the five years to June 30, 2026 the NAV returned 7.30% a year against the index's 7.36%, a gap of 0.06 percentage points, the size of the expense ratio. Over ten years the gap was 0.04 points (11.47% vs 11.51%). On October 2 the market close ($138.52) sat 0.02% below NAV ($138.54), the issuer's premium/discount figure. The index's earnings test keeps companies without recent GAAP profits from being added, which is the main way it differs from the Russell 2000.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Small companies swing more. The prospectus: "Compared to mid- and large-capitalization companies, small-capitalization companies may be less stable and more susceptible to adverse developments." Over the last five years IJR's monthly returns were 20.35% volatile a year, against 15.64% for the S&P 500 total-return index (our calculation). The issuer's 3-year beta against the S&P 500 is 1.06 (August 31, 2026).
02
The earnings test is checked at entry only. The methodology states that "the eligibility criteria are for addition to an index, not for continued membership", so a member that slips into losses is not removed for that reason alone; companies moving down from the S&P MidCap 400 or S&P 500 also skip the earnings test. The test says nothing about price: a profitable company can still fall sharply.
03
Committee judgement. Membership is decided by S&P's Index Committee, which also considers sector balance, rather than by a fixed size ranking. That makes the index's make-up less mechanical and harder to predict than a pure size cut-off.
04
Sector tilt. Financials (18.17%) and industrials (17.68%) are more than a third of the fund, and the prospectus lists consumer goods and services, financial and industrial companies as principal risks because of their weight in the index.
05
Winners graduate. Companies that grow beyond the index's size band tend to move to the S&P MidCap 400 or S&P 500, so the fund does not keep its biggest successes. Portfolio turnover was 25% of the average value of the portfolio in the most recent fiscal year (prospectus).
06
Sampling, swaps and lending. The fund samples rather than fully replicates, holds some exposure through swaps and futures, and may lend up to one-third of its assets. The prospectus lists tracking error and securities lending among its principal risks.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising prices for smaller U.S. companies, since the fund is the S&P SmallCap 600 less 0.06% a year. Periods in which small companies do better than large ones: IJR's 1-year NAV return to June 30, 2026 was 37.42%. Markets that punish unprofitable companies, when the index's earnings test at entry matters most; in 2022 IJR lost 16.20% against 20.48% for IWM. Strength in financials, industrials and technology, its three largest sectors. A broad spread of companies means no single failure moves the fund much: the largest holding is 0.66%.

What hurts it

Broad U.S. stock declines: the deepest daily fall in the window was −28.0% (November 2024 to April 2025) and its beta to the S&P 500 was 1.03 over five years (our calculation). Long stretches in which large companies lead: over these five years the S&P 500's total return was 92.58% against IJR's 37.24%. Rallies led by loss-making or very small companies, which the index holds less of; in 2025 IJR returned 5.95% against 12.69% for IWM. Weakness in banks, insurers and industrial companies, given their weights.

Higher volatility. A 100% small-company stock portfolio: five-year volatility of 20.35% a year against 15.64% for the S&P 500 (our calculation), and daily peak-to-trough falls of −26.5% (2021–2022) and −28.0% (2024–2025) inside this window.

03
Index design. Membership rests on a committee's decisions and an earnings test applied at entry. The design has led to years well ahead of and well behind the Russell 2000 (+26.69% vs +14.62% in 2021; +5.95% vs +12.69% in 2025), so the fund will not track "small caps" as other indexes define them.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

S&P SmallCap 600 (IJR, SPSM, VIOO): 600 companies chosen by an index committee from those within a size band, with at least 10% of shares available to the public, enough trading, and positive GAAP earnings over the latest four quarters and in the latest quarter (S&P U.S. Indices Methodology). Russell 2000 (IWM): the IWM prospectus describes it as the "approximately 1,933 smallest issuers in the Russell 3000 Index", a cut by size rank, and sets out no earnings test. On October 2, 2026 IJR and IWM overlapped by 47.6%; IWM held 1,986 lines against IJR's 640.
