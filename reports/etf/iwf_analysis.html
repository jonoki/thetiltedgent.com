01 Fund Overview

IWF holds the "growth" half of the thousand largest U.S. companies. Its July 31, 2026 summary prospectus states the objective: to "track the investment results of an index composed of large- and mid-capitalization U.S. equities that exhibit growth characteristics." That index is the Russell 1000 Growth Index, a subset of the Russell 1000 made of the companies with "higher price-to-book ratios, higher forecasted medium-term growth and higher sales-per-share historical growth" relative to all Russell 1000 companies. On March 31, 2026 it represented about 66% of the total market value of the Russell 1000 (prospectus).

The fund launched on May 22, 2000 and lists on NYSE Arca. Net assets were $128.17 billion on September 24, 2026, across 1,020,100,000 shares. The expense ratio is 0.18%, all of it management fee: under the advisory agreement BlackRock Fund Advisors pays the fund's other operating expenses, apart from items such as interest, taxes, brokerage and extraordinary costs. After the close on April 28, 2026 the fund split its shares 4-for-1; every per-share figure on this page, including the chart, is on the post-split basis.

Why IWF leads this group

Two U.S.-listed ETFs track the Russell 1000 Growth Index in U.S. dollars: IWF and Vanguard's VONG. IWF is the larger by any measure the issuers publish. Its net assets were $128,899.14 million on June 30, 2026 (fact sheet) and $128.17 billion on September 24; VONG's whole fund, all share classes, was $54.3 billion on August 31, 2026, and its ETF share class $45.9 billion.

How the index picks growth stocks

From FTSE Russell's Russell US Equity Indexes Construction and Methodology, v7.2 (August 2026), and the fund's prospectus:

1
Start from the Russell 1000. On rank day all eligible U.S. securities are ranked by total market capitalization; the largest 4,000 form the Russell 3000E, and the Russell 1000 is its large- and mid-cap part. Membership is reset at reconstitution, now twice a year (fourth Friday of June, second Friday of December).
2
Score every stock on three variables. "For value, book-to-price (B/P) ratio is used"; for growth, two variables are used: I/B/E/S forecast medium-term growth (two-year) and five-year historical growth in sales per share. The ranks are standardised; value counts for 50% and the two growth measures for the other 50%, combined into a composite value score (CVS).
3
Split, don't sort. A "non-linear probability" method assigns weights: stocks below the first quartile of the CVS distribution go 100% to growth, stocks above the third quartile 100% to value, and those in between are shared. A stock that is 20% in value is 80% in growth. Any stock more than 95% in one style is moved to 100%; roughly 70% of market value ends up all-growth or all-value.
4
Damp the turnover. If a company's CVS moves by no more than ±0.10 and it stays in the same core index, the old score is kept. Style is set each June; at the December reconstitution new style probabilities are calculated only for new members and for stocks moving to a new capitalization group, such as between the Russell 1000 and Russell 2000.
5
Cap the giants. Since the open of March 24, 2025 the standard Russell style indexes apply quarterly capping. The prospectus gives the rule: at each quarterly rebalance (after the close on the third Friday of March, June, September and December) no single company may exceed 22.5% of the index, and all companies above 4.5% together are capped at 45%. The caps apply per company, so Alphabet's two share classes count as one. In the fund's September 23 file the five companies above 4.5% (NVIDIA 15.45%, Alphabet 10.46%, Apple 7.75%, Microsoft 5.46%, Broadcom 4.85%) total 43.97% (our sum).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. The ten largest holdings are 57.65% of the fund, NVIDIA alone 15.45%, and information technology 56.03%. The prospectus lists concentration, technology companies and, as the fund can become "non-diversified" by index weighting, non-diversification among its principal risks.
02
Growth-style risk. The prospectus: growth securities "can decline rapidly and significantly in reaction to negative news" and "may underperform value securities." In 2022 the fund's NAV fell 29.26% (issuer); the S&P 500's 5-year volatility on the same monthly basis was 15.84% a year against IWF's 19.47% (our calculation, section 04).
03
Valuation of what it owns. iShares reports a P/E ratio of 43.06 and P/B of 15.96 for the holdings (September 23, 2026); its P/E method sets individual ratios above 60 to 60 (fact sheet glossary). The index buys the stocks with the highest price-to-book ratios by construction.
04
Index rules can force trades. The 22.5% single-company and 45% aggregate caps apply at each quarterly rebalance; on September 23 the five companies above 4.5% summed to 43.97% of the fund, close to the aggregate cap. Style scores also move stocks between the growth and value halves at reconstitution.
05
Higher fee than the equivalent. 0.18% against VONG's 0.06% for the same index; on the issuer's figures the fee is also the main source of IWF's gap to the index.
06
Low income. 30-day SEC yield 0.26% (August 31, 2026). The split-adjusted distributions in the issuer's own table add up to $0.4318 over the last four quarters, 0.34% of the September 24 close (section 04 explains the issuer's separate 1.14% figure).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising prices for large growth companies: the fund's NAV returned +42.49% in 2023 and +33.11% in 2024. Strength in semiconductors and large platform companies, since NVIDIA, Apple, Alphabet (both share classes), Microsoft and Broadcom alone are 43.97% of the fund (our sum). Rising U.S. stock markets in general: the fund's beta to the S&P 500 has been about 1.17 over five years, so it has tended to rise more than the market in up months. Strong earnings delivery by high-multiple companies, which supports the 43.06 P/E the holdings carry.

What hurts it

Rotations from growth to value stocks and falling valuation multiples: 2022 cost −29.26% at NAV and −32.7% at the daily trough. A sell-off in chip and AI-related stocks, given 56.03% in information technology and 15.45% in NVIDIA. Broad U.S. bear markets, with no bond, cash or foreign-stock cushion. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency.

Top 3 Risks

01
Concentration. Ten lines are 57.65% of the fund and one company 15.45%. The index's caps (22.5% per company, 45% for all companies above 4.5%) limit this only at quarterly rebalances, and the five companies above 4.5% already sum to 43.97%.
02
Style and valuation risk. The index selects stocks with high price-to-book ratios and strong growth forecasts, which trade at 43.06 times earnings and 15.96 times book (issuer, September 23, 2026). When expectations fall, such stocks can drop faster than the market: 5-year volatility is 19.47% a year against 15.84% for the S&P 500 (our calculation).
03
Full U.S. equity market risk. A 100% stock, almost entirely U.S. portfolio takes market falls in full: −32.7% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −23.1% in early 2025 inside this five-year window.
Category Commentary
"Growth" depends on who defines it
PRODUCT CATEGORY

U.S. large-cap growth ETFs track different index families, and the holdings files show how far apart they sit. IWF overlaps VONG, on the same Russell index, by 97.0%, but only 73–77% with funds on Morningstar or S&P growth indices. The differences are specific: Micron Technology, 3.55% of IWF, is absent from Vanguard's Morningstar-based VUG and MGK; Berkshire Hathaway and JPMorgan Chase sit in the S&P 500 Growth funds but not in IWF. Holdings counts range from 56 (MGK) to 371 (IWF), and fees from 0.03% (VUG) to 0.18% (IWF, IVW).

Russell's approach: partial membership
WHAT DIFFERS

The Russell method does not force every company into growth or value. Companies near the middle of the composite value score are split between the two indexes in proportion to their scores, which is why Amazon.com is 0.59% of IWF and 6.00% of iShares' Russell 1000 Value ETF, IWD (both files September 23, 2026). Since 2025 the index also caps its largest members each quarter, and in 2026 membership moved to two reconstitutions a year, June and December (FTSE Russell).
