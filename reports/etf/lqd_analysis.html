01 Fund Overview

LQD is a basket of bonds issued by large companies with investment-grade credit ratings, all in U.S. dollars. In the words of its summary prospectus, the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of U.S. dollar-denominated, investment-grade corporate bonds." There are no government bonds and no mortgage pools in it, only company debt, so its return depends on two things: the level of U.S. interest rates and the extra yield investors demand to lend to companies rather than to the U.S. Treasury.

The fund was launched on July 22, 2002 and is managed by BlackRock Fund Advisors. On October 2, 2026 it held 3,155 bonds worth USD 26.84 billion. The expense ratio is 0.14%, all of it management fee (summary prospectus dated June 29, 2026).

What "Liquid" means in the index name
The index takes only the larger, more frequently traded part of the investment-grade corporate market. Per the summary prospectus (June 29, 2026), a bond qualifies if it is a U.S.-dollar corporate bond from an issuer in a country S&P Dow Jones Indices classes as a developed market, has an average rating of investment grade (Fitch, Moody's and S&P Global Ratings are considered), comes from an issuer with at least $2 billion of bonds outstanding, has at least $750 million outstanding itself, and has at least three years to maturity (three years and six months for new entries). Weights follow market value, with a 3% cap per issuer. On February 28, 2026 the index had about 3,039 bonds, against 8,827 in the broader iBoxx USD Corporate Bond Index it is drawn from.
Structure
Physical, sampled
The fund owns the bonds directly, using "representative sampling" (prospectus): a set of bonds chosen to match the index's duration, ratings, sectors and yield, not necessarily every index bond. It must keep at least 80% of assets in index bonds. On October 2, 2026 the holdings file shows a 1.54% position in a BlackRock Treasury money-market fund and a −0.54% cash line.
Currency
U.S. dollars only
Every line in the October 2, 2026 holdings file is denominated in USD. 14.2% of the bond value comes from issuers the file locates outside the United States (U.K. 4.5%, Canada 2.5%, Japan 2.0% and others; our sum). They borrow in dollars, so there is no currency to hedge.
Distributions
Monthly
Ex-dividend on the first business day of each month, with a second December date instead of a January one (iShares distribution schedule). The twelve payments with ex-dates from November 3, 2025 to October 1, 2026 totalled $5.0488 per share, all income, no capital gains (our sum of the issuer's table).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

USD 26.84B net assets 0.01% median bid/ask 3,155 bonds, 3% issuer cap 100% investment grade Monthly income Since 2002

LQD holds the large, frequently traded slice of the U.S. dollar investment-grade corporate market in one share that trades heavily: about USD 5.0 billion changed hands on October 2, 2026 (our calculation from the issuer's volume and closing price). Its tracking is close but not free. Over five years to June 30, 2026 the NAV return was −0.30% a year against −0.20% for the index, and over ten years 2.41% against 2.52% (issuer). The gap, 0.10 to 0.11 points a year, is a little less than the 0.14% fee.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest rates. Effective duration is 7.53 years (Oct 2, 2026), so a one-point rise in yields cuts the price by roughly 7.5% before income. That is longer than broad corporate funds (USIG 6.07 years, SPBO 6.39), because the index drops bonds under three years and 23.82% of the fund matures in more than twenty years.
02
Credit spreads. The fund earns 87.37 basis points a year over Treasuries after adjusting for call features (issuer's option-adjusted spread, Oct 2, 2026). When investors fear a recession that spread widens, and the price falls even if Treasury yields do not move. 40.24% is rated BBB, the lowest investment-grade tier; a bond cut below BBB leaves the index.
03
Bank concentration. Banking is 22.70% of the fund and the four largest borrowers are U.S. banks (10.19% together, our sum). A banking-sector shock would hit a large share of the portfolio at once.
04
Price vs NAV in stress. The share price normally sits close to the fund's NAV: on every trading day from January to September 2026 it was between 0.10% below and 0.17% above (issuer's quarterly summaries). In 2025 the widest gaps were a 0.22% discount on April 8 and a 0.97% premium on April 9 (issuer's premium/discount summary).
05
Calls and income. Many corporate bonds can be repaid early. When rates fall, issuers call bonds and the fund reinvests at lower yields; the prospectus names call risk and income risk among the principal risks.
06
Fee and tax. The 0.14% expense ratio is several times that of the broad corporate peers in section 05 (0.03%–0.04%). Distributions may be taxable as ordinary income (prospectus): iShares' five-year after-tax figure to June 30, 2026 is −1.86% a year against −0.30% before tax.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling Treasury yields: with a 7.53-year duration, a one-point drop adds roughly 7.5% to the price, on top of income. Stable yields and spreads: the fund then earns roughly its yield (SEC yield 6.01%, yield to maturity 6.19%). Narrowing credit spreads, when investors grow more willing to lend to companies. Slowing inflation, since every coupon is fixed. Steady earnings at the banks and large technology and communications companies that make up the biggest blocks.

What hurts it

Rising yields, as in 2022, when the total return fell 24.5% from peak to trough, and as in September 2026, when the 10-year yield rose 0.54 points and the price fell 3.79%. Wider credit spreads in a recession scare, which can push corporate bonds down even while Treasuries rally. Downgrades among the 40.24% rated BBB. Inflation above expectations. Rising yields paired with falling stocks, which removes the diversification benefit (five-year correlation with the S&P 500: 0.71).

03
Diversification can fail. The five-year correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.71 and the Sharpe ratio −0.51 over the same window. Corporate bonds carry both rate and credit risk, so in a sell-off they can fall alongside stocks.
Category Commentary
Investment-grade corporate bond ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These funds lend to companies with investment-grade ratings and pay out the interest monthly. The broad ones differ mainly in index rules. LQD keeps only large issues from large issuers with three or more years left, so it holds 3,155 bonds and has the longest duration of the group (7.53 years). SPBO and VTC follow the Bloomberg U.S. Corporate index (issues of $300 million or more, from one year to maturity, per State Street; 4,393 and 5,321 bonds) and USIG the ICE BofA US Corporate index (11,557 bonds); their durations are 6.07 to 6.5 years. LQD charges 0.14% against 0.03%–0.04% for the others (issuer pages). iShares also sells corporate bond funds limited to one maturity band, such as IGSB (1–5 years) and IGIB (5–10 years) (USIG product page); these change the rate exposure while keeping investment-grade credit.

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

Two numbers set the return: the Treasury yield for the fund's duration and the credit spread on top of it. On October 2, 2026 the spread was 87.37 basis points for LQD and 82.14 for the Bloomberg U.S. Corporate index (issuer pages). The last five years show how much the starting yield matters. LQD began the window with the 10-year Treasury at 1.52% and returned −0.30% a year (NAV, five years to June 30, 2026). Its yield to maturity today, 6.19%, is about four times that 2021 Treasury yield.
