01 Fund Overview

MUB lends to U.S. states, cities and public agencies by holding their bonds, known as municipal bonds or "munis". Their interest is generally exempt from federal income tax, and the prospectus says the fund intends to distribute "primarily" net tax-exempt income. The summary prospectus (June 29, 2026) states the objective: the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of investment-grade U.S. municipal bonds." Its index is the ICE AMT-Free US National Municipal Index, which the prospectus describes as measuring "U.S. dollar denominated investment grade tax-exempt debt publicly issued in the U.S. domestic market by U.S. states and their political subdivisions."

AMT-free means the index includes only bonds whose interest is also not subject to the alternative minimum tax, a parallel federal tax calculation that can catch some muni interest. To enter the index a bond must be rated at least BBB- (S&P, Fitch) or Baa3 (Moody's), using the lowest rating, come from an offering of at least $100 million, have at least $15 million outstanding and at least one month left to maturity. The index is weighted by market value, capped to limit concentration, and rebalanced on the last calendar day of each month. On February 28, 2026 it held 26,009 issues (prospectus).

The fund owns the bonds directly, using "representative sampling": 7,292 bonds on October 2, 2026, against 26,009 issues in the index on February 28, 2026 (prospectus). It must keep at least 80% of assets in index bonds. Spare cash goes into a BlackRock municipal money market fund, 3.68% of net assets on October 2.

Tax-exempt income and the tax-equivalent yield

A muni yield is compared with a taxable yield by grossing it up for the tax the investor does not pay. iShares' definition: tax-equivalent yield = tax-free municipal bond yield ÷ (1 − tax rate), and for national funds it assumes "the highest Federal individual income tax rates." MUB's tax-equivalent SEC yield was 6.87% on October 2, 2026 (issuer). That equals the issuer's unrounded 4.067% SEC yield ÷ (1 − 0.408): the 37% top income-tax rate plus the 3.8% net investment income tax (our check of the issuer's figure). The IRS states that this 3.8% tax does not apply to "tax-exempt state or municipal bond interest" (IRS Topic 559).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.05% USD 47.95B net assets 7,292 bonds 79.0% rated AA or AAA Federally tax-exempt, AMT-free Monthly income Median spread 0.01%

The fund packages thousands of muni bonds, a market the prospectus says "may be less liquid" than other debt, into one exchange-traded share with a 30-day median bid-ask spread of 0.01% (issuer, October 2, 2026). Tracking is close: to June 30, 2026 the NAV return was 0.99% a year over five years against 1.04% for the index, and 1.92% against 2.06% over ten years (issuer). Net assets grew from USD 24.2 billion in February 2022 to USD 43.2 billion in February 2026 (annual financial statements) and USD 47.95 billion on October 2, 2026.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest rates. Effective duration was 7.07 years on October 2, 2026, so a one-point rise in yields cuts the price by roughly 7%, before income. In September 2026 the 10-year Treasury par yield went from 4.75% to 5.29% (U.S. Treasury) and the fund's NAV returned −3.78%, its worst month since September 2008 (issuer monthly series, our scan).
02
Rate sensitivity has grown. Effective duration rose from 6.18 years on June 30, 2026 (fact sheet) to 7.07 years on October 2, and weighted average life from 7.01 to 9.85 years. The prospectus lists call risk: when rates fall, issuers can repay callable bonds early and the fund reinvests at lower yields.
03
State concentration. New York 19.66%, California 16.85% and Texas 11.41%. The prospectus names "New York Municipal Securities Risk" as a principal risk on its own: developments specific to New York and its agencies can hit the fund.
04
Tax law. The case for munis rests on the federal exemption. The prospectus warns that if a bond loses its tax-exempt status, "its value may decline significantly." BlackRock's annual report for the year to February 28, 2026 describes early-period volatility as investors worried about changes to munis' tax-exempt status, which spurred a surge in issuance, until the status was preserved under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act.
05
Liquidity and pricing. The prospectus notes the muni market "may be less liquid" than other debt and that munis can be harder to value. The ETF share can drift from NAV: in the third quarter of 2026 it closed below NAV on 11 days and above it on 52 (issuer).
06
The tax benefit depends on the holder. At the 22% rate the 4.07% SEC yield is worth 5.21% in taxable terms, below the 10-year Treasury's 5.28% on October 2; at the top rate it is worth 6.87%. In an IRA or 401(k) the exemption adds nothing, and the prospectus calls the fund generally not appropriate for those accounts.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling interest rates: with a 7.07-year effective duration, a one-point drop in yields adds roughly 7% to the price, on top of income. Stable rates: the fund then earns roughly its yield (SEC yield 4.07%, yield to maturity 4.40%), federally tax-free. Higher tax rates, which raise the value of the exemption. Steady state and city finances, especially in New York, California and Texas. Light new issuance relative to demand.

What hurts it

Rising yields, as in 2022 (total return −11.56% from peak to trough) and September 2026 (NAV −3.78% in one month). Lower tax rates or any narrowing of the muni exemption, which the prospectus says could cut a bond's value "significantly". A credit shock in a large state or city; New York alone is 19.66% of the fund. Heavy issuance and selling that push the share below NAV. Rising yields paired with falling stocks (five-year correlation with the S&P 500: 0.66).

Top 3 Risks

01
Interest-rate risk. Effective duration 7.07 years and rising (6.18 on June 30). The five years to September 2026 produced a −0.08% annualised NAV return and a −11.56% drawdown in the dividend-adjusted share price, while the 10-year Treasury par yield rose from 1.52% to 5.29%.
02
Tax-law risk. The fund's appeal rests on the federal exemption: at the top rate its 4.07% SEC yield is worth 6.87% in taxable terms. A change in that exemption, or in tax rates, changes the value of every bond in the fund.
03
Geographic concentration. New York, California and Texas are 47.92% of the fund, and the largest issuer, NYC Transitional Finance Authority, is 4.51%. The prospectus singles out New York municipal securities risk.
Category Commentary
National municipal bond index ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

National muni ETFs hold tax-exempt bonds from issuers across the country in one share, paying monthly income that is generally free of federal income tax. The two largest funds in this table, MUB (USD 47.95 billion on October 2) and Vanguard's VTEB (USD 46.3 billion in the ETF shares on August 31), charge 0.05% and 0.03%. Schwab's SCMB also charges 0.03%; State Street's TFI charges 0.23%. What differs: index (ICE AMT-Free US National for MUB, S&P National AMT-Free for VTEB, ICE variants for SCMB and TFI); sampling depth (7,292 bonds for MUB, 10,721 for VTEB, 7,062 for SCMB, 1,966 for TFI); and maturity profile (TFI's index excludes bonds with under a year left). All four indexes exclude bonds subject to the alternative minimum tax.

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

With fees of a few hundredths of a percent and overlapping indexes, returns are set by the muni market itself: the level of Treasury yields, the supply of new muni bonds, and the credit of states and cities. Taxes decide who holds them. The tax-equivalent yield, yield ÷ (1 − tax rate), is the category's common yardstick, and at today's yields it ranges from 5.21% at the 22% rate to 6.87% at the top rate for MUB. Over the last five years the starting point mattered most: MUB began the window yielding far less than it does now and returned −0.08% a year at NAV to September 30, 2026.
