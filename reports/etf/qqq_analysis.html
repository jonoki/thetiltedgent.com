01 Fund Overview

QQQ is a way to own 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on Nasdaq through one share. Its summary prospectus (December 22, 2025) states that the fund "seeks to track the investment results, before fees and expenses, of the Nasdaq-100 Index®." Invesco's product page describes it as "a convenient way to invest in many of the largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq Stock Market." The prospectus says the fund invests substantially all of its assets in the index's securities and uses full replication: it holds the stocks themselves rather than a sample or derivatives.

Launched March 10, 1999, QQQ kept its name through a structural change in December 2025. In the prospectus's words: "Effective after market close on December 19, 2025, the Fund was reclassified as an open-end management investment company (the "Reclassification"). Prior to the Reclassification, the Fund operated as a unit investment trust ("UIT")." Since then it has paid Invesco a single unitary management fee of 0.18%, which is also its total expense ratio. Net assets were $500.94 billion on September 24, 2026 (Invesco).

Why this fund: the larger of Invesco's two Nasdaq-100 ETFs

QQQ and QQQM track the same index for the same issuer. On Invesco's daily figures for September 24, 2026, QQQ held $500.94 billion and QQQM $109.25 billion; QQQ is 4.59 times the size (our calculation: $500,943.9M ÷ $109,251.5M). Invesco's own page describes the split: "QQQ is often used by investors and traders seeking liquidity, while QQQM is designed for investors seeking long-term Nasdaq-100 exposure."

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. The ten largest lines are 46.75% of the fund and NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 21.28% (our sums). The prospectus describes the fund as non-diversified: it "can invest a greater portion of its assets in the obligations or securities of a small number of issuers" (statutory prospectus, December 22, 2025).
02
One dominant sector, one missing. Technology is 66.36% of holdings and Technology plus Consumer Discretionary 83.01%. Financials are excluded by the index rules, so the fund has none of the banks, insurers and payment companies that make up a large part of the broad U.S. market.
03
Larger swings than the broad market. Over the five years to August 2026 its monthly returns had a volatility of 21.09% a year against 15.84% for the S&P 500, and a beta of 1.23 (our calculation, section 04). The fall from the December 2021 peak to November 2022 was −35.1% on daily total-return data.
04
Index rules decide membership. Nasdaq chooses what enters and leaves. Methodology changes took effect on May 1, 2026, including a faster entry route for large newly listed companies (prospectus supplement, April 30, 2026). Space Exploration Technologies (SpaceX) became a component before the open on July 7, 2026 (Nasdaq) and was 2.66% of the fund on September 23.
05
Low income. The 30-day SEC yield is 0.39% and the trailing distribution yield 0.42%. The fund is built for growth in the index, not for income.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists "Authorized Participant Concentration Risk", "Market Trading Risk" and "Shares May Trade at Prices Different than NAV" among its principal risks. On the banner date the gap was −0.03%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising prices for the largest U.S. technology and internet companies, since the ten largest lines are 46.75% of the fund and Technology is 66.36% of holdings. Markets that reward growth more than value; the fund holds no financial companies. Broad equity rallies, which it has tended to amplify: a beta of 1.23 to the S&P 500 over five years, and +15.7% in April 2026 alone. Low turnover and a 0.18% fee keep the gap to the index small.

What hurts it

Sell-offs in large technology stocks: 2022 took −35.1% at the daily trough, against −24.5% for the S&P 500 total-return index over the same five-year window (January 3 to October 12, 2022, our calculation), and the worst quarter on the prospectus record is −22.33% (June 30, 2022). Periods when financials, energy or other sectors that are small or absent in the index lead the market. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency. Changes in index rules that bring in large new companies quickly.

Not the same as "growth" or "technology"
WHAT DIFFERS

The Nasdaq-100 is defined by where a company lists and by its size, not by a growth screen or a sector code. That is why its overlap with the largest growth funds (VUG 59.0%, IWF 59.1%) and technology funds (VGT 51.7%, XLK 56.0%) is only a little over half by weight (our calculations, section 05). It holds consumer, telecom and health care companies listed on Nasdaq that the technology funds leave out, and names absent from the growth funds' files (Intel, Cisco and Linde in IWF's case), while it leaves out any company listed on another exchange and every financial company.
