01 Fund Overview

RSP owns the same 500-odd companies as the S&P 500, but it gives each one the same slice of the money. In a standard S&P 500 fund the largest company counts for more than 8% of the portfolio and the smallest for a few thousandths of a percent; in RSP each company is reset to about 0.20% four times a year. Its August 28, 2026 summary prospectus says the fund "seeks to track the investment results (before fees and expenses) of the S&P 500® Equal Weight Index," which "assigns each component security the same weight at each quarterly rebalance," while the parent S&P 500 "employs a float-adjusted market capitalization weighted methodology."

Launched on April 24, 2003, RSP is the oldest of the equal-weight funds compared here and, with $95.43 billion of net assets on October 2, 2026 (issuer; NAV of $209.693466 × 455,102,663 shares), far larger than the other equal-weight U.S. funds in section 05. Invesco took it over from Guggenheim: its product page notes that "as the result of a reorganization on April 6, 2018, the returns presented reflect performance of the Guggenheim predecessor fund." The expense ratio is 0.20%, all of it management fee; the prospectus lists other expenses as "None".

What equal weighting changes

The same companies, held in very different amounts. On October 2, 2026 the ten largest holdings in State Street's SPY, a cap-weighted S&P 500 fund (nine companies: Alphabet has two share classes), made up 39.10% of that fund. The same ten lines made up 1.90% of RSP (our sums from both issuers' holdings files that day). NVIDIA, 8.50% of SPY, was 0.21% of RSP. The money goes instead to the other 490-odd companies: Invesco's page says the approach "tilts toward smaller companies in the S&P 500 Index" and gives "greater exposure to smaller companies," and its average market cap of holdings was $144.23 billion (September 30, 2026).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Largest line 0.29% $95.4B net assets Full replication, 506 holdings 5-yr gap to index −0.22 pts/yr No capital-gains distributions (issuer) Since April 2003

The fund spreads its money evenly, so no single company can dominate it: on October 2 its largest line was 0.29%, against 8.50% for NVIDIA in the cap-weighted SPY. Its results have stayed close to its own index. Over the five years to September 30, 2026 the NAV returned 8.56% a year against the index's 8.78%, a gap of 0.22 percentage points, about the 0.20% expense ratio; over ten years 11.43% against 11.66%. Despite quarterly selling of the stocks that rose, Invesco states that the fund has paid no capital-gains distribution since 2003. On October 2 the bid/ask midpoint was 0.017% above NAV (issuer).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Lags when the largest companies lead. Equal weight holds about 1.90% in the ten holdings (nine companies) that make up 39.10% of SPY, a cap-weighted S&P 500 fund. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 the S&P 500's total return was +92.6% and RSP's +52.2% (section 07). Invesco's 5-year NAV figure to September 30 is 8.56% a year against 13.79% for the S&P 500.
02
Sector tilts set by company counts. Industrials (16.60%), utilities, real estate and materials are two to three and a half times their cap-weighted shares; technology is 15.67% against 40.07%. The sector mix is a by-product of how many companies each sector has, not a view on the sectors.
03
Smaller companies, more of them. The prospectus lists mid-capitalization companies among its principal risks: their shares "may be more volatile and less liquid than those of larger, more established companies." The index's smallest member was $5.2 billion on June 30, 2026.
04
Higher cost and turnover than a cap-weighted fund. The 0.20% expense ratio is about twice SPY's 0.0945%. Portfolio turnover was 27% in the latest fiscal year (prospectus), because each quarter the fund sells what rose and buys what fell. Trading costs inside the fund are not in the expense ratio.
05
Index rules decide membership. The fund holds whatever the S&P 500 holds. On September 21, 2026 Bloom Energy, Everpure and Illumina joined and Molson Coors Beverage, The Trade Desk and Builders FirstSource left (S&P DJI release, September 4, 2026). In RSP each such change moves about 0.20% of the fund; in SPY the three newcomers were 0.06% to 0.13% on October 2.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares trade at a market price that can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists authorized-participant concentration, market trading and "Shares May Trade at Prices Different than NAV" among principal risks. On the banner date the gap was 0.017%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Periods in which the typical S&P 500 company does better than the largest ones, as in 2022 (RSP −11.6% vs the S&P 500's −18.1%). Strength in the sectors it holds more of than the S&P 500: industrials, utilities, real estate and materials at two to three and a half times their cap-weighted shares, financials and health care at about 1.3 to 1.4 times. A sell-off concentrated in mega-cap technology: SPY's ten largest holdings are about 1.90% of the fund. Lower valuations of what it holds: 19.53x earnings against 24.81x for the S&P 500 (different issuers' measures). The quarterly reset, which adds to companies that have fallen.

What hurts it

Markets led by a handful of very large companies, as in 2023–2025 and over the five-year window (+52.2% vs +92.6% total return). Broad U.S. bear markets: the fund holds no bonds or cash cushion, and fell 21.4% at the 2022 daily trough. Stress in smaller and mid-sized companies, which the prospectus flags as more volatile and less liquid. Its 0.20% fee and 27% turnover, both higher than a cap-weighted S&P 500 fund's.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full U.S. equity market risk. A 100% stock, mostly one-country portfolio takes market falls in full: −21.4% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −17.8% in 2024–25 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 15.98% a year (our calculation), slightly above the S&P 500's 15.64%.
02
Tracking the S&P 500 is not the goal. The fund follows the equal-weight index, not the S&P 500, and can trail or lead it by wide margins for years: 40.4 percentage points behind over five years, 6.5 points ahead in 2022. Correlation with the S&P 500 over 60 months was 0.90 and beta 0.92 (our calculation).
03
Rule-driven sector and size tilts. Industrials at 16.60% and technology at 15.67% (vs 8.12% and 40.07% in the S&P 500) come from company counts, not analysis, and move at each rebalance and index change. Smaller members carry the same weight as the largest, and the prospectus lists mid-capitalization companies among its principal risks.
Category Commentary
Same companies, different weights
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Equal-weight funds take a market-cap index's member list and drop its weights. RSP does this for the S&P 500, EUSA for MSCI's U.S. index (515 holdings) and EQAL for the Russell 1000 (1,014 holdings), where the index first gives each of 11 sector groups the same share. The result is a portfolio that holds more of mid-sized companies and of sectors with many members, and less of the few largest companies. RSP's turnover was 27% in its latest fiscal year, as each quarterly reset sells part of what rose and buys what fell.

Why returns differ from the S&P 500
WHAT DIFFERS

RSP and SPY hold the same 500-odd companies, but their weights overlap by only 43.7% (our calculation, October 2, 2026). The difference in returns comes from how much each fund holds of the largest companies. Over the ten years to September 30, 2026, Invesco reports 11.43% a year for RSP's NAV against 15.33% for the S&P 500; in its December 31, 2025 prospectus table the gap over ten years is 11.46% against 14.82%. Equal weight led in 2022 and trailed in 2023, 2024 and 2025 on Yahoo's data. The fund's own record against its index is close: 0.22 points a year behind over five years, about its 0.20% fee.
