01 Fund Overview

SCHD is a single share that holds about 100 U.S. dividend-paying companies picked by a rulebook. Its goal, in the words of its fact sheet, is "to track as closely as possible, before fees and expenses, the total return of the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100™ Index." Among the fund's highlights the fact sheet lists that it "Tracks an index focused on the quality and sustainability of dividends" and "Invests in stocks selected for fundamental strength relative to their peers, based on financial ratios." The rules behind that selection are set out in section 02.

Launched October 20, 2011 and listed on NYSE Arca, the fund had $112.3 billion in net assets on August 31, 2026 ($112,337,237,207 in its portfolio filing, Form N-PORT; $95.7 billion on the June 30 fact sheet). Its expense ratio is 0.06%, all of it management fee (other expenses 0.00%), per the summary prospectus of February 27, 2026. Unlike a broad market fund, which owns every company in proportion to its size, SCHD owns only the stocks that pass its index's dividend and quality tests. The selection is renewed every year, so the portfolio can change a great deal in one step: in 2026 a quarter of the fund's names were replaced (section 04).

How the index picks its 100 stocks

The rules as the fund's summary prospectus (February 27, 2026) describes the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index:

  • 1 · Universe
    U.S. stocks in the Dow Jones U.S. Broad Market Index, "excluding real estate investment trusts (REITs), master limited partnerships, preferred stocks and convertibles."
  • 2 · Eligibility
    "All index eligible stocks must have sustained at least 10 consecutive years of dividend payments," a float-adjusted market value of at least $500 million (the value of shares available to the public), and minimum trading liquidity.
  • 3 · Selection
    From the highest-yielding eligible stocks, the 100 components are chosen on "four fundamentals-based characteristics": "cash flow to total debt, return on equity, dividend yield and 5-year dividend growth rate." Together these test balance-sheet strength, profitability, current income and how fast the dividend has grown.
  • 4 · Weighting
    By modified market capitalisation: bigger companies get more weight, but "No single stock can represent more than 4.0% of the index" and no sector more than 25%.
  • 5 · Upkeep
    "The index composition is reviewed annually and rebalanced quarterly." The fund "may sell securities that are represented in the index in anticipation of their removal from the index."

The caps apply when the index is rebalanced; between rebalances, prices move weights. On May 31, 2026 the fund's filing showed QUALCOMM at 6.74% and Texas Instruments at 5.90%. On the June 30 fact sheet Texas Instruments was 3.92% and QUALCOMM was outside the top ten (below 3.68%); in the August 31 filing they were 3.12% and 2.52%, while Merck (4.76%), Amgen (4.70%), Abbott (4.68%), Coca-Cola (4.16%) and Chevron (4.01%) were above 4%.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.06% $112.3B net assets 10-year dividend record required 4% stock cap, 25% sector cap 5-yr gap to index −0.08 pts/yr 30-day SEC yield 3.35%

The fund has followed its index closely. Over the five years to June 30, 2026 its NAV returned 8.51% a year against the index's 8.59%, a gap of 0.08 percentage points; over ten years, 12.37% against 12.46%; since launch, 13.09% against 13.21% (fact sheet). The five- and ten-year gaps are close to the 0.06% expense ratio. The selection rules require a decade of unbroken dividends and test each company's cash flow against its debt. Its holdings traded at 18.41 times earnings on June 30, 2026 (fact sheet, FactSet data).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A style that can lag for years. Investment style risk is one of the principal risks in the prospectus: "The fund primarily invests in dividend paying stocks." Over the five years to October 30, 2026 SCHD's total return was +65.54% against +76.74% for the S&P 500 (our calculation, section 07). The fund owned none of NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon or Alphabet on August 31, 2026 (portfolio filing).
02
Sector concentration. Consumer Staples, Health Care and Energy are 54.3% of the fund (October 2, our sum). In the August 31 filing Health Care rested on five companies and Information Technology on four. The 25% sector cap limits any one sector, not the combined weight of three.
03
Big annual turnover. About a quarter of the fund changed around the 2026 index review: between the fund's February 28 and May 31 filings, 25 stocks came in and 23 went out (our count). Portfolio turnover was 42.28% over the year to June 30, 2026 (fact sheet), against 30% for the fiscal year to August 31, 2025 (prospectus). A rule-based screen can drop a holding after a fall in its yield or a rise in its debt, and add one after its price has already moved.
04
Dividends are not fixed. The fact sheet warns: "Stocks held by the Fund may reduce or stop paying dividends, affecting the Fund's ability to generate income." Payouts vary quarter to quarter: the June 2026 distribution ($0.2525) was 3.0% below June 2025's ($0.2602); September's ($0.2665) was 2.3% above the year before (our calculation).
05
Tax on income. A yield of about 3% means a larger share of the return arrives as taxable dividends in a taxable account. Morningstar's five-year tax cost ratio is 1.1 percentage points a year (August 31, 2026).
06
Trading at market price. "Shares of the Fund May Trade at Prices Other Than NAV" is one of the principal risks in the prospectus, along with market trading risk and tracking error. Shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from the value of the holdings.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Markets that favour established, cash-generating companies over fast-growing ones, as in 2022, when the fund's NAV fell 3.23% and the S&P 500 fell 18.1%. Strength in its largest sectors: consumer staples, health care and energy, 54.3% of the fund together. Steady or rising dividends from its holdings, which feed the yield. Higher oil and gas prices, through the energy sleeve. Low costs: 0.06% a year keeps the fund close to its index.

What hurts it

Markets led by companies the fund does not own, such as NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet (none was in the August 31 holdings): the S&P 500 beat SCHD in each calendar year from 2023 to 2025. Dividend cuts at holdings, or a fall in energy prices. A broad stock sell-off: the fund fell 16.8% in 2022 and 16.1% from November 2024 to April 2025 on daily data, with no bond cushion. A badly timed annual review that drops stocks after a fall and adds them after a rise. For investors spending another currency, a weaker U.S. dollar.

Top 3 Risks

01
Style risk. The fund owns one kind of stock. Over five years it returned 10.60% a year against 12.06% for the S&P 500 (total return, our calculation), and that kind of gap can last for years when markets reward growth over income.
02
Concentration in a few sectors and names. Three sectors are 54.3% of the fund; health care rests on five companies and technology on four; the ten largest holdings were 41.71% on August 31. Trouble at one large holding or one sector shows up quickly in the fund.
03
The index rules change the portfolio. Each year's review can replace a quarter of the fund, as in 2026 (25 stocks in and 23 out between the February and May filings), and the quarterly rebalance trims winners back to the 4% cap. The fund's future holdings depend on screens applied to data not yet published.

Dividend funds pick different stocks
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Funds sold as "dividend ETFs" share a label, not a portfolio. Of the five compared here, SCHD and the two whose indices are named for high yield report the highest SEC yields: SCHD 3.35% (June 30), HDV 3.34% (August 31) and VYM 2.35% (September 30). The two named for dividend growth report lower ones: DGRO 1.95% (August 31) and VIG 1.51% (September 30). Holdings counts range from 74 (HDV) to 603 (VYM). SCHD is among the more concentrated, with about 100 stocks, and it uses quality and growth tests alongside yield.

Overlap is low, so results differ
WHAT DIFFERS

By weight, SCHD's overlap with the four peers runs from 14.6% (VIG) to 40.1% (HDV) (our calculation, section 05). VYM holds 80 of SCHD's 99 stocks but spreads its money over 603, so the weight overlap is only 22.0%. With overlaps this low, the index rules rather than the fees decide what each fund owns: expense ratios in this group differ by at most 0.04 percentage points, from 0.04% to 0.08%.
