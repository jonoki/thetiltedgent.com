01 Fund Overview

SGOV holds short-term loans to the U.S. government: Treasury bills that are repaid within three months. A bill pays no coupon. It is bought below its face value and repaid at face value, and the difference is the interest. In the issuer's words, the fund "seeks to track an index that includes U.S. Treasury bills with maturities of 0–3 months" and "offers a way to preserve capital and manage liquidity while seeking minimal interest rate risk."

The index is the ICE 0-3 Month US Treasury Securities Index. The summary prospectus (June 29, 2026) describes it as fixed-coupon U.S. Treasury securities with three months or less to final maturity and at least $1 billion outstanding, not counting the amount held by the Federal Reserve. It excludes inflation-linked securities and stripped bonds, is weighted by market value and is rebalanced on the last calendar day of each month. It had 39 issues on February 28, 2026. On October 2, 2026 the fund had USD 113.1 billion in net assets and 1,126,200,000 shares outstanding (issuer).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Effective duration 0.10 years Positive total return in 58 of 60 months USD 113.1B net assets 0.01% median spread Monthly income Expense ratio 0.09%

The fund holds money for weeks, not years. With an effective duration of 0.10 years (issuer, Oct 2, 2026), a one-point rise in yields would cut the price by roughly 0.1%, and the bills that mature each week are reinvested at the new rate. Over the 60 months from September 2021 to September 2026 the fund's total return was negative in one month only, October 2021 (−0.002%, our calculation from Yahoo Finance's dividend-adjusted close). Over the five years to June 30, 2026 the fund returned 3.59% a year at NAV against 2.89% for its index (issuer).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Income follows the Fed down. Because the bills roll over within weeks, falling short-term rates show up in the income almost at once. The monthly distribution was $0.362484 per share in August 2025 and $0.306812 in August 2026 (issuer). The prospectus names this "income risk."
02
Inflation. The return is in nominal dollars. Nothing in the fund is linked to inflation, and the index excludes inflation-linked securities.
03
One borrower. Everything except the money market fund is owed by the U.S. Treasury. The prospectus states that although Treasury obligations are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, "circumstances could arise that could prevent the timely payment of interest or principal."
04
Fee against a thin yield. The 0.09% expense ratio is about 2.4% of the gross income at today's yields (0.09 ÷ (3.70 + 0.09), using the SEC yield, which is after fees; our calculation). Over the 60 months to September 2026 the fund's total return compounded to 20.37%, against 21.30% for the 3-month Treasury par yield compounded monthly (our calculation).
05
Tax. Distributions are taxed as income each year in taxable accounts. The issuer's five-year after-tax return on distributions is 2.17% a year against 3.59% before tax (to June 30, 2026), at the highest federal rates.
06
Index and trading quirks. The fund held 22 bills on October 2 against 39 index issues at the last published count (February 28, 2026), and its price can drift from NAV; the prospectus lists authorized-participant concentration, market-trading and securities-lending risks (it may lend up to one-third of its assets).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Short-term rates that stay high or rise: the bills roll over within weeks, so a higher Fed range feeds into income quickly, as after the September 2026 increase. Calm Treasury bill markets with tight spreads (0.01% median on Oct 2). Periods when longer bonds fall as yields rise, since a 0.10-year duration leaves the price almost unchanged. Inflation below the bill yield.

What hurts it

Rate cuts: monthly total return fell from 0.477% (May 2024) to 0.278% (February 2026), a period in which the Fed cut its range six times. Inflation above the bill yield, which leaves a negative real return. Strong stock markets, against which a 3.79% a year return lags (S&P 500 +92.6% over the same window). Any doubt about timely payment by the U.S. Treasury, the fund's only borrower.

Category Commentary
Treasury bill ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

T-bill ETFs hold the shortest U.S. government debt and sit at the low-risk end of the bond-fund range. The large ones are close substitutes; their SEC yields ranged from 3.64% to 3.78% on October 1–2, 2026 (issuer pages). What differs: maturity window (0–3 months for SGOV and VBIL, 1–3 months for BIL, out to one year for SHV, which also holds notes and has a 0.28-year average maturity); index family (ICE for SGOV and SHV, Bloomberg for BIL and VBIL); cost (0.06% for VBIL to 0.15% for SHV); and size, from USD 11.4 billion (VBIL, Aug 31) to USD 113.1 billion (SGOV, Oct 2). Overlap between them is partial: SGOV and BIL share 54.6% by weight, SGOV and SHV 39.4% (our calculation from the October 2 files).

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

The Federal Reserve's policy rate. In this five-year window SGOV's calendar-year NAV return went from 0.02% (2021) to 5.28% (2024) and back to 4.24% (2025) (issuer), following the Fed's target range from 0–0.25% up to 5.25–5.50%, down to 3.50–3.75% and, in September 2026, back up to 3.75–4.00% (Federal Reserve). Price changes matter far less: the fund's deepest daily total-return drawdown in the window was −0.03%. After fees, a T-bill fund returns roughly the bill rate of the past few weeks.
