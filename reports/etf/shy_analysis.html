01 Fund Overview

SHY lends to the U.S. government for one to three years. It holds Treasury notes: government IOUs that pay a fixed coupon every six months and repay their face value at maturity. In the issuer's words, the fund "seeks to track an index that includes U.S. Treasury bonds with remaining maturities between one and three years" and "offers a way to gain targeted exposure to short-term government debt." When a note falls below one year to maturity it leaves the index and newer notes enter it, so the average maturity stays near two years (1.95 years on October 2, 2026, issuer).

The index is the ICE U.S. Treasury 1-3 Year Bond Index. The summary prospectus (June 29, 2026) describes it as publicly issued, fixed-rate, U.S.-dollar Treasury securities with one to three years left to maturity and at least $300 million outstanding, not counting the amount held by the Federal Reserve. It excludes inflation-linked securities, Treasury bills, cash management bills, agency debt, zero-coupon and stripped securities and issues sold mainly to retail investors. It is weighted by market value and updated on the last calendar day of each month, and it had 96 issues on February 28, 2026. On October 2, 2026 the fund had 327,800,000 shares outstanding (issuer holdings file) and about USD 26.56 billion in net assets (shares × NAV of $81.02; our calculation).

Why this fund — launched in 2002, the priciest of the 1–3 year Treasury ETFs compared in section 05
SHY launched on July 22, 2002, before VGSH (Vanguard Short-Term Treasury ETF, November 19, 2009) and SPTS (State Street SPDR Portfolio Short Term Treasury ETF, November 30, 2011) (issuer pages). Its 0.15% expense ratio is five times the 0.03% charged by VGSH, SPTS and SCHO (Schwab Short-Term U.S. Treasury ETF). By size it sits between VGSH, whose ETF shares had USD 34.8B on August 31, 2026 (Vanguard), and SPTS at USD 7.18B on October 2, 2026 (State Street). SHY's own net assets were USD 25.17B on February 28, 2026 (annual report) and USD 25.36B on June 30, 2026 (fact sheet).
Structure
Physical, sampled
The fund owns the notes directly and uses "representative sampling", so it may not hold every index issue. It must keep at least 80% in index securities and at least 90% in U.S. Treasury securities, and may put up to 10% in futures, options and swaps (summary prospectus). On October 2 it held 90 notes; the index had 96 issues at its last published count (Feb 28, 2026).
Currency
U.S. dollars only
Every line in the October 2 holdings file is in U.S. dollars, and the index admits only dollar securities. There is no currency to hedge.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Effective duration 1.84 years Average yield to maturity 4.81% USD 26.56B net assets 0.01% median spread Monthly income Launched 2002

The fund sits one step out from cash. With an effective duration of 1.84 years (issuer, Oct 2, 2026), a one-point rise in yields would cut the price by roughly 1.8%. Notes leave the index once they fall below one year to maturity, at the monthly update (summary prospectus), so coupons and the proceeds of those notes go back in at prevailing yields. The worst calendar year in the window was 2022, when the Fed raised its range from 0–0.25% to 4.25–4.50% and the fund lost 3.90% at NAV (issuer). Over the five years to June 30, 2026 the fund returned 1.78% a year at NAV against 1.90% for its index (issuer).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rising short-term rates. The price falls when one- to three-year yields rise. The 2-year Treasury par yield went from 4.34% on August 31 to 4.88% on September 30, 2026 (U.S. Treasury), and the fund's NAV total return for September was −0.57% (our calculation from the issuer's monthly series), its largest monthly fall since October 2024.
02
Fee against peers. The 0.15% expense ratio compares with 0.03% at VGSH, SCHO and SPTS (section 05). On today's yields it is about 3.2% of the gross income (0.15 ÷ (4.56 + 0.15), using the SEC yield, which is after fees; our calculation).
03
Income follows rates. The prospectus names "income risk": maturing notes are replaced at whatever yields prevail. The monthly distribution was $0.269837 per share in August 2025 and $0.250772 in August 2026, 7.1% lower (issuer; our calculation).
04
Inflation. Coupons and principal are fixed in nominal dollars; the index excludes inflation-linked securities.
05
One borrower. All the notes are owed by the U.S. Treasury. The prospectus states that although Treasury obligations are backed by the full faith and credit of the United States, "circumstances could arise that could prevent the timely payment of interest or principal."
06
Tax and index quirks. Distributions are taxed as income each year in taxable accounts: the five-year after-tax return on distributions was 0.66% a year against 1.78% before tax (issuer, to June 30, 2026). The fund samples 90 of the index's issues, its price can drift from NAV, and the prospectus lists authorized-participant concentration, market-trading and securities-lending risks (it may lend up to one-third of its assets).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling one- to three-year yields: the price rises by roughly 1.8% for each point of decline. In March 2023, when the 2-year par yield fell 0.75 points, the fund returned +1.61%. Starting yields that are high relative to bills, since the coupons then carry more of the return (average yield to maturity 4.81% on October 2, 2026, against a 3-month par yield of 4.19%, U.S. Treasury). Calm Treasury markets with tight spreads (0.01% median on Oct 2).

What hurts it

Rising short-term yields: 2022 cost 3.90% at NAV as the Fed raised its range by 4.25 points, and September 2026 cost 0.57% after a 25-basis-point increase. Inflation above the yield, which leaves a negative real return. Strong stock markets, against which a 1.75% a year return lags (S&P 500 +92.6% over the same window). Any doubt about timely payment by the U.S. Treasury, the fund's only borrower.
