01 Fund Overview

SLV is a share in a vault of silver bars. The iShares Silver Trust owns London Good Delivery silver bars held by JPMorgan in London and New York, and each share is a fractional, undivided interest in that silver. The trust "seeks to reflect generally the performance of the price of silver," before its expenses, and "does not engage in any activities designed to obtain a profit from, or ameliorate losses caused by, changes in the price of silver" (Form 10-Q for the quarter to June 30, 2026). There is no index to replicate and no portfolio manager: the trustee "does not actively manage the silver held by the Trust" and uses no hedging (Form 10-K for 2025).

The shares began trading on April 21, 2006 on the American Stock Exchange and moved to NYSE Arca on December 4, 2008 (10-K). On October 2, 2026 the trust held 15,345.27 tonnes (493,361,870.50 troy ounces) worth $30.11 billion in net assets, against 546,300,000 shares outstanding (iShares product page). One share represented 0.9031 oz of silver: the day's basket of 50,000 shares was 45,153.60 oz. The trustee values the silver once a day at the LBMA Silver Price. Net assets divided by ounces come to $61.03 an ounce, and 0.903072 oz × $61.03 = $55.12, the published NAV per share (our check). The only recurring cost is a sponsor's fee of 0.50% a year, paid by selling a little silver each month.

Structure
Grantor trust, physical silver
The trust "does not hold or trade in commodity futures contracts or any other instruments regulated by the CEA." It "does not lend its silver," and the custodian "does not have the right to lend the Trust's silver" (10-K). SLV is not registered under the Investment Company Act of 1940 and is not a commodity pool under the Commodity Exchange Act, so holders do not get those acts' protections (10-K; fact sheet, June 30, 2026).
How shares are made
Baskets of 50,000
Only Authorized Participants create or redeem shares, in baskets of 50,000, against deliveries of silver. The 10-K names twelve, among them Goldman Sachs & Co., J.P. Morgan Securities, Morgan Stanley & Co., UBS Securities and Virtu Americas. From April to June 2026, 640 baskets were created and 874 redeemed (10-Q).
Distributions
None
The trust declared no cash distributions in 2024 or 2025 and "has no obligation to make periodic distributions" (10-K). It sells silver to pay the sponsor's fee, so "the amount of silver represented by each Share will decrease over the life of the Trust." Currency hedging does not arise: the silver is priced in U.S. dollars and the shares trade in U.S. dollars.
U.S. tax treatment
Collectibles rate, up to 28%
The trust is a grantor trust for U.S. federal income tax, so it pays no tax itself. Gains of individuals on "collectibles, including silver, held for more than one year are taxed at a maximum rate of 28%, rather than the current maximum 20% rate applicable to most other long-term capital gains" (10-K). The trust holds an IRS private letter ruling that buying the shares in an IRA is not the purchase of a collectible (10-K).

If no price is announced, the trustee uses the most recent one (10-Q). The trust has used it since August 15, 2014; before that it used the London Silver Fix (iShares; 10-K).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

$30.1B net assets Physical silver, no futures Silver not lent Bar list published online Annual full count by Bureau Veritas 17.6M shares traded Oct 2

The structure lets an investor hold silver bars through a brokerage account. Custody, trustee, listing and audit costs sit inside the 0.50% fee (10-Q). The bars are listed publicly and counted by an outside inspector. Trading is deep: on October 2, 2026, 17,586,892 shares changed hands, about $963 million at the close (our calculation from the iShares page). The 12 Authorized Participants named in the 10-K can create or redeem baskets every trading day, which ties the share price to the silver behind it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One volatile asset, no income. The share's value is the silver price times a slowly shrinking number of ounces. Five-year volatility of monthly NAV returns was 38.82% a year, more than twice the 17.49% shown for GLD in its report on the same method (section 04 there, August 2021–August 2026). From the January 28, 2026 close of $105.60 to the July 16 close of $50.39, SLV fell 52.3%. One 10-K risk factor reads: "Because the Trust holds only silver, an investment in the Trust may be more volatile than an investment in a more broadly diversified portfolio."
02
Large gaps between close and NAV. NAV uses the noon London auction price, while the shares trade until 4:00 p.m. in New York; the 10-K notes that non-concurrent trading hours and reduced liquidity after the London, Zurich and COMEX markets close can widen the premium or discount. In the iShares daily series from January 2, 2025 to October 2, 2026, the close sat on average 1.45% from NAV, with a range of −19.33% (January 30, 2026) to +8.68% (December 26, 2025); 225 of 438 days were more than 1% away (our calculation).
03
A higher fee than its closest U.S. ETF peer. SLV charges 0.50%. SIVR charges 0.30% under a voluntary waiver of its 0.45% gross ratio, expected to end on February 28, 2027 (SIVR fact sheet). The fee is paid in silver, so it reduces ounces per share every year (section 05).
04
One custodian, English law. All the silver is with JPMorgan. The trust "does not insure its silver" and "is not a beneficiary" of the custodian's insurance; the custodian's liability is limited to negligence, fraud or willful default. The custody agreement is governed by English law, and it "may be difficult or impossible for the Trust to sue the Custodian" in a U.S. court (10-K).
05
Creation can stall when deliverable silver is short. The 10-K warns that "the demand for silver may temporarily exceed available supply that is acceptable for delivery to the Trust," in which case "the Trust may suspend or restrict the issuance of Baskets," with possible deviations from NAV. Since March 7, 2022 new bars from six suspended Russian refiners are no longer acceptable as Good Delivery (10-K).
06
Tax and holder rights. Long-term gains for U.S. individuals are taxed at the collectibles rate of up to 28%. Shareholders do not elect directors or vote as corporate shareholders do, and the sponsor and trustee "may agree to amend the Trust Agreement, including to increase the Sponsor's fees, without Shareholder consent" after 30 days' notice (10-K).

Monthly returns rt = Vt/Vt−1 − 1 on the iShares growth-of-$10,000 NAV series at each month-end, September 30, 2021 to September 30, 2026 (60 returns); SLV pays no distributions, so NAV change is total return. Over these five years silver beat U.S. stocks by a wide margin: SLV's price return was 75 percentage points above the S&P 500's total return. SLV's NAV trailed the benchmark by 0.61 points a year, close to the 0.50% fee. The share price trailed the NAV further because the close and NAV are set at different times: on September 30, 2026 the close was 0.61% below NAV (iShares series). The two assets moved largely independently: the monthly correlation with the S&P 500 total return was 0.20 and the beta 0.48 (section 04). The window ends after a large silver rally and a large fall; the 2022 slide and the 2026 crash show how much the starting and ending dates matter.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

A rising silver price in U.S. dollars, since NAV is ounces per share times the LBMA Silver Price. The trust's 10-K lists what moves that price: economic conditions (silver is "used in a wide range of industrial applications"), global supply and demand from jewelry, technology and industry, bar and coin buying, producer hedging, central bank purchases and sales, production in countries such as China, Mexico and Peru, inflation expectations, interest rates, and trading by hedge funds and commodity funds. Periods when stocks and silver part ways also favour it within a portfolio: SLV's five-year correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.20. Trading is deep: about $963M changed hands on October 2, and the 30-day median bid/ask spread was 0.02% (iShares page, October 2, 2026).

What hurts it

A falling silver price, which passes straight through: −52.3% on daily closes from January 28 to July 16, 2026, and −27.5% on month-end closes in 2022. There is no income to cushion a decline. A recession can cut industrial demand, and the 10-K names a rise in producer hedging and a turn in speculators' views as other causes of price falls. The 0.50% fee removes about half a percent of the ounces behind each share every year. Because NAV is struck at noon in London and the shares trade until 4 p.m. in New York, a buyer or seller can deal at a price well away from NAV on a volatile day, as on January 30, 2026 (−19.33%).

Category Commentary
Physically backed silver funds
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These funds turn silver bars in a vault into exchange-listed shares. The three U.S.-dollar funds compared here held $46.90 billion of net assets on October 2, 2026 (our sum of the sponsors' figures), and SLV is 64.2% of that. They differ in four ways. Fee: from 0.30% (SIVR, under a waiver) to 0.52% (PSLV), with SLV at 0.50%. Structure: SLV and SIVR are grantor trusts that create and redeem shares daily, which keeps their prices close to NAV; PSLV is a closed-end trust, and its units closed 5.00% below NAV on October 2. Custody: JPMorgan in London and New York for SLV; ICBC Standard in London for SIVR; the Royal Canadian Mint for PSLV. Trading: on October 2, dollar volume was about $963M in SLV, $224M in SIVR and $152M in PSLV (shares × close, our calculation).

Returns look alike because the silver is the same
WHAT DIFFERS

With one asset underneath, the long-run gap between these funds is close to the gap in fees. Over five years to June 30, 2026, SLV's NAV returned 17.35% a year and SIVR's 17.59%, against 17.93% for the LBMA Silver Price in the iShares table (17.94% in abrdn's; both as of the same date). The 0.24-point gap between them is close to their 0.20-point fee difference. Silver behaves differently from gold: its five-year volatility (38.82%) is more than twice the 17.49% in the GLD report, and the 10-K stresses its use "in a wide range of industrial applications," so economic slowdowns weigh on demand. Flows into SLV swing with the price: 8,232 baskets were created and 6,752 redeemed in 2025, then 2,698 created and 3,751 redeemed in the first half of 2026 (10-K; 10-Q).
