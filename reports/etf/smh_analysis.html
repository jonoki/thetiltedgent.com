01 Fund Overview

SMH puts 25 of the largest chip companies listed on U.S. exchanges into one share: chip designers led by NVIDIA, the Taiwanese contract manufacturer TSMC, the makers of the machines that build chips, and the memory producers. VanEck describes the fund as seeking to replicate, before fees and expenses, the MVIS® US Listed Semiconductor 25 Index, "which is intended to track the overall performance of companies involved in semiconductor production and equipment" (fact sheet, September 30, 2026). The index admits only companies that earn at least half their revenue from semiconductors or chip-making equipment (a quarter for current members), and caps how much any one of them can weigh (section 02).

The fund began on December 20, 2011 and is a series of VanEck ETF Trust. Net assets were $66.44 billion on October 30, 2026 (VanEck fund page). The expense ratio is 0.35%, all of it management fee: under a unitary fee the adviser pays the fund's other expenses, with stated exceptions such as trading costs, taxes and extraordinary expenses, and has agreed to pay offering costs until at least February 1, 2027 (fact sheet). Under the 2023 split, each share became two from May 5, 2023 (Yahoo Finance split record for SMH); all prices on this page are adjusted for it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One industry. Every holding is a chip or chip-equipment company; Information Technology is 95.55% under GICS, with SK hynix unclassified. A downturn in chip demand or prices hits the whole fund at once. The prospectus lists semiconductor industry risk and index-related concentration risk among its principal risks.
02
One company near a fifth. NVIDIA is 20.19% and the top ten 69.05% (October 30). The index lets its largest member reach 20% at each rebalance, and the fund is non-diversified.
03
Taiwan and foreign listings. 21.37% sits in six companies based outside the U.S., including 9.58% in Taiwan Semiconductor; the prospectus names Taiwanese issuers, emerging-market issuers, foreign currency and depositary receipts among its principal risks.

05
Valuation of what it owns. The holdings traded at a price/earnings ratio of 37.21x and price/book of 11.12x (fact sheet, September 30, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets.
06
Index owner in the same group. The index belongs to MarketVector Indexes GmbH, a wholly owned VanEck subsidiary, which can change the rulebook; changes are approved under its own governance process (Index Guide, section 5.5). Version 1.01 in May 2025 adjusted the weighting scheme.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising spending on chips and on the factories that make them: data-center processors and accelerators drive the design segment (41.08%), new factory capacity drives the equipment makers (17.65%), and memory prices drive Micron and SK hynix (8.89%). Strength in NVIDIA in particular, at 20.19% of the fund. Periods in which investors pay more for growth: the holdings already trade at 37.21x earnings. Broad stock-market rallies, which the fund has amplified, with a beta of 1.78 over five years.

What hurts it

A turn in the chip cycle: excess inventory, falling memory prices or cuts to factory spending. A sell-off in AI-related stocks, given the weight of NVIDIA and the other data-center suppliers. Export restrictions, tariffs or tension around Taiwan, where the fund's second-largest holding is based. A contraction in valuations from 37.21x earnings. The fund's own record: −45.3% in 2022 at the daily trough, −35.7% from July 2024 to April 2025 and −24.6% in June–July 2026.

Top 3 Risks

01
Single-industry cycle risk. All 25 holdings depend on demand for chips and chip-making equipment. Five-year volatility is 35.55% a year, more than twice the S&P 500's, and the fund has had three falls of more than 24% from a daily peak inside this five-year window.
02
Concentration in a few names. NVIDIA is 20.19% and the top ten 69.05%; the index allows its largest member up to 20% at each rebalance, and the fund is non-diversified. The results of a handful of companies decide most of the fund's return.
03
Geography and supply chain. Taiwan Semiconductor (9.58%) is the fund's second-largest holding, and 21.37% of the fund is in companies based outside the U.S. The prospectus names Taiwanese issuers, emerging-market issuers and depositary receipts among its principal risks.
