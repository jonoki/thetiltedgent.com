01 Fund Overview

TIP holds U.S. Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities, known as TIPS: government bonds whose principal rises and falls with consumer prices. Its summary prospectus says the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of inflation-protected U.S. Treasury bonds." That index is the ICE U.S. Treasury Inflation Linked Bond Index. It holds TIPS with more than one year left to maturity and at least $300 million outstanding, not counting the Federal Reserve's holdings. Each bond is weighted by that outstanding amount, and the index is rebalanced on the last calendar day of each month (summary prospectus, February 27, 2026).

How a single TIPS works, from TreasuryDirect: the principal "goes up with inflation and down with deflation." Interest is paid every six months at a fixed rate on the adjusted principal, so the dollar amount of each payment moves with it. At maturity the holder gets the inflation-adjusted principal or the original amount, "whichever is greater." The Treasury sells TIPS with 5-, 10- and 30-year terms. iShares notes that the index used for the adjustment is the non-seasonally-adjusted Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U).

Real yield vs nominal yield — October 2, 2026
A nominal yield is what a regular Treasury pays in dollars. A real yield is what a TIPS pays on top of inflation. On the U.S. Treasury's daily par yield curves for October 2, 2026, the 10-year nominal yield was 5.28% and the 10-year real yield 2.92%. The gap, 2.36 points, is the 10-year breakeven inflation rate (our calculation). iShares describes breakeven as "the level of inflation required for investors to be indifferent" between the two bonds. If CPI inflation over the next ten years averages more than about 2.36% a year, a 10-year TIPS held to maturity ends up ahead of the regular note; if it averages less, it ends up behind. The 5-year breakeven that day was 2.37% (5.06% − 2.69%). For the fund as a whole, iShares quotes a real yield of 2.82% and a weighted average yield to maturity of 5.10% (October 2), a gap of 2.28 points.

How CPI shows up in the distributions

A TIPS owner who holds the bond directly receives the inflation adjustment only at maturity, as a larger principal. The fund works differently. iShares' "Mechanics of TIPS" brief says TIPS ETFs "pay out the earned income of the portfolio, including an inflation adjustment based on two months' prior CPI applied to the fund's underlying securities." It gives the monthly distribution as:

  •   Accrued coupon interest
  • − Premium amortization
  • + Discount accretion
  • − Net expenses
  • ± Inflation adjustment
  • = ETF earned income

The brief's timing example: January CPI is measured over January, released around February 20, applied day by day in March, and paid out in the distribution of April 1. When CPI falls, the inflation adjustment is negative and can cancel the coupon income. In the brief's words: "If there is deflation, the TIP ETF might omit or not pay a monthly distribution." For taxes, the principal adjustment "is classified as Treasury income and is distributed by the fund even though it is not distributed to individual TIPS security holders (known as phantom income)," so the cash arrives in the same year as the tax bill.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

100% U.S. Treasury Principal indexed to CPI-U 48 TIPS, 2027–2056 Inflation paid out monthly 5-yr NAV 0.83% vs index 1.00% Median bid/ask 0.01%

The fund turns a set of semi-annual-coupon government bonds into one exchange-traded share that pays monthly and passes through the inflation adjustment as cash. It has tracked its index within roughly its fee: over the five years to June 30, 2026 the NAV returned 0.83% a year against 1.00% for the index, and over ten years 2.41% against 2.57% (issuer). On October 2, 2026 the shares closed at a 0.09% premium to NAV, and the 30-day median bid/ask spread was 0.01% (issuer).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Real interest rates. Effective duration is 6.16 years (October 2, 2026). The prospectus says the value of these bonds falls "when real interest rates rise." In September 2026 the 10-year real par yield rose from 2.44% to 2.93% (U.S. Treasury) and TIP's month-end price fell 2.6%, from $106.82 to $104.04.
02
Inflation protection is not price protection. In 2022 CPI-U rose 6.5% (December to December, BLS; our calculation), yet TIP's NAV return for the year was −12.13% (issuer). Over the same year the 10-year real par yield went from −1.04% to 1.58% (U.S. Treasury, December 31, 2021 and December 30, 2022). iShares' TIPS brief notes the same 2022 split between CPI and TIPS returns.
03
Uneven income. Five of the nine ex-dates from February to October 2026 paid nothing (issuer's table). A falling CPI, as in June 2026 (−0.35%), turns the inflation adjustment negative, and the issuer warns the fund "might omit or not pay a monthly distribution."
04
No floor at the fund level. The Treasury guarantees each TIPS' original principal at maturity, but the fund's principal is not protected. The prospectus adds that a TIPS bought after its principal has been adjusted upward can pay back less than its purchase cost if inflation then falls.
05
Cost. The 0.18% expense ratio is six times the 0.03% of SCHP and STIP (section 05), and TIP and SPIP hold the same 48 bonds (90.2% overlap by weight, October 2, 2026).
06
Tax. The inflation adjustment is paid out as income, and the summary prospectus says distributions "may be taxable to you as ordinary income or capital gains" outside tax-deferred accounts. iShares' own five-year after-tax figure (to June 30, 2026) is −0.79% a year against 0.83% before tax.

Calculation note. Monthly returns rt = Vt/Vt−1 − 1 on the issuer's "growth of $10,000" NAV series (distributions reinvested, fees deducted), September 30, 2021 to September 30, 2026 (60 returns). That series reproduces the issuer's published returns to June 30, 2026 (1 year 3.20%, 3 years 3.81%, 5 years 0.83%). Volatility = stdev(r) × √12 (sample standard deviation). Risk-free rate: U.S. Treasury daily par yield curve, 3-month rate on the last business day of the prior month, ÷ 12. Sharpe = mean(e) ÷ stdev(e) × √12, where et = rt − rf,t (sample standard deviation of the excess returns). Beta = cov(r, rm) ÷ var(rm) and correlation = cov(r, rm) ÷ (sd(r) · sd(rm)), where rm is the monthly return of Yahoo Finance's ^SP500TR on the same month-ends. On Yahoo's dividend-adjusted market-price series instead, the results are volatility 6.11%, Sharpe −0.64, beta 0.27, correlation 0.69. All in USD.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling real yields: with a 6.16-year effective duration, a one-point drop adds roughly 6% to the price. Inflation above the breakeven rate (2.36% for 10 years on October 2, 2026), which raises the bonds' principal and the monthly payouts while a regular Treasury's coupons stay fixed. Rising monthly CPI readings, which show up in the distributions about three months later. Stable real yields, in which case the fund earns roughly its real yield of 2.82% plus inflation, before the 0.18% fee.

What hurts it

Rising real yields, as in 2022 and again in September 2026, when the 10-year real yield rose 0.49 points and the price fell 2.6%. Inflation below the breakeven rate, which leaves TIPS behind regular Treasuries of the same maturity. Months of falling CPI, which can reduce a distribution to zero (September and October 2026). Rising yields paired with falling stocks, which removes the diversification benefit (five-year correlation with the S&P 500: 0.68).

01
Real-rate risk. Effective duration 6.16 years. From November 9, 2021 to October 6, 2023 the total return fell 14.5% while the 10-year real yield rose from −1.17% to 2.47% (U.S. Treasury).
02
Income that follows CPI both ways. Five of nine 2026 ex-dates through October paid nothing. Investors who need steady monthly cash get the inflation adjustment as it accrues, including the months when it is negative.
03
Diversification can fail. The five-year correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.68 and the Sharpe ratio −0.65 over the same window. When real yields rise quickly, TIPS and stocks can fall together, as in 2022.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

TIPS were $2,165 billion of marketable U.S. government debt on June 30, 2026, 6.96% of the total (U.S. Treasury Monthly Statement of the Public Debt, as tabulated in iShares' TIPS brief). Every full-range TIPS ETF draws on the same few dozen bonds: TIP and SPIP held the same 48 on October 2, 2026, and SCHP reported 49 on August 31. What differs is cost (0.03% for SCHP against 0.18% for TIP), index provider (ICE for TIP; Bloomberg for SCHP and SPIP, with slightly different size rules: $300 million for TIP's index against $500 million for the two Bloomberg indexes) and maturity range. Short-maturity funds such as STIP (effective duration 2.27 years) keep most of the inflation link and less of the real-rate risk.

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

Two numbers set a TIPS fund's return: realised inflation, which arrives through the principal adjustment and the monthly payouts, and the change in real yields, which moves the price. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 they offset almost exactly: CPI-U rose about 22%, the 10-year real yield rose 3.77 points, and TIP's total return was −0.9%. The starting point is now different. The 10-year real yield was 2.92% on October 2, 2026; since November 24, 2008 only one daily reading has been higher, 2.93% on September 30, 2026. The 10-year breakeven inflation rate that day was 2.36%.
