01 Fund Overview

TLT holds one kind of asset: long-dated bonds of the U.S. government. In the words of its prospectus, it "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of U.S. Treasury bonds with remaining maturities greater than twenty years." The issuer's own summary is that the fund "offers a way to gain targeted exposure to long duration government debt." Every bond in it pays a fixed coupon in U.S. dollars and is repaid in 2046 or later, apart from a handful of near-zero leftovers.

That makes TLT one of the most rate-sensitive funds in the U.S. bond market. Its effective duration was 14.68 years on October 2, 2026 (issuer), so a one-point rise in long-term yields cuts the price by roughly 15%, and a one-point fall adds roughly the same, before income. The fund's assets were USD 47.20 billion that day, and the expense ratio is 0.15% (summary prospectus, June 29, 2026).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

USD 47.20B net assets 100% U.S. Treasuries 0.01% median bid/ask 61.4M shares traded Oct 2 Monthly income SEC yield 5.56%

The fund is a liquid, single-purpose tool: it gives pure exposure to long-term U.S. government interest rates with no company credit risk. Its NAV return has trailed the index by 0.09 to 0.13 points a year over one, three, five and ten years: five-year NAV return −6.66% a year against −6.57% for the index, and ten-year −1.93% against −1.83% (issuer, to June 30, 2026). On October 2 the market price ($77.48) sat 0.07% above NAV ($77.42) (issuer). Net share creations continued as prices fell: shares outstanding rose from 116.4 million on September 30, 2021 to 609.6 million on October 2, 2026 (issuer's daily history), more than five times as many.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest rates, at long duration. Effective duration is 14.68 years (Oct 2, 2026): a one-point rise in long yields cuts the price by roughly 15%. In September 2026 the 20-year Treasury par yield rose from 5.24% (Aug 31) to 5.68% (Sep 30) (U.S. Treasury), and the fund's NAV total return for the month was −5.27% (issuer).
02
Inflation. The index excludes inflation-linked bonds, so every coupon is fixed in dollars for 20 to 30 years. Higher inflation lowers the real value of that income and tends to push long yields up, which cuts the price.
03
Low-coupon bonds. 21.85% of the fund is in bonds paying under 2.5%, priced near half of face value with a 16.4-year duration (our weighting of the issuer file). They react more to rate changes than the rest of the portfolio.
04
One issuer. All 47 bonds are obligations of the U.S. Treasury. The prospectus notes that "changes in the U.S. government's financial condition or credit rating may cause the value of U.S. Treasury obligations to decline." The Treasury also keeps adding to the supply: it sells new 30-year bonds each quarter and reopens them in the other months, with a USD 22 billion reopening set for October 8, 2026 (TreasuryDirect).
05
Cost relative to peers. The 0.15% expense ratio is five times the 0.03% charged by VGLT and SPTL, which hold a wider 10-years-and-over set of Treasuries (section 05).
06
Diversification against stocks is not guaranteed. Over the 60 months to September 2026 the correlation of the fund's NAV returns with the S&P 500 was +0.56, and the fund rose in only 4 of the 23 months in which the S&P 500 total return fell (our calculation). In 2022 both fell: the fund −31.41% (issuer) and the S&P 500 total return −18.1% (our calculation from Yahoo ^SP500TR).

The 2022 drawdown

2022 was the fund's worst calendar year in the issuer's monthly NAV record, which starts in August 2002 (our compounding; the next worst was 2009 at −21.56%): NAV total return −31.41%, index −30.76% (issuer). The price went from $148.19 on December 31, 2021 to $99.56 on December 30, 2022, down 32.8%. Over the same year the 3-month Treasury yield rose from 0.06% to 4.42% as the Federal Reserve raised rates, and the 20-year yield from 1.94% to 4.14% (U.S. Treasury). 08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling long-term yields: with a 14.68-year duration, a one-point drop adds roughly 15% to the price, on top of income, as in November 2023 (+9.88% on NAV in one month). Stable yields: the fund then earns roughly its yield (SEC yield 5.56%, yield to maturity 5.69%). Slowing inflation, recessions and sell-offs in which investors move into Treasuries. Stronger confidence in U.S. government finances.

What hurts it

Rising long-term yields, as in 2022 (−31.41% NAV) and September 2026 (−5.27% NAV as the 20-year yield rose 44 basis points). Inflation above expectations, since no holding is inflation-protected. Rising yields paired with falling stocks, which removes the diversification benefit (five-year correlation with the S&P 500: +0.56). Heavier Treasury borrowing or doubts about U.S. finances that lift the yield investors demand on long bonds.

Category Commentary
Long-term U.S. Treasury ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These funds hold the far end of the Treasury market and give exposure to long-term U.S. interest rates with no company credit risk. They differ in three ways. Maturity cut-off and duration: TLT takes only bonds with more than 20 years left (duration 14.68 years); SPTL and VGLT take 10 years and over (13.56 and 13.5 years); EDV holds zero-coupon STRIPS of 20 to 30 years (24.0 years), so it moves most for a given change in yields. Cost: 0.15% for TLT against 0.03% to 0.05% for the others (issuer prospectus figures). Size and trading: TLT's USD 47.20 billion is more than the other three combined, and 61.4 million TLT shares traded on October 2 (issuer). Yields were close on the banner date: SEC yields of 5.56% to 5.63% for the coupon-bond funds and 5.97% for EDV (each issuer, October 1–2, 2026).

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

With all four funds holding the same issuer, returns are set almost entirely by the level and change of 20- to 30-year Treasury yields. The last five years show how much the starting yield matters: TLT began the window with the 20-year yield at 2.02% and returned −8.44% a year on NAV to September 30, 2026 (our compounding of issuer data). Today it starts from a 30-day SEC yield of 5.56% and a 20-year par yield of 5.67%, 0.01 point below the September 30 reading of 5.68%, the highest since June 2002 (U.S. Treasury). Income now covers more of a given price move, but at a 14.68-year duration the price swings still dominate over any one year.
