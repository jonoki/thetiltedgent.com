01 Fund Overview

TQQQ is a fund built to move three times as much as the Nasdaq-100 on any single day. In the words of its September 28, 2026 summary prospectus, it "seeks daily investment results, before fees and expenses, that correspond to three times (3x) the daily performance of the Nasdaq-100® Index." The same document adds the key limit: the fund "does not seek to achieve three times (3x) the daily performance of the Index (the "Daily Target") for any period other than a day." The index, in the prospectus's words, "is designed to measure the performance of 100 of the largest Nasdaq-listed non-financial companies," and its members are listed on Nasdaq-affiliated U.S. exchanges, "which include both U.S. and non-U.S. companies."

The fund does not borrow money in the usual sense. It holds some of the index's stocks directly and gets the rest of its exposure from total return swaps with ten banks and from Nasdaq-100 futures. On October 30, 2026 that exposure added up to 300.02% of net assets (our sum of the issuer's holdings; detail in section 02). Launched February 9, 2010, it had $31.82 billion of net assets on November 2, 2026 (issuer NAV history: 536.00 million shares × $59.3581). The expense ratio is 0.78% after a fee waiver that runs to September 30, 2027, and 0.94% before it.

How the daily reset works

"The Fund seeks to rebalance its portfolio each day so that its exposure to the Index is consistent with the Daily Target," the prospectus says, and "a day is measured from the time of one net asset value ("NAV") calculation to the next." After a rise, net assets are larger, so the fund adds exposure. After a fall, it cuts exposure. Because the multiple resets every day, the fund's return over weeks or years is the product of its daily returns, not three times the index's return for the whole period. The prospectus puts it plainly: "You may lose money when the return of the Index is flat (i.e., close to zero) and you may lose money when the Index rises." Key Vulnerabilities

01
Holding period and volatility decay. Over any period longer than a day the return is not 3x the index's. Over the five years to November 2, 2026, QQQ returned +81.75% with dividends and TQQQ +61.17%, about 0.75 times, not 3 times (our calculation, section 07). The prospectus lists this as "Holding Period Risk."
02
Deep drawdowns. From November 19, 2021 to December 28, 2022 the fund's total return fell 81.7% on daily data while QQQ's fell 35.1% over the same dates. A one-day index fall approaching 33% could wipe out the investment (prospectus).
03
Costs beyond the expense ratio. The prospectus: "The cost of obtaining this leverage will lower your returns," and the fund "pays transaction and financing costs associated with the purchase and sale of securities and derivatives. These costs are not reflected in the table or the example above." Swaps supply 80.0% of the exposure (our calculation: 239.88 ÷ 300.02), and the prospectus lists "financing costs associated with the use of derivatives" among the factors that "will adversely impact the Fund's ability to meet its Daily Target."
04
Counterparty concentration. Ten banks carry swap exposure equal to 239.88% of net assets; the largest five, 132.06%. The prospectus warns that counterparty risk "may increase during periods of financial market stress," and that a terminated swap may not be replaceable.
05
One concentrated index. The Nasdaq-100 holds about 100 non-financial companies and, per the prospectus, had "a significant portion of its value in issuers in the information technology industry group" at May 31, 2026. In the fund's own stock sleeve Apple, NVIDIA and Microsoft are 22.51% (our sum).
06
Buying during the day. "When shares are bought intraday, the performance of the Fund's shares relative to the Index until the Fund's next NAV calculation time will generally be higher or lower than the Daily Target" (prospectus, "Intraday Price Performance Risk"). The 3x applies from one 4:00 p.m. NAV to the next.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Steady rises in the Nasdaq-100 with small daily swings: the prospectus table shows a fund return above 3x the index's when gains are large and volatility low (index +20% at 10% volatility gives +67.7%, above +60%). Long trends in one direction, as in April–May 2026 (+52.4% and +33.1% in two months). Lower short-term interest rates, which reduce the financing cost of the leverage. Strength in the large technology companies that dominate the index.

What hurts it

Falling markets, which hit three times as hard each day: −79.03% in 2022. Choppy, sideways markets, where the daily reset loses money even if the index ends flat (index 0% at 25% volatility gives −17.1% in the prospectus table). High volatility of any kind. Higher short-term interest rates, which raise the cost of the swaps. A very large one-day fall in the index: near 33%, the investment could be wiped out. Stress at the banks on the other side of the swaps.

Top 3 Risks

01
Path dependence. The return over any period other than a day depends on the order and size of the daily moves. Over the last five years the daily reset turned a possible +500% (no swings) into +171% before costs, and the fund delivered +61.2%.
02
Loss of most of the investment. −81.7% from November 2021 to December 2022, and falls of 58.0%, 37.0% and 33.6% since, all within five years. Five-year volatility of 63.95% a year (our calculation).
03
Derivatives and counterparties. 84.1% of the exposure comes from swaps and futures; ten banks carry swaps equal to 239.88% of net assets. If a bank fails or closes out a swap, the fund may be unable to keep its 3x exposure (prospectus, "Swaps Risk" and "Counterparty Risk").
Category Commentary
Daily-reset leveraged ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These funds promise a multiple of an index's return for one day only, reset every day using swaps and futures. ProShares runs a ladder on the Nasdaq-100: QLD at 2x ($12.98 billion), TQQQ at 3x ($31.82 billion) and SQQQ at −3x ($2.05 billion) on November 2, 2026 (issuer NAV files). TQQQ alone has more than twice QLD's assets. The ProShares fund page tells holders that returns over longer periods "may be higher or lower than the Daily Target" and suggests monitoring positions; the prospectus says the fund "may not be suitable for all investors." Costs run higher than for index funds: 0.78%–0.94% a year for these ProShares funds against 0.18% for QQQ, before financing.

What five years show about the multiple
WHAT DIFFERS

From October 29, 2021 to November 2, 2026 the 2x QLD returned +92.38% and the 3x TQQQ +61.17%: the extra leverage cost 31.2 points while deepening the worst fall from −63.7% to −81.7% (our calculations). The −3x SQQQ lost 93.44% while the Nasdaq-100 price index rose 76.59%. Each fund did what its prospectus describes, one day at a time; over five years the results were set by the path of daily returns and the cost of the leverage rather than by the multiple alone.
