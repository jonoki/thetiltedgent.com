01 Fund Overview

USMV holds U.S. stocks chosen and weighted to make the whole basket swing less than the market. In the words of its November 28, 2025 summary prospectus, the fund "seeks to track the investment results of an index composed of U.S. equities that, in the aggregate, have lower volatility characteristics relative to the broader U.S. equity market." That index is the MSCI USA Minimum Volatility (USD) Index. MSCI builds it by running the 513 large and mid-sized stocks of the MSCI USA Index (its "parent") through a computer optimiser that searches for the mix with the lowest expected volatility, within a set of limits described in section 02.

The result is not a list of the calmest stocks. It is a portfolio of 174 stocks (October 2, 2026) whose combined price moves are expected to be smallest, because the optimiser also counts how stocks move together. Launched on October 18, 2011, the fund had $23.35 billion in net assets on October 2, 2026 ($23,353,178,061, issuer). Its expense ratio is 0.15%, all of it management fee: under the advisory agreement, BlackRock Fund Advisors pays the fund's other operating expenses except items such as interest, taxes, trading costs and extraordinary expenses (prospectus).

Structure
Physical, representative sampling
The prospectus says the adviser "uses a representative sampling indexing strategy": the fund "may or may not hold all of the components" of the index. It invests at least 80% of assets in index stocks and may use up to 20% for futures, options, swaps, cash and stocks outside the index. On October 2, 2026 it held 174 stocks against the index's 173 at September 30 (MSCI).
Currency
USD, no hedging needed
All 174 stock lines in the October 2 holdings file carry the location "United States" and trade in U.S. dollars. For an investor who spends another currency, the U.S. dollar's moves pass straight through.

How the index picks and weights stocks

From MSCI's Minimum Volatility Indexes Methodology (July 2025 edition). MSCI describes the aim as an index "that has the lowest absolute volatility for a given set of constraints," using an estimated co-variance matrix (how each stock's price moves relative to every other's) from its Barra equity risk model and the Barra Open Optimizer. No return forecasts are used, and short selling is not allowed.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
It lags in strong rallies. Calendar 2023: fund +10.21%, MSCI USA Index +27.10%. April 2026: fund +2.05% in total return, S&P 500 +10.49% (our calculation). The prospectus names this as "Low Volatility Risk." Over the full five years the fund's total return was less than half the S&P 500's (section 07).
02
It depends on a risk model. The weights come from Barra's estimates of how stocks will move together, based on past data. When those relationships change, the portfolio can be more volatile than intended; the prospectus says the fund "may experience more than minimum volatility."
03
Sector tilts. Health care, consumer staples and utilities are each about 4.5–5 points above their parent-index weights, and communication services and technology about 5–6 points below (MSCI, September 30, 2026). Months when those sectors move against the market move the fund away from it: in September 2026 the fund's total return was −3.70% against −0.35% for the S&P 500 (our calculation).
04
Still a stock fund. Lower volatility is not low risk. The fund fell 17.9% from December 2021 to October 2022 in daily total return, and the prospectus shows a −17.20% quarter (March 2020).
05
Turnover and its costs. Portfolio turnover was 22% of the average value of the portfolio in the fiscal year in the prospectus. The prospectus notes that transaction costs are not reflected in the expense ratio but affect the fund's performance.
06
Valuation of what it owns. The issuer reports a price/earnings ratio of 25.17 and price/book of 4.29 for the holdings (October 2, 2026). Defensive stocks are not automatically cheap.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling or choppy markets: in the 23 months the S&P 500 fell over the last five years, the fund lost 2.44% on average against 3.39% (our calculation). Sell-offs led by the largest technology and internet companies, which it holds at well below their market weight. Periods when investors favour the steadier businesses it overweights: health care, consumer staples and utilities. Its low cost of trading (0.01% median spread) and close tracking of the index mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Fast, broad rallies, especially ones led by the most volatile or the largest stocks: 2023 (+10.21% against +27.10% for the MSCI USA Index) and April 2026 (+2.05% against +10.49%). Months when its favoured sectors fall while the rest of the market holds, as in September 2026 (−3.70% against −0.35%). Deep bear markets still hurt: −17.9% at the 2022 trough. Shifts in how stocks move together, which the risk model learns only after the fact.

Top 3 Risks

01
Giving up return in rising markets. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 the fund's total return was +44.90% against +92.58% for the S&P 500. The prospectus states that a low-volatility strategy "may underperform the broader market during periods of strong, rising or speculative stock prices."
02
Equity market risk remains. A beta of 0.64 still means losses in a falling market: −17.9% daily peak to trough in 2022, and a −17.20% quarter in March 2020 (prospectus). Five-year volatility is 12.28% a year (our calculation).
03
Model and sector risk. The portfolio rests on a risk model's forecast of how 513 stocks will move together, and it leans toward a few sectors within ±5 points of the market's weights. When volatility or correlations shift, the fund can be more volatile than intended or move against the market, as it did in September 2026.
Category Commentary
Three ways to build a low-volatility fund
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Optimise the whole portfolio (USMV): MSCI's optimiser uses a risk model to find the lowest-volatility mix of parent-index stocks, within limits on stock, sector and style weights. Rank and weight stocks (SPLV, LGLV): Invesco's SPLV page says its S&P 500 Low Volatility Index "consists of the 100 securities from the S&P 500® Index with the lowest realized volatility over the past 12 months" and is "rebalanced and reconstituted quarterly in February, May, August and November"; State Street says LGLV's index starts from the largest 1,000 U.S. stocks and gives the highest weights to the least volatile, subject to liquidity limits. Active management (VFMV): Vanguard lists the fund as actively managed. The method shapes what each fund owns.

What the sector limit changes
WHAT DIFFERS

SPLV's index picks stocks by their own past volatility, rather than optimising a whole portfolio within sector limits as USMV's does. In SPLV's October 2, 2026 file, real estate investment trusts are 16.82% of assets (17 holdings, our sum), and its ten largest holdings were Berkshire Hathaway, Evergy, WEC Energy, Regency Centers, Duke Energy, FirstEnergy, Atmos Energy, Loews, Pinnacle West Capital and Alliant Energy. USMV's sector limit keeps it much closer to the market: 34.12% in technology and 2.54% in real estate. That difference is why USMV and SPLV share only 28.6% of their weight (our calculation), even though both aim at the same thing.
