01 Fund Overview

VBAL is a whole balanced portfolio in one ticker: roughly 60% stocks and 40% bonds, spread across Canada, the United States, other developed markets and emerging markets. It does not pick securities itself. It holds seven other Vanguard ETFs, four stock funds and three bond funds. Its objective, in the issuer's words: "Vanguard Balanced ETF Portfolio seeks to provide long-term capital growth with a moderate level of income by investing in equity and fixed income securities." The fund page adds that the sub-advisor "will strive to maintain a long-term strategic asset allocation of equity (approximately 60%) and fixed income (approximately 40%) securities," and that the mix "may be reconstituted and rebalanced from time to time at the discretion of the sub-advisor."

Launched January 25, 2018 (TSX listing February 1, 2018), it had CAD 5,886 million of net assets on August 31, 2026 (fact sheet). The issuer lists its benchmark as an "Internal Composite" rather than a published index; the indices sit one level down, in the underlying funds. The management fee is 0.17%, cut from 0.22% on November 18, 2025. The MER in the latest annual Management Report of Fund Performance (MRFP), for the year to March 31, 2026, is 0.22%. That year includes about seven and a half months at the old 0.22% fee (see section 04).

Structure
Fund of funds
Seven Canada-listed Vanguard index ETFs. The annual MRFP explains how fees avoid doubling up: VBAL's management fee "is reduced by the aggregate of the management fee payable by the underlying Canada-domiciled Vanguard Funds" and certain of their expenses. Portfolio turnover was 7.61% in the year to March 31, 2026.
Currency hedging
Bonds hedged, stocks not
The two foreign bond funds VBAL holds are CAD-hedged versions (VBG and VBU, both "CAD-hedged" in their names). The three foreign stock funds (VUN, VIU, VEE) are not; Vanguard's prospectus also lists CAD-hedged versions of VUN and VIU, which VBAL does not hold. Those three unhedged stock funds were 43.07% of the fund on August 31, 2026 (27.37% + 11.05% + 4.65%).

Quarterly

The ETF Facts note that in a non-registered account distributions are taxable "whether you get them in cash or have them reinvested."

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

13,721 stocks + 17,275 bonds ~60/40 mix kept by the sub-advisor Mgmt fee 0.17%, no fee duplication CAD 5.9B net assets Avg bid-ask spread 0.040% No trailing commission

The structure takes decisions away from the holder. Building the same mix from seven funds would mean seven trades, seven sets of distributions and periodic rebalancing between stocks and bonds by hand; VBAL does it inside the fund for one MER. Trading is liquid: in the twelve months to May 31, 2026 the ETF Facts report an average daily volume of 412,049 units, trading on 250 days, and an average bid-ask spread of 0.040%. The ETF Facts state that the fund pays no trailing commission.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Bonds do not always cushion. In 2022 rising interest rates pushed stocks and bonds down together. VBAL lost 11.45% over the calendar year, and its NAV total return fell 16.4% from December 29, 2021 to October 12, 2022 (our calculation from the issuer's daily NAV and distributions). A 40% bond share lowers volatility, but it does not guarantee a smaller loss when both asset classes fall.
02
Interest-rate sensitivity. The bond holdings have an average duration of 6.5 years (fact sheet, August 31, 2026). A one-percentage-point rise in yields would take roughly 6.5% off their value, or about 2.5% off the whole fund at a 38% bond weight (first-order approximation, our calculation).
03
Canadian home tilt. Canada is 30.14% of the stocks (issuer) and 59.61% of the bonds (issuer's market allocation). Canadian banks, energy and materials companies and Canadian government and provincial debt carry more weight than in a global market-weight portfolio.
04
Unhedged foreign stocks. The three foreign stock funds, 43.07% of VBAL, are not currency-hedged. When the Canadian dollar rises, those holdings are worth less in CAD. The reverse has helped recently: USD/CAD went from 1.2754 at end-September 2021 to 1.4220 on October 2, 2026 (Yahoo Finance CAD=X).
05
Fee layering and a lagging MER. The management fee is offset by the underlying funds' fees, and the MER includes the fund's share of their expenses. The reported 0.22% MER covers April 2025 to March 2026, when the management fee was 0.22% until November 18, 2025 and 0.17% after. The issuer has not published an MER for a full year at the new fee.
06
Tax frictions and no single benchmark. Foreign tax of CAD 0.05633 per unit was paid in 2025, and the reinvested year-end capital-gains distribution (CAD 0.43024 per unit in December 2025) is taxable in a non-registered account without any cash being paid out. The benchmark is an "Internal Composite", so there is no published index to measure tracking against.

Daily total-return index built from the issuer's daily NAV history: on each ex-date the cash distribution is reinvested at that day's NAV (the reinvested capital-gains portions are consolidated by the fund and leave the NAV unchanged, so they need no adjustment). 08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising stock markets, through the roughly 62% stock share, led by large U.S. technology companies and Canadian banks at the top of the look-through list. Falling interest rates, which raise the value of bonds with a 6.5-year average duration. Calm markets in which stocks and bonds do not fall together, so the bond share damps swings. A weaker Canadian dollar, which raises the CAD value of the 43% held in unhedged foreign stock funds. Automatic rebalancing and diversification across 13,721 stocks and 17,275 bonds mean no single company or issuer decides the outcome.

What hurts it

Inflation shocks that push yields up and stock prices down at the same time, as in 2022 (−11.45% for the calendar year, −16.4% daily peak to trough). Rising yields on their own, through the bond holdings' duration. A sell-off in U.S. technology or Canadian banks, given the top-ten list. A stronger Canadian dollar, which shrinks the CAD value of foreign stocks. In strong stock markets a 60/40 fund trails an all-stock fund by design, as the five-year comparison with the S&P 500 shows.

Top 3 Risks

01
Stocks and bonds falling together. The bond share reduces volatility (8.74% a year over five years, our calculation), but in 2022 both sides fell and the fund lost 16.4% from peak to trough. The worst 3-month return since launch was −10.37%, to March 31, 2020.
02
Interest-rate risk. About 38% of the fund is in bonds with an average duration of 6.5 years; a one-point rise in yields would cut the whole fund by roughly 2.5% through the bond side alone (first-order approximation).
03
Home bias and currency. Canada is 30.14% of the stocks and about 60% of the bonds (issuer's market allocation), while 43.07% of the fund sits in unhedged foreign stock funds. Returns depend on the Canadian economy and on the Canadian dollar as well as on global markets.

Canada's one-ticket balanced portfolios
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Balanced asset-allocation ETFs package several index funds into one listed unit with a fixed stock-bond target, rebalanced by the manager. The 60/40 versions from Vanguard, BlackRock and Global X look alike from a distance but make different choices. Canada weight: VBAL held 18.54% of the fund in Canadian stocks at August 31, 2026; HBAL's target for Canadian equities was 12.00% at July 31, 2026. Bonds: VBAL holds Canadian, U.S. and global ex-U.S. bond funds, the last two hedged to Canadian dollars; HBAL's target mix at July 31, 2026 was 28.00% Canadian and 12.00% U.S. fixed income, and its page says it may elect to hedge that currency exposure. Payout rhythm: quarterly (VBAL, XBAL), monthly (HBAL). Size: VBAL is the largest of the three at CAD 5.9 billion; XBAL holds CAD 3.5 billion and HBAL CAD 185 million. MERs across the three range from 0.18% to 0.22%.
