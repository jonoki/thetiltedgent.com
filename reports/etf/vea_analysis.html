01 Fund Overview

VEA is a way to own several thousand companies based outside the United States, in the developed economies of Europe, the Pacific region and Canada, through a single U.S.-listed share. Its April 28, 2026 summary prospectus says the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of stocks issued by companies located in Canada and the major markets of Europe and the Pacific region." That index is the FTSE Developed All Cap ex US Index, which covers large, mid-sized and small companies; Vanguard describes it as "approximately 3,700 common stocks." The fund "attempts to replicate the Target Index by investing all, or substantially all, of its assets in the stocks that make up the Target Index," and Vanguard's product summary calls this "a passively managed full-replication approach."

VEA started trading on July 20, 2007. It is not a stand-alone fund: the prospectus calls it "an exchange-traded share class of Vanguard Developed Markets Index Fund," itself a series of Vanguard Tax-Managed Funds, whose Admiral Shares date from August 17, 1999. All share classes own the same stocks. Vanguard reports $231.6 billion in the ETF share class (September 30, 2026) and $313.5 billion for the whole fund (September 30, 2026). The expense ratio is 0.03%: a 0.02% management fee plus 0.01% other expenses.

Structure
Physical, full replication
Vanguard's September 30, 2026 holdings file lists 3,898 stock lines and its fund data counts 3,903 stocks; the index had 3,828 stocks that day. The fund "may invest in derivatives such as total return swaps, equity futures, foreign currency exchange forward contracts" but, in the prospectus's words, "will not use derivatives for speculation or for the purpose of leveraging." The August 31, 2026 holdings file showed six equity index futures (Euro Stoxx 50, TOPIX, S&P/ASX SPI 200, S&P/TSX 60, KOSPI 200, FTSE 100).
Currency
Foreign currencies, unhedged
The shares trade in U.S. dollars, but the stocks are priced in yen, pounds, euros, Canadian dollars, won and more. The prospectus says the fund may use currency forwards "in order to maintain the same currency exposure as its Target Index" and to settle trades, so a U.S.-dollar investor carries the currency moves. It lists currency risk among the principal risks.
Distributions
Quarterly, uneven
Last four dividends (record dates): $1.0400 (Dec 19, 2025), $0.1090 (Mar 20, 2026), $0.3772 (Jun 18, 2026) and $0.1775 (Sep 18, 2026). The December payment was 61% of the 12-month total (our calculation: 1.0400 ÷ 1.7037). All six payments the issuer lists since June 2025 are dividends; none is a capital-gains distribution.
Foreign tax (prospectus)
Withholding, possible credit
The fund "may be subject to foreign taxes or foreign tax withholding" on dividends. If more than 50% of its assets are in foreign companies and it makes a special election, shareholders include their share of those taxes in income and "may qualify for an offsetting credit or deduction." The prospectus adds there is "no assurance" the fund will make the election in a given year.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Currency. Returns in U.S. dollars combine the stocks' local-currency moves with the currencies' moves against the dollar, and the fund keeps the index's currency exposure. A rising dollar lowers the fund's value even when foreign stocks are flat in their own currencies. The prospectus lists currency risk as a principal risk.
02
Country weights. Japan is 21.9% of the stocks, and Japan, the United Kingdom and Canada together are 43.8% (our sum). The prospectus warns that a fund with a large share in one country or region "may be hurt disproportionately" by poor performance there.
03
Korean chipmakers. Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are the two largest holdings, 5.08% together, plus 0.35% in Samsung preferred shares. Korea, at 9.2%, is included because FTSE Russell classes it as developed; FTSE's April 2026 review noted market-structure issues it is monitoring there, including short-selling rules and pre-funding of trades.
04
Index rules decide membership. FTSE Russell decides which countries count as developed and reviews its indices semi-annually in March and September, with quarterly reviews in June and December. Greece joined the developed list from September 21, 2026; FTSE Russell's annual country classification announcement of October 6, 2026 made no further change to the developed list.
05
Uneven income and foreign taxes. The December 2025 dividend ($1.04) was 61% of the 12-month total, so quarterly income swings widely. Dividends from foreign companies can be cut by foreign withholding tax; any credit depends on the fund's election and the investor's situation (prospectus).
06
Trading at market price, with foreign markets closed. VEA trades during U.S. hours, when the Asian and Pacific markets are closed and European markets close partway through the U.S. session, so its price can move away from the value of the holdings at their last local prices. On November 2, 2026 the close was 0.27% above NAV. The prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants among principal risks.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising stock markets in Japan, Europe, Canada, Korea and Australia, since the fund is their index less 0.03% a year. A weaker U.S. dollar, which raises the dollar value of foreign holdings. Periods in which non-U.S. companies outperform U.S. ones, as in 2025, when the fund's NAV returned +35.15% against +17.88% for the S&P 500 total-return index (Yahoo ^SP500TR, our calculation). Strength in banks and industrial companies, the two largest sectors (24.4% and 17.5%), and in the Korean and Dutch chip companies at the top of the holdings list. The holdings trade at 16.1x earnings (VOO's at 24.7x, Vanguard, September 30, 2026), and the trailing distribution yield is 2.45%.

What hurts it

Broad global bear markets: the fund is all stocks, with no bond or cash cushion; 2022 cost −15.35% over the calendar year and −29.3% at the daily trough. A stronger U.S. dollar, which lowers the dollar value of foreign holdings. Downturns concentrated in Japan (21.9%) or the United Kingdom and Canada (11.1% and 10.8%). A slump in memory chips, given Samsung Electronics and SK hynix at the top. Long periods in which U.S. stocks lead: over the five years shown, VEA's total return was 20.1 percentage points behind the S&P 500's.

The dollar is part of the product
WHAT DIFFERS FROM A U.S. FUND

An unhedged international fund delivers two returns at once: the foreign stocks' returns in their own currencies, and the change in those currencies against the U.S. dollar. Over the five years to November 2, 2026 the combination gave VEA +55.8% with dividends, against +75.8% for the S&P 500. Its holdings trade at lower multiples (16.1x earnings and 2.1x book value, against 24.7x and 5.3x for VOO's holdings, Vanguard, September 30, 2026), and its sector mix leans to banks and industrial companies: Financials are 24.4% and Technology 12.5% of VEA (September 30, 2026), against 11.4% Financials and 39.7% Information Technology in VOO (September 30, 2026).
