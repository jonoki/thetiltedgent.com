01 Fund Overview

VFV is a Canadian-dollar wrapper around one U.S. fund, the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO), which was 99.98% of VFV's assets on August 31, 2026. VFV trades on the Toronto Stock Exchange in CAD; VOO trades in New York in U.S. dollars and holds the S&P 500 stocks. Its objective, in the issuer's words: the fund "seeks to track, to the extent reasonably possible and before fees and expenses, the performance of a broad U.S. equity index that measures the investment return of large-capitalization U.S. stocks. Currently, this Vanguard ETF seeks to track the S&P 500 Index (or any successor thereto)." The June 2026 interim management report adds that the ETF "may invest its assets directly in the stocks that make up the Index."

Launched November 2, 2012 (TSX listing November 8, 2012), it had CAD 35,156 million of net assets and 185,910,000 units outstanding on August 31, 2026; Vanguard publishes net assets once a month. The MER was 0.08% annualised for the six months to June 30, 2026 (section 04).

The fund stays close to its index. For the six months to June 30, 2026 VFV returned 13.72% against 13.77% for its benchmark, and the issuer attributes 0.04 percentage points of the gap to fees and expenses; for 2025 it was 12.23% against 12.34% (annual MRFP). In the twelve months to May 31, 2026 the ETF Facts report an average daily volume of 793,279 units and an average bid-ask spread of 0.018%. On September 23, 2026 the $193.11 close was $0.02 below the NAV of $193.13. The fee is not charged twice: the interim MRFP states that VFV's fee "is reduced by the aggregate of the management fee payable by the U.S.-domiciled Vanguard Fund" and certain of its expenses.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. Information technology is 37.82% of the fund. NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 20.79%, and the ten largest lines 37.78% (our sums). The index weights companies by market value, so the biggest companies get bigger weights as their prices rise.
02
One country, large companies only. Every holding is a U.S. company by the issuer's country field, and the index is designed to represent "large-capitalization U.S. stocks." There is no Canadian, international or small-company exposure in the fund.
03
Currency, unhedged. The assets are priced in U.S. dollars and none of them is hedged (section 01). When the Canadian dollar rises, the fund loses value in CAD even if U.S. stocks are flat. The reverse helped over the last five years: USD/CAD went from 1.2754 at the end of September 2021 to 1.4066 on September 23, 2026 (Yahoo Finance CAD=X), a 10.3% rise in the U.S. dollar.
04
Wrapper and withholding tax. U.S. dividends reach VFV through VOO after U.S. tax: about 15.7% of foreign income in 2025 (section 01). The structure also depends on VOO; the MRFP allows the manager to hold the stocks directly instead.
05
Little income. The distribution yield is 0.84% (August 31, 2026); almost all of the return comes from price changes.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. stock prices, since the fund holds nothing else. Strength in the largest U.S. technology companies: NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft and Broadcom alone are 23.44% of the fund. A weaker Canadian dollar, as between 2021 and 2026. Low costs and close tracking keep the result close to the index's in CAD.

What hurts it

U.S. bear markets, with no other region, bonds or cash to offset them (2022: −12.69% for the calendar year). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology. A stronger Canadian dollar. Periods when other markets or smaller companies outperform large U.S. companies, since the fund owns neither.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

Canadian investors can buy the S&P 500 on the TSX in Canadian dollars from several issuers. VFV and its equivalents ZSP, XUS and HXS hold about CAD 80 billion between them (our sum of the figures shown, on different dates). They differ mainly in how they hold the index. Fund-on-fund: VFV holds VOO and XUS holds IVV, both U.S.-listed; U.S. tax is withheld on the dividends the U.S. fund passes up. Direct holding: ZSP owns the 505 securities itself. Swap: HXS owns no stocks; it receives the index's total return from bank counterparties and pays distributions only at the manager's discretion. Payout rhythm: quarterly (VFV, ZSP), semi-annual (XUS), discretionary (HXS). MERs range from 0.08% to 0.11%.
