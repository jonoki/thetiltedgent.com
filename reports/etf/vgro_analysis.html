01 Fund Overview

VGRO is a single ticket for a global portfolio that is mostly stocks with a bond cushion. It does not buy stocks or bonds itself: it holds seven other Vanguard ETFs covering Canadian, U.S., international developed and emerging-market stocks, plus Canadian, U.S. and global bonds. Its objective, in the issuer's words: "Vanguard Growth ETF Portfolio seeks to provide long-term capital growth by investing in equity and fixed income securities." Under normal market conditions the sub-advisor "will strive to maintain a long-term strategic asset allocation of equity (approximately 80%) and fixed income (approximately 20%) securities," and adds: "The portfolio asset mix may be reconstituted and rebalanced from time to time at the discretion of the sub-advisor."

Launched January 25, 2018 and listed on the TSX on February 1, 2018, it had CAD 10.94 billion of net assets and 229,260,900 units outstanding on September 30, 2026 (issuer fund data). Vanguard names its benchmark only as an "Internal Composite"; the published indices sit one level down, in the underlying funds. The management fee was cut to 0.17% from 0.22% on November 18, 2025, and the fact sheet states that "there shall be no duplication of management fees" between VGRO and the Vanguard funds it holds. The MER in the latest annual report, for the year to March 31, 2026, is 0.22% (see section 04).

Structure
Fund of funds
Seven Vanguard Canada ETFs at August 31, 2026: four stock funds (81.65% combined) and three bond funds (18.33%), with 0.02% in short-term reserves. One layer deeper, four of them hold U.S.-domiciled Vanguard ETFs: VUN invests primarily in the U.S.-domiciled Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF, VEE in the Vanguard FTSE Emerging Markets ETF, VBG in the Vanguard Total International Bond Market ETF and VBU in the Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF (each fund's Vanguard page).
Currency hedging
Stocks unhedged · foreign bonds hedged
Vanguard's fund data lists the hedging strategy as "No Hedge" for VGRO and its four stock funds, and "Full Portfolio Hedge" for the two foreign bond funds, VBG and VBU (7.13% of VGRO together). Stocks of companies outside Canada are 69.7% of the stock sleeve, so roughly 57% of VGRO moves with foreign currencies against the Canadian dollar (69.68% × 81.65%, our calculation).
Distributions
Quarterly
Ex-dates in 2026 were April 1, July 2 and October 1, and the year-end ex-date was December 30 in 2024 and 2025; the ETF Facts say payments are "expected to be paid within ten days of each quarter end". Every December from 2020 to 2025 also carried a reinvested capital-gains distribution (CAD 0.28237 per unit in December 2025). After such a reinvestment "the number of units outstanding was consolidated" (annual report), so the unit count does not change. The ETF Facts note that in a non-registered account distributions are taxable "whether you get them in cash or have them reinvested."
Foreign withholding tax (2025)
CAD 0.06495 / unit
The issuer's annual distribution table for 2025 shows CAD 0.51326 per unit of foreign income and CAD 0.06495 of foreign tax paid, about 12.65% of that income (our calculation). Tax withheld abroad on dividends reduces what reaches the fund; how much a holder can recover depends on account type, which is not covered here.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

80/20 mix in one ticker 13,721 stocks · 17,275 bonds Mgmt fee 0.17%, no fee duplication CAD 10.9B net assets Avg bid-ask spread 0.031% No trailing commission

What the structure does is remove decisions. An investor building this mix from the seven funds would have to rebalance by hand, handle seven sets of distributions and pay seven bid-ask spreads. VGRO does that inside the fund, with turnover of 2.60% in the year to March 31, 2026 and a trading expense ratio of 0.00% (annual report). Trading is liquid: in the twelve months to May 31, 2026 the ETF Facts report an average daily volume of 508,854 units, trading on 250 days, and an average bid-ask spread of 0.031%. The fund pays no trailing commission to advisers. The 18% in bonds is what separates it from an all-stock fund; 2022 was its only down calendar year of the seven in the ETF Facts, at −11.21%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Mostly stocks. At 81.65% equities, stock-market falls dominate the result. The bond sleeve softens a decline but does not stop it: the daily peak-to-trough fall in total return was −17.3% from December 29, 2021 to October 12, 2022 (our calculation from the issuer's NAV history).
02
Bonds and stocks can fall together. The cushion works only when bond prices hold up while stocks fall. The bond sleeve's average duration is 6.5 years (issuer, August 31, 2026), so a one-percentage-point rise in yields would cut its value by roughly 6.5%, about 1.2% of VGRO (duration approximation, 6.5% × 18.33%, our calculation).
03
Canadian home tilt. VCN is 24.71% of the fund, and Canada is 30.32% of the stocks by country of risk. Royal Bank, TD and Shopify are three of the top ten holdings, and financials are 20.78% of the stock sleeve.
04
Currency, unhedged on the stock side. About 57% of the fund is in foreign stocks with no currency hedge. When the Canadian dollar rises, those holdings are worth less in CAD. The U.S. dollar went from CAD 1.2754 at end-September 2021 to CAD 1.4220 on October 2, 2026 (Yahoo Finance CAD=X), which added to returns over this window.
05
Layers and tax frictions. Four of the seven funds hold U.S.-domiciled Vanguard ETFs, so there are two fund layers between VGRO and many of its securities. Foreign tax of CAD 0.06495 per unit was paid in 2025, and the reinvested capital gains each December (CAD 0.28237 in 2025) are taxable in a non-registered account without any cash being paid.
06
No published benchmark. VGRO's benchmark is an "Internal Composite" that the issuer does not publish returns for, so there is no tracking-error figure to judge it by. Its results reflect Vanguard's chosen mix (for example, about a quarter of the fund in Canadian stocks) as much as the markets.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising global stock markets, since 81.65% of the fund is stocks. Strength in large U.S. technology companies, which make up most of the top ten look-through holdings (NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft and Broadcom alone are 7.54%). Falling interest rates, which lift bond prices in the 18% bond sleeve (average duration 6.5 years). A weaker Canadian dollar, which raises the CAD value of the roughly 57% held in unhedged foreign stocks, as it did from 2021 to 2026. Strong Canadian banks, energy and materials, through the quarter of the fund in VCN.

What hurts it

Broad equity bear markets: the bond sleeve is a cushion, not a floor. Periods when stocks and bonds fall together. In 2022 VGRO lost 11.21% over the calendar year and 17.3% at the daily trough. A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology, given the top-10 concentration. Rising interest rates, through the bond sleeve. A stronger Canadian dollar, which shrinks the CAD value of foreign stocks. Weak Canadian banks or commodity prices, through the home tilt. When the U.S. market leads, VGRO trails it, as it did by a wide margin from 2021 to 2026.

Top 3 Risks

01
Equity market risk. With about 80% in stocks, drawdowns arrive mostly in full: −17.3% daily peak to trough in 2021–22 and −12.5% in early 2025 inside this five-year window, and −13.95% over three months in early 2020. Five-year volatility is 10.09% a year (our calculation).
02
Stocks and bonds falling together. The 18% bond sleeve only cushions a stock decline when bond prices hold up. Rising rates lower bond prices; the sleeve's 6.5-year duration means a one-point rise in yields costs it about 6.5%.
03
Unhedged currency exposure. About 57% of the fund is foreign stocks priced in other currencies. The U.S. dollar's 11.5% rise against the Canadian dollar from September 2021 to October 2026 helped; a reversal would reduce CAD returns even if foreign markets were flat.
Category Commentary
Canada's one-ticket growth portfolios
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Asset-allocation ETFs package several index funds into one listed unit with a target mix, rebalanced by the manager. The "growth" versions hold about 80% stocks and 20% bonds. Vanguard's VGRO and BlackRock's XGRO both state that 80/20 split and now charge the same 0.17% management fee (VGRO since November 18, 2025; XGRO since December 18, 2025). Their published results are close: three-year annualised NAV returns to August 31, 2026 were 18.18% for VGRO and 18.24% for XGRO, and five-year returns 10.45% and 10.55% (each issuer). Calendar 2022 was −11.21% for VGRO and −11.00% for XGRO; 2024 was +20.24% and +20.47%. With the same broad markets underneath, the gaps between such funds can come from regional weights (VGRO puts about a quarter of the fund in Canadian stocks), the bond mix and its currency hedging, and fees.

One family, three mixes
WHAT DIFFERS

Vanguard's VBAL, VGRO and VEQT are built from the same funds; only the bond share differs (about 38%, 18% and none at August 31, 2026). Their issuer-published results to September 30, 2026 line up in that order: one-year NAV returns of 10.35%, 14.79% and 18.99%, and five-year annualised returns of 8.09%, 11.05% and 13.96%. Over this window, more bonds came with lower returns.
