01 Fund Overview

VGT holds the U.S. companies that the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) places in its information technology sector — large, mid-size and small — through a single share. Its December 19, 2025 summary prospectus says the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of information technology stocks." It does so by trying to hold all, or substantially all, of the index's stocks in about the same proportions as the index. The target is the MSCI US Investable Market Information Technology 25/50 Index. MSCI's definition of the sector: companies that "offer software and information technology services, manufacturers and distributors of technology hardware & equipment such as communications equipment, cellular phones, computers & peripherals, electronic equipment and related instruments, and semiconductors and related equipment & materials" (GICS sector definitions, effective March 17, 2023).

Launched January 26, 2004, VGT is the ETF share class of Vanguard Information Technology Index Fund, a series of Vanguard World Fund; the same portfolio has an Admiral share class, VITAX. Vanguard reports $147.6 billion for the ETF share class and $170.2 billion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026 (rounded by the issuer; the June 30 fact sheet gives $146,578 million and $169,218 million). The expense ratio is 0.09%, made up of a 0.08% management fee and 0.01% other expenses. On April 21, 2026 each share became eight (see section 04); all per-share prices on this page before that date are divided by eight.

Why this fund: the larger of the two MSCI information technology 25/50 ETFs

The group is VGT and FTEC, the two funds in this report that track an MSCI information technology 25/50 index. On each issuer's latest documents VGT's ETF share class ($147.6 billion, August 31, 2026) is about seven times FTEC's portfolio assets ($20,956.5 million, June 30, 2026). How the index caps its biggest names

The "25/50" in the index name is a weight limit. MSCI's methodology (MSCI 25/50 Indexes Methodology, October 2024) explains the origin: for a U.S. fund to qualify as a "regulated investment company," "no more than 25% of the value of the RIC's assets may be invested in a single issuer and the sum of the weights of all issuers representing more than 5% of the fund should not exceed 50% of the fund's total assets." MSCI applies these limits to the uncapped, market-cap-weighted index for the same sector (the "Parent Index"). The rules:

01
Limits with a buffer. At each rebalancing "the weight of any single group entity cannot exceed 22.5% of the index weight and all group entities with weight above 4.5% cannot exceed 45% of the index weight" — a 10% cushion under the 25% and 50% limits, "to reduce the risk of non-compliance due to short term market movements between two quarterly rebalancing." A "group entity" treats companies under common control as one issuer.
02
Quarterly, and only quarterly. Changes take effect "as of the close of the last business day of each February, May, August and November," announced in general nine business days earlier. "There is no index rebalancing due to non-compliance between Quarterly Index Reviews": between reviews each company's cap factor stays fixed, so weights drift with prices.
03
Stay close to the uncapped index. MSCI uses its Barra Optimizer to choose weights that meet the limits while "minimizing index turnover, tracking error and extreme deviation from the Parent Index." No company may be given more than four times its uncapped weight, a limit MSCI sets "in order to avoid excess weight allocation to the smaller securities relative to their market cap weight."

Where the fund stood: on MSCI's August 31, 2026 factsheet the three companies above 5% — NVIDIA 17.78%, Apple 15.84%, Microsoft 11.33% — add up to 44.95%, just inside the 45% level that applies at each rebalance (our sum). In VGT's own file on the same date the three are 45.03%. At each review, every company weighted above 4.5% counts toward that 45% limit.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Three companies are 45% of the fund. NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft are 45.03% and the top ten 63.03% (August 31, 2026). The fund is classified as nondiversified; Vanguard's risk summary says such funds' "performance may be negatively impacted by relatively few securities or even a single security." The 25/50 caps limit how far this can go, but at the 45% level the three already sit at the index's rebalancing limit.
02
One sector. Vanguard's risk summary: "Due to the fund’s heavy investment in companies within the information technology sector, its performance will be impacted by the general condition of the sector." Semiconductors alone are 36.9%. There is no offset from other sectors when technology falls; in 2022 the fund's NAV fell 29.67%.
03
Higher market sensitivity. Our beta against the S&P 500 total-return index is 1.35 over five years (section 04); Vanguard's own figure against the Dow Jones U.S. Total Stock Market Index is 1.45, R-squared 0.76 (August 31, 2026). Five-year volatility is 23.61% a year, against 15.84% for the S&P 500.
04
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 33.2x and price/book of 10.6x for the holdings, with an earnings growth rate of 34.7% (August 31, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets.
05
Index rules decide the weights. The caps are reset only four times a year; between reviews "there is no index rebalancing due to non-compliance," so the fund's top weights move with prices until the next February, May, August or November review. When a cap binds, the fund must sell the capped names at the review.
06
Low income. The 30-day SEC yield is 0.33% and the trailing distribution yield 0.38%. Almost all of the return has come from price.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. technology stocks, since the fund is its index less 0.09% a year. Strength in the three largest companies, NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft, which are 45.03% of the fund, and in semiconductors (36.9%). Earnings growth among the holdings: Vanguard reports 34.7% (August 31, 2026). Periods of rising broad markets, which the fund has amplified: beta 1.35 to the S&P 500 over five years. Low costs and low turnover mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Falls in technology stocks, with no other sectors to cushion them: 2022 cost −29.67% over the calendar year and −35.1% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −21.35% (June 30, 2022). A fall in one of the three largest holdings, given their weight. A contraction in valuations from a price/earnings ratio of 33.2x and price/book of 10.6x, which lowers prices even if earnings hold. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency.

Top 3 Risks

01
Single-sector equity risk. A 100% stock, one-sector, one-country portfolio: −35.1% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −27.2% in early 2025 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 23.61% a year against 15.84% for the S&P 500 (our calculation).
02
Concentration up to the legal limit. Three companies are 45.03% of the fund and the top ten 63.03%. The index's 25/50 rules are what stop the weights from going higher; at the 45% rebalancing level, further gains by the largest names are trimmed back at each quarterly review. The prospectus classifies the fund as nondiversified.
03
Starting valuation. The holdings trade at 33.2x earnings and 10.6x book value (Vanguard, August 31, 2026). The fund cannot move away from expensive stocks; its future return depends on earnings and on the multiple the market pays for them.

One sector, several rulebooks
PRODUCT CATEGORY

U.S. technology-sector ETFs differ less in what they want to own than in how their index defines and weights it. Breadth: VGT and FTEC hold large, mid and small companies (318 and 290 stock lines, August 31, 2026); XLK holds 74; IGM adds communication services and Canadian companies. Capping: MSCI's 25/50 rules follow the U.S. tax-code limits for regulated investment companies, with a 22.5%/45% buffer at each quarterly rebalance; IYW's index name carries its own 22.5/45 cap. Cost: 0.08% (XLK) to 0.37% (IYW, IGM). Share price: about $125 (VGT, after its April split) to about $299 (FTEC). VGT, XLK, IYW and IGM list quarterly distributions on their issuer pages.
