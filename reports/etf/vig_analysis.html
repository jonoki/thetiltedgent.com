01 Fund Overview

VIG is a single share that holds about 330 U.S. companies with a record of raising their dividends. Its prospectus of May 28, 2026 states the goal: the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of common stocks of companies that have a record of increasing dividends over time." The benchmark is the S&P U.S. Dividend Growers Index, which the prospectus describes as "a modified market capitalization-weighted index": larger companies get more weight, within limits set by the index provider. The rules are set out in section 02.

The fund opened on April 21, 2006 and its ETF shares trade on NYSE Arca. VIG is the exchange-traded share class of Vanguard Dividend Appreciation Index Fund, which also has an Admiral share class; together they held $132.4 billion on August 31, 2026, of which the ETF class was $109.1 billion on September 30. The expense ratio is 0.04%: a 0.03% management fee plus 0.01% of other expenses (prospectus fee table, "restated to reflect current fees" after Vanguard cut it from 0.05% on February 1, 2026). The name says "dividend appreciation", but the fund is not built for a high payout: its 30-day SEC yield was 1.51% on September 30, 2026. It selects companies by how consistently they have raised their dividends, not by how much they pay now.

Structure
Physical, full replication
The prospectus: the fund "attempts to replicate the Target Index by investing in the stocks that make up the Target Index, holding each stock in approximately the same proportion as its weighting in the Target Index." Vanguard's August 31, 2026 holdings file lists 333 stock lines worth 99.29% of net assets, short-term reserves of 0.30%, S&P 500 E-mini futures and total return swaps on five stocks (Visa, Goldman Sachs, Kroger, Elevance Health, Raymond James). Vanguard's fund page: "To help stay fully invested and to reduce transaction costs, the fund may invest, to a limited extent, in derivatives."
Currency
USD, U.S. companies
Foreign holdings were 0.2% of the fund on August 31, 2026 (Vanguard). The fund page adds: "It is not expected that the fund will invest more than 5% of its assets in foreign securities." A U.S.-dollar investor has no currency exposure; for an investor who spends another currency, the dollar's moves pass straight through.

How the index picks its stocks

The rules of the S&P U.S. Dividend Growers Index as the fund's prospectus (May 28, 2026) describes them, with the record length and the yield screen from the index entry in the glossary of another Vanguard prospectus (Vanguard Wellington Dividend Growth Active ETF, November 13, 2025):

  • 1 · Universe
    "[C]ommon stocks of U.S. companies that have a record of increasing their dividends over time (excluding real estate trusts ("REITs"))."
  • 2 · Record
    Dividends raised every year for at least 10 consecutive years (Vanguard glossary).
  • 3 · Yield screen
    The highest-yielding 25% of eligible companies are left out (Vanguard glossary), so the index does not chase payout. The prospectus: "The Fund may purchase stocks that have relatively low dividend yields if the company issuing the stock has increased its dividends in recent years."
  • 4 · Weighting
    "A modified market capitalization-weighted index": companies are weighted by stock-market value, with adjustments set by the index provider. On August 31, 2026 the four largest holdings were each above 4% of the fund: Microsoft 4.67%, Apple 4.50%, Broadcom 4.34% and JPMorgan Chase 4.07%.
  • 5 · Annual review
    "New securities are added to and removed from the Target Index during the annual reconstitution in March."
  • 6 · Monthly check
    "[A]t its discretion on a monthly basis, the index provider reviews the index constituents and may remove any securities that no longer qualify as of the first business day of the following month."

The index had 338 constituents on January 31, 2026 (prospectus) and 333 on August 31, 2026 (Vanguard). A longer record does not mean a place in the index, as a comparison with NOBL shows, a fund of S&P 500 companies that have raised dividends for at least 25 years (ProShares): 17 of NOBL's 69 stocks on October 2, 2026 were not in VIG's August 31 list, among them Chevron, Target, Kimberly-Clark, Clorox and Hormel Foods, and three REITs (Realty Income, Essex Property Trust, Federal Realty) (our match by ticker).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.04% $132.4B net assets Full replication, 333 stocks Dividend-growth record required 5-yr gap to index −0.06 pts/yr No capital-gains payouts in 5 fiscal years

The fund has followed its benchmark closely. Over the five years to September 30, 2026 its NAV returned 10.72% a year against 10.78% for the index; over ten years, 12.86% against 12.93% for the spliced benchmark (Vanguard). Those gaps are close to the expense ratio. Annual turnover is low for a rule-based fund: 8% in the fiscal year to January 31, 2026, against 26% in the year to January 2022, which included the switch of index (prospectus financial highlights). Its holdings traded at 25.3 times earnings on August 31, 2026, with a 29.4% return on equity and 11.4% earnings growth (Vanguard figures for the holdings).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The dividend screen leaves out some of the largest companies. Dividend investing is one of the principal risks in the prospectus: "The Fund's emphasis on dividend-paying stocks could cause the Fund to underperform other funds that invest without consideration of a company's track record of paying dividends." NVIDIA, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta Platforms and Tesla were not in the August 31, 2026 holdings file. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 VIG's total return was +67.76% against +92.58% for the S&P 500 (our calculation, section 07).
02
Concentration at the top. Microsoft, Apple, Broadcom and JPMorgan Chase were each above 4% of the fund on August 31, 2026, and the ten largest holdings were 33.54%. The prospectus warns the fund "could become nondiversified" by tracking its index, in which case its performance "may be negatively impacted by relatively few securities or even a single security."
03
Three sectors carry two-thirds of the money. Information technology, financials and health care were 65.9% of the fund (August 31, our sum), with no communication services or real estate at all. A fall in any of the three shows in the fund.
04
Dividends are not fixed. Individual quarters vary: the March 2026 dividend ($0.8334) was 11.2% below March 2025's ($0.938), while June 2026's ($0.9988) was 14.6% above June 2025's ($0.8712) (Vanguard and Yahoo Finance dividend history; our calculation). Total distributions per share rose in each fiscal year from 2022 to 2026, from $2.660 to $3.558 (prospectus).
05
The index provider decides. The prospectus: "The securities that make up the Target Index and their weighting in the Target Index are determined by the index provider," and "Any gains, losses, or costs associated with or resulting from an error made by the index provider will generally be borne by the Fund and, as a result, the Fund's shareholders." A company can leave at the March reconstitution or at a monthly review.
06
Trading at market price. The prospectus: "there may be times when the market price of an ETF Share and its NAV differ significantly." On October 2, 2026 the gap was small: market price $235.05 against NAV $234.99, a 0.03% premium (Vanguard).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Markets led by large, profitable companies that keep raising their dividends, such as Microsoft, Apple, Broadcom and JPMorgan Chase, its four largest holdings. Falls in which steady earners hold up better than the rest, as in 2022, when the fund's NAV fell 9.79% and the S&P 500 fell 18.1%. Strength in technology, financials and health care, 65.9% of the fund together. Low costs: 0.04% a year keeps the fund close to its index.

What hurts it

Markets led by companies the fund does not own, such as NVIDIA, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta Platforms and Tesla (none was in the August 31 holdings): the S&P 500's total return beat VIG's in 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 to date. Trouble at its largest holdings, or in technology or financials. A broad stock sell-off: the fund fell 20.4% in 2022 and 15.0% from February to April 2025 on daily data, with no bond cushion. For investors spending another currency, a weaker U.S. dollar.

Top 3 Risks

01
Style risk. The dividend-growth screen keeps out companies that pay no dividend or have no record of raising it. Over five years the fund returned 10.89% a year against 13.99% for the S&P 500 (total return, our calculation), and a gap of that kind can last for years when the market's leaders are non-payers.
02
Concentration in a few names and sectors. Four stocks were above 4% each and the ten largest were 33.54% on August 31, 2026; technology, financials and health care were 65.9%. Trouble at one large holding or one sector shows up quickly in the fund.
03
Stock-market risk with a modest income cushion. At a 1.51% SEC yield, dividends offset little of a fall: the fund lost 20.4% from January to September 2022 on daily total-return data. The prospectus lists general market risk first among its principal risks.
Category Commentary
Dividend growth is not dividend yield
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Funds built on a record of dividend increases differ in how long a record they require and whether they also chase yield. VIG's index asks for 10 years of increases and leaves out the highest-yielding quarter of eligible companies; NOBL's asks for 25 years. SDY, whose index requires a 20-year record and weights by yield, reported a 30-day SEC yield of 2.58% (October 2); DGRO reported 1.95% (August 31) and VIG, which weights by company size, 1.51% (September 30). Holdings run from 69 (NOBL) to 390 (DGRO), and expense ratios from 0.04% (VIG) to 0.35% (SDY and NOBL).

Overlap shows which rules matter
WHAT DIFFERS

By weight, VIG's overlap with DGRO is 70.5%, against 29.0% with SDY and 24.0% with NOBL (our calculation, section 05). DGRO's 390 stocks include 246 of VIG's, and those 246 make up 96.7% of VIG's stock weight. SDY and NOBL hold many of the same companies by name (119 and 52) but weight them differently and add high-yield companies VIG does not hold, so their results can diverge from VIG's even when the stock lists look alike.
