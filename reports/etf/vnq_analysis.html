01 Fund Overview

VNQ is a way to own most of the publicly traded U.S. real estate sector through one share: offices, warehouses, apartments, shopping centres, health care property, hotels, data centres and telecom towers. Most of these companies are REITs (real estate investment trusts). In the words of its May 28, 2026 prospectus, the fund "seeks to provide a high level of income and moderate long-term capital appreciation by tracking the performance of a benchmark index that measures the performance of publicly traded equity REITs and other real estate-related investments." That index is the MSCI US Investable Market Real Estate 25/50 Index, which covers large, mid-size and small U.S. companies in the real estate sector of the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS).

VNQ is the ETF share class of Vanguard Real Estate Index Fund, a series of Vanguard Specialized Funds that also has mutual-fund share classes. Vanguard reports $35.9 billion for the ETF share class (September 30, 2026) and $70.8 billion for the whole fund (August 31, 2026). The prospectus says the fund replicates the index "either directly or indirectly" through Vanguard Real Estate II Index Fund, a separate Vanguard fund that tracks the same index. On August 31, 2026 that fund was VNQ's largest single line: 445.6 million shares worth $10.34 billion, 14.53% of assets. The expense ratio is 0.13%: a 0.11% management fee, 0.01% other expenses and 0.01% "acquired fund fees and expenses," the costs of the funds it holds.

How REIT distributions are taxed

The fund passes on what its REITs pay, and REIT payments are a mix of dividend income, return of capital and capital gains. Vanguard explains that this mix "cannot be determined until after the end of the year since the REITs in which the fund invests do not designate the composition" of their payments until the new calendar year. That is why the fund sends its tax statements in February rather than January (prospectus). The prospectus sets out the federal rules for a taxable account:

01
Dividend distributions are taxed as ordinary income. An individual who meets holding-period requirements "may be eligible for reduced tax rates on 'qualified dividend income,' if any, or a special tax deduction on 'qualified REIT dividends,' if any."
02
Return of capital is not taxed when received. It lowers the investor's cost basis, and once the basis reaches zero, further return of capital is "treated as capital gains."
03
Dividends from the fund's REIT holdings "are generally not eligible for the corporate dividends-received deduction."
04
In the fiscal year to January 31, 2026, the fund paid $3.472 per share: $2.620 as dividends from net investment income and $0.852 as return of capital, so 24.5% was return of capital (our calculation from the prospectus's financial highlights). The return-of-capital amounts in the four earlier years were $0.821, $0.898, $1.074 and $1.023 per share.

This is why Vanguard quotes two yields rather than a 30-day SEC yield. The unadjusted effective yield (3.63%) "is based on the full amount of REIT distributions." The adjusted effective yield (2.89%) "reflects a reduction in the income included in the yield based on the average return of capital and capital gain distributions received from the fund's REIT investments for the past 2 calendar years" (both September 30, 2026). Tax-advantaged accounts follow different rules.

The 25/50 rule

Where the name comes from. To qualify as a "regulated investment company" "for favorable tax treatment under the Internal Revenue Code," a fund must meet diversification standards. The prospectus states them: no more than 25% of assets in any one security, and at least 50% in securities that are each no more than 5% of assets. MSCI says its 25/50 indexes "take into account these investment limits," so a fund that tracks one can stay within them.

How MSCI applies it (MSCI 25/50 Indexes Methodology, October 2024): no group entity may exceed 25% of the index, and all group entities above 5% may not together exceed 50%. At each rebalancing MSCI uses tighter limits, a buffer "to reduce the risk of non-compliance due to short term market movements": 22.5% for a single entity and 45% for the sum of those above 4.5%. The index is rebalanced as of the close of the last business day of each February, May, August and November, and "there is no index rebalancing due to non-compliance between Quarterly Index Reviews."

VNQ on August 31, 2026 (look-through, our calculation): the largest holding, Welltower, was 10.22%, under half the 22.5% limit. Four companies were above 4.5%: Welltower, Prologis (8.06%), Equinix (6.38%) and American Tower (5.06%), together 29.72% against the 45% limit. Neither cap was binding on that date, so the weights were close to plain market-value weights.

Structural strengths:

The fund owns its index, and its results have stayed close to it. Over the five years to September 30, 2026 the fund's NAV returned 1.34% a year against 1.48% for the Real Estate Spliced Index (Vanguard's benchmark series, which over that window is the MSCI US IMI Real Estate 25/50), a gap of 0.14 percentage points a year, about the size of the 0.13% expense ratio. Trading costs are small: Vanguard reports a 30-day median bid-ask spread of 0.011%, and on October 2 the market price ($89.50) sat $0.01, or 0.01%, above NAV ($89.49).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest rates. The prospectus names rates among the REIT risks: rate increases "can make it more difficult and costly to acquire financing," and "during periods of rising interest rates, REIT stock prices overall may decline." In 2022, the worst calendar year in the last five, the fund's NAV fell 26.20%.
02
One sector, a few large names. Everything is real estate, and the ten largest holdings are 50.49% of the fund (look-through). The fund is "nondiversified" under the 1940 Act, which the prospectus says means its performance "may be negatively impacted by relatively few securities or even a single security."
03
Leverage inside the holdings. REITs borrow to buy property. The prospectus: real estate companies "may rely on leverage, which, while used to help magnify returns, also has the negative effect of magnifying losses," and the sector "historically has been sensitive to economic downturns."
04
Tax drag in a taxable account. Most of the return comes as distributions taxed mainly as ordinary income. Over five years to September 30, 2026, Vanguard's 1.34% annual market-price return drops to 0.26% after tax on distributions (calculated at the highest individual federal income tax rates).
05
A fund inside the fund. 14.53% of assets sit in Vanguard Real Estate II Index Fund. It tracks the same index, but the prospectus notes that differences in the two funds' "cash flows and expenses" are "expected to produce slightly different investment returns."
06
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 31.3x and price/book of 2.6x for the holdings, with a 7.0% return on equity (August 31, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling or stable interest rates, which lower REITs' borrowing costs; the prospectus names rising rates as a source of REIT price declines. Steady demand and rents for the property types it holds most: health care (18.0%), retail (14.1%), industrial (11.5%) and data centres (10.7%). Strength at Welltower and Prologis, together 18.28% of the fund. Distributions: over the last five years they made the difference between a −12.1% price change and a +6.8% total return. Low costs and turnover mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Rising interest rates and tighter credit, as in 2022 (NAV −26.20%) and the fall to October 2023 (−34.5% at the daily trough). Economic downturns that cut demand for space; the prospectus calls the sector "historically" sensitive to them. Trouble at the largest holdings, given a 50.49% top-10 share. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency. In a taxable account, the tax on distributions, most of it at ordinary income rates.

Top 3 Risks

01
Interest-rate and credit sensitivity. REITs borrow to own property, and the prospectus ties REIT prices to interest rates. The five-year window shows the effect: −34.5% daily peak to trough between December 2021 and October 2023, and a −0.04 Sharpe ratio over five years (our calculations).
02
Concentration inside one sector. One industry, ten companies at 50.49% of the fund, and Welltower alone at 10.22%. The 25/50 caps only bite at 22.5% for one company; on August 31 none was close.
03
Income that is taxed, and partly not income. Distributions are most of the return, they are largely taxed as ordinary income, and about a quarter of last fiscal year's payout was return of capital, which lowers the investor's cost basis. The yield an investor sees depends on which measure is used: 3.81% trailing, 3.63% or 2.89% effective.
Category Commentary
Four ways to slice U.S. real estate
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Broad U.S. real estate ETFs differ mainly in what their index admits. Breadth: from 30 companies (XLRE, the S&P 500's real estate members) to 139 (VNQ, large to small companies). What counts as real estate: VNQ and XLRE follow the GICS real estate sector, which includes property services firms such as CBRE; USRT's index covers equity REITs; IYR also holds four Financials-sector companies such as Annaly and AGNC. Caps: VNQ's index applies the 25/50 rule, USRT's index is "40 Act Capped," IYR's is "Capped." Cost: 0.08% (XLRE, USRT) to 0.37% (IYR). Size: VNQ's ETF share class is $35.9 billion; the three peers are $4.1–7.6 billion each.

The same landlords in every fund
WHAT DIFFERS

Welltower and Prologis are the two largest holdings in VNQ, XLRE and IYR on the latest files, and VNQ's overlap with the peers runs from 73.5% (XLRE) to 84.9% (IYR) by weight (our calculation). The differences that remain are the long tail of smaller companies, the treatment of mortgage REITs and services firms, and costs. The whole category shares the property cycle, the sensitivity to interest rates and the tax character of REIT income described above.
