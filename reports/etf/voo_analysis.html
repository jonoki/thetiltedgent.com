01 Fund Overview

VOO is a way to own the 500-odd large U.S. companies in the S&P 500 through a single share. In the words of its April 28, 2026 summary prospectus, the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of large-capitalization stocks," and it does so by holding "each stock in approximately the same proportion as its weighting in the Target Index." Vanguard's fact sheet calls this "a passively managed, full-replication strategy": the fund buys the index's stocks themselves rather than a sample of them or derivatives that mimic them.

Launched September 7, 2010, VOO is not a stand-alone fund. It is the ETF share class of a larger portfolio whose mutual-fund share classes include Vanguard 500 Index Fund Admiral Shares (VFIAX), as the issuer's fund profile links them. Vanguard reports two asset figures: $1,032,260 million for the ETF share class and $1,732,017 million for the whole fund at September 30, 2026 (fact sheet; $978,960 million and $1,675,038 million on June 30). All share classes own the same stocks. The expense ratio is 0.03%, made up of a 0.02% management fee and 0.01% other expenses.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.03% $1.0T ETF share class Full replication, 505 stocks 5-yr gap to index −0.04 pts/yr No capital-gains payouts since Mar 2025 Portfolio turnover 2.4%

The fund does one thing: it owns the index. Its results have stayed close to it. Over the five years to September 30, 2026 the fund's NAV returned 13.75% a year against the index's 13.79%, a gap of 0.04 percentage points, a little above the 0.03% expense ratio. Over ten years the gap was also 0.04 points (15.29% vs 15.33%). Turnover is low: 2.4% in fiscal 2025 on Vanguard's data, "2%" in the prospectus.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. The ten largest holdings were 37.81% of the fund on August 31, and NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone 20.81% (our sum). Information technology was 39.7% on September 30. The prospectus lists concentration and the information technology sector among its principal risks. A market-cap-weighted index puts the most weight on the companies whose market value has risen most.
02
One country. Foreign holdings are 0.0%. The fund has no exposure to companies based outside the U.S., so a period in which U.S. stocks lag the rest of the world hits it in full.
03
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 24.7x and price/book of 5.3x for the holdings (September 30, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets; it has no mechanism to sell expensive stocks.
04
Index rules decide membership. S&P Dow Jones Indices chooses what enters and leaves. On September 21, 2026 it added Bloom Energy, Everpure and Illumina and removed Molson Coors Beverage, The Trade Desk and Builders FirstSource; the fund followed.
05
Low income. The 30-day SEC yield is 0.98% and the trailing distribution yield about 1.07%. The fund is built for growth in the index, not for income. The dividends it does pay are taxable in a taxable account.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares) among principal risks.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. large-cap stocks, since the fund is the S&P 500 less 0.03% a year. Strength in the largest technology and internet companies, which dominate the top ten (NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 20.81%). Periods in which U.S. companies outperform the rest of the world. Earnings growth at the large companies it holds: Vanguard reports a 24.5% earnings growth rate for the holdings (September 30, 2026). Low costs and low turnover mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Broad U.S. bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion and no foreign stock sleeve. 2022 cost −18.15% over the calendar year and −24.5% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −19.63% (March 31, 2020). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology, given the top-10 concentration. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency. A contraction in valuations from a price/earnings ratio of 24.7x, which lowers prices even if earnings hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full U.S. equity market risk. A 100% stock, one-country portfolio takes market falls in full: −24.5% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −18.7% in early 2025 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 15.46% a year (our calculation).
02
Concentration inside the diversification. The ten largest holdings were 37.81% of the fund on August 31 and information technology 39.7% on September 30. The fund's return depends heavily on a small group of very large U.S. companies, and the prospectus flags both concentration and the technology sector as principal risks.
03
Starting valuation. The holdings trade at 24.7x earnings and 5.3x book value (Vanguard, September 30, 2026). The fund cannot move away from expensive stocks; its future return depends on earnings and on the multiple the market pays for them.
