01 Fund Overview

VOO is a way to own the 500-odd large U.S. companies in the S&P 500 through a single share. In the words of its April 28, 2026 summary prospectus, the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of large-capitalization stocks," and it does so by holding "each stock in approximately the same proportion as its weighting in the Target Index." Vanguard's fact sheet calls this "a passively managed, full-replication strategy": the fund buys the index's stocks themselves rather than a sample of them or derivatives that mimic them.

Launched September 7, 2010, VOO is not a stand-alone fund. It is the ETF share class of a larger portfolio whose mutual-fund share classes include Vanguard 500 Index Fund Admiral Shares (VFIAX), as the issuer's fund profile links them. Vanguard reports two asset figures: $1.0 trillion for the ETF share class and $1.8 trillion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026 (rounded by the issuer; the June 30 fact sheet gives $978,960 million and $1,675,038 million). All share classes own the same stocks. The expense ratio is 0.03%, made up of a 0.02% management fee and 0.01% other expenses.

Structural strengths:

The fund does one thing: it owns the index. Its results have stayed close to it. Over the five years to August 31, 2026 the fund's NAV returned 12.75% a year against the index's 12.79%, a gap of 0.04 percentage points, a little above the 0.03% expense ratio. Over ten years the gap was 0.03 points (15.34% vs 15.37%). Turnover is low: 2.4% in fiscal 2025 on Vanguard's data, "2%" in the prospectus. On September 23 the market close ($707.60) sat 0.03% above NAV ($707.38), our calculation.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. The ten largest holdings are 37.81% of the fund, and NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 20.81% (our sum). Information technology is 37.9%. The prospectus lists concentration and the information technology sector among its principal risks. A market-cap-weighted index puts the most weight on the companies whose market value has risen most.
02
One country. Foreign holdings are 0.0%. The fund has no exposure to companies based outside the U.S., so a period in which U.S. stocks lag the rest of the world hits it in full.
03
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 25.2x and price/book of 5.3x for the holdings (August 31, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets; it has no mechanism to sell expensive stocks.
04
Index rules decide membership. S&P Dow Jones Indices chooses what enters and leaves. On September 21, 2026 it added Bloom Energy, Everpure and Illumina and removed Molson Coors Beverage, The Trade Desk and Builders FirstSource; the fund followed.
05
Low income. The 30-day SEC yield is 0.98% and the trailing distribution yield about 1.04%. The fund is built for growth in the index, not for income. The dividends it does pay are taxable in a taxable account.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares) among principal risks. On the banner date the gap was 0.03%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. large-cap stocks, since the fund is the S&P 500 less 0.03% a year. Strength in the largest technology and internet companies, which dominate the top ten (NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 20.81%). Periods in which U.S. companies outperform the rest of the world. Earnings growth at the large companies it holds: Vanguard reports a 24.1% earnings growth rate for the holdings (August 31, 2026). Low costs and low turnover mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Broad U.S. bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion and no foreign stock sleeve. 2022 cost −18.15% over the calendar year and −24.5% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −19.63% (March 31, 2020). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology, given the top-10 concentration. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency. A contraction in valuations from a price/earnings ratio of 25.2x, which lowers prices even if earnings hold.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

The four S&P 500 funds in the Equivalent ETFs table hold the same stocks, so the choice between them comes down to details. Cost: 0.02% (SPYM) to 0.0945% (SPY). Age: SPY listed in January 1993, IVV in May 2000, VOO in September 2010; SPYM has tracked the S&P 500 only since January 2020.
