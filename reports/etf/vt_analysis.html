01 Fund Overview

VT is a way to own almost the whole world's stock market through a single share: large, mid-sized and small companies in the United States, the other developed markets and the emerging markets. Its June 30, 2026 fact sheet sums up the approach in three lines: the fund "Seeks to track the performance of the FTSE Global All Cap Index," is "Passively managed, using index sampling," and "remains fully invested." Sampling means the fund holds a representative selection of the index's stocks rather than every one of them. At August 31, 2026 the fund held 10,088 stocks against 10,112 in the index (issuer's characteristics).

Launched June 24, 2008, VT is the ETF share class of Vanguard Total World Stock Index Fund, which also has Admiral and Institutional mutual-fund share classes (issuer's fund profile). All share classes own the same portfolio. Vanguard reports $81.9 billion for the ETF share class and $101.7 billion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026; the June 30 fact sheet gives $77,627 million and $96,997 million. The expense ratio is 0.06%: a 0.05% management fee plus 0.01% other expenses (summary prospectus, February 27, 2026).

Why this fund: the largest of the U.S.-listed global stock ETFs compared here

The comparison set is four U.S.-listed ETFs that hold U.S. and non-U.S. stocks in one fund by tracking a global index: VT, iShares MSCI ACWI (ACWI), State Street SPDR Portfolio MSCI Global Stock Market (SPGM) and iShares MSCI World (URTH). On each issuer's latest figure, VT's ETF share class alone is about 2.5 times the size of ACWI, the next largest (our calculation: $81.9 billion ÷ $33.1 billion). The dates differ: Vanguard publishes net assets at month-end, the others daily.

Structure
Physical, sampled
The fund buys the stocks themselves. The prospectus describes the index as weighted by float-adjusted market capitalization. Because the fund samples, Vanguard's statement of its principal risks adds that "the representative sample of securities selected by the advisor will, in the aggregate, vary from the investment profile of the full Target index."

USD shares, global holdings
Vanguard reports foreign holdings of 37.7% (August 31, 2026). The shares trade in U.S. dollars, but most non-U.S. stocks are priced in their own currencies, so exchange rates feed into the fund's value. 03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.06% 10,088 stocks Large, mid and small caps 5-yr gap to index −0.05 pts/yr $101.7B whole fund Portfolio turnover 3.4%

The fund does one thing: it owns a sample of the world stock market in proportion to market value. Its results have stayed close to its benchmark. Over the five years to September 30, 2026 the fund's NAV returned 11.34% a year against 11.39% for Vanguard's spliced benchmark, a gap of 0.05 percentage points, close to the 0.06% expense ratio. Over ten years the gap was 0.08 points (12.32% vs 12.40%). Turnover is low: 3.4% in the fiscal year to October 31, 2025 on Vanguard's data, "3%" in the prospectus. On October 2 the market close ($159.13) sat 0.07% above NAV ($159.02), our calculation.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Still mostly American. The United States is 62.0% of the fund and North America 65.0%. A global fund weighted by market value mirrors where the world's stock-market value sits, so a fall in U.S. stocks moves VT far more than a fall anywhere else.
02
Concentration at the top. The ten largest lines are 21.21% of the fund and Technology is 33.3% under ICB. NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 11.24% (our sum). The prospectus lists concentration risk: if the index becomes concentrated in an industry, the fund follows it.
03
Currency. 37.7% of holdings are foreign (Vanguard). Vanguard's risk statement warns that "foreign currency will perform differently than U.S. dollars and increase the potential loss to the fund." A rising dollar lowers the dollar value of non-U.S. stocks.
04
Emerging markets. Vanguard counts 10.0% of the fund in Emerging Markets. Vanguard's risk statement says these markets can have "greater custodial and operational risks" and "less efficient trading markets with lower overall liquidity and more volatile currency exchange rates."
05
Sampling and index rules. The fund holds a sample, not every index stock, which Vanguard's risk statement calls "tracking error risk." FTSE Russell decides membership and country classifications; Greece moved from advanced emerging to developed on September 21, 2026 (ground rules), and the fund follows such changes.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares) among principal risks. On the banner date the gap was 0.07%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising stock markets broadly, since the fund is close to the whole world market less 0.06% a year. U.S. strength still matters most at 62.0% of the fund, and the largest technology companies dominate the top ten. Periods in which non-U.S. markets, small companies or emerging markets outperform, since VT holds all three where a U.S. large-cap fund holds none. A weaker U.S. dollar, which raises the dollar value of the 37.7% held abroad. Low costs and low turnover mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Global bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion. 2022 cost −18.00% over the calendar year and −26.4% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −22.27% (March 31, 2020). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology, given that nine of the top ten holdings are U.S. companies. A stronger U.S. dollar, which lowers the dollar value of foreign holdings. Periods in which U.S. large caps lead the world, as in this five-year window, when VT trailed the S&P 500 by about 2.5 points a year.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full equity market risk. A 100% stock portfolio takes market falls in full: −26.4% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −16.5% in early 2025 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 14.92% a year (our calculation), a little below the S&P 500's 15.64%.
02
U.S. weight and concentration inside the diversification. Ten thousand holdings, but the U.S. is 62.0% of the fund, the ten largest lines are 21.21% and Technology is 33.3%. The fund's return depends heavily on a small group of very large U.S. companies.
03
Currency and emerging-market risk. 37.7% of holdings are foreign and 10.0% sit in emerging markets. Exchange rates and the custody, liquidity and political risks Vanguard lists for emerging markets add swings that a U.S.-only fund does not have.
Category Commentary
One fund for the world market
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Global stock ETFs hold U.S. and non-U.S. companies in a single fund, in proportion to market value, so the investor does not choose the split between countries. The four compared here differ in what "the world" means. Coverage: VT (FTSE Global All Cap) and SPGM (MSCI ACWI IMI) include small caps; ACWI covers large and mid caps; URTH leaves out emerging markets. Holdings: from 1,252 (URTH) to 10,088 (VT). Cost: 0.06% (VT) to 0.32% (ACWI). Distributions: VT pays quarterly; the three others semi-annually. Index family: VT's index comes from FTSE Russell and uses the ICB sector scheme; the three others track MSCI indexes, and the iShares holdings files label sectors on the GICS scheme.

Why the U.S. share is so large
WHAT DRIVES IT

A market-value-weighted global fund holds each country in proportion to the value of its listed companies. VT's U.S. weight was 63.1% at August 31, 2025 and 62.0% a year later (issuer). The country mix changes as markets move and as index providers reclassify countries, such as Greece's move to developed status in September 2026. The fund's weights follow; there is no target allocation by country.
