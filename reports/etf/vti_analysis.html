01 Fund Overview

VTI is a way to own almost the entire U.S. stock market through a single share: large companies, but also mid-sized, small and very small ones. Its April 28, 2026 summary prospectus says the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of the overall stock market." Vanguard describes the index as one that "represents approximately 100% of the investable U.S. stock market and includes large-, mid-, small-, and micro-cap stocks regularly traded on the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq."

VTI does not buy every stock in its index in exact proportion. The prospectus says it "invests by sampling the Target Index, meaning that it holds a range of securities that, in the aggregate, approximates the full Target Index in terms of key risk factors and other characteristics." In practice it holds slightly more lines than the index: Vanguard counts 3,507 stocks in the fund against 3,450 in the index (August 31, 2026).

Launched May 24, 2001, VTI is the ETF share class of a larger fund. That fund also has mutual-fund share classes, and Vanguard reports two asset figures: $689.2 billion for the ETF share class (September 30, 2026) and $2.3 trillion for the whole fund (August 31, 2026, rounded by the issuer). All share classes own the same stocks. The expense ratio is 0.03%: a 0.03% management fee and 0.00% other expenses.

A new name in July 2026, the same index

Morningstar, Inc. agreed to buy the Center for Research in Security Prices (CRSP), the index provider behind VTI's benchmark, from the University of Chicago, in a deal announced on September 23, 2025. Morningstar's 10-Q for the quarter to June 30, 2026 says the purchase closed on February 2, 2026, for closing consideration of $363.0 million in cash. Morningstar then renamed the CRSP Market Indexes as the Morningstar Market Indexes from July 28, 2026; its announcement describes them as "Built on CRSP's trusted research foundation and proven methodology." Vanguard followed with a prospectus supplement dated July 29, 2026: the fund became Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market Index Fund, the ETF became Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF, and the target index name changed from the CRSP US Total Market Index to the Morningstar US Total Market Index. The supplement adds that each fund's objective and strategies are unchanged. The ticker stays VTI.

The candidates are four U.S.-listed ETFs that each track a broad index of large, mid-sized and small U.S. companies: VTI, iShares' ITOT and IWV, and State Street's SPTM (the peers in section 05). Physical, sampled
The fund owns the stocks themselves but not every index stock in exact proportion. Vanguard says the sample matches the index on "industry weightings and market capitalization, as well as certain financial measures such as price/earnings ratio and dividend yield." Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. The ten largest holdings are 33.41% of the fund, and NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 18.27% (our sum). ICB Technology is 40.9%. Adding the 2,970 holdings outside the S&P 500 dilutes the giants only slightly: about 89% of the money is still in S&P 500 companies. Vanguard's summary of principal risks includes concentration and the information technology sector.
02
One country. Foreign holdings are 0.2%. The fund has almost no exposure to companies based outside the U.S., so a period in which U.S. stocks lag the rest of the world hits it in full.
03
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 24.7x and price/book of 4.8x for the holdings (August 31, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets; it has no mechanism to sell expensive stocks.
04
Sampling and the index provider. Because the fund samples rather than buying every index stock, Vanguard warns that the sample "will, in the aggregate, vary from the investment profile of the full target index." The fund "also could be negatively impacted by changes to the target index made by the index provider or by errors made by the index provider." The provider changed owner in February 2026; Morningstar describes the renamed indexes as built on CRSP's methodology.
05
Small and micro-cap tail. Vanguard's risk text notes that small- and mid-cap stocks "have typically been more volatile due to the effects of changing economic conditions." That part of the fund is small (the last 2,471 lines are 3.47% of its value), so it adds breadth more than weight.
06
Low income and trading at market price. The 30-day SEC yield is 1.03% and the trailing distribution yield about 1.04%; the fund is built for market return, not income. ETF shares trade at a market price that can differ from NAV; Vanguard's principal-risk summary includes ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. stock prices across the board, since the fund is the whole investable market less 0.03% a year. Strength in the largest technology and internet companies, which dominate the top ten (NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 18.27%). Periods in which mid, small and micro-cap companies outperform the largest ones, since those make up the 11% that the S&P 500 does not hold. Periods in which U.S. companies outperform the rest of the world. Earnings growth at its holdings: Vanguard reports a 22.9% earnings growth rate (August 31, 2026).

What hurts it

Broad U.S. bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion and almost no foreign stock sleeve. 2022 cost −19.50% over the calendar year and −25.4% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −20.89% (March 31, 2020). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology, given the top-10 concentration. Periods in which smaller companies lag the largest, as in the five years to October 2026. A contraction in valuations from a price/earnings ratio of 24.7x, which lowers prices even if earnings hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full U.S. equity market risk. A 100% stock, one-country portfolio takes market falls in full: −25.4% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −19.3% in early 2025 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 15.94% a year (our calculation).

Concentration inside the diversification. More than 3,400 stocks, yet the ten largest are 33.41% of the fund and ICB Technology is 40.9%. The fund's return depends heavily on a small group of very large U.S. companies, and Vanguard lists both concentration and the technology sector among its principal risks.
03
Starting valuation. The holdings trade at 24.7x earnings and 4.8x book value (Vanguard, August 31, 2026). The fund cannot move away from expensive stocks; its future return depends on earnings and on the multiple the market pays for them.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

Total-market funds differ first in where their index stops. VTI's index had 3,450 stocks (August 31, 2026); ITOT held 2,460, IWV 2,602 and SPTM 1,511 (October 2, 2026). Cost: 0.03% for VTI, ITOT and SPTM; 0.20% for IWV. Age: IWV listed in May 2000, SPTM in October 2000, VTI in May 2001, ITOT in January 2004. Structure: VTI is a share class of a fund that also has mutual-fund share classes, and it samples its index. Share price: from about $168 (ITOT) to about $436 (IWV). Distributions: all four pay quarterly.

Total market vs S&P 500
WHAT DIFFERS

A total-market fund is mostly an S&P 500 fund: about 89% of VTI's value sits in S&P 500 companies (our calculation from Vanguard's August 31, 2026 files). The difference is the remaining 11%, spread over 2,970 stock lines of companies outside the S&P 500. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 that slice lowered the result: VTI returned +82.47% with dividends against +92.58% for the S&P 500 total-return index. Over other periods the sign can reverse; the fund's design simply holds both groups at their market weights.
