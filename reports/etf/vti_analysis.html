01 Fund Overview

VTI is a way to own nearly every publicly traded U.S. company, large and small, through a single share. Vanguard's fund page states that it "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of the overall stock market." That index, the Morningstar US Total Market Index, "represents approximately 100% of the investable U.S. stock market and includes large-, mid-, small-, and micro-cap stocks regularly traded on the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq," in the issuer's words. The fund does not buy every index stock: "The fund invests by sampling the index, meaning that it holds a broadly diversified collection of securities that, in the aggregate, approximates the full index in terms of key characteristics."

Launched May 24, 2001, VTI is the ETF share class of Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market Index Fund, which also has mutual-fund share classes; all classes own the same stocks. Vanguard reports $690.1 billion for the ETF share class and $2.3 trillion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026 (the June 30 fact sheet gives $663,488 million and $2,298,312 million). The expense ratio is 0.03%: a 0.03% management fee and 0.00% other expenses (summary prospectus, April 28, 2026).

A new name, the same index. Until July 2026 the fund was Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF and its index the CRSP US Total Market Index. Morningstar bought CRSP (the Center for Research in Security Prices) in February 2026 and renamed the CRSP indexes after itself; its July 28, 2026 release says the rebrand changed names only and left the index methodologies unchanged. Vanguard announced on April 29, 2026 that its U.S. equity index funds tracking these indexes would add "Morningstar" to their names, a change that "will not affect the funds’ investment objectives or how they are managed." Its prospectus supplement of July 29, 2026 (filed with the SEC) made it formal: "The name changes are effective as of July 29, 2026." The same supplement renamed each fund's target index, as a result of Morningstar's acquisition of CRSP, also with effect from July 29, 2026, and states that each fund's investment objective, strategies and policies are unchanged. It lists the index change for this fund as CRSP US Total Market Index to Morningstar US Total Market Index. Vanguard's performance tables use a spliced benchmark: the Dow Jones U.S. Total Stock Market Index through April 22, 2005, the MSCI US Broad Market Index through June 2, 2013, and the CRSP (now Morningstar) US Total Market Index since.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.03% $690.1B ETF share class 3,507 stocks, all sizes 5-yr NAV return = index, 11.71%/yr No capital-gains payouts listed since Mar 2025 Portfolio turnover 2.6%

The fund does one thing: it owns the U.S. market. Its annualised NAV returns over three, five and ten years equal its benchmark's to the second decimal; over one year the gap is 0.01 points (20.24% against 20.25%). Over the five years to August 31, 2026 the fund's NAV returned 11.71% a year, the same as the spliced index; over ten years both returned 14.81%, and over three years both 20.65%. Sampling has not cost measurable tracking at these horizons. Turnover was 2.6% in fiscal 2025 on Vanguard's data ("3%" in the prospectus). On September 24 the market close ($378.08) sat $0.02 above NAV ($378.06), a premium of 0.005% (our calculation).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. Despite 3,507 stocks, the ten largest holdings are 33.41% of the fund, and NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 18.27% (our sum). ICB Technology is 40.9%. The prospectus lists industry concentration and the information technology sector among its principal risks; it notes that information technology stocks "made up a significant portion of the Target Index."
02
Small companies add little diversification by weight. The ~2,970 companies outside the S&P 500 are 11.09% of the fund. Over the five years in section 04 VTI's correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.9965 (our calculation): a total-market fund behaves much like a large-cap fund.
03
One country. Foreign holdings are 0.2%. A period in which U.S. stocks lag the rest of the world hits the fund in full.
04
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 24.7x and price/book of 4.8x for the holdings (August 31, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets.
05
Index rules decide membership and weight. Morningstar sets entry, exit and weights at each quarterly reconstitution. Its "fast-track" rule brought SpaceX into the index five days after its June 2026 IPO; because of its limited public float the stock was 0.1% of the index at the end of August (Morningstar, September 23, 2026), and 0.11% of VTI's file.
06
Trading at market price, and low income. ETF shares trade at a market price that can differ from NAV; the prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares) among principal risks. The 30-day SEC yield is 1.01%: the fund is built to track the market, not for income.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. stocks of every size, since the fund is the whole U.S. market less 0.03% a year. Strength in the largest technology and internet companies, which dominate the top ten (NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 18.27%). Periods when mid, small and micro caps outpace the largest companies, which lifts VTI relative to an S&P 500 fund; Morningstar reports micro caps leading all size segments over the past year. Earnings growth at the companies it holds: Vanguard reports a 22.9% earnings growth rate for the holdings (August 31, 2026).

What hurts it

Broad U.S. bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion and no foreign stock sleeve. 2022 cost −19.50% over the calendar year and −25.4% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −20.89% (March 31, 2020). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology, given the top-10 concentration. Periods when smaller companies lag the largest, as over the five years to September 2026. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency. A contraction in valuations from a price/earnings ratio of 24.7x.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full U.S. equity market risk. A 100% stock, one-country portfolio takes market falls in full: −25.4% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −19.3% in early 2025 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 16.11% a year (our calculation).
02
Concentration inside the diversification. The ten largest holdings are 33.41% of the fund and ICB Technology is 40.9%. Owning 3,507 stocks does not remove the dependence on a small group of very large U.S. companies; the prospectus lists industry concentration and the information technology sector as principal risks.
03
Starting valuation. The holdings trade at 24.7x earnings and 4.8x book value (Vanguard, August 31, 2026). The fund cannot move away from expensive stocks; its future return depends on earnings and on the multiple the market pays for them.
Category Commentary
Four wrappers around the whole U.S. market
PRODUCT CATEGORY

VTI, ITOT, IWV and VTHR follow three different index families (Morningstar, S&P and Russell) that each aim to cover the U.S. stock market across all company sizes. Their stock lists differ in the smallest names, which carry little weight, so the portfolios overlap by 96–98% by weight. The details that separate them: cost, 0.03% (VTI, ITOT) to 0.20% (IWV); size, $690.1 billion for VTI's ETF share class against $4.8 billion for VTHR's; method, sampling (VTI, ITOT, IWV) or full replication (VTHR); listing, NYSE Arca or Nasdaq. All four pay quarterly.

Total market vs S&P 500
WHAT DIFFERS

A total-market fund adds roughly 3,000 companies to the S&P 500, but by weight they are about a ninth of the fund: 11.09% of VTI on August 31, 2026. The two move together (correlation 0.9965 over five years), and the difference in results comes from how that ninth performs against the large caps. Over the five years to September 24, 2026 it cost VTI 10.0 percentage points of total return relative to the S&P 500; in periods when smaller companies lead, the sign reverses.
