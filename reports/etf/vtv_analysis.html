01 Fund Overview

VTV owns the large U.S. companies that look cheapest on measures such as earnings, book value and sales. In the words of its April 28, 2026 summary prospectus, the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of large-capitalization value stocks." It does so by "investing all, or substantially all, of its assets in the stocks that make up the Target Index, holding each stock in approximately the same proportion as its weighting in the Target Index." Vanguard's fact sheet calls this "a passively managed, full-replication strategy."

The index is the Morningstar US Large Cap Value Index. It is the same index the fund has followed since April 2013 under the name CRSP US Large Cap Value Index: Morningstar bought the Center for Research in Security Prices (CRSP) and its indexes, and on July 29, 2026 the index and the fund were renamed. Vanguard's supplement of that date lists the Value Index Fund's target index changing from "CRSP US Large Cap Value Index" to "Morningstar US Large Cap Value Index" and states: "Each Fund's investment objective, strategies, and polices remain unchanged." The ETF share class became Vanguard Morningstar Value ETF; the ticker stayed VTV.

Launched January 26, 2004, VTV is the ETF share class of a larger fund that also has mutual-fund share classes. Vanguard reports $192.2 billion for the ETF share class and $262.4 billion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026 (the June 30 fact sheet: $186,106 million and $254,207 million). The expense ratio is 0.03%: a 0.02% management fee and 0.01% other expenses.

Why this fund leads the report

Of the U.S. large-cap value ETFs compared on this page, VTV has by far the most money in it: $192.2 billion in the ETF share class on August 31, 2026, against $81.82 billion for IWD, $48.95 billion for IVE and $36.17 billion for SPYV at their issuers' September 23–24 figures (section 05). How the index picks value stocks

From Morningstar's paper on the Morningstar Market Indexes (July 2026), the family this index belongs to:

01
Start from the whole market, then cut by size. The parent Morningstar US Total Market Index is "designed to represent 100% of the investable US equity market." Size segments are defined by shares of cumulative market cap rather than fixed company counts. The breakpoints are reset each quarter, so the number of companies in each segment changes while the coverage target stays fixed.
02
Score every stock on value and on growth, separately. Five value factors: price/forward earnings (30%), price/book (22%), price/sales (22%), price/earnings (15%) and dividend yield (11%). Six growth factors: long-term projected earnings growth (22%), short-term projected earnings growth (22%), historical sales growth (22%), historical earnings growth (11%), return on assets (11%) and current investments/assets (11%). Weights as shown in the paper's Exhibit 6; they are adjusted when data are missing.
03
Judge style against the parent index, without a forced 50/50 split. A stock's style is assessed "relative to its parent index," and the indexes "do not force equal allocation of capitalization between Value and Growth." A company can show traits of both.
04
Weight by float-adjusted market cap. Shares held by insiders or subject to restrictions are left out of the weight.
05
Move slowly. Size and style are reassessed at each quarterly reconstitution. Banding puts buffers around the size and style breakpoints; packeting moves "only 50% of a company's float-adjusted market cap at a time," and required changes are phased in "over five trading days."

Banding and packeting mean a company near a breakpoint can be split between adjacent categories, part in value and part in growth; the paper describes this as "splitting companies near breakpoints between adjacent categories."

The fund owns its index and has stayed on top of it. Key Vulnerabilities

01
Style can lag for years. The prospectus lists value investing as a principal risk: "The Fund's approach to value investing could cause it to underperform other stock funds that use a different investment style." Over the five years to September 24, 2026 VTV's total return was +82.0% against +92.1% for the S&P 500 (our calculation, section 07).
02
Sector tilt. Financials (22.1%), Industrials (15.9%) and Health Care (14.8%) make up 52.8% of the fund (our sum of Vanguard's ICB weights). A shock to banks and insurers hits more than a fifth of the portfolio at once.
03
Index rules decide what counts as value. Membership shifts with the provider's scores. At its September 2026 quarterly reconstitution Morningstar moved Intel, Applied Materials, Dell and Corning from value to growth, and McDonald's, Uber, Adobe and Intuit from growth to value (Morningstar release, September 23, 2026). The fund follows its index.
04
One country. Foreign holdings are 0.2%. A period in which U.S. stocks lag the rest of the world hits the fund in full.
05
Cheaper is not cheap. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 20.6x and price/book of 3.2x for the holdings (August 31, 2026), against 25.2x and 5.3x for its S&P 500 fund, VOO, on the same date. "Value" is relative to other large U.S. companies, and the fund buys at whatever price the market sets.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares) among its principal risks. On the banner date the gap was zero.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Markets that favour cheaper, established companies over the fastest growers, as in 2022, when the fund's NAV fell 2.05% while the S&P 500 fell 18.11%. Strength in banks, insurers, energy and industrial companies, which make up much of the portfolio. Steady dividends: the 30-day SEC yield is 1.79% (August 31, 2026) and dividends made up about 20 of the 82 points of five-year total return. Low costs and low tracking difference keep nearly all of the index return for the holder.

What hurts it

Rallies led by the largest technology and internet companies, which the fund does not own: over the five years to September 24, 2026 it trailed the S&P 500 by 10.1 points in total return. Stress in the financial sector, 22.1% of the fund. Broad U.S. bear markets: the fund holds only stocks, and its worst calendar quarter in the prospectus is −25.03% (March 31, 2020). A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency.

Top 3 Risks

01
Style risk. The fund's definition of value excludes the biggest growth companies. When they lead the market, as over this five-year window as a whole, VTV trails; its own prospectus names value investing as a principal risk.
02
Sector concentration. Financials, Industrials and Health Care are 52.8% of the fund. A downturn centred on banks and insurers, or on industrial demand, would weigh on more than half the portfolio.
03
Full U.S. equity market risk. A 100% stock, one-country portfolio takes market falls in full: −17.0% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −14.5% from late 2024 to April 2025 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 14.03% a year (our calculation).
Category Commentary
Three definitions of "value"
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Large-cap value ETFs look alike from the outside and hold different portfolios. The index behind each fund decides what value means. Morningstar's index scores each stock on five value and six growth factors, and VTV held 306 stocks on August 31; the Russell 1000 Value index behind IWD holds 870; the S&P 500 Value index behind IVE and SPYV holds 436. Their holdings overlap VTV's by only 63% to 68% by weight (our calculation), partly because the other indexes keep Apple and Amazon and VTV's does not. Cost: 0.03% (VTV) to 0.18% (IWD, IVE). Age: IWD and IVE date from May 2000, SPYV from September 2000, VTV from January 2004. Distributions: all pay quarterly.

Two "Morningstar Value" ETFs, two indexes
NAMES

Since July 29, 2026 Vanguard's fund is called Vanguard Morningstar Value ETF. iShares runs the iShares Morningstar Value ETF (ILCV), which tracks a different index, the Morningstar US Large-Mid Cap Broad Value Index, and holds Microsoft (8.20%), Apple (7.18%) and Micron among its 372 stocks. The two overlap by 64.3% by weight (our calculation; VTV file August 31, ILCV file September 23, 2026). The index in a fund's prospectus, not the word in its name, sets what it owns.
