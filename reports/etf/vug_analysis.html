01 Fund Overview

VUG holds the "growth" side of large U.S. companies: the ones whose earnings and sales have been rising fastest, or are expected to, measured against their share prices. Its April 28, 2026 prospectus states the objective: the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of large-capitalization growth stocks." It does so by "holding each stock in approximately the same proportion as its weighting in the Target Index," buying the stocks themselves rather than a sample or derivatives.

Launched January 26, 2004, VUG is the ETF share class of a larger portfolio, now called Vanguard Morningstar Growth Index Fund, which also has mutual-fund share classes; all classes own the same stocks. Vanguard reports $232.9 billion for the ETF share class (September 30, 2026) and $384.6 billion for the whole fund (August 31, 2026). The expense ratio is 0.03%, made up of a 0.02% management fee and 0.01% other expenses.

Two changes in 2026 altered the fund's labels but not its holdings. On April 21 each share became six (a 6-for-1 split), which took the price from the $400s to below $100; every price on this page is adjusted for it. On July 29 the index and the fund were renamed after Morningstar bought the index provider CRSP: the CRSP US Large Cap Growth Index became the Morningstar US Large Cap Growth Index, and Vanguard Growth ETF became Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF. Vanguard's supplement of that date says each fund's "investment objective, strategies, and polices remain unchanged."

How the index sorts growth from value

The rules come from the Morningstar Market Indexes Methodology Guide (July 2026), the former CRSP methodology under its new name. The index is built in two steps.

1 · Size: what counts as large
Top 85% of market value
Eligible U.S. companies are ranked by total market value. The large-cap index covers companies up to 85% of the cumulative market value of the eligible universe (the mega-cap and mid-cap segments together, with breakpoints at 70% and 85%). Bands around each breakpoint (81% to 89% for the 85% line) stop companies near the edge from switching back and forth.
2 · Style: eleven measures
5 value + 6 growth factors
Value: book-to-price, future and historical earnings-to-price, dividend-to-price, sales-to-price. Growth: analysts' expected long-term and short-term growth in earnings per share, 3-year historical growth in earnings per share and in sales per share, current investment-to-assets, and return on assets. Each factor becomes a z-score; the scores combine into one value score and one growth score per company.
3 · The cut and the packets
Average rank below 0.5 = growth
Each company's value and growth scores are ranked by market value within its size segment and averaged into an "Average Rank" from 0 (most growth) to 1 (most value). New companies at or below 0.5 go fully to growth. Existing members move in two halves ("packets"): crossing the band of 0.3333 to 0.6667 moves 50%, and the rest moves only if the company stays across at the next quarterly review, so a company can sit partly in growth and partly in value.

The guide notes that the value and growth indexes "are not designed to represent 50% (each)" of the parent index. The review runs quarterly. In the September 2026 review, Morningstar's release of September 23 reported that "Intel (INTC), Applied Materials (AMAT), Dell (DELL), and Corning (GLW) moved from value to growth, while McDonald's (MCD), Uber (UBER), Adobe (ADBE) and Intuit (INTU) moved from growth to value." In the fund's August 31 file, before that review, Applied Materials weighed 0.99%, McDonald's 0.52% and Intel 0.52%.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.03% $232.9B ETF share class Full replication, 147 stocks 5-yr gap to index −0.03 pts/yr No capital-gains payouts since Jun 2025 Banded style rules limit turnover

The fund owns its index and has stayed close to it. Over the five years to September 30, 2026 the fund's NAV returned 13.87% a year against 13.90% for Vanguard's spliced growth benchmark, a gap of 0.03 percentage points, equal to the expense ratio; over ten years 17.94% against 17.97%. Turnover was 12.3% in fiscal 2025 on Vanguard's data ("12%" in the prospectus). Companies can leave the fund by moving to value as well as by leaving the large-cap segment; the methodology's bands and two-step packets are designed for that; of the size bands the guide says the approach "balances the desire to maximize style purity while minimizing index turnover," and the style bands follow the same packet rules. In Morningstar's September 23 release, Rodney Comegys, chief investment officer of Vanguard Capital Management and head of Global Equity, said: "One reason Vanguard has long valued the Morningstar methodology is its use of gradual transitions, which help reduce turnover and market impact when companies move between market segments." On October 2 the market close ($91.17) sat 0.01% above NAV ($91.16), our calculation.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Heavy concentration. The ten largest holdings are 63.44% of the fund, and NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 36.23% (our sum). Technology is 69.8% on Vanguard's ICB breakdown. The prospectus lists concentration, nondiversification and the information technology sector among its principal risks.
02
One style. The prospectus's "Growth Investing" risk: the fund's approach "could cause it to underperform other stock funds that use a different investment style." In 2022 the fund's NAV fell 33.13%, against −18.11% for the S&P 500 total-return index (Yahoo Finance data, our calculation).
03
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 31.0x and price/book of 11.6x for the holdings (August 31, 2026), alongside an earnings growth rate of 34.3% and return on equity of 38.1%. An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets.
04
Index rules decide membership. Morningstar's quarterly review moves companies between growth and value in 50% packets; in September 2026 it moved Intel, Applied Materials, Dell and Corning toward growth and McDonald's, Uber, Adobe and Intuit toward value. The prospectus adds that the fund "could be negatively impacted by changes to the Target Index made by the index provider or by errors made by the index provider."
05
Low income. The 30-day SEC yield is 0.35% and the trailing distribution yield about 0.38%. The prospectus notes that growth stocks "typically produce lower yields because growth companies prefer to reinvest earnings into research and development."
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares) among principal risks. On the banner date the gap was 0.01%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Markets that reward fast-growing large companies over cheaper, slower ones, as in 2023 (+46.78% NAV) and 2024 (+32.68%). Strength in the largest technology and internet companies: NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 36.23% of the fund. Earnings growth at those companies, which Vanguard puts at 34.3% for the holdings (August 31, 2026). Low costs: 0.03% a year and a 0.03-point gap to the index.

What hurts it

A rotation from growth to value, or a sell-off in mega-cap technology: in 2022 the fund lost 33.13% while the S&P 500 lost 18.11%. A contraction in valuations from 31.0x earnings and 11.6x book, which lowers prices even if earnings hold. Setbacks at one or two of the largest holdings, given 63.44% in the top ten. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency.

Top 3 Risks

01
Concentration in a few companies. The ten largest holdings are 63.44% of the fund and technology is 69.8% on Vanguard's ICB breakdown. The fund's return rests on a handful of very large U.S. companies; the prospectus flags concentration, nondiversification and the IT sector as principal risks.
02
Higher swings than the market. Five-year volatility of 20.35% against 15.64% for the S&P 500, and a beta of 1.22 (our calculations). Inside this window the fund fell 35.6% from peak to trough in 2021–22 and 22.8% in early 2025 on daily data.
03
Style and valuation risk. Growth can lag value for years, and the prospectus warns the style "could cause it to underperform other stock funds that use a different investment style." The holdings trade at 31.0x earnings (Vanguard, August 31, 2026); future returns depend on earnings and on the multiple the market pays for them.
Category Commentary
One label, several definitions of growth
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Large-cap growth ETFs share a label, not a rulebook. Morningstar's index sorts with eleven measures, five of value and six of growth, and moves companies between styles in halves. The S&P 500 Growth and Russell 1000 Growth indexes use their own measures and universes: SPYG holds 151 stocks drawn from the S&P 500, IWF 373 that reach into mid-caps, VUG 147 and MGK 56 (issuer counts). Cost: 0.03% (VUG) to 0.18% (IWF). Age: IWF listed in May 2000, SPYG in September 2000, VUG in January 2004, MGK in December 2007. Distributions: all pay quarterly.

Same giants on top, different tails
WHAT DIFFERS

NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Broadcom and Meta are among the ten largest holdings of all four funds, so their returns move together. Amazon shows how the definitions differ: 4.76% of VUG but 0.59% of IWF, its 31st-largest line (October 2 file; our ranking). VUG overlaps by weight 76.7% with IWF, 76.5% with SPYG and 86.3% with MGK (our calculations, files of August 31 and October 2, 2026). The remaining quarter is where the definitions show: which mid-sized companies count, and where companies like McDonald's, Intel or Adobe sit between growth and value at a given review. Growth funds as a group are far more concentrated than the broad market: 63.44% in VUG's top ten against 37.81% in Vanguard's S&P 500 ETF (August 31, 2026).
