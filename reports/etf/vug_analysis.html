01 Fund Overview

VUG holds the growth side of the large U.S. stock market in one share. Its April 28, 2026 summary prospectus sets the objective as tracking a benchmark index of large-capitalization growth stocks, and Vanguard's fact sheet describes the method as "a passively managed, full-replication strategy": the fund buys the index's stocks in roughly their index weights rather than a sample or derivatives. The benchmark is the Morningstar US Large Cap Growth Index. Until July 29, 2026 the same index was called the CRSP US Large Cap Growth Index. Morningstar bought CRSP (the Center for Research in Security Prices) in February 2026 and renamed its indexes in July 2026, and Vanguard renamed the fund from Vanguard Growth ETF to Vanguard Morningstar Growth ETF on the same date. Vanguard's supplement of July 29, 2026 states that each fund's objective, strategies and policies are unchanged.

Launched January 26, 2004, VUG is the ETF share class of Vanguard Morningstar Growth Index Fund, which also has Investor, Admiral and Institutional mutual-fund share classes. Vanguard reports $227.0 billion for the ETF share class and $384.6 billion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026 (the June 30 fact sheet gives $223,246 million and $378,844 million). All share classes own the same stocks. The expense ratio is 0.03% (0.02% management fee, 0.01% other expenses), down from 0.04% on February 1, 2026. On April 21, 2026 each share became six, which cut the share price to a sixth without changing the value of a holding.

How the index picks growth stocks

From Morningstar's Market Indexes Methodology Guide (July 2026):

1
Large cap = the top 85%. U.S. companies are ranked by total market capitalization; the Large Cap Index covers the companies that make up the first 85% of the cumulative market cap of the eligible universe, with a band from 81% to 89% around that breakpoint. There is no fixed number of members.
2
Six growth factors, five value factors. Growth: expected long-term and short-term earnings-per-share growth, three-year historical growth in earnings per share and in sales per share, the current investment-to-assets ratio, and return on assets. Value: book-to-price, forward and historical earnings-to-price, dividend-to-price and sales-to-price. Each factor is winsorised at the 5th and 95th percentiles and turned into a z-score, then combined into a composite growth score and a composite value score.
3
One average rank decides. The two scores are converted into market-cap-weighted ranks and averaged. A company with an average rank below 0.5 counts as growth, above 0.5 as value. Every company in the large-cap index is fully allocated between the Large Cap Growth and Large Cap Value indexes.
4
Slow migration. Companies with an average rank between 0.3333 and 0.6667 stay where they are. A company that crosses the band moves half its weight ("one packet") at that ranking and the other half only if it is still across at the next one, so a company can be held partly in growth and partly in value.
5
Quarterly, over five days. The review is run after the close of the first Friday of March, June, September and December; the changes are then phased in over a five-day transition, 20% of the move per day. Weights are float-adjusted market capitalization.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. The ten largest holdings are 63.44% of the fund, and NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 36.23% (our sum). The prospectus lists concentration, nondiversification and the information technology sector among its principal risks. A market-cap-weighted index puts the most weight on the companies whose market value has risen most.
02
One sector. Technology is 69.8% on Vanguard's ICB basis; on our GICS mapping, Information Technology and Communication Services together are 72.04%. A sell-off in large technology and internet companies hits most of the fund at once.
03
Style risk. Growth investing is a named principal risk in the prospectus: the fund can lag stock funds that use a different style. In 2022 its NAV fell 33.13%, against −18.15% for Vanguard's S&P 500 ETF in the same year (Vanguard fund data).
04
Valuation of what it owns. Vanguard reports a price/earnings ratio of 31.0x and price/book of 11.6x for the holdings (August 31, 2026), alongside a 34.3% earnings growth rate and 38.1% return on equity. An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets.
05
One country. Foreign holdings are 0.2%. A period in which U.S. stocks lag the rest of the world hits the fund in full.
06
Index rules decide membership. Morningstar's style model, bands and packeting decide which companies count as growth and how fast they move. The index changed owner and name in 2026; Vanguard states the fund's objective and strategy are unchanged.
07
Trading at market price. ETF shares trade at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants (the large firms that create and redeem shares) among principal risks. On the banner date the gap was 0.01%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising prices for the largest U.S. technology and internet companies, which make up most of the fund (the top ten are 63.44%; ICB Technology is 69.8%). Strong years for large growth stocks: in 2023 and 2024 the fund's NAV returned +46.78% and +32.68%. Earnings growth at the companies it holds: Vanguard reports a 34.3% earnings growth rate for the holdings (August 31, 2026). Low costs mean little is lost between the index and the fund.

What hurts it

Years in which large growth stocks fall harder than the broad market, as in 2022, when the fund's NAV fell 33.13% (Vanguard's S&P 500 ETF: −18.15%) and the daily trough reached −35.6%. A sell-off concentrated in a handful of mega-cap technology companies: NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 36.23%. A contraction in valuations from a price/earnings ratio of 31.0x and price/book of 11.6x, which lowers prices even if earnings hold. A falling U.S. dollar, for investors who measure returns in another currency.

Top 3 Risks

01
Concentration in a few companies and one sector. The ten largest holdings are 63.44% of the fund and three companies are 36.23%. On Vanguard's ICB basis 69.8% is technology. The prospectus names concentration, nondiversification and the information technology sector among principal risks.
02
Higher market sensitivity. Five-year volatility is 20.56% a year and beta to the S&P 500 is 1.22 (our calculation): the fund has moved further than the broad market in both directions, and its 2022 fall was deeper by about 11 percentage points at the daily trough.
03
Starting valuation. The holdings trade at 31.0x earnings and 11.6x book value (Vanguard, August 31, 2026). The fund cannot move away from expensive stocks; its future return depends on earnings growth and on the multiple the market pays for it.
Category Commentary
"Growth" depends on who draws the line
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Large-cap growth ETFs follow different index definitions, and the definitions produce different portfolios. VUG's Morningstar index scores companies on six growth and five value factors within the top 85% of the market and holds 146 stocks. iShares describes IWF's Russell 1000 Growth index as large- and mid-cap stocks "selected using metrics such as growth forecasts and sales growth" (371 holdings), and IVW's S&P 500 Growth index as large-cap stocks selected "using metrics such as above-average earnings and revenue growth" (151 holdings). The overlap between VUG and each of them is about 77% by weight (our calculation), so roughly a quarter of the money in each sits in different stocks or different weights.

Costs are a small part of the difference
WHAT DIFFERS

Expense ratios in this group run from 0.03% (VUG) to 0.18% (IWF and IVW), a gap of $15 a year per $10,000. Over the five years to September 24, 2026 the difference in total return between VUG (+92.00%) and IWF (+88.73%) was 3.27 percentage points, against roughly 0.75 points that a 0.15-point fee gap accounts for over five years (0.15 points × 5 years); the rest reflects the different stocks the two funds hold (our calculation from Yahoo Finance dividend-adjusted closes). Vanguard's own mega-cap version, MGK, holds 56 of VUG's names and is a narrower, more concentrated slice of the same index family.
