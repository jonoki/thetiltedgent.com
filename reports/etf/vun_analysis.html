01 Fund Overview

VUN is a Canadian-dollar wrapper around one U.S. fund, the Vanguard Morningstar Total Stock Market ETF (VTI), which was 99.97% of VUN's assets on August 31, 2026. VUN trades on the Toronto Stock Exchange in CAD; VTI is a U.S.-listed ETF priced in U.S. dollars that holds about 3,500 U.S. stocks of every size. In the issuer's words, the fund "seeks to track, to the extent reasonably possible and before fees and expenses, the performance of a broad U.S. equity index that measures the investment returns of primarily large-capitalization U.S. stocks. Currently, this Vanguard ETF seeks to track the Morningstar US Total Market Index (or any successor thereto)." The index, per the fact sheet, is "a market-capitalization-weighted index representing the returns of large-, mid-, small- and micro-capitalization stocks in the U.S."

Name and index. Until July 29, 2026 the fund was Vanguard U.S. Total Market Index ETF and its index was the CRSP US Total Market Index. The June 2026 interim management report states: "Effective July 29, 2026, Vanguard U.S. Total Market Index ETF was renamed Vanguard Morningstar U.S. Total Market Index ETF, and the CRSP US Total Market Index was renamed the Morningstar US Total Market Index. The ETF and its investment objective otherwise remained unchanged." Prospectus Amendment No. 2 of the same date makes the change and names Morningstar, Inc. as the index provider. The August 2026 fact sheet still lists the index ticker as CRSPTMCT.

Launched August 2, 2013 (TSX listing August 12, 2013), it had CAD 19,674 million of net assets on August 31, 2026 and 138,181,100 units outstanding. The MER was 0.16% annualised for the six months to June 30, 2026 (section 04).

Structural strengths:

The fund stays close to its index. Over 36 months the fact sheet gives an R-squared of 100.00%, a beta of 1.00 and a tracking error of 0.20%. In the twelve months to May 31, 2026 the ETF Facts report an average daily volume of 170,026 units and an average bid-ask spread of 0.027%. The fee is not charged twice: the interim MRFP states that VUN's fee "is reduced by the aggregate of the management fee payable by the U.S.-domiciled Vanguard Fund" and certain of its expenses.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. Technology is 40.9% of the fund on the ICB basis. NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft alone are 18.26%, and the ten largest lines 33.42% (our sums). Holding 3,500 stocks does not spread the money evenly: the index weights companies by market value, and the 500 largest lines are 90.56% of VTI's stock holdings (our calculation).
02
One country. 99.82% of the fund is in U.S. companies by the issuer's country field. There is no Canadian or international exposure.
03
Currency, unhedged. The assets are priced in U.S. dollars and none of them is hedged (section 01). When the Canadian dollar rises, the fund loses value in CAD even if U.S. stocks are flat. The reverse helped over the last five years: USD/CAD went from 1.2754 at the end of September 2021 to 1.4105 on September 24, 2026 (Yahoo Finance CAD=X), a 10.6% rise in the U.S. dollar.
04
Wrapper and withholding tax. U.S. dividends reach VUN through VTI after U.S. tax: 16.8% of foreign income in 2025 (section 01). The structure depends on VTI; the MRFP allows the manager to hold the stocks directly instead.
05
Index provider change of name. The index was renamed from CRSP to Morningstar on July 29, 2026; the MRFP states the objective "otherwise remained unchanged." Any future methodology change would come from Morningstar, Inc. as index provider.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising U.S. stock prices across all sizes, since the fund holds nothing else. Strength in the largest U.S. technology companies: NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft and Broadcom alone are 20.64% of the fund. Periods when mid, small and micro caps do better than large caps, which lifts the 11% an S&P 500 fund does not own. A weaker Canadian dollar, as between 2021 and 2026.

What hurts it

U.S. bear markets, with no other region, bonds or cash to offset them (2022: −14.17% for the calendar year). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology. Periods when smaller U.S. companies lag the largest. A stronger Canadian dollar.

Canada's U.S. total-market ETFs: two indexes, one market
PRODUCT CATEGORY

VUN (Morningstar US Total Market Index) and iShares' XTOT and XUU (S&P Total Market Index) are TSX-listed, Canadian-dollar, unhedged funds that each track an index of the whole U.S. stock market. They held CAD 30,999 million between them on August 31, 2026 (our sum of the fact-sheet figures), 63% of it in VUN. All three are fund-on-fund: VUN holds VTI, XTOT holds ITOT, and XUU holds ITOT and IVV with smaller mid- and small-cap ETFs; U.S. tax is withheld on the dividends the U.S. funds pass up. MERs run from 0.07% (XUU) to 0.16% (VUN). Each has a CAD-hedged counterpart that is a different product: VUS, XTOH and XUH.

Total market versus S&P 500
WHAT DIFFERS

A total-market fund holds the S&P 500 companies plus about 3,000 smaller ones. By weight the difference is small: VUN overlapped the S&P 500 funds VFV and XUS by 88.90% on August 31, 2026, and VUN overlapped XTOT and XUU by 98.47% and 97.35% (our calculations). One-year NAV returns to August 31, 2026 were 20.95% for VUN and 21.08% for VFV (fact sheets). Over five years to September 24, 2026 the gap was larger: VFV's NAV total return was 1.29 points a year higher than VUN's (section 07).
