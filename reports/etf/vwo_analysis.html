01 Fund Overview

VWO holds thousands of stocks from about two dozen emerging economies through a single share. Its summary prospectus (February 27, 2026) states the objective: the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of stocks issued by companies located in emerging market countries." The index is the FTSE Emerging Markets All Cap China A Inclusion Index, which Vanguard's fact sheet describes as a market-capitalization-weighted index of large-, mid- and small-cap stocks of companies located in emerging markets. The "China A Inclusion" part means mainland-listed Chinese shares are in the index, but only partly: the index contains "FTSE China A All Cap Index securities adjusted for the aggregate approved QFII and RQFII quotas available to foreign investors" (fact sheet, June 30, 2026).

Launched March 4, 2005, VWO is the ETF share class of Vanguard Emerging Markets Stock Index Fund, the name its prospectus uses; the fund also has investor, Admiral, institutional and institutional plus share classes, per Vanguard's fund data. Vanguard reports $127.3 billion for the ETF share class and $168.5 billion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026. The expense ratio is 0.06%: a 0.02% management fee plus 0.04% other expenses.

Structure
Physical, index sampling
The prospectus: the fund "invests by sampling the Target Index, meaning that it holds a range of securities that, in the aggregate, approximates the full Target Index in terms of key risk factors and other characteristics." Under normal circumstances at least 80% of net assets is in the index's stocks. Vanguard counts 6,348 stocks in the fund against 4,849 in the index (August 31, 2026).
Currency
Local currencies
The shares trade in U.S. dollars, but the stocks underneath are priced in the currencies of more than 20 markets (Taiwan dollar, Hong Kong dollar, yuan, rupee, real and others). The prospectus lists currency risk among the principal risks: "Changes in currency exchange rates can affect the value of the Fund's holdings."

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.06% 6,348 stocks, small caps included $168.5B whole fund Turnover 5.9% China A-shares included Since 2005

The fund's main strength is cost and breadth. Vanguard's fact sheet sets its 0.06% expense ratio against averages of 1.17% for emerging-markets funds and 0.50% for emerging-markets ETFs (Lipper and Vanguard, December 31, 2025). It covers large, mid and small companies, so a single purchase reaches far down the size range in markets where direct access can be difficult. Portfolio turnover was 5.9% in the fiscal year to October 31, 2025, which keeps trading costs inside the fund low.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One company, one industry. TSMC alone is 14.69% of the fund, and five Taiwanese technology companies are 18.31%. The prospectus says the fund "may become nondiversified … solely as a result of tracking an index." A downturn in the semiconductor cycle reaches the whole fund.
02
Three markets carry three-quarters. Taiwan, China and India are 75.7% of the fund. Policy in Beijing, cross-strait relations and Indian valuations each move a large share of the portfolio.
03
Emerging-market plumbing. The prospectus: emerging markets "can have greater custodial and operational risks; less developed legal, tax, regulatory, financial reporting, accounting, and recordkeeping systems" and generally have "less efficient trading markets with lower overall liquidity and more volatile currency exchange rates." On China specifically, the fund "may not be able to access its desired amount" of A-shares or H-shares.
04
Currency. Every holding is priced in a local currency. When the U.S. dollar strengthens against the Taiwan dollar, yuan or rupee, the fund loses value in dollar terms even if local share prices are unchanged.
05
The index provider's map. FTSE's country list decides what VWO owns. South Korea and Poland are excluded as developed markets; Greece's move to developed status was effective September 21, 2026, as was the start of Vietnam's entry, in tranches that run into 2027; Egypt is on FTSE's watch list for demotion to frontier status (April 7, 2026). Each change forces trades.
06
Uneven income. About 69% of the last four dividends came in December, and Vanguard lists no March 2026 payment. A holder relying on quarterly income gets very different amounts each quarter.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Strong semiconductor demand, since TSMC is 14.69% of the fund and Taiwan 32.7%. Rising Chinese and Indian stock markets, which with Taiwan make up 75.7%. A weaker U.S. dollar, which raises the dollar value of holdings priced in local currencies. Recoveries in small and mid-sized emerging-market companies, which this all-cap index holds and large-cap funds do not. Low cost: 0.06% a year leaves more of the market's return with the holder.

What hurts it

A downturn in chips or a shock specific to TSMC. Tension across the Taiwan Strait or policy shifts in Beijing, given the combined 59.2% in Taiwan and China. A stronger dollar. Periods when South Korean stocks lead, since FTSE's index excludes them (IEMG, which includes them, returned 14.1 points more over the last five years). Broad risk-off markets: 2022 cost −17.72% over the calendar year and −30.2% at the daily trough.

Category Commentary
Emerging-markets index ETFs: whose map?
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Broad emerging-markets ETFs look alike but follow three index families with different country lists. FTSE (VWO, SCHE) classifies South Korea and Poland as developed and, from September 21, 2026, Greece too; Iceland is in its secondary-emerging group. MSCI (IEMG, EEM) includes South Korea, which was 20.91% of IEMG and 21.72% of EEM on September 23, 2026. S&P (SPEM) held no Korean won stocks in its September 23 file and 1.40% in Poland. The second difference is size range: VWO, IEMG and SPEM include small caps; EEM and SCHE stop at large and mid caps. Fees in this group run from 0.06% (VWO, SCHE) to 0.72% (EEM).

Taiwan has become the largest market
WHAT CHANGED

In Vanguard's figures Taiwan went from 20.4% of VWO (August 31, 2025) to 32.7% a year later, while China fell from 34.2% to 26.5%. Most of Taiwan's weight is TSMC, so the category has become more exposed to a single semiconductor company than its breadth suggests. In MSCI-based funds South Korea takes about a fifth of the portfolio and Taiwan less (28.41% of IEMG on September 23, 2026).
