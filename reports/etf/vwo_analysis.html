01 Fund Overview

VWO is a single share that holds thousands of companies in the stock markets that index providers class as emerging: Taiwan, mainland China and Hong Kong-listed Chinese companies, India, Brazil, South Africa, Saudi Arabia, Mexico and sixteen smaller markets (Vanguard's August 31, 2026 allocation). The issuer's fund page states the aim: the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of stocks issued by companies located in emerging market countries." The benchmark is the FTSE Emerging Markets All Cap China A Inclusion Index, which Vanguard describes as a market-capitalization-weighted index of "large-, mid-, and small-cap companies located in emerging markets around the world."

Launched March 4, 2005, VWO is the exchange-traded share class of Vanguard Emerging Markets Stock Index Fund, which also has Admiral (VEMAX), Investor (VEIEX), Institutional (VEMIX) and Institutional Plus (VEMRX) share classes, described in February 27, 2026 summary prospectuses on SEC EDGAR (Institutional and Institutional Plus share one). All classes own the same portfolio. Vanguard reports $125.4 billion for the ETF share class (September 30, 2026) and $168.5 billion for the whole fund (August 31, 2026). The expense ratio is 0.06%, made up of a 0.02% management fee and 0.04% other expenses; Vanguard cut it from 0.07% effective February 1, 2026.

What sets this fund apart: FTSE's map of emerging markets

Which countries count as "emerging" is decided by the index provider, and the providers disagree. That choice, more than cost, separates VWO from the other large emerging-markets ETFs.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.06% $125.4B ETF share class 6,348 stocks, all caps 5-yr gap to index −0.03 pts/yr 30-day median spread 0.017% Turnover 5.9%

The fund tracks a very wide index cheaply. Over the five years to September 30, 2026 its NAV returned 6.62% a year against 6.65% for Vanguard's spliced benchmark, a gap of 0.03 percentage points; over ten years the gap was 0.15 points (7.66% vs 7.81%). Turnover was 5.9% in the fiscal year to October 31, 2025 (Vanguard), "6%" in the prospectus. Trading costs in the ETF itself are small: Vanguard shows a 30-day median bid-ask spread of 0.017% in its price data dated October 2 (read October 5). On October 2 the market close ($59.56) sat 0.08% below NAV ($59.61), our calculation.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One company, one island. TSMC is 14.67% of the fund and Taiwan 32.7% of the market allocation. Technology is 36.1% of the fund by ICB sector. Vanguard's risk summary lists nondiversification and concentration among the fund's principal risks; a fall in Taiwanese chip stocks, or a shock to Taiwan itself, would move the whole fund.
02
China-specific risk. China is 26.5% of the market allocation. Vanguard's risk summary names risks for investments tied to China: possible limits on access to A-shares and H-shares, U.S. restrictions or sanctions, and variable interest entities, the contract structures through which some Chinese companies are held; investing through one "does not offer the same level of investor protection as direct ownership."
03
Currencies. Every holding is priced in a foreign currency, and the fund does not hedge. A stronger U.S. dollar lowers the fund's dollar value even if local share prices are unchanged; Vanguard's risk summary says emerging markets generally have "more volatile currency exchange rates."
04
Market access can disappear. When FTSE deleted Russia from its equity indices from March 7, 2022, it cited sanctions and the Russian central bank's ban on non-resident investors selling securities. Russia now shows 0.0% in the fund. Emerging markets, in Vanguard's risk summary, can have "less developed legal, tax, regulatory" systems and "greater political, social, and economic instability."
05
Index rules decide the map. FTSE Russell decides which countries count as emerging. Greece left on September 21, 2026; Vietnam is being added in four steps to September 2027; South Korea is outside the index altogether. Each change moves money in or out of the fund on the index's schedule.
06
Premiums and discounts. The NAV is calculated as of 4 p.m. Eastern time (fact sheet), when the Asian exchanges where most of the portfolio trades are closed, and the ETF can trade away from it. In the third quarter of 2026 the market close was above NAV on 51 of 64 trading days, between −0.46% (September 23) and +0.73% (July 30) (issuer daily data, our count).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising stock markets in Taiwan, China and India, which make up three-quarters of the market allocation. Strength in Asian chipmaking, given TSMC's 14.67% weight and technology's 36.1%. A weaker U.S. dollar, which lifts the dollar value of local-currency holdings. Periods in which emerging markets outpace the U.S.: the holdings trade at 16.1x earnings and 2.5x book value (Vanguard, August 31, 2026). A steady dividend stream, which supplied about half of the five-year total return.

What hurts it

Broad emerging-market sell-offs: −17.72% in calendar 2022 and −30.2% at the daily trough inside this window; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −24.55% (March 31, 2020). A stronger U.S. dollar. Political or trade shocks around China or Taiwan, and U.S. restrictions on Chinese securities, which Vanguard's risk summary names. Market closures or sanctions that make a country uninvestable, as with Russia in 2022. Index changes that remove a market the fund holds.

Top 3 Risks

01
Concentration in TSMC and Taiwan. One company is 14.67% of the fund and one market 32.7% of the allocation. The fund's return depends heavily on a single chipmaker and on Taiwan's market and currency.
02
Emerging-market and China risk. China is 26.5% of the allocation, with the access, sanctions and VIE risks Vanguard's risk summary sets out. The 2022 drawdown (−30.2% daily peak to trough) and the deletion of Russia from FTSE's indices show how quickly losses and market access can change. Five-year volatility is 14.74% a year (our calculation).
03
Currency exposure without a hedge. All holdings are priced in foreign currencies. For a U.S.-dollar investor, the dollar's moves against the New Taiwan dollar, the renminbi, the rupee and the other local currencies pass straight into the fund's return.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

"Emerging markets" funds follow an index provider's country list, and the lists differ. South Korea: FTSE classes it as Developed, so VWO holds none, and SPEM's file has no Korean lines; the MSCI indexes behind IEMG and EEM include it, at 21–22% of those funds on October 2, 2026, where Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix rank second and third. Company size: VWO and IEMG reach into small caps (6,348 and 2,860 holdings); EEM holds large and mid caps (1,186). Cost: 0.06% (VWO) to 0.72% (EEM). Distributions: VWO pays quarterly; the three peers semi-annually.

Tracking a moving index
WHAT DIFFERS

The country lists change. FTSE dropped Russia in March 2022, added Iceland in September 2022, moved Greece up to Developed and began adding Vietnam on September 21, 2026. China's A-shares enter through quota-adjusted weights. A fund that tracks such an index buys and sells on the provider's schedule, and its holdings count can run well above the index's (6,348 vs 4,849 for VWO) because it samples and keeps small positions. Over five years VWO's NAV return stayed within 0.03 percentage points a year of its benchmark.
