01 Fund Overview

VXUS is a single share in almost the whole stock market outside the United States: large, mid and small companies in developed and emerging countries. Its February 27, 2026 prospectus states the objective: it "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of stocks issued by companies located in developed and emerging markets, excluding the United States." The index is the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index, and the fund "uses the replication method of indexing, meaning that the fund generally holds the same stocks as those in its Target Index and in approximately the same proportions."

Launched January 26, 2011, VXUS is the ETF share class of Vanguard Total International Stock Index Fund, a series of Vanguard STAR Funds, whose other share classes are mutual funds. Vanguard reports $164.9 billion for the ETF share class and $665.7 billion for the whole fund at August 31, 2026 (June 30 fact sheet: $156,510 million and $651,036 million). All share classes own the same portfolio. The expense ratio is 0.05%: a 0.03% management fee and 0.02% other expenses.

Why this fund: the largest of the group by net assets

Basis: each ETF's own net assets on June 30, 2026, the latest date with an exact figure from both issuers' fact sheets. VXUS's ETF share class held $156,510 million, 2.67 times IXUS's $58,515.16 million (our calculation). The latest figures keep the same order: VXUS $164.9 billion (Aug 31), IXUS $59.17 billion (Sep 24).

Structure
Physical, full replication
Vanguard's August 31, 2026 file lists 8,794 equity lines worth $659.47 billion, 99.1% of the whole fund's net assets (our calculation). Most are ordinary shares; the file also carries 227 REITs, 75 depositary receipts, 51 preferred shares, rights, warrants and one ETF, the sister fund Vanguard FTSE Emerging Markets ETF ($0.77 billion). It also lists 7 index futures, 126 currency forwards and 61 swap and spot-currency lines; the prospectus says derivatives are used "to help stay fully invested and to reduce transaction costs."
Currency
Foreign currencies pass through
The fund owns stocks priced in yen, pounds, euros, Taiwan dollars, won and dozens of other currencies; foreign holdings are 99.0% (Vanguard, Aug 31, 2026). The prospectus lists currency risk as a principal risk: "Changes in currency exchange rates can affect the value of the Fund's holdings." A weaker U.S. dollar raises the dollar value of those holdings; a stronger one lowers it.
Distributions
Quarterly, uneven
Income dividends "generally are distributed quarterly in March, June, September, and December; capital gains distributions, if any, generally occur annually in December" (prospectus). The last four dividends were $1.3631 (record date Dec 19, 2025), $0.0795 (Mar 20, 2026), $0.3861 (Jun 18, 2026) and $0.1562 (Sep 18, 2026). All seven payments the issuer lists since March 2025 are dividends.
Foreign tax
Pass-through election
The prospectus: if more than half of the fund's assets are foreign securities and the fund "makes a special election," shareholders include their share of the foreign taxes the fund paid in income and "may qualify for an offsetting credit or deduction under U.S. tax laws," with "no assurance that the Fund will make this election for a taxable year." Vanguard's after-tax figures (Jun 30, 2026): 5-year NAV return 8.81% before tax, 7.92% after tax on distributions.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

Expense ratio 0.05% 8,790 stocks, 49 markets $665.7B whole fund 5-yr gap to index −0.03 pts/yr Turnover 4.4% 30-day median spread 0.011%

The fund owns the index, and its results have stayed close to it. Over the five years to August 31, 2026 its NAV returned 9.17% a year against 9.20% for Vanguard's spliced benchmark, a gap of 0.03 percentage points; over ten years 9.64% against 9.72%. Turnover was 4.4% in the fiscal year to October 31, 2025. Trading is cheap: Vanguard's price data show a 30-day median bid-ask spread of 0.011%. The market close ($85.59) sat 0.21% above NAV ($85.41) on September 24. NAV is struck as of 4 p.m. Eastern time (fact sheet), and the prospectus provides for fair-value pricing when a security's value "has been materially affected by events occurring after the close of the principal exchange or market …"

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Currency. Nearly all holdings are priced in foreign currencies. For a U.S.-dollar investor, returns are the local stock returns plus or minus the change in those currencies against the dollar, and the prospectus lists currency risk among principal risks.
02
Emerging markets and China. Emerging markets are 26.21% of the fund (issuer region data). The prospectus flags "greater custodial and operational risks" there, and a separate risk for investments economically tied to China, including access limits on A-shares and H-shares, possible U.S. restrictions or sanctions, and exposure through variable interest entities (VIEs), which "does not offer the same level of investor protection as direct ownership."
03
A semiconductor cluster at the top. Taiwan Semiconductor, Samsung, SK hynix and ASML are 9.00% of the stock portfolio (our sum). Taiwan is 8.7% and Korea 6.4% of the fund, up from 5.6% and 3.2% a year earlier, much of it through these companies.
04
Index rules decide the map. FTSE sets which countries are developed, emerging or excluded. The September 2026 review moved Greece to developed status and began adding Vietnam in four steps; the fund follows each change.
05
Uneven income. Quarterly dividends ranged from $0.0795 (March 2026) to $1.3631 (December 2025) over the last four quarters. The trailing yield (2.32%) depends heavily on the December payment.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at market prices that can differ from NAV; the prospectus lists ETF share trading and reliance on authorized participants among principal risks.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising stock markets outside the U.S., since the fund is its index less 0.05% a year. A weaker U.S. dollar, which raises the dollar value of foreign holdings. Strength in Asian semiconductors, the largest cluster at the top (Taiwan Semiconductor, Samsung, SK hynix and ASML together 9.00%). Periods when non-U.S. stocks outperform U.S. stocks, as in 2025 (+32.23% NAV against +17.88% for the S&P 500 total-return index). A lower starting valuation than U.S. large caps: 16.4x earnings and 2.2x book value for the holdings (Aug 31, 2026).

What hurts it

Global bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion. 2022 cost −15.99% over the calendar year and −28.9% at the daily trough; the worst quarter in the prospectus is −24.30% (March 31, 2020). A stronger U.S. dollar. Setbacks in emerging markets and China-linked companies, including sanctions or access limits named in the prospectus. Long stretches of U.S. outperformance: over the five years to September 24, 2026 the S&P 500's total return was 92.07% against VXUS's 57.66%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full equity market risk, worldwide. A 100% stock portfolio takes market falls in full: −28.9% daily peak to trough in 2022 and −13.6% in spring 2025 inside this five-year window. Five-year volatility is 15.21% a year (our calculation), close to the S&P 500's 15.84%.
02
Currency and country risk. Returns for a U.S.-dollar holder depend on dozens of currencies and on political and legal conditions in 49 markets, a quarter of them by weight in emerging markets (26.21%). The prospectus lists foreign markets, emerging markets, currency and China-linked investments as principal risks.
03
Chip concentration inside broad diversification. The top ten holdings are only 13.16%, but four semiconductor companies make up 9.00% of the stock portfolio, and Taiwan and Korea together are 15.1% of the fund (issuer, Aug 31, 2026), up from 8.8% a year earlier.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

VXUS, IXUS and VEU each hold developed and emerging markets outside the U.S. in one fund; by weight, 92.2% of VXUS overlaps with IXUS and 91.2% with VEU (our calculation).
