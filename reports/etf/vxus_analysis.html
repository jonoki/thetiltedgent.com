01 Fund Overview

VXUS holds close to 8,900 stocks from outside the United States in a single U.S.-listed share: companies of every size in the developed economies of Europe, the Pacific region, Canada and Israel, and in the emerging markets of Asia, Latin America, Eastern Europe, the Middle East and Africa. Its February 27, 2026 summary prospectus says the fund "seeks to track the performance of a benchmark index that measures the investment return of stocks issued by companies located in developed and emerging markets, excluding the United States." That index is the FTSE Global All Cap ex US Index, which the prospectus describes as "a float-adjusted, market capitalization-weighted index": each company counts in proportion to the market value of the shares available to public investors. Vanguard's fact sheet says the index "covers 98% of the world's non-U.S. markets."

VXUS started trading on January 26, 2011. The prospectus calls it "an exchange-traded share class of Vanguard Total International Stock Index Fund," a series of Vanguard STAR Funds; all share classes own the same stocks. Vanguard reports $163.2 billion in the ETF share class and $649.3 billion for the whole fund (both September 30, 2026). The expense ratio is 0.05%: a 0.03% management fee plus 0.02% other expenses.

Structure
Physical, replication
In the prospectus's words, the fund "uses the replication method of indexing, meaning that the fund generally holds the same stocks as those in its Target Index and in approximately the same proportions," and invests at least 80% of net assets in the index's stocks. On September 30, 2026 Vanguard counted 8,901 stocks in the fund against 8,675 in the index.
Currency
Dozens of currencies
The shares trade in U.S. dollars, but the stocks are priced in yen, Taiwan dollars, pounds, Canadian dollars, yuan and Hong Kong dollars, won, euros, rupees and more, so a U.S.-dollar investor carries the currency moves. The prospectus lists currency risk: "foreign currency will perform differently than U.S. dollars and increase the potential loss to the Fund."

Last four dividends (record dates): $1.3631 (Dec 19, 2025), $0.0795 (Mar 20, 2026), $0.3861 (Jun 18, 2026) and $0.1562 (Sep 18, 2026). Structural strengths:

The fund owns its index and has stayed close to it. Key Vulnerabilities

01
Currency. Returns in U.S. dollars combine the stocks' local-currency moves with the currencies' moves against the dollar. A rising dollar lowers the fund's value even when foreign stocks are flat in their own currencies. The prospectus lists currency risk as a principal risk.
02
Emerging markets. 26.6% of the fund sits in emerging markets (Vanguard's region data, September 30, 2026). The prospectus warns that these "can have greater custodial and operational risks; less developed legal, tax, regulatory, financial reporting, accounting, and recordkeeping systems; and greater political, social, and economic instability than developed markets."
03
Chip concentration at the top. TSMC, Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and ASML are 9.45% together, plus 0.25% in Samsung preferred shares, and Taiwan and Korea together are 16.0% of the fund (our sum, September 30, 2026). Their combined weight in the country list rose from 8.8% to 15.1% over the year to August 31, 2026.
04
Country weights. Japan is 16.1% of the fund. The prospectus warns that a fund with a large share in one country or region "may be hurt disproportionately by the poor performance of its investments in such country or region."
05
Index rules decide membership. FTSE Russell decides which markets are in the index and how they are classified. Greece moved from Advanced Emerging to Developed status on September 21, 2026, and Vietnam's entry as a Secondary Emerging market is being phased in from September 2026 into 2027; the fund follows such changes.
06
Uneven income and foreign markets closed during U.S. trading. The December 2025 dividend ($1.3631) was 69% of the 12-month total, so quarterly income swings widely. VXUS trades during U.S. hours, when Asian markets are closed and European markets close partway through the session, so its price can move away from the value of the holdings at their last local prices.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising stock markets outside the U.S., developed and emerging alike, since the fund is their index less 0.05% a year. A weaker U.S. dollar, which raises the dollar value of foreign holdings. Periods in which non-U.S. companies outperform U.S. ones, as in 2025, when the fund's NAV returned +32.23% against +17.88% for the S&P 500 total-return index (Yahoo ^SP500TR, our calculation). Strength in banks and industrial companies, two of the three largest sectors (23.1% and 15.2%), and in the Taiwanese, Korean and Dutch chip companies at the top of the holdings list. The holdings trade at 16.1x earnings (VOO's at 24.7x, Vanguard, September 30, 2026), and the trailing distribution yield is 2.40%.

What hurts it

Broad global bear markets: the fund is all stocks, with no bond or cash cushion; 2022 cost −15.99% over the calendar year and −28.9% at the daily trough. A stronger U.S. dollar, which lowers the dollar value of foreign holdings. Downturns concentrated in Japan (16.1%) or in emerging markets (26.6%), including political or market-access shocks in China, Taiwan or India. A slump in chips, given the four chip companies at the top. Long periods in which U.S. stocks lead: over the five years shown, VXUS's total return was 28.0 percentage points behind the S&P 500's.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full equity market risk across more than forty countries. A 100% stock portfolio takes market falls in full: −28.9% daily peak to trough in 2021–22 and −13.6% in spring 2025 inside this window. Five-year volatility is 15.24% a year (our calculation); Vanguard rates the fund 5 of 5, "aggressive".
02
Currency and emerging-market risk. The stocks are priced in dozens of currencies, and the dollar's moves pass straight into the return of a U.S.-dollar investor. 26.6% of the fund is in emerging markets, where the prospectus cites greater custodial, operational and political risks.
03
Country, chip and index-rule concentration. Japan is 16.1% of the fund and Taiwan and Korea together 16.0%, with four chip companies the largest holdings. Which countries are in at all, and how they are classified, is FTSE Russell's decision; its October 6, 2026 announcement kept Egypt as a Secondary Emerging market and added Oman to the watch list for a possible upgrade from Frontier.

The dollar is part of the product
WHAT DIFFERS FROM A U.S. FUND

An unhedged international fund delivers two returns at once: the foreign stocks' returns in their own currencies, and the change in those currencies against the U.S. dollar. Over the five years to November 2, 2026 the combination gave VXUS +47.9% with dividends, against +75.8% for the S&P 500. Its holdings trade at lower multiples (16.1x earnings and 2.2x book value, against 24.7x and 5.3x for VOO's holdings, Vanguard, September 30, 2026), and its sector mix leans to banks and industrial companies: Financials are 23.1% and Technology 18.7% of VXUS (September 30, 2026), against 11.4% Financials and 39.7% Information Technology in VOO (September 30, 2026).
