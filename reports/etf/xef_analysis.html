01 Fund Overview

XEF is a Canadian-dollar index fund for the developed stock markets outside North America — Japan, the United Kingdom, continental Europe, Australia, Israel, Hong Kong, Singapore and New Zealand. Its objective, in the issuer's words: "Seeks long-term capital growth by replicating the performance of the MSCI EAFE® Investable Market Index, net of expenses." The ETF Facts describe that index as "a free-float market capitalization-weighted index that includes securities from developed market countries or jurisdictions (as defined by the index provider), excluding the U.S. and Canada." IMI means small companies are included as well as large and mid caps, which is why the fund holds about 2,500 stocks rather than several hundred.

Launched April 10, 2013, the CAD units had CAD 23.90 billion of net assets and 463,100,000 units outstanding on September 24, 2026. The MER on the product page and the August 2026 fact sheet is 0.22%, on a management fee of 0.20%; the June 2026 ETF Facts show 0.23% for 2025 (see section 04).

XEF's strength is breadth at low concentration: about 2,500 companies located in 22 countries (issuer's "Location" field, look-through), with the ten largest at 12% of the fund. Tracking has been close: over five years to August 31, 2026 the fund returned 10.82% a year against 10.83% for its index (issuer). The fund pays no trailing commission to advisers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Currency, unhedged. 99.8% of the look-through portfolio trades in currencies other than the Canadian dollar, led by the euro (31.9%) and yen (26.1%). A rising Canadian dollar against those currencies lowers XEF's value in CAD even if foreign share prices do not move. XFH is the hedged alternative from the same issuer.
02
Japan and Europe carry the fund. Japan is 26.04% and the European countries together about 61% (our sum). Financials (24.44%) and industrials (19.48%) are 44% of the fund, so bank profits and European and Japanese industrial cycles drive much of the result.
03
Price vs NAV gaps. The TSX price can sit away from the NAV per unit: on September 24 the close was $51.93 against a NAV of $51.61, 0.61% above. On the 226 days in the twelve months to September 24 for which the issuer's history gives both, the gap ranged from −0.98% (May 7, 2026) to +2.12% (March 9, 2026) (our calculation). A buyer or seller on a given day can pay or receive more or less than the value of the holdings.
04
Other funds own a large share of it. On September 23, XEQT held 106,202,193 XEF units (CAD 5,516.1M) and XFH held 63,047,748 (CAD 3,274.7M) — together about 36% of the CAD 24,196.9M market value of XEF's own holdings that day (our calculation from the three issuers' September 23 holdings files; XEF's figure covers both unit classes). Rebalancing or flows in those two funds show up as creations and redemptions in XEF.
05
A U.S.-listed fund inside. Part of the portfolio sits in IEFA (0.97% on September 23; 3.67% on August 31). The fact sheet states that XEF's MER "includes the fund's proportionate share of the MER, if any, of any underlying fund in which the fund has invested." The weight changes from month to month.
06
Tax frictions. Foreign tax of CAD 0.10686 per unit was paid in 2024 on CAD 1.02906 of foreign income. Reinvested year-end distributions (CAD 0.32991 per unit in December 2025) are taxable in non-registered accounts without cash being paid out.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising stock markets in Japan, Europe and Australia, since the fund is almost entirely equities. Strong bank and insurer profits, as financials are 24.44% of the fund, and strong industrial cycles (19.48%). Stronger euro, yen, pound and Swiss franc against the Canadian dollar, which lift the CAD value of an unhedged portfolio. Periods when non-U.S. markets outpace the U.S., as in calendar 2025 (+25.36% NAV). Breadth across about 2,500 companies means no single company decides the outcome; ASML, the largest, is 2.64%.

What hurts it

Broad equity bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion. 2022 cost −9.27% over the calendar year and −23.5% at the daily trough, and the worst 3-month period of the past ten years was −15.97% (to March 31, 2020). A stronger Canadian dollar against the euro and yen. Weakness in European and Japanese banks and industrials. When U.S. technology leads global markets, XEF lags a U.S. fund, as it did from 2021 to 2026 (section 07); information technology is only 10.62% of XEF.

Category Commentary
Canada's international developed-market index ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

These funds give Canadian investors the developed stock markets outside North America in one TSX-listed unit. The main versions differ in which index draws the line. Company size: XEF's MSCI EAFE IMI index includes small caps (2,466 holdings); ZEA's MSCI EAFE index covers large and mid caps, which BMO's fact sheet says is "approximately 85% of the free float-adjusted market capitalization in each country" (676 securities). Country list: VIU's FTSE index adds South Korea, whose Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are VIU's two largest holdings; XEF holds no Korean companies. Currency: XEF, VIU and ZEA are unhedged; XFH hedges XEF's portfolio back to CAD with forwards. Payout rhythm: semi-annual (XEF, XFH), quarterly (VIU, ZEA). MERs across the four Canadian funds are 0.21% to 0.22%.

Funds inside funds
WHAT DIFFERS

Canadian international funds often hold U.S.-listed ETFs on the same index alongside the stocks: XEF held IEFA at 0.97% on September 23, 2026 and ZEA held it at 5.14% on August 31, 2026. BMO's fact sheet states that ZEA's management fees "are reduced by the management fees paid on the underlying ETFs"; XEF's fact sheet states that its MER includes its share of any underlying fund's MER. The flow also runs the other way: XEQT and XFH together held XEF units worth about 36% of XEF's portfolio on September 23, 2026. Five-year returns to August 31, 2026 were 10.82% a year for XEF and 11.13% for ZEA (each issuer's fact sheet); the two track different indices.
