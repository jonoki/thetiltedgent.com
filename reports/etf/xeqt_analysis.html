01 Fund Overview

XEQT is a single ticket for a global stock portfolio. It does not buy stocks itself: it holds five other iShares ETFs that together cover Canada, the United States, developed markets outside North America and emerging markets. Its stated objective, in the issuer's words: "The Fund seeks to provide long-term capital growth by investing primarily in one or more exchange-traded funds managed by BlackRock Canada or an affiliate that provide exposure to equity securities." The portfolio targets a strategic allocation of 100% equity and is, per the issuer, "continuously monitored and automatically rebalanced, as needed, in order to maintain asset class target weights."

Launched August 7, 2019, it had CAD 23.46 billion of net assets and 525,200,000 units outstanding on November 2, 2026. The issuer lists no benchmark index for XEQT itself — the indices sit one level down, in the underlying funds. Its management fee of 0.17% (cut from 0.18% on December 18, 2025) "includes all underlying ETF management fees," and the MER shown on the product page and the August 2026 fact sheet is 0.19% (the June 2026 ETF Facts show 0.20% for 2025; see section 04).

Structure
Fund of funds
The October 30, 2026 holdings file lists only five iShares ETFs plus CAD and USD cash (0.09% combined) — no swaps or derivatives at the XEQT level. One layer deeper, XTOT is itself a wrapper: 99.93% of it is ITOT, the U.S.-listed fund XEQT also holds directly.
Currency hedging
Not hedged
XEQT holds the unhedged versions of its U.S. and international funds (XTOT and XEF; their issuer pages say "This exposure is also available hedged to the Canadian dollar" in XTOH and XFH). XEQT's own product page does not state a hedging policy. About 75% of the look-through portfolio sits outside Canada, so the Canadian dollar's moves feed straight into returns.
Distributions
Quarterly
Ex-dates in March, June, September and December; the December distribution is paid in early January. Year-end distributions in 2021, 2022 and 2025 also carried a reinvested (non-cash) portion. The issuer's ETF Facts note that in a non-registered account distributions count as taxable income "whether you get them in cash or have them reinvested."
Foreign withholding tax (2025)
CAD 0.0485 / unit
The issuer's 2025 tax breakdown shows CAD 0.43118 per unit of foreign income and CAD 0.04850 of foreign tax paid — about 11.2% of that income (our calculation). Tax withheld abroad on dividends reduces what reaches the fund; how much a holder can recover depends on account type, which is not covered here.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

8,273 underlying holdings Automatic rebalancing to targets Mgmt fee 0.17% incl. underlying fees CAD 23.5B net assets Avg bid-ask spread 0.03% No trailing commission

What the structure does well is remove decisions. An investor who built this mix from the five funds would have to rebalance by hand, handle five sets of distributions and pay five bid-ask spreads. XEQT does all of that inside the fund for one MER. Trading is liquid: in the twelve months to April 30, 2026 the ETF Facts report an average daily volume of 1,898,649 units, trading on 251 of 251 days, and an average bid-ask spread of 0.03%. The fund pays no trailing commission to advisers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. The U.S. is 45.64% of the portfolio and information technology is 22.51%. Apple, NVIDIA and Microsoft alone are 8.60% (our sum). Broad diversification by count does not stop a sell-off in U.S. large-cap technology from hitting the fund hard.
02
Canadian home tilt. A 25% target in XIC puts a quarter of the money in one mid-sized market. That market leans on banks, energy and materials; financials are XEQT's second-largest sector at 21.25%, and five Canadian banks (Royal Bank, TD, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC) rank among its 16 largest look-through holdings.
03
Currency, unhedged. Roughly 75% of the portfolio is outside Canada (100% − 24.34% Canada − 0.46% cash), and none of it is hedged. When the Canadian dollar rises, foreign holdings are worth less in CAD. The reverse has helped recently: USD/CAD went from 1.2738 at end-November 2021 to 1.3970 on November 2, 2026 (Yahoo Finance CAD=X), adding to the CAD value of U.S. holdings.
04
Fee layering and the wrapper. The management fee covers the underlying funds' fees, and the MER includes the fund's share of their MERs. There is still a layer between XEQT and its largest U.S. holding: XTOT (MER 0.08%) holds ITOT (expense ratio 0.03%). It exists because of limits on sales of U.S.-domiciled ETF shares to non-U.S. funds. If those limits bind harder, more of the U.S. sleeve moves into the wrapper.
05
Tax frictions. Foreign tax of CAD 0.0485 per unit was paid in 2025 on CAD 0.43118 of foreign income. Reinvested year-end distributions (CAD 0.32215 per unit in December 2025) are taxable in non-registered accounts without any cash being paid out.
06
No single benchmark. XEQT has no index of its own, so there is no published tracking-error figure against a benchmark. Its results reflect the issuer's chosen weights (for example, 25% Canada) as much as the markets.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising global stock markets, since the fund is 99.91% equities. Strength in large U.S. technology companies, four of the top ten look-through holdings (Apple, NVIDIA, Microsoft and Broadcom alone are 9.56%). A weaker Canadian dollar, which raises the CAD value of the roughly 75% held abroad, as it did between 2021 and 2026. Strong Canadian banks, energy and materials, through the 25% Canada sleeve. Diversification across 8,273 securities and automatic rebalancing mean no single company decides the outcome.

What hurts it

Broad equity bear markets: there is no bond or cash cushion. 2022 cost −10.93% over the calendar year and −19.0% at the daily trough, and the worst 3-month period since launch was −16.41% (to March 31, 2020). A sell-off in U.S. mega-cap technology, given the top-10 concentration. A stronger Canadian dollar, which shrinks the CAD value of foreign holdings. Weak Canadian banks or commodity prices, through the home tilt. When the rest of the world lags the U.S., XEQT trails a U.S.-only fund; the section 07 comparison suggests this happened from 2021 to 2026.

Top 3 Risks

01
Full equity market risk. A 100% stock allocation means drawdowns arrive in full: −19.0% daily peak to trough in 2021–22 and −15.1% in early 2025 inside this five-year window, and −16.41% over three months in early 2020. Five-year volatility is 11.54% a year (our calculation).
02
Unhedged currency exposure. About 75% of the portfolio is priced in other currencies. The U.S. dollar's 9.7% rise against the Canadian dollar from November 2021 to November 2026 helped. A reversal would reduce CAD returns even if foreign markets were flat.
03
Concentration inside the diversification. The ten largest look-through holdings are 17.69% of the fund, information technology is 22.51%, and U.S. stocks are 45.64%. The fund's return depends heavily on a small group of U.S. companies.

Canada's one-ticket equity portfolios
PRODUCT CATEGORY

All-in-one asset-allocation ETFs package several index funds into one listed unit with a fixed target mix, rebalanced by the manager. The all-equity versions from four issuers (XEQT, VEQT, ZEQT, HEQT) look alike from a distance but make different choices. Canada weight: XEQT targets 25% (XIC was 25.72% on October 30, 2026); VEQT's underlying Canadian fund was 30.6% at August 31, 2026; ZEQT's Canadian index ETF was 25.56%; HEQT's TSX 60 fund was 20.76%. U.S. coverage: XEQT and VEQT use total-market funds; ZEQT pairs an S&P 500 fund with mid- and small-cap sleeves; HEQT combines S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 and Russell 2000 funds. Emerging markets: 5% target at XEQT, 7.0% at VEQT, 8.97% at ZEQT, 8.71% at HEQT (each issuer's most recent published date). Payout rhythm: quarterly (XEQT, ZEQT), annual (VEQT), monthly (HEQT). MERs across the four range from 0.18% to 0.22%.

Returns look alike because the markets are the same
WHAT DIFFERS

The issuers' own three-year NAV returns to August 31, 2026 are close together: XEQT 21.82%, VEQT 21.96%, ZEQT 21.80% (annualised, from each issuer). With the same broad markets underneath, return gaps between these funds can come from regional weights, how much small-cap stock each holds, and fees. The fee differences are a few hundredths of a percentage point a year. HEQT's page says it does not hedge currency, and XEQT's holdings show no hedged funds.
