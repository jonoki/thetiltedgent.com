01 Fund Overview

XIC is a one-ticket holding of the Canadian stock market. It buys the stocks of the S&P/TSX Capped Composite Index directly: 217 companies on September 23, 2026, every one of them listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange and priced in Canadian dollars. Its objective, in the issuer's words, is to seek "long-term capital growth by replicating the performance of the S&P®/TSX® Capped Composite Index, net of expenses." The issuer describes the index as "the largest (by market capitalization) and most liquid securities listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange, selected by S&P Dow Jones Indices LLC using its industrial classifications and guidelines for evaluating issuer capitalization, liquidity and fundamentals."

Launched February 16, 2001, XIC had CAD 34.73 billion of net assets and 610,200,000 units outstanding on September 24, 2026. The management fee is 0.05% and the MER 0.06%. Its largest single holder is another iShares fund: XEQT, the iShares Core Equity ETF Portfolio, held 100,697,352 XIC units worth CAD 5.77 billion on September 23, 2026, 25.24% of XEQT and about 16.5% of XIC (our calculation from the two funds' holdings files, both dated September 23, 2026).

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

MER 0.06% 217 Canadian stocks Holds the stocks directly CAD 34.7B net assets No currency conversion 25 years of history

XIC does one thing: it owns the index. The gap between the fund and its benchmark is small and steady — over the ten years to July 31, 2026 the issuer reports 12.47% a year for the fund against 12.50% for the index, a difference of 0.03 percentage points a year, and over one year 32.16% against 32.26%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Three sectors carry the fund. Financials, materials and energy are 69.43% of XIC; health care is 0.33% and consumer discretionary 2.86%. Information technology is 8.14%, and three companies — Shopify, Celestica and Constellation Software — are 7.18% of that (our sum).
02
Bank concentration. Six banks are 23.91% of the fund. A domestic credit or housing shock would reach nearly a quarter of the portfolio at once.
03
Commodity prices. Gold and precious-metals producers (8.80% in the five largest alone), oil and gas producers and pipelines tie a large share of returns to commodity cycles the fund cannot hedge.
04
Single-country exposure. 97.96% of the fund is in companies located in Canada (issuer location field). There is no offset from other economies or currencies inside the fund.
05
Non-cash distributions. Year-end reinvested distributions (CAD 1.07857 per unit in December 2022) are taxable in non-registered accounts without cash being paid. Yahoo Finance books the December 2021 one as a 987:1000 "split", a data-site artifact rather than a unit split.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Strong Canadian bank earnings, since six banks are 23.91% of the fund. Rising gold and precious-metals prices, through the five precious-metals companies among the materials leaders (8.80%). Firm oil, gas and uranium markets for the 16.15% in energy. Growth at the few large technology names (Shopify 4.76%, Celestica, Constellation Software). Rising Canadian equity markets generally: the fund holds 217 stocks and tracks its index within about 0.1 percentage point a year.

What hurts it

A domestic credit or housing downturn that hits the banks. Falling commodity prices, which reach materials and energy — together 35.24% of the fund. Periods when global markets are led by sectors Canada barely has, such as health care (0.33%) and consumer technology. Broad equity bear markets: the fund is 99.73% stocks, fell 16.3% peak to trough on daily data in 2022, and the market's downturns arrive in full.

01

02
Full equity market risk. Five-year volatility is 12.47% a year (our calculation); the deepest fall in the window was −16.3% (April–July 2022).
03
One country. 97.96% of the fund is in companies located in Canada. Canadian economic and policy conditions drive nearly all of the return, and there is no foreign-currency offset inside the fund.
Category Commentary
Canada's core index funds
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Broad Canadian equity index ETFs split into two index families and two structures. Index: XIC and ZCN track the S&P/TSX Capped Composite; VCN tracks the FTSE Canada All Cap Domestic Index. Their August 31, 2026 portfolios overlapped by 97.4% (XIC vs VCN, our calculation), and the sector mixes are close: financials 34.9%, basic materials 20.4% and energy 15.4% at VCN (ICB sectors, Vanguard fact sheet) against 34.19%, 19.09% and 16.15% at XIC (GICS-style, September 23). Structure: XIC, ZCN and VCN hold the stocks and pay quarterly distributions; Global X's HXCN reaches the same index through a total return swap and pays distributions only at the manager's discretion. Cost: MERs across XIC, ZCN, VCN and HXCN run from 0.05% to 0.06%; the S&P/TSX 60 funds (XIU 0.17%, HXT 0.08%) cover 60 of the largest names only.

Returns look alike because the market is the same
WHAT DIFFERS
