01 Fund Overview

XIU owns the 60 stocks of the S&P/TSX 60 Index directly, in about the same proportions as the index. Its objective, in the issuer's words: the fund "Seeks long-term capital growth by replicating the performance of the S&P/TSX 60 Index, net of expenses." The June 2026 interim management report confirms the method: "The Fund's current principal investment strategy is to employ a replicating strategy." The ETF Facts describe the index: "The Index is comprised of 60 of the largest (by market capitalization) and most liquid (as determined by the index provider) constituents of the S&P/TSX Composite Index. Sectors are intended to mirror sector weights of the S&P/TSX Composite Index." Within the index, each company is weighted by the market value of its freely traded shares (BMO's ZIU fact sheet calls it "a float adjusted market capitalization-weighted index").

On October 2, 2026 the fund had CAD 23,361.5 million of net assets and 442,500,000 units outstanding (product page). The MER was 0.17% annualised for the six months to June 30, 2026, down from 0.18% in each year from 2021 to 2025 (interim MRFP; section 04).

Where it came from

The product page lists among the reasons to own XIU that it "Started trading in 1990, making it the first ETF in the world", while giving the fund's own inception date as September 28, 1999. The 1990 date belongs to XIU's predecessor. That year the Toronto Stock Exchange launched the Toronto 35 Index Participation Fund, known as TIPs, which tracked the Toronto 35 Index of the exchange's 35 largest companies; The Globe and Mail (February 19, 2017) calls it "the world's first successful exchange-traded fund" and says TIPs is "now known as the iShares S&P/TSX 60 index ETF, and owned by BlackRock Inc." CBC News (2010) describes the same 1990 launch.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Three sectors. Financials, energy and materials are 71.27% of the fund; consumer companies are 6.45% and there is no health care company. The index is built to mirror the sector weights of the broader S&P/TSX Composite, so the tilt is a feature of the Canadian market itself.
02
Banks. Six Canadian banks are 29.79% of the fund (our sum), so their earnings, loan losses and dividends move a large part of the result.
03
Commodity prices. Energy and materials together are 30.80%. Oil and gas prices and the gold price feed directly into those companies' profits.
04
One country, large companies only. 97.86% of the fund is in companies the issuer locates in Canada (98.01% with Canadian-dollar cash); there is no small-company or international exposure. The ten largest positions are 47.53% of the fund, and Shopify alone 6.43%.
05
Higher fee than its peers. The MER of 0.17% compares with 0.06% for XIC and 0.08% for HXT (section 05).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising Canadian large-cap stocks, since the fund holds nothing else. Strong results and dividends from the six big banks (29.79% of the fund). Higher oil, gas and gold prices, which lift the energy and materials companies (30.80% together). Close tracking and a trading expense ratio of 0.00% keep the result near the index's.

What hurts it

A Canadian bear market, with no other country, bonds or cash to offset it (2022: −6.36% for the calendar year; −16.35% peak to trough on daily data). Bank stress such as rising loan losses. Falling commodity prices. Periods when technology and health care lead markets, since the fund has 10.34% in technology and no health care.

Top 3 Risks

01
Sector concentration. Financials, energy and materials are 71.27% of the fund; the six banks alone 29.79%.
02
Single-market equity risk. Everything rides on 60 large Canadian companies: daily peak-to-trough falls of −16.35% (2022) and −12.30% (early 2025); five-year volatility 12.34% (our calculations).
03
Commodity cycles. 30.80% of the fund is in oil and gas, pipelines, uranium, fertilizer and metals companies whose profits follow commodity prices.

PRODUCT CATEGORY

XIU, ZIU and HXT all track the S&P/TSX 60 in Canadian dollars on the TSX. Direct holding: XIU and ZIU own the 60 stocks and pay quarterly distributions. Swap: HXT owns no stocks; it receives the index's total return from bank counterparties, and Global X says investors in its swap-based funds "are not expected to receive any taxable distributions." Size differs widely: CAD 23.4 billion for XIU and 5.5 billion for HXT on October 2, 2026, and 0.3 billion for ZIU on August 31. The S&P/TSX Capped Composite (XIC, 217 holdings) adds the smaller companies of the Canadian market; on October 2, 2026 the 60 large companies were 80.04% of XIC (our calculation). MERs range from 0.06% (XIC) to 0.17% (XIU).
