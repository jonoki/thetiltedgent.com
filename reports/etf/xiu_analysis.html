01 Fund Overview

XIU holds the 60 companies of the S&P/TSX 60 Index, a large-company slice of the Toronto Stock Exchange. Its objective, in the issuer's words: "Seeks long-term capital growth by replicating the performance of the S&P/TSX 60 Index, net of expenses." The ETF Facts describe the index as "comprised of 60 of the largest (by market capitalization) and most liquid (as determined by the index provider) constituents of the S&P/TSX Composite Index. Sectors are intended to mirror sector weights of the S&P/TSX Composite Index." The fund owns the stocks directly: "The Fund's current principal investment strategy is to employ a replicating strategy" (interim MRFP, June 30, 2026).

XIU itself started on September 28, 1999 as the iUnits S&P/TSE 60 Index Participation Fund (the date in the ETF Facts and on the product page). Its roots go back to 1990: the Toronto 35 Index Participation Units (TIPs), launched on the Toronto Stock Exchange on March 9, 1990, which TSX calls "the world's first exchange-traded, index-linked product" (TMX Group release, March 6, 2025). After the unitholders approved it on February 28, 2000, the TIPs 35 and TIPs 100 funds were merged into the iUnits S&P/TSE 60 fund after the market close on March 6, 2000, and TIPs holders received units of it; those units "are traded on the TSE under the symbol 'XIU'" (BC Securities Commission exemption order of March 3, 2000; Toronto Stock Exchange release of March 9, 2000). In TSX's words, "the original TIPs product has evolved into what is known today as the iShares S&P/TSX 60 Index ETF (TSX:XIU)" (release of March 9, 2020). On September 24, 2026 the fund had CAD 22,449.9 million of net assets and 424,000,000 units outstanding. The MER was 0.17% annualised for the six months to June 30, 2026, after 0.18% in each year from 2021 to 2025 (section 04).

Why XIU leads this group

Of the three Canadian-dollar funds on the S&P/TSX 60 above, XIU is the largest: CAD 22,449.9M on September 24, 2026, against CAD 5,542.6M for HXT (September 23, all classes) and CAD 296.5M for ZIU (August 31). The U.S.-dollar listing HXT.U belongs in a different group. XIU's charts, returns and metrics are used for the whole group.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

60 large Canadian companies in one unit CAD 22.4B net assets Avg daily volume 9.9M units Avg bid-ask spread 0.02% Trading expense ratio 0.00% No trailing commission

The fund stays close to its index. For the six months to June 30, 2026 it returned 11.48% against 11.57% for the index; the MRFP attributes −0.07 points of the gap to management fees and −0.02 to "other miscellaneous factors." Over five years to July 31, 2026 the gap was 0.20 points a year (14.62% against 14.82%, fact sheet). In the twelve months to April 30, 2026 the ETF Facts report an average daily volume of 9,903,603 units and an average bid-ask spread of 0.02%. On September 24, 2026 the $52.95 close was within a fraction of a cent of the NAV of $52.9478. Securities lending brought in CAD 96,672 in the first half of 2026 (interim MRFP).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Bank concentration. Financials are 40.58% of the fund and six banks alone 29.88%. Royal Bank of Canada is 9.57% on its own (our sums, September 23, 2026).
02
Commodity exposure. Energy (16.82%) and materials (14.42%) together are 31.24% of the fund, including 11.00% in five gold miners and precious-metals royalty and streaming companies. Oil, gas and gold prices move a large part of the portfolio.
03
One country, large companies only. 98.05% of the fund is in companies the issuer locates in Canada. There are no mid- or small-cap companies, no foreign markets and no health care company in the fund.
04
Top-heavy index. The index weights by float-adjusted market value, so the largest companies get the largest weights: the top ten lines are 47.03% of the fund and Shopify alone 5.94%.
05
Higher fee than its equivalents. At 0.17% XIU's MER is above ZIU's 0.14% and HXT's 0.08% (HXT adds a 0.19% trading expense ratio and a swap fee of up to 0.20%).

Calculation note. Total-return series: the issuer's daily NAV per unit with each distribution reinvested at the ex-date NAV, TRt = TRt−1 × (NAVt + Dt) ÷ NAVt−1, with NAVs and distribution amounts from the issuer's fund data download. The December 30, 2021 amount of CAD 0.10921 is left out: the issuer's distribution table lists it as a reinvested distribution with a cash amount of CAD 0.00000, and the issuer's December 2021 return (3.40%) is reproduced only without it; counting it as cash gives 3.75%. The series reproduces all 59 of the issuer's monthly NAV returns from September 2021 to July 2026 to within 0.000001%, and its 1-, 3- and 5-year returns to July 31, 2026 (31.56%, 22.34%, 14.62%). Monthly returns rt = TRt/TRt−1 − 1 at month-ends, August 31, 2021 to August 31, 2026 (60 returns). Volatility = stdev(r) × √12 (sample standard deviation). Risk-free rate: Bank of Canada Valet series V122531 ("Treasury Bills - 3 month"), monthly value ÷ 12 for the same month. Sharpe = mean(e) ÷ stdev(e) × √12, where et = rt − rf,t is the monthly excess return (sample standard deviation of the excess returns). Beta = cov(r, rm) ÷ var(rm), where rm is the monthly return of Yahoo Finance's ^SP500TR converted to CAD with Bank of Canada series FXUSDCAD at each month-end (or in USD, or Yahoo's ^GSPTSE).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising prices for Canada's large banks, since six of them are 29.88% of the fund. Higher oil, gas and gold prices, which carry the energy (16.82%) and materials (14.42%) holdings. A strong Canadian economy and steady bank dividends, which feed the 2.15% trailing yield. Low trading costs and close tracking keep the result near the index's.

What hurts it

Stress in Canadian banking or housing, given the weight of the banks. Falling commodity prices. Periods when technology or health care leads, since technology is 9.60% of the fund, most of it Shopify (5.94%), and health care is absent. Canadian bear markets, with no bonds, cash or foreign stocks to offset them (2022: −6.36% for the calendar year).

Canada's S&P/TSX 60 ETFs: one index, two structures
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Three Canadian-dollar ETFs on the TSX follow the S&P/TSX 60. Direct holding: XIU and ZIU own the 60 stocks and pay quarterly distributions; XIU also lends securities for extra income. Swap: HXT receives the index's total return from bank counterparties and pays distributions only at the manager's discretion; its fund-level costs include a trading expense ratio of 0.19% and a swap fee of up to 0.20% besides the 0.08% MER. MERs run from 0.08% (HXT) to 0.17% (XIU). XIU is the largest of the three, with CAD 22.4 billion. Its average daily volume was 9,903,603 units with an average bid-ask spread of 0.02% (ETF Facts, 12 months to April 30, 2026); ZIU's was 9,675 units with a 0.06% spread (ETF Facts, 12 months to December 31, 2025).

WHAT DIFFERS

The broad Canadian index funds (XIC, ZCN, VCN) hold 212 to 220 companies for MERs of 0.05%–0.06%. XIU's 60 companies are 80.0% of XIC by overlap (our calculation, September 23, 2026): the same large banks, pipelines and miners, at higher weights, without the mid-sized companies. Distribution yields are similar (XIU 2.17%, XIC 2.03%, VCN 12-month yield 1.96% on August 31). Five-year returns to August 31, 2026 were 14.86% for HXT (issuer page) and 14.83% for XIU (our calculation). U.S.-dollar listings such as HXT.U are different products and are not compared here.
