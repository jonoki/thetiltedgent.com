01 Fund Overview

XLE is a single share that holds the energy companies of the S&P 500: oil and gas producers, refiners, pipeline operators and oilfield-service firms. Its January 31, 2026 summary prospectus states the goal: to "provide investment results that, before expenses, correspond generally to the price and yield performance of publicly traded equity securities of companies in the Energy Select Sector Index." The issuer adds that the index "seeks to provide an effective representation of the energy sector of the S&P 500 Index." On October 2, 2026 that meant 21 companies, against 22 when the prospectus counted them on November 30, 2025.

The fund was incepted on December 16, 1998 and listed on December 22, 1998. It is one of eleven Select Sector SPDR funds whose indices together cover every company in the S&P 500. It held $40.17 billion on October 2, 2026 ($40,167.89 million, 639.40 million shares). The expense ratio is 0.08%: a 0.03% management fee, 0.02% distribution (12b-1) fee and 0.03% other expenses. The issuer quotes it as a gross figure, with no fee waiver.

Structure
Physical, full replication
The prospectus describes "a replication strategy": the fund "typically invests in substantially all of the securities represented in the Index in approximately the same proportions," with at least 95% of assets in index stocks. It is a series of the Select Sector SPDR Trust and is classed as "non-diversified": it may hold fewer securities than a diversified fund.

How the index caps its largest companies

The Select Sector indices are weighted by market value, with limits that the trust's statement of additional information (January 31, 2026) calls asset diversification requirements. Two limits apply: no single company may exceed 24% of the index, and companies weighing more than 4.8% may not add up to more than 50%. The limits are tested at the quarterly rebalance, priced at the close on the second Friday of March, June, September and December and effective after the close on the third Friday, and again on the second-to-last business day of those months. When a company approaches a limit, the rule cuts the company to 23% and the over-4.8% group to 45%, and spreads the excess across the smaller companies, none of which may go above 4.5%.

Between test dates the weights move with prices. On October 2, 2026 ExxonMobil's index weight was 23.78%, just under the 24% line. Five companies sat above 4.8% (ExxonMobil, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Marathon Petroleum and Valero) and together made up 58.09% of the fund (our sum). Marathon Petroleum and Valero were 0.12 and 0.06 points above the line. The next tests fall in December 2026.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Two companies carry 41.64%. ExxonMobil (23.73%) and Chevron (17.91%) together outweigh the 15 smallest holdings combined (our sums). The prospectus lists non-diversification as a principal risk: "As a 'non-diversified' fund, the Fund may hold a smaller number of portfolio securities than many other funds."
02
One sector, tied to commodity prices. The prospectus's energy sector risk: "The Fund's assets will be concentrated in the energy sector, which means the Fund will be more affected by the performance of the energy sector than a fund that is more diversified." It names commodity price swings, large capital spending, debt, exploration risk, regulation and environmental liability.
03
Large swings. Five-year volatility is 26.02% a year against 15.64% for the S&P 500 total-return index (our calculation). The worst calendar quarter in the prospectus is −50.72% (first quarter of 2020); the best is +38.97% (first quarter of 2022).
04
Index rules shape the weights. The 24% and 50% limits keep ExxonMobil and the largest names below their market-value weights and push money toward smaller companies at each rebalance. The fund follows; it cannot choose which companies to own.
05
Large companies only. The index takes only S&P 500 members, so smaller U.S. energy firms are absent. Funds on broader energy indices hold 40 to 111 stocks (section 05).
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares change hands at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists fluctuation of NAV, share premiums and discounts as a principal risk. On the banner date the closing price matched NAV to the cent; the issuer's bid/ask-midpoint measure showed a 0.04% premium.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Rising oil and natural-gas prices, which lift the producers' revenue directly: integrated companies and producers are 63.64% of the fund (our sum). Wide margins between crude and fuel prices, for the three refiners at 14.49%. Steady volumes through pipelines, for the four transport companies at 13.60%. Strong results at ExxonMobil and Chevron, which together are 41.64%. Periods when energy runs on a different cycle from the rest of the market: the fund's correlation with the S&P 500 was 0.29 over five years (our calculation).

What hurts it

Falling oil and gas prices: the prospectus lists commodity price swings first among the sector's risks. Weak refining margins and lower drilling activity, which hit refiners and the service companies. A setback at either of the two largest holdings. Regulation and environmental liabilities, which the prospectus names. Long stretches when the sector lags the market: from November 2022 to December 2025 the price stayed in a range while the S&P 500 rose 67.8% (^GSPC month-end closes, our calculation).

The four energy funds compared here hold the same large companies at the top and differ in three ways. Universe: XLE takes only the S&P 500's 21 energy companies; IYE holds 40 and VDE 111, reaching into smaller firms. Caps: each index limits its largest names by its own rule (24% and 50% for XLE's index, 22.5% and 45% in IYE's index name, 25/50 in the MSCI indices of VDE and FENY), which is why ExxonMobil's weight runs from 21.90% to 23.73% across them. Cost: 0.08% to 0.37% a year. Overlap by weight with XLE is 89.37% for IYE and 82.11% for VDE (our calculation).
