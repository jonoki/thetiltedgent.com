01 Fund Overview

XLF holds the financial companies of the S&P 500 — banks, card networks, insurers, brokers, exchanges and asset managers — in one share. Its January 31, 2026 summary prospectus says the fund "seeks to provide investment results that, before expenses, correspond generally to the price and yield performance of publicly traded equity securities of companies in the Financial Select Sector Index." State Street describes the index as one that "seeks to provide an effective representation of the financial sector of the S&P 500 Index." The eleven Select Sector indexes together cover every company in the S&P 500; XLF's is the financial one of the eleven, a split by sector rather than by size.

The fund started on December 16, 1998; the issuer gives December 22, 1998 as its listing date, and it trades on NYSE Arca. On October 2, 2026 it held $50,004.43 million of net assets and 935.30 million shares outstanding (issuer). The expense ratio is 0.08%: a 0.03% management fee, a 0.02% distribution and service (12b-1) fee and 0.03% of other expenses. The issuer's page now carries the State Street name in the fund's title and notes that "State Street Global Advisors (SSGA) is now State Street Investment Management."

Of the peers in section 05 with published net assets, XLF is the largest: $50.0 billion against $14.8 billion for Vanguard's VFH (whole fund, August 31, 2026) and $3.8 billion for iShares' IYF (October 2, 2026).

Physical, replication
The prospectus says the fund "employs a replication strategy" and invests "substantially all, but at least 95%" of its total assets in the index's stocks. The fund is "non-diversified" under its prospectus, so it may hold fewer securities than many funds.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

The fund owns its index and has stayed close to it. Over the five years to August 31, 2026 the NAV returned 10.36% a year against the index's 10.45%, a gap of 0.09 percentage points, about the size of the 0.08% expense ratio. Over ten years the gap was 0.12 points (13.28% vs 13.40%). Turnover was 6% "of the average value of its portfolio" in the most recent fiscal year (prospectus). On October 2 the market close ($53.49) sat 0.05% above NAV ($53.463779), our calculation.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One sector. The prospectus lists financial sector risk among its principal risks: the fund "will be more affected by the performance of the financial sector than a fund that is more diversified." Interest rates, credit losses, regulation and market activity hit most holdings at the same time.
02
Concentration at the top. The ten largest holdings are 57.51% of the fund, and Berkshire Hathaway and JPMorgan Chase are 24.12%. The capping rules leave room for more: no company is cut until it passes 24%, and the group above 4.8% can reach 50%.
03
Bank failures and confidence shocks. Banks fund themselves with deposits that can leave quickly. In March 2023, after California regulators closed Silicon Valley Bank on March 10 (FDIC release), XLF's month-end price fell 9.99%, the second-largest monthly fall in the five-year window.
04
Index rules decide membership. Classification changes move whole groups in or out. The March 2023 GICS change brought the payment companies in; Visa and Mastercard are now 14.07% of the fund (our sum). The fund follows the S&P 500's own additions and deletions.
05
Non-diversified fund. The prospectus notes that as a "non-diversified" fund it "may hold a smaller number of portfolio securities than many other funds." With 76 stocks, any one company's setback weighs more than in a 500-stock fund.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares trade at the market price, which can differ from NAV. The prospectus lists premium and discount risk among principal risks; State Street warns that ETFs "may trade at significant discounts in periods of market stress." On the banner date the gap was 0.05%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Strong results at the largest holdings, since Berkshire Hathaway, JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard are 38.19% of the fund. Steady credit conditions, with low loan losses at the banks. Active markets: trading volumes, deal-making and rising asset prices feed the brokers, exchanges and asset managers that make up 25.43% of the fund. Consumer spending on cards, which drives Visa, Mastercard and American Express. Interest-rate conditions that widen what banks earn on loans over what they pay on deposits.

What hurts it

Bank failures or deposit runs, as in March 2023 (−9.99% that month). Broad bear markets: 2022 cost −10.59% in total return over the calendar year and −25.8% at the daily trough. A sharp rise in interest rates that slows borrowing, deal-making and trading, the backdrop Yahoo Finance described for September 2026 (−7.47% that month). Rising loan losses in a recession. Tighter regulation of banks' capital or of card fees.

Top 3 Risks

01
One-sector exposure. All 76 holdings are financial companies whose earnings share the same drivers: rates, credit and market activity. Five-year volatility is 18.06% a year against 15.64% for the S&P 500, and the five-year total return trailed the index by 37.27 points (our calculations).
02
Concentration in a few names. The ten largest holdings are 57.51% of the fund; Berkshire Hathaway and JPMorgan Chase alone are 24.12%. The capping rules allow one company up to 24% before any cut.
03
Confidence shocks. Banks depend on depositors and lenders staying put. The March 2023 failures took the month-end price down 9.99% in a month and 13.13% from January to May 2023, even though most of the fund's holdings were not directly involved.
Category Commentary
Same sector, different definitions
PRODUCT CATEGORY

Financial-sector ETFs differ mainly in which companies count as "financial" and how far down in size they go. XLF holds only the S&P 500's financials: 76 large companies. VFH and FNCL track MSCI's investable-market (IMI) financials index, which reaches further down in company size (424 stocks in VFH on August 31, 2026). IYF tracks a Russell 1000 financials index with 143 stocks that leaves out the card networks and payment companies. Cost: 0.08% (XLF) to 0.37% (IYF). Age: XLF from December 1998, IYF May 2000, VFH January 2004.

Different rules, different top weights
WHAT DIFFERS

Each index sets its own rules for which companies count and how they are weighted; XLF's capping rules are set out in section 02. The largest weights differ: Berkshire Hathaway is 12.41% of XLF and 11.92% of IYF (October 2, 2026) but 7.84% of VFH (August 31, 2026), where Visa is 4.35% against 8.15% in XLF. With overlaps of 77.9% to 83.5% by weight (our calculations), the funds share most of their money but not all of it.
