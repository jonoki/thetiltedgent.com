01 Fund Overview

XLK is a way to own the technology companies of the S&P 500, 74 of them on October 2, 2026, through a single share. In State Street's words, the fund seeks results that, before expenses, "correspond generally to the price and yield performance" of the Technology Select Sector Index. State Street describes that index as one that "seeks to provide an effective representation of the technology sector of the S&P 500 Index," drawn from six industries: semiconductors and chip equipment, software, technology hardware, communications equipment, electronic equipment and IT services. The fund uses a replication strategy, holding substantially all of the index's stocks in about the same proportions.

XLK started on December 16, 1998 and listed on NYSE Arca on December 22, 1998 (issuer). It is one of eleven sector funds in the Select Sector SPDR Trust; the issuer notes that the eleven Select Sector indexes "together comprise all of the companies in the S&P 500." Two changes came in late 2025. On December 1, 2025 the fund's name changed from "The Technology Select Sector SPDR Fund" to "State Street Technology Select Sector SPDR ETF" (prospectus supplement, November 14, 2025). On December 5, 2025 its shares began trading after a 2-for-1 split (issuer NAV history: $291.04 on December 4, $146.62 on December 5). All per-share prices on this page are split-adjusted. The expense ratio is 0.08%: a 0.03% management fee, 0.02% distribution (12b-1) fee and 0.03% other expenses.

How the index caps its largest companies

Under plain market-value weighting, three companies would dominate. The prospectus says the index uses a "modified market capitalization" method, so the weights of "single stock concentrations" can be modified "in order to conform to the requirements of the Internal Revenue Code" (the diversification tests a U.S. fund must pass to keep its tax status). The trust's Statement of Additional Information (January 31, 2026) sets out the steps, checked on the second Friday of each quarter-end month. A company above 24% of the index is cut to 23%. If the companies that each exceed 4.8% add up to more than 50%, they are scaled down in proportion until together they are 45%. A company between 4.5% and 4.8% is trimmed to 4.5%. The weight removed is spread in proportion across the companies below 4.5%. The new weights take effect after the close on the third Friday of March, June, September and December.

What it leaves out

Alphabet, Meta Platforms and Amazon are not in XLK's holdings: GICS places them in other sectors (iShares labels Meta and Alphabet "Communication" in its IYW file). Technology companies outside the S&P 500 are also absent. The fund is a single-country portfolio of S&P 500 members, so the useful mix is by size and industry rather than by region.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration at the top. NVIDIA is 15.52% of the fund, and with Apple and Microsoft, 39.41%. The top ten are 64.16%. The prospectus lists non-diversification risk: the fund can hold large positions in a few companies.
02
One sector, and mostly one industry. Everything is technology, and semiconductors alone are 46.24%. A downturn in chip demand or chip prices reaches nearly half the fund at once.
03
Capping quirks. The caps keep the fund within U.S. diversification rules but can move money at each quarterly rebalance. If the companies above 4.8% pass 50% together, all of them are scaled back to 45% and the weight removed goes to the smaller holdings, so the fund sells its largest positions and buys the rest. NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft and AMD were 44.54% on October 2.
04
Valuation of what it owns. The issuer reports a price/earnings ratio of 34.25 for the index (25.03 on next-year earnings forecasts) and price/book of 12.71 for the fund (October 2, 2026). An index fund buys at whatever price the market sets.
05
Bigger swings than the market. Five-year volatility was 24.0% a year against 15.6% for the S&P 500, and beta 1.34 (our calculation): on average XLK moved about 1.3% for each 1% move in the S&P 500. It fell 33.6% from December 2021 to October 2022.
06
Low income. The 30-day SEC yield is 0.41% and the distribution yield 0.42%. Returns come almost entirely from price.

A handful of very large companies dominate U.S. technology, so all four indexes here cap them to stay within U.S. fund diversification rules, each in its own way.
