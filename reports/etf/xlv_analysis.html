01 Fund Overview

XLV holds the health care companies of the S&P 500 in one share: drug makers, biotechnology firms, health insurers, hospital operators, drug distributors, medical-device makers and the suppliers of laboratory equipment. Its January 31, 2026 summary prospectus states the objective as investment results that, before expenses, "correspond generally to the price and yield performance of publicly traded equity securities of companies in the Health Care Select Sector Index". S&P Dow Jones Indices calculates the index. The fund uses a replication strategy, holding substantially all of the index's stocks in about the same proportions, and puts at least 95% of its total assets into them.

The index is one of eleven Select Sector Indexes that together cover every company in the S&P 500. Membership follows the Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS, the sector system S&P's indices use): a company joins when it is in the S&P 500 and GICS places it in Health Care. On October 2, 2026 that meant 60 stocks. Unlike the S&P 500 itself, the index is not weighted purely by market value: S&P Dow Jones Indices applies what the prospectus calls a "modified market capitalization" method that trims the largest weights so that funds tracking it can meet U.S. tax diversification rules (the capping rules are set out in section 02). Launched December 16, 1998 and listed on December 22, 1998, XLV had $42.81 billion of net assets and 257.77 million shares outstanding on October 2, 2026.

Why this fund: the largest of the U.S. health care sector ETFs compared here

The candidates are the broad U.S. health care sector ETFs of State Street, Vanguard, iShares and Fidelity: XLV, VHT, IYH and FHLC. Funds that hold only one industry (biotechnology, devices, providers) are a different product and are left out. On each issuer's latest published figure XLV is the largest, at about twice the net assets of Vanguard's whole Health Care Index Fund.

How the weights are capped

A U.S. fund has to meet diversification tests to keep its tax status, so the index cannot simply follow market value when a few companies dominate a sector. The fund's Statement of Additional Information (January 31, 2026) sets out S&P DJI's rules. The index is rebalanced at the closing prices of the second Friday of March, June, September and December, effective after the close on the third Friday:

01
Single company. A company whose weight is above 24% is cut to 23%.
02
The big names together. The companies weighing more than 4.8% may not add up to more than 50% of the index. If they do, they are cut back in proportion to a combined 45%; any that this pushes below 4.5% are topped back up to 4.5% from the removed weight.
03
Where the excess goes. Companies between 4.5% and 4.8% are cut to 4.5%. The rest of the removed weight is spread in proportion over the companies below 4.5%, none of which may then exceed 4.5%.
04
Check before quarter-end. If on the second-to-last business day of a rebalance month a company is above 24% or the over-4.8% group is above 50%, a second reweighting takes effect after the close on the month's last business day.

On October 2, 2026 five holdings were above 4.8%: Eli Lilly, Johnson & Johnson, AbbVie, Merck and UnitedHealth, together 44.48% of the fund (44.60% of the index on State Street's index top holdings). That is below the 50% limit and close to the 45% level the rule resets the group to. No company is near the 24% single-company trigger; Lilly, the largest, is 15.14%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One sector. Every holding is a health care company. The prospectus lists health care sector risk among its principal risks: companies in the sector are "subject to extensive government regulation", and their profits depend on government reimbursement, pricing pressure, patents and product approvals.
02
A few names carry the fund. Eli Lilly is 15.14% and the top ten 60.48%. The fund is "non-diversified" under U.S. fund rules, which, in the prospectus's words, allows it to "hold a smaller number of portfolio securities than many other funds." A setback at Lilly or Johnson & Johnson moves the fund far more than it moves the S&P 500.
03
Capping changes the weights. Under the diversification caps in section 02, if the companies above 4.8% add up to more than 50% at a rebalance, they are cut back to a combined 45%. When the caps cut a weight, the fund holds less of its largest companies than their market value alone would give, and the removed weight goes to smaller ones.
04
It has lagged the broad market. From September 30, 2021 to October 2, 2026 XLV's total return was +41.60% against +92.58% for the S&P 500 (our calculation, section 07). The sector's lower volatility came with lower returns in this window.
05
Index membership comes from S&P. S&P DJI decides S&P 500 membership and GICS decides the sector; the fund follows both. Illumina, for example, entered the S&P 500, and so the fund, on September 21, 2026.
06
Trading at market price. ETF shares trade at a market price that can differ from NAV. In the third quarter of 2026 the fund's 4 p.m. bid/ask midpoint was above NAV on 42 days and below it on 22 (issuer). On the banner date the issuer's premium was 0.04% (closing price 0.05% above NAV).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Strength in the large drug makers, above all Eli Lilly, Johnson & Johnson and AbbVie, which are a third of the fund. Markets that favour steady earners over fast growers: in 2022, when the S&P 500 lost 18.11% with dividends, XLV lost 2.08%. Its beta of 0.55 against the S&P 500 means broad market falls have reached it only in part. Product approvals, patent protection and stable government reimbursement for the companies it holds. Low costs and low turnover keep the fund close to its index.

What hurts it

Policy and regulation aimed at the sector: pricing pressure and limits on government reimbursement both appear among the prospectus's sector risks. Trouble at one of the largest holdings, given a 15.14% weight in Eli Lilly and 60.48% in the top ten. Patent expiries and failed trials. Markets led by technology and other growth sectors, as in 2023 and 2024, when the fund returned about 2% a year while the S&P 500 returned more than 25%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Sector and policy risk. The whole fund is one regulated sector. Government reimbursement, pricing pressure, patents and approvals drive the earnings of the companies it holds, and the prospectus names each of them as a risk.
02
Concentration in a few companies. Five holdings are 44.48% of the fund, and Eli Lilly alone is 15.14%; the index's caps cut that group back to 45% whenever it passes 50% at a rebalance. The fund is classed as non-diversified.
03
Opportunity cost against the broad market. Over the five years to October 2, 2026 the fund returned 7.2% a year with dividends against 14.0% for the S&P 500. Lower volatility (14.77% a year) and a beta of 0.55 came with that lower return in this window.
Category Commentary
Sector funds slice up the market
PRODUCT CATEGORY

A sector ETF holds one part of the market instead of all of it. The eleven Select Sector SPDR ETFs split the S&P 500 by GICS sector, so together they hold every company in it; XLV is the health care slice. State Street presents them as a way to "take strategic or tactical positions at a more targeted level than traditional style based investing." Because each fund holds a single sector, it carries that sector's risks in full and owns nothing that offsets them.

Same sector, different indices
WHAT DIFFERS

The four health care funds compared here draw from different universes. Breadth: XLV holds the 60 S&P 500 health care companies; IYH holds 100 from the Russell 1000; VHT (416 stocks) and FHLC (341) add mid and small companies. Capping: each index has its own rule for limiting large weights ("25/50", "22.5/45", or S&P's 24%/4.8%/50% steps). Classification: the indices do not draw the sector's borders in the same place; IYH, for example, holds none of the drug distributors McKesson, Cencora and Cardinal Health, which XLV holds. Cost: 0.08% to 0.37%. With the largest drug makers at the top of all of them, XLV's overlap with IYH is 90.7% by weight and with VHT 85.3% (our calculation).
