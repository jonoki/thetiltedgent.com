01 Fund Overview

ZAG holds the Canadian investment-grade bond market in one fund: bonds issued by the federal government, the provinces, cities and companies, all paying in Canadian dollars. In the issuer's words, the fund "has been designed to replicate, to the extent possible, the performance of the FTSE Canada Universe Bond IndexTM, net of expenses." The fact sheet describes the index as "a broad measure of the Canadian investment-grade fixed income market consisting of Government of Canada (including Crown Corporations), Provincial and Corporate bonds": fixed-rate bonds paying interest twice a year, with more than one year to maturity and a credit rating of BBB or higher, each weighted by its market value.

The fund has roughly doubled in size since the end of 2021. Net assets were CAD 6,408.7 million at the end of 2021 and CAD 13,013.9 million on June 30, 2026, and units outstanding rose from 406.7 million to 940.2 million (semi-annual management report of fund performance, MRFP). On September 30, 2026 the fact sheet shows CAD 12,920.69 million and 970,486,521 units. The management expense ratio (MER) was 0.09% for the six months to June 30, 2026, as in each year from 2021 to 2025.

Structure
Physical bonds
The ETF Facts say it replicates the index "by investing in individual securities and other BMO ETFs." Since December 8, 2020 its strategy has been to hold the index's bonds "in the same proportion as they are reflected in" the index (MRFP). It held 1,919 securities on September 30, 2026 (fact sheet). Portfolio turnover was 35.29% in 2025 and 21.02% in the first half of 2026 (MRFP).
Currency
Canadian dollars only
The index holds only bonds "denominated in Canadian dollars" (fact sheet). The fact sheet's geographic allocation is 97.90% Canada, 1.97% United States, 0.12% France and 0.01% United Kingdom (Sep 30, 2026); since the index holds only Canadian-dollar bonds, the non-Canadian lines are Canadian-dollar bonds of foreign borrowers. There is no foreign currency to hedge.

03 Structural Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Structural strengths:

MER 0.09% CAD 12.92B in assets 1,919 securities 73.62% rated AAA or AA Monthly income 5-yr NAV 0.21% vs index 0.30%

The fund holds the Canadian investment-grade bond market at low cost and stays close to its index. For the six months to June 30, 2026 it returned 2.20% against 2.24% for the index. BMO attributes the 0.04-point gap to the MER (−0.05 points) and to sampling and other factors (+0.01 points) (MRFP). Over the five years to September 30, 2026 the NAV return was 0.21% a year against 0.30% for the index, and over ten years 1.18% against 1.28% (fact sheet). The trading expense ratio was nil in every period from 2022 to June 2026 (MRFP).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Interest rates. Weighted average duration is 6.69 years (Sep 30, 2026). Duration means a one-point rise in yields cuts the price by roughly 6.7%, before income. In September 2026 the 10-year Government of Canada benchmark yield rose from 3.73% (Aug 31) to 3.99% (Sep 29) (Bank of Canada), and ZAG closed September at $13.30, its lowest close since May 2024.
02
Inflation. The index holds fixed-rate bonds only, so no coupon rises with inflation. Rising inflation lowers the real value of the income and usually pushes yields up at the same time.
03
One country's governments. Federal, provincial and municipal debt is 75.06% of the fund (our sum) and Canada is 97.90% of the fact sheet's geographic allocation. Canadian government borrowing costs and Bank of Canada policy drive most of the return.
04
The long end. 25.27% of the fund matures in more than ten years (our sum of the issuer's buckets). These bonds carry the most rate sensitivity. The largest holdings include a Government of Canada bond maturing in 2057.
05
Credit spreads. Company bonds are 24.90% of the fund and 10.37% is rated BBB. In a recession, company bond prices can fall even when government bonds rally. The index takes only bonds rated BBB or higher (fact sheet).
06
Tax in non-registered accounts. Interest income is distributed monthly and taxed as income every year whether paid in cash or reinvested (ETF Facts). Registered accounts (RRSP, TFSA) are a separate case.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What helps it

Falling Canadian interest rates: with a 6.69-year duration, a one-point drop in yields adds roughly 6.7% to the price, on top of income. Stable rates: the fund then earns roughly its yield (yield to maturity 4.16%, distribution yield 3.43%, both Sep 30). Bank of Canada rate cuts, as in 2024 and 2025, and weak economies or stock sell-offs that send money into government bonds, which are 75.06% of the fund. Slowing inflation. Narrowing credit spreads help the 24.90% in company bonds.

What hurts it

Rising yields, as in 2022, when the total return fell 15.5% from peak to trough, and as in September 2026, when a 26-basis-point rise in the 10-year yield took the price to its lowest close since May 2024. Inflation above expectations, since every coupon is fixed. Rising yields paired with falling stocks, which removes the diversification benefit (five-year correlation with the S&P 500 in CAD: 0.59). Wider credit spreads on the 10.37% rated BBB.

Category Commentary
Canadian universe and aggregate bond ETFs
PRODUCT CATEGORY

"Universe" or "aggregate" Canadian bond funds are the fixed-income counterpart of a total-market stock fund: one holding for the Canadian-dollar investment-grade bond market. The large ones are close substitutes. On August 31, 2026 ZAG, XBB and VAB all reported a 3.88% yield to maturity, durations of 6.71 to 6.9 years, and five-year NAV returns of 0.11% to 0.18% a year (each issuer's fact sheet). What differs: index (ZAG and XBB track the FTSE Canada Universe Bond Index, VAB a Bloomberg index, HBB a Solactive index); cost (MERs from 0.08% to 0.10%); structure (ZAG, XBB and VAB hold bonds; HBB holds a total-return swap with a bank, adds a 0.29% trading expense ratio and pays distributions only at the manager's discretion).

What drives returns across the category
WHAT MATTERS

With fees around a tenth of a percent and nearly identical portfolios, returns across these funds are set by the bond market itself: the level and change of Government of Canada and provincial yields, Bank of Canada policy and credit spreads. The last five years show how much the starting yield matters. ZAG began the five years to September 30, 2026 with the 10-year federal yield at 1.51% (September 29, 2021) and returned 0.21% a year (NAV). Its yield to maturity on September 30 (4.16%) was more than twice that starting level.
