01 Company Overview

Entergy is a regulated electric utility holding company serving roughly 3.1 million customers across Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi and Texas — including New Orleans — from about 25,000 megawatts of owned generating capacity spanning gas, nuclear, coal, hydro and solar. FY2025 revenue was $12.95B, up 8.98% from $11.88B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $13.48B, up 9.5%. At a $50.52B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #498 globally.

The central debate on this page is not whether Entergy is a good utility — by the usual regulated-utility tests it is performing. It is whether the market has already paid for the load-growth story. Entergy trades at a trailing P/E of 27.07 and a forward P/E of 22.57 — a premium to where regulated electrics have historically cleared — while burning −$3.10B of trailing free cash flow against $9.17B of trailing capital expenditure. That combination is the whole argument: a utility spending far more than it earns is either compounding rate base into a genuine multi-decade demand shift, or it is funding a capital programme whose returns depend entirely on four separate state regulators staying friendly. Sections 04 and 07 take that apart.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
LEGAL MONOPOLY FRANCHISE 4-STATE RATE BASE 25,000 MW OWNED GENERATION NUCLEAR BASELOAD GULF INDUSTRIAL CORRIDOR INTERCONNECTION QUEUE POSITION BETA 0.48

The moat is statutory, not commercial. Entergy's five operating utilities hold exclusive retail franchises in their service territories; no rival can sell a kilowatt-hour to a residential customer in Jackson, Mississippi. What that buys is not pricing power in the ordinary sense — regulators set the price — but the right to earn a permitted return on every dollar of prudently incurred capital. The moat therefore scales with the capital programme, which is why a utility with $34.63B of debt and negative free cash flow can nonetheless be a defensive holding: the spending is the product. The secondary, harder-to-replicate asset is location. A hyperscaler choosing where to put a gigawatt-scale campus cares about land, water, tax treatment and — decisively — how fast a utility can interconnect it. Entergy sits on cheap Gulf gas, has nuclear baseload already in the ground, and serves states actively competing for that investment. That combination is not something a peer utility in a congested Northeastern market can copy.

01
Four regulators, four separate risks. Earnings depend on the Louisiana Public Service Commission, the Arkansas PSC, the Mississippi PSC, the New Orleans City Council and the PUCT all continuing to authorise recovery on a very large capital programme. Entergy's own "Fair Share Plus" framing — routing data-centre revenue into customer bill relief — is evidence that the political cost of large-load growth is real and being actively managed. A single adverse rate order in Louisiana, the largest jurisdiction, would matter more than any operating result.
02
The financing gap is structural, not cyclical. Trailing operating cash flow is $6.07B against $9.17B of capex — a $3.10B annual shortfall funded by debt and equity issuance. Debt-to-equity is 1.87 and the current ratio is 0.91. This is normal for the sector, but it makes the equity a leveraged bet on the cost of capital: every 100 basis points of higher long rates directly compresses the spread between allowed ROE and the cost of the debt funding rate base.
03
Hurricane and storm-cost exposure. The service territory is the Gulf Coast. Storm restoration costs run into the billions in a bad year and are recovered only with a regulatory lag, through securitisation or storm-cost riders. The August 2026 announcement of a $270 million grid resilience project in Jefferson Parish, Louisiana is the mitigation — and also a reminder of the magnitude of the underlying problem.
04
Large-load concentration risk cuts both ways. Building generation and transmission for named hyperscalers concentrates the forward growth in a handful of counterparties whose AI capital plans are themselves subject to revision. If a campus is deferred after the plant is built, the stranded-asset conversation happens in front of a state commission, and existing ratepayers — not the hyperscaler — are the ones asked to absorb it. That is a political risk as much as a financial one.

RSI compares the size of recent up-months to the size of recent down-months on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen sharply relative to its own recent history (traders call this "overbought"); below 30 means the opposite. It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a stock can stay overbought for a long time while the business keeps improving.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $51.51 (split-adjusted) to $105.73 on September 10, 2026, ETR returned +105.3% on price alone, before roughly $11 per share of dividends paid over the period. That is a doubling from a regulated utility — an outcome that would have been considered implausible when the period began, and which is almost entirely a story of the last twenty-four months. From October 2021 through September 2023 the stock went nowhere: $51.51 to $46.25, a 10% loss across two years, as rising rates compressed every bond-proxy multiple in the market. Everything ETR has earned as an investment was earned after that.

The inflection is datable to the second half of 2024, when the market began to price AI-driven electricity demand into utilities with available generation and cheap gas. ETR moved from $46.25 (Sep 2023) to $78.09 (Nov 2024) — a 69% gain in fourteen months with no comparable change in earnings. That is multiple expansion, not compounding, and it is the reason the current 27× trailing P/E has to be held up to the light. The subsequent leg from $75.82 (Dec 2024) to the all-time high of $117.91 (Apr 2026) added another 55%. The last five months have given back 10.3% of that.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Entergy owns a legal monopoly across four states that are actively winning hyperscale data-centre investment, with 25,000 MW of existing generation, cheap Gulf gas and nuclear baseload already in the ground. The Amazon agreement in Mississippi is now demonstrably lowering customer bills — the regulatory template that makes the next ten approvals easier. Consensus sees EPS rising from $3.91 to $4.40 in 2026 and $5.10 in 2027, a 14% two-year compound rate that would take the forward multiple from 22.6× to under 21× on flat price, while the dividend has risen every single year on record here and the payout ratio is a comfortable 65.5%. At $105.73 against a $123.03 average target, the sell side sees 16.4% upside.

Bear Case

A 27× trailing P/E on a utility earning a 3.95% return on invested capital and burning $3.10B of free cash flow annually is a valuation that requires everything to go right. The company must fund a capital programme running roughly 1.5× operating cash flow from debt and equity markets while already carrying $34.63B of debt at 1.87× equity — any sustained rise in long rates compresses the spread between allowed ROE and funding cost directly. Every sell-side action in August 2026 was a cut, with Morgan Stanley's target now below the spot price. Short interest at 4.35% of shares is three to four times the utility norm. And the load-growth revenue is being deliberately shared with ratepayers, which caps the earnings uplift per megawatt of new load.

1
Regulatory recovery risk across five commissions. Earnings are the product of rate base and allowed ROE, both set by regulators in Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Orleans and Texas. An adverse order on cost recovery for the new Texas plants, Ironwood, or the Jefferson Parish resilience spend would hit earnings power directly and permanently, not cyclically. Specific allowed ROEs by jurisdiction were not verified for this report.
2
Cost of capital. With negative free cash flow every year since at least 2021 and 1.87× debt-to-equity, Entergy is a perpetual issuer. Higher long rates raise the funding cost immediately while allowed returns reset only at the next rate case — a lag that has repeatedly been the mechanism by which utilities de-rate, as the −23% drawdown into September 2023 demonstrated.
3
Storm exposure concentrated on the Gulf Coast. One severe hurricane season can generate billions in restoration costs recovered only over years and only with regulatory consent. This is the risk that is structurally uninsurable at the equity level and is the reason ETR's beta understates its true tail risk.
Industry Commentary
Data-centre load is arriving — and the economics are being shared
EMERGING TAILWIND

For the first time in roughly two decades, US electricity demand is growing meaningfully, and the Gulf South is a preferred destination: cheap gas, available land, accommodating regulators. Entergy's August 27, 2026 release is the most concrete public evidence available here — an Amazon hyperscale facility in Mississippi generating enough revenue to eliminate nearly $5 of monthly increase on customer bills, under a "Fair Share Plus" pledge. Entergy's own materials also reference infrastructure investment supporting Meta's data centre in Louisiana. But read the Mississippi disclosure for what it actually says: the surplus is being routed to ratepayers. That is politically necessary and financially dilutive to the growth story simultaneously. The bull assumption that each new gigawatt converts one-for-one into shareholder earnings is not supported by the structure the company is publicly promoting.

The capital cycle has outrun the cash flow
ONGOING HEADWIND

Entergy spent $9.17B on capital expenditure in the trailing twelve months against $6.07B of operating cash flow — a gap of $3.10B funded externally. That is not an anomaly: free cash flow was negative in every year sourced, from −$4.05B in 2021 to −$2.61B in 2025. The company frames the historical scale as "more than $10 billion on strengthening transmission and distribution electric infrastructure over the last five years"; the trailing-twelve-month rate alone is nearly as large as that entire five-year T&D figure, which tells you how sharply the programme has accelerated. For the equity, the consequence is arithmetic: ROIC of 3.95% against a rising cost of debt leaves very little spread, and the value created depends almost entirely on regulators granting timely recovery at an adequate allowed return. Investors buying ETR for AI load growth are, whether they frame it this way or not, underwriting five state commissions.

What would change the verdict
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The bull and bear cases resolve on one observable. If the October 28 print shows management quantifying contracted large-load megawatts, disclosing the associated revenue mechanism, and reaffirming FY2026 EPS near $4.40 with the capital plan intact, then 22.6× forward earnings for a monopoly with a two-decade demand tailwind is defensible and the $123 consensus target is reachable. If instead the large-load pipeline continues to be described only in customer-bill anecdotes while capex keeps climbing and free cash flow stays at −$3B, then the market is paying a 27× trailing multiple for a business earning below its cost of capital — and the August pattern of uniform target cuts, plus 4.35% short interest, says a meaningful cohort of professionals has already made that call. The stock at $105.73, ten percent below its April high and sitting almost exactly on Morgan Stanley's target, is priced roughly at the midpoint of those two outcomes.
