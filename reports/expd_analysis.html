01 Company Overview

Expeditors is a freight forwarder and customs broker that owns no planes and no ships. It buys space on other people's aircraft and vessels, consolidates many customers' cargo into it, clears that cargo through customs at the other end and moves it the last leg — from 171 district offices on six continents, run on a single in-house IT system. It is a non-asset-based model, which is why capital expenditure over the last twelve months was just $48.7M against $12.04B of revenue. FY2025 revenue was $11.07B (+4.4%) with diluted EPS of $5.95. At a $24.98B market value it ranks #981 globally.

The story of the last year is that the world got more complicated to ship through — and Expeditors sells a way through the complication. Tariff changes, the end of US de minimis entry, a conflict in the Middle East that took passenger-plane belly capacity out of the air, and AI data-centre builders who now need servers flown on the upper deck of freighters. In Q2 2026 revenue rose 32% to $3.50B and diluted EPS rose 51% to $2.03; airfreight tonnage grew 14% and customs revenue grew double digits for a second straight quarter. The stock went with it: from a $121.90 close at the end of October 2025 to $192.15 on September 21, 2026, +57.6% in under eleven months. The central debate is whether that is a structurally better business or a cyclical peak in a spread business — a period when scarce air capacity and a temporary surge of tariff filings are lifting both volume and price at once. The consensus of 17 analysts is Hold, with an average target of $177.71 — 7.5% below the price.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Global network — 171 districts Single in-house IT system Customs & compliance know-how Asset-light (TTM capex $48.7M) Profit-linked compensation culture Net cash, no borrowings

The moat is know-how at scale, not assets. Anyone can book a container; far fewer can guarantee that a high-value server shipment clears customs in three jurisdictions under tariff rules that changed last month. Every new layer of trade complexity — tariffs, forced-labour enforcement, tighter customs rules — raises the value of that service. The asset-light model does the rest: $923.9M of trailing free cash flow on $48.7M of capex, a 42.7% return on equity and 57.0% return on invested capital. The company's risk disclosures name its "compensation model" and "desire to maintain historical unitary profitability" as core to its strategy — pay tied to branch profit. The vulnerability is that the whole model earns a spread between carriers and shippers, and it owns none of the capacity that sets that spread.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Scarcity is the tailwind, and scarcity ends. Q2 air profit rode "highly elevated" rates caused by lost belly capacity and a Middle East conflict. When capacity returns, the spread normalises. The Q2 2026 net revenue shows the risk: ocean net revenue fell 6.9% YoY while ocean gross revenue rose, because carrier pricing moved faster than Expeditors' pricing.
02
Some of the customs boost is labelled temporary by the company itself. "A temporary surge in IEEPA-related filings drove higher pricing." Customs is the largest profit pool; if tariff filings normalise, part of the Q2 pricing goes with them.
03
Customer concentration in AI demand. Management names AI hyperscalers as a driver in both airfreight and customs. That is a strong customer group, but it ties a meaningful slice of growth to one capex cycle.
04
Receivables are consuming cash. Accounts receivable rose from $2.02B at December 31, 2025 to $2.63B at June 30, 2026 as revenue surged. First-half operating cash flow was $487.9M against $495.8M of net earnings, and cash fell from $1.31B to $1.03B after $748M of buybacks and dividends.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — an expensive stock can stay overbought for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 close of $119.13 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $192.15, EXPD gained 61.3% on price, before dividends. But almost none of that came in the first four years. The stock peaked at $134.29 in December 2021 with the pandemic freight boom, fell to $88.31 by September 2022 as freight rates collapsed (FY2023 revenue fell 45.5%), and then spent three years between roughly $100 and $131. As late as October 2025 it closed at $121.90 — barely above where the window began. The entire five-year gain was made in the last eleven months.

The 6-period monthly RSI is 87.5, just below the five-year high of 88.8 set in January 2026 — and this time it is sustained: above 70 on each of the last five readings (May through the September 21 point). Before November 2025 it never went above 70. The 10-month moving average is about $161.64, some 16% below the price. Momentum is as strong as it has been in five years; so is the distance the stock has travelled from its own trend.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Expeditors is winning share at the moment the world made shipping harder. Q2 2026 revenue rose 32%, net revenue 20.8% and operating income 41%, with airfreight and customs both growing double digits. The business needs almost no capital — $48.7M of trailing capex against $923.9M of free cash flow and a 57% ROIC — and returns it all: $748M of buybacks and dividends in six months and a share count down 3.7% in a year. Tariffs, customs enforcement and AI hardware logistics are multi-year trends, not one-quarter events, and customs is the highest-spread line. Add $50M of annual technology savings from Q3 and an ocean market that may be bottoming, and 2026's earnings may be a floor rather than a peak.

Bear Case

This is a spread business at the top of a spread cycle. Management itself calls the IEEPA filing surge "temporary" and ties air rates to lost belly capacity from a Middle East conflict — both can reverse. Consensus sees FY2027 EPS growth of just 3.5% after +28.8% in 2026, yet the stock trades at 28.0x trailing and 25.1x forward earnings, above the $177.71 average target, with more Sell ratings (5) than Buy ratings (3) and a monthly RSI of 87.5. The last time freight was this tight, in 2021, EPS peaked at $8.27 and the stock then fell 34% in nine months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Air capacity normalises. Airfreight is 42.7% of revenue and its Q2 profit rode elevated rates from constrained capacity. A return of passenger belly space or a Middle East de-escalation would compress the spread the growth depends on.
02
Customs pricing mean-reverts. Customs & other is the largest net-revenue line ($546M in Q2). The company labels part of its Q2 pricing as a temporary IEEPA-related surge.
03
Valuation leaves little room. At 25.1x forward earnings with consensus expecting 3.5% EPS growth in 2027, the price already sits 8.1% above the average target. A normal quarter could be enough to disappoint.
Industry Commentary
Trade complexity as a product
EMERGING TAILWIND

For a customs broker, every rule change is billable work. Over the past year the company has cited new tariffs, the end of de minimis entry, IEEPA filings, and — in its own client briefings over the past month, as summarised on StockAnalysis — a US executive order that treats importing as a privilege rather than a right, Canadian counter-tariffs on more than 700 US products and new Section 301 and forced-labour duties. Customs, Transcon, Distribution and Order Management each grew double digits for two straight quarters. The open question is how much of that is permanent complexity and how much is a one-time rush of filings.

Air cargo capacity and the AI build-out
CYCLICAL

Two forces met in the air market this year: fewer passenger flights (and so less belly space) because of the Middle East conflict, and demand from AI hyperscalers moving servers that need upper-deck freighter space. Management adds rising fuel costs, routing problems and returning North Asia e-commerce, and says carriers "are under enormous strain and may continue to be for some time." For a forwarder that is a windfall while it lasts. Its own briefings, as summarised on StockAnalysis, also note that air cargo is sensitive to AI cycles — the same demand that lifted this year could turn quickly if AI capex slows.

The ocean downturn may be flattening
WATCH

Ocean was the weak line: container volume flat YoY in Q2 and ocean net revenue down 6.9%. But June volume was +9% YoY, Q2 volume rose 7% on Q1 — the first sequential gain since Q3 2025 — and profit per container improved late in the quarter. If ocean turns while air and customs hold, the bull case gets a third leg. If air and customs fade before ocean recovers, the bear case arrives all at once.
