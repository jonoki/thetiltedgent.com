01 Company Overview

Expeditors is a freight forwarder and customs broker that owns no planes and no ships. It buys space on other people's aircraft and vessels, consolidates many customers' cargo into it, clears that cargo through customs at the other end and moves it the last leg — from 172 district offices worldwide, run on a single in-house IT system. It is a non-asset-based model, which is why capital expenditure over the last twelve months was just $55.9M against $13.25B of revenue. FY2025 revenue was $11.07B (+4.4%) with diluted EPS of $5.95; the first nine months of 2026 already earned $6.38. At a $25.52B market value it ranks #363 in the S&P 500.

The story of the last year is that the world got more complicated to ship through — and Expeditors sells a way through the complication. Tariff changes, the end of US de minimis entry, a conflict in the Middle East that took passenger-plane belly capacity out of the air, and AI data-centre builders who now need servers flown on the upper deck of freighters. This autumn the big Asian ports jammed up as well. In Q3 2026 (reported November 3) revenue rose 41.9% to $4.11B and diluted EPS rose 61.6% to $2.65, against a Nasdaq consensus of $2.24. Airfreight tonnage grew 13%, ocean container volume grew 7% after a flat Q2, and the company kept 36.7% of its net revenue as operating income, against a historical target of 30%. The stock went with it: from a $121.90 close at the end of October 2025 to $197.69 on November 4, 2026, +62.2% in just over a year. The central debate is whether that is a structurally better business or a cyclical peak in a spread business — a period when scarce air capacity, port congestion and tariff paperwork are lifting both volume and price at once. Analysts lean to the second reading: consensus has 2027 EPS slightly below 2026. The consensus of 18 analysts is Hold, with an average target of $189.47 — 4.2% below the price.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Global network — 172 districts Single in-house IT system Customs & compliance know-how Asset-light (TTM capex $55.9M) Profit-linked compensation culture Net cash, no borrowings

The moat is know-how at scale, not assets. Anyone can book a container; far fewer can guarantee that a high-value server shipment clears customs in three jurisdictions under tariff rules that changed last month. Every new layer of trade complexity — tariffs, forced-labour enforcement, tighter customs rules — raises the value of that service. The asset-light model does the rest: $927.9M of trailing free cash flow on $55.9M of capex, a 45.1% return on equity and 58.4% return on invested capital. The 2025 annual report describes a "compensation model" in which incentive pay changes "in proportion to changes in our operating income" — pay tied to profit — and warns that carrier pricing swings could hurt its ability to "maintain historical unitary profitability" (profit per shipment). The vulnerability is that the whole model earns a spread between carriers and shippers, and it owns none of the capacity that sets that spread.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Scarcity is the tailwind, and scarcity ends. Q3 air profit rode rates the release calls elevated, with capacity "constrained by limited fleet expansion and ongoing new aircraft delivery delays"; Q3 ocean profit rode port congestion the company expects to last "through the remainder of 2026." When capacity returns and ports clear, spreads normalise. Q2 showed how fast that can run the other way: ocean net revenue fell 6.9% YoY while ocean gross revenue rose, because carrier pricing moved faster than Expeditors' pricing.
02
Some of the customs boost was labelled temporary by the company itself. In Q2 it said "a temporary surge in IEEPA-related filings drove higher pricing." Customs is the largest profit pool; if tariff filings normalise, part of that pricing goes with them.
03
Customer concentration in AI demand. Management names hyperscalers among the customers whose demand is outpacing the market. That is a strong customer group, but it ties a meaningful slice of growth to one capex cycle.
04
Receivables are consuming cash. Accounts receivable rose from $2.02B at December 31, 2025 to $3.09B at September 30, 2026 as revenue surged. Nine-month operating cash flow was $700.4M against $840.3M of net earnings, and cash fell from $1.31B to $1.09B after $946M of buybacks and dividends.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — an expensive stock can stay overbought for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Expeditors is winning share at the moment the world made shipping harder. Q3 2026 revenue rose 41.9%, net revenue 27.1% and operating income 54.6%, and all three service lines grew net revenue by double digits, ocean included. Operating efficiency reached 36.7% against a 30% historical target, with headcount up just 1.5%. The business needs almost no capital — $55.9M of trailing capex against $927.9M of free cash flow and a 58% ROIC — and returns it: $946M of buybacks and dividends in nine months and a share count down 3.7% in a year. Tariffs, customs enforcement and AI hardware logistics are multi-year trends, not one-quarter events, and customs is the highest-spread line. If the November 18 Investor Day raises the long-term margin target, 2026 may look less like a peak and more like a new base.

Bear Case

This is a spread business at the top of a spread cycle. Air rates are high because aircraft are scarce, ocean is up because Asian ports are jammed, and in Q2 management itself called the IEEPA filing surge "temporary" — all three can reverse, and together. Consensus sees FY2027 EPS 1.7% below 2026 and FY2027 revenue down 6.8%, yet the stock trades at 25.1x trailing and 23.2x 2027 earnings, above the $189.47 average target, with more Sell ratings (5) than Buy ratings (3) and a monthly RSI of 81.8. The last time freight was this tight, in 2021, EPS peaked at $8.27 and the stock then fell 34% in nine months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Air capacity normalises. Airfreight is 37.6% of revenue and its Q3 profit rode elevated rates from constrained capacity. More aircraft deliveries, a return of passenger belly space or a Middle East de-escalation would compress the spread the growth depends on.
02
Congestion and tariff work fade. Customs & other is the largest net-revenue line ($597M in Q3) and includes tariff-related fees; in Q2 the company called part of its customs pricing a temporary IEEPA-related surge. Ocean's Q3 recovery leans on port congestion the company expects to last through 2026.
03
Valuation leaves little room. At 23.2x 2027 earnings with consensus expecting 2027 EPS to slip 1.7%, the price already sits 4.3% above the average target. The stock fell 3.2% the day after a quarter that beat consensus by 18%.
Industry Commentary
Trade complexity as a product
EMERGING TAILWIND

For a customs broker, every rule change is billable work. Over the past year the company has cited new tariffs, the end of de minimis entry, IEEPA filings and, in Q3, tariff-related additional-line fees and post-entry work. Customs, Transcon, Distribution and Order Management each grew double digits in Q2, and the Q3 release says all of them did again. The open question is how much of that is permanent complexity and how much is a one-time rush of filings.

Air cargo capacity and the AI build-out
CYCLICAL

Two forces met in the air market this year: fewer passenger flights (and so less belly space) because of the Middle East conflict, and demand from AI hyperscalers moving servers that need upper-deck freighter space. In August management added rising fuel costs, routing problems and returning North Asia e-commerce, and said carriers "are under enormous strain and may continue to be for some time." In November the Q3 release added that "air capacity remains constrained by limited fleet expansion and ongoing new aircraft delivery delays." For a forwarder that is a windfall while it lasts, and the same AI demand that lifted this year could turn quickly if AI spending slows.

The ocean downturn turned
WATCH

Ocean was the weak line through Q2: container volume flat YoY and ocean net revenue down 6.9%. In Q3 volume rose 7%, revenue 47.8% and net revenue 18.4%, on congestion at Asian ports and transshipment hubs that the company expects to last through the rest of 2026. With ocean turning while air and customs hold, the bull case has its third leg. The bear version: all three lines now ride conditions — short air capacity, jammed ports, tariff paperwork — that tend to clear, and they could clear at the same time.
