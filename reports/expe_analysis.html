01 Company Overview

Expedia Group is an online travel marketplace holding company. It sells lodging, air, car, cruise and activities directly to travellers through Brand Expedia, Hotels.com and Vrbo (the B2C segment), it white-labels and wholesales that same supply and technology to airlines, banks, corporate travel managers and other retailers (the B2B segment), and it runs trivago, a majority-owned hotel metasearch and advertising business listed separately in Frankfurt. FY2025 revenue was $14.73B, up 7.6% from $13.69B; the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $15.70B, up 12.0%. The company ranks roughly #723 globally by market capitalisation at $33.2B — a reminder that the second-largest Western online travel agency is a mid-cap, not a mega-cap.

The central debate on this page is not whether travel demand holds up. It is mix. Expedia's consumer brands grew revenue +8% last quarter while its B2B arm grew +23% and trivago +48%. A B2B dollar is a lower-margin, lower-brand-equity, higher-retention dollar than a B2C dollar, and it is increasingly the marginal dollar. Meanwhile the company has bought back so much stock that book equity has been ground down to $2.47B total — of which only $1.21B is attributable to Expedia Group shareholders — which makes the headline ROE of 89.5% a statement about the balance sheet rather than about the business. Sections 04 and 07 take both of those apart.

Three consumer brands pointed at three different travellers. Brand Expedia is the full-service package and multi-product site; Hotels.com is the loyalty-led hotel specialist; Vrbo is whole-home alternative accommodation and the direct answer to Airbnb. The segment's economics are the classic OTA (online travel agency) shape: high gross margin on a merchant or agency booking, but enormous variable marketing spend to buy the traveller in the first place — Expedia and its peers are among the largest buyers of search advertising on earth. That is why an +8% growth rate matters more than it looks: in B2C, growth bought with paid channels does not convert cleanly into EBITDA, and slower, loyalty-driven growth can be worth more per dollar. Management has spent two years consolidating these brands onto one technology platform and one loyalty programme (One Key) precisely to reduce that marketing tax. The recent product push has been on the Vrbo side — the company announced 12 new partner products and functionalities across Vrbo and its property-management software, aimed at pulling more supply in directly rather than renting it from a channel manager.

Revenue Mix

This is where the market's attention is, and it deserves to be. B2B takes Expedia's supply inventory, rates, and booking technology and rents them to someone else's brand: an airline's hotel-attach page, a bank's rewards portal, a corporate travel manager, an offline agency, a regional OTA that does not want to build its own lodging supply chain. The traveller often never sees the Expedia name. Three things make this segment structurally more attractive than its lower gross margin suggests. First, there is almost no consumer marketing spend — the partner buys the customer, so revenue converts to contribution far more efficiently than a paid-search B2C booking. Second, integrations are sticky: once a bank's rewards portal runs on Expedia's API, switching means an engineering project, not a price comparison. Third, it is the only part of the company growing at roughly three times the rate of the core. At +23% on $1.49B in a single quarter, B2B is now large enough that its growth alone accounts for the majority of consolidated revenue growth. The bear reading is equally simple: B2B is a wholesale business, its pricing is negotiated by sophisticated counterparties, and its take rate is structurally below B2C's — so a mix shift toward it is a slow tax on blended margin that the current operating leverage happens to be masking.

Watch item: B2B take rate is not disclosed separately. The consolidated revenue margin of 12.7% is the only public handle on it — if that number stops rising while B2B mix keeps rising, the mix tax has arrived.

trivago is a separately listed, majority-owned hotel price-comparison site that makes its money selling clicks to OTAs and hotels — including to Expedia itself. It is small, it is cyclical with advertising budgets, and its +48% growth off a $145M base contributes about $47M of incremental quarterly revenue, so it is not moving the consolidated needle. It matters for two structural reasons. One, it is the reason Expedia's balance sheet carries a meaningful non-controlling interest: of $2.471B total equity, only $1.209B is attributable to Expedia Group shareholders — the gap is largely trivago minorities, and it is why any per-share book-value calculation has to start from the smaller number. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Two-sided supply network B2B API lock-in Vrbo whole-home supply One Key loyalty 30 years of booking data Scale in paid acquisition 90%+ gross margin

The durable advantage is not the consumer brand — consumers are famously promiscuous about where they book a hotel, and Expedia buys a large share of its traffic. The durable advantage is the supply side: several hundred thousand negotiated lodging relationships, rates and content, assembled over three decades, which a new entrant cannot recreate with capital alone because the bottleneck is thousands of individual hotelier contracts. That asset is what the B2B segment monetises, and B2B is precisely where the switching costs live — an airline or bank that has embedded Expedia's lodging API into its rewards portal is running an integration, not a vendor relationship. The financial fingerprint of that asset is the 90.4% gross margin and the $4.46B of trailing FCF against a $33.2B market cap. A business converting 28% of revenue to free cash flow is not a commodity.

Key Vulnerabilities

A
Google sits upstream of the entire funnel. Expedia's customer acquisition runs through search, and Google owns both the auction and a competing product (Google Hotels / Flights) that can be placed above the organic results. Every incremental point of margin Expedia earns is, in principle, a point Google can decide to take in the auction. This is a permanent structural tax with no negotiated ceiling.
B
AI trip planning attacks the search box, not the supply. If travellers plan and book inside a conversational assistant, the value of being a destination website falls while the value of being an inventory API rises. That is survivable — arguably it is why B2B matters — but it means the high-margin B2C dollar is the one at risk, and the replacement dollar is the lower-take-rate one.
C
Booking Holdings is four times the market cap with a better European hotel position. BKNG's $145B capitalisation on $28.2B of revenue versus EXPE's $33.2B on $15.7B is not a rounding difference in sentiment; it reflects a persistently higher take rate and stronger international agency-model economics. Expedia competes with a structurally better-capitalised rival in the same auctions.
D
Travel is the most cyclical discretionary category there is. Revenue fell to $8.60B in FY2021 and the stock lost roughly 55% peak-to-trough in 2022. Gross bookings are a leading indicator with a lag of one to two quarters; a consumer slowdown shows up in bookings before it shows up in revenue, and in revenue before it shows up in EBITDA.

RSI compares the size of recent gains with the size of recent losses and scores the result from 0 to 100. Above 70 the stock has risen hard enough, fast enough, that buyers may be exhausted; below 30 the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation one — a stock can stay overbought for months in a genuine uptrend.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $164.41 to the September 10, 2026 close of $276.88, EXPE returned +68.4% in price terms — roughly 11.0% a year compounded, before the modest dividend. That headline flatters a violent path. The stock lost 55.3% from its February 2022 close of $196.11 to its December 2022 low of $87.60, then spent two full years — through October 2023 at $95.29 — failing to escape double digits. The entire five-year return was earned in the 22 months from October 2023 to August 2026, during which the stock tripled from $95.29 to $317.12.

The shape matters for what you are buying today. The 2022–2023 trough was a multiple event, not an earnings event: revenue grew 35.7% in FY2022 and 10.0% in FY2023 while the stock halved, because the market repriced discretionary travel against rising rates. The 2024–2026 recovery is the reverse — revenue growth decelerated to 6.6% and 7.6% while earnings per share went from $5.31 to $16.09, driven by operating leverage on a fixed cost base, buybacks, and a rising take rate. That is a higher-quality re-rating than a demand rebound, but it is also a harder one to repeat: you cannot take the share count down 6% a year forever, and 90% gross margin leaves limited room for further mix-driven leverage.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A 12.4x forward earnings multiple and a 13.3% free-cash-flow yield on a business compounding adjusted EBITDA at 23% with a rising take rate is a genuine mispricing, and the mispricing has an identifiable cause: exclusion from the NASDAQ-100 and a "legacy OTA" label that predates the B2B pivot. B2B at +23% on $1.49B a quarter is now big enough to set the consolidated growth rate, it carries near-zero consumer-acquisition cost, and its API integrations are sticky. With $4.46B of trailing free cash flow, a net cash position of $1.44B, and a share count falling 6% a year, the company can compound per-share value without needing the multiple to move at all.

Bear Case

The re-rating is running on borrowed fuel. Revenue growth is 12% only because take rate is rising; room nights — the actual volume metric — grew just 6%. EPS growth of 95% is largely buyback and non-recurring items; adjusted EPS grew 36%. Meanwhile the mix is shifting toward the lower-take-rate B2B segment, which caps the very lever currently driving the beat, and net margin of 13.0% already sits well below the 20.5% industry aggregate. Add Google upstream of the funnel, AI assistants attacking the consumer search box, and Booking Holdings competing with four times the capital, and the discount to the index starts to look earned rather than accidental.

01
Take-rate reversal as B2B mix rises. Consolidated revenue margin of 12.7% is the only public window into segment take rates. B2B is growing three times faster than B2C at a structurally lower take rate; arithmetic says the blend must eventually fall. If revenue margin flattens or declines in Q3 while B2B mix keeps climbing, the "+14% revenue on +6% room nights" story reverts to a +6% story.
02
Discretionary travel is the first line item consumers cut. Gross bookings lead revenue by a quarter or two, and revenue leads EBITDA. FY2021 revenue of $8.60B against FY2025's $14.73B is the historical range of what a demand shock does to this P&L. With a beta of 1.25, a current ratio of 0.80 and $5.69B of debt, the equity absorbs that cyclicality with leverage on top.
03
Channel disintermediation by AI and by Google. Expedia's high-margin dollar is the one where a traveller types a query into its own site. Both an AI assistant that plans and books, and a Google results page that answers the query above the fold, remove that dollar and replace it — at best — with a lower-take-rate wholesale dollar. This is not a quarter-to-quarter risk; it is the reason a 90%-gross-margin, 13%-FCF-yield business trades at 17x earnings.

CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The single most important number in the Q2 release is not the EBITDA beat — it is room nights +6% against gross bookings +12% and revenue +14%. Expedia is selling roughly the same incremental volume of hotel rooms as the underlying travel market is producing, and manufacturing the rest of its growth from price per night and take rate. Take rate rose 27 basis points to 12.7%. That is a real achievement — it reflects advertising attach, package mix and B2B contract terms — but it is a finite lever, and it is the lever most directly threatened by the segment mix shift described above. The whole industry has the same problem: post-pandemic travel volume has normalised, and the growth left in online travel is monetisation growth, not unit growth.

The customer-acquisition tax is set by someone else
ONGOING HEADWIND

Online travel agencies convert 90%+ gross margin into 17% operating margin, and the gap is almost entirely selling and marketing. Expedia's operating margin of 17.44% against a 90.43% gross margin means roughly 73 points of revenue disappear between the two lines. A large share of that is paid to search and performance-marketing platforms that also compete with Expedia's product. This is why the loyalty programme, the brand consolidation and the B2B channel all matter more than they sound: each is an attempt to source a booking without paying the auction. The 196-basis-point adjusted EBITDA margin expansion in Q2 is the scoreboard on that effort, and it is genuinely good — but it is being measured against a cost line Expedia does not control.

B2B travel technology is the tailwind — and the valuation question
EMERGING TAILWIND

Every bank rewards portal, airline attach page, corporate travel tool and regional OTA that wants lodging inventory faces a build-or-rent decision, and the build side keeps losing. That is why Expedia's B2B segment grew 23% to $1.493B in a quarter while its consumer brands grew 8%. The bull framing: the market is valuing the whole company at 12.4x forward earnings as though it were a declining consumer web property, while a third of revenue is a growing, low-marketing-cost infrastructure business with contractual switching costs — re-rate that third alone toward a software-adjacent multiple and the stock is worth considerably more than $339. The bear framing: B2B is wholesale distribution, not software; its counterparties are large, sophisticated and price-sensitive; the take rate is lower than B2C's by construction; and the faster it grows, the faster it dilutes the margin structure that produced this year's beat. Both readings are consistent with every number on this page. That is precisely why 21 analysts sit on Hold while 17 sit on Buy.
