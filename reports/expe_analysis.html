01 Company Overview

Expedia Group is an online travel marketplace holding company. It sells lodging, air, car, cruise and activities directly to travellers through Brand Expedia, Hotels.com and Vrbo (the B2C segment), it sells that same supply and technology, often under the partner's own name, to airlines, banks, corporate travel managers and other retailers (the B2B segment), and it runs trivago, a majority-owned, separately listed hotel metasearch and advertising business. FY2025 revenue was $14.73B, up 7.6% from $13.69B; the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 2026 is $16.23B, up 12.9%. At $30.7B it is worth about a quarter of Booking Holdings ($119.5B) and of Airbnb ($111.6B), although it takes in more revenue than Airbnb.

The central debate on this page is not whether travel demand holds up. It is mix. Expedia's consumer brands grew revenue +6% in the third quarter while its B2B arm grew +23% and trivago +20%. A B2B dollar earns less than a B2C dollar: adjusted EBITDA was 27.5% of revenue in B2B against 43.5% in the consumer brands in Q3, and the B2B figure fell from 28.9% a year earlier. B2B is also where most of the growth now comes from. Meanwhile the company has bought back so much stock that book equity has been ground down to $2.92B total — of which only $1.65B is attributable to Expedia Group shareholders — which makes the headline ROE of 76.8% a statement about the balance sheet rather than about the business. Sections 04 and 07 take both of those apart. The third-quarter print added a second debate: whether a fourth-quarter guide of 3.3–5.2% bookings growth is a one-quarter pause or the start of a slower run.

Three consumer brands pointed at three different travellers. Brand Expedia is the full-service package and multi-product site; Hotels.com is the loyalty-led hotel specialist; Vrbo is whole-home alternative accommodation and the direct answer to Airbnb. The segment's economics are the usual OTA (online travel agency) shape: a high gross margin on each booking, but heavy marketing spend to win the traveller in the first place. Management has moved these brands onto one technology platform and one loyalty programme (One Key), and on the Q3 call the CFO pointed to improved consumer marketing returns. This is where the market's attention is, and it deserves to be. B2B takes Expedia's supply inventory, rates, and booking technology and rents them to someone else's brand: an airline's hotel-booking page, a bank's rewards portal, a corporate travel manager, an offline agency, a regional OTA that does not want to build its own lodging supply chain. The traveller often never sees the Expedia name. Two things make this segment attractive. First, integrations are hard to leave: once a bank's rewards portal runs on Expedia's API (the software link between the two systems), switching means an engineering project, not a price comparison. Second, it is growing nearly four times as fast as the consumer brands. At +23% on $1.71B in a single quarter, B2B accounted for about 60% of the $526M rise in consolidated revenue. The catch is what that revenue costs to earn. In Q3 B2B revenue was 15.3% of its gross bookings against 13.4% for B2C, so it keeps more of each booking as revenue, but its direct selling and marketing was $1.05B, 61% of its revenue against 34% for B2C. Its pricing is negotiated by large partners, and a shift toward it lowers the company's overall profit margin. CFO Derek Andersen told the Q3 call, "I think in the very near term, we are seeing a little pressure on the B2B margins." B2B adjusted EBITDA rose 17% on revenue up 23%, and its margin fell to 27.5% from 28.9%.

Watch item: Expedia prints only one company-wide revenue margin (14.5% in Q3, up 16 basis points from a year earlier), but its segment tables give the rest: B2B revenue was 15.3% of B2B gross bookings (14.8% a year earlier) and B2C's was 13.4% (13.5%). The line to watch is B2B's adjusted EBITDA margin, 27.5% from 28.9%: if it keeps falling while B2B grows faster, the growth is costing more to win.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Two-sided supply network B2B API lock-in Vrbo whole-home supply One Key loyalty 30 years of booking data Scale in paid acquisition 90%+ gross margin

The durable advantage is not the consumer brand — consumers are famously promiscuous about where they book a hotel, and Expedia buys a large share of its traffic. The durable advantage is the supply side: negotiated lodging relationships, rates and content, assembled over three decades, which a new entrant cannot recreate with capital alone because the bottleneck is thousands of individual hotelier contracts. That asset is what the B2B segment sells, and B2B is where the switching costs live — an airline or bank that has embedded Expedia's lodging API into its rewards portal is running an integration, not a vendor relationship. The financial fingerprint of that asset is the 90.5% gross margin and the $3.75B of trailing FCF against a $30.7B market cap. A business converting 23% of revenue to free cash flow is not a commodity.

Key Vulnerabilities

A
Google sits upstream of the entire funnel. Expedia's customer acquisition runs through search, and Google owns both the auction and a competing product (Google Hotels / Flights) that can be placed above the organic results. Every incremental point of margin Expedia earns is, in principle, a point Google can decide to take in the auction. This is a permanent cost with no negotiated limit.
B
AI trip planning attacks the search box, not the supply. If travellers plan and book inside a conversational assistant, the value of being a destination website falls while the value of being an inventory API rises. That is survivable — arguably it is why B2B matters — but it means the high-margin B2C dollar is the one at risk, and the replacement dollar is the lower-margin one.
C
Booking Holdings is nearly four times the market cap. BKNG's $119.5B market value on $28.1B of revenue is about 4.2 times sales, against about 1.9 times for EXPE's $30.7B on $16.2B. Expedia competes with a far larger rival in the same advertising auctions.
D
Travel spending is highly cyclical. FY2021 revenue of $8.60B was up 65.4% from the pandemic year, and the stock still lost roughly 55% from its February 2022 month-end close to its December 2022 close. Gross bookings are a leading indicator with a lag of one to two quarters; a consumer slowdown shows up in bookings before it shows up in revenue, and in revenue before it shows up in EBITDA.

Notable One-Off Items. Three distortions matter here. First, the trailing EPS leans on two strong quarters that carry non-operating items: Q2 2026 diluted EPS of $7.16 rose 188%, helped by a $280M gain on minority equity investments, against adjusted EPS of $5.76, up 36%, and Q3 diluted EPS of $8.42 rose 15% against adjusted EPS of $9.39, up 24%. Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 were $1.60 and a $0.05 loss. Use adjusted figures for the underlying pace of earnings, and do not annualise a single quarter. Second, the falling share count does part of the work: Q3 net income attributable to Expedia rose 7% to $1.03B, while diluted EPS rose 15%. Third, shareholders' equity is positive: $2.916B in total, $1.652B attributable to Expedia Group. ROE and debt-to-equity are shown above, but both are flagged ⚠ because equity shrunk by buybacks makes them measures of the balance sheet rather than of business quality. Anyone modelling this company should use EV/EBITDA or free-cash-flow yield instead: enterprise value (market cap plus debt, less cash and short-term investments) is about $30.63B, or 7.3 times the $4.198B of trailing adjusted EBITDA in the release.

RSI compares the size of recent gains with the size of recent losses and scores the result from 0 to 100. Above 70 the stock has risen hard enough, fast enough, that buyers may be exhausted; below 30 the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation one — a stock can stay overbought for months in a genuine uptrend.

Over the five years from the November 2021 close of $161.09 to the November 6, 2026 close of $259.24, EXPE returned +60.9% in price terms — roughly 10.1% a year compounded, before the modest dividend. That headline hides a violent path. The stock lost 55.3% from its February 2022 close of $196.11 to its December 2022 close of $87.60, and in October 2023, twenty months after that peak, it was still at $95.29. All of the five-year gain came after that: from $95.29 to $317.12 at the August 2026 month-end, more than tripling in 34 months, before falling back to today's price.

The shape matters for what you are buying today. The 2022–2023 fall did not come with falling sales: revenue grew 35.7% in FY2022 and 10.0% in FY2023 while the stock halved. The 2024–2026 recovery is the reverse. Revenue growth slowed to 6.6% and 7.6% while EPS went from $5.31 in FY2023 to $17.13 trailing, helped by costs rising more slowly than sales, buybacks and a rising take rate. That is a harder recovery to repeat: you cannot take the share count down 6–7% a year forever, and the fourth-quarter guide of 3.3–5.2% bookings growth shows what the slower phase looks like.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At 10.6 times analysts' 2027 adjusted earnings estimate and a 12.2% free-cash-flow yield, the shares price a business whose adjusted EBITDA just grew 17% as if the growth were over. B2B at +23% on $1.71B a quarter accounts for about 60% of the revenue growth. Management beat the top of its guidance and raised the year, and with $3.75B of trailing free cash flow and about $5.1B of buyback authorization left, the company can keep shrinking its share count without needing investors to pay a higher price for each dollar of earnings.

Bear Case

The fourth-quarter guide is the warning: gross bookings +3.3–5.2% after +11%, and adjusted EBITDA of $830–880M against a $917.3M consensus (StockStory). The CFO said B2B margins are under some near-term pressure and that marketing savings will come more slowly. B2B is growing nearly four times as fast as the consumer brands at a lower margin (27.5% against 43.5%), net margin of 13.0% sits well below the 20.2% industry aggregate, and EPS growth leans on a falling share count (Q3 net income +7%, diluted EPS +15%). Add Google upstream of the funnel, AI assistants attacking the consumer search box and Booking Holdings competing with nearly four times the market value, and the discount to the index starts to look earned.

01
Margins squeezed as B2B grows. B2B keeps more of each booking as revenue than B2C (15.3% against 13.4% in Q3), so the shift has not hurt the company-wide revenue margin of 14.5%. The cost shows up lower down: B2B's adjusted EBITDA margin was 27.5% against 43.5% for B2C, and it is growing nearly four times as fast. If B2B's margin keeps falling, the "+12% revenue on +7% room nights" story earns less at the bottom line.
02
Travel is an easy expense for consumers to cut. Gross bookings lead revenue by a quarter or two, and revenue leads EBITDA. FY2021 revenue of $8.60B, even after a 65.4% rebound from the pandemic year, against FY2025's $14.73B shows how far a demand shock can take this business. With a beta of 1.39, a current ratio of 0.77 and $5.46B of debt, shareholders take that swing with debt on top.
03
AI and Google cutting Expedia out of the booking. Expedia's high-margin dollar is the one where a traveller types a query into its own site. Both an AI assistant that plans and books, and a Google results page that answers the query at the top of the page, remove that dollar and replace it — at best — with a lower-margin wholesale dollar. This is not a quarter-to-quarter risk; it is the reason a 90%-gross-margin, 12%-FCF-yield business trades at 15 times earnings. On the Q3 call CEO Ariane Gorin said, "Agentic AI's real strength is in discovery," and "people still want to book with a trusted brand where they know that they will be taken care of."
Industry Commentary
Volume growth has been lagging revenue growth, and both are guided lower
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The numbers to read in the Q3 release are room nights +7%, gross bookings +11% and revenue +12%, and then the guide. The gap between them came from the average booking (booked hotel rates rose 2%) and from the slice of each booking Expedia keeps, up 16 basis points in the quarter. In Q3 that slice rose in B2B and slipped in the consumer brands: 15.3% from 14.8% and 13.4% from 13.5% (segment revenue ÷ segment gross bookings). For the fourth quarter the company guides to gross bookings up 3.3–5.2% and revenue up 4.0–5.7%, a sharp step down that management links to the U.S. election cycle, Middle East and Europe disruption, tougher B2B comparisons and the timing of its fall sale.

The customer-acquisition tax is set by someone else
ONGOING HEADWIND

Expedia turns a 90%+ gross margin into a 17% operating margin, and most of the gap is selling and marketing: in Q3 that line was $2.39B of the $3.19B between gross profit and operating income. Expedia's trailing operating margin of 17.24% against a 90.48% gross margin means roughly 73 points of revenue disappear between the two lines. Part of that is paid to search and advertising platforms that also compete with Expedia's product. This is why the loyalty programme and the brand consolidation matter more than they sound: each is an attempt to win a booking without paying the auction. The 135-basis-point adjusted EBITDA margin expansion in Q3 is the scoreboard on that effort, and it is genuinely good, but on the call management said savings on this line will come more slowly from here, and the fourth-quarter guide implies a 22.5–23.5% adjusted EBITDA margin.

B2B travel technology is the tailwind — and the valuation question
EMERGING TAILWIND

Every bank rewards portal, airline booking page, corporate travel tool and regional OTA that wants lodging inventory faces a build-or-rent decision, and the build side keeps losing. That is why Expedia's B2B segment grew 23% to $1.708B in a quarter while its consumer brands grew 6%. The bull framing: the market is valuing the whole company at 10.6 times analysts' 2027 adjusted earnings estimate as though it were a shrinking consumer web property, while a third of revenue is a growing infrastructure business with contracts that are costly to leave. Value that third the way investors value software companies and the stock is worth considerably more than today's price. The bear framing: B2B is wholesale distribution, not software; its partners are large, sophisticated and price-sensitive; it spends 61% of its revenue on direct selling and marketing against 34% for B2C; and the faster it grows, the more it lowers the company's overall profit margin. Both readings fit every number on this page, which is why 19 analysts sit on Hold while 17 sit on Buy.
