01 Company Overview

Ford is a 123-year-old automaker that today reports as three separate automotive businesses plus a bank. Ford Blue builds and sells internal-combustion and hybrid vehicles; Ford Pro sells commercial vans, trucks, chassis, parts, service and attached software to fleets; Ford Model e designs and sells electric vehicles; and Ford Credit is a captive finance company that lends to Ford's customers and dealers. FY2025 revenue was $187.3B, up 1.2% — a fifth consecutive year of revenue growth — but the company posted a net loss of $8.2B and −$2.06 of EPS on it. Ford ranks roughly #438 globally by market capitalisation at $58.30B, which is less than one twenty-fourth of Tesla's $1.40T on 1.8× Tesla's revenue.

The central debate is not "is Ford cheap." It is what you are actually buying when you buy the consolidated entity. In FY2025, Ford Pro earned $6.8B of EBIT, Ford Blue earned $3.0B, Ford Credit earned $2.6B of pre-tax profit — and Model e lost $4.8B. Two profitable industrial businesses and a finance company were writing a roughly $4.8B annual cheque to a third business that sold $6.7B of product. Everything else on this page — the warranty drag, the tariff bill, the dividend, the leverage optics — sits downstream of that one fact. Since Q4 2025 management has been dismantling the arrangement, and the market is trying to price how much of the EV loss is genuinely going away and how much is being reclassified as a one-time charge.

The legacy company: F-150, Bronco, Explorer, Mustang, Maverick, plus the hybrid variants that have become the actual demand story as pure-EV growth stalled. It is the largest business by revenue and by a wide margin the least profitable per dollar of sales — a 3.0% FY2025 EBIT margin on $101.0B. That is where the warranty and recall problem discussed in Section 03 lands hardest, because retail buyers of ICE trucks are exactly the customers whose repair costs Ford absorbs. The encouraging signal is that this is improving fast on price and mix rather than volume: in Q2 2026 Blue's revenue rose just 1% to $26.1B on an 8% decline in wholesales to 639,000 units, yet EBIT rose 72% to $1.135B and margin expanded from 2.6% to 4.4%, attributed to favourable product mix, higher net pricing and disciplined channel management. In H1 2026 Blue produced $3.077B of EBIT — already above the entire FY2025 result.

This is the single most important number on the page: Model e lost $4.8B of EBIT in FY2025 on $6.7B of revenue — a −72.1% margin. Ford lost roughly seventy-two cents of operating profit on every dollar of electric vehicle it sold, and Blue, Pro and Ford Credit funded it. That is not a start-up burn rate on a small base; it is very close in size to Ford Pro's entire $6.8B of annual profit. Management guided Model e to a loss of approximately $4B for 2026 — better, but still an amount that would rank as a large-cap company's entire earnings. The direction has at last turned: Q2 2026 revenue fell 56% to $1.0B on wholesales down 53% to 28,000 units, and the EBIT loss narrowed 31% to $919M — the third consecutive quarter of improvement, achieved by structural cost reduction and by simply building far fewer of them. H1 2026 EBIT was −$1.696B. The strategy from here is the Universal EV (UEV) platform: a scalable architecture whose first product is targeted at roughly $30,000, described by management as offering more cabin room than a Toyota RAV4 plus a pickup bed, with bi-directional charging, and with customer deliveries beginning in 2027. Everything that does not fit that plan is being cancelled — see Section 06.

Honest framing: at a −72% FY2025 margin, shrinking Model e is worth more to consolidated EBIT than growing it. That is an unusual thing to be able to say about a growth segment, and it is why the retreat has been received as good news.

Ford Credit originates retail loans and leases for Ford buyers and floorplan financing for Ford dealers. It is a bank in all but name, and it is doing well: $2.6B of FY2025 pre-tax earnings, $783M in Q1 2026 (+$203M YoY) and $757M in Q2 2026 (+$112M YoY) on strong financing margins and, per management, disciplined risk management. As of the Q2 2026 10-Q the book held $82.5B of consumer finance receivables and $23.4B of dealer financing, against a total allowance for credit losses of $968M, with 1.4% of the consumer portfolio 30+ days past due. That is roughly a 0.9% reserve against a portfolio performing normally — worth monitoring in a consumer-credit downturn, but not currently a stress signal.

Why this matters for the ratios: a lender funds its receivables with debt. Ford's consolidated debt-to-equity of 4.57× on $163.30B of total debt is dominated by Ford Credit's funding, not by industrial borrowings — see Section 04.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
F-Series truck franchise Ford Pro fleet lock-in ~1.6M paid software subs Dealer & service network Captive finance (Ford Credit) 21% import exposure vs GM 46% 123-year brand

Ford's real moat is not the brand and it is not the truck — it is the switching cost embedded in a commercial fleet. A consumer chooses a vehicle every five to seven years and can be bought away with an incentive. A utility company that has standardised 4,000 Transits on one telematics platform, one upfit spec, one parts inventory and one service relationship faces a genuine cost to change, and that cost is why Ford Pro earns a 10.3% margin selling essentially the same steel that Ford Blue sells at 3.0%. The software subscriptions — up ~50% year over year to roughly 1.6 million — deepen that lock-in each quarter, and unlike vehicle revenue they recur. Ford Credit widens the moat again: it lets Ford move metal in a tight credit market when a captive-less rival cannot, and it earns $2.6B a year for the privilege. What Ford has not demonstrated is any moat at all in electric vehicles, where a −72.1% segment margin says it is a price-taker facing manufacturers with a structural cost advantage.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Warranty and field service actions — the persistent, Ford-specific drag. The Q2 2026 10-Q carries a recorded warranty accrual of $17.571 billion, plus an estimate that reasonably possible additional warranty and field-service-action costs could run a further $2.0 billion beyond what is booked. To put $17.6B in scale: it is roughly 30% of Ford's entire market capitalisation, and roughly two and a half times FY2025 adjusted EBIT of $6.8B. This is the single clearest reason Ford Blue earns 3.0% while a peer group earns more — a recall is paid out of the same gross margin the vehicle earned on sale, years after the revenue was booked. The trend is finally the right way: management stated on the Q2 2026 call that recalls are down about 40% year over year, and the FY2026 guidance explicitly assumes $1 billion of combined material and warranty cost reduction. That $1B is not a rounding item — it is roughly 10% of the midpoint of the $10–11B EBIT guide. If it does not arrive, the guide does not hold.
02
Tariffs on imported vehicles, parts and metals. Ford quantified the FY2025 hit precisely: a gross cost of about $2.5 billion and, after mitigation, a net adverse adjusted EBIT impact of about $1.5 billion, disclosed on May 6, 2025 alongside a withdrawal of 2025 guidance. For 2026 the picture is messier and better: Ford projects roughly $1 billion of adverse tariff impact for the full year, excluding a $1.3 billion one-time IEEPA tariff benefit booked in Q1 2026 and excluding temporary Novelis-related costs. The Q2 2026 10-Q adds that Ford expects approximately $3 billion of federal and supplier tariff reimbursements, of which about $500 million is anticipated in H2 2026. Netting those, tariffs are currently a cash tailwind and a policy risk — the reimbursements are recoveries of amounts already paid, and the underlying rate structure can change with a signature.
03
The EV write-down cycle. Between Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 Ford recognised $17.356 billion of pre-tax special items in FY2025 alone — including roughly $10.7B of Model e asset impairment and EV programme cancellations, ~$3.2B on the BlueOval SK joint-venture disposition, ~$0.8B on cancelling an all-electric three-row SUV and ~$0.4B of European restructuring — then a further $4.179 billion of special charges in Q2 2026. Management has flagged up to $2 billion of additional pre-tax charges still to come. Every one of these is excluded from adjusted EBIT. An investor paying 8.77× forward earnings is paying for the adjusted number while the GAAP number has been negative for two of the last five years.
04
Single-supplier concentration. A single aluminium supplier — Novelis — constrained Ford Pro production enough to knock segment margin from 12.3% to 9.7% in one quarter and to be named as the cause. For a company whose best business is aluminium-intensive trucks and vans, that is a live structural exposure, not a one-off.

Notable one-off items — and they are not small. The honest summary: neither the trailing loss nor the adjusted profit is the right number on its own, and H1 2026 net income of $1.221B (against $435M in H1 2025) is probably the cleanest recent read.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $17.08 to the September 4, 2026 close of $14.62, Ford's share price fell 14.4% over five years. Adding back roughly $2.85 per share of regular dividends paid across the window (approximate — the exact per-period sum was not verified), total return is approximately break-even (est.). Five years of no capital return, in a period when the company grew revenue every single year. That is the whole indictment of the business model: revenue grew from $136.3B in FY2021 to $187.3B in FY2025, up 37%, and shareholders got nothing but the coupon. The path was violent — a peak monthly close of $20.77 in December 2021, a trough of $9.55 in February 2025 (−54.0% from the peak), then a +53.1% recovery to today. Against the S&P 500, which rose substantially over the same window, this is severe underperformance; against the NASDAQ-100 it is worse still. Neither index's precise five-year return was verified from the sources fetched, so no percentage gap is stated — but the direction is not in doubt. Within the peer group the picture is more flattering: year to date Ford is +15.30% against GM's +8.63%, Toyota's −7.92%, Tesla's −21.27% and Stellantis's −48.94%. Ford is winning the sector race in 2026 and has lost the market race over five years.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Read Ford as a sum of parts, because that is how it reports. FY2025: Ford Pro earned $6.8B of EBIT on $66.3B of revenue (10.3%). Ford Blue earned $3.0B on $101.0B (3.0%). Ford Credit earned $2.6B of pre-tax profit. Model e lost $4.8B on $6.7B (−72.1%). Add the three profitable pieces and you get $12.4B of pre-tax earnings power; subtract Model e and roughly $5.6B of unallocated corporate and interest items and you arrive at the $6.8B of adjusted EBIT the company actually reported. The entire equity is capitalised at $58.30B. Ford Pro alone, valued on any reasonable multiple for a 10%-margin commercial-vehicle-plus-software business, plausibly accounts for most of that — which is the sum-of-parts argument in one line, and the reason the segment split matters more than any headline number.

The Model e number is the fulcrum. A $4.8B annual loss inside a company whose whole market capitalisation is $58.3B is not a rounding error — it is roughly one and a half times what a full year of Ford Pro's profit is worth after tax. Management has guided the loss to ~$4B in 2026 and has narrowed it three quarters running (Q2 2026: −$919M, an improvement of 31% year over year, achieved on revenue down 56%). The mechanism is straightforward and slightly uncomfortable: Ford is losing less money on EVs by selling far fewer of them. Wholesales fell 53% year over year to 28,000 units in the quarter. That works arithmetically — a business with a −72% margin does destroy less value at lower volume — but it is a retreat, not a fix, and the actual fix, the Universal EV platform at roughly $30,000, does not deliver a vehicle to a customer until 2027. Between now and then, the EV business is a shrinking loss and a series of restructuring charges of up to $2B more.

Warranty is the reason Blue earns 3% and not 6%. The Q2 2026 10-Q shows a booked warranty accrual of $17.571B, with a further $2.0B of reasonably possible additional warranty and field-service-action cost above that. Ford has spent several years as an outlier on recall volume, and the cost of a field service action is charged against the gross margin of vehicles sold years earlier — which is precisely why a 7.12% consolidated gross margin looks the way it does. The improvement is real and recent: recalls down about 40% year over year per the Q2 call, and $1B of material and warranty cost reduction assumed inside the FY2026 guide. That $1B is about 10% of the EBIT guidance midpoint. If Ford delivers it, the $10–11B guide holds and the forward multiple of 8.77× is defensible. If it slips, the guide slips with it. There is no other single line item in this company where a billion dollars turns so directly on execution rather than on the economy.

The leverage ratio is not what it looks like. Ford's consolidated debt-to-equity of 4.57× on $163.30B of total debt would be alarming for a manufacturer. It is not a manufacturer's ratio. Ford Credit held $82.5B of consumer finance receivables and $23.4B of dealer financing at Q2 2026 — assets that a captive lender funds with borrowings by construction, exactly as a bank does. Printing 4.57× as "Ford's leverage" conflates a loan book with a truck plant. The exact split between Ford Credit and industrial debt was not verified from the sources fetched and so is not stated, but the direction is unambiguous, and the relevant solvency facts are the ~$50B of total liquidity reported at FY2025 year-end, the $18.6B of cash plus $12.7B of marketable securities at Q2 2026, and the $4.0B minimum-liquidity covenant attached to the assumed DOE loan. Ford Credit's own credit quality — a $968M allowance and 1.4% of the consumer book 30+ days past due — is the number to watch if the consumer weakens, not the consolidated ratio.

The dividend, and whether it is covered. This is the reason a large share of Ford's register owns it. The regular dividend is $0.60 a year, yielding 4.10%, with a $0.15 Q3 2026 dividend declared and, per management, fifteen consecutive years of payment. On ~3.99B shares the annual cost is approximately $2.4B. Test it three ways. (i) Against TTM free cash flow of $7.28B — covered 3.0×. (ii) Against Ford's own FY2026 adjusted free cash flow guidance of $6–7B — covered 2.5–2.9×. (iii) Against FY2025 adjusted free cash flow of $3.5B, the worst recent year — covered 1.5×. Against GAAP earnings it is not covered at all, because there were none. The conclusion: the dividend is comfortably covered by cash generation in a normal year and thinly covered in a bad one, and it is cash flow rather than accounting profit that pays it. The genuine risk is not this year's coverage; it is that Ford must simultaneously fund $9.5–10.5B of annual capex, a still-loss-making EV programme and up to $2B of restructuring charges. In the 2020 downturn Ford suspended the dividend outright. Investors should own this yield knowing it is a cyclical's dividend attached to a 1.84 beta, not a utility's.

Bull Case

You are paying $58.3B for a company containing a Ford Pro that earned $6.8B of EBIT at a 10.3% margin with software subscriptions up ~50% year over year, a Ford Credit earning $2.6B pre-tax with a clean book, and a Ford Blue whose Q2 margin just expanded from 2.6% to 4.4% on price and mix while volumes fell. The $4.8B annual EV loss — the thing that was destroying all of it — is being deliberately shut down, guided to ~$4B in 2026 and improving three quarters running. Guidance has been raised twice in 2026, to $10–11B of adjusted EBIT and $6–7B of adjusted FCF. Recalls are down ~40%, tariffs are net-positive in 2026 on ~$3B of expected reimbursements, and you are collecting 4.10% at 8.77× forward earnings while you wait.

Bear Case

Ford has produced a GAAP loss in two of the last five years, lost $8.2B in FY2025, and has taken $17.4B of special items in one year with up to $2B more flagged. The entire investment case rests on adjusted numbers that exclude those charges. Gross margin is 7.12%, ROIC is 0.91% — below any cost of capital — and capex runs $9.5–10.5B a year against $6.8B of FY2025 adjusted EBIT. A $17.6B warranty accrual and $2.0B of possible additional field-action cost sit on the balance sheet. Model e's improvement comes from selling 53% fewer EVs, not from fixing the economics, and the replacement product is 2027 at the earliest into a market where Chinese manufacturers set the global cost floor. Over five years the stock has returned roughly nothing while revenue grew 37%.

01
The $1B warranty-and-material saving does not land. It is roughly 10% of the FY2026 adjusted EBIT guidance midpoint and it is an execution assumption, not a market assumption. A $17.571B accrual with $2.0B of reasonably possible additional exposure is the balance-sheet evidence that this problem has been persistent and expensive; one strong quarter of recall data does not retire it.
02
Chinese manufacturers set the cost floor Ford must eventually meet. Ford's answer to affordable electrification is a ~$30,000 vehicle on the Universal EV platform delivering in 2027. Chinese OEMs are already producing at cost structures Western automakers have not matched, and tariffs are the only thing currently holding that competition out of Ford's core markets — a policy protection, not a moat. 03
Cyclical exposure with a 1.84 beta and a leveraged captive. Ford is a consumer-discretionary manufacturer selling $50,000+ financed purchases. A consumer recession hits three ways at once: unit volumes fall, mix and pricing (the entire source of Blue's Q2 margin gain) reverse, and Ford Credit's $82.5B consumer book — reserved at $968M, with 1.4% currently 30+ days past due — deteriorates. The dividend was suspended in 2020; that precedent exists.
  Industry Commentary
The Western EV Retreat Is Now Industry-Wide
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Ford's FY2025 disclosure is the clearest quantification of a problem every legacy Western automaker shares: $4.8B of EBIT lost on $6.7B of EV revenue, followed by roughly $10.7B of Model e impairments and programme cancellations, a $0.8B three-row electric SUV cancellation, the exit from the BlueOval SK battery joint venture, the cancellation of three further EVs and the end of F-150 Lightning production. Ford's own EV strategy is now to build fewer of them until a low-cost platform arrives in 2027. The battery-plant buildout that was supposed to be the moat has instead become a $3.8B DOE loan on Ford's balance sheet with a $4.0B liquidity covenant attached. Stellantis, down 48.94% year to date, is the market's verdict on a peer that has handled the same transition worse.
Tariffs Have Become a Competitive Variable, Not Just a Cost
ONGOING HEADWIND
The 25% duties on imported vehicles and parts, plus steel and aluminium tariffs, cost Ford a ~$2.5B gross / ~$1.5B net adverse EBIT impact in 2025 and forced the withdrawal of guidance in May of that year. In 2026 the same policy is running the other way: roughly $1B of adverse impact, offset by a $1.3B one-time IEEPA benefit in Q1 and approximately $3B of expected federal and supplier reimbursements. The strategic asymmetry matters more than the annual number: Ford imports roughly 21% of the vehicles it sells in the US against roughly 46% for GM, so a tariff regime that damages the whole sector damages Ford's closest competitor more than twice as much. Ford's CEO called tariffs "an opportunity for Ford." Management has publicly pressed for USMCA terms that improve competitiveness against Japanese and Korean imports. The obvious hazard: a benefit that arrived by executive action can depart the same way, and Ford's aluminium exposure at Novelis just cost Ford Pro 260 basis points of margin in a single quarter.
Commercial Vehicles and Attached Software Are the Real Margin Pool
EMERGING TAILWIND
The most under-discussed structural shift in the sector is that fleet customers, not retail buyers, are where durable automotive margin now lives — and that the margin comes increasingly from what is attached to the vehicle rather than the vehicle itself. Ford Pro's 10.3% FY2025 EBIT margin against Ford Blue's 3.0% is the cleanest available evidence, and its ~1.6M paid subscriptions, up ~50% year over year, are recurring revenue growing at software rates inside a company trading at 0.31× sales. Bull framing: if the market ever values Ford Pro on its own economics rather than blending it into a $58B automaker with a 7% gross margin, the sum-of-parts gap closes and the 8.77× forward multiple looks like a mispricing. Bear framing: Ford does not sell Ford Pro separately, it will not, and until it does, Pro's cash is what funds Model e's losses and Blue's warranty bill. You do not get to own the good business on its own — that is precisely why it is available at this price.
