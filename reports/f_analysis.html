01 Company Overview

Ford is a 123-year-old automaker that today reports as three separate automotive businesses plus a bank. Ford Blue builds and sells internal-combustion and hybrid vehicles; Ford Pro sells commercial vans, trucks, chassis, parts, service and attached software to fleets; Ford Model e designs and sells electric vehicles; and Ford Credit is a captive finance company that lends to Ford's customers and dealers. FY2025 revenue was $187.3B, up 1.2% — a fifth consecutive year of revenue growth — but the company posted a net loss of $8.2B and −$2.06 of EPS on it. At the October 30, 2026 close Ford's market capitalisation was $48.92B (3.99B shares × $12.25, our calculation from the Q3 2026 10-Q share count), about one thirty-second of Tesla's $1.58T on 1.8× Tesla's revenue.

The central debate is not "is Ford cheap." It is what you are actually buying when you buy the consolidated entity. In FY2025, Ford Pro earned $6.8B of EBIT, Ford Blue earned $3.0B, Ford Credit earned $2.6B of pre-tax profit — and Model e lost $4.8B. Two profitable industrial businesses and a finance company were writing a roughly $4.8B annual cheque to a third business that sold $6.7B of product. Everything else on this page — the warranty drag, the tariff bill, the dividend, the leverage optics — sits downstream of that one fact. Since Q4 2025 management has been dismantling the arrangement, and the market is trying to price how much of the EV loss is genuinely going away and how much is being reclassified as a one-time charge. The third quarter of 2026, reported on October 28, kept the pattern: adjusted EBIT of $2.7B (up 3%), net income of $2.0B, a Model e loss 25% smaller than a year earlier, and the low end of the full-year profit guide raised to $10.5B. What it added was a warning: commodity costs are rising, and the CEO said 2027 profit is targeted to be roughly in line with 2026, not higher.

This is the part of Ford that the equity market underrates, and the segment disclosure makes the case without help. Ford Pro sells Transit vans, Super Duty trucks and stripped chassis to plumbers, utilities, municipalities and rental fleets — then keeps earning on them through parts, warranty-adjacent service, and telematics software billed by subscription. In FY2025 it produced a 10.3% EBIT margin against Ford Blue's 3.0%: a commercial customer buys on total cost of ownership and uptime, not on incentives, and a fleet that has standardised on Transit does not re-shop the decision every model year. The attached-software line is growing fastest: Ford Pro's paid software subscriptions grew 30% in 2025 (FY2025 release), and Ford put its paid subscriptions at roughly 1.6 million in Q2 2026, up ~50% year over year. The legacy company: F-150, Bronco, Explorer, Mustang, Maverick, plus the hybrid variants that have become the actual demand story as pure-EV growth stalled. It is the largest business by revenue and by a wide margin the least profitable per dollar of sales — a 3.0% FY2025 EBIT margin on $101.0B. That is where the warranty and recall problem discussed in Section 03 lands hardest, because retail buyers of ICE trucks are exactly the customers whose repair costs Ford absorbs. The encouraging signal in 2026 has been price and mix rather than volume: in Q2 Blue's EBIT rose 72%, which Ford tied to favourable mix and pricing power in trucks, off-roaders and hybrids. This is the single most important number on the page: Model e lost $4.8B of EBIT in FY2025 on $6.7B of revenue — a −72.1% margin. Ford lost roughly seventy-two cents of operating profit on every dollar of electric vehicle it sold, and Blue, Pro and Ford Credit funded it. That is not a start-up burn rate on a small base; it is about 70% of Ford Pro's entire $6.8B of annual profit. Management reaffirmed a Model e loss of about $4B for 2026 in the Q3 release — better, but still an amount that would rank as a large-cap company's entire earnings. The loss is shrinking because Ford is building far fewer EVs while it cuts costs: Q3 2026 revenue fell 31% to $1.2B on wholesales down 31% to 34,000 units, and the EBIT loss narrowed 25% to $1.058B, even though the margin on what it did sell worsened to −86.5% from −79.1%. Nine-month EBIT was −$2.754B against −$3.588B a year earlier. The strategy from here is the Universal EV (UEV) platform: a scalable architecture whose first product is targeted at roughly $30,000, described by management as offering more cabin room than a Toyota RAV4 plus a pickup bed, with bi-directional charging. On the Q3 call Jim Farley said "The launch remains on track for fourth quarter next year", and Sherry House said Ford is "still targeting breakeven by 2029" for the EV business. Everything that does not fit that plan is being cancelled — see Section 06.

Honest framing: at a −72% FY2025 margin, shrinking Model e is worth more to consolidated EBIT than growing it. That is an unusual thing to be able to say about a growth segment, and it is why the retreat has been received as good news.

Ford Credit originates retail loans and leases for Ford buyers and floorplan financing for Ford dealers. It is a bank in all but name, and it is doing well: $2.6B of FY2025 pre-tax earnings, $783M in Q1 2026, $757M in Q2 and $803M in Q3 (up 27% from $631M), the last helped by favourable derivative mark-to-market, healthy financing margins and supportive used-vehicle prices. Ford guides it to about $2.8B of pre-tax profit for 2026, and it paid $2.625B of distributions to the parent in the first nine months. At September 30, 2026 (Q3 10-Q) the book held $82.9B of consumer finance receivables and $24.0B of dealer financing, with 1.4% of the consumer portfolio more than 30 days past due. The allowance for credit losses was $984M (Ford Credit's Q3 release), about 0.9% of the $105.9B of net Ford Credit finance receivables on Ford's balance sheet — a portfolio performing normally, worth monitoring in a consumer-credit downturn but not currently a stress signal. The provision for credit losses did rise, to $173M in Q3 from $135M a year earlier.

Why this matters for the ratios: a lender funds its receivables with debt. Ford's consolidated debt-to-equity of 4.42× on $163.08B of total debt at September 30, 2026 is dominated by Ford Credit's funding, not by industrial borrowings — see Section 04.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
F-Series truck franchise Ford Pro fleet lock-in ~1.6M paid software subs Dealer & service network Captive finance (Ford Credit) 21% imported vs GM 49% 123-year brand

Ford's real moat is not the brand and it is not the truck — it is the switching cost embedded in a commercial fleet. A consumer chooses a vehicle every five to seven years and can be bought away with an incentive. A utility company that has standardised 4,000 Transits on one telematics platform, one upfit spec, one parts inventory and one service relationship faces a genuine cost to change, and that cost is why Ford Pro earns a 10.3% margin selling essentially the same steel that Ford Blue sells at 3.0%. The software subscriptions — up ~50% year over year to roughly 1.6 million at Q2 2026 — deepen that lock-in each quarter, and unlike vehicle revenue they recur. Ford Credit widens the moat again: it lets Ford move metal in a tight credit market when a captive-less rival cannot, and it earns $2.6B a year for the privilege. What Ford has not demonstrated is any moat at all in electric vehicles, where a −72.1% segment margin says it has to accept prices set by manufacturers with a structural cost advantage.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Warranty and field service actions — the persistent, Ford-specific drag. The Q3 2026 10-Q (September 30, 2026) carries a recorded warranty accrual of $17.699 billion, up from $17.190 billion at the end of 2025, plus an estimate that reasonably possible additional field-service-action costs could run a further $1.8 billion beyond what is booked. To put $17.7B in scale: it is roughly 36% of Ford's entire market capitalisation at the October 30 close, and roughly two and a half times FY2025 adjusted EBIT of $6.8B. This is the single clearest reason Ford Blue earns 3.0% while a peer group earns more — a recall is paid out of the same gross margin the vehicle earned on sale, years after the revenue was booked. The trend is finally the right way: management stated on the Q2 2026 call that recalls are down about 40% year over year, and the FY2026 guidance explicitly assumes $1 billion of combined material and warranty cost reduction. On the Q3 call CFO Sherry House said Ford is "firmly on plan towards reducing these costs by $1 billion this year, excluding Novelis and tariffs." That $1B is not a rounding item — it is about 9% of the midpoint of the $10.5–11B EBIT guide. If it does not arrive, the guide does not hold.
02
Tariffs on imported vehicles, parts and metals. With its Q1 2025 results in May 2025, Ford estimated the 2025 hit at a gross cost of about $2.5 billion and, after mitigation, a net adverse adjusted EBIT impact of about $1.5 billion, and withdrew its 2025 guidance. For 2026 the picture is messier and better: Ford projects roughly $1 billion of adverse tariff impact for the full year, excluding a $1.3 billion one-time IEEPA tariff benefit booked in Q1 2026 and excluding temporary Novelis-related costs. The Q2 2026 10-Q adds that Ford expects approximately $3 billion of federal and supplier tariff reimbursements, of which about $500 million is anticipated in H2 2026; in Q3 Ford said lower tariffs helped Ford Pro's results. Netting those, tariffs are currently a cash tailwind and a policy risk — the reimbursements are recoveries of amounts already paid, and the underlying rate structure can change with a signature.
03
The EV write-down cycle. Ford recognised $17.356 billion of pre-tax special items in FY2025 alone — including roughly $10.7B of Model e asset impairment and EV programme cancellations, ~$3.2B on the BlueOval SK joint-venture disposition, ~$1.2B on cancelling an all-electric three-row SUV and ~$0.7B of European restructuring — then a further $4.179 billion of special charges in Q2 2026 and a much smaller net $127 million in Q3. At the Q2 print management flagged up to $2 billion of additional pre-tax charges still to come. Every one of these is excluded from adjusted EBIT. An investor paying 6.41× this year's expected earnings is paying for the adjusted number while the GAAP number was negative in two of the last five fiscal years (FY2022 and FY2025).
04
Single-supplier concentration. A single aluminium supplier — Novelis — constrained Ford Pro production enough to knock segment margin from 12.3% to 9.7% in Q2 2026 and to be named as the cause; on the Q3 call Sherry House said "we have incurred about $1 billion in Novelis-related temporary costs year-to-date". Then, per Ford's October 2 sales release, "At the end of September, production of F-150 trucks was affected by a short-term supplier issue unrelated to aluminum", which Ford said was containable within its full-year guidance. For a company whose best business is aluminium-intensive trucks and vans, that is a live structural exposure, not a one-off.

Notable one-off items — and they are not small. Ford's GAAP figures for the last four reported quarters are dominated by special items, and reading the TTM numbers without adjusting for them will mislead. FY2025 carried $17.356B of pre-tax special items: approximately $10.7B of Model e asset impairment and EV programme cancellations, ~$3.2B on the BlueOval SK joint-venture disposition, ~$1.2B on cancelling the all-electric three-row SUV, and ~$0.7B of European restructuring. That produced a Q4 2025 net loss of $11.064B against FY2025 adjusted EBIT of $6.8B. Q2 2026 then carried $4.179B of special charges — principally a $3.6B charge on the BlueOval SK disposition plus about $0.5B for EV programmes cancelled in December 2025 — turning a $2.5B adjusted EBIT quarter into a $1.327B net loss. At the Q2 print management signalled up to $2B more to come. Q1 2026 ran the other way, benefiting from a $1.3B one-time IEEPA tariff benefit. Q3 2026 was nearly clean: net special items of $127M, a $0.3B pension remeasurement gain against EV programme cancellations, a $0.2B legal matter and European restructuring, so GAAP net income of $2.0B sat close to adjusted results. The honest summary: neither the trailing loss nor the adjusted profit is the right number on its own, and nine-month 2026 net income of $3.27B (against $2.9B a year earlier) is probably the cleanest recent read.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 month-end close of $17.08 to the October 30, 2026 close of $12.25, Ford's share price fell 28.3%. Ford paid $3.83 a share of dividends across the window (twenty ex-dates in Yahoo Finance's history, including specials of $0.65 in 2023, $0.18 in 2024 and $0.15 in 2025); adding them back without reinvesting, a holder is still down about 6%. Five years of negative return, in a period when the company grew revenue every single year. That is the whole indictment of the business model: revenue grew from $136.3B in FY2021 to $187.3B in FY2025, up 37%, and shareholders got the dividends and a lower share price. The path was violent — a peak monthly close of $20.77 in December 2021, a trough of $9.55 in February 2025 (−54.0% from the peak), a run to $17.44 in May 2026, and a close of $12.25 now, 28.3% above the trough. Over the same dates (October 29, 2021 to October 30, 2026) SPY rose 64.6% and QQQ 78.8% on price: severe underperformance. Within the peer group, year to date (December 31, 2025 close to October 30, 2026): Ford −6.6%, GM +7.5%, Toyota's US shares −8.6%, Tesla −11.1%, Rivian −38.3% and Stellantis −57.4%. Ford trails GM in 2026, is ahead of the rest of this group, and has lost the market race over five years.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Read Ford as a sum of parts, because that is how it reports. FY2025: Ford Pro earned $6.8B of EBIT on $66.3B of revenue (10.3%). Ford Blue earned $3.0B on $101.0B (3.0%). Ford Credit earned $2.6B of pre-tax profit. Model e lost $4.8B on $6.7B (−72.1%). Add the three profitable pieces and you get $12.4B of pre-tax earnings power; subtract Model e's loss and roughly $0.8B of corporate and other costs and you arrive at the $6.8B of adjusted EBIT the company actually reported. The entire equity is capitalised at $48.92B at the October 30, 2026 close. Ford Pro alone, valued on any reasonable multiple for a 10%-margin commercial-vehicle-plus-software business, plausibly accounts for most of that — which is the sum-of-parts argument in one line, and the reason the segment split matters more than any headline number.

The Model e number is the fulcrum. A $4.8B annual loss inside a company whose whole market capitalisation is $48.9B is not a rounding error — it is about 70% of Ford Pro's entire FY2025 EBIT of $6.8B. Management has guided the loss to ~$4B in 2026, and Q3 was smaller than a year earlier again (−$1.058B, 25% better, on revenue down 31%). The mechanism is straightforward and slightly uncomfortable: Ford is losing less money on EVs by selling far fewer of them. Wholesales fell 31% year over year to 34,000 units in the quarter, and the margin on those it sold got worse, not better. That works arithmetically — a business losing this much per vehicle does destroy less value at lower volume — but it is a retreat, not a fix, and the actual fix, the Universal EV platform at roughly $30,000, is due to launch in the fourth quarter of 2027, with EV breakeven targeted for 2029. Between now and then, the EV business is a shrinking loss and a series of restructuring charges of up to $2B more.

Warranty is the main drag on Blue's margin. The Q3 2026 10-Q shows a booked warranty accrual of $17.699B at September 30, with a further $1.8B of reasonably possible additional field-service-action cost above that. Ford has spent several years as an outlier on recall volume, and the cost of a field service action is charged against the gross margin of vehicles sold years earlier — which is precisely why a 7.39% consolidated gross margin looks the way it does. The improvement is real and recent: recalls down about 40% year over year per the Q2 call, and $1B of material and warranty cost reduction assumed inside the FY2026 guide. That $1B is about 9% of the EBIT guidance midpoint, and on the Q3 call the CFO said Ford is on plan for it. If Ford delivers it, the $10.5–11B guide holds and the forward P/E of 6.41× is defensible. If it slips, the guide slips with it. There is no other single line item in this company where a billion dollars turns so directly on execution rather than on the economy.

The leverage ratio is not what it looks like. Ford's consolidated debt-to-equity of 4.42× on $163.08B of total debt at September 30, 2026 would be alarming for a manufacturer. It is not a manufacturer's ratio. Ford Credit held $82.9B of consumer finance receivables and $24.0B of dealer financing at September 30, 2026 — assets that a captive lender funds with borrowings by construction, exactly as a bank does. Printing 4.42× as "Ford's leverage" conflates a loan book with a truck plant. The relevant solvency facts are the $46.4B of total liquidity and $25.3B of cash Ford reported at September 30, 2026, and the $4.0B minimum-liquidity covenant attached to the assumed DOE loan. Ford Credit's own credit quality — a $984M allowance and 1.4% of the consumer book more than 30 days past due, both at September 30 — is the number to watch if the consumer weakens, not the consolidated ratio.

The dividend, and whether it is covered. This is the reason many shareholders own it. The regular dividend is $0.60 a year, yielding 4.90% at the $12.25 close, with a $0.15 Q4 2026 dividend declared on October 28, payable December 1. On ~3.99B shares the annual cost is approximately $2.4B. Test it three ways. (i) Against TTM free cash flow of $5.15B — covered 2.1×. (ii) Against Ford's own FY2026 adjusted free cash flow guidance of $6–7B — covered 2.5–2.9×. (iii) Against FY2025 adjusted free cash flow of $3.5B, the worst recent year — covered 1.5×. Against GAAP earnings it is not covered at all, because there were none. The conclusion: the dividend is comfortably covered by cash generation in a normal year and thinly covered in a bad one, and it is cash flow rather than accounting profit that pays it. The genuine risk is not this year's coverage; it is that Ford must simultaneously fund $9.5–10.5B of annual capex, a still-loss-making EV programme and up to $2B of restructuring charges. In the 2020 downturn Ford suspended the dividend outright. Ford paid nothing between early 2020 and late 2021. Investors should own this yield knowing it is paid by a company whose profits rise and fall with the economy, on a stock with a 1.94 beta, not by a utility.

Bull Case

You are paying $48.9B for a company containing a Ford Pro that earned $6.8B of EBIT at a 10.3% margin in FY2025 with software subscriptions up ~50% year over year, a Ford Credit earning about $2.8B pre-tax this year with a clean book, and a Ford Blue whose nine-month EBIT of $4.47B is nearly double last year's on price and mix while volumes fell. The $4.8B annual EV loss — the thing that was destroying all of it — is being deliberately shut down, guided to ~$4B in 2026 and 25% smaller in Q3 than a year earlier. Guidance went up at the Q2 print and its low end went up again at Q3, to $10.5–11B of adjusted EBIT with $6–7B of adjusted FCF. Recalls were down ~40% at the Q2 call, tariffs are a net help in 2026 on ~$3B of expected reimbursements, and you are collecting 4.90% at 6.41× this year's expected earnings while you wait.

Bear Case

Ford has produced a GAAP loss in two of the last five years, lost $8.2B in FY2025, and has taken $17.4B of special items in one year with up to $2B more flagged. The entire investment case rests on adjusted numbers that exclude those charges. Gross margin is 7.39%, ROIC is 0.98% — below any cost of capital — and capex runs $9.5–10.5B a year against $6.8B of FY2025 adjusted EBIT. A $17.7B warranty accrual and $1.8B of possible additional field-action cost sit on the balance sheet. Model e's improvement comes from selling fewer EVs (Q3 wholesales −31%) while losing more per dollar of sales, not from fixing the economics, and the replacement product launches in late 2027 into a market where Chinese manufacturers set the global cost floor. Costs are now rising: the CFO put 2026 commodity headwinds near $2.5B, the CEO expects about $2B more from commodities, memory chips and logistics in 2027 and targets 2027 profit roughly flat. Over five years the share price fell 28% while revenue grew 37%.

Top 3 Risks

01
The $1B warranty-and-material saving does not land. It is about 9% of the FY2026 adjusted EBIT guidance midpoint and it is an execution assumption, not a market assumption. A $17.699B accrual with $1.8B of reasonably possible additional exposure is the balance-sheet evidence that this problem has been persistent and expensive; one strong quarter of recall data does not retire it.
02
Chinese manufacturers set the cost floor Ford must eventually meet. Ford's answer to affordable electrification is a ~$30,000 vehicle on the Universal EV platform delivering in 2027. Chinese OEMs are already producing at cost structures Western automakers have not matched, and trade barriers currently keep most of that competition out of the US — a policy protection, not a moat. The structural point stands on Ford's own −72.1% FY2025 EV margin.
03
Cyclical exposure with a 1.94 beta and a leveraged captive. Ford is a consumer-discretionary manufacturer selling $50,000+ financed purchases. A consumer recession hits three ways at once: unit volumes fall, mix and pricing (the entire source of Blue's Q2 margin gain) reverse, and Ford Credit's $82.9B consumer book — with a $984M allowance and 1.4% more than 30 days past due at September 30 — deteriorates. The dividend was suspended in 2020; that precedent exists.
  Industry Commentary
The Western EV Retreat Is Now Industry-Wide
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Ford's FY2025 disclosure is the clearest quantification of a problem every legacy Western automaker shares: $4.8B of EBIT lost on $6.7B of EV revenue, followed by roughly $10.7B of Model e impairments and programme cancellations, a $1.2B three-row electric SUV cancellation, the exit from the BlueOval SK battery joint venture, the cancellation of three further EVs and the end of F-150 Lightning production. Ford's own EV strategy is now to build fewer of them until a low-cost platform arrives in 2027. The battery-plant buildout that was supposed to be the moat has instead become a $3.8B DOE loan on Ford's balance sheet with a $4.0B liquidity covenant attached. Stellantis, with a $17.63B net loss over the last twelve months (stockanalysis.com), is down 57.4% this year to the October 30 close.
Tariffs Have Become a Competitive Variable, Not Just a Cost
ONGOING HEADWIND
The 25% duties on imported vehicles and parts, plus steel and aluminium tariffs, led Ford in May 2025 to estimate a ~$2.5B gross / ~$1.5B net adverse EBIT impact for that year and to withdraw its guidance. In 2026 the same policy is running the other way: roughly $1B of adverse impact, offset by a $1.3B one-time IEEPA benefit in Q1 and approximately $3B of expected federal and supplier reimbursements. The strategic asymmetry matters more than the annual number: Ford imports roughly 21% of the vehicles it sells in the US against 49% for GM (Bank of America analysts, March 2025), so a tariff regime that damages the whole sector damages Ford's closest competitor more than twice as much. Management has publicly pressed for USMCA terms that improve competitiveness against Japanese and Korean imports. The obvious hazard: a benefit that arrived by executive action can depart the same way, and Ford's aluminium exposure at Novelis has cost about $1B of temporary costs in the first nine months of 2026, per the CFO on the Q3 call.
Commercial Vehicles and Attached Software Are the Real Margin Pool
EMERGING TAILWIND
The most under-discussed structural shift in the sector is that fleet customers, not retail buyers, are where durable automotive margin now lives — and that the margin comes increasingly from what is attached to the vehicle rather than the vehicle itself. Ford Pro's 10.3% FY2025 EBIT margin against Ford Blue's 3.0% is the cleanest available evidence, and Ford's ~1.6M paid software subscriptions, up ~50% year over year at Q2 2026, are recurring revenue growing at software rates inside a company valued at 0.26× its annual sales. Bull framing: if the market ever values Ford Pro on its own economics rather than blending it into a $49B automaker with a 7% gross margin, the sum-of-parts gap closes and the 6.41× forward P/E looks like a mispricing. Bear framing: Ford does not sell Ford Pro separately, it will not, and until it does, Pro's cash is what funds Model e's losses and Blue's warranty bill. You do not get to own the good business on its own — that is precisely why it is available at this price.
