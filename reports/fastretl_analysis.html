01 Company Overview

Fast Retailing is the holding company behind UNIQLO, the basics-and-essentials clothing chain it calls "LifeWear", plus the lower-priced fashion chain GU and a small portfolio of other labels (Theory, PLST, Comptoir des Cotonniers, Princesse tam.tam). It designs its own products, has them made by partner factories and sells them in its own stores and online — 3,510 stores at August 31, 2026. In the year to August 31, 2026 (FY2026) revenue was ¥3.9633 trillion, up 16.6%, business profit was ¥718.4 billion (+30.4%) and profit attributable to shareholders ¥542.5 billion (+25.3%), or EPS of ¥1,768.08. A year earlier (FY2025) revenue was ¥3.4005 trillion, business profit ¥551.2 billion and EPS ¥1,411.44.

The company is no longer mainly a Japanese retailer. In FY2026 UNIQLO International produced 60.8% of group revenue and grew it 26.2% (about 18% in local currencies), against 5.7% at UNIQLO Japan. Group business profit rose 30.4% to ¥718.4 billion, the fifth record year in a row, and the company said North America and Europe together now sell more than Greater China does. The results came out after the Tokyo close on October 8, and three things in them changed the picture. First, the dividend: the FY2026 payout was lifted to ¥850 a share from the ¥640 forecast in July, and ¥900 is forecast for FY2027; the CFO said the company can keep paying out roughly 50% of profit, against 35.4% a year earlier. Second, the outlook: for FY2027 it forecasts revenue of ¥4.45 trillion (+12.3%) and business profit of ¥830.0 billion (+15.5%), but profit attributable to shareholders of ¥560.0 billion, up only 3.2%; the plan has finance income of ¥50.0 billion (¥59.3 billion in FY2026), no currency gains or other income (¥24.6 billion in FY2026) and a tax rate in line with recent years (FY2026's was lower than usual). Third, the soft spots: in the June–August quarter UNIQLO Japan's same-store sales fell 4.0%, warm weather held back Europe, and GU slipped to a small business loss. The stock fell 4.0% on the first session after the release (¥74,800 to ¥71,780), then recovered to ¥76,400 by October 15. It rose 45.7% in the year to October 15, 2026 and trades at 43.2 times trailing earnings, a growth-company multiple for a clothing chain, and 13.1% below its 52-week closing high of ¥87,900 on July 7. The question for the next year is whether overseas growth can keep outrunning the cost side: the company's own plan has gross margin slipping in Japan, at GU, in South Korea and Southeast Asia, and in Europe, on a weaker yen and other currencies and higher freight costs.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Own-label design-to-store control Core basics that sell every season Global store estate (3,510) Flagship-led brand building Forward-hedged dollar sourcing Net cash balance sheet Yanai stake (13.77%)

The moat is scale in basics. UNIQLO's range is built on core everyday items — jeans, easy pants, T-shirts, knitwear, parkas — that the company updates with new materials and silhouettes rather than replaces. In FY2026 the company credited its margin gains to better control of discounting (in Europe, "controlled discounting") and to spreading fixed costs over strong sales. It buys in US dollars in every market and uses forward currency contracts to smooth sudden moves, and it pays for growth from cash: FY2026 operating cash flow was ¥788.6 billion against ¥79.9 billion spent on property, plant and equipment and ¥25.0 billion on intangible assets. The flagship stores are the marketing — Shanghai reopens on October 30 and Kyoto opens on November 6 — and the company's partnership with the Los Angeles Dodgers includes an education programme at the stadium field that carries the UNIQLO name. The weakness is that a business built on everyday basics has limited pricing power — when its costs rise, it has to choose between margin and its price promise.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The weak yen raises the cost of goods. Products are bought in dollars; the yen moved from ¥146.9 to ¥160.0 per dollar between September 1, 2025 and August 31, 2026, per the company. Hedges delay the hit, they do not cancel it. The FY2027 plan now shows it: the company expects a slightly lower gross margin at UNIQLO Japan and GU (it cites weaker yen rates on its forward contracts), in South Korea and Southeast Asia (weaker local currencies against the dollar) and in Europe (higher transport costs), and a roughly steady one in North America. Its group forecast still assumes an average of ¥156.2 per dollar, below the August 31 rate. At the July analyst meeting the CFO said: "I think the gross profit margin will inevitably be under pressure in FY2027 so we must consider how we can control and alleviate that pressure."
02
Japan is a weather-and-tourist business. June same-store sales fell 14.1% and the June–August quarter −4.0%, which the company put down to lower temperatures and typhoons; for FY2027 it plans same-store sales up only about 2% and flat business profit. About 11% of FY2026 sales were to overseas visitors, a share that can shrink as quickly as it grew.
03
Greater China is still being rebuilt, and GU has slipped. The largest overseas region grew revenue 11.3% in yen but only about 3% in local currency in Mainland China, where the store count fell from 902 to 851 during FY2026. GU, meanwhile, lost money in the June–August quarter, and the company said its sales were still below plan at the end of September.
04
The valuation leaves little room. At 43.2 times trailing earnings and a 1.11% dividend yield, the price assumes years of double-digit profit growth, while the company's own FY2027 forecast has profit attributable to shareholders up 3.2%. The stock fell 4.0% on the first session after a record year, a bigger dividend and a business profit forecast up 15.5%.

The fourth quarter against July's forecast, and what FY2027 assumes

In July the company forecast FY2026 revenue of ¥3,970.0B, business profit of ¥710.0B and profit attributable of ¥500.0B. The actuals were ¥3,963.3B (−0.2%), ¥718.4B (+1.2%) and ¥542.5B (+8.5%). By our arithmetic from the nine-month and full-year figures, the June–August quarter was about ¥898B of revenue (+14.6% YoY), ¥125.8B of business profit and ¥116.4B of profit attributable (+24% from ¥93.9B), against the ¥904.8B, ¥117.3B and ¥73.9B that the July forecast implied. Most of the profit gap sits below business profit: full-year net finance income came in at ¥59.3B against a forecast ¥50.0B, other income and expenses at +¥24.6B against ¥20.0B, and the company said the tax rate was lower than usual.

For FY2027 the company forecasts revenue of ¥4.45T (+12.3%), business profit of ¥830.0B (+15.5%), operating profit of ¥830.0B (+11.7%), profit before tax of ¥880.0B (+9.7%) and profit attributable of ¥560.0B (+3.2%), or EPS of ¥1,825.06. The plan books no other income or currency gains (FY2026: ¥24.6B), ¥50.0B of net finance income (FY2026: ¥59.3B) and a tax rate in line with recent years, and it assumes exchange rates of ¥156.2 per dollar and ¥181.8 per euro. CFO Okazaki said the 12.3% revenue growth "is not a conservative estimate, but I think it is achievable if we keep pursuing our current policy." 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

UNIQLO has turned from a Japanese chain into a global brand that is still growing fast: International revenue grew 26.2% in FY2026, the company says North America and Europe together now sell more than Greater China, and the group earned ¥683.7B of free cash flow on a ¥23.4T market value with ¥2.14T of cash and short-term investments. Management is now paying out about half its profit — ¥850 a share for FY2026 and ¥900 forecast for FY2027 — while still planning ¥133.4B of capital spending in FY2027, and forecasts business profit up 15.5%. The CFO said the 12.3% revenue forecast "is not a conservative estimate, but I think it is achievable if we keep pursuing our current policy", and the July forecast was beaten on profit. September's +10.8% Japan same-store sales followed a June slump the company put down to cool weather and typhoons.

Bear Case

At 43.2x trailing earnings the stock is priced for perfection, and the company's own plan has profit attributable to shareholders up just 3.2% in FY2027 — EPS of ¥1,825.06 against ¥1,768.08 — with gross margin slipping in Japan, at GU, in South Korea and Southeast Asia and in Europe. Some of the past year's growth was currency translation rather than demand (about 4.4 points of FY2026 revenue growth), UNIQLO Japan's June–August same-store sales fell 4.0%, Europe was held back by warm weather, GU lost money in the quarter and was still below plan at the end of September, and Greater China is still in reform. When July's results ran ahead of the company's own forecast and the forecast was raised, the stock fell 3.6% the next session and then had three down months; after the October results it fell 4.0% on the first session. A multiple this high does not need bad news to fall — just growth that slows from the 20s to the low teens.

Top 3 Risks

01
Gross-margin squeeze from the yen. Goods are bought in dollars and hedged forward, so a yen that moved from ¥146.9 to ¥160.0 per dollar during FY2026 reaches the income statement over the following seasons. The FY2027 plan already has gross margin slipping at UNIQLO Japan and GU, and the company's group forecast assumes an average of ¥156.2 per dollar, below the August 31 rate of ¥160.0.
02
Valuation compression. A 43x multiple on a business whose COO targets a 15–20% margin depends on revenue growth staying in double digits and on profit following it; the FY2027 forecast has business profit up 15.5% but profit attributable up 3.2%. A year of single-digit growth could shrink the price investors are willing to pay for each yen of profit, even with record profits.
03
Execution at speed overseas. Chairman Yanai said in the Q&A, of Southeast Asia: "You can open a store straight away, but it takes years to nurture the right personnel." The company plans 175 openings and 140 closures in FY2027, a net 35 stores, including 30 openings in Southeast Asia, India and Australia, 20 in North America and 15 in Europe.
Industry Commentary
The weak yen: a translation gain and a cost problem
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For a Japanese company that sells mostly abroad, a weaker yen inflates reported revenue and profit when foreign sales are translated back into yen — currency added about 4.4 points to Fast Retailing's FY2026 revenue growth, per the company's currency-effect table. But UNIQLO buys its goods in dollars in every market, so a weaker yen also raises the cost of what it sells in Japan, and a weaker local currency does the same anywhere else. The company assumes ¥156.2 per dollar for FY2027, but FY2026 ended at ¥160.0. In July the CFO said: "I think currency depreciation is a bigger factor in terms of its potential impact on gross profit margins." Forward contracts buy time; they do not change the destination.

Weather, tourists and the Japanese consumer
ONGOING HEADWIND

Seasonal clothing sells when the season arrives. UNIQLO Japan's monthly same-store sales swung from +10.1% in May to −14.1% in June and +10.8% in September, almost entirely on temperature, by the company's own account. Visitors from abroad made up about 11% of FY2026 sales — a tailwind that reverses if the yen strengthens or travel slows. The home market is mature: same-store sales rose 5.1% for the full year to August 2026, with customer numbers up 0.2% and spending per customer up 4.8%, and the company plans about 2% for FY2027.

LifeWear against the listed peers
EMERGING TAILWIND

Fast Retailing describes UNIQLO's LifeWear as "the ultimate in everyday clothing": core items such as jeans, sweatshirts and easy pants, updated each season with new materials and silhouettes, while GU carries the more trend-led ranges. Over the past year the market rewarded that far more than the listed European chains: Fast Retailing's shares rose 45.7% while Inditex (Zara) rose 15.7%, Next 18.3% and H&M fell 1.6% (price only, each in its own currency). The bull reading is that a range built on core items, sold with controlled discounting, travels well into new countries. The bear reading is that the share price has run ahead of profit: the stock rose 45.7% in a year in which EPS grew 25.3%, leaving it at 43.2x trailing earnings. UNIQLO's own ranges also borrow from trends, such as Barrel Pants.
