01 Company Overview

Freeport-McMoRan is one of the world's largest publicly traded copper producers, operating large-scale, long-lived mines across North America (Morenci, Bagdad, Safford, Sierrita and Miami in Arizona; Chino and Tyrone in New Mexico; Henderson and Climax molybdenum in Colorado), South America (Cerro Verde in Peru, El Abra in Chile) and Indonesia, where its 48.76%-owned subsidiary PT Freeport Indonesia operates the Grasberg minerals district — one of the world's largest copper and gold deposits by recoverable reserves. FY2025 revenue was $25.92B, up 1.8% YoY, on consolidated sales of 3.574 billion pounds of copper, 1.066 million ounces of gold and 83 million pounds of molybdenum. The company ranked third globally in attributable copper production in 2025 (~1.08 million tonnes) behind BHP and Chile's state-owned Codelco, and ranks #125 in the S&P 500 at a $95.6B market capitalisation.

Two things drive the investment case right now. First, copper is near its highs: front-month COMEX copper futures closed at $6.80/lb on September 9, 2026, their highest close in two years, and closed at $6.64/lb on October 28. Freeport realised an average $6.54/lb in Q3 2026, against $4.75/lb for FY2025. Second, a fatal mud-rush incident at the Grasberg Block Cave on September 8, 2025 forced a force-majeure declaration (a legal notice suspending delivery obligations) and gutted Indonesian volumes. The restart is on the company's schedule: Grasberg's mills ran at about 67% of capacity in Q3 2026, and management expects about 80% by mid-2027 and close to full capacity by end-2027. The price of the outage shows in the guidance: 2026 copper sales were guided at 3.4 billion pounds in January, about 3.1 billion in July and about 3.0 billion in October, with gold at about 649 thousand ounces. On the Q3 call management pointed to roughly 3.7 billion pounds in 2027 and more than 4 billion in 2028. The stock is up 61.8% over 52 weeks (from $41.17 on October 28, 2025) because the copper price and the restart path have more than made up for the lost volume.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
GRASBERG ORE BODY NEGATIVE-COST GOLD CREDITS LIFE-OF-RESOURCE IUPK LARGEST U.S. COPPER PRODUCER DECADES-LONG RESERVE LIFE LEACHING TECHNOLOGY EDGE INTEGRATED SMELTING

Freeport's moat is geological before it is operational. Tier-one copper deposits cannot be manufactured, permitted quickly, or replicated: a new greenfield copper mine typically takes 15–20 years from discovery to first production, and global average ore grades have been falling for decades. Grasberg is the extreme version of this — a copper-gold system so rich in precious-metal by-products that PT-FI's unit net cash cost was negative $3.53 per pound in Q1 2026. When by-products pay for the mine, the asset generates cash across essentially the entire copper cycle. The February 2026 life-of-resource IUPK agreement would remove the single largest overhang on that asset by pushing operating rights past the prior 2041 expiry, at the price of FCX's stake stepping down from 48.76% to about 37% from 2042; PT-FI applied for the amended licence in June 2026 and is still waiting for it. Layered on top: an irreplaceable U.S. footprint at a moment when Washington treats copper as strategic, and a proprietary leaching programme now adding about 220 million pounds a year of copper from already-mined material, targeted at 300 million in 2027, at minimal capital cost — economics no greenfield project can match.

Single-asset and single-country concentration. Grasberg is simultaneously the moat and the vulnerability. The September 2025 mud rush removed a large share of the company's highest-margin volume in one event, has cut 2026 copper sales guidance from 3.4B to about 3.0B pounds and gold from 800koz to about 649koz, and cost $934M of idle-facility and restoration spending in the first nine months of 2026 alone. Indonesia also imposes export duties, domestic-smelting rules and a schedule for handing equity to the state; policy there is negotiated, not contractual in the Western sense. PT-FI is now seeking clarity on exporting refined gold, where current regulations set a 10% export duty that conflicts with the terms of its licence.

Pure commodity price-taking. Freeport has no pricing power whatsoever. Its realised copper price went from $4.75/lb (FY2025) to $6.54/lb (Q3 2026) and the earnings picture changed with it — adjusted EPS rose from $0.50 in Q3 2025 to $0.88 in Q3 2026 even though copper sales fell 23% (977 to 749 million pounds). The same leverage runs in reverse: at about 3 billion pounds a year, every $0.10/lb move in copper is worth roughly $300 million of annual revenue ($0.10 × 3.0 billion lb, our arithmetic).

Cost inflation and grade decline. North American unit net cash costs of $3.16/lb in Q3 2026 are the highest in the portfolio, against $2.39 in South America and a net credit in Indonesia. Consolidated unit costs are guided at about $1.90/lb for 2026, and the Q3 figure of $2.07/lb came in 4% above the July estimate of $2.00/lb. On the call management put the 2026 rise in energy costs at about $500M, mostly diesel, and said sulfuric acid prices have more than doubled this year. If copper falls back while costs stay sticky, margins shrink fast.

Policy and tariff dependence. Since August 1, 2025 the Section 232 regime has charged a 50% duty on semi-finished copper products but not on refined cathode; the Commerce Secretary recommended a phased tariff on refined copper of 15% from 2027 and 30% from 2028, and the July 2025 proclamation left that decision to the President after a June 30, 2026 update. When reports on September 10, 2026 said the White House had not yet decided, FCX fell 6.6% in a day. Any change, in either direction, moves the gap between U.S. and London copper prices and Freeport's U.S. realised price without Freeport doing anything.

04 Key Financial Metrics

Read this table with the cycle in mind. Freeport is a commodity cyclical: reported earnings are a function of the copper price, not of management execution, and the classic trap is that a miner looks expensive on trailing P/E at the bottom of the cycle (depressed earnings) and cheap at the top (peak earnings). FCX's 27.98x trailing P/E ($66.60 ÷ $2.38) is on TTM earnings that still carry Grasberg idle-facility charges and only part of the benefit of copper above $6/lb. Both forward numbers assume today's copper price holds and the Grasberg ramp lands on schedule. On EV/EBITDA the stock trades at about 10.2x (stockanalysis enterprise value $101.66B ÷ TTM EBITDA $9.96B). In short: today's price already assumes copper stays near its highs and the recovery keeps to plan.

Notable One-Off Items

FCX's reported results carry unusually large non-recurring distortions right now, and GAAP earnings materially understate underlying economics:

Grasberg incident charges. Idle-facility, direct recovery and restoration costs were $244M in Q3 2026 and $934M in the first nine months, against $171M in each period of 2025. Q3's adjusting items came to $101M after tax ($0.07 a share), the gap between GAAP EPS of $0.81 and adjusted EPS of $0.88. From 2027 these idle-facility costs will be counted inside unit costs, which the CFO described on the call as mainly an accounting reclassification.

Insurance recovery. The first nine months of 2026 include a $699M pre-tax gain on the PT-FI mud-rush insurance settlement, matched by $699M of proceeds in the cash flow statement. It is genuine cash but a one-time item — do not extrapolate it into run-rate cash flow.

Gold sales timing. About 60,000 ounces of refined gold moved from Q3 into Q4 sales because of PT-FI's sales timing, so Q3 gold sales were 105,000 ounces (336,000 a year earlier) and Q4 is guided at about 300,000. That shift lifted Q3 unit costs and will flatter Q4's (guided at about $1.68/lb).

Smelter restart. PT-FI's own smelter re-commenced operations in late August 2026 and PT Smelting resumed in late 2025, giving combined capacity of up to about 800,000 metric tons of copper cathode a year. Until both run at normal rates, Freeport expects PT-FI's production and sales to diverge more than usual from quarter to quarter.

Section 232 Decision on Refined Copper Tariffs
The 50% duty on semi-finished copper products has applied since August 1, 2025; refined cathode is exempt. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Freeport owns irreplaceable tier-one copper assets into a structural supply deficit: electrification, grid rebuild and data-center power demand are growing copper consumption while global grades decline and new mines take 15–20 years. Grasberg's negative unit net cash cost ($3.53/lb in Q1 2026, before the gold-timing shift) is close to unique, and the February 2026 life-of-resource IUPK agreement, once the amended licence is issued, removes the biggest single overhang on it. The restart is on schedule at 67% of capacity, heading for 80% by mid-2027 and close to full by end-2027, which management says lifts copper sales from about 3.0 billion pounds in 2026 to about 3.7 billion in 2027 and over 4 billion in 2028 — consensus sees EPS going from $2.38 TTM (GAAP) to $3.25 (2026E) and $4.34 (2027E, adjusted). Add a leaching programme aiming for 300 million pounds in 2027, a Bagdad doubling decision due by year-end, a 0.28 debt-to-equity ratio, and the strongest domestic position in a market where Washington may yet tariff refined copper imports.

Bear Case

You are buying a boom-and-bust business that takes whatever price copper fetches, with copper near its highs, at 28.0x trailing earnings and about 10.2x EV/EBITDA. Volumes keep slipping: 2026 copper guidance has gone from 3.4B to about 3.0B pounds and 2027 was trimmed about 2.5% on the Q3 call. Costs are rising too: Q3 unit costs of $2.07/lb ran 4% over the July estimate, energy costs are up about $500M this year, and 2027 capex steps up to about $5.2B before any Bagdad spending. The $699M insurance gain will not repeat. The post-print analyst round leaned to cuts (JPMorgan, UBS, Wells Fargo and Scotiabank lowered targets), the refined-copper tariff the stock would benefit from is undecided, and TTM free cash flow of $1.95B leaves little cushion if copper falls back toward its FY2025 realised level of $4.75/lb.

01
Copper price reversion. Every dollar of Freeport's earnings leverage runs through a price it does not control. Copper at $6.64/lb (COMEX, October 28) versus an FY2025 realised $4.75/lb is doing all the work; a reversion toward mid-cycle would compress EPS faster than the Grasberg ramp can offset, and the current multiple assumes no such reversion.
02
Grasberg execution and further disruption. The ramp schedule runs 14 more months to end-2027, and the same geotechnical conditions that produced the September 2025 mud rush have not gone away. Another incident, or a slip to the 80%-by-mid-2027 milestone, would break the 2027 consensus EPS of $4.34 and re-open the volume hole.
03
Indonesian and Latin American policy risk. PT-FI operates on a negotiated licence in a country that requires domestic smelting, levies export duties (current rules set a 10% duty on refined gold exports that conflicts with its licence terms, and PT-FI is seeking clarity) and has FCX's stake falling to about 37% from 2042; the amended IUPK is still awaiting issue. Peruvian and Chilean tax, royalty, water and community risk compound it. In Q3 2026, 56% of Freeport's copper sales (416 of 749 million pounds) came from outside the United States.
  Industry Commentary
Electrification, Grids and AI Power Demand
EMERGING TAILWIND
Front-month COMEX copper closed at $6.80/lb on September 9, 2026, its highest close in two years, and closed at $6.64/lb on October 28 — 40% above Freeport's FY2025 realised price of $4.75/lb. The demand narrative has broadened beyond electric vehicles and charging infrastructure to transmission and distribution rebuild and, most recently, the electrical infrastructure required by AI data centers. Copper intensity per unit of new electricity capacity is materially higher than in the fossil era. Freeport gives more direct exposure to this than the diversified giants: about 3.0 billion pounds of guided 2026 copper sales, without the iron ore of BHP or the energy and trading business of Glencore.
Mine Supply Constraint and Grade Decline
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The supply side is the tighter half of the equation. Chile, the world's largest producing country, mined 5.3 million tonnes in 2025 (USGS estimate). The top ten producers accounted for roughly 9.4 million tonnes of 2025 attributable output (BHP 1.47Mt, Codelco 1.44Mt, Freeport 1.08Mt, Southern Copper 953kt, Zijin 886kt, Glencore 852kt, CMOC 741kt, Rio Tinto 735kt, Anglo American 695kt, KGHM 592kt) against global mine production of about 23 Mt (USGS estimate). Grasberg's own outage removed a meaningful slice of that in 2025–26 and was itself a driver of the price spike. New greenfield copper takes 15–20 years from discovery to production, so supply cannot respond to price on any investable horizon — which is precisely why Freeport's leaching programme (about 220 million pounds a year at the current run rate, targeting 300 million in 2027, from already-mined material) is strategically valuable out of proportion to its size.
Tariffs, Trade Policy and the COMEX–LME Spread
ONGOING HEADWIND
Section 232 has imposed a 50% duty on semi-finished copper products since August 1, 2025, while refined cathode is exempt; the Commerce Secretary recommended a phased refined-copper tariff of 15% from 2027 and 30% from 2028, leaving the decision to the President. The gap between U.S. (COMEX) and London (LME) copper prices now swings on tariff expectations. Bull implication: Freeport is the largest U.S. producer with domestic rod and refining assets, so a refined-metal tariff would flow almost directly into its realised price with no cost offset. Bear implication: the same policy sensitivity cuts both ways on a headline — on September 10, 2026, reports that no decision had been made coincided with a 4.9% fall in COMEX copper futures, a 6.6% fall in FCX and a 7.2% fall in Southern Copper.
