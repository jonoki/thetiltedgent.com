01 Company Overview

Freeport-McMoRan is one of the world's largest publicly traded copper producers, operating large-scale, long-lived mines across North America (Morenci, Bagdad, Safford, Sierrita and Miami in Arizona; Chino and Tyrone in New Mexico; Henderson and Climax molybdenum in Colorado), South America (Cerro Verde in Peru, El Abra in Chile) and Indonesia, where its 48.76%-owned subsidiary PT Freeport Indonesia operates the Grasberg minerals district — one of the world's largest copper and gold deposits by recoverable reserves. FY2025 revenue was $25.92B, up 1.8% YoY, on consolidated sales of 3.574 billion pounds of copper, 1.066 million ounces of gold and 83 million pounds of molybdenum. The company ranked third globally in attributable copper production in 2025 (~1.08 million tonnes) behind BHP and Chile's state-owned Codelco, and is roughly the 252nd most valuable listed company in the world at a $99.2B market capitalisation.

The investment case is dominated by two things right now. First, copper is at record levels — spot copper printed an all-time high of $6.83/lb in August 2026 and sits at $6.46/lb, against FCX's FY2025 realised price of $4.75/lb. Second, a fatal mud-rush incident at the Grasberg Block Cave on September 8, 2025 forced a force-majeure declaration and gutted Indonesian volumes; the phased restart began in Q2 2026 and management now guides to roughly 65% of capacity in H2 2026, ~80% by mid-2027 and near-full capacity by end-2027. Full-year 2026 copper sales guidance was cut from 3.4 billion pounds (January) to ~3.1 billion pounds (July), and gold from 0.8 million ounces to 650 thousand ounces. The stock has nonetheless risen 65.5% over 52 weeks because the copper price move has more than offset the volume loss.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
GRASBERG ORE BODY NEGATIVE-COST GOLD CREDITS LIFE-OF-RESOURCE IUPK LARGEST U.S. COPPER PRODUCER DECADES-LONG RESERVE LIFE LEACHING TECHNOLOGY EDGE INTEGRATED SMELTING

Freeport's moat is geological before it is operational. Tier-one copper deposits cannot be manufactured, permitted quickly, or replicated: a new greenfield copper mine typically takes 15–20 years from discovery to first production, and global average ore grades have been falling for decades. Grasberg is the extreme version of this — a copper-gold system so rich in precious-metal by-products that PT-FI's unit net cash cost was negative $3.53 per pound in Q1 2026. When by-products pay for the mine, the asset generates cash across essentially the entire copper cycle, which is the definition of a low-cost-curve position. The February 2026 life-of-resource IUPK extension removes the single largest overhang on that asset by pushing operating rights past the prior 2041 expiry, at the price of FCX's stake stepping down from 48.76% to roughly 37% after 2041 (with government reimbursement of pro-rata costs at book value for post-2041 investments). Layered on top: an irreplaceable U.S. footprint at a moment when Washington treats copper as strategic, and a proprietary leaching programme that is adding 200–300 million pounds a year of copper from already-mined material at minimal capital cost — economics no greenfield project can match.

Single-asset and single-country concentration. Grasberg is simultaneously the moat and the vulnerability. The September 2025 mud rush removed a large share of the company's highest-margin volume in one event, cut FY2026 copper guidance from 3.4B to 3.1B pounds and gold from 800koz to 650koz, and produced $824M of 2025 charges plus $284M more in Q2 2026. Indonesia also imposes export duties, domestic-smelting mandates and a divestment schedule; policy there is negotiated, not contractual in the Western sense.

Pure commodity price-taking. Freeport has no pricing power whatsoever. Realised copper moved from $4.75/lb (FY2025) to $6.17/lb (Q2 2026) and the entire earnings picture changed with it — adjusted EPS went from $0.47 in Q4 2025 to $0.74 in Q2 2026 on lower volumes. The same leverage runs in reverse. Roughly every $0.10/lb change in copper is worth on the order of several hundred million dollars of annualised operating cash flow at ~3.1–3.5B pounds of sales.

Cost inflation and grade decline. North American unit net cash costs of $2.93/lb are far above the Indonesian and South American operations and above the global cost-curve midpoint. Consolidated unit costs are guided at ~$1.90/lb for 2026 versus $1.65/lb actual in 2025 and $1.75/lb originally guided — the deterioration is driven by lost Grasberg by-product credits and idle-facility costs. If copper mean-reverts while costs stay sticky, margin compression is fast.

Policy and tariff dependence. The Section 232 regime currently applies a 50% duty to semi-finished and derivative copper products while deferring tariffs on refined cathode, with a Commerce review contemplating 15% by 2027 and 30% by 2028. Traders price only a 14.6% probability of the 15% refined tariff by January 2027 and 37% for a 30% tariff by January 2028. Any change — in either direction — moves the COMEX–LME spread and the U.S. realised price without Freeport doing anything.

04 Key Financial Metrics

Read this table with the cycle in mind. Freeport is a commodity cyclical: reported earnings are a function of the copper price, not of management execution, and the classic trap is that a miner looks expensive on trailing P/E at the bottom of the cycle (depressed earnings) and cheap at the top (peak earnings). FCX's 33.9x trailing P/E is on TTM earnings that still carry Grasberg outage charges and only partial benefit from record copper. The 20.5x forward multiple and 0.64 PEG reflect consensus EPS of $3.00 for 2026 and $4.12 for 2027. Mid-cycle framing: at ~3.5–4.0 billion pounds of normalised copper sales, a mid-cycle copper price nearer $4.50–$5.00/lb and unit costs around $1.75–$2.00/lb, normalised EPS is plausibly in the $2.00–$2.75 range — implying a mid-cycle multiple in the high-20s at today's price. On EV/EBITDA the stock trades at 11.0x, well above the 5–7x range typically paid for diversified miners at mid-cycle. In short: today's price already embeds both a copper price near all-time highs and a successful Grasberg recovery.

Notable One-Off Items

FCX's reported results carry unusually large non-recurring distortions right now, and GAAP earnings materially understate underlying economics:

Grasberg incident charges. FY2025 absorbed $625M of idle-facility and direct recovery costs in production expense, plus $118M of DD&A and $81M of asset impairments — roughly $824M in total, all excluded from adjusted results. Q2 2026 added a further $284M of restoration cost. These should fade as the block cave ramps, but at 60–65% capacity through 2026 they will not disappear this year.

Insurance recoveries. Freeport carries up to $1.0B of coverage (a $0.7B underground sub-limit after a $0.5B deductible) and collected $700M of insurance settlement proceeds in Q2 2026, recorded as a gain. This is a genuine cash inflow but a one-time item — do not extrapolate it into run-rate cash flow.

Indonesian export duties and tax settlements. Export duties, which had run at $0.26/lb of copper in the prior year, were nil in Q4 2025 as PT-FI's own smelting capacity came online — a structural cost reduction as long as domestic downstreaming holds. Separately, resolution of the Cerro Verde 2020 Peruvian tax audit produced a $54M income-tax benefit, and PT-FI's 2020/2021 Indonesian audits produced net credits (81% attributable to FCX under the shareholder agreement, with $12M of pre-tax credits flowing through Q2 2026).

Smelter start-up costs. The Manyar smelter in Eastern Java produced its first copper cathode in July 2025 and the associated precious-metals refinery started in December 2024; PT Smelting at Gresik was temporarily suspended in Q4 2025 and restarted in late December 2025. Start-up and reduced-rate running costs have weighed on Indonesian unit economics and will persist until concentrate feed normalises in H2 2026 and beyond.

Legacy liabilities. FY2025 also carried $118M of charges for impairments and abandonment obligations on legacy oil and gas properties — small, but a reminder that Freeport still carries non-mining legacy exposure from its 2013–2016 energy detour.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Freeport owns irreplaceable tier-one copper assets into a structural supply deficit: electrification, grid rebuild and data-center power demand are growing copper consumption while global grades decline and new mines take 15–20 years. Grasberg's negative unit net cash cost of $3.53/lb is close to unique, and the February 2026 life-of-resource IUPK extension removed the biggest single overhang on it. The restart to ~80% by mid-2027 and full capacity by end-2027 restores roughly 900 million pounds of the highest-margin copper in the portfolio just as prices sit at records — consensus sees EPS going from $2.04 TTM to $3.00 (2026E) to $4.12 (2027E). Add 300 million pounds a year of near-zero-capital leaching copper, a 0.32 debt-to-equity ratio, and the strongest domestic position in a market where Washington may yet tariff refined copper imports.

Bear Case

You are buying a price-taking cyclical at a 52-week high, 4.4% off the peak, with copper at an all-time record and a 33.9x trailing / 11.0x EV-EBITDA multiple — well above the 5–7x diversified miners typically fetch mid-cycle. The average analyst target of $71.73 implies only 3.8% upside. Volumes are still impaired: FY2026 copper guidance was cut from 3.4B to 3.1B pounds and gold from 800koz to 650koz, unit costs raised from $1.75 to ~$1.90/lb, and the block cave is chute-constrained to ~60–65% until mid-2027. Q2's $700M insurance gain will not repeat. Meanwhile LME inventories are rebuilding, the refined-copper tariff the market is partly hoping for carries only a 14.6% market-implied probability by January 2027, and free cash flow of $1.76B against a $4.3B capex programme leaves little cushion if copper mean-reverts toward $4.50/lb.

01
Copper price reversion. Every dollar of Freeport's earnings leverage runs through a price it does not control. Copper at $6.46/lb versus an FY2025 realised $4.75/lb is doing all the work; a reversion toward mid-cycle would compress EPS faster than the Grasberg ramp can offset, and the current multiple assumes no such reversion.
02
Grasberg execution and further disruption. The block cave restart is chute-constrained, the ramp schedule runs 18 more months, and the same geotechnical conditions that produced the September 2025 mud rush have not gone away. Another incident, or a slip to the 80%-by-mid-2027 milestone, would break the 2027 consensus EPS of $4.12 and re-open the volume hole.
03
Indonesian and Latin American policy risk. PT-FI operates on a negotiated licence in a jurisdiction that mandates domestic smelting, levies export duties (previously $0.26/lb) and has a divestment path taking FCX to ~37% post-2041; the amended IUPK is still pending issuance. Peruvian and Chilean tax, royalty, water and community risk compound it. Roughly two-thirds of Freeport's copper sits outside the United States.
  Industry Commentary
Electrification, Grids and AI Power Demand
EMERGING TAILWIND
Copper reached an all-time high of $6.83/lb in August 2026 and trades at $6.46/lb — versus Freeport's FY2025 realised price of $4.75/lb, a 36% uplift. The demand narrative has broadened beyond electric vehicles and charging infrastructure to transmission and distribution rebuild and, most recently, the electrical infrastructure required by AI data centers. Copper intensity per unit of new electricity capacity is materially higher than in the fossil era. Freeport is the purest large-cap U.S. listed expression of this: 3.1 billion pounds of guided 2026 sales, with essentially no non-copper diversification to dilute the exposure — unlike BHP (iron ore) or Glencore (energy and trading).
Mine Supply Constraint and Grade Decline
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The supply side is the tighter half of the equation. Chile — the world's largest producer — is expected to see output decline again this year on disruptions and falling ore grades. The top ten producers accounted for roughly 9.4 million tonnes of 2025 attributable output (BHP 1.47Mt, Codelco 1.44Mt, Freeport 1.08Mt, Southern Copper 953kt, Zijin 886kt, Glencore 852kt, CMOC 741kt, Rio Tinto 735kt, Anglo American 695kt, KGHM 592kt) against global mine supply of roughly 23 Mt (est.). Grasberg's own outage removed a meaningful slice of that in 2025–26 and was itself a driver of the price spike. New greenfield copper takes 15–20 years from discovery to production, so supply cannot respond to price on any investable horizon — which is precisely why Freeport's leaching programme (214 Mlbs in 2025, targeting a 300 Mlb run rate by end-2026 from already-mined material) is strategically valuable out of proportion to its size.
Tariffs, Trade Policy and the COMEX–LME Spread
ONGOING HEADWIND
Section 232 currently imposes a 50% duty on semi-finished and derivative copper products while deferring tariffs on refined cathode; a Commerce review contemplates 15% by 2027 and 30% by 2028. Market-implied probabilities are only 14.6% for a 15% refined tariff by January 2027 and 37% for 30% by January 2028. The consequence is that copper "has effectively become a policy-driven asset": the COMEX–LME spread, historically a ~$33/tonne premium, now swings on tariff expectations and pulls physical metal from London to U.S. warehouses. Bull implication: Freeport is the largest U.S. producer with domestic rod and refining assets, so a refined-metal tariff would flow almost directly into its realised price with no cost offset. Bear implication: the same policy sensitivity means the U.S. premium can vanish overnight on a headline, and tariff-driven stockpiling has pulled forward demand that will have to be worked off — LME inventories rose 20,000 tonnes in a single mid-August day, the largest build since April, and prices immediately gave ground.
