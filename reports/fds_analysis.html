01 Company Overview

FactSet sells the screen an investment professional stares at all day: a financial-data workstation, data feeds, portfolio analytics and reporting tools, sold by subscription to asset managers, wealth managers, banks and corporates. Almost all of its revenue is recurring, which is why FactSet reports ASV rather than bookings: organic ASV was $2,568.2M at August 31, 2026, up 7.0% year over year. Fiscal 2026 (to August 31, 2026) revenue was $2.48B, up 6.7%, which the company counts as its 47th consecutive year of revenue growth. GAAP EPS fell to $14.57 from $15.55 on higher operating costs, including one-time restructuring charges, while adjusted EPS rose 6.1% to $18.01.

Here is the puzzle. The business kept growing and the stock got cut in half. FactSet closed November 2024 at $490.67, its highest month-end in five years, and February 2026 at $216.81, down 56%. Over the same stretch annual revenue rose from $2.20B (FY2024) to $2.48B (FY2026) and annual ASV retention stayed above 95%. Two things did the damage. First, margins. The fiscal 2026 outlook released on September 18, 2025 guided adjusted EPS to $16.90–$17.60, below the $18.27 consensus, after a fourth quarter in which higher technology spending and the reset of a low prior-year bonus accrual had cut the adjusted operating margin to 33.8% from 35.8%. September 2025 was a −23.3% month. Second, a question the market could not stop asking: if an AI model can read filings, pull estimates and build the comp table, what is a data terminal worth?

On September 30, 2026 the company gave the next answer, its fiscal 2027 outlook. It guides organic ASV growth to 5.0–6.5%, below the 7.0% just delivered. It guides adjusted operating margin to 34.75–35.25%, a quarter to three-quarters of a point above fiscal 2026's 34.5% after a year in which the margin fell from 36.3%. Adjusted EPS is guided to $19.25–$19.65, a little under what analysts expected. The stock still rose 6.8% over the two sessions after the release, to $277.61, 28% above the February low. The central debate is unchanged. Is FactSet the content the AI agents need, with its data now plugged into Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's ChatGPT for Financial Services, or the workstation the agents replace?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Recurring subscriptions (organic ASV $2.57B) >95% ASV retention Workflow embeddedness (260,000+ users) Data connectors into Claude & ChatGPT 27 straight years of dividend increases Cash generation (FY26 FCF $707M)

The moat is habit plus plumbing. An analyst who has built ten years of screens, models and report templates on one platform does not switch lightly, and a portfolio-analytics installation feeding a firm's client reporting is harder still to rip out — which is what above-95% retention measures. The newer argument is plumbing: FactSet's data is now offered as a connector inside Anthropic's Claude ("Connect to FactSet global prices, fundamental analysis, earnings estimates, and research insights into Claude," per the connector page) and, per Fortune, banks using OpenAI's ChatGPT for Financial Services can connect existing subscriptions to datasets "from companies such as Bloomberg or FactSet." If the AI assistant becomes the front end, FactSet wants to be the licensed data behind it. The vulnerability is that plumbing is priced lower than a terminal.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The front end may stop being FactSet's. The workstation is where FactSet earns its seat price. If research, screening and deck-building move into a general AI assistant that merely calls FactSet data, the customer relationship — and pricing power — migrates to whoever owns the assistant. On September 10, 2026, the day OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services, FDS closed down 5.82%.
02
Margins are going backwards to pay for the defence. Gross margin went from 54.07% in FY2024 to 52.72% in FY2025 and 50.61% in FY2026 (our calculation from the release: revenue less cost of services). Adjusted operating margin fell from 36.3% to 34.5%. GAAP EPS fell 6.3% for the year, and 15.4% in Q4 ($3.41 vs $4.03), even as adjusted EPS rose 6.1% and 11.6%. The fiscal 2027 guide promises only a modest margin recovery (25–75bps). The AI investment has to show up in ASV, or FactSet is simply a lower-margin version of the same company.
03
Buy-side budgets and a slower guide. Active asset managers are exposed to fee compression and to the shift from active to passive management. Neither is caused by AI, and AI fixes neither. Management's fiscal 2027 guide of 5.0–6.5% organic ASV growth, against 7.0% just delivered, is the company's own forecast of slower subscription growth. On the call the CEO said FactSet prefers to guide to a range it has high confidence in, and that the year is weighted to the second half.
04
A thinner balance-sheet cushion than it looks. Balance-sheet debt of $1.37B, of which $499.4M is due within twelve months and now sits in current liabilities, against $233.3M of cash. That pushed the current ratio down to 0.60, from 1.40 a year earlier. It is manageable against $707.5M of fiscal 2026 free cash flow. But the company is paying dividends, buying back stock and funding AI at the same time. On the call the CFO estimated that a higher assumed tax rate and extra interest from refinancing the $500M of notes maturing in March 2027 together make about a 2% headwind to EPS growth.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 1, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 close of $394.78 to the October 1, 2026 settled close of $277.61, FDS lost 29.7% of its price. With dividends reinvested, the loss is about 25.8% (our calculation from Yahoo Finance's dividend-adjusted closes). The shape is two stories. For three years the stock went sideways in a $380–$490 band, the premium multiple of a steady compounder. Then, from the November 2024 peak of $490.67 to the February 2026 trough of $216.81, it lost 56% as a margin reset and the AI-disruption thesis arrived together. It has since recovered 28%, with a −13.9% September 2026 in between.

The 6-period monthly RSI tells the same story in numbers. It printed 11.7 in October 2025, the most oversold reading in the five-year series, and stayed below 30 for nine consecutive months, August 2025 through April 2026. June 2026, at 28.8, dipped below 30 once more. After 63.2 in August 2026 it now reads 50.3, neutral. The 10-month moving average sits at about $251.56, below the current price, so the long-term trend line is still underneath the stock.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 19.05x trailing GAAP earnings, depressed by $0.97 a share of restructuring charges, and about 14x forward earnings. In return you get a subscription business with above-95% retention and organic ASV growth of 7.0% in fiscal 2026, up from 5.7%, in the very year the market decided it was obsolete. Add a 27-year dividend-increase streak, 17.5% ROIC and a 7.2% free-cash-flow yield. The first fiscal 2027 guide answered the margin question the bulls needed answered: adjusted operating margin up 25–75bps rather than down again, and adjusted EPS of $19.25–$19.65, up 7–9%. The AI story may be a distribution story. FactSet's data is wired into both Claude and ChatGPT for Financial Services, and the company says AI-solutions ASV added more than doubled in fiscal 2026. If the November 10 investor day puts numbers on that, the de-rating has room to reverse further.

Bear Case

The bear case accepts every reported number and disputes the future. FactSet's premium came from owning the analyst's screen. If the screen becomes a general AI assistant, FactSet becomes a data supplier priced as one. The company's own fiscal 2027 guide already has organic ASV growth slowing to 5.0–6.5% from 7.0%, and adjusted EPS guided below consensus. Gross margin is down about 350bps in two years (54.07% to 50.61%), and GAAP EPS fell 6.3% in fiscal 2026, to fund a defence whose payoff is unproven. Six of 18 analysts rate it a Sell, the average target of $277.56 sits on the price, and 10.9% of the float was sold short at the September 15 settlement.

Top 3 Risks

01
Disintermediation by AI front ends. The Claude connector and ChatGPT for Financial Services integration make FactSet data available inside tools FactSet does not control. The stock fell 5.82% on the day OpenAI's product launched. Whether this ends as a channel or a squeeze is the single largest open question.
02
ASV deceleration. The fiscal 2027 guide of 5.0–6.5% organic ASV growth is below fiscal 2026's 7.0%. On the call the CEO said the company prefers to guide to a range it has high confidence in, and that the year is weighted to the second half. If growth lands at the low end, the story shifts from "growth is accelerating despite AI" to "growth is slowing because of it."
03
Margin recovery that doesn't come. On the call the CEO tied the lift to 34.75–35.25% to fiscal 2026 investments that will not repeat, such as the move to several cloud providers, to merging the business units into one product organisation, to exiting some businesses, and to efficiencies from AI agents. Meanwhile $499.4M of debt falls due within a year, and the CFO expects to refinance it at a higher rate than its 2.9% cost. If AI spending keeps the margin flat while ASV growth slows, earnings growth drifts to low single digits, and the 19x GAAP multiple becomes the expensive part.
Industry Commentary
Generative AI vs. the terminal business model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Financial-data vendors have sold two things bundled together: the data and the interface to it. Large language models unbundle them. When OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services on September 10, 2026, Fortune described banks connecting "existing subscriptions to datasets from companies such as Bloomberg or FactSet" — FactSet as a named data source, not as the product. Anthropic's Claude has a FactSet connector for "global prices, fundamental analysis, earnings estimates, and research insights." For FactSet this cuts both ways: every assistant that needs trustworthy numbers is a new consumer of its data, and every analyst who works in the assistant instead of the workstation is a weaker reason to pay for a seat.

Buy-side budget pressure
ONGOING HEADWIND

Active asset managers, a core FactSet client group, have spent years under fee pressure, and data spend is one of the few large discretionary lines. Even so, fiscal 2026 ended with 7.0% organic ASV growth, retention above 95% and AI-solutions ASV additions more than doubled. The fiscal 2027 guide of 5.0–6.5% is management's own estimate of how much of that growth carries into next year.

The valuation reset — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At its November 2024 peak FactSet traded near $490; at $277.61 it trades at 19.05x trailing GAAP earnings and about 14x forward earnings. The bull framing: the market priced a business whose growth was accelerating as if it were in decline, and the multiple has room to recover as retention holds and AI products are monetised. The bear framing: the old multiple was paying for a moat, the interface, that AI is dissolving, and a high-teens multiple is a fair price for a data supplier. The fiscal 2027 guide gave each side something. It promises a slightly higher margin, which the bulls wanted, and slower ASV growth, which the bears predicted. The way to settle it is quarterly organic ASV growth against the 5.0–6.5% guide, and the adjusted margin against 34.75–35.25%.
