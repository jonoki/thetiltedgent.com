01 Company Overview

FactSet sells the screen an investment professional stares at all day: a financial-data workstation, data feeds, portfolio analytics and reporting tools, sold by subscription to asset managers, wealth managers, banks and corporates. The company describes itself as "a global financial digital platform and enterprise solutions provider" (FY2025 results release). Almost all of its revenue is recurring, which is why FactSet reports ASV rather than bookings: organic ASV was $2,485.6M at May 31, 2026, up 7.1% year over year. FY2025 (to August 31, 2025) revenue was $2.32B, up 5.39%, with GAAP EPS of $15.55 and adjusted EPS of $16.98.

Here is the puzzle. The business kept growing and the stock got cut in half. FactSet closed November 2024 at $490.67, its highest month-end in five years, and February 2026 at $216.81 — down 56% — while trailing revenue went from $2.20B to $2.44B and annual ASV retention stayed above 95%. Two things did the damage. First, margins: the fiscal 2026 outlook released on September 18, 2025 guided adjusted EPS to $16.90–$17.60, below the $18.27 consensus, on higher technology spending and a bonus-accrual reset — September 2025 was a −23.3% month. Second, a question the market could not stop asking: if an AI model can read filings, pull estimates and build the comp table, what is a data terminal worth? The stock has since recovered to $284.10, 31% above the February low. The central debate: is FactSet the content the AI agents need — its data now plugs directly into Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's ChatGPT for Financial Services — or the workstation the agents replace?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Recurring subscriptions (ASV $2.49B) >95% ASV retention Workflow embeddedness (247,000+ users) Data connectors into Claude & ChatGPT 27 straight years of dividend increases Cash generation (TTM FCF $708M)

The moat is habit plus plumbing. An analyst who has built ten years of screens, models and report templates on one platform does not switch lightly, and a portfolio-analytics installation feeding a firm's client reporting is harder still to rip out — which is what above-95% retention measures. The newer argument is plumbing: FactSet's data is now offered as a connector inside Anthropic's Claude ("Connect to FactSet global prices, fundamental analysis, earnings estimates, and research insights into Claude," per the connector page) and, per Fortune, banks using OpenAI's ChatGPT for Financial Services can connect existing subscriptions to datasets "from companies such as Bloomberg or FactSet." If the AI assistant becomes the front end, FactSet wants to be the licensed data behind it. The vulnerability is that plumbing is priced lower than a terminal.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The front end may stop being FactSet's. The workstation is where FactSet earns its seat price. If research, screening and deck-building move into a general AI assistant that merely calls FactSet data, the customer relationship — and pricing power — migrates to whoever owns the assistant. On September 10, 2026, the day OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services, FDS closed down 5.82%.
02
Margins are going backwards to pay for the defence. Gross margin went from 54.07% in FY2024 to 52.72% in FY2025 and 51.38% on a trailing basis; Q3 FY2026 adjusted operating margin fell 280bps year over year. GAAP EPS in Q3 was down 9.6% even as adjusted EPS rose 6.1%. The AI investment has to show up in ASV or it is simply a lower-margin version of the same company.
03
Buy-side budgets. TIKR's February 2026 analysis pointed to "ongoing pressure from cost-cutting initiatives at asset management clients" and said the institutional buy-side "continues to struggle." FactSet's largest client group is exposed to fee compression and to the shift from active to passive management, neither of which AI causes and neither of which AI fixes.
04
A thinner balance-sheet cushion than it looks. Debt of $1.57B against $304M of cash and a current ratio of 0.68. Manageable against $708M of trailing free cash flow — but the company is spending on buybacks, dividends and AI at the same time, and it cannot do all three indefinitely if growth slows.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 close of $394.78 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $284.10, FDS lost 28.0% of its price. Adding back roughly $20 per share of dividends (our approximation from the annual dividend-per-share figures) brings the total return to roughly −23%. The shape is two stories. For three years the stock went sideways in a $380–$490 band — the premium multiple of a steady compounder. Then, from the November 2024 peak of $490.67 to the February 2026 trough of $216.81, it lost 56% as a margin reset and the AI-disruption thesis arrived together. Since February it has recovered 31%.

The 6-period monthly RSI tells the same story in numbers. It printed 11.7 in October 2025 — the most oversold reading in the five-year series — and stayed below 30 for nine consecutive months, August 2025 through April 2026 (June 2026, at 28.8, dipped below 30 once more). It now reads 51.8, neutral. The 10-month moving average sits at about $254.23, below the current price: for the first time since the slide began, the long-term trend line is underneath the stock rather than above it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 18.7x trailing and roughly 14.8x forward earnings for a subscription business with above-95% retention, organic ASV growth that accelerated from 5.7% to 7.1% during the very year the market decided it was obsolete, a 27-year dividend-increase streak, 18.4% ROIC and a ~7% free-cash-flow yield. The company bought back stock in Q3 at $219.21 on average. The AI story may be a distribution story: FactSet's data is being wired into both Claude and ChatGPT for Financial Services, and an AI agent that needs clean, licensed estimates and fundamentals needs a vendor. If the September 30 guide and the November 10 investor day show margins bottoming, the de-rating has room to reverse further.

Bear Case

The bear case accepts every reported number and disputes the future. FactSet's premium came from owning the analyst's screen; if the screen becomes a general AI assistant, FactSet becomes a data supplier priced as one. Margins are already falling — gross margin down roughly 270bps in two years, Q3 adjusted operating margin down 280bps, GAAP EPS down 9.6% in the quarter — to fund a defence whose payoff is unproven. Six of 18 analysts rate it a Sell, the average target sits 6.1% below the price after a 31% rally, and 12.6% of the float is sold short.

Top 3 Risks

01
Disintermediation by AI front ends. The Claude connector and ChatGPT for Financial Services integration make FactSet data available inside tools FactSet does not control. The stock fell 5.82% on the day OpenAI's product launched. Whether this ends as a channel or a squeeze is the single largest open question.
02
Guidance risk on September 30. The FY2026 guide is what triggered September 2025's 23.3% monthly fall. The stock has since rallied 31% from the February low into the FY2027 guide, against a consensus target below the current price.
03
Margin compression without growth payoff. If AI spending keeps pushing adjusted operating margin down while organic ASV growth stalls around 7%, earnings growth drifts to low single digits — and the 18.7x multiple would then be the expensive part.
Industry Commentary
Generative AI vs. the terminal business model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Financial-data vendors have sold two things bundled together: the data and the interface to it. Large language models unbundle them. When OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Financial Services on September 10, 2026, Fortune described banks connecting "existing subscriptions to datasets from companies such as Bloomberg or FactSet" — FactSet as a named data source, not as the product. Anthropic's Claude has a FactSet connector for "global prices, fundamental analysis, earnings estimates, and research insights." For FactSet this cuts both ways: every assistant that needs trustworthy numbers is a new consumer of its data, and every analyst who works in the assistant instead of the workstation is a weaker reason to pay for a seat.

Buy-side budget pressure
ONGOING HEADWIND

FactSet's core customers — active asset managers — have spent years under fee pressure, and data spend is one of the few large discretionary lines. TIKR's February 2026 review cited "ongoing pressure from cost-cutting initiatives at asset management clients." The Q3 FY2026 release nonetheless credited institutional buy-side and wealth-management clients for the quarter's growth, which suggests the pressure is being offset by wealth and by new products rather than disappearing.

The valuation reset — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At its November 2024 peak FactSet traded near $490; at $284.10 it trades at 18.7x trailing GAAP earnings. The bull framing: the market priced a business whose growth was accelerating as if it were in decline, and the multiple has room to recover as retention holds and AI products are monetised. The bear framing: the old multiple was paying for a moat — the interface — that AI is dissolving, and 18.7x is a fair price for a data supplier whose margins are heading down. The way to adjudicate is organic ASV growth and adjusted operating margin over the next four quarters, both reported. The FY2027 guide on September 30 is the first data point.
