01 Company Overview

FedEx Freight describes itself as North America's largest less-than-truckload (LTL) carrier: 365 locations, nearly 30,000 vehicles, 40,000 team members and roughly 90,000 shipments a day, sold as Priority, Economy and Direct service plus the expedited FedEx Custom Critical subsidiary. Until May 31, 2026 it was a segment of FedEx Corporation. On June 1, 2026 FedEx spun it off — one FDXF share for every two FDX shares held on the May 15 record date — and the stock began regular-way trading on the NYSE the same morning. S&P Dow Jones Indices added it to the S&P 500 on day one, an unusual fast-track for a spin-off.

The hand it was dealt is the whole story. Before the separation, FedEx Freight paid its parent a ~$4.1B cash dividend, funded in part by a $3.7B senior notes offering in February 2026. It walked out the door with $6.37B of total debt, $251M of cash and negative book equity (−$497M), while FedEx kept 19.9% of the shares to dispose of within 24 months. The stock opened at $164.00, closed its first day at $149.53, peaked at a $188.46 close on June 9 — and has since fallen to $117.60, 37.6% below that peak and 21.4% below its debut close. The business underneath is a pricing story in a volume drought: in the quarter to May 31, 2026, daily shipments fell 5.9% while revenue per shipment rose 11.5%. The central debate is whether that pricing discipline holds long enough for volume to come back — with a balance sheet that no longer has a parent behind it.

Revenue Mix

The terminal network is the product. A shipper's three pallets are picked up, cross-docked at a service center, line-hauled overnight and delivered — Priority offers next-business-day service on lanes up to 600 miles; Economy trades speed for price. The economics are fixed-cost heavy: terminals, doors, tractors and drivers cost roughly the same whether trailers leave full or half-empty, so density (weight per trailer, shipments per door) drives margin. In Q4 FY2026 average daily shipments fell 5.9% to 86.7 thousand, but weight per shipment rose 3.0% to 948 lb, revenue per hundredweight rose 8.2% to $43.79 and revenue per shipment rose 11.5% to $415.22. That is price and mix doing the work volume could not.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

365-location terminal network Largest North American LTL carrier ~30,000 vehicles Priority next-day on lanes ≤600 miles FedEx brand & customer relationships Record-low DOT preventable accidents

LTL's moat is real estate and density. A national network of service centers with enough doors, in the right metro areas, takes decades and zoning battles to replicate — which is why the industry prices rationally even in downturns and why FedEx Freight could lift revenue per hundredweight 8.2% while volume fell. Scale lets a large carrier fill more trailers per lane than a small one. The vulnerability is that density cuts both ways: every lost shipment leaves a fixed cost uncovered. And the market is making a comparison in plain sight — FedEx Freight's $8.80B of trailing revenue is larger than Old Dominion's $5.60B, yet its $17.58B market value is less than half of Old Dominion's $36.36B. Investors are paying about 2.0x sales for FDXF against ~6.5x for ODFL (computed from StockAnalysis market cap ÷ TTM revenue). That gap is a verdict on margins and balance sheet, not on size.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Born levered. The ~$4.1B pre-spin dividend to FedEx left FDXF with $6.37B of total debt, $251M of cash and negative book equity. Guidance calls for ~$135M of interest expense in the seven months to December 31, 2026. Net debt of $6.11B is about 5.5x FY2026 adjusted operating income of $1.108B (computed; adjusted EBITDA not sourced). A carrier that used to lean on a parent's balance sheet through a freight recession now carries its own.
02
Volume is still going the wrong way. Shipments fell 5.9% YoY in Q4 FY2026. Pricing can offset shrinking volume for a while, but not indefinitely — and the CEO has already said the recovery will not be "in a straight line" and that margin will "dip" after the spin.
03
A 19.9% overhang. FedEx must dispose of its retained stake — roughly 30M shares — within 24 months of June 1, 2026. Whether that happens through debt-for-equity exchanges or distributions to FDX holders, it is a known future supply of stock that buyers can wait for.
04
Standalone costs and messy comparables. Spin-off costs were $492M in FY2026. Pre-spin history is a carve-out from FedEx's accounts, so historical margins and per-share numbers were produced under a cost structure that no longer exists. The fiscal-year change to December adds a seven-month transition period that makes year-on-year comparisons awkward until 2027.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying North America's largest LTL network at about 2.0x sales, against ~6.5x for Old Dominion, after a 37.6% fall from the June peak that has not come with a single guidance cut. Pricing is working — revenue per hundredweight +8.2%, revenue per shipment +11.5% — while volume is at a cyclical low that Transport Topics describes as the tail of a 3½-year downcycle. Strip out $492M of one-off spin costs and FY2026 adjusted operating margin was 12.6%, with Q4 at 15.1%. Management targets 10–12% annual adjusted operating income growth and a 15% margin by 2029. When volume returns to a fixed-cost network, operating leverage is violent in the good direction, and 14 analysts' average target of $164 sits 39% above the price.

Bear Case

This is a cyclical business that was separated at the bottom of its cycle and loaded with $6.37B of debt to pay its former parent ~$4.1B. It has negative book equity and negative trailing free cash flow (−$212M), and must pay ~$135M of interest in seven months. Shipments are still falling (−5.9%). The CEO has pre-announced a margin "dip". Nearly 20% of the stock is held by a seller with a two-year deadline. Its peers command premium multiples because they have spent years proving better operating ratios; FDXF has proved nothing yet as a standalone company. Trading has been one-directional since June 9, and the consensus target has yet to catch up with the tape.

Top 3 Risks

01
Leverage into a soft freight market. Net debt of $6.11B against $1.108B of adjusted operating income (about 5.5x, computed) leaves limited room if the freight recovery stalls. Every quarter of falling shipments is absorbed by a fixed-cost network and a fixed interest bill.
02
Volume recovery that does not arrive. Pricing gains on shrinking volume have a ceiling: push yield too far and freight migrates to competitors. Watch average daily shipments in the October report — another decline would suggest the pricing is costing share.
03
The FedEx stake and standalone execution. FedEx's 19.9% has to come to market by June 2028, and the company must build standalone functions it used to rent from its parent. Spin-off costs ran $492M in FY2026. Further separation costs or dis-synergies would hit the margin targets directly.
Industry Commentary
An LTL cycle turning — slowly
ONGOING HEADWIND

Per Transport Topics (June 1, 2026), LTL spot and contract rates rose over the first five months of 2026 after a downcycle lasting more than 3½ years, but headwinds remain: inflation, diesel prices elevated by Middle East tensions, and trade-policy uncertainty. CEO John Smith's framing — opportunity "for the market to turn" but not "in a straight line" — matches FedEx Freight's own Q4: pricing up, volume down. For a network business the order matters. Price first protects margin today; volume later is what delivers the 15% margin target.

Industry pricing discipline
EMERGING TAILWIND

LTL is a concentrated market with high barriers to entry: terminals cannot be conjured. That is why FedEx Freight could raise revenue per hundredweight 8.2% with shipments down 5.9%, and why Old Dominion is pushing a 4.9% general rate increase from October 5. The bull framing: rational pricing plus a cyclical volume recovery is the best combination in trucking, and the largest network benefits most. The bear framing: pricing power in a downturn is partly volume given away, and a newly independent, levered carrier has less room than its peers to wait out the cycle.

Spin-off mechanics
STRUCTURAL

Spin-offs hand stock to shareholders who never chose it. FDX holders received one FDXF share per two FDX shares, and many parcel-focused or index-constrained holders had reason to sell. S&P 500 inclusion on day one absorbed some of that — index funds had to buy — but the stock's steady decline since June 9 is consistent with continued distribution by unwilling holders. The retained 19.9% stake is the next chapter of the same process. None of this changes the business. All of it affects the price, for as long as the register is still settling.
