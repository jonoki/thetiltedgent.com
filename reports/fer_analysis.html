01 Company Overview

Ferrovial builds and operates transport infrastructure across four reported segments — Highways (toll roads), Airports, Construction (Ferrovial Construction, Budimex in Poland, Webber in the US) and Energy. FY2025 revenue was €9,627M, up 5.2% from €9,148M in FY2024; the trailing-twelve-month figure through H1 2026 is $11.26B, up 5.4% as StockAnalysis reports it in dollars. At a $41.75B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #625 globally (companiesmarketcap.com, September 2026). It has been listed on Nasdaq since May 2024, alongside Amsterdam and Madrid.

The central debate is not "is construction growing." It is that this is a sum-of-the-parts asset story wearing an operating company's income statement. In H1 2026 the Highways division produced €740M of revenue — 15.7% of the group's €4.7B — but €530M of adjusted EBITDA, roughly 71% of the group's €746M. The crown jewels, the 407 ETR toll road in Toronto and the Texas Managed Lanes (NTE, LBJ, NTE 35W), are held through stakes accounted for by the equity method or as non-recourse project companies — so the consolidated revenue line understates them and the earnings line is dominated by accounting choices and disposal gains. That is why the trailing P/E of 60.3 is close to meaningless, and why the honest way to look at Ferrovial is asset by asset. One correction to a common framing up front: Ferrovial no longer owns any stake in Heathrow — management stated on the H1 2026 call that the company fully exited its Heathrow investment in 2025.

The 407 ETR is an electronically tolled highway across the northern edge of the Greater Toronto Area with the pricing freedom that makes it unusual among toll assets: rates are set by schedule rather than negotiated annually with a regulator. The H1 2026 numbers show exactly what that freedom is worth and exactly where the risk sits. Vehicle-kilometres travelled grew just 1.8%, while revenue per trip grew 17.7% and total revenue grew 18.7%, toll revenue 20.2%. In other words, essentially all of the growth is price, not volume. Management confirmed the January 1, 2026 annual increase went through and then signalled a strategy change: away from a single annual across-the-board rise and toward "value capture" combining toll increases, targeted promotions and congestion management — an admission that the blunt-instrument price rise is nearing its useful limit. On cash: CAD 500M of dividends were paid in H1 2026 and a further CAD 550M was approved for Q3 2026 distribution, with the board due to update on further distributions in October. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
TOLL PRICING FREEDOM PHYSICALLY IRREPLACEABLE ROUTES CONGESTION-PRICED DEMAND LONG-DATED CONCESSIONS NON-RECOURSE PROJECT DEBT €18.0B ORDER BOOK NET CASH EX-PROJECTS

The moat is geographic and contractual, not technological. You cannot build a second highway across the top of Toronto, and you cannot add capacity to an American urban corridor without a decade of process; that scarcity is what allows the 407 ETR to raise revenue per trip 17.7% while traffic moves 1.8%. The financing structure reinforces it: project debt sits inside non-recourse concession vehicles, which is why the group can show €6,283M of consolidated net debt and simultaneously €1,307M of net cash excluding infrastructure projects alongside €4.7B of liquidity. The holding company is not levered; the roads are — and the roads are the ones with contractual, inflation-linked pricing power. What that structure does not do is make the earnings legible, which is the subject of Section 04.

Price is doing all the work
Across the entire toll portfolio, volume is flat to negative and revenue growth is almost entirely rate. 407 ETR: VKT +1.8%, revenue/trip +17.7%. NTE transactions −2.0%, I-77 −5.2%. Management's own shift toward "targeted promotions and congestion management" rather than a single annual increase reads as an acknowledgement that the price lever is getting stiff. Pricing power that has to be exercised ever more carefully is pricing power that is being spent.
Single-asset concentration
With Highways at ≈71% of group adjusted EBITDA and the 407 ETR the largest piece of it, a regulatory, legal or traffic shock to one Canadian road is a shock to the majority of Ferrovial's economic value. The Heathrow exit removed a diversifying dividend stream and increased that concentration rather than reducing it.
Accounting opacity
Equity-method and non-recourse structures mean reported revenue, EBITDA and earnings each describe a different perimeter. Trailing P/E is 60.3 and EV/EBITDA 29.17, neither of which describes the asset value. Investors must do a sum-of-the-parts by hand, and the disclosure does not make that easy.
Execution risk at JFK
New Terminal One is 92% complete with Phase A occupancy pushed to March 2027 and €500,000/day of liquidated damages accruing against the contractor since June 26, 2026. Contractual protection is not the same as cash flow: a terminal that opens late earns nothing late, and Ferrovial has already injected $1.1B of equity into it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a portfolio of irreplaceable, inflation-linked concessions at a price the income statement makes look expensive and the cash flow makes look reasonable: $2.19B of free cash flow, a 19.07% FCF margin, against a 6.15% net margin distorted by equity-method accounting. The 407 ETR raised revenue per trip 17.7% and is distributing CAD 550M in Q3 alone; the group holds €1.3B net cash excluding project debt and €4.7B liquidity; the construction order book is at an all-time high of €18.0B with 47.9% in North America. JFK New Terminal One flips from cash consumption to cash generation in 2027, €500M of buyback authorisation is unspent, and even the lowest published analyst target ($70.26) sits 21% above the current price.

Bear Case

Every toll asset is growing on price with flat or falling volume — 407 ETR traffic +1.8%, NTE transactions −2.0%, I-77 −5.2% — and management is already pivoting away from blunt annual increases toward "promotions and congestion management," which is what a company says when the price lever gets stiff. The Heathrow exit removed a diversifying dividend stream and left ≈71% of EBITDA in Highways. Reported profit halved in H1 2026 (€258M vs €540M) and the trailing P/E of 60.3 offers no valuation support if the sum-of-the-parts narrative loses the market's confidence. The sell side has moved from Buy-weighted to Hold-weighted across four downgrades in nine months, and the stock is down 15.5% since mid-June on good operating news.

01
Toll-price elasticity finally binds. The entire earnings model now rests on raising rates faster than traffic falls. 407 ETR revenue per trip rose 17.7% on 1.8% volume growth; two US assets are already losing transactions. If drivers begin substituting to free alternatives at scale, revenue growth stops abruptly and the terminal-value assumptions in every sum-of-the-parts model move at once.
02
Concentration in a single Canadian road. With Highways at ≈71% of adjusted EBITDA and 407 ETR the dominant piece, any adverse regulatory, legal or political development in Ontario — toll-rate challenge, concession renegotiation, tax change — hits the majority of Ferrovial's economic value with no offsetting asset. The 2025 Heathrow exit made this concentration worse, not better.
03
Discount-rate sensitivity and valuation opacity. Very long-dated cash flows are extremely sensitive to rates, and Ferrovial's disclosure makes the underlying asset value hard to verify: equity-method holdings, non-recourse project debt of a different perimeter from the reported net debt, and a trailing P/E of 60.3 that describes nothing. A higher-for-longer rate path plus a market unwilling to do the sum-of-the-parts by hand is precisely the combination that produced the last eleven weeks.
Industry Commentary
All price, no volume — across the whole toll portfolio
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

This is not one asset's problem; it is the pattern everywhere Ferrovial collects a toll. 407 ETR: VKT +1.8%, revenue per trip +17.7%. NTE: transactions −2.0%, revenue per transaction +18.9%. LBJ: +2.9% / +11.7%. NTE 35W: +0.4% / +17.3%. I-77: −5.2% / +11.8%. I-66: +8.5% / +8.7%. Only I-66 is growing meaningfully on both. Management has offered two mitigants — adjacent-corridor construction easing by early 2027, and vehicle-classification technology lifting revenue per transaction — and both are honest, but neither is demand growth. Layer on the strategic shift at 407 ETR toward "targeted promotions and congestion management" instead of a single annual increase, and the picture is of a business optimising the last increments of a pricing lever rather than opening a new one.

North American infrastructure build-out and the capital-light pivot
EMERGING TAILWIND

The construction order book is at an all-time high of €18,046M (+2.8%) with 47.9% in North America, 22.9% in Poland and 14.0% in Spain — a mix weighted toward the two regions running the largest public infrastructure programmes. More interesting is what Ferrovial is doing with it: the ≈€1B data centre programme in Madrid and Warsaw is explicitly structured as a "capital-light model with partners contributing equity." That is the concession playbook — originate, build, part-own, collect distributions — pointed at digital infrastructure instead of highways. If it works, it addresses the single biggest structural criticism of the company: that its growth requires either more toll increases or enormous new capital commitments. It is early, unquantified in the segment disclosure, and should be sized as an option, not a forecast.

The sum-of-the-parts discount — bull and bear framed together
ONGOING HEADWIND

Ferrovial's shares are a bet on whether the market will do the arithmetic. The bull framing: a 60.3× trailing P/E is an accounting artefact; the real signal is $2.19B of free cash flow, a 19.07% FCF margin, €1.3B of net cash outside the project vehicles and CAD 1.05B of 407 ETR distributions declared in 2026 so far. On that basis the shares are a discounted collection of very long-lived, inflation-linked assets, with three published analyst targets ranging from $70.26 to $90.32. The bear framing: a discount that never closes is not a discount, it is the price. Reported profit halved in H1 2026, four downgrades have landed in nine months, coverage is thin (3–8 analysts for a $41.75B company), the diversifying Heathrow stake is gone, and volume growth across the toll network is approximately zero. Both framings use the same facts. The difference is entirely about whether toll pricing power has years left to run or is being spent down — which is why the January 1, 2027 rate revision and the Q3 traffic split matter more than any earnings headline.
