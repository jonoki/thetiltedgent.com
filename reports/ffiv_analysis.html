01 Company Overview

F5 sits in the plumbing between the internet and the application. Its BIG-IP boxes and software — application delivery controllers — decide which server answers a request, decrypt and inspect the traffic, and block the attacks riding on it. Founded in 1996 as F5 Labs, it listed on NASDAQ as F5 Networks in June 1999 and renamed itself F5, Inc. in 2021, after buying NGINX ($670M, 2019), Shape Security ($1B, 2020) and Volterra ($500M, 2021) to push the franchise from hardware into software and cloud security. FY2026 (the year to September 30, 2026) revenue was $3.42B, up 11%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $12.52 (FY2025: $3.09B and $11.80).

The last twelve months are a hand most players would have folded. On October 15, 2025 F5 disclosed that a nation-state actor had been inside its product-development systems and had stolen portions of BIG-IP source code and information on undisclosed vulnerabilities; CISA issued an emergency directive the same day ordering federal agencies to inventory and patch their F5 gear. Twelve days later management guided FY2026 revenue growth to just 0% to 4%, warning customers would pause to remediate. The stock closed October at $253.05, down 21.7% in a month. What followed was the surprise: customers did not walk. They refreshed. Guidance was raised to 5–6%, then 7–8% in April, then 9–10% in July, and the year finished at 11% growth, with systems (hardware) revenue up 31% and product revenue up double digits for a ninth straight quarter. The stock rose with it, to a $479.83 close on October 9, 2026. Then, on October 27, F5 gave its first look at FY2027: revenue growth of 8% to 9%, non-GAAP EPS of $18.00–$18.30 (up only 3–5% on FY2026's $17.49), lower margins on memory costs, and a CFO saying hardware growth "likely will be slower in the second half just from the cyclical part of the refresh." The next day the stock fell 20.43%, and it closed October 29 at $363.46. The breach question is settled; the question now is what F5 earns once the refresh cycle ends, at 29.0x trailing earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base in the traffic path iRules / config lock-in $2.20B deferred revenue Hardware + software + SaaS in one vendor Net cash $1.38B 81.9% gross margin

The moat is position. A BIG-IP device sits in front of a bank's login page or an airline's booking engine, carrying years of custom traffic rules written in F5's own scripting language. Ripping it out means rewriting those rules and accepting outage risk on the most visible systems a company runs. The October 2025 breach was the live stress test of that claim: the vendor itself was compromised, the US government told agencies to isolate its products, and customers still bought 31% more systems in FY2026 than a year earlier. Switching cost beat reputational damage. What the moat does not do is keep a refresh going: once the installed base has been upgraded, hardware sales fall back toward replacement pace, which is what the FY2027 guide describes. The vulnerability is the flip side of the same fact — F5's franchise is the on-premises and hybrid data centre, and every workload that moves to a cloud provider's native load balancer or a CDN-edge security service is one F5 has to follow rather than own.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The breach tail. Stolen source code and undisclosed-vulnerability research are assets an attacker can use for years. Reviews by NCC Group and IOActive found no critical vulnerabilities in the code (Help Net Security), but any future BIG-IP exploit will be read through the October 2025 lens, and the FY2026 results still carry $28.0M of cyber-incident costs and $10.0M of insurance recoveries as adjustments.
02
The hardware cycle is turning. Systems grew 31% in FY2026; management now expects upper-single-digit growth in FY2027, slowing in the second half. Consensus has FY2027 revenue up 8.46% and EPS up 4.26% (StockAnalysis). Even after the October 28 fall, the stock trades at 29.0x trailing GAAP earnings.
03
Memory costs. The FY2027 guide puts non-GAAP gross margin at 80.5%–82.5%, down from 84.0% in FY2026, and operating margin at 33%–35% against 35.1%. Price increases can offset higher component costs only as long as demand is strong enough to absorb them.
04
Software has to take over. The strategic line grew 8% in FY2026 while hardware grew 31%. The FY2027 plan needs double-digit software growth, back-weighted to the second half, to fill the gap as systems slow. If the AI security platform and SaaS offerings do not scale, F5 remains a hardware-refresh story with a subscription veneer.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items shape the reported figures. First, the security incident: the Q4 FY2026 release carries $28.0M of FY2026 cyber-incident costs ($1.5M in Q4) and $10.0M of related insurance recoveries as items reconciling GAAP to non-GAAP results. Second, the GAAP/non-GAAP gap is wide — Q4 FY2026 GAAP EPS $3.23 versus non-GAAP $4.42, and a GAAP operating margin of 23.1% against 33.7% non-GAAP — mainly stock-based compensation and acquisition amortisation. Q4 cash flow was also held down on purpose: $118M from operations and $91M of free cash flow, after what the CFO called "deliberate and strategic working capital decisions to mitigate supply chain risk." Forward P/E and guidance are on the non-GAAP basis; trailing P/E is on GAAP. Comparing them directly overstates the growth the market is being promised.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

F5 took the worst hit a security vendor can take — its own source code stolen by a state actor and a federal emergency directive against its products — and grew through it. Guidance went from 0–4% to a finished 11% in one fiscal year, product revenue has grown double digits for nine straight quarters, and direct AI bookings passed $100M in FY2026. The business converts 21% of revenue into net income, holds $1.38B of net cash, and has just added $1.5B to its buyback, about 7% of its $20.65B market value. The October 28 fall took the price to 19.9x consensus FY2027 earnings, while management still expects a third straight year of double-digit product revenue growth. If software growth picks up as hardware slows — the plan management laid out on the call — the stock is being priced for the end of a cycle rather than a hand-off from hardware to software.

Bear Case

The growth was a hardware refresh, and the company now says it ends: systems growth slows in the second half of FY2027. Guided non-GAAP EPS of $18.00–$18.30 is only 3–5% above FY2026's $17.49, gross margin is guided down to 80.5%–82.5% from 84.0% on memory costs, and Q4 cash flow was spent on inventory and supplier prepayments. At 29.0x trailing GAAP earnings and a 2.51 PEG, the stock still prices more growth than that guide shows. The breach's stolen code and vulnerability research remain in someone else's hands. Industry Commentary
When the security vendor is the breach
RESOLVED? NOT YET

F5 learned of the intrusion in August 2025 and disclosed it on October 15. The attackers took files from product-development and engineering knowledge systems, including BIG-IP source code, undisclosed-vulnerability research and configuration details for a small percentage of customers. F5 engaged CrowdStrike, Mandiant, NCC Group and IOActive, and said reviews found no critical vulnerabilities in the code. CISA's directive required federal agencies to submit a detailed inventory by December 3, 2025. The commercial result was the opposite of what the October guidance feared: remediation meant upgrading, and upgrading meant buying.

Memory prices and the hardware P&L
ONGOING HEADWIND

AI data-centre demand has tightened memory supply, and a vendor whose fastest-growing line is appliances absorbs that in cost of goods. F5's answer is price increases, tighter discounting and buying parts early: in Q4 it built inventory and prepaid suppliers to secure FY2027 supply. Non-GAAP gross margin was 84.0% in FY2026 and is guided to 80.5%–82.5% for FY2027. A margin step-down arriving just as growth slows is the combination that makes investors pay less for each dollar of earnings — which is what October 28 looked like.

AI inference moves on-premises
EMERGING TAILWIND

Management frames demand around hybrid multicloud, an expanding threat landscape and AI inference run on companies' own hardware. The CEO's sharpest point in the July release was about attackers, not buyers: "AI has empowered attackers and compressed the time between vulnerability discovery and exploitation." If that is right, the layer that inspects traffic before it reaches an unpatched application becomes more valuable — which is the job BIG-IP was built for. Direct AI bookings of well over $100M in FY2026 are the scoreboard; against $3.42B of revenue they need to keep growing fast for the thesis to carry the stock.
