01 Company Overview

Every American who has applied for a mortgage, a car loan or a credit card has been priced, in part, by a three-digit number that Fair Isaac invented. FICO sells two things: the Scores — the FICO Score licensed to lenders through the credit bureaus, plus consumer products via myFICO.com — and Software, an analytics and decisioning platform used by banks for originations, fraud and customer management. The Scores business is the money machine: in the quarter to September 30, 2026 it was 63% of revenue, and for fiscal 2026 the company ran an 86.1% gross margin and a 49.9% operating margin on revenue of $2.53B, up 27%. Earnings per share (EPS) for the year were $36.32 on the standard accounting basis (GAAP).

The story of the last sixteen months is a toll-booth owner discovering that the road belongs to someone else. FICO's recent growth came overwhelmingly from price: in the September quarter, the release says, B2B Scores revenue (sales to lenders) rose 39%, mainly from a higher unit price for mortgage-origination scores, partly offset by lower mortgage-origination volume. The road in question — the mortgages bought by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac — is run by the FHFA. On September 4, 2026 it gave VantageScore 4.0, a rival score owned by the three credit bureaus, full approval for all lenders selling to the two agencies, and FICO stock fell 16.68% in a day. Then, late on September 28, FHFA Director Bill Pulte wrote on X: "Fannie and Freddie are hereby moving to ONE PRICING GRID with VantageScore joining the existing FICO Classic pricing grid" (as quoted by the Motley Fool). Rocket Mortgage said the same day it would make VantageScore 4.0 its preferred score on all eligible loans from the fourth quarter of 2026, and TransUnion said on September 29 it would keep VantageScore 4.0 at $0.99 a score through December 2028. FICO stock fell 26.52% on September 29, from $840.89 to $617.87, and closed September at $592.47. After the close on October 23 FICO said it would hold its wholesale mortgage score price at $10 for lenders in its new Preferred Program, with the $10 now covering both Classic FICO and FICO Score 10T. On October 26 the stock fell another 13.15%; MT Newswires tied the fall to that decision.

The company reported fiscal Q4 after the close on November 4: revenue of $657.0M (+27%), GAAP EPS of $8.12 and non-GAAP EPS (profit with some one-off and non-cash costs left out) of $11.10, and a full year that landed slightly above the revenue and non-GAAP EPS guidance given in July. But it gave no fiscal 2027 guidance, saying it is moving to bespoke, customer-by-customer mortgage-origination pricing and that the programs are still being finalized. It also booked a $65M charge for a proposed settlement of antitrust class actions tied to Scores, and a $27M charge for a workforce reduction of about 15% of positions. On the call management said it had paused share buybacks in the quarter to repay the term loan behind the June-quarter buyback. The stock rose 0.91% the next day. At $704.55 it is 62.1% below its 52-week high (intraday $1,858.91, December 12, 2025) and 70.3% below its November 2024 month-end peak. The question for the next year is simple to state: how much of FICO's pricing was product, and how much was monopoly?

Revenue Mix

FICO does not collect credit data; the three bureaus do. FICO licenses the algorithm that turns a credit file into a score, and gets paid a royalty each time a score is pulled. In Q4 FY2026, B2B revenue (lenders) rose 39%, mainly from a higher mortgage-origination unit price, partly offset by lower mortgage-origination volume; B2C (myFICO.com and scores sold to consumers via the bureaus) rose 3%. On the Q4 call FICO said mortgage-origination revenue rose 72% while volumes fell by high single digits, that it saw no meaningful loss of volume to competition in the quarter, and that mortgage originations were 67% of B2B Scores and 58% of all Scores revenue. The growth was price. That is why the FHFA matters so much: roughly half of US mortgages flow through Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (per the Motley Fool's account of the July 2025 decision), and they now accept VantageScore 4.0 on the same pricing grid as FICO. FICO's responses are the FICO Preferred Program, launched in October — enrolled lenders pay no 2027 increase in the wholesale price and get Classic FICO and FICO Score 10T together for $10; the release says several deals have been announced and other programs covering calendar 2027 are in discussion — and FICO Score 10T, which management said on the Q3 call third-party analysis, including Milliman's, found more predictive than VantageScore. The FHA will make 10T available to approved lenders from January 1, 2027; Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are still reviewing it, per the Q4 call.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Industry-standard score Lender models built around FICO Mortgage pricing power (under test) 86.1% gross margin FICO Score 10T validation Platform net retention 156% Consumer brand (myFICO)

The real moat was never the algorithm alone — it was that everyone downstream of a loan speaks FICO. Underwriting cut-offs, mortgage-insurance grids, mortgage-bond disclosures and rating-agency models are written in FICO points, which is why the company could lift mortgage score prices and watch revenue climb on flat volumes. The vulnerability is that the single largest buyer of that utility — the government-controlled mortgage agencies — can change the rulebook by directive, and twice in September 2026 it did: full approval of VantageScore 4.0 on the 4th, then a single pricing grid for both scores on the 28th. A standard protected by habit is weaker than one protected by rule.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A regulator that wants the price lower. The FHFA opened the door to VantageScore 4.0 in July 2025, granted full approval on September 4, 2026, and announced on September 28 that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac would move to one pricing grid with VantageScore joining the FICO Classic grid. Between May 1, 2026 and the end of August, VantageScore 4.0 was the only score used on more than 9% of the mortgages Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac packaged into bonds (per Parameter's report). Rocket Mortgage said on September 28 it would default to VantageScore 4.0 on loans delivered to Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac and the VA from the fourth quarter of 2026.
02
A competitor priced at a tenth. TransUnion said on September 29 it would keep VantageScore 4.0 at $0.99 a score through December 2028, against FICO's $10 a score for 2026 (HousingWire), a price FICO said on October 23 it would hold for lenders in its Preferred Program. VantageScore was created by Equifax, Experian and TransUnion — the same bureaus that distribute FICO's score, which is exactly why FICO launched a direct-license route around them.
03
Growth is price, and price is the target. Q4 B2B Scores revenue rose 39% on a higher unit price with mortgage volume lower. FICO gave no FY2027 guidance because its 2027 mortgage pricing programs are still being finalized, so the market has no company number for the year in which the price is tested.
04
Leverage built for a monopoly. $5.26B of debt, $217.6M of cash, and a shareholders' deficit of $3.87B after years of buybacks (September 30, 2026 balance sheet). Debt is about 4.0 times annual operating cash earnings (EBITDA), per StockAnalysis; net of cash, FICO's own figure was 3.29 times at quarter end — comfortable for a toll road with pricing power, less so if the toll gets cut.
05
A legal bill for the pricing. Q4 results include a $65M accrual for a proposed settlement of antitrust class action lawsuits related to the Scores business. The 10-K says FICO and the plaintiffs signed a term sheet on September 29, 2026 for a $65.0M cash payment and compliance undertakings, with no admission of wrongdoing; the settlement needs final court approval.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 6, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over five years, from the November 2021 month-end close of $353.13 to the November 6, 2026 settled close of $704.55, FICO is still up 99.5% on price (it pays no dividend, so that is also the total return). But the shape is a round trip: a three-year climb that multiplied the price more than six-fold to a $2,375.03 month-end peak in November 2024, then a two-year descent that has taken 70.3% off that peak. The climb was the pricing story — mortgage score prices rising on flat volume. Two of the three largest monthly falls in the series came in months when the FHFA acted: July 2025 and September 2026.

The 6-period monthly RSI captures the turn. It sat above 70 in all but two months of 2023–2024 and peaked at 95.4 in November 2024 — about as one-sided as a chart gets. It now reads 27.6, oversold, after a low of 18.8 in September 2026. The 10-month moving average, at about $1,022.57, sits well above the price (the price is 31% below it): the long-term trend is firmly down, and nothing on the chart yet says otherwise.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At 19.4x trailing GAAP earnings and about 15.7x next year's consensus, you are buying an 86% gross-margin, 80% ROIC business after a 70% fall from its peak month-end, with a $1B-a-year free-cash-flow stream (FY2026: $1,034.4M). The year just reported beat the revenue and non-GAAP EPS guidance given in July, platform software is growing 72% with 156% net retention, and the restructuring cuts about 15% of positions. Lenders who must satisfy investors, insurers and rating agencies calibrated in FICO points will not switch overnight, and the Preferred Program, with pricing tailored to each customer, is FICO's way of keeping the volume. Management says third-party studies favor FICO Score 10T, and the direct-license route would let FICO cut the price at the tri-merge (the combined three-bureau credit report) while keeping a fee on each closed loan. A 16.6% gap to the average target says analysts, on average, think the fall went too far.

Bear Case

The last three years of growth were price, and the biggest customer's regulator has now put VantageScore on the same pricing grid as FICO, with a rival score priced at $0.99 through December 2028. Mortgage originations were 58% of Scores revenue in Q4 and grew 72% while volumes fell by high single digits; even a partial price concession falls almost straight to the bottom line. FICO gave no FY2027 guidance, so there is no company number to anchor on, and it booked a $65M accrual for a proposed antitrust settlement. The balance sheet was built for a toll road that could only raise tolls: $5.26B of debt, a $3.87B shareholders' deficit, and $3.05B of buybacks in FY2026, with the year's purchases made at an average of $1,204, 71% above today's price. With 11.74% of the tradable shares sold short at the October 15 settlement (per StockAnalysis), some investors are betting that earnings, not just the share price, have to come down.

Mortgage score pricing under regulatory pressure. The FHFA's September 4 full approval of VantageScore 4.0 and its September 28 move to one pricing grid remove FICO's exclusive position in GSE mortgages. The risk is less lost volume in the near term than forced price cuts to keep it, and FICO's own FY2027 pricing is not yet set.

Leverage and capital allocation. Net debt of about $5.04B, 3.29x EBITDA on FICO's own measure, and negative book equity leave less room if Scores earnings reset. FY2026 buybacks at an average of $1,204 now look expensive against a $704.55 share price, and buybacks are paused while debt is repaid.
Industry Commentary
The FHFA and the end of the single-score mortgage
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For decades a GSE mortgage meant a FICO score. That changed in three steps: in July 2025 FHFA Director Bill Pulte said lenders could use VantageScore 4.0 as an alternative; on September 4, 2026 — after a pilot in which, from May 1 to the end of August, VantageScore was the only score on more than 9% of the mortgages packaged into bonds by the two agencies — the agency made the choice available to every lender, effective immediately; and on September 28 he announced that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac would use one pricing grid, with VantageScore joining the existing FICO Classic grid. The Motley Fool's account of the September 4 directive says Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were told to accept loans issued in reliance on a Vantage credit score rather than a FICO score. The industry question is no longer whether lenders can switch but whether the downstream chain — mortgage insurers, investors, rating agencies — will price risk in VantageScore terms fast enough to make switching worthwhile.

Fighting price with structure: direct licensing and the Preferred Program
ONGOING HEADWIND

FICO's October 2025 direct-license program lets tri-merge resellers compute the FICO Score themselves, which FICO says halves the average per-score fee by removing bureau mark-ups — $4.95 a score plus a $33 per-borrower, per-score fee only when a FICO-scored loan closes. CEO Will Lansing said it "brings transparency, competition, and cost-efficiency"; the Mortgage Bankers Association's Bob Broeksmit called it "a step in the right direction"; Pulte himself said he appreciated FICO "taking Constructive Criticism." On the November 4 call Lansing said the program is live apart from score computation by the resellers, which still awaits approval from Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. In October FICO added a second track, the Preferred Program: enrolled lenders pay $10 for a bundle of Classic FICO and FICO Score 10T, with no 2027 increase in the wholesale price. The first track's logic is to shift the charge from every inquiry to the loans that actually close. Other deals are still being negotiated customer by customer, and on the call Lansing indicated FICO will stop sharing every detail of its pricing programs.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

FICO trades at 14.6 times its free cash flow after a 70% fall from its peak month-end. The bull framing: this is a high-quality business whose share price fell far more than its earnings have so far; lender inertia, the downstream calibration to FICO points and a strong platform-software business mean earnings fall less than the price did. The bear framing: the price the market paid was always a bet on pricing power, and pricing power is what the regulator just changed; forecasts made before September 28 are out of date, and FICO itself is not offering a new one. The way to decide is not the P/E but two things FICO discusses every quarter: mortgage-origination pricing and mortgage score volume.
