01 Overview

CORRA is the interest rate on overnight loans of Canadian dollars secured by federal government debt. The loans are repurchase agreements, or repos: one side sells securities for cash and buys them back the next day. The Bank of Canada describes it as "Canada's risk-free rate" and has calculated and published it since June 15, 2020, taking over from Refinitiv.

The header rate, 2.30%, was set from $62.3 billion of eligible trades on October 2, 2026, 0.05 pp above the Bank's 2.25% target for the overnight rate.

From CDOR to CORRA

CORRA replaced CDOR, the older Canadian-dollar benchmark: a committed bank lending rate whose determination was "based predominantly on expert judgement," with "only six contributing banks" left on its panel (CARR, the Canadian Alternative Reference Rate working group). Refinitiv Benchmark Services (UK) announced on May 16, 2022 that it would stop publishing all three CDOR tenors after June 28, 2024.

More: the Bank's definition and the CDOR transition

The Bank of Canada: "CORRA measures the cost of overnight general collateral funding in Canadian dollars using Government of Canada treasury bills and bonds as collateral for repurchase transactions."

The banking regulator, OSFI, expected no new CDOR derivatives or securities after June 30, 2023; loans had until June 2024 and could move to Term CORRA, "a forward-looking version of overnight CORRA" based on CORRA futures. CARR has since been wound down.

02 How It's Priced

CORRA is computed from trades, not quoted. Only overnight repos settled the same day, in Canadian dollars, against Government of Canada collateral count. The Bank "orders eligible repos from the lowest rate to the highest rate and then removes the 25 percent of the volume of repos with the lowest rates," then takes the volume-weighted median: "CORRA is a rate at which half of the remaining trading volume is above and half is below it." It is rounded to one basis point (0.01 pp).

More: the minimum volume threshold

Threshold rule (methodology page): "the greater of $3 billion or 30% of a 5-day moving average of previously published trimmed CORRA volumes"; Valet shows it above $3 billion every day since Feb 27, 2026. Below it, a fallback rate applies: the target plus the mean CORRA–target spread of the previous five days.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Built from actual trades Run by the central bank $62.3B traded Oct 2 Price risk ≈ 0

Credit. Each trade is secured by Government of Canada bills or bonds, so the rate carries little bank credit risk. Anchoring. In the 249 publications from October 3, 2025 to October 2, 2026, CORRA was above the target (2.50%, then 2.25% from October 30) on 200 days, equal on 42 and below on 7; the gap ranged from −0.01 to +0.12 pp (our count from Valet).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate cuts pass through overnight. Nothing is locked in: the month-end rate fell from 5.02% (May 2024) to 2.25% (November 2025).
02
Inflation. CPI rose 3.0% in the year to August 2026, 0.70 pp above today's 2.30% before tax (our arithmetic).
03
Day-to-day pressure. On July 31, 2026 CORRA printed 2.37%, 0.12 pp over the target, the widest gap of the past year; by August 5 it was 2.31%.
04
Fallback days. On August 11, 2025 the Bank published a fallback rate, 2.76% (the target plus the previous five days' average spread), instead of a trade-based median: the only such day since October 2021 (Valet).

05 Market & Policy Expectations

CORRA follows the policy rate. On September 2, 2026 the Bank of Canada "held its target for the overnight rate at 2.25%, with the Bank Rate at 2.5% and the deposit rate at 2.20%," and said upside risks to its inflation forecast had increased. The Bank publishes no rate path. Its July 15, 2026 Monetary Policy Report assumes the neutral rate is "at the midpoint of its estimated 2.25% to 3.25% range."

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the policy rate holds or rises: overnight money earns the new rate from the next day, with no price loss to absorb.

When it does poorly

When the policy rate is cut: income drops at once, with no locked-in rate or price gain to offset it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Lower policy rates. The July MPR places neutral at 2.25–3.25%; the target sits at the bottom of that range.
02
Inflation. The July MPR sees CPI averaging 2.5% in 2026, above today's 2.30%.
03
Currency. A Canadian-dollar rate; for other-currency holders, exchange-rate moves add to or subtract from it.
