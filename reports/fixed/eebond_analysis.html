01 Overview

A Series EE savings bond is a loan to the U.S. government, bought from and cashed with Treasury. Savings bonds are "non-marketable, which means they are registered to a specific owner and cannot be bought and sold to other people in the 'secondary market' by brokers and dealers" (TreasuryDirect). Since May 2005 each bond earns a fixed rate for its first 20 years, added monthly and compounded semiannually, all paid when it is cashed or reaches 30 years. TreasuryDirect: "we guarantee that the bond will double in value in 20 years, even if we have to add money at 20 years to make that happen."

The header, 2.90%, is the fixed rate for bonds issued November 1, 2026 to April 30, 2027. At that rate $10,000 grows to $17,786.15 in 20 years; the guarantee lifts it to $20,000, or 3.53% a year (3.50% compounded semiannually).

02 How It's Priced

No auction and no market price: each May 1 and November 1 Treasury sets the rate for the next six months. "To set the interest rate, we take market yields and adjust them to account for components that are unique to savings bonds," such as cashing after one year and deferring tax.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Full faith and credit of the U.S. Doubles by year 20 No market-price risk No state or local income tax Tax deferral up to 30 years

Credit. "Backed by the full faith and credit of the U.S. government" (TreasuryDirect). Moody's rates the U.S. Aa1 (stable) since May 16, 2025. Value path. The fixed rate, not trading, sets the value, so it rises every month whatever market yields do.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The guarantee needs the full 20 years. $10,000 is worth $17,531.94 at 19½ years, $20,000 at 20.
02
Below market yields. The 3.50% doubling rate is 1.81 pp under the 5.31% 20-year Treasury yield.
03
Inflation. A fixed dollar rate; 20-year breakeven inflation 2.40%.
04
Access and size. 12-month lock, 3-month penalty before year 5, $10,000 yearly cap.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

Held the full 20 years while market yields fall below the 3.50% doubling rate: the guaranteed result holds while new bonds pay less. Rising yields cut traded bond prices, not EE values.

When it does poorly

Cashed before year 20 (2.90% a year, less 3 months' interest before year 5); market yields staying above the doubling rate; inflation above the rate earned.

Top 3 Risks

01
Cashing before 20 years. $17,531.94 at 19½ years vs $20,000 at 20, per $10,000.
02
Return given up. 3.50% vs 5.31% on the 20-year Treasury on November 2.
03
Inflation. A fixed dollar return; 2.40% 20-year breakeven inflation on November 2.
Rates Commentary
The guarantee sets the 20-year return
MECHANICS

At 2.90% doubling takes 24.1 years, so for a 20-year holder the guarantee decides the result: the $2,213.85 top-up is 22% of the $10,000 gain.

A wide gap to market yields
COMPARISON

5.31% on the 20-year Treasury vs 3.50% for a 20-year EE holder, before tax. The EE bond adds no price risk, no state or local tax and a choice of when to pay federal tax.
