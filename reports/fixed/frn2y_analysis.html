01 Overview

A Treasury floating rate note (FRN) is a two-year loan to the U.S. government whose rate moves every week. TreasuryDirect: "The interest rate of an FRN is the sum of two components: an index rate and a spread." The index is "tied to the highest accepted discount rate of the most recent 13-week Treasury bill", auctioned weekly; "The spread stays the same for the life of an FRN." Interest is paid every three months.

The header, 4.20%, is the on-the-run note's published accrual rate for October 2, 2026: the September 28 bill auction's high rate of 4.110%, converted to a money-market yield of 4.153%, plus the note's fixed spread of 0.050%. It was 3.80% at the end of August.

02 How It's Priced

The FRN earns a 3-month rate but matures at the 2-year point (both marked). A year ago the 3-month yield (4.02%) was above the 2-year (3.55%); on October 2, 2026 it was below it (4.19% vs 4.83%). Restated on the fixed note's basis (365-day year, semiannual compounding), the FRN's 4.20% is 4.28%, so the fixed two-year note yields about 0.55 pp more.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Falling rates. Income follows the bill rate down within a week.
02
Spread (discount-margin) risk. If buyers later demand 0.10 pp more over the index, the price falls about 0.17%.
03
Inflation risk. 2026 PCE median 3.7% vs a 4.20% pre-tax rate.
04
Fiscal and supply risk. $86B of this one note offered in three auctions since July; the rating fell to Aa1 in May 2025.
04 Key Figures

An FRN has two durations (our calculations). Rate duration is about the time to the next index reset: 4 days, as the October 5 auction sets the index from October 6; convexity is negligible. Spread duration, the price response to a change in the required margin, is close to the time left to maturity.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When short rates rise or stay high: each weekly bill auction resets the income and the price hardly moves. It beats a fixed note when rates rise faster than the curve implied.

When it does poorly

When short rates fall: income drops within a week (0.08% at end-October 2021), with no long duration to gain from falling yields. A wider required margin lowers the price before maturity.

Top 3 Risks

01
Falling short rates. The SEP longer-run median is 3.2%, below the current 4.15% index.
02
Spread risk before maturity. The 0.050% spread is the lowest on a new 2-year FRN since July 2022 (0.037%); a seller faces whatever margin the market requires.
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Moody's cut the U.S. to Aa1 in May 2025; Treasury sells a 2-year FRN every month ($30B new, $28B reopenings in 2026).

The FRN and the bill
POLICY

On October 2 the FRN accrued at 4.20%; the last 13-week auction's investment rate was 4.211% and the 3-month par yield 4.19%. The note holds a bill-like rate for two years without rolling bills.

Floating or fixed for two years
CURVE

The 2-year fixed note yielded 4.83%. To match it, the index would need to average about 4.68% to July 2028 (4.83% restated on the FRN's 360-day, quarterly basis is 4.73%, less the 0.05 spread), against 4.15% today. A year ago the FRN paid 4.06% and the 2-year 3.55%.
