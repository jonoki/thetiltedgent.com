01 Overview

A Treasury floating rate note (FRN) is a two-year loan to the U.S. government whose rate moves every week: an index tied to the most recent 13-week Treasury bill, auctioned weekly, plus a spread that stays the same for the life of the note. Interest is paid every three months.

The header, 4.18%, is the on-the-run note's published accrual rate for October 15, 2026: the October 13 bill auction's high rate of 4.085%, converted to a money-market yield of 4.128%, plus the note's fixed spread of 0.050%.

More: Treasury's definitions

TreasuryDirect: "The interest rate of an FRN is the sum of two components: an index rate and a spread." The index is "tied to the highest accepted discount rate of the most recent 13-week Treasury bill"; "The spread stays the same for the life of an FRN."

02 How It's Priced

The FRN earns a 3-month rate but matures at the 2-year point (both marked). A year ago the 3-month yield (4.03%) was above the 2-year (3.50%); on October 15, 2026 it was below it (4.28% vs 4.77%). Restated on the fixed note's basis (365-day year, semiannual compounding), the FRN's 4.18% is 4.26%, so the fixed two-year note yields about 0.51 pp more.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Falling rates. Income follows the bill rate down within a week.
02

Fiscal and supply risk. $86B of this one note offered in three auctions since July.

More: how the durations are calculated

y = 0.04178, T = 5/365 = 0.01370. Rate duration D = T/(1 + y·T) = 0.0137; C = 2T²/(1 + y·T)² = 0.00037. Spread duration: modified duration of the eight remaining quarterly payments (Oct 31, 2026 → Jul 31, 2028) discounted at 4.178%, quarterly compounding = 1.71. Breakeven = nominal − real 10-year par yield.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When short rates rise or stay high: each weekly bill auction resets the income and the price hardly moves. It beats a fixed note when rates rise faster than the curve implied.

When it does poorly

When short rates fall: income drops within a week (0.08% at end-October 2021), with no long duration to gain from falling yields. A wider required margin lowers the price before maturity.

Top 3 Risks

01
Falling short rates. The SEP longer-run median is 3.2%, below the current 4.13% index.
02
Spread risk before maturity. The 0.050% spread is the lowest on a new 2-year FRN since July 2022 (0.037%); a seller faces whatever margin the market requires.
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Moody's cut the U.S. to Aa1 in May 2025; Treasury sells a 2-year FRN every month ($30B new, $28B reopenings in 2026).
