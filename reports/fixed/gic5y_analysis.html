01 Overview

A guaranteed investment certificate is, in the words of the Ontario Securities Commission (OSC), "a kind of deposit": you lend a bank or other deposit-taker money for a set term and get it back, with interest, at the end. This page follows the Bank of Canada's weekly 5-year non-redeemable GIC rate, "the most typical" rate posted by the six largest chartered banks. At 2.75% for the week of October 28, 2026, it is unchanged from a year earlier (2.75%, Oct 29, 2025) and from end-September.

02 How It's Priced

A GIC has no market price: each bank sets and posts its own rates, and the OSC suggests savers "ask if you can get a better rate than the one posted." The posted rates sit below Government of Canada yields at every term shown, by 0.76 pp at five years. The OSC notes that "in general, the longer the term, the higher the interest rate you will earn," but on October 28 the posted 3-year rate (3.20%) was above the 5-year (2.75%).

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Principal back at maturity Rate fixed for 5 years CDIC: $100,000 per category No purchase fee

Credit. The promise is the issuer's, backed at CDIC member institutions by deposit insurance in nine separate categories (one name, joint, trust, and each of RRSP, RRIF, TFSA, RDSP, RESP and FHSA). The CDIC says "No one has ever lost a dollar insured by CDIC." Certainty. At 2.75% compounded yearly, $10,000 becomes $11,452.73 after five years (our arithmetic).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Locked in. Cashing a regular GIC early can mean a penalty, and per the OSC "you may not earn any interest on your money." Cashable GICs have no penalty but, per the OSC, a lower rate.
02
Inflation. CPI rose 3.1% in the year to September; 2.75% minus 3.1% is a real rate of about −0.35 pp.
03
Reinvestment. A GIC bought at the 4.90% posted in late November 2023 matures in 2028 at whatever rate is posted then.
04
Coverage limits. Principal and interest together count toward the $100,000; amounts above it in one category at one member are uninsured.

How we calculated these

Our calculation for a compound GIC paying everything at maturity: one cash flow at T = 5 years. Rate y = 0.0275, yearly compounding. PV = CF/(1 + y)T. Macaulay = T = 5.00. Modified D = T/(1 + y) = 4.87. Convexity C = T(T + 1)/(1 + y)² = 28.4. Full repricing: [(1 + y)/(1 + y + Δy)]5 − 1. The Bank of Canada series does not state a compounding convention.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When rates fall after purchase: the locked-in rate beats what new deposits earn, about +5.0% in value per 1-point drop. Also when inflation runs below the rate.

When it does poorly

When rates rise after purchase: the money stays at the old rate, about −4.7% in value per point. Also when inflation runs above the rate, or cash is needed early.

Top 3 Risks

01
Inflation. The Bank expects CPI inflation "to average 3% in 2027"; a fixed 2.75% loses ground if CPI stays near 3%.
02
Rising rates. −13.4% in economic value at +3 points on full repricing.
03
Liquidity. Early access means a penalty or forgone interest.
