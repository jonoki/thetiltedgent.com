01 Overview

A Government of Canada bond is a loan to the federal government in Canadian dollars. The Bank of Canada's daily 10-year benchmark yield is the "mid-market closing" yield of one selected issue: since June 23, 2026, the 3.25% bond due June 1, 2036. At 3.83% on September 22, 2026, it is 0.64 pp above a year earlier and 0.10 pp above end-August.

At auction
Through a dealer
The Bank "sells the securities at auction to financial market distributors and dealers." Customers bid through a distributor, with a Bank-issued bidder ID and a $100,000 minimum bid; "natural persons are ineligible to be bidders" (Standard Terms for Auctions).

Auction and coupon mechanics

A new bond's coupon is "set to the nearest one-quarter of one percent below the average yield of the accepted competitive bids": the benchmark's first auction averaged 3.463%, so its coupon is 3.25%. At reopenings winners pay "the price equivalents of the accepted bid yields." Eight auctions took it to $41.0B. Since July the 10-year sold at auction is the 3.50% due December 1, 2036; the Bank changes its benchmark "typically after the last auction for that bond."

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall: about +8.5% per 1-point drop, plus the coupon. That tends to follow inflation easing toward target or weaker growth. Held to June 2036, it repays face value whatever the price does.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: about −7.7% per point, more than two years of coupons. That tends to follow inflation staying above target, a higher policy path, or rising global long yields.

Top 3 Risks

01
Higher yields. −21.2% at +3 points on full repricing.
02
Sticky inflation. The Bank: CPI "has been hovering around 3% in recent months."
03
Global spillover. U.S. 10-year par yield: 4.96% on Sep 22, 2026.
