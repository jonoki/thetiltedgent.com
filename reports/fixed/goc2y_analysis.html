01 Overview

A Government of Canada bond is a loan to the federal government in Canadian dollars. The Bank of Canada's daily 2-year benchmark yield is the yield of one selected issue: since October 23, 2026, the 3.00% bond due November 1, 2028. Two years is the shortest term in the government's regular bond auctions. At 3.28% on October 30, 2026, it is 0.86 pp above a year earlier and 0.09 pp below end-September.

02 How It's Priced

A 2-year yield roughly reflects the overnight rate investors expect over two years, plus a small term premium. Over the year the 3-month bill rose 0.30 pp (2.23% → 2.53%); the 1-year rose 0.72 pp, the 2-year 0.86 pp, the 3-year 0.95 pp. The policy rate rose from 2.25% to 2.50%.

Auction and coupon mechanics

A new bond's coupon is "set to the nearest one-quarter of one percent below the average yield of the accepted competitive bids": the benchmark's first auction averaged 3.023%, so its coupon is 3.00%. At reopenings winners pay "the price equivalents of the accepted bid yields." Five $5.5B auctions took the benchmark to $27.5B, inside the $26–34B target size for 2-year benchmarks. From November 12 the 2-year sold at auction is a new bond due February 1, 2029; the Bank changes its benchmark "typically after the last auction for that bond."

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. A 1-point rise in yield cuts the price about 1.9%.
02
Policy surprises. The 2-year reacts quickly to the Bank's decisions: +0.82 pp in March 2022, when hikes began.
03
Reinvestment. Principal comes back in November 2028 and must be reinvested at whatever yields are then.

Inflation and supply. CPI rose 3.1% in the year to September; three more 2-year auctions are scheduled this year.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall: about +1.9% per 1-point drop, plus the coupon, as with rate cuts or weaker growth and inflation. Held to November 2028, it repays face value.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: about −1.9% per point, less than one year of coupons, as with inflation above target or a higher expected policy path.

Top 3 Risks

01
Higher yields. −5.56% at +3 points on full repricing.
02
Sticky inflation. The Bank: "CPI inflation has been around 3% since April and is expected to rise to about 3½% by the end of the year."
03
Reinvestment. Cuts before 2028 lower what the returned money earns.
