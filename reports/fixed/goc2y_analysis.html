01 Overview

A Government of Canada bond is a loan to the federal government in Canadian dollars. The Bank of Canada's daily 2-year benchmark yield is the yield of one selected issue: since August 6, 2026, the 2.75% bond due August 1, 2028. Two years is the shortest term in the government's regular bond auctions. At 3.25% on October 2, 2026, it is 0.80 pp above a year earlier and 0.24 pp above end-August.

02 How It's Priced

A 2-year yield roughly reflects the overnight rate investors expect over two years, plus a small term premium. Over the year the 3-month bill barely moved (2.42% → 2.39%); the 1-year rose 0.57 pp, the 2-year 0.80 pp, the 3-year 0.89 pp. The policy rate fell from 2.50% to 2.25%.

Auction and coupon mechanics

A new bond's coupon is "set to the nearest one-quarter of one percent below the average yield of the accepted competitive bids": the benchmark's first auction averaged 2.954%, so its coupon is 2.75%. At reopenings winners pay "the price equivalents of the accepted bid yields." Five $5.5B auctions took the benchmark to $27.5B, inside the $26–34B target size for 2-year benchmarks. Since August 19 the 2-year sold at auction is the 3.00% due November 1, 2028; the Bank changes its benchmark "typically after the last auction for that bond."

Key Vulnerabilities

Rate risk. A 1-point rise in yield cuts the price about 1.7%.
02
Policy surprises. The 2-year reacts quickly to the Bank's decisions: +0.82 pp in March 2022, when hikes began.
03
Reinvestment. Principal comes back in August 2028 and must be reinvested at whatever yields are then.

Inflation and supply. CPI rose 3.0% in the year to August; five more 2-year auctions are scheduled this year.

05 Market & Policy Expectations

On September 2, 2026 the Bank of Canada "held its target for the overnight rate at 2.25%," unchanged since October 2025, adding that "upside risks to inflation have increased, while new tariffs make growth prospects more uncertain." Its July 15, 2026 Monetary Policy Report assumes a neutral rate "at the midpoint of its estimated 2.25% to 3.25% range" and sees inflation returning "to around 2% in early 2027."

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall: about +1.8% per 1-point drop, plus the coupon, as with rate cuts or weaker growth and inflation. Held to August 2028, it repays face value.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: about −1.7% per point, less than one year of coupons, as with inflation above target or a higher expected policy path.

Top 3 Risks

01
Higher yields. −5.09% at +3 points on full repricing.
02
Sticky inflation. The Bank: CPI "has been hovering around 3% in recent months."
03
Reinvestment. Cuts before 2028 lower what the returned money earns.
