01 Overview

A Government of Canada bond is a loan to the federal government in Canadian dollars. The Bank of Canada's daily long-term benchmark yield is the mid-market closing yield of one selected issue: since March 6, 2026, the 3.50% bond due December 1, 2057, about 31 years from now. At 4.28% on October 2, 2026, it is 0.62 pp above a year earlier and 0.02 pp below end-September.

At auction

The Bank of Canada auctions the bonds to government securities distributors, who also bid for customers; each bid is at least $100,000 and "Natural persons are ineligible to be bidders" (Standard Terms for Auctions).

Auction and coupon mechanics

For a new bond "the coupon rate will be set to the nearest one-quarter of one percent below the average yield of the accepted competitive bids": the benchmark's first auction averaged 3.503%, so its coupon is 3.50%. At reopenings winners pay "the price equivalents of the accepted bid yields." Twelve auctions took it to $35.0B. Since July the 30-year sold at auction is the 3.75% due June 1, 2059; the Bank changes its 30-year benchmark "typically after the last auction for that bond."

Credit. Payments are "a direct charge on, and payable out of, the Consolidated Revenue Fund of Canada" (Standard Terms). Morningstar DBRS confirmed the Government of Canada at AAA, Stable trends, on August 29, 2026. Liquidity. The benchmark's twelve auctions drew $2.03–$2.71 of bids per $1 sold.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. A 1-point rise in yield cuts the price about 15.6%, about four and a half years of coupons.
02
Inflation. The coupon is fixed for 31 years; CPI rose 3.0% in the year to August.
03
Reinvestment. Coupons reinvested below 4.28% lower the realised return.
04
Supply. Two 30-year auctions in July and September put $6.0B of the new June 2059 bond in the market; two more are scheduled this quarter.

05 Market & Policy Expectations

On September 2, 2026 the Bank of Canada "held its target for the overnight rate at 2.25%," unchanged since October 2025, noting that "Long-term bond yields have moved up globally, including in Canada." The Bank publishes no rate path. Its July 15, 2026 Monetary Policy Report assumes a nominal neutral rate "at the midpoint of its estimated 2.25% to 3.25% range" and sees inflation returning "to around 2% in early 2027."

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When long yields fall: about +20% per 1-point drop, plus the coupon, as when inflation eases or growth weakens. Held to December 2057, it repays face value.

When it does poorly

When long yields rise: about −15.6% per point, as when inflation stays above target or global long yields climb.

Top 3 Risks

01
Higher yields. −37.5% at +3 points on full repricing.
02
Sticky inflation. The Bank: "CPI inflation has been hovering around 3% in recent months."
03
Global spillover. U.S. 30-year par yield: 5.63% on Oct 2, 2026.
Rates Commentary
The long end versus the policy rate
CURVE

The policy rate is an overnight rate; this bond runs 31 years. From June 2024 to October 2025 the Bank cut 2.75 pp while the long bond's month-end yield rose 0.19 pp; it now sits 2.03 pp above the 2.25% policy rate.
