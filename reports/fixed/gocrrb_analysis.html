01 Overview

A Real Return Bond (RRB) is a federal loan whose payments move with the Consumer Price Index for Canada: coupons are paid every six months on inflation-adjusted principal, and at maturity the holder gets principal plus Inflation Compensation "(whether positive or negative)". Unlike U.S. TIPS, there is no par floor if prices fall.

The header, 1.97%, is the Bank of Canada's long-term RRB yield for October 2, 2026, on the 0.50% bond due December 1, 2050 (its benchmark since June 1, 2020). With the long nominal benchmark at 4.28%, the rough breakeven inflation was 2.31%.

No new bonds. On November 3, 2022 the Department of Finance wrote: "The government has decided to cease issuance of Real Return Bonds (RRBs) effective immediately," adding that the decision "reflects low demand for this product." The last auction (September 1, 2022) sold $300M of the 2054 bond.

Index ratio and coupons

Payments are scaled by an index ratio. The reference CPI for the first day of a month "is the CPI for the third preceding calendar month," interpolated daily. On October 2 the 2050 bond's ratio was 1.30723: $1,000 of principal stood at $1,307.23, and each coupon is half the annual rate on that amount.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Direct federal obligation Principal and coupons indexed to CPI Real yield fixed at purchase No new supply since 2022

Credit. The offering terms call RRBs "direct, unsecured, unconditional obligations" of the Crown. Morningstar DBRS: Canada AAA, Stable trends (July 22, 2026 release). Inflation. CPI rose 3.0% in the year to August; the 2050 bond's index ratio rose 3.0% in the year to October 2.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Real-rate risk. A 1-point rise in the real yield cuts the price about 19.7%.
02
No deflation floor. Falling CPI lowers the principal repaid, even below the original amount.
03
Liquidity. The terms warn RRBs "have been and may continue to be less liquid than conventional Government of Canada issues"; with issuance ended, supply only shrinks.
04
Tax before cash. The inflation uplift is taxed as interest each year; the principal arrives at maturity.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When real yields fall (about +25% for a 1-point drop), and when CPI inflation beats the 2.31% breakeven: principal and coupons rise with the index.

When it does poorly

When real yields rise (about −20% for a 1-point rise), and when inflation runs below the breakeven or CPI falls: there is no par floor at maturity.

Top 3 Risks

01
Higher real yields. −47.6% at +3 pp on full repricing.
02
Inflation below the breakeven. A long nominal bond then earns more.
03
A shrinking market. No new issues; the 2026 series matures in December.
Rates Commentary
The rise is mostly in the breakeven
