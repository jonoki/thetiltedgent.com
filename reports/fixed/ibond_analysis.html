01 Overview

A Series I savings bond is a loan to the U.S. Treasury whose rate follows inflation. TreasuryDirect: "Non-marketable: cannot be bought or sold in secondary securities market"; it is cashed back with the Treasury at its accrued value. The rate has two parts: a fixed rate set at purchase ("The fixed rate never changes") and an inflation rate reset every May 1 and November 1 from the non-seasonally adjusted CPI-U.

The header, 4.26%, is the composite rate for I bonds issued May 1 to October 31, 2026: a 0.90% fixed rate plus a 1.67% semiannual inflation rate. Interest is added every six months and paid when the bond is cashed or reaches 30 years.

02 How It's Priced

No auction, no market price. TreasuryDirect's formula: composite = [Fixed rate + (2 x semiannual inflation rate) + (fixed rate x semiannual inflation rate)]. For the current issue: 0.0090 + 2 × 0.0167 + 0.0090 × 0.0167 = 0.04255, rounded to 4.26%. If deflation would push the composite below zero, TreasuryDirect: "We stop at zero."

The 1.67% is the CPI-U change from September 2025 (324.800) to March 2026 (330.213), the six-month window Treasury's savings bond rules set for the May rate (31 CFR 359.11); the November rate uses March to September. On October 2 the composite sat between the 3-month (4.19%) and 6-month (4.27%) bill yields; the 0.90% fixed rate sat below the 2.69% five-year TIPS real yield.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Issued by the U.S. Treasury Moody's Aa1 (stable) Rate resets with CPI-U No market price to fall No state or local income tax

Credit. Moody's: "Moody's Ratings downgrades United States ratings to Aa1 from Aaa; changes outlook to stable" (May 16, 2025). S&P and Fitch: n/v. Value. Cashed at principal plus accrued interest, so rising market yields do not lower it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Lock-up and penalty. TreasuryDirect: "You can cash in (redeem) your I bond after 12 months. However, if you cash in the bond in less than 5 years, you lose the last 3 months of interest."
02
Low fixed rate. 0.90% for life, against 2.69% on five-year TIPS (October 2).
03
Inflation rate falls when CPI cools. New-issue composite: 9.62% (May 2022) to 3.11% (November 2024). Deflation can pull it below the fixed rate.
04
Size cap. $10,000 a year per SSN or EIN; gifts count against the recipient's limit.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When CPI-U rises faster: the inflation part resets higher for every I bond outstanding. When market yields jump: its value does not fall.

When it does poorly

When inflation cools: the composite resets lower, below the fixed rate in deflation. When real yields elsewhere stay above the 0.90% fixed rate. When cash is needed within 12 months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Lower inflation resets. From 9.62% to 3.11% for new issues in two and a half years.
02
Liquidity. 12-month lock; 3 months of interest lost before 5 years ($109.34 on $10,000 cashed at 12 months, our calculation).
03
Low fixed rate. The 0.90% stays for 30 years, whatever real yields do later.
Rates Commentary
Two real rates, one gap
COMPARISON

Both pay a real rate plus CPI-U: on October 2, 0.90% for the I bond, 2.69% for five-year TIPS. The I bond gives up the gap for a value that does not fall, a zero floor and tax deferral.

The rate looks back six months
MECHANICS

The 4.26% reflects CPI-U from September 2025 to March 2026. March to August 2026 added 1.44%; September's reading (October 14) completes the six months behind the November 1 reset.
