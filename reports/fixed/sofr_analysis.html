01 Overview

The New York Fed: "The Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) is a broad measure of the cost of borrowing cash overnight collateralized by Treasury securities." The borrowing happens in the repo market: a dealer or fund hands over Treasuries, receives cash, and repays it the next day with interest.

The Alternative Reference Rates Committee (ARRC) calls it "the dominant U.S. dollar interest rate benchmark". You cannot buy it directly; savers earn something close to it through funds (Section 05). Tax depends on the vehicle: on Treasury floating-rate notes held directly, federal tax is due each year on the interest, with no state or local tax (TreasuryDirect).

From LIBOR to SOFR

SOFR replaced LIBOR. The ARRC: "LIBOR was not anchored in actual market activity, which left it vulnerable to manipulation." The ARRC, formed in 2014, selected SOFR in 2017, and all USD LIBOR panels ended on June 30, 2023.

More: the switch from LIBOR, step by step

CME Group launched SOFR futures on May 7, 2018. U.S. banking regulators told the banks they supervise to stop new USD LIBOR use as of December 31, 2021. For contracts with no workable fallback, the Adjustable Interest Rate (LIBOR) Act and a Fed rule (December 16, 2022) substituted SOFR-based rates. The ARRC also formally recommended CME's Term SOFR rates (July 29, 2021), with a limited scope of use.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Built from ~$3 trillion of daily trades Treasury collateral Resets every business day Price moves ~0.003% per 1 pp

Credit and liquidity. Each trade is secured by Treasuries; volume behind the rate rose from $928B (October 1, 2021) to $3,013B (five-year high: $3,508B on January 2, 2026). A saver's exposure is to the fund holding the cash.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reinvestment risk. Income resets daily: SOFR ran from a 0.03% daily low (October 19–21, 2021) to a 5.40% daily high (December 28, 2023; January 2 and July 1, 2024).
02
Month-end and funding squeezes. SOFR printed above the previous business day on 42 of the last 60 month-ends. It closed above the top of the Fed's target range on 16 business days from October 15 to December 31, 2025.
03
Access through a wrapper. Savers earn SOFR only through funds, net of their fees, and a money market fund "is not guaranteed by the FDIC like bank accounts are" (SEC, investor.gov).

More: how we calculated it, and sources

Our calculation, at 3.88% for one day. T = 1/365, y = 0.0388. Modified D = T/(1 + y·T) = 0.00274. C = 2T²/(1 + y·T)² = 0.000015. ΔP/P ≈ −D·Δy + ½·C·Δy². Income change = $10,000 × Δy, one year, before compounding.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the policy rate rises or stays high: income follows rates up the next business day, with no price loss.

When it does poorly

When rates fall: income drops with each cut (0.05% at end-February 2022), with no duration to gain from falling yields.

Top 3 Risks

01
Falling rates. The SEP longer-run median is 3.2%, below today's 3.88%.
02
Inflation. The Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%, close to SOFR before tax and fees.
03
Funding stress and fund wrappers. Month-end jumps, fund fees and no FDIC guarantee on money market funds (Section 03).
