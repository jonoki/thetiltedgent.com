01 Overview

A Treasury Inflation-Protected Security (TIPS) is a loan to the U.S. government whose principal moves with consumer prices. TreasuryDirect: "Unlike other Treasury securities, where the principal is fixed, the principal of a TIPS can go up or down over its term." The index is the CPI-U. The fixed coupon is paid on the adjusted principal, and at maturity: "you get either the increased (inflation-adjusted) price or the original principal, whichever is greater. You never get less than the original principal."

The header, 2.76%, is Treasury's 10-year par real yield for September 23, 2026, the highest since November 25, 2008 (2.79%). With the nominal 10-year at 5.11%, breakeven inflation was 2.35%.

Auction, coupon and index ratio

In a single-price auction every winner pays the price set by the highest accepted real yield. The coupon is set at the first auction ("never less than 0.125%"); reopenings sell more of the same note. Price paid = real price × index ratio. On September 23 the ratio was 0.99987 (333.92707 ÷ 333.96974), so $1,000 of principal stood at $999.87.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Real-rate risk. A 1-point rise in the real yield cuts the price about 8.2% (duration 8.63).
02
Low or falling CPI. Principal falls with deflation; the floor applies only at maturity. The index ratio was below 1 on September 23.
03
Tax on principal growth. Principal increases can be taxed in the year they occur; the cash comes at maturity or sale.
04
Supply. $99B of 10-year TIPS offered in 2026 to September.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When real yields fall (about +9.1% for a 1-point drop), and when CPI-U inflation runs above the 2.35% breakeven: principal and coupons rise with the index while a nominal note's stay fixed.

When it does poorly

When real yields rise (about −8.2% for a 1-point rise), and when inflation runs below the breakeven or CPI falls: principal is marked down, and the par floor applies only at maturity.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further rises in real yields. −22.4% at +3 pp (full repricing).
02
Inflation below the 2.35% breakeven. The nominal note then earns more.
03
Tax before cash. Principal increases can be taxed yearly; the cash comes later.
Rates Commentary
The rise is in the real component
