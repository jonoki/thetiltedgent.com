01 Overview

A Treasury Inflation-Protected Security (TIPS) is a loan to the U.S. government whose principal moves with consumer prices. TreasuryDirect: "Unlike other Treasury securities, where the principal is fixed, the principal of a TIPS can go up or down over its term." The index is the CPI-U. The fixed coupon is paid on the adjusted principal, and at maturity: "you get either the increased (inflation-adjusted) price or the original principal, whichever is greater. You never get less than the original principal." The 5-year is the shortest TIPS Treasury sells; the others are 10 and 30 years.

The header, 2.69%, is Treasury's 5-year par real yield for October 2, 2026. On September 28 and 30 it closed at 2.73%, the highest since November 28, 2008. With the nominal 5-year at 5.06%, 5-year breakeven inflation was 2.37%.

Auction, coupon and index ratio

In a single-price auction every winner pays the price set by the highest accepted real yield. The coupon is set at the first auction ("never less than 0.125%"); reopenings sell more of the same note. Price paid = real price × index ratio. On October 2 the ratio was 1.02450 (333.95226 ÷ 325.96740), so $1,000 of principal stood at $1,024.50.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Direct U.S. Treasury obligation Moody's Aa1 (stable) Principal indexed to CPI-U Par floor at maturity No state or local income tax

Credit. Moody's cut the United States to Aa1 from Aaa on May 16, 2025, with a stable outlook. S&P and Fitch: n/v. Demand. The June 18 reopening drew $62.63B of bids for $24.0B; April's new issue $66.81B for $26.0B.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Real-rate risk. A 1-point rise in the real yield cuts the price about 4.2% (duration 4.33).
02
Low or falling CPI. Principal falls with deflation; the floor applies only at maturity, and only to the original principal.
03
Tax on principal growth. Principal increases can be taxed in the year they occur; the cash comes at maturity or sale.
04
Supply. $50B of 5-year TIPS offered in 2026 to September; a new issue (Oct 22) and a reopening (Dec 22) are scheduled.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When real yields fall (about +4.4% for a 1-point drop), and when CPI-U inflation runs above the 2.37% breakeven: principal and coupons rise with the index while a nominal note's stay fixed.

When it does poorly

When real yields rise (about −4.2% for a 1-point rise), and when inflation runs below the breakeven or CPI falls: principal is marked down, and the par floor applies only at maturity.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further rises in real yields. −12.07% at +3 pp (full repricing).
02
Inflation below the 2.37% breakeven. The nominal 5-year note then earns more.
03
Tax before cash. Principal increases can be taxed yearly; the cash comes later.
Rates Commentary
