01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. The 10-year note is the longest note Treasury sells; longer maturities are bonds.

The header's 5.11% is Treasury's official 10-year par yield for September 23, 2026: the highest since July 13, 2007.

More: method, steps

TreasuryDirect describes notes as securities sold "for a term of 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years" that "pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature."

Treasury builds the curve from "indicative, bid-side market price quotations (not actual transactions) for the most recently auctioned securities obtained by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York at or near 3:30 PM each trading day."

July 13, 2007 was also 5.11%; the last higher reading was 5.13% on July 12, 2007. The rise came in steps: 4.75% at the end of August, 5.01% on September 16 (the day the Federal Reserve raised rates), 4.96% on September 22, then +0.15 pp to 5.11% on September 23.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities

Strengths:

Direct U.S. Treasury obligation Moody's Aa1 (stable) Fixed coupon, known end date No state or local income tax $100 minimum at auction $105.8B bid for $39B (Sep 9) Official daily yield

Credit quality. Moody's cut the U.S. from Aaa to Aa1 on May 16, 2025. Liquidity. Measured by auction demand (bid-to-cover, Section 02).

More: Moody's wording

Moody's, May 16, 2025: "Moody's Ratings (Moody's) has downgraded the Government of United States of America's (US) long-term issuer and senior unsecured ratings to Aa1 from Aaa and changed the outlook to stable from negative."

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. With a modified duration of 7.77, a 1-point rise in yield cuts the price about 7.4%: why the current issue fell from 99.54 at the August auction to 98.36 at the September reopening.
02
Inflation risk. The coupon is fixed in dollars: if inflation runs above the 10-year breakeven inflation rate (2.35%), the note trails the same-maturity TIPS.
03
Reinvestment risk. The 5.11% yield assumes coupons are reinvested at 5.11%; if rates fall, the realised return ends up below it.
04
Fiscal and supply risk. Treasury is selling $39–42B of this one maturity every month in 2026; what buyers demand for it shows up in auction high yields and bid-to-cover ratios.

More: formulas

The par yield stands in for the note's own market yield (an approximation). Yields are Treasury's par curves for each date; the real yield is from Treasury's daily par real yield curve. FRED T10YIE also shows 2.35% breakeven for Sep 23, 2026. Settlement is Sep 24, 2026 (T+1). The 52-week range uses the daily 10-year par yield, Sep 24, 2025 – Sep 23, 2026; its high is the banner date. Moody's action of May 16, 2025.

Formulas (our calculation, semi-annual coupons, actual/actual accrual): y = 0.0511, c = 2.3125 per 100 each half-year, settlement Sep 24, 2026. Accrued fraction a = 40/184 days (Aug 15 → Sep 24 of the Aug 15 → Feb 15 period); first cash flow in w = 144/184 = 0.7826 half-years, then 19 more (n = 20, last one 102.3125 on Aug 15, 2036). tk = w + k half-years; PVk = CFk / (1 + y/2)tk; Pfull = ΣPVk = 96.77. Macaulay D = Σ tk·PVk / P / 2 = 7.97 years. Modified D = Macaulay / (1 + y/2) = 7.77. Convexity C = Σ PVk·tk(tk+1) / [P·(1 + y/2)²·4] = 73.0. Price change ΔP/P ≈ −D·Δy + ½·C·Δy², with Δy = −0.01 and +0.01 for the two rows above; full repricing at 4.11% gives +8.14% and at 6.11% −7.41%. Clean price = 96.77 − 2.3125 × 40/184 = 96.27 (full price 96.77 less accrued 0.50).

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall: about +8.1% per 1-point drop, plus the 4.625% coupon. Typically when inflation comes in below expectations, the Fed's projected path moves lower, or investors move out of riskier assets into Treasuries. Held to August 2036, a buyer at 5.11% gets the coupons and face value whatever the price does.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: about −7.4% per 1-point rise, more than a year and a half of coupon income. Typically when inflation surprises upward, the policy path is revised higher, or buyers ask more to absorb new supply.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further rises in yields. On full repricing, a 3-point rise cuts the price 20.3%.
02
Inflation above the breakeven. 2.35% priced over ten years; the Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7% (core 3.4%).
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Weaker auctions would push yields up across outstanding notes.
Rates Commentary
Splitting the yield: real return plus expected inflation
DECOMPOSITION

The whole 0.99-point rise over the year is in the real yield (1.75% → 2.76%, +1.01), not in breakeven inflation (2.37% → 2.35%, −0.02).

Curve shape: the front end moved most
CURVE

2s10s narrowed from 0.59 to 0.26 points; 10-year minus 3-month widened from 0.12 to 0.92. Two-year yields track the expected policy path most closely, and the September SEP raised that path at every horizon it shares with June.

Supply and auctions
MECHANICS

Every 2026 10-year auction on the TreasuryDirect record was $42B for new notes and $39B for reopenings. The November 10 new issue will carry a coupon set near the yields prevailing then.

More: notes

The Fed's 2026 PCE median (3.7%) is well above the 10-year breakeven (2.35%), though the two cover different horizons: one year against ten.

A weaker auction (Top 3 Risks, 03): lower bid-to-cover, a high yield above pre-auction levels.
