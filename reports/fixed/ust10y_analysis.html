01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. The 10-year note is the longest note Treasury sells; longer maturities are bonds.

The header's 4.91% is Treasury's official 10-year par yield for November 2, 2026, down from 5.31% on October 5, 2026, the highest daily reading of the past 52 weeks.

More: method, steps

TreasuryDirect describes notes as securities sold "for a term of 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years" that "pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature."

Treasury builds the curve from "indicative, bid-side market price quotations (not actual transactions) for the most recently auctioned securities obtained by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York at or near 3:30 PM each trading day."

The path since the last edition: 4.75% at the end of August, 5.01% on September 16 (the day the Federal Reserve raised rates), 5.11% on September 23, 5.29% at the end of September, a high of 5.31% on October 5, 4.94% at the end of October, then 4.91% on November 2.

More: Moody's wording

Moody's, May 16, 2025: "Moody's Ratings (Moody's) has downgraded the Government of United States of America's (US) long-term issuer and senior unsecured ratings to Aa1 from Aaa and changed the outlook to stable from negative."

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. With a modified duration of 7.69, a 1-point rise in yield cuts the price about 7.3%: why the current issue fell from 99.54 at the August auction to 98.36 at the September reopening and 94.86 at the October reopening.
02
Inflation risk. The coupon is fixed in dollars: if inflation runs above the 10-year breakeven inflation rate (2.29%), the note trails the same-maturity TIPS.
03
Reinvestment risk. The 4.91% yield assumes coupons are reinvested at 4.91%; if rates fall, the realised return ends up below it.
04
Fiscal and supply risk. Treasury is selling $39–42B of this one maturity every month in 2026; what buyers demand for it shows up in auction high yields and bid-to-cover ratios.

More: formulas

The par yield stands in for the note's own market yield (an approximation). Yields are Treasury's par curves for each date; the real yield is from Treasury's daily par real yield curve. Settlement is Nov 3, 2026 (T+1). The 52-week range uses the daily 10-year par yield, Nov 3, 2025 – Nov 2, 2026; its high is Oct 5, 2026. Moody's action of May 16, 2025.

Formulas (our calculation, semi-annual coupons, actual/actual accrual): y = 0.0491, c = 2.3125 per 100 each half-year, settlement Nov 3, 2026. Accrued fraction a = 80/184 days (Aug 15 → Nov 3 of the Aug 15 → Feb 15 period); first cash flow in w = 104/184 = 0.5652 half-years, then 19 more (n = 20, last one 102.3125 on Aug 15, 2036). tk = w + k half-years; PVk = CFk / (1 + y/2)tk; Pfull = ΣPVk = 98.81. Macaulay D = Σ tk·PVk / P / 2 = 7.87 years. Modified D = Macaulay / (1 + y/2) = 7.69. Convexity C = Σ PVk·tk(tk+1) / [P·(1 + y/2)²·4] = 71.6. Price change ΔP/P ≈ −D·Δy + ½·C·Δy², with Δy = −0.01 and +0.01 for the two rows above; full repricing at 3.91% gives +8.06% and at 5.91% −7.34%. Clean price = 98.81 − 2.3125 × 80/184 = 97.80 (full price 98.81 less accrued 1.01).

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall: about +8% per 1-point drop, plus the 4.625% coupon. Typically when inflation comes in below expectations, the Fed's projected path moves lower, or investors move out of riskier assets into Treasuries. Held to August 2036, a buyer at 4.91% gets the coupons and face value whatever the price does.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: about −7.3% per 1-point rise, more than a year and a half of coupon income. Typically when inflation surprises upward, the policy path is revised higher, or buyers ask more to absorb new supply.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further rises in yields. On full repricing, a 3-point rise cuts the price 20.1%.
02
Inflation above the breakeven. 2.29% priced over ten years; the Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7% (core 3.4%).
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Weaker auctions would push yields up across outstanding notes.

Supply and auctions
MECHANICS

Every 2026 10-year auction on the TreasuryDirect record was $42B for new notes and $39B for reopenings. The November 10 new issue will carry a coupon set near the yields prevailing then.

More: notes

A weaker auction (Top 3 Risks, 03): lower bid-to-cover, a high yield above pre-auction levels.
