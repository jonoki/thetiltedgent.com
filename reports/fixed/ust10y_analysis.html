01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. TreasuryDirect describes notes as securities sold "for a term of 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years" that "pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature." The rate is fixed at auction and does not change over the life of the note. The 10-year note is the longest note Treasury sells; longer maturities are bonds.

The yield in the header, 5.11%, is Treasury's official 10-year par yield for September 23, 2026. Treasury builds the curve from "indicative, bid-side market price quotations (not actual transactions) for the most recently auctioned securities obtained by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York at or near 3:30 PM each trading day." It is the highest 10-year reading in Treasury's daily par yield history since July 13, 2007 (also 5.11%); the last higher reading was 5.13% on July 12, 2007. The rise came in steps: 4.75% at the end of August, 5.01% on September 16 (the day the Federal Reserve raised rates), 4.96% on September 22, then +0.15 pp to 5.11% on September 23.

How an individual buys it

At auction
$100 minimum
Through TreasuryDirect or a bank/broker. Minimum purchase $100, in increments of $100. A non-competitive bid is capped at $10 million; a competitive bid at 35% of the offering (TreasuryDirect). New 10-year notes are auctioned in February, May, August and November, with reopenings 8 times a year.
Secondary market
Via a broker
Notes already issued can be bought or sold through a brokerage account at the market price. TreasuryDirect: "You can hold a note until it matures or sell it before it matures."
Through an ETF
UTEN · IEF · SPTI
UTEN holds the most recently issued 10-year note; IEF holds 7–10-year Treasuries; SPTI holds 3–10-year Treasuries. Fees and sizes are in Section 05.
Tax
Federal only
TreasuryDirect: "Federal tax due each year on interest earned. No state or local taxes." ETF distributions follow the fund's own tax reporting; this report does not cover that.
Issuer & Market Structure
U.S. Treasury
Auctions are run by Treasury and announced about a week ahead. The 2026 auction record lists every new 10-year note at $42B offered and every reopening at $39B. The official yield is published daily by Treasury; FRED republishes it as series DGS10.
Principal & interest
Every six months
The current issue pays $23.125 per $1,000 every six months (4.625% a year) on February 15 and August 15, starting February 15, 2027. The $1,000 face value is repaid on August 15, 2036.

Auction and coupon mechanics

Treasury sells notes in a single-price auction: every winning bidder pays the same price, set by the highest accepted yield (the "high yield"). A new 10-year note gets its coupon at its first auction; the reopenings in the following months sell more of the same note at the same coupon, so a buyer pays more or less than face value depending on where yields have moved. Example from the current issue: at the August 12 auction the high yield was 4.683% and the 4.625% note sold at 99.540696 per 100; at the September 9 reopening the high yield was 4.834% and it sold at 98.361116. Reopening buyers also pay the interest that has built up since August 15 ($3.89606 per $1,000).

Credit quality. The note is a direct obligation of the U.S. Treasury. Moody's rates the U.S. government Aa1 with a stable outlook after its May 16, 2025 action: "Moody's Ratings (Moody's) has downgraded the Government of United States of America's (US) long-term issuer and senior unsecured ratings to Aa1 from Aaa and changed the outlook to stable from negative." A measure the sources support is auction demand: the September 9 reopening drew $105.81B of bids for $39.0B of notes (bid-to-cover 2.71), and the August new issue drew $116.96B, of which $10.62B came from the Federal Reserve's SOMA account. Secondary-market trading volume was not sourced.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. With a modified duration of 7.77, a 1-percentage-point rise in yield cuts the price by about 7.4%. That is the whole-note effect of yields moving, and it is why the price of the current issue fell from 99.54 at the August auction to 98.36 at the September reopening.
02
Inflation risk. The coupon is fixed in dollars. The 10-year breakeven inflation rate is 2.35%, while the Fed's September median projection for 2026 PCE inflation is 3.7%. If inflation runs above the breakeven over the note's life, the note earns less after inflation than the same-maturity TIPS.
03
Reinvestment risk. The 5.11% yield assumes each coupon is reinvested at that same rate. If rates fall, coupons are reinvested at lower yields and the realised return ends up below the yield at purchase.
04
Fiscal and supply risk. Moody's removed the last Aaa rating in May 2025. Treasury is selling $39–42B of this one maturity every month in 2026 (four new issues and eight reopenings a year). How much buyers demand in yield for that supply shows up in auction high yields and bid-to-cover ratios.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall. With a duration of 7.77, a 1-point drop in the 10-year yield raises the note's price by about 8.1%, on top of the 4.625% coupon. That tends to happen when inflation comes in below expectations (the breakeven is 2.35%), when the Fed's projected path moves lower, or when investors move out of riskier assets into Treasuries. A buyer at 5.11% who holds to August 2036 receives the coupons and face value whatever happens to the price along the way.

When it does poorly

When yields rise. A 1-point rise cuts the price by about 7.4%, which wipes out more than a year and a half of coupon income. It tends to happen when inflation surprises upward (the Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%), when the policy path is revised higher (the 2026 median moved from 3.8% in June to 4.1% in September), or when buyers ask more to absorb new supply. In September 2026 real yields drove the move: the 10-year TIPS yield rose 0.32 pp.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further rises in yields. Price falls about 7.4% for each 1-point rise (−20.3% at +3 points on full repricing). The Fed raised rates in September 2026, and the SEP median (4.1%) is consistent with one more quarter-point by year-end.
02
Inflation above the breakeven. The market is pricing 2.35% average inflation over ten years; the Fed's own 2026 PCE median is 3.7% and core 3.4%. A fixed coupon loses purchasing power if inflation stays above the breakeven.
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Moody's cut the U.S. to Aa1 in May 2025. Treasury is selling $39–42B of 10-year notes every month; weaker auctions (lower bid-to-cover, a high yield above pre-auction levels) would push yields up across outstanding notes.
Rates Commentary
Splitting the yield: real return plus expected inflation
DECOMPOSITION

The 5.11% nominal 10-year yield splits into a 2.76% real yield (the 10-year TIPS yield) and 2.35% breakeven inflation. A year earlier the split was 1.75% real and 2.37% breakeven on a 4.12% nominal yield. Of the 0.99-point rise over the year, the real yield accounts for 1.01 points, and breakeven inflation edged down 0.02. In this split, the whole rise is in the real-yield component, not in market-implied inflation. The Fed's 2026 PCE median (3.7%) is well above the 10-year breakeven (2.35%), though the two cover different horizons: one year against ten.

Curve shape: the front end moved most
CURVE

Over the past year the 2-year rose 1.32 points, more than the 10-year (0.99) or the 30-year (0.67), so the 2s10s spread narrowed from 0.59 to 0.26 points. The 3-month bill rose only 0.19, so the 10-year minus 3-month spread widened from 0.12 to 0.92 points. Two-year yields track the expected policy path most closely, and the September SEP raised that path at every horizon it shares with June (2026: 3.8 → 4.1; 2027: 3.6 → 4.1; 2028: 3.4 → 3.9).

Supply and auctions
MECHANICS

Every 2026 10-year auction on the TreasuryDirect record was $42B for new notes and $39B for reopenings. The two auctions of the current note cleared at 4.683% (August) and 4.834% (September), both below where the par yield stands now. The next test is the October 7 reopening, then the November 10 new issue, which will carry a coupon set near the yields prevailing then.
