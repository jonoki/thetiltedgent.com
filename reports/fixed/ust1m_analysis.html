01 Overview

A Treasury bill is a short loan to the U.S. federal government with no interest payments. TreasuryDirect: "Bills are sold at a discount or at par (face value). When the bill matures, you are paid its face value." The 4-week bill is the shortest of the regular terms TreasuryDirect lists ("4, 6, 8, 13, 17, 26, and 52 weeks") and the curve's 1-month point: at the October 1 auction a buyer paid $996.97 for $1,000 due November 3, 2026.

The header yield, 4.04%, is Treasury's 1-month par yield for October 2, 2026; the day before, at 4.06%, it was the highest since November 26, 2025.

Three ways to quote one bill

The discount rate (3.890%) measures the gain against face value on a 360-day year; the investment rate (3.956%) measures it against the price paid on a 365-day year, comparable with coupon yields. The par yield (4.04%) is Treasury's curve reading the next day.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reinvestment risk. The main one for bills: the rate is locked for four weeks, then the holder gets the next auction's rate. Month-end yields ranged from 0.03% to 5.60% in five years.
02
Rate risk. Small: about −0.08% of price per 1-point rise; face value at maturity.
03
Inflation risk. 2026 PCE median 3.7% vs a 4.04% pre-tax yield.
04
Debt-limit risk. In the 2023 standoff the 1-month yield hit 6.02% on May 26, 0.68 pp above the 3-month. The Fiscal Responsibility Act, suspending the debt limit through January 1, 2025, became law on June 3, 2023.

More: the formulas

T = 28/365 = 0.0767, y = 0.0404. P = 100/(1 + y·T) = 99.69. Modified D = T/(1 + y·T) = 0.0765. C = 2T²/(1 + y·T)² = 0.0117. ΔP/P ≈ −D·Δy + ½·C·Δy².

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the policy rate rises or stays high. Each new bill is bought at the latest rate, so income follows rates up within four weeks.

When it does poorly

When rates fall. A holder rolling bills earns less at each auction (0.03% at end-January 2022), with almost no duration to gain from falling yields.

Top 3 Risks

01
Falling rates on reinvestment. The yield is locked for only 28 days. The SEP longer-run median is 3.2%, below today's 4.04%.
02
Inflation. The Fed's 2026 PCE median, 3.7%, is close to the bill yield before tax.
03
Debt limit, credit and supply. Moody's cut the U.S. to Aa1 in May 2025. Treasury offered $90–110B of 4-week bills at each auction from July 30 to October 1.
