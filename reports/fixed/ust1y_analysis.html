01 Overview

A Treasury bill is a short loan to the U.S. federal government with no interest payments. TreasuryDirect: "Bills are sold at a discount or at par (face value). When the bill matures, you are paid its face value." The 52-week bill is the longest bill Treasury sells and the curve's 1-year point. It is auctioned every four weeks: at the September 29 auction a buyer paid $955.51 for $1,000 due September 30, 2027.

The header yield, 4.46%, is Treasury's 1-year par yield for October 2, 2026; the September 28 reading, 4.59%, was the highest since August 1, 2024.

Three ways to quote one bill

Bills sell in a single-price auction: every winner pays the price set by the highest accepted discount rate. The discount rate (4.400%) measures the gain against face value on a 360-day year; the investment rate (4.616%) measures it against the price paid, comparable with coupon yields. The par yield (4.46%) is Treasury's bond-equivalent curve reading three days later.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. About −0.97% of price per 1-point rise if sold early; face value at maturity.
02
Reinvestment risk. After a year the holder gets the next auction's rate; month-end yields ranged from 0.15% to 5.46% in five years.

Inflation risk. 2026 PCE median 3.7% vs a 4.46% pre-tax yield.
04
Fiscal and supply risk. Offering size raised from $50B to $52B in July and $54B in September 2026.

How we calculated these

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When rates fall after purchase: the holder keeps the locked yield while new bills pay less, and the price rises about 1% per point of decline.

When it does poorly

When rates rise after purchase: new bills pay more, and selling early costs about 1% per point of rise. After inflation and tax the return can be negative.

Top 3 Risks

01
Rates rising during the year. The SEP 2026 median (4.1%) sits one quarter-point increase above the midpoint of today’s 3.75–4% range; a faster path would leave a 4.46% bill behind new issues.
02
Inflation. The Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%, close to the 4.46% bill yield before tax.
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Moody's cut the U.S. to Aa1 in May 2025; Treasury raised the 52-week offering twice in 2026.
