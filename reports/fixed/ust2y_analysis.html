01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. The 2-year is the shortest note; anything shorter is a bill. Because it matures within two years, its yield tracks where the Federal Reserve is expected to set short-term rates over that period.

The header yield, 4.83%, is Treasury's official 2-year par yield for October 2, 2026. It was 4.34% at the end of August, 4.74% on September 16 (the day the Fed raised rates) and 4.92% on September 28, the highest reading since May 30, 2024.

Definition and how the par yield is built

TreasuryDirect: "We sell Treasury Notes for a term of 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years. Notes pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature."

Treasury builds the par yield from "indicative, bid-side market price quotations (not actual transactions)" for the latest issues.

Auction and coupon mechanics

Treasury auctions a 2-year note near the end of every month in a single-price auction: every winning bidder pays the price set by the highest accepted yield (the "high yield"), and the coupon is fixed there. Each new issue replaces the last as the "on-the-run" 2-year.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Direct U.S. Treasury obligation Moody's Aa1 (stable) Low duration: 1.87 No state or local income tax $181.3B bid for $69B (Sep 22)

Credit quality. Moody's cut the U.S. rating from Aaa to Aa1 on May 16, 2025, and changed the outlook to stable. S&P and Fitch: n/v. Liquidity. Bid-to-cover at the nine fixed-rate 2-year auctions of 2026 ranged from 2.44 (March) to 2.75 (January), each for $69B.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Policy-path risk. The yield moves with expectations for the Fed's rate (price impact: Section 04).
02
Reinvestment risk. The money comes back in September 2028; if rates are lower then, it is reinvested at lower yields.
03
Inflation risk. The coupon is fixed in dollars. 5-year breakeven inflation is 2.37%; the Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%.

How we calculated price, duration and convexity

Our calculation: y = 0.0483, coupon 2.375 per 100 per half-year, settlement Oct 5, 2026, accrued 5/182 of a period; cash flows at tk = 177/182 + k half-years, k = 0…3. P = Σ CFk/(1 + y/2)tk = 99.91 full, 99.85 clean. Macaulay = Σ tk·PVk/P/2; modified = Macaulay/(1 + y/2); convexity = Σ PVk·tk(tk+1)/[P·(1 + y/2)²·4]; ΔP/P ≈ −D·Δy + ½·C·Δy². Breakeven = nominal − real par yield.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the expected policy path moves lower (softer inflation, weaker jobs data, a lower SEP): a 1-point drop in yield adds about 1.9% to the price, on top of the 4.75% coupon.

When it does poorly

When the path moves higher, as in September 2026: the Fed raised rates, the 2027 median rose from 3.6 to 4.1, and the 2-year rose 0.54 pp. A 1-point rise costs about 1.85%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further Fed increases. The end-2026 median of 4.1 is consistent with one more quarter-point (our arithmetic on the range midpoint).
02
Reinvestment. In five years the 2-year ranged from 0.27% to 5.19%; income is fixed only until 2028.
03
Inflation above breakeven. 5-year breakeven 2.37% against a 2026 PCE median of 3.7%.
Rates Commentary
The 2-year and the policy rate
POLICY

A year ago the 2-year yielded 0.575 pp below the midpoint of the Fed's range; now 0.955 pp above it. The policy rate moved a net −0.25 pp over the year (two cuts, one increase) while the 2-year rose 1.28 pp.

Real yields did the moving
DECOMPOSITION

At five years, the shortest point on Treasury's real curve, the nominal yield (3.67% → 5.06%) and the real yield (1.30% → 2.69%) both rose 1.39 pp over the year; breakeven inflation stayed at 2.37%.
