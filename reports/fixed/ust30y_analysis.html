01 Overview

TreasuryDirect: "We sell Treasury Bonds for a term of either 20 or 30 years. Bonds pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature." The header is the official 30-year par yield, fitted daily from bid-side quotes. At 5.40% it is the highest reading in Treasury's daily files since February 14, 2002 (5.42%). The files have no 30-year yield from February 19, 2002 to February 8, 2006; since they resumed, the previous high was 5.37% (September 10, 2026).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. Modified duration 14.82: a 1-point rise cuts the price about 13.2%, some 2.6 years of coupons.
02
Inflation risk. Fixed dollar coupons for 30 years. Inflation above the 2.26% breakeven leaves it behind the 30-year TIPS.
03
Reinvestment risk. The 5.40% yield assumes coupons are reinvested at 5.40%.
04
Fiscal and supply risk. A 30-year auction every month of 2026 so far, $22–25B each.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When long yields fall: −1 pp lifts the price about 16.5%, plus the 5.125% coupon. Drivers: inflation below the 2.26% breakeven, a lower policy path, a flight to safety. Held to 2056, it pays face value whatever the price does.

When it does poorly

When long yields rise: +1 pp cuts the price about 13.2%. Drivers: inflation surprises (2026 PCE median 3.7%), a higher policy path (2026 fed funds median 3.8 → 4.1), heavier supply.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further rises in long yields. −13.3% at +1 pp, −32.8% at +3 pp (full repricing).
02
Inflation above 2.26% for decades. The Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%.
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Aa1 since May 2025; $22–25B auctioned monthly.
Rates Commentary
The rise is all real yield
DECOMPOSITION

5.40% = 3.14% real yield (30-year TIPS) + 2.26% breakeven. A year earlier: 2.47% + 2.26%. Breakeven did not move, so the 0.67-point rise is all real yield.

The long end moved least
CURVE

The 30-year minus 2-year gap fell from 1.20 to 0.55 points over the year. The September SEP raised every projected policy rate shared with June.
