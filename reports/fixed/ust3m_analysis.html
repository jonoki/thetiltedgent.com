01 Overview

A Treasury bill is a short loan to the U.S. federal government with no interest payments. TreasuryDirect: "Bills are sold at a discount or at par (face value). When the bill matures, you are paid its face value." The 13-week bill is the curve's 3-month point: at the November 2 auction a buyer paid $989.79 for $1,000 due February 4, 2027.

The header yield, 4.21%, is Treasury's 3-month par yield for November 2, 2026. It was 4.16% at the end of October.

Three ways to quote one bill

Bills sell in a single-price auction: every winner pays the price set by the highest accepted discount rate. The discount rate (4.040%) measures the gain against face value on a 360-day year; the investment rate (4.138%) measures it against the price paid on a 365-day year, comparable with coupon yields. The par yield (4.21%) is Treasury's bond-equivalent curve reading for the same day.

Credit quality. Moody's rates the U.S. government Aa1, stable, after downgrading it from Aaa on May 16, 2025. Liquidity. The November 2 auction drew $279.51B of bids (including $5.91B from the Fed's SOMA account) for $95B offered.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reinvestment risk. The main one for bills: the rate is locked for 13 weeks, then the holder gets the next auction's rate. Month-end yields ranged from 0.05% to 5.59% in five years.
02
Rate risk. Small: about −0.25% of price per 1-point rise; face value at maturity.
03
Inflation risk. 2026 PCE median 3.7% vs a 4.21% pre-tax yield.
04
Fiscal and supply risk. Weekly supply ($95B an auction since September 28); rating cut to Aa1 in May 2025.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the policy rate rises or stays high. Each new bill is bought at the latest rate, so income follows rates up within weeks; a 1-point rise costs about 0.25% of price.

When it does poorly

When rates fall. A holder rolling bills earns less at each auction (0.05% at end-November 2021), with no long duration to gain from falling yields. After inflation and tax the return can be negative.

Top 3 Risks

01
Falling rates on reinvestment. The yield is locked for only 91 days. The SEP longer-run median is 3.2%, below today's 4.21%.
02
Inflation. The Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%, close to the 4.21% bill yield before tax.
03
Fiscal credit and supply. Moody's cut the U.S. to Aa1 in May 2025. Treasury has offered $95B of 13-week bills at every auction since September 28 (previously $92B); weaker auctions would push bill yields up.
