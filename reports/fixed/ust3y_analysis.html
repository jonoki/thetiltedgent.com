01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. TreasuryDirect: "We sell Treasury Notes for a term of 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years. Notes pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature." The 3-year sits one step out from the 2-year: its yield reflects where markets expect the Federal Reserve to hold short-term rates over the next three years, plus a small premium for locking money up longer.

The header yield, 4.96%, is Treasury's official 3-year par yield for October 2, 2026. Treasury builds the curve from "indicative, bid-side market price quotations (not actual transactions) for the most recently auctioned securities". The 3-year was 4.40% at the end of August, 4.82% on September 16 (the day the Fed raised rates) and 5.01% on September 28, the highest reading since October 19, 2023.

Auction and coupon mechanics

Treasury auctions a new 3-year note early in every month in a single-price auction: every winning bidder pays the price set by the highest accepted yield (the "high yield"). The coupon is set at the auction, at or just below the high yield, and does not change over the life of the note. Each new issue replaces the last as the "on-the-run" 3-year. The offering grew from $40B in July 2023 to $58B in April 2024 and has been $58B at every 3-year auction since.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Direct U.S. Treasury obligation $58B auctioned monthly Modified duration: 2.72 No state or local income tax $157.9B bid for $58B (Sep 8)

Credit quality. A direct obligation of the U.S. government; Moody's rates it Aa1 (stable) since May 16, 2025. Liquidity. Bid-to-cover at the nine 3-year auctions of 2026 ranged from 2.54 (May) to 2.72 (September), each for $58B.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. The yield moves with expectations for the Fed's rate over three years. A 1-point rise cuts the price about 2.68%.
02
Reinvestment risk. The money comes back in September 2029; if rates are lower then, it is reinvested at lower yields.
03
Inflation risk. The coupon is fixed in dollars. 5-year breakeven inflation is 2.37%; the Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%.

05 Market & Policy Expectations

On September 16, 2026 the FOMC voted 12–0: "The Committee decided to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent". It was the first increase since July 2023. The Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) released that day raised the median rate path for every year it shares with June. Its 2026–2029 medians span the life of the current note; the 3-year yield sits 0.86–1.36 pp above them (our arithmetic).

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the expected policy path moves lower (softer inflation, weaker jobs data, a lower SEP): a 1-point drop in yield adds about 2.8% to the price, on top of the 4.375% coupon.

When it does poorly

When the path moves higher, as in September 2026: the Fed raised rates, the 2027 median rose from 3.6 to 4.1, and the 3-year rose 0.60 pp. A 1-point rise costs about 2.7%. Over longer horizons, falling rates hurt instead, through reinvestment.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further Fed increases. The end-2026 median of 4.1 is consistent with one more quarter-point (our arithmetic on the range midpoint).
02
Reinvestment. In five years the 3-year ranged from 0.49% to 5.03%; income is fixed only until 2029.
03
Inflation above breakeven. 5-year breakeven 2.37% against a 2026 PCE median of 3.7%.
Rates Commentary
The 3-year and the policy rate
POLICY

A year ago the 3-year yielded 0.565 pp below the midpoint of the Fed's range; now 1.085 pp above it. The policy rate moved a net −0.25 pp over the year (two cuts, one increase) while the 3-year rose 1.40 pp.

Real yields did the moving
DECOMPOSITION

At five years, the shortest point on Treasury's real curve, the nominal yield (3.67% → 5.06%) and the real yield (1.30% → 2.69%) both rose 1.39 pp over the year; breakeven inflation stayed at 2.37%.
