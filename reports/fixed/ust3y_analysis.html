01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. The 3-year sits one step out from the 2-year: its yield reflects where markets expect the Federal Reserve to hold short-term rates over the next three years, plus a small premium for locking money up longer.

4.65% is Treasury's official 3-year par yield for November 2, 2026. The 3-year was 4.40% at the end of August, 4.82% on September 16 (the day the Fed raised rates) and 5.01% on September 28, the highest reading since October 19, 2023.

Auction and coupon mechanics

Treasury auctions a new 3-year note early in every month in a single-price auction: every winning bidder pays the price set by the highest accepted yield (the "high yield"). The coupon is set at the auction, at or just below the high yield, and does not change over the life of the note. Each new issue becomes the "on-the-run" 3-year. The offering grew from $40B in July 2023 to $58B in April 2024 and has been $58B at every 3-year auction since.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. The yield tracks the expected Fed path. A 1-point rise cuts the price about 2.67%.
02
Reinvestment risk. The money comes back in October 2029; if rates are lower then, it is reinvested at lower yields.
03
Inflation risk. The coupon is fixed in dollars. 5-year breakeven inflation is 2.39%; the Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%.

More: how we calculated it

Yields are Treasury's par curves; the note is priced at the 3-year par yield.

Our calculation: y = 0.0465, coupon 2.4375 per 100 per half-year, settlement Nov 3, 2026, accrued 19/182 of a period; cash flows at tk = 163/182 + k half-years, k = 0…5. P = Σ CFk/(1 + y/2)tk = 100.87 full, 100.61 clean. Macaulay = Σ tk·PVk/P/2; modified = Macaulay/(1 + y/2); convexity = Σ PVk·tk(tk+1)/[P·(1 + y/2)²·4]; ΔP/P ≈ −D·Δy + ½·C·Δy². Breakeven = nominal − real par yield.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the expected policy path moves lower (softer inflation, weaker jobs data, a lower SEP): a 1-point drop in yield adds about 2.8% to the price, on top of the 4.875% coupon.

When it does poorly

When the path moves higher, as in September 2026: the Fed raised rates, the 2027 median rose from 3.6 to 4.1, and the 3-year rose 0.60 pp. A 1-point rise costs about 2.7%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further Fed increases. The end-2026 median of 4.1 is consistent with one more quarter-point (our arithmetic on the range midpoint).
02
Reinvestment. In five years the 3-year ranged from 0.66% to 5.03%; income is fixed only until 2029.
03
Inflation above breakeven. 5-year breakeven 2.39% against a 2026 PCE median of 3.7%.
Rates Commentary
The 3-year and the policy rate
POLICY

A year ago the 3-year yielded 0.275 pp below the midpoint of the Fed's range; now 0.775 pp above it, while the range is unchanged at 3.75–4.00% (one cut, one increase).

Real yields did the moving
DECOMPOSITION

At five years, the shortest point on Treasury's real curve, the nominal yield (3.71% → 4.70%) rose 0.99 pp and the real yield (1.31% → 2.31%) rose 1.00 pp over the year; breakeven inflation stayed near 2.4% (2.40% → 2.39%).
