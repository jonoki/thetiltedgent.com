01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. The 5-year note sits in the middle of the curve and is auctioned monthly. The header yield, 5.06%, is Treasury's official 5-year par yield for October 2, 2026. On September 30 it closed at 5.09%, the highest in Treasury's daily history since July 6, 2007 (5.10%).

Built from "indicative, bid-side market price quotations (not actual transactions) for the most recently auctioned securities."

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Direct U.S. Treasury obligation Moody's Aa1 (stable) Fixed coupon, known end date No state or local income tax $154.9B bid for $70B (Sep 23)

Credit quality. Moody's, May 16, 2025: "Moody's Ratings downgrades United States ratings to Aa1 from Aaa; changes outlook to stable." Liquidity. The September 23 auction drew $154.85B of bids; the Fed's SOMA account took a further $10.54B on top of the offering.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. Modified duration 4.36: a 1-point rise in yield cuts the price by about 4.25%.
02
Inflation risk. The 5-year breakeven inflation rate is 2.37%; inflation above it would leave the note behind the same-maturity TIPS.
03
Reinvestment risk. Coupons, and the principal in 2031, are reinvested at whatever yields are then.
04
Supply. $70B of new 5-year notes a month; bid-to-cover slipped from 2.37 in August to 2.21 in September.

How we calculated it

Yields are Treasury's par curves.

Our calculation, semi-annual, actual/actual: y = 0.0506, coupon 2.5 per 100 per half-year, settlement Oct 5, 2026 (T+1); first cash flow in w = 177/182 half-years, n = 10. P = Σ CFk/(1 + y/2)w+k = 99.81 (clean 99.74 after accrued 2.5 × 5/182). Macaulay = Σ t·PV / P / 2; Modified = Macaulay / (1 + y/2); C = Σ PV·t(t+1) / [P·(1 + y/2)²·4].

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall: a 1-point drop raises the price about 4.5%, on top of the 5.000% coupon. That tends to come with inflation below the 2.37% breakeven, a lower Fed path, or a move into Treasuries from riskier assets. Held to September 2031, the note pays its coupons and face value whatever the price does.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: a 1-point rise cuts the price about 4.25%, close to ten months of coupon income. That tends to come with inflation surprises (the Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%), a higher policy path (the 2026 median moved from 3.8% to 4.1%), or weak demand for new supply.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further rises in yields. −12.13% at +3 points on full repricing. The end-2026 SEP median (4.1%) is above today's 3.875% midpoint.
02
Inflation above the breakeven. Markets price 2.37% a year over five years; the Fed's 2026 core PCE median is 3.4%.
03
Auction demand. Weaker auctions would push yields up across outstanding notes.
Rates Commentary
The rise is in the real yield
DECOMPOSITION

The 5.06% yield splits into a 2.69% real yield (5-year TIPS) and 2.37% breakeven inflation. The whole 1.39-point rise is in the real yield; since end-August the real yield accounts for 0.51 of the 0.57-point move.
