01 Overview

A Treasury note is a loan to the U.S. federal government. TreasuryDirect: "We sell Treasury Notes for a term of 2, 3, 5, 7, or 10 years. Notes pay a fixed rate of interest every six months until they mature." The 7-year is sold as a new note every month. The header is Treasury's official 7-year par yield. Its September 30 reading of 5.19% was the highest in Treasury's daily data since June 2007.

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Direct U.S. Treasury obligation Moody's Aa1 (stable) Fixed coupon, known end date No state or local income tax New $44B issue every month

Moody's: "Moody's Ratings downgrades United States ratings to Aa1 from Aaa; changes outlook to stable" (May 16, 2025). S&P and Fitch: —.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rate risk. A 1-point rise in yield cuts the price about 5.7%, more than a year of coupons.
02
Inflation risk. The 7-year breakeven is 2.34%; inflation above it leaves the note behind a 7-year TIPS.
03
Reinvestment risk. If rates fall, coupons are reinvested below 4.79%.
04
Supply. $44B of new 7-year notes every month; weak demand shows up in the bid-to-cover.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When yields fall: about +6.1% per 1-point drop, plus the 4.875% coupon. That tends to follow lower-than-expected inflation, a lower projected policy path, or a move into Treasuries. Held to 2033, it repays face value regardless.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: about −5.7% per 1-point rise. That tends to follow upside inflation surprises, a higher policy path (the 2026 median rose from 3.8% to 4.1%), or heavy supply.

Top 3 Risks

01
Higher yields. −15.9% at +3 points on full repricing.
02
Inflation above 2.34%. The Fed's 2026 PCE median is 3.7%.
03
Credit and supply. Aa1 at Moody's; $44B of new supply each month.
