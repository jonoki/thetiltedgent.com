01 Company Overview

Flex Ltd. is one of the world's largest contract manufacturers and supply-chain integrators, building electronics and electro-mechanical systems for data centres, communications, automotive, medical, industrial and consumer customers across the Americas, Asia and Europe. In fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) the company generated $27.91B of revenue, up 8.1% YoY, with operating income of $1.51B (5.39% margin) and diluted EPS of $2.33. At a $43.0B market capitalisation, Flex ranks roughly #603 globally by market value and sits in the S&P 500 at approximately the 255th weight (0.06% of the index).

The central debate in this report is simple to state and hard to price: Flex has stopped being valued as a contract manufacturer and started being valued as an AI-infrastructure supplier. Contract manufacturing is a thin-margin business by construction — Flex's gross margin is 9.5% and its net margin 3.3% — and that has historically earned the stock a high-single-digit to low-teens earnings multiple. Today it trades at 41.7× trailing earnings because the fastest-growing part of the business is data-centre power and cooling, and because management has announced it intends to spin that business off. The stock has roughly doubled over twelve months and then given back a third of the move since June. Everything that matters here turns on whether the Cloud and Power Infrastructure franchise is a durable, differentiated asset or a cyclical order surge attached to a commodity-assembly parent.

The core advantage is that Flex owns the boring, capital-heavy middle of an industry everyone suddenly needs. Anybody can design a power pod; very few organisations can qualify one, manufacture it at rack scale in multiple geographies, and deliver it on the schedule a hyperscaler's construction timeline demands. Flex's edge is the combination of physical footprint (149,686 employees across the Americas, Asia and Europe), regulatory qualifications in medical and automotive that take years to replicate, and — newly — owned intellectual property in liquid cooling and power conversion acquired rather than built. That last point is the important one and also the caveat: the moat in CPI was bought (JetCool, Electrical Power Products at $1.187B, EPC Power at $4.4B announced), not grown, which means it is priced at acquisition multiples and has to be earned back through execution.

Key Vulnerabilities

Structurally thin margins leave no error budget.
Gross margin is 9.53% and net margin 3.32%. A 100-basis-point miss on gross margin is roughly a third of net income. Contract manufacturing works only at scale and with disciplined program selection; when a large program is mispriced or a customer pushes out volume, the operating leverage runs violently in reverse. The multiple currently applied to these earnings — 41.7× trailing — assumes essentially no such accident.
The spin-off removes the growth engine from the parent.
CPI is 27.8% of revenue and by far the fastest-growing piece (+35% YoY versus +12% and +9%). If the separation completes in early 2027 as targeted, today's FLEX shareholder ends up holding two securities, one of which is a low-growth EMS business. How the market values the residual RemainCo, and how much stranded corporate cost it carries, is unresolved — and $53M of separation costs already hit Q1 FY2027, $24M of it in cash.
Acquisition-funded growth on a levered balance sheet.
Debt-to-equity is 1.08 with $5.93B of total debt against $2.84B of cash. On top of the $1.187B Electrical Power Products deal that closed in the June quarter, Flex agreed on September 3, 2026 to buy EPC Power for $4.4B, closing expected in Q4 2026. That is roughly 10% of the entire market capitalisation, on a business with $826M of trailing free cash flow. Integration risk and financing risk are now live, simultaneous, and large.
Customer and end-market concentration in an AI capex cycle.
CPI's growth is levered to a small number of hyperscale and data-centre customers whose capital budgets are set annually and can be revised. Meanwhile ITS growth was partly driven by memory pricing — a pass-through that flatters revenue without adding proportional profit, and that reverses when component prices normalise. Neither of those is a durable driver.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Flex is executing a genuine mix shift: gross margin has gone from 7.01% in FY2023 to 9.76% in the March-2026 quarter, revenue growth has accelerated five quarters running to +20.6%, and the fastest-growing segment — data-centre power and cooling — is compounding at 35% and about to be separated into a pure play with its own multiple. Guidance implies ~23% revenue growth and ~39% adjusted EPS growth this fiscal year, a PEG of 0.45, ROIC of 15.1%, and eleven analysts with no holds and no sells and an average target 49% above the close. If CPI separates cleanly in early 2027 and EPC Power integrates, the sum of the parts is worth meaningfully more than $43B.

Bear Case

Strip away the narrative and this is a 3.3% net-margin contract manufacturer trading at 41.7× trailing earnings — roughly four times its own historical multiple — with $5.93B of debt, a $4.4B acquisition pending, and a plan to hand its growth engine to shareholders as a separate certificate. The +35% in CPI is partly bought, not grown; the +12% in ITS is partly memory pass-through, not volume. The stock has already fallen 35% from its June high, and the sell side cut targets the day after a beat-and-raise. If AI data-centre capex plans slip even one budget cycle, there is 9.5 cents of gross profit per revenue dollar to absorb the shock.

Top 3 Risks

01
Spin-off execution and RemainCo value. Separation is targeted for Q1 2027 and is already costing real money ($53M in Q1 FY2027 alone). If stranded costs are larger than expected, or if the residual EMS business re-rates down to a mid-teens multiple, the sum of the parts can be worth less than the whole.
02
Acquisition digestion at scale. Electrical Power Products ($1.187B, closed) and EPC Power ($4.4B, pending) together represent well over 10% of market capitalisation deployed inside twelve months, against $826M of trailing free cash flow. Overpaying or mis-integrating either one would break the ROIC and the leverage story simultaneously.
03
Multiple compression on any AI-capex wobble. At 41.7× trailing earnings on 3.3% net margins, almost all of the valuation is discounting future CPI growth. The July 2026 −30% month showed how fast that premium evaporates without any deterioration in the reported numbers at all.

Industry Commentary

EMERGING TAILWIND
Data-centre power and thermal is the real product
As sourced from Flex's Q1 FY2027 release and earnings call: the company describes itself as "helping customers accelerate data center deployment by solving power, heat, and scale challenges through cutting-edge power and cooling technology," CPI revenue grew 35% to $2,202M on "Power and Cloud and Cooling strength," management noted the liquid-cooling market is expanding as air cooling is displaced, and the pending EPC Power deal is explicitly targeted at "AI data center and grid infrastructure" including 800V architectures. Every one of those statements is the company's own; none is independently verified here, and no third-party market-size or share data was sourced for this report.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Component pricing cuts both ways
ITS grew 12% partly on "stronger Communications and memory pricing." In a contract-manufacturing model, component inflation passes through to revenue largely without margin — it inflates the top line and dilutes the percentage margin at the same time. When memory pricing normalises, reported growth falls without any change in units shipped. Meanwhile consumer end markets were called out as weak in the same quarter, so the segment is carrying a mix headwind underneath a pricing tailwind.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The margin floor is the whole argument
Bull framing: every 100bp of gross margin on $29.3B of revenue is roughly $293M of gross profit, against $973M of trailing net income — so the mix shift toward CPI is enormously levered, and management has guided to at least 100bp of full-year margin improvement. Bear framing: that leverage is symmetric. At a 9.53% gross margin there is no cushion; a program loss, a customer push-out or an integration stumble hits earnings at multiples of its revenue impact. At 41.7× trailing earnings, the market is pricing the upside version of that leverage and none of the downside.
