01 Company Overview

Flex Ltd. is one of the world's largest contract manufacturers and supply-chain integrators, building electronics and electro-mechanical systems for data centres, communications, automotive, medical, industrial and consumer customers across the Americas, Asia and Europe. In fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) the company generated $27.91B of revenue, up 8.1% YoY, with operating income of $1.51B (5.39% margin) and diluted EPS of $2.33. In the quarter to September 25, 2026 (Q2 fiscal 2027) sales reached $8.49B, up 25%, taking trailing-twelve-month revenue to $30.95B. Its market capitalisation is $34.4B (369.46M shares as listed by StockAnalysis × the October 29 close).

The central debate in this report is simple to state and hard to price: Flex has stopped being valued as a contract manufacturer and started being valued as an AI-infrastructure supplier. Contract manufacturing is a thin-margin business by construction — Flex's gross margin is 9.7% and its net margin 3.4% over the last twelve months — and that has historically earned the stock a single-digit to mid-teens earnings multiple (7× to 16× trailing GAAP earnings at the March 2022–2025 fiscal year-ends). Today it trades at 33.6× trailing earnings because the fastest-growing part of the business is data-centre power and cooling, and because management plans to spin that business off as a separate listed company called Axiom, expected in the first quarter of calendar 2027. The stock is up 41% over twelve months (from $66.10 on October 29, 2025) but sits 44% below its June high. Everything that matters here turns on whether the Cloud and Power Infrastructure franchise is a durable, differentiated asset or a cyclical order surge attached to a commodity-assembly parent.

The core advantage is that Flex owns the boring, capital-heavy middle of an industry everyone suddenly needs. Anybody can design a power pod; very few organisations can qualify one, manufacture it at rack scale in multiple geographies, and deliver it on the schedule a hyperscaler's construction timeline demands. Flex's edge is the combination of physical footprint (149,686 employees across the Americas, Asia and Europe), regulatory qualifications in medical and automotive that take years to replicate, and — newly — owned intellectual property in liquid cooling and power conversion acquired rather than built. That last point is the important one and also the caveat: the moat in CPI was bought (JetCool, Electrical Power Products at $1.187B, EPC Power at $4.4B, closed October 14, 2026), not grown, which means Flex paid full acquisition prices for it and has to earn them back through execution.

Key Vulnerabilities

Structurally thin margins leave no error budget.
Over the last twelve months gross margin is 9.67% and net margin 3.35%. One percentage point of gross margin on $30.95B of revenue is about $310M, close to a third of the $1.04B of trailing net income. Contract manufacturing works only at scale and with disciplined program selection; because costs are largely fixed, a mispriced program or a customer pushing out volume cuts profit far faster than it cuts revenue. The 33.6× trailing multiple leaves little room for such an accident.
The spin-off removes the growth engine from the parent.
CPI is about 29% of revenue and by far the fastest-growing piece (+54% YoY in the September quarter versus +22% and +9%). If the separation completes in the first quarter of 2027 as planned, today's FLEX shareholder ends up holding two stocks, one of them a slower-growing EMS business. How the market values the Flex that remains, and how much shared corporate cost it is left carrying, is unresolved — and the spin-off is already expensive: about $144M of legal and other charges in the first half of fiscal 2027, mostly for the separation, and $85M of separation cash costs that came out of free cash flow.
Acquisition-funded growth on a levered balance sheet.
At September 25, debt-to-equity was 1.03, with $5.98B of total debt against $3.08B of cash. On October 14 Flex paid $4.4B in cash for EPC Power, funding it with $3.3B under a term loan and $1.1B under a bridge loan, both maturing October 13, 2027, plus cash on hand (8-K filed October 14, 2026). The CFO said the $2.0B Axiom preferred investment, once it closes (it needs antitrust clearance), will repay about $2B of that debt. If the spin-off is not completed by December 31, 2027, Flex guarantees that Axiom buys the preferred back at 115% of the price in cash or 125% in Flex shares. EPC Power alone cost about 13% of today's market value, against $783M of trailing free cash flow.
Customer and end-market concentration in an AI capex cycle.
CPI's growth is levered to a small number of hyperscale and data-centre customers whose capital budgets are set annually and can be revised. Meanwhile ITS growth was partly driven by memory pricing — a pass-through that flatters revenue without adding proportional profit, and that reverses when component prices normalise. Neither of those is a durable driver.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Flex is executing a genuine mix shift: gross margin has gone from 7.01% in FY2023 to 9.67% over the last twelve months, revenue growth has risen four quarters running to +24.8%, and the fastest-growing segment — data-centre power and cooling — grew 54% last quarter at a 10.7% adjusted margin and is about to become a separately listed company, Axiom, valued on its own. The quarter beat the company's own guidance on sales and adjusted EPS, and raised guidance implies ~27% revenue growth and ~52% adjusted EPS growth this fiscal year, a PEG of 0.31, ROIC of 14.3%, and eleven analysts with no holds and no sells and an average target 68% above the close. Outside investors are putting $2.0B into Axiom before it lists. If Axiom separates cleanly in early 2027 and EPC Power integrates, the parts could be worth meaningfully more than today's $34.4B market value.

Bear Case

Strip away the story and this is a 3.4% net-margin contract manufacturer trading at 33.6× trailing earnings — more than double the 7×–16× it traded at in fiscal 2022–2025 — with $5.98B of debt at the quarter end, another $4.4B borrowed in October for EPC Power, and a plan to hand its growth engine to shareholders as a separate stock. The +54% in CPI is partly bought, not grown; ITS has leaned on memory-chip prices and is seeing consumer weakness. The stock is 44% below its June high, and even after the beat Barclays cut its target to $128. If AI data-centre capex plans slip even one budget cycle, there are under 10 cents of gross profit per revenue dollar to absorb the shock.

Top 3 Risks

01
Spin-off execution and the value of the Flex left behind. Separation is expected in the first quarter of 2027, needs a shareholder vote (November 2) and Singapore court approval, and is already costing real money (about $144M of charges in the first half of fiscal 2027). If shared costs left with Flex are larger than expected, or if the remaining EMS business ends up valued at a mid-teens earnings multiple, the parts can be worth less than the whole. If the spin-off slips past December 31, 2027, Flex must stand behind buying back the $2.0B Axiom preferred at a premium.
02
Digesting big acquisitions. Electrical Power Products ($1.187B) and EPC Power ($4.4B) were both bought in fiscal 2027, together about 16% of today's market value, against $783M of trailing free cash flow; EPC Power was paid for with short-term borrowing. Overpaying or mis-integrating either one would hurt returns on capital and leave Flex carrying more debt at the same time.
03
The valuation falls fast on any AI-capex wobble. At 33.6× trailing earnings on a 3.4% net margin, most of the value rests on future CPI growth. The −30% July 2026 month and the 30% slide from September 30 to October 23 showed how fast that premium can disappear without any deterioration in the reported numbers.

Industry Commentary

EMERGING TAILWIND
Data-centre power and thermal is the real product
In the company's own words and numbers: on the October 28 call CEO Revathi Advaithi said AI infrastructure "depends on power, cooling, compute and the ability to deploy and operate infrastructure at scale"; CPI revenue grew 54% to about $2.5B in the September quarter; Flex now guides CPI growth of 75%–85% for the fiscal year; and EPC Power, closed October 14, is aimed at "higher-density AI infrastructure and next-generation 800V data center power architectures." The investors putting $2.0B into Axiom are betting on the same demand.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Component pricing cuts both ways
In the June quarter ITS grew 20%, which the 10-Q put down to strong Communications demand "and increased memory pricing in both Communications and our Lifestyle businesses." In a contract-manufacturing model, component price rises pass through to revenue largely without extra profit — they inflate sales and dilute the percentage margin at the same time. When memory prices normalise, reported growth falls without any change in units shipped. Meanwhile consumer end markets were called out as weak again in the September quarter, so the segment carries a weaker mix underneath a pricing tailwind.
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The margin floor is the whole argument
Bull framing: every percentage point of gross margin on $30.95B of revenue is roughly $310M of gross profit, against $1,037M of trailing net income — so a small mix shift toward CPI moves profit a lot, and management has just raised its full-year adjusted operating margin guidance to 7.6%–7.8% from 7.0%–7.2%. Bear framing: that works both ways. At a 9.67% gross margin there is no cushion; a program loss, a customer push-out or an integration stumble hits earnings many times harder than revenue. At 33.6× trailing earnings, the market is pricing the upside version and little of the downside.
