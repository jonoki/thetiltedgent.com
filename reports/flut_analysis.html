01 Company Overview

Flutter Entertainment is the parent of FanDuel, the largest online sportsbook and online casino operator in the United States by gross gaming revenue in the states where it is active, and it runs a portfolio of online betting and gaming businesses across the UK and Ireland, Southern Europe, Australia, Central and Eastern Europe and Brazil. It is the subject of this report, which is why it is named; this page does not promote betting and carries no operator branding. FY2025 revenue was $16,383M, up 17%, but the group posted a GAAP net loss of $310M and diluted EPS of −$1.75. At a $15.07B market value it ranks roughly #1,481 globally and #20 among Ireland-domiciled companies by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap country ranking).

The poker lens fits this company better than any other on the site, because Flutter is the house. A sportsbook does not need to know who wins Sunday's game. It prices both sides so that, across millions of bets, it keeps a predictable slice of every dollar wagered — the structural hold. That slice is the rake. What is breaking in 2026 is not the rake — FanDuel's structural hold actually rose 40bps to 14.0% in Q2 2026 — it is everything around it: more promotional rakeback, unlucky results, a new competing product (prediction markets) that sits outside state sportsbook licensing, tax hikes on both sides of the Atlantic, and four consecutive guidance cuts. The stock closed at $86.86 on September 21, 2026, down 69.8% from its 52-week high of $287.44 and within 0.4% of its 52-week intraday low, set the same day.

The house edge: structural hold, read like a rake

At a poker table the house does not play; it takes a rake from every pot and lets the players fight over the rest. A sportsbook's version is hold: revenue as a percentage of handle. Structural hold is the hold the book expects over a large sample given the mix of bets it takes — the expected rake. Actual hold swings around it with results, exactly as a winning poker player's session results swing around his edge. Then promotions (free bets, odds boosts) are handed back — that is rakeback. The line FanDuel reports as net revenue margin is: structural rake, plus or minus luck, minus rakeback.

The −170bps bridge
+40 −70 −140
Structural hold +40bps, sports results −70bps (luck ran against the house), promotional reinvestment −140bps (more rakeback). Sum: −170bps. The rake held; the rakeback and the variance did the damage.
Why parlays raise the rake
edge ≈ 1 − 0.9545ⁿ
Illustration, not company data. A standard −110 line on a coin-flip returns 0.5 × 1.909 = 0.9545 per $1 staked: a 4.55% house edge. Chain n such legs and the bettor's expected return compounds: 2 legs 91.1% (8.9% edge), 4 legs 83.0% (17.0%), 6 legs 75.6% (24.4%). More legs per bet mechanically lifts structural hold — which is why parlay mix is the lever.

The poker read: this house has a better rake than it did a year ago, and is paying more of it back to keep players at its table while a new table — prediction markets, regulated federally rather than by state gaming commissions — opens next door. Handle fell 9% in Q1 2026 and grew only 2% in Q2 despite the World Cup. A rising rake on flat volume is not a growth story; it is a mature-casino story, and the stock is now priced like one.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

#1 US GGR share (39% / 27%) State licences Parlay pricing & risk engine 14.2M average monthly players iGaming cross-sell Multi-country diversification

The moat is scale in a licensed market: each US state is a separate licence, a separate tax regime and a separate player pool, and the biggest book can afford the most pricing, product and marketing per state. Scale also improves pricing — a bigger book sees more flow and can offer more same-game combinations, which is where structural hold comes from. The vulnerability is that the moat is regulatory: it protects against other licensed sportsbooks, not against a product that reaches the same customer under a different regulator, and not against states raising the tax on the licence itself.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Prediction markets route around the licence. Event contracts on sports outcomes are offered nationally by exchanges outside state gaming regulation. Flutter's response — FanDuel Predicts — cost US adjusted EBITDA in 2026 and produced $6M of Q2 revenue. A 2026 operating cost of $200–300M for the product was reported around the February 2026 results (TIKR/press summaries; not confirmed in the Q2 release).
02
The government takes a bigger rake. UK remote gaming duty went from 21% to 40% in April 2026 ($320M total 2026 adjusted EBITDA impact before $85M of mitigation, per the Q2 release). In Q2 the company also booked $95M of historical tax provisions ($62M India GST, $33M US sales and use tax).
03
Rakeback is rising. Promotional reinvestment took 140bps off US sportsbook margin in Q2 2026. Promotions that buy volume in a share fight are a recurring cost, not a one-off.
04
Leverage while earnings fall. Net debt of $10.48B at 4.3x adjusted EBITDA, up from 3.7x, with guidance for FY2026 adjusted EBITDA down 7%. Interest expense rose $52M YoY to $162M in Q2.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The house's rake has not broken: structural hold rose to 14.0% and FanDuel still holds 39% of sportsbook and 27% of iGaming GGR in its states. The damage is from promotions, bad luck and investment, and luck mean-reverts. At $86.86 the equity trades at 0.88x sales, ~8.2% FCF yield, 17.5x FY2026 and 11.9x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS, with 23 of 32 analysts at Buy and the lowest target at $85. A new CEO from the growing segment, $500M of targeted savings and prediction-market revenue that the company expects to reach ~$50M in 2026 give a path to re-rating if the guide stops falling.

Bear Case

A rising rake on flat handle is the signature of a maturing market, not a growth one. Guidance has been cut four times in 2026; US adjusted EBITDA fell 70% in Q2; the UK doubled its remote gaming duty; and a federally regulated competitor product reaches FanDuel's customers without a state licence. With 4.3x leverage, falling EBITDA widens the equity's losses faster than the business's — and a GAAP loss-maker with no dividend has nothing to anchor valuation except management's forecasts, which have not held.

Top 3 Risks

01
Prediction markets erode the licensed-sportsbook model. If event contracts stay available nationally, the value of state licences — the core of the US moat — falls, and FanDuel competes on price with a lower-tax product.
02
Guidance credibility. Four cuts in 2026 (Rothschild & Co Redburn count). A fifth at Q3 under a new CEO would reset the base again.
03
Tax and leverage together. Higher gaming taxes cut EBITDA directly; with net debt at $10.48B and leverage at 4.3x, each lost dollar of EBITDA raises leverage and lowers the equity value, which is only $15.07B.
Industry Commentary
Prediction markets vs. state-licensed sportsbooks
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A sportsbook sets a price and keeps the hold; an exchange matches two customers and takes a fee. Prediction markets bring the exchange model to sports outcomes under a federal regulator rather than state gaming commissions. For Flutter that is a direct threat to the volume behind its structural hold. Its answer is to join in: FanDuel Predicts, with player props, custom combos and a market-making capability the company expects to bring ~$50M of revenue in 2026. At that scale it is a hedge, not a replacement: $50M is under 1% of FY2026 US revenue guidance of $7.4B.

Governments raising the rake
ONGOING HEADWIND

Gaming duty is the tax on the house's rake, and it is rising. The UK nearly doubled remote gaming duty to 40% in April 2026, which the Q2 release puts at $320M of total 2026 adjusted EBITDA impact before $85M of first-order mitigation. Q2 also carried $95M of historical provisions from India and US sales-and-use tax. Individual US state tax changes were not verified in this session. Taxes on revenue land before operating costs, so they fall on margin one-for-one.

Parlay mix — the house's best pricing lever
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Each leg added to a parlay compounds the house edge (Section 02: ~4.5% for one leg at −110, ~17% for four). The shift towards same-game parlays is why FanDuel's structural hold rose to 14.0% in Q2 2026, with the World Cup pushing parlay use higher. The bull reading: the pricing edge is real and growing, and whoever sees the most flow can price the most combinations. The bear reading: a higher hold means customers lose their deposits faster, which raises promotional cost to keep them and invites regulators and tax authorities to take a larger share. Both are visible in the Q2 bridge: +40bps of structural hold, −140bps of promotions.
