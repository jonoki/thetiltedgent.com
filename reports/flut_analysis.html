01 Company Overview

Flutter Entertainment is the parent of FanDuel, the largest online sportsbook and online casino operator in the United States by gross gaming revenue in the states where it is active, and it runs a portfolio of online betting and gaming businesses across the UK and Ireland, Southern Europe, Australia, Central and Eastern Europe and Brazil. It is the subject of this report, which is why it is named; this page does not promote betting and carries no operator branding. FY2025 revenue was $16,383M, up 17%, but the group posted a GAAP net loss of $407M ($310M attributable to Flutter's shareholders) and diluted EPS of −$1.75. At the November 6, 2026 close its market value is $14.21B (173.87M shares on the 10-Q cover × $81.70, our calculation).

The poker lens fits this company better than any other on the site, because Flutter is the house. A sportsbook does not need to know who wins Sunday's game. It prices both sides so that, across millions of bets, it keeps a predictable slice of every dollar wagered — the structural hold. That slice is the rake. What is breaking in 2026 is not the rake — FanDuel's structural hold was unchanged at 12.9% in Q3 2026 — it is everything around it: more promotional rakeback, unlucky results in both segments, a new competing product (prediction markets) that sits outside state sportsbook licensing, tax hikes on both sides of the Atlantic, a ban in Brazil, and a 2026 guide that has been lowered at each of the three quarterly reports since it was set in February. The stock closed at $81.70 on November 6, 2026, down 65.3% from its 52-week high of $235.77 and 12.6% above its 52-week intraday low of $72.56, set on October 14.

The house edge: structural hold, read like a rake

At a poker table the house does not play; it takes a rake from every pot and lets the players fight over the rest. A sportsbook's version is hold: revenue as a percentage of handle. Structural hold is the hold the book expects over a large sample given the mix of bets it takes — the expected rake. Actual hold swings around it with results, exactly as a winning poker player's session results swing around his edge. Then promotions (free bets, odds boosts) are handed back — that is rakeback. The line FanDuel reports as net revenue margin is: structural rake, plus or minus luck, minus rakeback.

Why parlays raise the rake
edge ≈ 1 − 0.9545ⁿ
Illustration, not company data. A standard −110 line on a coin-flip returns 0.5 × 1.909 = 0.9545 per $1 staked: a 4.55% house edge. Chain n such legs and the bettor's expected return compounds: 2 legs 91.1% (8.9% edge), 4 legs 83.0% (17.0%), 6 legs 75.6% (24.4%). More legs per bet mechanically lifts structural hold — which is why parlay mix is the lever.

The poker read: this house has the same rake as a year ago, and is paying more of it back to keep players at its table while a new table — prediction markets, regulated federally rather than by state gaming commissions — opens next door. Handle fell 9% in Q1 2026, grew 2% in Q2 and jumped 13% in Q3, but sportsbook revenue grew only 4% in Q3 because the volume came with more promotions. Until handle growth shows up in revenue without extra rakeback, this is a mature-casino story, and the stock is priced like one.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

#1 US GGR share (36% / 27%) State licences Parlay pricing & risk engine 13.7M average monthly players iGaming cross-sell Multi-country diversification

The moat is scale in a licensed market: each US state is a separate licence, a separate tax regime and a separate player pool, and the biggest book can afford the most pricing, product and marketing per state. Scale also improves pricing — a bigger book sees more flow and can offer more same-game combinations, which is where structural hold comes from. The vulnerability is that the moat is regulatory: it protects against other licensed sportsbooks, not against a product that reaches the same customer under a different regulator, and not against states raising the tax on the licence itself.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Prediction markets route around the licence. Event contracts on sports outcomes are offered nationally by exchanges outside state gaming regulation. Flutter's response, FanDuel Predicts, had 66,000 average monthly players in Q3 and revenue the company calls not material, and it is expected to produce an adjusted EBITDA loss of about $200M in 2026, with investment at similar or higher levels in 2027 (Q3 release).
02
The government takes a bigger rake. UK remote gaming duty went from 21% to 40% in April 2026, and sports-betting duty outside horseracing rises to 25% in April 2027. Flutter puts the 2027 cost of the iGaming changes at $540M of adjusted EBITDA before $201M of offsetting actions (release of October 20, 2026). Brazil showed the harder version of the same risk: a government ban that forced an $873M write-down.
03
Rakeback is rising. Promotional spend took 0.6 percentage points off US sportsbook margin in Q3 2026, after 1.4 in Q2, and the company expects similar levels in Q4 and 2027. Promotions that buy volume in a share fight are a recurring cost, not a one-off.
04
Leverage while earnings fall. Net debt of $10.66B at 4.6x adjusted EBITDA, up from 3.7x at year-end, with 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance cut again. Net interest expense was $159M in Q3 ($152M a year earlier), and about $640M is guided for the year. On October 30 Flutter extended its revolving credit line and bank term loans to May 2032, raised the revolver to $1.86B and replaced its debt test with one that counts only first-lien debt (the debt with first claim on the assets), set at 5.70 times (8-K, November 4, 2026).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the first NYSE month-end close of $207.64 (January 2024) to the November 6, 2026 settled close of $81.70, FLUT lost 60.7%, while SPY gained 52.3% and QQQ 57.3% over the same dates (price only). Since then it has fallen 73.4% from that peak, with the damage concentrated in January 2026 (−23.2%), February 2026 (−35.7%) and September 2026 (−25.6%). The stock pays no dividend, so price return is total return.

The 6-period monthly RSI has been below 30 on every reading since January 2026 — eleven in a row, counting the part-month November point — bottoming at 16.3 in March 2026 and at 23.9 now. The 10-month moving average sits near $95.87, 17.3% above the price. Eleven readings of "oversold" without a lasting bounce is the chart's way of saying the market has changed its mind about what the business is worth, rather than over-reacting to one quarter.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The house's rake has not broken: structural hold held at 12.9% in Q3 and FanDuel still has 36% of sportsbook and 27% of iGaming gross gaming revenue in its states. US handle grew 13% and US revenue 6%. Much of the damage is from promotions, bad luck and investment, and luck evens out over time. At $81.70 the equity trades at 0.82x sales, 19.3x FY2026 and 13.5x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS, with 23 of 33 analysts at Buy and the lowest target at $80. A new CEO from the International side, a further $500M of targeted savings by 2029, a UK & Ireland restructuring and bank debt pushed out to 2032 give a path to a higher valuation if the guide stops falling.

Bear Case

Handle growth bought with more promotions, on an unchanged rake, is the signature of a maturing market, not a growth one. FY2026 guidance has been lowered at all three reports since February; US adjusted EBITDA fell 94% in Q3; the UK nearly doubled its remote gaming duty; Brazil's ban forced an $873M write-down; and a federally regulated competitor product reaches FanDuel's customers without a state licence. With 4.6x leverage, trailing free cash flow of only $515M against $10.66B of net debt and the buyback paused, falling EBITDA widens the equity's losses faster than the business's — and a GAAP loss-maker with no dividend has nothing to anchor valuation except management's forecasts, which have not held.

Top 3 Risks

01
Prediction markets erode the licensed-sportsbook model. If event contracts stay available nationally, the value of state licences — the core of the US moat — falls, and FanDuel competes on price with a lower-tax product.
02
Guidance credibility. Since February the 2026 midpoints have gone from $18.40B to $17.61B of revenue and from $2.97B to $2.495B of adjusted EBITDA, lowered at each of three reports. A new CEO now has to set targets the market will believe.
03
Tax and leverage together. Higher gaming taxes cut EBITDA directly; with net debt at $10.66B and leverage at 4.6x, each lost dollar of EBITDA raises leverage and lowers the equity value, which is only $14.21B.
Industry Commentary
Prediction markets vs. state-licensed sportsbooks
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A sportsbook sets a price and keeps the hold; an exchange matches two customers and takes a fee. Prediction markets bring the exchange model to sports outcomes under a federal regulator rather than state gaming commissions. For Flutter that is a direct threat to the volume behind its structural hold. Its answer is to join in: FanDuel Predicts had 66,000 average monthly players and $119M of volume in Q3, with revenue the company calls not material, and is expected to cost about $200M of adjusted EBITDA in 2026. For now it is a hedge that costs money, not a replacement.

Governments raising the rake
ONGOING HEADWIND

Gaming duty is the tax on the house's rake, and it is rising. The UK nearly doubled remote gaming duty to 40% in April 2026, and Flutter puts the 2027 cost of the iGaming changes at $540M of adjusted EBITDA before $201M of offsetting actions. Sports-betting duty outside horseracing rises to 25% in April 2027. Brazil went further than a tax rise, banning online betting and gaming outright in September 2026. Taxes on revenue land before operating costs, so they fall on margin one-for-one.

Parlay mix — the house's best pricing lever
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Each leg added to a parlay compounds the house edge (Section 02: ~4.5% for one leg at −110, ~17% for four). The company tied Q2 2026's 14.0% structural hold to high same-game parlay use during the World Cup; in Q3 it was unchanged at 12.9%. The bull reading: the pricing edge is real and growing, and whoever sees the most flow can price the most combinations. The bear reading: a higher hold means customers lose their deposits faster, which raises promotional cost to keep them and invites regulators and tax authorities to take a larger share. The Q3 numbers show it: an unchanged structural hold, and promotional spend up 0.6 points of handle.
