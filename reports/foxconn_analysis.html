01 Company Overview

Hon Hai — trading as Foxconn — calls itself the world's largest electronics manufacturer, and says its share of global electronics manufacturing services (EMS) exceeds 40%. It builds other companies' products: smartphones, PCs, servers, networking gear and the components inside them, across more than 240 sites in 24 countries. FY2025 revenue was NT$8.10 trillion, up 18% (about US$260 billion by the company's own conversion), with EPS of NT$13.61, up 24%. The third quarter of 2026 set records on every line: revenue of NT$3.02 trillion (+46%), operating profit of NT$115.4 billion (+64%), the first quarter above NT$100 billion, and net profit of NT$77.5 billion (+34%), or EPS of NT$5.51. Reuters put the LSEG consensus estimate for net profit at NT$69.6 billion, so the result beat it by about 11% (our arithmetic). Over nine months revenue is NT$7.66 trillion (+39%) and EPS NT$13.35, already close to the NT$13.61 of all of FY2025. The results came after the market closed on November 12; the shares had slipped 0.83% that day, rose 0.42% the next session and then fell 9.34% on November 16, the second session after the release, in a market-wide rout.

The story is a change of hand. Foxconn is still best known for assembling phones; in Q3 2026 its Cloud and Networking segment — AI server racks, general servers and switches — reached 54% of revenue (51% in Q2, 40% for FY2025). The catch is the margin. Foxconn keeps about 6 cents of gross profit and under 4 cents of operating profit on each dollar of sales (Q3 2026: 6.02% gross, 3.83% operating). That thin slice on an enormous base is the whole investment case: management's target is to hold operating margin above 3% while revenue keeps growing, and it is beating it — 3.73% for the first nine months of 2026 against 3.2% for all of FY2025, per the company's release. The company kept its target of strong growth for 2026, raised its outlook for all four product segments, and said demand is expected to keep exceeding supply in 2027 because chips, memory and key components are in short supply.

The market has paid for that, but less than for Taiwan as a whole, and the last two weeks were violent. From the October 2021 month-end close of NT$107.00 to the November 20, 2026 close of NT$210.00 the shares rose 96.3%; the TAIEX rose 112.1% over the same window and 31.4% in the twelve months to November 20, against Foxconn's −11.2%. On November 16 the TAIEX fell 6.03% to 41,040.08, its second-largest point drop on record, and Foxconn fell 9.34% to NT$218.50; dealers quoted by Taiwan's Central News Agency pointed to concerns over Nvidia's November 12 announcement that its CEO, Jensen Huang, had resigned, and to futures-related selling. On November 20 the TAIEX fell another 6.23% to 36,033.29, down 17.5% on the week (Xinhua), while Foxconn closed at NT$210.00, down 12.9% on the week and 32.0% below its June 3 closing high. At 12.7x trailing and 10.9x consensus 2026 earnings, the question is whether a 3–4% operating-margin assembler deserves a higher price relative to its earnings because the thing it assembles is now AI infrastructure — or whether the market is now pricing the risk that the AI build-out slows.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale: 240+ sites, 24 countries >50% in-house AI-rack components Near-100% automated CNC & SMT Co-design with tier-1 customers "Local for Local" factories S&P A− credit rating NT$1.45T cash & short-term investments

In contract manufacturing the moat is not a patent; it is the ability to build a new, complicated product in volume, on time, in the right country, at a price no one else can match. An AI rack is a far bigger and more complex product than a phone, and a customer launching a new chip platform cannot afford a supplier that slips a quarter. Foxconn's argument on the Q2 call was that standardisation of rack designs raises, not lowers, the barrier, because the winner is whoever can automate and scale fastest — and that it went to 100% automation on compute trays with the new generation. Its global footprint, built out over five to six years of "Local for Local" investment, also turns tariff and localisation pressure into business: it is adding R&D and manufacturing in Texas, Wisconsin, Ohio and California for US customers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The margin is a rounding error. Gross margin was 6.02% in Q3 2026, down 0.33 points from a year earlier and 0.10 points from Q2. Management said on the Q2 call that "we may not be able to control the direction of gross margin rate." An AI rack carries expensive chips bought from others; on a buy-and-sell basis their value passes through Foxconn's revenue, so the more racks it ships that way, the lower the percentage margin. The company picks buy-and-sell or consignment by customer and project, and says its custom-chip (ASIC) server projects are mostly on consignment.
02
Growth eats cash. In the first nine months of 2026 operating cash flow was an outflow of NT$125.7B and free cash flow an outflow of NT$287.4B, as receivables and inventory swelled with revenue and capital spending rose 43% to NT$161.7B. On StockAnalysis's measure Foxconn went from net cash of NT$166.9B at June 30 to net debt of NT$71.8B at September 30, and the debt ratio (total liabilities over total assets) rose to 64% from 62%. On the Q2 call the CFO said there are no plans for a cash capital increase and that debt will be one of the main funding sources, with net debt kept below 1.5 times a year's EBITDA (operating earnings before depreciation); the Q3 release says the group can issue corporate bonds and plans to use convertible bonds for flexible financing.
03
A handful of customers. Foxconn does not name customers, but the business is built around a few very large brand owners and cloud providers. Its two biggest engines — the phone cycle and the AI-rack cycle — each depend on another company's product calendar; a delayed chip platform or a weak phone launch shows up in the next monthly revenue report.
04
Geopolitics is the operating environment. A Taiwanese company with large Chinese manufacturing campuses selling mostly to US customers sits on every fault line of the US–China relationship. The August 2026 revenue report itself flagged the need to "monitor the impact of the volatile global political and economic situation." The Taiwan market is also concentrated in AI and chip stocks: on November 16 CNA reported that TSMC alone accounted for about 1,000 points of the TAIEX's fall and over 40% of the local market's total value, so a sell-off in AI shares moves Foxconn with it, whatever its own results.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items separate operating performance from the bottom line. First, net profit grew more slowly than operating profit: Q3 net profit of NT$77.5 billion was 67% of operating profit of NT$115.4 billion, against 82% a year earlier (NT$57.7 billion of NT$70.5 billion) and 63% in Q2 (our arithmetic from the release and StockAnalysis's quarterly rows). That is why net profit rose 34% while operating profit rose 64%. In Q2 the CFO had attributed the gap to NT$5.9 billion less in fund-investment gains and NT$2.7 billion more in net interest expense as AI working capital grew, and a tax rate 2.2 points higher at 26.2%. Second, share count: shares outstanding were 14.07 billion at September 30 against 14.03 billion at June 30 (StockAnalysis), after convertible-bond conversions added 25.2 million shares in Q1 2026 and 39.2 million in 2025 and the 2025 share exchange with Teco Electric & Machinery added 72.5 million — small dilution, but a reason per-share growth trails profit growth slightly.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Foxconn builds the racks the cloud providers' AI spending is buying, and says it is gaining share: Cloud and Networking reached 54% of revenue in Q3 2026, the Q3 release expects AI server revenue to keep growing in Q4, and operating margin is rising (3.73% over nine months against a 3% target) even as gross margin dips. Q3 net profit of NT$77.5 billion beat the LSEG consensus estimate of NT$69.6 billion by about 11%, and the company raised its outlook for all four segments. At 12.7x trailing and 10.9x 2026 consensus earnings, with a 3.4% dividend yield and EPS growing 24–29%, the stock trades at a PEG of about 0.30 (the P/E divided by expected earnings growth), while all 21 analysts rate it a buy with an average target 62% above the price; Nomura and Macquarie set targets of NT$435 the day after the Q2 print, and management said in August it was moving toward half of the Vera Rubin rack market. The November fall was market-wide: Foxconn's 12.9% drop in the week to November 20 was smaller than the TAIEX's 17.5%.

Bear Case

Revenue is not profit, and profit is not cash. Gross margin is drifting down (6.25% in 2024, 6.15% in 2025, 6.02% in Q3 2026), with costly rack components passing through revenue; free cash flow was −NT$287.4 billion in the first nine months as receivables and inventory ballooned, capital spending rose 43%, and on StockAnalysis's measure net cash has turned into net debt of NT$71.8 billion. The business answers to a few customers' product calendars and sits on the US–China fault line. The market has noticed: a record quarter that beat the consensus estimate was followed by a 9.34% fall two sessions later as Taiwan's AI-heavy market sold off, the stock is 16.3% below its price of early October and 32% below its June high, and it has trailed the TAIEX by about 43 points over twelve months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margin compression as rack values rise. Each new AI generation carries more expensive components; on buy-and-sell contracts that inflates revenue and dilutes margin. Management says it may not be able to control the direction of the gross-margin rate, and its custom-chip projects are mostly on consignment. If operating margin falls back toward 3%, earnings growth will lag revenue growth.
02
Working capital and leverage. In the first nine months of 2026 operating cash flow was −NT$125.7B; on StockAnalysis's measure net cash went from +NT$166.9B at June 30 to −NT$71.8B at September 30; capital spending is up 43%, with debt one of the main funding sources. The company's own limit — net debt below 1.5x EBITDA — is the line to watch.
03
Customer and policy concentration. A few brand owners and cloud providers, US tariff and localisation policy, and large manufacturing in China make Foxconn's results depend on decisions it does not make. The company itself flagged the "volatile global political and economic situation" in its August revenue report.
Industry Commentary
From box-builder to rack-builder
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

The AI build-out has changed what a contract manufacturer sells. A cloud provider no longer orders servers; it orders finished, liquid-cooled racks, tested and ready to wire into a data center, sometimes a whole modular data center. That rewards the firms that can design for manufacturing from day one, make the cooling, power and connectors themselves and ship at volume on several continents. Foxconn's answer on the Q2 call was that product standardisation "does not mean that entry barriers are lower," and that large customers will concentrate on suppliers with scaled delivery. Bob Chen, president of Foxconn's semiconductor business group, put the shift this way in a fireside chat during Taiwan's semiconductor conference week (company release, September 2, 2026): "The system itself becomes the new unit of innovation and competition." The open question for every rack builder is who keeps the margin when the rack's chips are worth far more than the assembly.

Buy-and-sell vs. consignment
ACCOUNTING MATTERS

How a rack is contracted changes the reported numbers. Under buy-and-sell, Foxconn buys the GPUs and other expensive parts, books them as inventory and bills them through revenue: sales and working capital balloon, margin percentages fall. Under consignment the customer supplies those parts: revenue is smaller, margins look higher, cash needs drop. Chiang said the company chooses between the two by customer and project, and that higher-customisation ASIC servers are mostly consignment. For a reader, that means revenue growth overstates and gross-margin percentages understate what is happening to profit in NT$ — operating profit, up 64% in Q3 2026, is the cleaner measure. CFO David Huang gave the scale on the Q2 call: about 10% of AI-server revenue came from ASIC servers in 2025 and slightly more in H1 2026, and because those projects are mostly consignment, the revenue in the accounts "does not fully reflect the actual business scale." He expects the consignment share to rise gradually.

Localisation as a business line
DOUBLE-EDGED

Tariffs and industrial policy push customers to build closer to where they sell. Foxconn says it anticipated the trend five to six years ago and nearly doubled its number of sites; it is now expanding in Texas, Wisconsin, Ohio and California for US customers, and in Mexico and Vietnam. That is a reason customers choose the largest supplier — few others can follow them everywhere — but it is also why capex is rising more than 30% this year, and new factories pay off only if the volumes arrive as planned. Chairman Young Liu put it this way at SEMICON Taiwan on September 2, 2026 (CNA/Focus Taiwan): "We all have to think about how to make with Taiwan, not in Taiwan."
