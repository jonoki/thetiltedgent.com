01 Company Overview

Hon Hai — trading as Foxconn — calls itself the world's largest electronics manufacturer, and says its share of global electronics manufacturing services (EMS) exceeds 40%. It builds other companies' products: smartphones, PCs, servers, networking gear and the components inside them, across more than 240 sites in 24 countries. FY2025 revenue was NT$8.10 trillion, up 18% (about US$260 billion by the company's own conversion), with EPS of NT$13.61, up 24%. In the first half of 2026 revenue rose another 35% to NT$4.65 trillion and operating profit 65%. Second-quarter net profit of NT$59.98 billion (+35%) came in ahead of the NT$58 billion analyst average estimate reported by the Taipei Times; the shares still slipped 2.96% in the next session.

The story is a change of hand. Foxconn is still best known for assembling phones; in Q2 2026 its Cloud and Networking segment — AI server racks, general servers and switches — passed 50% of revenue for the first time, against 40% for FY2025. The catch is the margin. Foxconn keeps about 6 cents of gross profit and under 4 cents of operating profit on each dollar of sales (Q2 2026: 6.12% gross, 3.75% operating). That thin slice on an enormous base is the whole investment case: management's target is to hold operating margin above 3% while revenue compounds, and it is beating it — 3.67% in H1 2026 against 3.00% a year earlier.

The market has paid for that, but less than for Taiwan as a whole. From the October 2021 month-end close of NT$107.00 to the October 2, 2026 close of NT$251.00 the shares rose 134.6%; the TAIEX rose 185.4% over the same window and 83.8% in the last twelve months, against Foxconn's 11.3%. At 16.5x trailing and 13.5x consensus 2026 earnings, the question is whether a 3–4% operating-margin assembler deserves a higher multiple because the thing it assembles is now AI infrastructure.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale: 240+ sites, 24 countries >50% in-house AI-rack components Near-100% automated CNC & SMT Co-design with tier-1 customers "Local for Local" factories S&P A− credit rating NT$1.52T cash & time deposits

In contract manufacturing the moat is not a patent; it is the ability to build a new, complicated product in volume, on time, in the right country, at a price no one else can match. An AI rack is a far bigger and more complex product than a phone, and a customer launching a new chip platform cannot afford a supplier that slips a quarter. Foxconn's argument on the Q2 call was that standardisation of rack designs raises, not lowers, the barrier, because the winner is whoever can automate and scale fastest — and that it went to 100% automation on compute trays with the new generation. Its global footprint, built out over five to six years of "Local for Local" investment, also turns tariff and localisation pressure into business: it is adding R&D and manufacturing in Texas, Wisconsin, Ohio and California for US customers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The margin is a rounding error. Gross margin was 6.12% in Q2 2026, down 0.21 points from a year earlier because of product mix. Management said on the call that "we may not be able to control the direction of gross margin rate." An AI rack carries expensive chips bought from others; on a buy-and-sell basis their value passes through Foxconn's revenue, so the more racks it ships that way, the lower the percentage margin. The company picks buy-and-sell or consignment by customer and project, and says its custom-chip (ASIC) server projects are mostly on consignment.
02
Growth eats cash. H1 2026 operating cash flow was an outflow of NT$69.1B and free cash flow an outflow of NT$150.0B, as receivables and inventory swelled with revenue. Full-year capex is guided up more than 30% on 2025's NT$173.8B. Net cash fell to NT$219.8B from NT$325.1B at March 31. The CFO said there are no plans for a cash capital increase and that debt will be one of the main funding sources, with net debt kept below 1.5x EBITDA.
03
A handful of customers. Foxconn does not name customers, but the business is built around a few very large brand owners and cloud providers. Its two biggest engines — the phone cycle and the AI-rack cycle — each depend on another company's product calendar; a delayed chip platform or a weak phone launch shows up in the next monthly revenue report.
04
Geopolitics is the operating environment. A Taiwanese company with large Chinese manufacturing campuses selling mostly to US customers sits on every fault line of the US–China relationship. The August 2026 revenue report itself flagged the need to "monitor the impact of the volatile global political and economic situation."

Notable One-Off Items

Three items separate operating performance from the bottom line. First, non-operating income collapsed: NT$63 million in Q2 2026 against NT$8.55 billion a year earlier. The CFO attributed it to NT$5.9 billion less in fund-investment gains (some investees had listed and rerated in 2025) and NT$2.7 billion more in net interest expense as AI working capital grew. That is why net profit rose 35% while operating profit rose 68%. Second, tax: the effective rate rose 2.2 points to 26.2%, including an estimated NT$2.6 billion provision for repatriating subsidiary earnings. Third, share count: convertible-bond conversions added 25.2 million shares in Q1 2026 and 39.2 million in 2025, and the 2025 share exchange with Teco Electric & Machinery added 72.5 million — small dilution against 14.03 billion shares, but a reason per-share growth trails profit growth slightly.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Foxconn builds the racks the cloud providers' AI spending is buying, and says it is gaining share: Cloud and Networking passed half of revenue in Q2 2026, AI rack shipments are guided to more than double this year, and operating margin is rising (3.67% in H1 against a 3% target) even as gross margin dips. July and August revenue alone came to 90.7% of last year's Q3. At 16.5x trailing and 13.5x 2026 consensus earnings, with a 2.9% dividend yield and EPS growing 24–26%, the stock trades at a PEG of about 0.45, while 19 of 21 analysts rate it a buy with an average target 38% above the price; Nomura and Macquarie reiterated Buy and lifted their targets to NT$435 the day after the Q2 print, and management says it is moving toward half of the Vera Rubin rack market. Four flat month-ends after a record run is consolidation, not deterioration.

Bear Case

Revenue is not profit, and profit is not cash. Gross margin is drifting down (6.25% in 2024, 6.15% in 2025, 6.12% in Q2 2026) on product mix, as management put it, with costly rack components passing through revenue; non-operating income has vanished; and free cash flow was −NT$150.0B in H1 2026 as receivables and inventory ballooned, with capex now guided up more than 30% and debt one of the main funding sources. The business answers to a few customers' product calendars and sits on the US–China fault line. The market has noticed: a Q2 net-profit beat (NT$59.98 billion against a NT$58 billion average estimate) still met a 2.96% fall the next session, and the stock has made no progress since June while June–August revenue ran more than 50% above a year earlier, and it has trailed the TAIEX by more than 70 points over twelve months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margin compression as rack values rise. Each new AI generation carries more expensive components; on buy-and-sell contracts that inflates revenue and dilutes margin. Management says it may not be able to control the direction of the gross-margin rate, and its custom-chip projects are mostly on consignment. If operating margin falls back toward 3%, earnings growth will lag revenue growth.
02
Working capital and leverage. H1 2026 operating cash flow was −NT$69.1B; net cash has fallen from NT$325.1B to NT$219.8B in one quarter; capex is guided up 30%+, with debt one of the main funding sources. The company's own limit — net debt below 1.5x EBITDA — is the line to watch.
03
Customer and policy concentration. A few brand owners and cloud providers, US tariff and localisation policy, and large manufacturing in China make Foxconn's results depend on decisions it does not make. The company itself flagged the "volatile global political and economic situation" in its August revenue report.
Industry Commentary
From box-builder to rack-builder
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

The AI build-out has changed what a contract manufacturer sells. A cloud provider no longer orders servers; it orders finished, liquid-cooled racks, tested and ready to wire into a data center, sometimes a whole modular data center. That rewards the firms that can design for manufacturing from day one, make the cooling, power and connectors themselves and ship at volume on several continents. Foxconn's answer on the Q2 call was that product standardisation "does not mean that entry barriers are lower," and that large customers will concentrate on suppliers with scaled delivery. Bob Chen, president of Foxconn's semiconductor business group, put the shift this way in a fireside chat during Taiwan's semiconductor conference week (company release, September 2, 2026): "The system itself becomes the new unit of innovation and competition." The open question for every rack builder is who keeps the margin when the rack's chips are worth far more than the assembly.

Buy-and-sell vs. consignment
ACCOUNTING MATTERS

How a rack is contracted changes the reported numbers. Under buy-and-sell, Foxconn buys the GPUs and other expensive parts, books them as inventory and bills them through revenue: sales and working capital balloon, margin percentages fall. Under consignment the customer supplies those parts: revenue is smaller, margins look higher, cash needs drop. Chiang said the company chooses between the two by customer and project, and that higher-customisation ASIC servers are mostly consignment. For a reader, that means revenue growth overstates and gross-margin percentages understate what is happening to profit in NT$ — operating profit, up 68% in Q2 2026, is the cleaner measure. CFO David Huang gave the scale on the Q2 call: about 10% of AI-server revenue came from ASIC servers in 2025 and slightly more in H1 2026, and because those projects are mostly consignment, the revenue in the accounts "does not fully reflect the actual business scale." He expects the consignment share to rise gradually.

Localisation as a business line
DOUBLE-EDGED

Tariffs and industrial policy push customers to build closer to where they sell. Foxconn says it anticipated the trend five to six years ago and nearly doubled its number of sites; it is now expanding in Texas, Wisconsin, Ohio and California for US customers, and in Mexico and Vietnam. That is a reason customers choose the largest supplier — few others can follow them everywhere — but it is also why capex is rising more than 30% this year, and new factories pay off only if the volumes arrive as planned. Chairman Young Liu framed it in a fireside chat the same week (company release, September 2, 2026): "From now on, we have to think about how to 'Made With Taiwan', not 'Made In Taiwan'."
