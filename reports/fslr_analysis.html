01 Company Overview

First Solar is a US-headquartered maker of utility-scale solar panels, and it builds them differently from its import competition. Instead of crystalline-silicon wafers, First Solar deposits a thin film of cadmium telluride (CdTe) onto glass in a single, continuous factory process. It sells utility-scale modules to developers, independent power producers and utilities; 96% of 2025 net sales came from the United States. FY2025 revenue was $5.22B (FY2024: $4.2B), GAAP diluted EPS $14.21, on a record 17.5 GW of modules sold.

Here is the hand the market is actually playing. In 2025 First Solar recognised $1.6B of Section 45X manufacturing tax credits — a federal payment for every watt of solar hardware made and sold in America, created by the Inflation Reduction Act. Its operating income that year was $1.60B. Put the two side by side and the uncomfortable arithmetic is that, before the credit, the business earned roughly nothing at the operating line; the CFO put 2025 gross margin excluding 45X at about 7% against 41% reported. For 2026 management now guides $2.07–$2.19B of credits against an unchanged $2.4–$2.6B of gross profit; in the first nine months of 2026, $1.50B of government grants (the line where 45X credits are booked) went into cost of sales against $1.80B of gross profit. So the stock at $185.90 and 10.8x trailing earnings is really a price on two things: how long Washington keeps paying, and whether tariffs on imported panels let First Solar charge enough to stand on its own when it stops. The share price has fallen from a $306.79 May 2026 month-end (all-time closing high $318.25 on June 3) to $185.90 on November 3 without the profits getting worse: Q3 2026 diluted EPS rose 25% to $5.30 even as net sales fell 23%. That gap between the income statement and the share price is the whole report.

Statutory step-down
100% → 0% by 2033
Credit value falls to 75% in 2030, 50% in 2031, 25% in 2032 and nothing from 2033 (26 U.S.C. §45X(b)(3); Crux 45X guide). From January 1, 2026 the One Big Beautiful Bill Act also layers on prohibited-foreign-entity sourcing thresholds. The backlog runs "through 2030" — the first step-down year.

Illustrative math (estimate, not guidance): credit-years left from 2026 = 4 full years (2026–2029) + 0.75 + 0.50 + 0.25 = 5.5. At the 2026 guide midpoint of $2.13B a year and flat qualifying volume, that is ≈ 5.5 × $2.13B ≈ $11.7B undiscounted — against an enterprise value of $18.68B. Roughly 63% of what the market pays for the whole company could be accounted for by remaining credits before any discounting, compliance risk or volume change. The other ~37% is the bet that a US-made, non-silicon panel can earn a living after 2032.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

No polysilicon in the supply chain US manufacturing scale → 45X Domestic-content demand (~41 GW of backlog at June 30) Backlog through 2030 Tariff & import-floor shelter Net cash $1.5B · D/E 0.03

The real moat is where the atoms come from. Because CdTe needs no polysilicon, First Solar does not buy the polysilicon, wafers and silicon cells that trade actions target — so the August 2026 Section 232 proclamation (15% tariff plus minimum import prices of $21/kg for polysilicon, $0.22/W for cells and $0.38/W for modules, effective December 4, 2026) raises competitors' floor and leaves First Solar's cost base alone. The weakness is the mirror image: a moat built from policy can be drained by policy. Tariffs, domestic-content bonuses and 45X are all acts of government, and so is their repeal.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The earnings are mostly a tax credit. $1.6B of 45X against $1.60B of 2025 operating income; a 2026 guide in which credits are 80–91% of gross profit. Any change to eligibility — including the prohibited-foreign-entity sourcing tests in force since January 1, 2026 — hits the income statement almost dollar for dollar.
02
The backlog is shrinking. 50.1 GW at year-end 2025, 44.0 GW at September 30, 2026 — three straight quarterly declines — and 2026 volume guidance was cut to 16.0–17.0 GW at Q3. Management says it is choosing price over volume; if that choice persists into 2027, volume — and with it 45X, which is paid per watt — flattens.
03
Stand-alone economics are thin. ~7% gross margin ex-45X in 2025. The step-down starts in 2030; the business has roughly four full-credit years to prove that tariff-protected US pricing (US bookings at ~$0.36/W versus a $0.38/W import floor) can replace the subsidy.
04
Litigation and insider flow. Swift Current Energy, an independent power producer, sued First Solar in New York state court in September 2026 over more than 2.4 million Series 6 Plus and Series 7 modules bought in 2021–2024, alleging manufacturing errors in more than 1.6 million of them (PV Tech, October 19, 2026); the CEO said on the Q3 call the company would file its response shortly. First Solar is also the plaintiff in patent suits over TOPCon solar cells, the latest against JA Solar and Corning's American Panel Solutions, and said in mid-September it would withdraw its complaint at the US International Trade Commission (pv magazine, October 5, 2026). MarketBeat lists no insider purchases against 13 sales over the last 12 months.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the credits are being earned but not cashed: government grants receivable rose from $0.63B at December 31, 2025 to $1.87B at September 30, 2026, and nine-month operating cash flow was −$403M against +$815M a year earlier; the CFO said on the Q3 call that no decision has been made on selling 2026 credits. The trailing figures also still hold Q2 2026's one-off $89M net benefit from a tariff recovery under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (pv magazine USA, August 3, 2026) — without it, Q2 operating income of $450M would have been closer to $361M. Second, selling credits costs money: in 2025 First Solar sold $701.9M of credits for $668.1M and a further $699.7M for $668.2M — roughly 4.5–4.8 cents lost per dollar (our arithmetic) — and a $15.6M transfer loss hit Q2 2025 cost of sales. The 2026 guide assumes no credit sales, which flatters 2026 margins relative to 2025 and delays the cash. Third, the net cash balance fell from $2.4B at December 31, 2025 to $1.5B at September 30, 2026 as capex ran and credits sat on the balance sheet as receivables.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 10.8x trailing and ~10.6x FY2026 consensus earnings for what management calls the only fully integrated utility-scale solar manufacturer without dependency on the Chinese crystalline-silicon supply chain, with $1.5B of net cash, a new $500M buyback, a 44.0 GW / $13.5B backlog through 2030 and a government that puts a $0.38/W floor under imported modules from December 4. The credits are not a rumour — they are statute through 2029, and the illustrative math says remaining credits alone could cover roughly 63% of the enterprise value before discounting. Consensus sees EPS climbing to $23.08 in 2027. Management says it has raised prices since the Section 232 action; if US pricing holds behind the new tariff wall, the business earns its way toward standing without the subsidy before the step-down begins.

Bear Case

Strip out 45X and 2025 gross margin was ~7%; the 2026 credit guide is 80–91% of gross profit. That is not a manufacturer on a low multiple — it is a subsidy stream with a factory attached, and the stream steps down from 2030 and ends in 2033. The backlog has shrunk three quarters running (50.1 → 44.0 GW in nine months), Q3 revenue fell 23%, 2026 volume guidance was cut, nine-month operating cash flow was negative as credits went unsold, a customer is suing over module defects, insiders sold without a single purchase, and 11.4% of the float is short. Every leg of the thesis — credits, tariffs, domestic-content demand — can be changed by the next Congress or the next proclamation.

Top 3 Risks

01
Policy concentration. 45X ($2.07–2.19B guided for 2026), tariffs and domestic-content incentives together underpin most of the profit. The foreign-entity-of-concern (FEOC) sourcing tests in force since January 1, 2026 add a compliance layer on top of the statutory step-down.
02
Backlog attrition. Three consecutive quarterly declines in contracted backlog (50.1 → 47.9 → 45.1 → 44.0 GW), and 2026 volume guidance cut to 16.0–17.0 GW. Because 45X is paid per watt sold, lower volume cuts both revenue and credits.
03
Selling the credits, and when the cash arrives. Credits sold in 2025 cleared at ~95 cents on the dollar; no 2026 credits had been sold by the Q3 call, leaving $1.87B of grants receivable on the balance sheet. Net cash fell from $2.4B to $1.5B in nine months, and nine-month operating cash flow was −$403M.
Industry Commentary
Section 45X: the credit that is the earnings
CRITICAL DEPENDENCY

Section 45X was added to the tax code on August 16, 2022, by the Inflation Reduction Act, for components produced and sold after December 31, 2022. It pays per unit of US-made hardware — $0.07 per watt for a finished solar module, with separate component credits — and can be sold to other taxpayers, which is why First Solar can turn it into cash (in 2025, $1.4B sold at roughly 95 cents on the dollar). Its growth in First Solar's P&L — $1.0B (2024), $1.6B (2025), $2.07–2.19B guided (2026) — tracks US factory output. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act kept the solar credit but added prohibited-foreign-entity sourcing thresholds from January 1, 2026 and left the 2030–2032 phase-down in place. For a CdTe producer with no polysilicon, those sourcing rules are less of a burden than for silicon rivals — but they are one more thing an auditor has to sign.

Trade walls: Section 232 and the import floor
POLICY TAILWIND

The August 2026 Section 232 proclamation imposes a 15% ad valorem tariff and minimum import prices — $21/kg polysilicon, $100/kg ingots and wafers, $0.22/W cells, $0.38/W modules — effective December 4, 2026. On the Q3 call Mark Widmar said: "Since the announcement of 232, we have revised and increased our pricing," but that "the price discovery is going to take a while before you'll start to see that volume being executed." pv magazine USA's account of the call has developers holding back on 2029–2030 module purchases amid uncertainty over foreign-entity sourcing rules, the minimum import prices and anti-stockpiling enforcement. The market's response to the proclamation was muted — August closed down 4.3% — because a tariff floor raises what competitors must charge but does not change First Solar's 2026 volume, which is largely contracted.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

The bull framing: 10.8x trailing earnings, 7.5x EV/EBITDA, a PEG of 0.37 and net cash — for a company whose policy moat just got a wall built around it. The credit runs at full value through 2029; that is four more years of cash to fund capacity and technology (the CuRe program) that could lower cost enough to compete after the step-down. The bear framing: a low multiple on subsidised earnings is not cheap, it is correct. The honest way to value FSLR is in two pieces — a declining annuity of credits (≈$11.7B undiscounted at flat volume, our estimate) plus a stand-alone module business that earned ~7% gross margin in 2025. How you size the second piece is the whole trade. Watch US booking prices against the $0.38/W import floor in 2027 contracts: that is where the answer shows up first.
