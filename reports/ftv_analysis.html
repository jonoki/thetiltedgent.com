01 Company Overview

Fortive is a company that has been cut in half twice and kept the half it liked. Born in July 2016 as a spin-off from Danaher, it joined the S&P 500 right away (July 1, 2016). It later separated Vontier. Then on June 28, 2025 it spun off its Precision Technologies segment as Ralliant (NYSE: RAL), handing shareholders one Ralliant share for every three Fortive shares held on June 16, 2025. About 113 million Ralliant shares went out the door. What is left — "new Fortive" — is two segments: Intelligent Operating Solutions (Fluke, Industrial Scientific, Accruent, Gordian, Intelex, ServiceChannel) and Advanced Healthcare Solutions (ASP, Censis, Fluke Biomedical, Landauer, Provation). Management described it at the split as roughly 50% recurring revenue.

Trailing-twelve-month revenue from continuing operations is $4.40B, up 7.0%, with a 63.5% gross margin (our sum of the last four quarters from Fortive's releases). Q3 2026, reported on October 28, was the fastest-growing quarter since the split: revenue $1,107.5M, +7.8% reported / +7.2% core, adjusted EPS of $0.77, up 13.2%, and full-year adjusted EPS guidance raised a second time, to $3.10–$3.14. In July the market sold a similar beat; this time the stock rose 4.04% on the day and 2.70% the next. The business is steady and growing a little faster. The per-share story is still driven by the cash coming out of the company: about $2.3 billion of buybacks since the spin, roughly 13% of the shares, with the share count down 9.10% in a year (StockAnalysis). Adjusted net earnings rose only 3.1% in Q3; the rest of the EPS growth came from having fewer shares. The question for a holder is how much of the earnings growth is the business and how much is the buyback, and whether a 31.2x trailing GAAP multiple pays for either.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Trusted instrument brands (Fluke) ~50% recurring revenue Compliance-driven demand Software + hardware installed base Fortive Business System 63% gross margin

The moat is trust in niche tools that nobody wants to get wrong. A gas detector on a refinery worker's belt, a dosimeter badge in a radiology suite, a sterilizer in a surgical department: these are bought for reliability and compliance, and switching brands carries safety and audit risk that dwarfs the price difference. Management has spent a decade adding software and services around those instruments to turn one-time sales into recurring revenue. The 63.5% gross margin is the evidence it works: that is closer to software economics than to an industrial manufacturer's. The weakness is the price paid to build it. Goodwill of $7.33B plus $1.93B of other intangibles make up $9.27B of $11.50B total assets (October 2, 2026). The returns on that capital are thin: ROIC (return on invested capital) of 8.32% against a cost of capital StockAnalysis estimates at 8.70%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Returns below the cost of capital. ROIC of 8.32% and ROE of 10.55% on an acquisition-heavy balance sheet (StockAnalysis). The business earns high margins on sales but low returns on what it cost to assemble. Buybacks lift per-share figures without fixing that ratio.
02
Debt-assisted buybacks. Repurchases in the first nine months of 2026 ($1,000.4M) exceeded operating cash flow ($832.4M). Net commercial paper rose $455.7M, and long-term debt stands at $3.51B (October 2, 2026, none of it due within a year) after $1.1B of 4.75% and 5.25% notes priced in May 2026 to refinance 3.15% notes that matured in June 2026. The refinancing costs more interest than the debt it replaced. Debt is 2.67 times a year's EBITDA (StockAnalysis).
03
Healthcare margin slippage. AHS adjusted EBITDA margin fell 0.8 percentage points in Q2 2026 and another 2.3 points in Q3, to 25.8%, while core growth slowed to 3.1%. The segment is under a third of revenue, and it is the part of Fortive most exposed to hospital budgets and to the "healthcare and government spending policy" uncertainty management flagged at the spin.
04
Tariffs cut both ways. Tariff-related pricing pressure hit demand in mid-2025. In Q2 2026, Fortive booked a $4.5M pretax recovery for refunds of IEEPA tariffs after the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 ruling, and excluded it from adjusted results. Trade policy is a named, recurring swing factor in this company's reported numbers.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the figures. First, the spin itself: FY2025 GAAP net income of $579.2M includes $46.5M from discontinued operations (Ralliant's first half of 2025, less later charges); continuing-operations net income for FY2025 was $532.7M (Q4 2025 release). Any trailing ratio that mixes the two bases is wrong. Second, the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is structural, not one-off: Q3 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.57 against adjusted $0.77, and the main add-back is amortization of acquired intangibles, which recurs every quarter. Third, the year-ago quarters were weaker on a GAAP basis than on an adjusted one, which is why GAAP EPS growth (+62.9% in Q3) runs far ahead of adjusted growth (+13.2%). The tax rate (11.04% TTM effective, StockAnalysis) is also low, and it flatters net margin compared with a normal rate.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

How the spin affects the chart. This chart plots FTV's actual month-end closes — split-adjusted, but not adjusted for dividends and not adjusted for the Ralliant spin-off — so there is a real cliff in June 2025. The last pre-spin month-end was $70.19 (May 2025); the first post-spin month-end was $52.13 (June 2025), a −25.7% drop. That drop was not a loss to a continuing holder: shareholders received one Ralliant share for every three Fortive shares held, and the FTV price fell by roughly the value handed over. Yahoo's spin-adjusted history, which divides pre-spin prices by 1.327, implies that about 25% of the pre-spin share price left with Ralliant (1 − 1 ÷ 1.327 ≈ 0.25). A holder who kept both FTV and RAL therefore fared meaningfully better than this FTV-only chart shows — this page does not track RAL, so that combined return is not given. From June 2025 onward there is no adjustment issue: the actual and dividend-adjusted prices differ only by pennies. This edition uses plain closes throughout; the June–August 2026 points ($61.09, $59.21, $58.84) are Yahoo's month-end closes, 7 cents above the dividend-adjusted figures the previous edition carried.

On this actual-price basis, FTV went from $75.71 (October 2021 month-end) to $65.84 (October 29, 2026), a price return of −13.0% on the chart. That FTV-only figure understates what a holder who kept the distributed Ralliant shares earned. Yahoo's price history, which treats the Ralliant distribution as a fractional split, starts the same day at $57.05 and gives +15.4% over the five years to October 29, 2026, against +67.5% for SPY and +85.3% for QQQ (price returns, dividends excluded, same dates). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

New Fortive is doing what a spin is supposed to do. Core growth has accelerated from +5.3% to +6.7% to +7.2% in three quarters, IOS core growth reached 9.1% with its GAAP operating margin up 3.3 points to 29.1%, adjusted EBITDA margin is 30.6%, and management has raised full-year guidance twice, now to $3.10–$3.14. At 21.1x consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS (19.9x FY2027) you get a 63% gross-margin business with trusted brands and ~50% recurring revenue, generating $1.06B of free cash flow (5.4% yield) and shrinking its share count by about 9% a year. If the business keeps growing mid-single digits and buybacks keep adding high single digits per share, double-digit EPS growth is arithmetic rather than a forecast. Five analysts raised their targets the day after the print.

Bear Case

Strip out the buyback and the growth is ordinary. Adjusted net earnings rose 3.1% in Q3; about 10 of the 13.2 points of adjusted-EPS growth came from a 9% smaller share count. In the first nine months of 2026 the company spent $1.0B on repurchases while generating $832M of operating cash, and borrowed the difference, after refinancing 3.15% debt at 4.75–5.25%. On GAAP earnings the stock is 31.2x trailing. ROIC of 8.32% sits below an estimated 8.70% cost of capital, on $9.27B of goodwill and intangibles. Healthcare margins have now fallen two quarters running and its growth slowed to 3.1%. After the rally the stock sits less than 1% below the average analyst target, and two sells and ten holds out of sixteen analysts is not a crowded bet on the upside.

Top 3 Risks

01
Buyback-dependent EPS growth on a levered balance sheet. Net debt is $3.22B, about 2.5 times a year's EBITDA. Repurchases have been outrunning operating cash flow and the refinancing coupon is rising. If the buyback slows, reported EPS growth slows by up to ten points with no change in the business.
02
Growth carried by one segment. Q3's 7.2% core growth came from IOS (+9.1%) while Healthcare slowed to +3.1%. Subtracting the first nine months from the new full-year range leaves Q4 adjusted EPS of roughly $0.89–$0.93, about flat on Q4 2025's $0.90, so per-share growth is set to slow sharply in the last quarter of the year.
03
Healthcare policy and tariff exposure. AHS is under a third of revenue and its adjusted EBITDA margin fell 2.3 percentage points in Q3 2026, after 0.8 points in Q2. Management has named healthcare and government spending policy and tariffs as headwinds, and tariff refunds and charges already move reported results.
Industry Commentary
Spin-offs and the restated past
READ WITH CARE

Fortive is an example of the conglomerate break-up cycle: carved out of Danaher in 2016, it later separated Vontier and now Ralliant. Each spin leaves a statistical trail. Share prices need adjusting, revenue histories get restated, and third-party databases often stitch restated and unrestated years together. For FTV specifically, StockAnalysis's FY2021–22 revenue includes Precision Technologies and FY2023 onward does not. The company's 2025 comparison figures are restated continuing-operations numbers. The 5-year beta mostly describes the old company; the 52-week window now runs entirely after the spin. The practical rule is to treat anything dated before June 28, 2025 as describing a larger, different business, and to use continuing-operations figures only.

Tariffs, refunds and industrial demand
ONGOING SWING FACTOR

In June 2025 Fortive cited "increased pressure on tariff-related pricing and customer demand driven largely by heightened uncertainty in trade, healthcare and government spending policy." In 2026 the direction reversed in part. After the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 decision invalidating certain tariffs imposed under IEEPA, U.S. Customs began processing refund claims, and Fortive recorded a $4.5M pretax recovery in Q2 2026, excluded from adjusted results. The amounts are small next to $1.1B of quarterly revenue. What matters more is that trade policy now appears as a named line in the non-GAAP reconciliation, and as a demand variable for customers buying instruments.

The valuation — bull and bear framing
DEBATE

FTV trades at 17.37x EV/EBITDA, 21.1x forward adjusted EPS and 31.2x trailing GAAP EPS, with a 5.4% FCF yield on company figures. The bull framing: adjusted EPS is the right lens because the main add-back is amortization of past acquisitions, a non-cash charge, and free cash flow of $1.06B exceeding TTM GAAP net income from continuing operations of $658M confirms it. At 18.5x FCF (market cap ÷ FCF) for a high-margin instrument and software franchise shrinking its share count about 9% a year, the stock is reasonably priced. The bear framing: amortization is the cost of growth bought rather than built, and the returns on those purchases (ROIC 8.32%) do not clear the cost of capital. Buybacks funded partly with debt are a timing bet on the share price, not a return on the business. The test is simple: whether core growth stays above 5% once the comparisons get harder, and whether ROIC rises as the share count falls.
