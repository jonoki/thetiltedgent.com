01 Company Overview

Fortive is a company that has been cut in half twice and kept the half it liked. Born in July 2016 as a spin-off from Danaher, it joined the S&P 500 right away (July 1, 2016). It later separated Vontier. Then on June 28, 2025 it spun off its Precision Technologies segment as Ralliant (NYSE: RAL), handing shareholders one Ralliant share for every three Fortive shares held on June 16, 2025. About 113 million Ralliant shares went out the door. What is left — "new Fortive" — is two segments: Intelligent Operating Solutions (Fluke, Industrial Scientific, Accruent, Gordian, Intelex, ServiceChannel) and Advanced Healthcare Solutions (ASP, Censis, Fluke Biomedical, Landauer, Provation). Management described it at the split as roughly 50% recurring revenue.

Trailing-twelve-month revenue from continuing operations is $4.32B, up 5.6%, with a 63.2% gross margin. Q2 2026 was the best quarter since the split: revenue $1,097M, +7.9% reported / +6.7% core, adjusted EPS of $0.74, up 28.5%, and full-year adjusted EPS guidance raised to $2.95–$3.05. The stock fell 7.10% the day those numbers came out. That tells you what this report is about. The business is steady. The per-share story is being driven by the cash coming out of the company: about $2 billion of buybacks over four quarters on a $16.6B market cap, with the share count down 8.24% in a year. The question for a holder is how much of the earnings growth is the business and how much is the buyback, and whether a 32.8x trailing GAAP multiple pays for either.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Trusted instrument brands (Fluke) ~50% recurring revenue Compliance-driven demand Software + hardware installed base Fortive Business System 63% gross margin

The moat is trust in niche tools that nobody wants to get wrong. A gas detector on a refinery worker's belt, a dosimeter badge in a radiology suite, a sterilizer in a surgical department: these are bought for reliability and compliance, and switching brands carries safety and audit risk that dwarfs the price difference. Management has spent a decade adding software and services around those instruments to turn one-time sales into recurring revenue. The 63.2% gross margin is the evidence it works: that is closer to software economics than to an industrial manufacturer's. The weakness is the price paid to build it. Goodwill of $7.34B plus $2.02B of other intangibles make up $9.36B of $11.62B total assets (July 3, 2026). The returns on that capital are thin: ROIC 7.58% against a WACC StockAnalysis estimates at 8.44%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Returns below the cost of capital. ROIC of 7.58% and ROE of 7.32% on an acquisition-heavy balance sheet (StockAnalysis). The business earns high margins on sales but low returns on what it cost to assemble. Buybacks lift per-share figures without fixing that ratio.
02
Debt-assisted buybacks. H1 2026 repurchases ($700.3M) exceeded operating cash flow ($521.0M). Net commercial paper rose $433.6M, and long-term debt stands at $3.51B after $1.1B of 4.75% and 5.25% notes priced in May 2026 to refinance 3.15% notes that matured in June 2026. The refinancing costs more interest than the debt it replaced. Debt/EBITDA is 2.84x.
03
Healthcare margin slippage. AHS adjusted EBITDA margin fell 80bps in Q2 2026 on 6% revenue growth. The segment is a third of revenue, and it is the part of Fortive most exposed to hospital budgets and to the "healthcare and government spending policy" uncertainty management flagged at the spin.
04
Tariffs cut both ways. Tariff-related pricing pressure hit demand in mid-2025. In Q2 2026, Fortive booked a $4.5M pretax recovery for refunds of IEEPA tariffs after the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 ruling, and excluded it from adjusted results. Trade policy is a named, recurring swing factor in this company's reported numbers.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the figures. First, the spin itself: FY2025 GAAP net income of $579.2M includes $114.3M from discontinued operations (Ralliant's first half of 2025). Continuing-operations net income for FY2025 was about $464.9M (derived: $579.2M − $114.3M). Any trailing ratio that mixes the two bases is wrong. Second, the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is structural, not one-off: Q2 2026 GAAP EPS was $0.51 against adjusted $0.74, and $0.30 of the difference is amortization of acquired intangibles, which recurs every quarter. Third, Q2 2026 includes small gains excluded from adjusted results: a $4.6M foreign-currency gain on euro debt and $4.5M of IEEPA tariff refunds. The tax rate (10.39% TTM effective) is also low, and it flatters net margin compared with a normal rate.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

How the spin affects the chart. The raw FTV price series has a cliff in June 2025. The last pre-spin month-end was $70.17 (May 2025); the first post-spin month-end was $52.13 (June 2025). That drop was not a loss. Shareholders received one Ralliant share per three Fortive shares, and the price fell by roughly the value handed over. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

New Fortive is doing what a spin is supposed to do. Core growth has accelerated from +5.3% to +6.7% in two quarters, IOS operating margin is up 230bps, adjusted EBITDA margin is 29.5%, and management has raised guidance to $2.95–$3.05. At 18.2x consensus adjusted EPS you get a 63% gross-margin business with trusted brands and ~50% recurring revenue, generating $1.04B of free cash flow (6.3% yield) and shrinking its share count by 8% a year. If the business keeps compounding mid-single digits and buybacks keep adding high single digits per share, double-digit EPS growth is arithmetic rather than a forecast. The consensus target of $64.31 sits 16.7% above the price, and the stock is below its 10-month average after selling off on a beat.

Bear Case

Strip out the buyback and the growth is ordinary. About 11 of the 28.5 points of Q2 adjusted-EPS growth came from a 10% smaller share count. In H1 2026 the company spent $700M on repurchases while generating $521M of operating cash, and borrowed the difference at 4.75–5.25% to replace 3.15% money. On GAAP earnings the stock is 32.8x trailing. ROIC of 7.58% sits below an estimated 8.44% cost of capital, on $9.36B of goodwill and intangibles. Healthcare margins slipped last quarter, the Q2 comparison base was a soft post-spin quarter, and the market sold the best print since the split by 7%. Two sells and nine holds out of fifteen analysts is not a crowded long.

Top 3 Risks

01
Buyback-dependent EPS growth on a levered balance sheet. Net debt is $3.22B (2.84x EBITDA). Repurchases have been outrunning operating cash flow and the refinancing coupon is rising. If the buyback slows, reported EPS growth slows by up to ten points with no change in the business.
02
Growth normalisation as the easy comparison rolls off. Q2 2026 lapped a post-spin quarter management had guided "flat to slightly down". Q3 and Q4 comparisons will be tougher, and the 6.7% core print is the high-water mark the market will now measure against.
03
Healthcare policy and tariff exposure. AHS is a third of revenue and its adjusted EBITDA margin fell 80bps last quarter. Management has named healthcare and government spending policy and tariffs as headwinds, and tariff refunds and charges already move reported results.
Industry Commentary
Spin-offs and the restated past
READ WITH CARE

Fortive is an example of the conglomerate break-up cycle: carved out of Danaher in 2016, it later separated Vontier and now Ralliant. Each spin leaves a statistical trail. Share prices need adjusting, revenue histories get restated, and third-party databases often stitch restated and unrestated years together. For FTV specifically, StockAnalysis's FY2021–22 revenue includes Precision Technologies and FY2023 onward does not. The company's Q2 2025 comparison figures are restated continuing-operations numbers. The 5-year beta and 52-week statistics mostly describe the old company. The practical rule is to treat anything dated before June 28, 2025 as describing a larger, different business, and to use continuing-operations figures only.

Tariffs, refunds and industrial demand
ONGOING SWING FACTOR

In June 2025 Fortive cited "increased pressure on tariff-related pricing and customer demand driven largely by heightened uncertainty in trade, healthcare and government spending policy." In 2026 the direction reversed in part. After the Supreme Court's February 20, 2026 decision invalidating certain tariffs imposed under IEEPA, U.S. Customs began processing refund claims, and Fortive recorded a $4.5M pretax recovery in Q2 2026, excluded from adjusted results. The amounts are small next to $1.1B of quarterly revenue. What matters more is that trade policy now appears as a named line in the non-GAAP reconciliation, and as a demand variable for customers buying instruments.

The valuation — bull and bear framing
DEBATE

FTV trades at 16.07x EV/EBITDA, 18.2x forward adjusted EPS and 32.8x trailing GAAP EPS, with a 6.3% FCF yield on company figures. The bull framing: adjusted EPS is the right lens because the main add-back is amortization of past acquisitions, a non-cash charge, and free cash flow of $1.04B exceeding GAAP net income of $534M confirms it.
