01 Company Overview

GoDaddy describes itself as the world's largest domain name registrar. The business starts with a name — a small business buys a domain — and then tries to sell everything that hangs off it: hosting, a website builder, email, security, payments, and now an AI assistant called Airo. It has 20.5 million customers, an average revenue per user of $250 a year, and $4.42B of annualized recurring revenue. FY2025 revenue was $4.951B, up 8.26%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $6.22. Trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is $1.70B — against a market value of $13.27B at the September 21 close.

That ratio is the whole story. Twenty months ago the market was paying for GoDaddy as an AI winner: the stock closed January 2025 at $212.65. It then fell in eleven consecutive month-ends from May 2025 to March 2026, bottoming at an $82.67 month-end close — down 61% from the peak — while revenue, margins and cash flow all kept rising. Two things broke the spell. On February 24, 2026 the company disclosed that Q4 bookings growth had slowed to 5%, and the stock fell from $92.30 to $79.12 in a day (figures as stated in the securities class action filed afterwards). Then on July 30, 2026 a solid Q2 came with a narrowed revenue guide and just 22 thousand net new customers in the quarter; the stock closed down 16.70% the next day. The question underneath both: is GoDaddy a compounding subscription business that happens to be growing slowly, or a customer base that is quietly going ex-growth while AI changes how small businesses find — and build — a web presence?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Domain renewal lock-in 20.5M-customer installed base Brand at the point of first purchase Bundling (domain → site → email → payments) 33% FCF margin funds buybacks

The moat is inertia. Moving a domain, a website and a business email address is a chore with a downtime risk attached, and a small-business owner has better things to do. That is why ARPU can rise 8.7% while the customer count stands still: the installed base pays more every year and mostly stays. The vulnerability is the front door. GoDaddy's funnel starts when someone decides they need a domain and a website. If AI assistants answer the customer's question without a click, or build the "website" inside another platform, fewer people walk through that door — and a flat customer count stops being a choice and becomes a trend.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customer growth has stalled. 22 thousand net adds in Q2 2026 on a 20.5M base. Pricing and upsell can carry revenue for a while, but ARPU growth on a static base eventually runs into churn.
02
AI changes discovery. Wells Fargo's Alec Brondolo downgraded the stock to Underweight on August 26, 2026, arguing (as reported by The Motley Fool) that AI Overviews now appear in a large share of searches and tend to recommend cheaper services than GoDaddy's, and that the company risks losing share unless its pricing comes into line. That is a structural question for the whole funnel, not a quarterly one.
03
Credibility of the growth story is in court. A securities class action (class period September 3, 2025 – February 24, 2026) alleges the company attributed growth to strategy while relying on an undisclosed promotion of short-term, low-value contracts. These are allegations, not findings — but they go to the quality of the bookings number investors lean on.
04
Leverage with almost no equity cushion. $3.8B of debt, $2.7B net, and buybacks have taken stockholders' equity close to zero (StockAnalysis shows debt-to-equity of 573x). Debt/EBITDA of 2.74x is manageable on this cash flow, but the balance sheet has been spent on the share count.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 15.5x trailing GAAP earnings and a ~12.8% free-cash-flow yield for a business with 63.8% gross margins, operating margin that has doubled since FY2021, ROIC of 37%, and a board retiring about 7% of the diluted share count in seven months. Revenue has grown every year of the window, and the FY2026 guide still calls for ~6%. The A&C segment is growing 11% at a 46.8% EBITDA margin, and Airo went from a $10M to a $50M bookings run rate in a quarter. If the December 1 Investor Night lays out credible targets, a cash machine priced at half its 2025 peak does not need growth to accelerate to work — it only needs growth not to stop.

Bear Case

The customer count has stopped growing — 22 thousand net adds on 20.5 million — and the revenue line is being carried by price and upsell. Bookings and ARR are both growing about 5.7%, slower than revenue. A class action alleges that past growth leaned on undisclosed promotions. Wells Fargo is betting that AI search reduces the clicks that feed the funnel. The balance sheet has already been spent on buybacks, leaving near-zero equity and $2.7B of net debt. A low multiple on a business whose front door is narrowing is not cheap; it is the market pricing a slow decline in the customer base.

Top 3 Risks

01
The funnel. Net customer adds of 22 thousand in Q2 2026. If AI assistants and AI search reduce the number of people who start a business online by buying a domain, Core shrinks first and A&C loses its source of upsell.
02
Bookings credibility and litigation. The class action (lead-plaintiff deadline October 20, 2026) alleges misleading statements about the source of growth. An adverse development would hit the one metric the bull case relies on most.
03
Leverage after buybacks. $3.8B of gross debt against near-zero equity. Comfortable at 2.74x debt/EBITDA while free cash flow runs at ~$1.8B a year — less so if the customer base starts shrinking.
Industry Commentary
AI and the small-business web
CRITICAL QUESTION

GoDaddy sits on both sides of the AI trade. As a seller, it is building Airo into an "agentic operating system" for small businesses, has launched a developer platform so "developers and AI systems" can buy and manage domains directly, and is contributing an Agent Name Service to the Linux Foundation. As a funnel, it depends on people deciding they need a website — and a world where AI answers the question before anyone clicks is a world with fewer websites to build. The Q2 numbers fit both readings: Airo up 5x in a quarter, customers flat.

Growth quality under scrutiny
ONGOING OVERHANG

The class action alleges that between September 2025 and February 2026 GoDaddy attributed its results to strategy while relying on promotions for short-term, low-value contracts, and that this came out when Q4 bookings growth "sharply decelerated to 5%" (the plaintiffs' wording). Management's Q2 release instead points to higher ARPU and profitability. The two readings are testable: if ARPU keeps rising while customers stay flat, the price-over-volume strategy is holding; if ARPU stalls, the promotions story gains weight.

The valuation reset — bull and bear framing
OPPORTUNITY OR TRAP

At $104.77 the stock trades at about 15.5x trailing GAAP earnings, 11.5x EV/EBITDA and a ~12.8% free-cash-flow yield (all our calculations). The bull framing: a 33%-FCF-margin subscription business, still growing and with a 7% gross reduction in diluted shares already this year, is priced like a declining one. The bear framing: the multiple compressed because the customer base stopped growing, and a buyback cannot fix a narrowing funnel. The way to decide is not the P/E; it is net customer adds and bookings growth over the next two or three quarters, starting October 29.
