01 Company Overview

GE HealthCare designs, sells and services diagnostic imaging hardware — MRI (magnetic resonance imaging), CT (computed tomography), X-ray, molecular imaging and ultrasound — together with patient monitoring and anaesthesia equipment and a portfolio of injectable contrast agents and radiopharmaceuticals. It has been an independent public company only since January 4, 2023, when General Electric completed the separation. FY2025 revenue was $20.63B, up 4.84% from $19.67B in FY2024; the trailing-twelve-month figure through Q2 2026 is $21.27B, up 6.51%. Market capitalisation is $31.10B, which places it in the middle of the large-cap medtech pack rather than at the top of it.

The central debate here is a valuation one. GEHC trades at 15.87× trailing and 13.31× forward earnings — a visible discount to the multiples large-cap medical device companies usually command. The bull argument is that the discount is a leftover from the spin and from a China air-pocket that is now in the base. The bear argument is that a business growing organically at 3–4%, running a 39.5% gross margin and carrying $10.5B of debt against $2.1B of cash simply is not a premium asset. This page tests that by segment: one very good business (Pharmaceutical Diagnostics), one large adequate one (Advanced Imaging Solutions), and one that is currently broken (Patient Care Solutions).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED BASE SERVICE ANNUITY $23.9B BACKLOG FDA / REGULATORY BARRIERS RADIOPHARMA SUPPLY CHAIN CLINICAL SWITCHING COSTS GLOBAL SERVICE FOOTPRINT

The durable advantage is not the scanner — it is what happens after the scanner is bolted to the floor. An MRI or CT system is a decade-long relationship: service contracts, software upgrades, consumables, trained radiographers and a hospital IT integration nobody wants to redo. That produces a revenue stream far more stable than the equipment order book implies, and it is why a 1.15× book-to-bill and a $23.9B backlog matter more than any single quarter's revenue print. Layered on top, PDx is a genuinely different moat: manufacturing and distributing radioactive tracers with short half-lives requires a licensed production and logistics network that cannot be assembled quickly, which is why a new product like Flyrcado can be pushed through the existing channel at 30% margins rather than requiring a new commercial build.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China is a structural drag, not a one-quarter event. China revenue has fallen for two consecutive years — $2.56B in FY2023 (13.1% of revenue) → $2.14B in FY2024 (10.9%) → $2.03B in FY2025 (9.9%), a −20.7% decline over two years while the rest of the company grew. Management told investors on the Q2 2026 call it still expects a year-over-year decline in China in 2026, unchanged from prior guidance. The stimulus-and-anti-corruption cycle that stalled Chinese hospital equipment tenders has not reversed; the company's response has been to stand up a provincial government-affairs group and to lean into volume-based procurement (VBP) tenders, which it argues favour transparency. That is a strategy for competing in a smaller pool, not for growing the pool.
02
Input cost and tariff exposure on a globally manufactured product. Imaging systems are assembled from components sourced across borders, so trade policy hits the cost line directly. GEHC quantified a $250M full-year 2026 headwind tied to memory chips, oil and freight, and described inflation as roughly a 120 basis point margin drag in Q2 2026 — "some of the highest we've seen in years in a specific quarter." Working the other way, the company recognised $23M of IEEPA tariff refunds in Q2 adjusted EBIT and $107M in cash ($129M net income benefit, about $0.04 of adjusted EPS). Note what that means: a chunk of 2026 earnings quality is a refund of a tax, which is not a repeatable source of profit.
03
Growth is structurally low single digit. Full-year 2026 guidance is 3.0–4.0% organic revenue growth. Reported revenue has compounded from $18.34B (FY2022) to $20.63B (FY2025) — about 4.0% a year. At that rate the entire equity story depends on margin expansion and mix shift toward PDx, not on the top line. The PEG of 1.58 says the market is already giving credit for growth GEHC has not historically delivered.
04
Leverage inherited from the separation, and a CFO seat in transition. The company carries $10.54B of total debt against $2.08B of cash — roughly $8.5B net debt, against TTM operating income of $2.99B, and a debt-to-equity of 0.94. Enterprise value of $39.57B is 27% above the $31.10B market cap for exactly that reason: the debt is a real claim ahead of equity holders. That balance sheet is manageable at 3–4% growth but leaves little room for a large acquisition, and it is being managed by an interim CFO while a permanent successor is found.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Trajectory since the spin. From the first month-end close of $69.52 (January 2023) to $68.86 on September 4, 2026, GEHC has returned −0.9% over three years and eight months, plus a dividend yielding 0.20%. That is not a drawdown story and not a growth story — it is a stock that has gone precisely nowhere while revenue grew from $18.3B to $21.3B and net income from $1.92B to $1.99B. The market has spent nearly four years compressing the multiple at roughly the same rate the business has grown. Inside that flat line there was real movement: a +35.0% run from the October 2023 monthly low of $66.57 to the September 2024 peak close of $93.85, then a −35.2% grind down to the April 2026 close of $60.84, then a +17.1% bounce into August's $71.23 before September gave some back.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 13.3× forward earnings and 11.8% ROIC for an installed base with a $23.9B backlog, a record 11.1% organic order growth and 1.15× book-to-bill — the demand signal is the strongest in the company's short history. Inside it sits Pharmaceutical Diagnostics, growing 14.6% organically at a 29.6% EBIT margin with Flyrcado targeted at $500M+ of revenue by 2028. If PCS is sold, the corporate margin steps up mechanically and net debt falls; if the Q2 supply problem was genuinely timing, H2 revenue and cash flow recover on their own. China is already guided to decline, so it is a source of upside surprise rather than downside.

Bear Case

The discount looks deserved. Organic growth is guided to 3.0–4.0%, gross margin is 39.5% — capital-equipment economics, not device economics — and free cash flow has fallen from $1.80B in FY2022 to $1.58B TTM while revenue rose 16%. AIS segment EBIT was lower in FY2025 than FY2024 on higher revenue. China has shrunk 20.7% in two years with no inflection guided. Roughly $8.5B of net debt means enterprise value exceeds market cap by 27%. And a large chunk of 2026's earnings improvement is a tariff refund — a one-time recovery of a tax, not operating leverage. With only $180M of a ~$1.6B cash-flow year banked at the half, the guidance carries real risk.

Top 3 Risks

01
Second-half cash-flow concentration. $180M of free cash flow in H1 against ~$1.6B guided for the year means roughly 89% of the year's cash has to arrive in H2, and it depends on converting backlog through the same supply chain that just failed in PCS. A miss here would hit the multiple harder than a revenue miss, because cash conversion is the one thing a low-growth compounder has to get right.
02
China does not stabilise. Revenue there has gone $2.56B → $2.14B → $2.03B and is guided down again in 2026. Domestic Chinese imaging vendors are structurally advantaged in VBP tenders. If China settles at a permanently smaller, lower-priced share of the business, the 3–4% organic growth guide becomes the ceiling rather than the floor — and there is no obvious replacement growth pool of that size.
03
Execution and governance during a transition. A segment reorganisation, a broken segment under strategic review, an interim CFO and a $250M inflation headwind are running simultaneously. Any one is manageable; together they raise the odds of a guidance revision, and this stock has already shown it will fall 28% in two months when something goes wrong.

China Is No Longer a Growth Market for Western Imaging
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The numbers are unambiguous: GEHC's China revenue fell from $2.56B (13.1% of total) in FY2023 to $2.14B (10.9%) in FY2024 to $2.03B (9.85%) in FY2025 — down 20.7% in two years while total revenue grew 5.5%. Two forces did it. The anti-corruption campaign in Chinese healthcare procurement froze tender activity and made hospital purchasing decisions slower and more scrutinised; the equipment-renewal stimulus programmes that were supposed to offset it have not restored the prior run rate. Meanwhile volume-based procurement is spreading from drugs into devices, which is a price-compression mechanism by design. GEHC's response — a provincial government-affairs function and a stated preference for VBP transparency — is a competent adaptation to a smaller market, not a route back to growth. This is the single largest reason the peer discount exists, and the honest answer is that this portion of the discount is deserved.
Tariffs and Input Costs Meet a Global Assembly Line
ONGOING HEADWIND
An MRI system is not made in one country. That makes imaging unusually exposed to trade policy relative to, say, a software company or a domestic services business, and 2025–2026 has been a live demonstration: an 80-cent EPS tariff impact forced a 2025 guidance cut, and 2026 carries a $250M full-year headwind from memory chips, oil and freight, worth about 120 basis points of margin in Q2 alone — the highest single-quarter inflation drag management said it had seen in years. The $107M of IEEPA refunds received in cash in Q2 shows the policy can also run the other way, which is precisely the problem: the cost line is now a function of litigation and policy timing rather than of manufacturing efficiency. For a business with a 39.5% gross margin, a 120bp swing is roughly 8% of the operating margin.
The Value Is Migrating from the Scanner to the Molecule and the Model
EMERGING TAILWIND
The industry's profit pool is shifting away from hardware. GEHC's own numbers make the case: PDx earns a 29.6% EBIT margin versus 13.9% for imaging systems, and it is growing four times faster. The same logic applies to software — deep-learning reconstruction tools like True Definition DL are sold on subscription, converting a one-time feature into recurring revenue against an installed base the company already owns, and Arduini's "D3" framing (devices, disease-state focus, digital/AI) is explicitly a strategy to sell the drug and the algorithm alongside the machine. Bull framing: if PDx and software keep compounding at mid-teens rates, mix alone re-rates the company — the consolidated 39.5% gross margin is an average of a 30%-margin business and a slower, thinner one, and averages move when weights move. Bear framing: PDx is 14.1% of revenue. At 15% growth against a 71%-of-revenue segment growing 4–5%, mix shift is a decade-long process, not a catalyst — and the market is being asked to pay for 2030's mix with 2026's cash flow.
