01 Company Overview

Gen Digital is the company behind the antivirus on your parents' laptop. It sells Cyber Safety subscriptions — Norton, Avast, AVG, Avira, LifeLock, ReputationDefender, CCleaner — and, since April 2025, the MoneyLion financial app, to individuals, families and small businesses rather than to corporate IT departments. Fiscal 2026 (year ended April 3, 2026) revenue crossed $5.00B for the first time, up 27%, with GAAP EPS of $1.57 and non-GAAP EPS of $2.56. It has 81 million paid customers and about 3,900 employees — roughly $1.3M of revenue per head.

One company, three names — and one continuous price series. This is the old Symantec (SYMC). In November 2019 it sold its enterprise-security business to Broadcom for $10.7B and renamed the consumer remainder NortonLifeLock (NLOK). After completing its $8.1B merger with Avast in September 2022, it renamed itself Gen Digital and began trading as GEN on November 8, 2022. The ticker changed; the listed security did not. The five-year chart in Section 07 therefore runs straight through the rename: the September 2021 – October 2022 points are NLOK closes, the rest are GEN closes, from one unbroken history on digrin.com with no splice. The last stock split was a 2:1 in December 2004, outside the window.

The story of the last year is a stress test of that model. From a $27.19 close at the end of 2025 the stock fell to $18.83 at the end of March 2026 — down 31% in a quarter — as a geopolitical risk-off move hit what one April 7 note called "debt-heavy tech" and analysts trimmed estimates amid "ongoing worries about the company's high financial leverage." Then the numbers arrived: $5B of revenue, FY27 guidance raised in August, a fifth straight quarter of double-digit adjusted growth. By August it closed at $31.00, a five-year high on month-end closes. The question underneath: is Gen a durable, 78%-gross-margin subscription annuity that happens to carry $8.2B of debt, or a mature antivirus franchise that is buying its growth with borrowed money and a consumer-lending app?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brand portfolio (Norton, Avast, LifeLock) 81M paid subscribers 78% gross margin ~90% LifeLock retention Threat-data scale (500M+ scams blocked / qtr) $1.55B TTM free cash flow Financial-account data (110M+ connected)

The moat is inertia with a brand on it. Most people do not shop for antivirus every year; they renew what is already installed, and a consumer who has handed a company their identity-monitoring details has little reason to do it twice. That shows up as a 78% gross margin, near-record retention and $1.55B of trailing free cash flow on $5.08B of revenue. As a KoalaGains competitor analysis put it, most listed security peers — CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Zscaler, Fortinet — "sell to businesses," while "Gen instead sells directly to everyday consumers." That is both the moat and the ceiling: consumer subscriptions do not grow like enterprise security, which is exactly why management went and bought MoneyLion.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The balance sheet is doing a lot of work. $8.18B of total debt against $2.66B of book equity is a debt-to-equity of 3.08; the current ratio is 0.47 and the Altman Z-score 1.42 (StockAnalysis). Enterprise value is $25.09B against a $17.45B market cap — more than 30% of what you are buying is owed to lenders. When the market sold off in early April 2026, the stock was hit as "debt-heavy tech."
02
The core is barely growing. Cyber Safety Platform — two-thirds of revenue and the high-margin part — grew 4% adjusted in Q1 FY27, and its GAAP revenue line was lower year over year ($846M vs $869M, a comparison management attributes to an extra week in the prior-year quarter). Group growth is increasingly a function of the 30%-margin Trust-Based segment.
03
A security company now runs consumer credit. MoneyLion's Instacash advances are short-duration consumer credit. Gen sells the receivables to Sound Point to keep them off its balance sheet, but origination volumes, loss rates and funding-partner appetite are now inputs to a business that used to be pure software.
04
GAAP and "adjusted" tell different stories. Q1 FY27 revenue grew 6% GAAP and 11% adjusted; operating income fell 1% GAAP and rose 9% non-GAAP. In Q4 FY26 it ran the other way — GAAP operating income of $803M exceeded non-GAAP $641M, thanks to a $353M litigation-costs credit. Anyone valuing this stock has to pick a lens, and the P/E changes by 40% depending on which.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the comparisons. First, Q4 FY26: GAAP operating income was $803M (+93%) against non-GAAP $641M, and GAAP EPS $0.84 (+269%) against non-GAAP $0.67. GAAP rarely exceeds non-GAAP; the company's GAAP-to-non-GAAP reconciliation (FY26 results release, exhibit 99.1 of its May 2026 8-K) shows a $353M litigation-costs credit in the quarter — a pre-tax benefit that non-GAAP strips out. The case behind it is not named in that table. It sits inside the $1.71 TTM EPS, so the 17.0x trailing P/E is flattered by a non-recurring item. Second, the calendar: Q1 FY26 contained an extra fiscal week and Q1 FY27 includes MoneyLion stub financials, which is why management's "adjusted" growth (11%) differs from GAAP (6%). Third, FY2023 net income of $1,334M ($2.14 EPS) stands well above the years either side ($607M in FY2024, $836M in FY2022); the cause was not sourced, so long-run per-share trend lines drawn through FY2023 should be treated with care. Capital returns: $634M of buybacks in FY26 and $100M in Q1 FY27; the share count fell 1.52% year over year.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 10x forward non-GAAP earnings and an 8.9% free-cash-flow yield for a 78%-gross-margin subscription business with 81 million paying customers, near-record retention and five straight quarters of double-digit adjusted growth. Management has raised FY27 guidance once already, to 9–11% revenue growth and 14–18% EPS growth. AI-driven scams — deepfakes, voice clones — are a new reason for consumers to pay for protection, and MoneyLion gives Gen a second product to sell to a base that already trusts it with its identity. Net leverage is inside target at 2.95x, and every quarter of $400M+ free cash flow pays it down further. If the Engine cross-sell doubles as guided, the market will stop valuing this as an antivirus company in decline.

Bear Case

Strip out acquisitions and the core Cyber Safety franchise is growing 4% — and its GAAP revenue line actually shrank year over year. Growth is being bought: Avast and MoneyLion brought $8.2B of debt, a 0.47 current ratio, an Altman Z of 1.42 and a consumer-credit business with loss and funding risk that antivirus never had. The 17x trailing P/E rests on a GAAP quarter lifted by a one-time $353M litigation-costs credit, the "adjusted" growth rate depends on calendar and stub adjustments, and 7.69% of the float is short. When risk appetite fell in April 2026, the market sold this as levered tech — down 31% in a quarter — and nothing about the capital structure has changed since.

Top 3 Risks

01
Leverage in a risk-off market. $8.18B of debt, 3.08x debt-to-equity and a $25.09B enterprise value on a $17.45B market cap make the equity a levered claim. The Q1 2026 drawdown showed how the stock trades when credit appetite fades.
02
Core stagnation masked by acquisitions. Cyber Safety Platform is two-thirds of revenue and grew 4% adjusted. If Trust-Based growth slows as MoneyLion laps its acquisition anniversary, group growth reverts toward low single digits — below the 9–11% guide.
03
Consumer-credit exposure inside a security company. Instacash originations are growing at a record pace. Gen sells the receivables to Sound Point, but loss rates, funding-partner terms and regulation of earned-wage and cash-advance products are now part of the risk profile.
Industry Commentary
AI scams as a demand driver
EMERGING TAILWIND

For most software companies in 2026, AI is framed as a threat to the business model. For consumer security it has, so far, been the opposite: cheaper, more convincing scams raise the value of protection. Norton blocked more than 500 million scam attacks in Q1 FY27 including deepfakes and voice clones, and Gen's own survey found more than 60% of consumers said AI-driven scams made them more likely to buy protection. StockAnalysis's news feed also lists Norton Genie's expansion to Claude for AI-powered scam detection. The caveat: a company survey is marketing evidence, not market data, and the paid-customer count (+5M YoY) is the independent check.

Consumer security vs. enterprise security
STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINT

Gen is often screened against enterprise security names — CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Zscaler, Fortinet — which, as a KoalaGains comparison put it, "sell to businesses (enterprises) and grow revenue fast, often 20% or more per year." Gen sells to households. Its direct consumer rivals named on fetched pages, McAfee and Bitdefender, are private, so there is no clean listed comparable. The consequence for valuation is that Gen is priced like a cash-generating consumer subscription with leverage, not a security growth stock — which is why the forward multiple sits near 10x while the enterprise names carry far higher ones.

Leverage and the rate cycle
ONGOING HEADWIND

Gen's growth strategy since 2019 has been to sell the enterprise business, buy consumer assets (Avast for $8.1B, MoneyLion for $1B) and fund part of it with debt. Interest coverage is 4.05x and debt/EBITDA 3.36x. That works while free cash flow ($1.55B TTM) comfortably services the debt, and it did. But it makes the equity sensitive to credit conditions in a way the underlying subscriptions are not: the April 2026 sell-off hit GEN as "debt-heavy tech" even though nothing in the operating results had deteriorated. The bull framing: every year of free cash flow retires leverage and shifts value from lenders to shareholders. The bear framing: management keeps using that cash capacity for acquisitions and buybacks instead, so the leverage never really falls.
