01 Company Overview
Gilead Sciences is a Foster City–based biopharmaceutical company that discovers, develops and commercializes antiviral and oncology medicines, and it is the dominant global franchise in HIV therapy. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $29.44B, up 2.4% YoY, with a single product — Biktarvy at $14.33B — accounting for roughly 49% of the top line; trailing-twelve-month revenue has since accelerated to $30.46B (+5.5%) as the long-acting PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) franchise inflected. At a $177.9B market capitalization, Gilead ranks roughly #128 globally and #71 among US-listed companies, and it converts revenue to cash at an unusually high rate: $12.94B of trailing FCF on a 79.6% gross margin.

Revenue Mix
The cash engine and the entire investment case. Biktarvy — a single-tablet bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide regimen — did $14.33B in FY2025 (+7%) and $3.80B in Q2 2026 (+7%), making it roughly half of all Gilead revenue and one of the largest branded drugs in the world. Descovy, historically the oral PrEP standard, did $2.80B in FY2025 (+31%) and accelerated to $967M in Q2 2026 (+48%), of which ~$801M was PrEP (+60% YoY) on higher realized price from channel mix plus demand.

The growth story, however, is Yeztugo (lenacapavir) — the twice-yearly injectable for PrEP. It booked $232M in Q2 2026, up 40% sequentially, against a company target of roughly $1B for full-year 2026. More than 70% of users returned for the six-month re-injection, an unusually strong persistence signal for an injectable, and management describes it as the leading long-acting option among PrEP-naïve individuals and the overall leader in the switch market after only four quarters on the market. Gilead's total PrEP franchise crossed $1B in a single quarter for the first time — a ~$4B annualized run rate — growing over 100% YoY against a PrEP market growing ~14%, which is a straightforward share-gain statement. On that strength, management raised full-year HIV franchise growth guidance to 9–10% from 8%. Roughly 80–85% of the addressable market still sits on daily orals, so the weekly-oral lenacapavir program targets conversion rather than cannibalization.

The other half of the lenacapavir story is global access. Gilead supplies the drug at no profit through a bridging arrangement, and has licensed six generic manufacturers (Dr. Reddy's, Emcure, Eva Pharma, Ferozsons, Hetero, Mylan/Viatris) to serve 120 low- and lower-middle-income countries at roughly $40 per person per year starting in 2027. Gilead committed supply for up to 3 million people through 2028 via PEPFAR and the Global Fund, expanded by another 1 million in April 2026. This is margin-neutral volume, but it materially de-risks the political and reputational overhang that normally attaches to a blockbuster prevention drug.

The clock: Biktarvy's loss of exclusivity was long modelled at 2033, but October 2025 settlements with Lupin, Cipla and Laurus Labs pushed US generic entry to April 1, 2036 — roughly three additional years of the single most important revenue stream. Gilead has laid out a plan of up to seven HIV launches through 2033 spanning 12 new molecules and 11 combination programs, including once-weekly oral lenacapavir, islatravir-based combinations partnered with Merck, and next-generation long-acting regimens.

Gilead's moat is unusually concrete for a biopharma company: it is a single therapeutic area held for three decades with roughly half the US treatment market, reinforced by a patent estate that now runs to April 2036 on the flagship. Three things compound it. First, switching friction in HIV is real — patients on a suppressive single-tablet regimen and their infectious-disease physicians are structurally reluctant to change a working regimen, which is why Biktarvy still grew 7% in FY2025 at a $14.3B base. Second, Gilead owns the long-acting transition it is disrupting itself with: rather than defending oral share against an injectable competitor, it is converting its own patients to Yeztugo while adding new ones, evidenced by PrEP growth of over 100% against ~14% market growth. Third, the global-access architecture is a genuine competitive asset, not just corporate responsibility — six licensed generic manufacturers, 120 countries, ~$40/person/year from 2027, and a 3-million-person supply commitment through PEPFAR and the Global Fund extended by another million in April 2026. That structure occupies the low-income market at no profit and, in doing so, forecloses the political case for compulsory licensing or price controls in the high-income markets where the margin actually lives. Layer on 79.6% gross margins, $12.94B of trailing FCF and a 34.4% ROIC, and the company can absorb $11B of acquisition charges in a single quarter without the operating business flinching.
KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. Extreme single-product concentration. Biktarvy is ~49% of total revenue on its own, and the HIV franchise is 71.8% of product sales. Nothing in the portfolio is close to a second leg: the entire oncology franchise at $3.24B is less than a quarter of Biktarvy. A safety signal, a formulary loss, or an adverse patent ruling on one molecule would reprice the whole equity. The 2036 settlement bought time; it did not diversify anything.

2. M&A-funded pipeline and a leveraging balance sheet. Gilead has repeatedly bought its next product rather than discovered it — Immunomedics, CymaBay, and in 2026 Arcellx ($7.0B), Tubulis ($3.1B) and Ouro Medicines ($1.0B). Cash fell from $10.61B at Dec 31, 2025 to $3.18B at Jun 30, 2026, funded partly by $3.0B of new senior notes plus a $1.1B term loan; total debt is now $26.25B against a debt-to-equity of 2.23. The hit rate on these deals is mixed, and the same quarter that recorded $11.2B of acquired IPR&D also wrote off $1.75B of the earlier Immunomedics IPR&D.

3. Cell therapy is losing structurally, not cyclically. Yescarta -12% and Tecartus -24% in Q2 2026 is not a one-quarter blip; autologous CAR-T is being displaced at the margin by off-the-shelf bispecifics that require no apheresis, no manufacturing slot and no certified centre. Anito-cel's December 23 PDUFA is the swing factor, but it enters a myeloma market where BMS's Abecma and J&J/Legend's Carvykti already sit — and an approval fixes the product problem, not the modality problem.

4. Policy and public-funding dependency. A meaningful slice of HIV volume — domestically through Medicaid, ADAP and the USPSTF zero-cost-sharing mandate for PrEP, internationally through PEPFAR and the Global Fund — is a political appropriation rather than a commercial contract. Medicare price negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act reaches high-spend products on a rolling basis, and the $40/year LLMIC generic price is a permanent, publicly-cited reference point in any future US pricing debate.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS
The trailing twelve months are not a usable earnings picture, and the distortion is very large. Q2 2026 alone carried $11.183B of acquired in-process research and development (IPR&D) expense — the accounting treatment that immediately expenses the value of clinical-stage assets bought via asset acquisition rather than capitalizing them. The three deals: Arcellx (~$7.0B, anito-cel BCMA CAR-T), Tubulis (~$3.1B, next-generation ADCs) and Ouro Medicines (~$1.0B). On top of that sat a $1.750B IPR&D impairment on assets from the earlier Immunomedics acquisition — a write-down of the Trodelvy-adjacent pipeline that did not deliver, notwithstanding Trodelvy itself growing 26%. Management quantifies the combined after-tax hit at $(9.08) per share, which converts a normal profitable quarter into a $(10.496)B GAAP net loss and $(8.45) GAAP EPS (non-GAAP $(6.75)).

This is the same pattern as FY2024, when the ~$4.3B CymaBay acquisition drove GAAP EPS down to $0.38 versus $4.62 non-GAAP — Gilead's GAAP earnings are structurally lumpy because its pipeline is bought rather than grown. Full-year 2026 guidance embeds the charge explicitly: GAAP EPS of $(3.75)–$(3.40) against non-GAAP EPS of $(0.65)–$(0.30). Note that even the non-GAAP guide is negative, because Gilead does not add back acquired IPR&D. Also non-recurring in the period: $3.0B of senior unsecured notes plus a $1.1B term loan facility raised to fund the deals, and ongoing amortization of Immunomedics (Trodelvy) and CymaBay (Livdelzi) intangibles that depresses GAAP cost of sales relative to the cash economics. Free cash flow of $12.94B is entirely unaffected by any of it — the charges are non-cash or are investing outflows, not operating ones.

In plain English: RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been. Readings above 70 mean the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 means it has been beaten down and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.
The five-year trajectory is a textbook re-rating, back-loaded almost entirely into the last twenty months. From a September 2021 close of $58.78, GILD went essentially nowhere for two and a half years — bottoming at $51.09 in April 2022 as the Veklury COVID windfall faded and the post-hepatitis-C growth vacuum was fully priced in — before a violent October–November 2022 move to $77.43 and then another eighteen months of dead money through a $60.81 trough in April 2024. The entire return came afterward: from $59.95 in May 2024 to $143.44 today is +139% in twenty-seven months. Over the full five-year window the stock is up +144% on price alone, and roughly +170% on a total-return basis including approximately $15/share of dividends (estimated) — comfortably ahead of the S&P 500 over the same span and dramatically ahead of the large-cap biotech complex, which spent most of 2022–2024 in a drawdown. The last twelve months alone delivered +21.18%.

The re-rating is not a multiple story so much as a narrative one: the market stopped valuing Gilead as a melting HIV annuity awaiting a 2033 patent cliff, and began valuing it as a company with a genuine growth product (lenacapavir), an extended patent runway (April 2036), and a defensible 79.6% gross margin. Forward P/E of 15.20 against a NASDAQ-100 at ~33 says that re-rating is far from complete — or that the market still discounts the durability. Notably, at a beta of 0.34 the stock achieved this with very little market correlation; the drivers were company-specific.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

Gilead is a 79.6%-gross-margin, $12.94B-free-cash-flow franchise trading at 15.2x forward earnings — roughly half the NASDAQ-100 multiple — with a 2.29% dividend yield and a 0.34 beta. The patent cliff that suppressed the multiple for a decade was pushed from 2033 to April 2036, and the growth engine everyone said Gilead lacked has arrived: the PrEP franchise crossed $1B in a quarter for the first time, growing over 100% YoY against a market growing 14%, with Yeztugo persistence above 70% at six months. Management raised HIV growth guidance to 9–10% on that strength. The global-access architecture — 120 countries, six generic licensees, ~$40/year from 2027 — occupies the low-margin market at zero profit while insulating the high-margin one from political attack.

▼ Bear Case

This is a one-product company with an expensive habit. Biktarvy alone is ~49% of revenue, and the pipeline that is meant to replace it is being bought, not discovered — $11.2B of acquired IPR&D in a single quarter on top of the $1.75B write-off of the previous acquisition's assets. Cash fell from $10.6B to $3.2B in six months, $4.1B of new debt was raised, and debt-to-equity is 2.23. Even non-GAAP full-year 2026 EPS is guided negative. Cell therapy is in structural decline (-14% YoY), the anito-cel approval is binary and enters a crowded myeloma market, and a material share of HIV volume depends on PEPFAR, Medicaid and USPSTF mandates that are political appropriations, not contracts.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Biktarvy concentration and the 2036 cliff. A $14.33B product representing ~49% of revenue faces a hard, known US generic date of April 1, 2036. Every year that lenacapavir and the seven planned HIV launches fail to build a credible replacement base narrows the runway, and the terminal-value assumption in every model on the Street is a function of that transition working.
02
Serial acquisition risk and balance-sheet erosion. The pattern — Immunomedics, CymaBay, Arcellx, Tubulis, Ouro — has now produced a $1.75B write-off of the first one alongside $11.2B of fresh charges. Cash is down to $3.18B against $26.25B of debt. If anito-cel misses on December 23 or the Tubulis ADC platform disappoints, the capital is already spent and unrecoverable.
03
Pricing policy and public-health funding. IRA Medicare negotiation, the widely-publicized $40/person/year LLMIC generic reference price, and dependence on PEPFAR/Global Fund appropriations (a 3-million-person commitment through 2028, expanded by 1 million in April 2026) together create durable pressure on US realized pricing — the source of essentially all Gilead profit. This risk is slow-moving, hard to hedge, and entirely outside management's control.
  Industry Commentary
HIV Prevention Market Expansion
EMERGING TAILWIND
Prevention is becoming a larger commercial market than anyone modelled five years ago, and the long-acting format is the reason. The overall PrEP market grew roughly 14% YoY in the most recent quarter; Gilead's PrEP franchise grew over 100% in the same period, crossing $1B in a quarter for the first time on a ~$4B annualized run rate. Yeztugo contributed $232M (+40% sequentially) with >70% six-month re-injection persistence, while Descovy PrEP added ~$801M (+60%). Critically, management notes 80–85% of the market remains on daily orals — the conversion runway is the bulk of the opportunity, not the part already captured. Gilead is taking a growing share of a growing market, which is the rarest configuration in large-cap pharma.
Drug Pricing Policy & Global-Health Funding
ONGOING HEADWIND
Gilead's economics are concentrated in US commercial and Medicare pricing on a small number of very high-spend products — exactly the profile the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare negotiation mechanism targets. Compounding it: Gilead itself has established a public reference price of ~$40 per person per year for generic lenacapavir across 120 countries from 2027, a figure that will be quoted in every subsequent US pricing hearing. On the demand side, the 3-million-person PEPFAR/Global Fund supply commitment through 2028 (expanded by 1 million in April 2026) and domestic ADAP/Medicaid coverage make a real slice of volume dependent on annual appropriations. Access strategy is genuine and strategically shrewd — but it hands critics a price anchor that cannot be walked back.
Biotech M&A Appetite — Bull vs Bear
DEFINES THE THESIS
Large-cap pharma facing 2028–2036 patent cliffs is buying clinical-stage biotech aggressively, and Gilead is among the most active buyers: $11.1B across Arcellx ($7.0B), Tubulis ($3.1B) and Ouro Medicines ($1.0B) in a single quarter, funded by $3.0B of senior notes, a $1.1B term loan and a cash drawdown from $10.61B to $3.18B.

Bull framing: acquired IPR&D is an investing decision wearing an expense costume. Free cash flow of $12.94B was untouched, ROIC is 34.4%, and Gilead is buying de-risked Phase 2/3 assets — anito-cel with a December PDUFA — at a moment when biotech valuations remain well below their 2021 peak. Trodelvy, from the Immunomedics deal, is now growing 26%.

Bear framing: the same quarter that spent $11.2B also wrote off $1.75B of Immunomedics IPR&D — the prior deal's pipeline, impaired. Buying growth at 15x forward earnings works only if the hit rate holds; with debt-to-equity at 2.23, cash at $3.18B and even non-GAAP 2026 EPS guided negative, the margin for a miss has narrowed considerably.
