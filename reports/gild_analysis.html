01 Company Overview
Gilead Sciences is a Foster City–based biopharmaceutical company that discovers, develops and commercializes antiviral and oncology medicines, and it is the dominant global franchise in HIV therapy. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $29.44B, up 2.4% YoY, with a single product — Biktarvy at $14.33B — accounting for roughly 49% of the top line; trailing-twelve-month revenue reached $30.99B (+6.5%) through September 2026 as the long-acting PrEP (pre-exposure prophylaxis) franchise took off; in the third quarter, product sales excluding the COVID drug Veklury grew 16%, which management called its strongest base-business growth in more than a decade. At a $211.3B market capitalization, Gilead is a member of both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq-100, and it converts revenue to cash at an unusually high rate: $13.78B of trailing FCF on a 79.95% gross margin.

The cash engine and the entire investment case. Biktarvy — a single-tablet bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide regimen — did $14.33B in FY2025 (+7%) and $4.06B in Q3 2026 (+10%), making it roughly half of all Gilead revenue and one of the largest branded drugs in the world. Descovy, historically the oral PrEP standard, did $2.76B in FY2025 (+31%) and $1.05B in Q3 2026 (+49%), about 80% of it for PrEP, according to the Q3 call.

The growth story, however, is Yeztugo (lenacapavir) — the twice-yearly injectable for PrEP. It booked $305M in Q3 2026, up 31% from Q2's $232M, and management still expects roughly $1B for full-year 2026. More than 70% of users have returned for their second injection, commercial chief Johanna Mercier said on the Q3 call, an unusually strong persistence signal for an injectable. Gilead's HIV prevention sales nearly doubled year over year in Q3, outpacing the US market, and CEO Daniel O'Day put Yeztugo plus Descovy for PrEP at about half the market and a ~$4B annual run rate. On that strength, management now expects HIV sales to grow about 12% in 2026, up from the 9–10% it guided in August and 8% before that.

The other half of the lenacapavir story is global access. Gilead supplies the drug at no profit through a bridging arrangement, and has licensed six generic manufacturers (Dr. Reddy's, Emcure, Eva Pharma, Ferozsons, Hetero, Mylan/Viatris) to serve 120 low- and lower-middle-income countries at roughly $40 per person per year starting in 2027. Gilead's supply commitment through PEPFAR and the Global Fund covers up to 3 million people through 2028, after an April 2026 expansion that added 1 million, and a September 2026 agreement with the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) opens a route for twice-yearly lenacapavir in Latin America and the Caribbean. Gilead makes no profit on this volume, but it eases the political and reputational pressure that usually follows a blockbuster prevention drug.

The clock: Biktarvy's loss of exclusivity was long modelled at 2033, but October 2025 settlements with Lupin, Cipla and Laurus Labs pushed US generic entry to April 1, 2036 — roughly three additional years of the single most important revenue stream. The nearer change is price: in October 2026 Medicare set Biktarvy's negotiated Maximum Fair Price at $2,600 per 30-day supply from January 1, 2028, and CFO Andrew Dickinson expects a total net hit of about $1.5B to Biktarvy revenue in 2028, counting knock-on effects on other US payers. A new regimen arrived in August 2026: Bixlenvo, a once-daily bictegravir/lenacapavir tablet for adults whose virus is already suppressed. Gilead has laid out a plan of up to seven HIV launches through 2033 spanning 12 new molecules and 11 combination programs, including once-weekly oral lenacapavir, islatravir-based combinations partnered with Merck, and next-generation long-acting regimens.

Gilead's moat is unusually concrete for a biopharma company: it is a single therapeutic area held for three decades with roughly half the US treatment market, reinforced by a patent estate that now runs to April 2036 on the flagship. Three things compound it. First, switching friction in HIV is real — patients on a suppressive single-tablet regimen and their infectious-disease physicians are structurally reluctant to change a working regimen, which is why Biktarvy still grew 7% in FY2025 at a $14.3B base. Second, Gilead owns the long-acting transition it is disrupting itself with: rather than defending oral share against an injectable competitor, it is converting its own patients to Yeztugo while adding new ones, evidenced by HIV prevention sales that nearly doubled in Q3 2026 while outgrowing the US market. Third, the global-access architecture is a genuine competitive asset, not just corporate responsibility — six licensed generic manufacturers, 120 countries, ~$40/person/year from 2027, and a PEPFAR and Global Fund supply commitment that reached 3 million people through 2028 when it was expanded by 1 million in April 2026. That structure occupies the low-income market at no profit and weakens the political case for compulsory licensing, though it did not keep Biktarvy out of Medicare price negotiation (vulnerability 4). Layer on 79.95% gross margins, $13.78B of trailing FCF and a 36.8% ROIC, and the company can absorb $11B of acquisition charges in a single quarter without the operating business flinching.
KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. Extreme single-product concentration. Biktarvy is ~49% of total revenue on its own, and the HIV franchise is 71.8% of product sales. Nothing in the portfolio is close to a second leg: the entire oncology franchise at $3.24B is less than a quarter of Biktarvy. A safety signal, being dropped from insurers' lists of covered drugs, or an adverse patent ruling on one molecule would hit the whole share price. The 2036 settlement bought time; it did not diversify anything.

2. M&A-funded pipeline and a leveraging balance sheet. Gilead has repeatedly bought its next product rather than discovered it — Immunomedics, CymaBay, and in 2026 Arcellx ($7.0B), Tubulis ($3.1B), Ouro Medicines ($1.0B) and, pending, PepStar ($1.275B). Cash and marketable securities fell from $10.61B at Dec 31, 2025 to $6.44B at Sep 30, 2026 after $11.3B of acquisition payments this year, partly offset by $4.1B of new borrowing ($3.0B of senior notes in May plus a term loan); total debt was $26.23B at September 30 against a debt-to-equity of 2.02. The hit rate on these deals is mixed: Q2 2026, the quarter that recorded $11.2B of acquired IPR&D also wrote off $1.75B of the earlier Immunomedics IPR&D.

3. Cell therapy is losing structurally, not cyclically. Yescarta -16% and Tecartus -28% in Q3 2026, after -12% and -24% in Q2, is not a one-quarter blip; CAR-T made from each patient's own cells is gradually losing ground to off-the-shelf bispecific antibodies that require no apheresis, no manufacturing slot and no certified centre. Anito-cel's December 23 PDUFA is the swing factor, but it enters a myeloma market where BMS's Abecma and J&J/Legend's Carvykti already sit — and an approval fixes the product problem, not the problem with this type of treatment.

4. Policy and public-funding dependency. A meaningful slice of HIV volume — domestically through Medicaid, ADAP and the USPSTF zero-cost-sharing mandate for PrEP, internationally through PEPFAR and the Global Fund — is a political appropriation rather than a commercial contract. Medicare price negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act has now reached Biktarvy: a Maximum Fair Price of $2,600 per 30-day supply from January 2028, which the CFO expects to cost about $1.5B of net Biktarvy revenue in 2028. And the ~$40/year generic price for low- and lower-middle-income countries is a permanent, publicly-cited reference point in any future US pricing debate.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS
The trailing twelve months are not a usable earnings picture, and the distortion is very large. Q2 2026 alone carried $11.183B of acquired in-process research and development (IPR&D) expense — the accounting treatment that immediately expenses the value of clinical-stage assets bought via asset acquisition rather than capitalizing them. The three deals: Arcellx (~$7.0B, anito-cel BCMA CAR-T), Tubulis (~$3.1B, next-generation ADCs) and Ouro Medicines (~$1.0B). On top of that sat a $1.750B IPR&D impairment on assets from the earlier Immunomedics acquisition — a write-down of the Trodelvy-adjacent pipeline that did not deliver, notwithstanding Trodelvy itself growing 26%. Gilead puts the hit from the acquired IPR&D and related taxes at $(9.08) per share; with the impairment, that converts a normal profitable quarter into a $(10.496)B GAAP net loss and $(8.45) GAAP EPS (non-GAAP $(6.75)). Q3 2026 was clean by comparison: $18M of acquired IPR&D, and a $164M net loss on equity investments against a $483M gain a year earlier, which is why GAAP EPS fell to $1.94 from $2.43 while non-GAAP EPS rose to $2.55 from $2.47.

This is the same pattern as FY2024, when the ~$4.3B CymaBay acquisition drove GAAP EPS down to $0.38 versus $4.62 non-GAAP — Gilead's GAAP earnings are structurally lumpy because its pipeline is bought rather than grown. Full-year 2026 guidance, updated October 29, embeds the charges explicitly: GAAP EPS of $(4.40) to $(4.05) against non-GAAP EPS of $(1.20) to $(0.85), including about $9.88 a share of acquired IPR&D from $12.4B of charges on the Arcellx, Tubulis, Ouro Medicines and PepStar deals (net of the Lakefront collaboration and taxes). PepStar alone is expected to cut Q4 EPS by about $0.79–0.83. Note that even the non-GAAP guide is negative, because Gilead does not add back acquired IPR&D. Also non-recurring in the period: $4.1B of new borrowing, $3.0B of it senior unsecured notes and the rest a term loan, raised to help fund the deals, and ongoing amortization of Immunomedics (Trodelvy) and CymaBay (Livdelzi) intangibles that depresses GAAP cost of sales relative to the cash economics. Trailing free cash flow of $13.78B is entirely unaffected by any of it — the charges are non-cash or are investing outflows, not operating ones.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

Gilead is a ~80%-gross-margin, $13.78B-free-cash-flow franchise trading at 16.9x 2027 consensus earnings — below the Nasdaq-100's forward multiple — with a 1.93% dividend yield and a 0.33 beta. The patent cliff that weighed on the stock was pushed from 2033 to April 2036, and the growth engine everyone said Gilead lacked has arrived: HIV prevention sales nearly doubled year over year in Q3 2026, Yeztugo reached $305M in the quarter with more than 70% of users returning for a second injection, and management now expects HIV sales to grow about 12% in 2026. Product sales excluding Veklury grew 16% in Q3, the fastest base-business growth in more than a decade by management's count. The global-access architecture — 120 countries, six generic licensees, ~$40/year from 2027 — occupies the low-margin market at zero profit while insulating the high-margin one from political attack.

▼ Bear Case

This is a one-product company with an expensive habit. Biktarvy alone is ~49% of revenue, and the pipeline that is meant to replace it is being bought, not discovered — $11.2B of acquired IPR&D in a single quarter on top of the $1.75B write-off of the previous acquisition's assets. Cash and securities fell from $10.6B to $6.4B in nine months after $11.3B of deal payments, $4.1B of new debt was raised, debt-to-equity is 2.02, and PepStar adds another $1.275B. Even non-GAAP full-year 2026 EPS is guided negative. From 2028 Medicare pays a negotiated $2,600 per 30-day supply for Biktarvy, about $1.5B of net revenue in that year by the CFO's estimate. Cell therapy is in structural decline (-18% YoY in Q3), the anito-cel approval is binary and enters a crowded myeloma market, and a material share of HIV volume depends on PEPFAR, Medicaid and USPSTF mandates that are political appropriations, not contracts.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Biktarvy concentration and the 2036 cliff. A $14.33B product representing ~49% of revenue faces a Medicare price cut from January 1, 2028 (about $1.5B of net revenue in 2028, per the CFO) and a hard, known US generic date of April 1, 2036. Every year that lenacapavir and the seven planned HIV launches fail to build a credible replacement base narrows the runway, and any long-run valuation of Gilead depends on that hand-over working.
02
Serial acquisition risk and balance-sheet erosion. The pattern — Immunomedics, CymaBay, Arcellx, Tubulis, Ouro, and now PepStar — has produced a $1.75B write-off of the first one alongside $11.2B of fresh charges. Cash was $6.44B against $26.23B of debt at September 30, 2026. If anito-cel misses on December 23 or the Tubulis ADC platform disappoints, the capital is already spent and unrecoverable.
03
Pricing policy and public-health funding. IRA Medicare negotiation, now set for Biktarvy at $2,600 per 30-day supply from 2028, the widely publicized $40/person/year LLMIC generic reference price, and dependence on PEPFAR/Global Fund appropriations (a commitment covering up to 3 million people through 2028, including 1 million added in April 2026) together create durable pressure on US realized pricing — the source of essentially all Gilead profit. This risk is slow-moving, hard to hedge, and entirely outside management's control.
  Industry Commentary
HIV Prevention Market Expansion
EMERGING TAILWIND
Prevention is becoming a larger commercial market than anyone modelled five years ago, and the long-acting format is the reason. In Q3 2026 Gilead's HIV prevention sales nearly doubled year over year, outpacing the US market, and the CEO put Yeztugo and Descovy for PrEP together at about half the market on a ~$4B annual run rate. Yeztugo contributed $305M (+31% on Q2) with more than 70% of users returning for a second injection, and Descovy, about 80% of it PrEP, did $1.05B (+49%). Gilead is taking a growing share of a growing market, an uncommon position in large-cap pharma.
Drug Pricing Policy & Global-Health Funding
ONGOING HEADWIND
Gilead's economics are concentrated in US commercial and Medicare pricing on a small number of very high-spend products — exactly the profile the Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare negotiation mechanism targets, and in October 2026 it reached the flagship: Medicare set Biktarvy's Maximum Fair Price at $2,600 per 30-day supply from January 1, 2028, about $1.5B of net Biktarvy revenue in 2028 by the CFO's estimate. Compounding it: Gilead itself has established a public reference price of ~$40 per person per year for generic lenacapavir across 120 countries from 2027, a figure that will be quoted in every subsequent US pricing hearing. On the demand side, the PEPFAR/Global Fund supply commitment for up to 3 million people through 2028 (1 million of them added in April 2026) and domestic ADAP/Medicaid coverage make a real slice of volume dependent on annual appropriations. Access strategy is genuine and strategically shrewd — but it hands critics a price anchor that cannot be walked back.
Biotech M&A Appetite — Bull vs Bear
DEFINES THE THESIS
Large-cap pharma facing 2028–2036 patent cliffs is buying clinical-stage biotech aggressively, and Gilead is among the most active buyers: $11.1B across Arcellx ($7.0B), Tubulis ($3.1B) and Ouro Medicines ($1.0B) in a single quarter, funded by $3.0B of senior notes, a term loan and cash, followed in October by a $1.275B deal for PepStar's oral IL-23R program.

Bull framing: acquired IPR&D is an investing decision wearing an expense costume. Trailing free cash flow of $13.78B was untouched, ROIC is 36.8%, and Gilead is buying de-risked Phase 2/3 assets — anito-cel with a December PDUFA — at a moment when biotech valuations remain well below their 2021 peak. Trodelvy, from the Immunomedics deal, grew 42% in Q3 2026.

Bear framing: the same quarter that spent $11.2B also wrote off $1.75B of Immunomedics IPR&D — the prior deal's pipeline, impaired. Buying growth at 17x 2027 earnings works only if the hit rate holds; with debt-to-equity at 2.02, cash at $6.44B and even non-GAAP 2026 EPS guided negative, the margin for a miss has narrowed considerably.
