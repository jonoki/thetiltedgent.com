01 Company Overview

General Mills is the company behind the cereal aisle's most familiar boxes — Cheerios, Betty Crocker, Pillsbury — plus Häagen-Dazs and, since its pet-food push, Blue Buffalo. It sells through grocery stores, e-commerce, foodservice operators and specialty retailers, in four reported segments: North America Retail, North America Pet, North America Foodservice and International. Fiscal 2026, the 53-week year that ended May 31, 2026, delivered net sales of $18.43B, down 5.45%, organic net sales down 2%, and a GAAP diluted loss of −$0.16 per share against $4.10 the year before. Adjusted diluted EPS was $3.55, down 16% in constant currency.

That loss is an accounting event, not a cash one — but it is an honest confession. On July 1, 2026 General Mills wrote down $1.5B of North America Pet goodwill and $250M on the Nudges and True Chews brands, took a $1,032M valuation loss on the Brazil business it was selling, and had already impaired the Uncle Toby's brand by $53M. Goodwill is the premium you paid for a business over its hard assets; writing it down is management telling you the premium was too much. At the same time the company has been shrinking on purpose: the U.S. yogurt business went to Groupe Lactalis (a $1,046M gain booked in Q1 FY2026), Canadian yogurt went to Sodiaal International (a $96M gain in Q3 FY2025), and Brazil has been sold to Café Três Corações. The stock tells the rest: $88.63 at the April 2023 month-end peak, $35.41 now — down 60% — with a 6.89% dividend yield that is the market asking whether the $2.44 payout survives a fiscal 2027 in which management itself guides adjusted operating profit down 8% to 13%. The next data point lands on September 23, 2026, two trading days after this snapshot.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

30+ major brands Cheerios · Betty Crocker · Pillsbury Häagen-Dazs · Blue Buffalo Grocery shelf scale 127 years of uninterrupted dividends ~95% FCF conversion target

The moat is habit plus shelf space. A brand that has been in the pantry for decades gets bought without a second thought, and a manufacturer with more than 30 major brands gets a hearing from every grocery buyer. That is what produced a 15.7% operating margin before impairments on $18.4B of sales even in a bad year. The weakness is that habit is exactly what breaks when the price gap to private label widens, or when appetite itself shrinks — and the $1.75B Pet write-down shows that buying growth has not been a reliable escape route.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Volume has to be bought back. FY2026 organic sales fell 2% and North America Retail profit fell 20% while the company invested in price and value. The February 17, 2026 outlook cut blamed weak consumer sentiment and a volume recovery that was progressing "slower and more expensively" than expected. The FY2027 guide still has organic sales at −1.5% to +0.5%.
02
Appetite and trade-down. Coverage of the stock repeatedly cites two forces: shoppers switching to cheaper private label, and weight-loss drugs (GLP-1s) reducing how much people eat. Neither shows up as a line item, so neither can be measured precisely from the reported numbers. Both show up in organic volume.
03
A levered balance sheet under a big dividend. Total debt $13.94B against $453.8M of cash; debt-to-equity 1.89, debt-to-EBITDA 3.84, current ratio 0.68. On September 3, 2026, Campbell's cut its dividend 36% to accelerate debt reduction, and GIS fell 3.25% that day on the read-across. General Mills has not signalled a cut — it declared $0.61 in July — but the market is now pricing the possibility.
04
The pet thesis has been marked down. The $1.5B North America Pet goodwill impairment and the $250M Nudges/True Chews charge mean the growth premium once paid for pet has been partly written off. Pet organic sales fell 3% in FY2026.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay about 10x trailing adjusted earnings and 11.8x forward for a portfolio of brands that have been in American kitchens for generations, with a 6.89% dividend backed by 127 years without interruption and $1.63B of free cash flow even in a year of write-downs. The worst of the portfolio surgery is done: yogurt and Brazil are gone, the Pet goodwill has been marked to a more honest number, and the 53rd week and divestitures are known headwinds already inside the FY2027 guide. Q4 showed organic sales stabilising at flat and adjusted operating profit up 13% in constant currency. If the $3B cost programme delivers its ≥$750M this year and volumes hold, earnings can trough in FY2027 — and an 8.6% FCF yield does not need growth to work.

Bear Case

Organic sales have fallen and are guided to fall again; adjusted EPS is guided down another 10–15% to $3.00–$3.20; the dividend already consumes 80% of free cash flow; and the balance sheet carries $13.9B of debt at 3.8x EBITDA with a 0.68 current ratio. The company spent a year buying volume with price and got −2% organic for it. The $1.75B Pet write-down says the growth leg was overpaid for. Campbell's has just shown what happens when a food company's cash flow stops covering both debt reduction and the dividend. With four Sell ratings, an 8.6% short interest and a Bernstein target of $31, the market is treating the yield as a warning, not a gift.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume doesn't come back. Private-label trade-down and GLP-1-driven changes in eating are cited in coverage of the stock; FY2026 organic sales fell 2% and the FY2027 guide spans −1.5% to +0.5%. A result at the bottom of that range, with price investment still running, would put the $3.00 EPS floor at risk.
02
Dividend coverage. $2.44 per share against $3.00–$3.20 of guided adjusted EPS (76–81%) and an 80.21% FCF payout, on a balance sheet with 1.89 debt-to-equity. A peer cut its dividend 36% on September 3. The payout has not been cut here — but the yield says the market is pricing the risk.
03
More write-downs. FY2026 carried $2.8B of impairments and valuation losses. Goodwill and brand intangibles are tested against forecasts; if Pet or other brands underperform the new assumptions, further non-cash charges follow — no cash effect, but a signal the market has already learned to punish.
Industry Commentary
Big Food's volume problem
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The WSJ's July 2026 headline — "Big Food Is Running Out of Moves with Shoppers and Investors" — described General Mills as "squeezed by everything from inflation to GLP-1s." The shape of the problem, as the coverage describes it: shoppers trading down to cheaper private label, appetite pressure from weight-loss drugs, and companies having to invest in price to win volume back. General Mills' own numbers trace the arc — gross margin 35.00% in FY2024, 34.60% in FY2025, 33.73% in FY2026 — alongside three straight years of falling revenue.

Portfolio reshaping and dividend stress across the group
ONGOING HEADWIND

The response across packaged food has been to sell slower businesses, cut costs and protect the payout. General Mills sold yogurt in the U.S. and Canada and its Brazil business, and has launched a $3B savings programme. Campbell's went further on September 3, 2026, cutting its dividend 36% to pay down debt; its shares fell 9% that day, and General Mills and Kraft Heinz fell with it. The lesson the market drew is that a high yield in this sector is only as safe as the free cash flow behind it after debt service — and that one company's reset gets priced into its peers.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At 9.49x EV/EBITDA, ~10x adjusted trailing earnings and an 8.6% FCF yield, General Mills is priced like a business in decline. The bull framing: the decline is largely self-inflicted and finite — divestitures, an extra week to lap, and a deliberate price investment — and FY2027 is the trough year management has already guided to. Every dollar of the $750M savings target that sticks is a dollar that doesn't have to come from volume. The bear framing: it is a business in decline. Revenue has fallen three years running, the growth acquisition has been written down, and the guide is for lower profit again. The way to adjudicate is not the P/E; it is two numbers per quarter — organic volume and free cash flow after the dividend. The first report under the FY2027 guide arrives on September 23.
