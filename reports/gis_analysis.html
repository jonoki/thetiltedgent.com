01 Company Overview

General Mills is the company behind the cereal aisle's most familiar boxes — Cheerios, Betty Crocker, Pillsbury — plus Häagen-Dazs and, since its pet-food push, Blue Buffalo. It sells through grocery stores, e-commerce, foodservice operators and specialty retailers, in four reported segments: North America Retail, North America Pet, North America Foodservice and International. Fiscal 2026, the 53-week year that ended May 31, 2026, delivered net sales of $18.43B, down 5.45%, organic net sales down 2%, and a GAAP diluted loss of −$0.16 per share against $4.10 the year before. Adjusted diluted EPS was $3.55, down 16% in constant currency.

That loss is an accounting event, not a cash one — but it is an honest confession. On July 1, 2026 General Mills wrote down $1.5B of North America Pet goodwill and $250M on the Nudges and True Chews brands, took a $1,032M valuation loss on the Brazil business it was selling, and had already impaired the Uncle Toby's brand by $53M. Goodwill is the premium you paid for a business over its hard assets; writing it down is management telling you the premium was too much. At the same time the company has been shrinking on purpose: the U.S. yogurt business went to Groupe Lactalis (a $1,046M gain booked in Q1 FY2026), Canadian yogurt went to Sodiaal International (a $96M gain in Q3 FY2025), and Brazil has been sold to Café Três Corações. The stock tells the rest: $88.63 at the April 2023 month-end peak, $33.82 now — down 62% — with a 7.21% dividend yield that is the market asking whether the $2.44 payout survives a fiscal 2027 in which management itself guides adjusted operating profit down 8% to 13%. The first quarter of that year, reported on September 23, 2026, kept the plan intact: organic net sales were flat, adjusted EPS of $0.75 beat the $0.72 consensus, and management reaffirmed its fiscal 2027 targets.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

100+ brands Cheerios · Betty Crocker · Pillsbury Häagen-Dazs · Blue Buffalo Grocery shelf scale 128 years of uninterrupted dividends ~95% FCF conversion target

The moat is habit plus shelf space. A brand that has been in the pantry for decades gets bought without a second thought, and a manufacturer with more than 100 brands gets a hearing from every grocery buyer. That is what produced a 15.7% operating margin before impairments on $18.4B of sales even in a bad year. The weakness is that habit is exactly what breaks when the price gap to private label widens, or when appetite itself shrinks — and the $1.75B Pet write-down shows that buying growth has not been a reliable escape route.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Volume has to be bought back. FY2026 organic sales fell 2% and North America Retail profit fell 20% while the company invested in price and value. The February 17, 2026 outlook cut blamed weak consumer sentiment and a volume recovery that was proving slower and costlier than expected. The FY2027 guide still has organic sales at −1.5% to +0.5%; Q1 FY2027 came in flat overall, with North America Retail still −3%.
02
Appetite and trade-down. The Wall Street Journal's July 2026 reporting on Big Food named two forces: shoppers switching to cheaper store brands, and weight-loss drugs (GLP-1s) changing what people eat. Neither shows up as a line item, so neither can be measured precisely from the reported numbers. Both show up in organic volume.
03
A levered balance sheet under a big dividend. Total debt $13.62B against $433.1M of cash at August 30, 2026; debt-to-equity 1.82, debt-to-EBITDA 3.79, current ratio 0.70. On September 3, 2026, Campbell's cut its dividend 36% to accelerate debt reduction, and GIS closed down 3.25% the same day. General Mills has not signalled a cut — it declared $0.61 again on September 29 — but at a 7.2% yield the market is pricing the possibility, and on the Q1 call the CFO put a return to 3x leverage "at least a couple of years" away.
04
The pet thesis has been marked down. The $1.5B North America Pet goodwill impairment and the $250M Nudges/True Chews charge mean the growth premium once paid for pet has been partly written off. Pet organic sales fell 3% in FY2026.

Notable One-Off Items

Fiscal 2026 is almost entirely one-offs. Charges: $1,500M North America Pet goodwill impairment, $250M Nudges and True Chews brand impairment, $53M Uncle Toby's brand impairment, and a $1,032M non-cash valuation loss on the Brazil business. Gains: $1,046M on the sale of U.S. yogurt to Groupe Lactalis in Q1 FY2026 — which is why that quarter showed net income of $1,204M on revenue of $4.52B. Calendar: a 53rd week added ~7 points to Q4 net sales and ~2 points to the year. The practical consequence: GAAP EPS (−$0.16) and adjusted EPS ($3.55) tell very different stories, and FY2027 comparisons will be flattered on GAAP and penalised on adjusted as the extra week and divested sales roll off. Share repurchases were $500M in FY2026; average diluted shares fell 4% to 538 million. Q1 FY2027 was nearly clean: a further $24M non-cash loss on the Brazil disposal group, with mark-to-market and transaction items netting to one cent ($0.74 GAAP vs $0.75 adjusted EPS). No shares were repurchased in the quarter, against $500M a year earlier.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF SEP 29, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 month-end close of $59.82 to the September 29, 2026 settled close of $33.82, GIS lost 43.5% of its price. Adding back the $11.50 per share of dividends that went ex between October 2021 and July 2026 (20 quarterly payments per Yahoo Finance's dividend history, not reinvested) brings the total return to roughly −24%. The shape is a round trip that kept going: a steady climb to $88.63 in April 2023 — then an almost uninterrupted three-year slide as volumes softened and revenue began to shrink. From that peak the stock is down 62%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay about 9.8x trailing adjusted earnings and 11x forward for a portfolio of brands that have been in American kitchens for generations, with a 7.21% dividend backed by 128 years without interruption and $1.63B of free cash flow even in a year of write-downs. The worst of the portfolio surgery is done: yogurt and Brazil are gone, the Pet goodwill has been marked to a more honest number, and the 53rd week and divestitures are known headwinds already inside the FY2027 guide. Q4 showed organic sales stabilising at flat and adjusted operating profit up 13% in constant currency, and Q1 FY2027 held organic sales flat with the full-year guide reaffirmed. If the $3B cost programme delivers its ≥$750M this year and volumes hold, earnings can trough in FY2027 — and an 8.6% FCF yield does not need growth to work.

Bear Case

Organic sales have fallen and are guided to fall again; adjusted EPS is guided down another 10–15% to $3.00–$3.20; the dividend already consumes 84% of free cash flow; and the balance sheet carries $13.6B of debt at 3.8x EBITDA with a 0.70 current ratio. The company spent a year buying volume with price and got −2% organic for it. The $1.75B Pet write-down says the growth leg was overpaid for. Campbell's has just shown what happens when a food company's cash flow stops covering both debt reduction and the dividend. With four Sell ratings, an 8.4% short interest and a Bernstein target of $30, the market is treating the yield as a warning, not a gift.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume doesn't come back. Store-brand trade-down and GLP-1-driven changes in eating were the forces the Wall Street Journal named in July 2026; FY2026 organic sales fell 2% and the FY2027 guide spans −1.5% to +0.5%. A result at the bottom of that range, with price investment still running, would put the $3.00 EPS floor at risk.
02
Dividend coverage. $2.44 per share against $3.00–$3.20 of guided adjusted EPS (76–81%) and an 84.38% FCF payout, on a balance sheet with 1.82 debt-to-equity. A peer cut its dividend 36% on September 3. The payout was held at $0.61 on September 29 — but the yield says the market is pricing the risk.
03
More write-downs. FY2026 carried $2.8B of impairments and valuation losses. Goodwill and brand intangibles are tested against forecasts; if Pet or other brands underperform the new assumptions, further non-cash charges follow — no cash effect, but a signal the market has already learned to punish.
Industry Commentary
Big Food's volume problem
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

In July 2026 the Wall Street Journal reported on Big Food's slide, as summarised by Entrepreneur: consumers moving toward fresh food and cleaner ingredients, store brands at 24% of grocery market share (Private Label Manufacturers Association figure), more than 10% of American adults taking a GLP-1 weight-loss drug, and price cuts and protein-added recipes that have not turned the trend. General Mills, Campbell's and Kraft Heinz were named among companies whose shares had lost 50% to 70% over a decade while the S&P 500 tripled. General Mills' own numbers trace the arc — gross margin 35.00% in FY2024, 34.60% in FY2025, 33.73% in FY2026 — alongside three straight years of falling revenue.

Portfolio reshaping and dividend stress across the group
ONGOING HEADWIND

The response across packaged food has been to sell slower businesses, cut costs and protect the payout. General Mills sold yogurt in the U.S. and Canada and its Brazil business, and has launched a $3B savings programme. Campbell's went further on September 3, 2026, cutting its dividend 36% to pay down debt; its shares closed down 6.9% that day, and General Mills and Kraft Heinz closed lower too. The point for an income investor: a high yield in this sector is only as safe as the free cash flow behind it after debt service.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At 9.16x EV/EBITDA, ~9.8x adjusted trailing earnings and an 8.6% FCF yield, General Mills is priced like a business in decline. The bull framing: the decline is largely self-inflicted and finite — divestitures, an extra week to lap, and a deliberate price investment — and FY2027 is the trough year management has already guided to. Every dollar of the $750M savings target that sticks is a dollar that doesn't have to come from volume. The bear framing: it is a business in decline. Revenue has fallen three years running, the growth acquisition has been written down, and the guide is for lower profit again. The way to adjudicate is not the P/E; it is two numbers per quarter — organic volume and free cash flow after the dividend. The first report under the FY2027 guide, on September 23, had organic sales flat and the guide reaffirmed; adjusted operating profit still fell 11%.
