01 Company Overview

Glencore is two companies under one roof. The Industrial side owns and runs mines, smelters and refineries — copper in Chile, Peru, Argentina and the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), cobalt as a copper by-product in the DRC, zinc, nickel, ferroalloys, coal in Australia, Colombia, South Africa and Canada, and oil refining. The Marketing side is one of the world's largest commodity traders: it buys, stores, ships and sells more than 60 commodities, its own output and other producers', and earns a margin on logistics, timing and price differences between places. Glencore was founded in 1974 as Marc Rich + Co AG, renamed after a management buyout in 1993, listed in London in May 2011 and merged with Xstrata in May 2013 (company timeline). Its primary listing is the London Stock Exchange, where it is one of the largest members of the FTSE 100; it also trades in Johannesburg, and its CDIs start trading on the Australian Securities Exchange as ASX:GLC on October 14, 2026.

The latest reported period is the first half of 2026 (six months to June 30, 2026), published August 5, 2026; Glencore reports half-yearly, with quarterly production reports in between. Revenue was US$174.43B, up 49% — most of it is traded volume passing through Marketing, so the profit lines matter more. Adjusted EBITDA rose 86% to US$10.12B; income attributable to shareholders was US$4.41B, against a US$655M loss in H1 2025; basic earnings per share were US$0.37 (US$0.31 before significant items).

What changed in 2026 is where the profit came from. The escalation of the Middle East conflict and the near halt of shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, in the company's words, "triggered a scramble for alternative supplies". That is a trader's market: Marketing Adjusted EBIT jumped 142% to US$3.29B, and inside it the Energy and steelmaking coal traders went from US$40M to US$2.66B, led by oil and gas. CEO Gary Nagle: "Marketing Adjusted EBIT was $3.3 billion, up 142% compared with the prior period, demonstrating the resilience and responsiveness of the business amid heightened geopolitical uncertainty and market volatility." The mines did well too — Industrial Adjusted EBITDA rose 72% to US$6.47B on copper prices up 39% — but the same conflict pushed up diesel, sulphur, sulphuric acid and freight costs. On October 2 Glencore said it now expects full-year 2026 Marketing Adjusted EBIT to exceed US$5B, above the top of its long-term US$2.3–3.5B range. Earlier in the year Glencore's Board rejected a combination with Rio Tinto, which said on February 5, 2026 that it would not make an offer; the Board said the proposed ownership split "significantly undervalued Glencore's underlying relative value contribution to the combined group".

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

19-year Marketing track record Producer + trader in one Global logistics & storage Copper growth pipeline Negative-cost zinc Investment-grade balance sheet US$14.0B committed liquidity

Glencore's edge is the combination. Its mines give the traders a steady flow of physical metal and coal; the traders give the mines a sales channel and market intelligence; and the trading book earns most when prices are disrupted, which is often when a pure miner suffers. Glencore points to a 19-year through-the-cycle track record of Marketing Adjusted EBIT, and the H1 2026 result shows the upside of that optionality. On credit, it is rated A3 by Moody's and BBB+ by S&P, with US$14.0B of committed liquidity at June 30 and bank facilities that carry no financial covenants. The weakness is that the trading profit is lumpy and the mining portfolio carries heavy jurisdictional and legal baggage.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Marketing earnings revert. A US$3.29B half is well above the full-year long-term range of US$2.3–3.5B. Glencore itself expects H2 volatility to stay above normal "albeit at lower levels than experienced during the first half". From 2027 its new guidance method puts the mid-point at c.US$3.5B a year (range c.US$2.8–4.2B) — so the 2026 figure above US$5B is not the base case.
02
The DRC. KCC and Mutanda drive the copper growth and all of the cobalt, and the government's export quota cut cobalt output by 46%. A non-binding plan to sell a 40% stake in the two assets to the US-backed Orion Critical Mineral Consortium, at an implied combined enterprise value of about US$9B, was announced on February 3, 2026; the H1 presentation describes the sale process as underway.
03
Legal overhang. The 2022 US and UK bribery resolutions and the 2024 Swiss and Dutch ones are closed, but Jersey's Economic Crime and Confiscation Unit has been investigating the company since September 2024, and UK shareholders are suing over the 2011 and 2013 prospectuses. Glencore says the possible effects "could be material" and cannot yet be estimated.
04
Permits and costs. On May 15, 2026 a Chilean environmental tribunal ruling purported to set aside the 2021 permit for Collahuasi's capacity project (including a nearly complete desalination plant); Glencore said it did not expect any immediate impact on production. Separately, the Murrin Murrin nickel operation in Australia took a US$457M impairment in H1 on a stronger Australian dollar and higher sulphur costs.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

How to read the series. The line is the London closing price in pence, as Yahoo Finance reports it: Glencore has not split its shares in the window, and the many cash distributions are not added back, so each ex-distribution date shows up as a small step down and the price return understates what a holder earned. Glencore pays in US dollars, so a sterling holder's income also moves with the exchange rate.

From the September 2021 close of 351.85p to the October 2, 2026 close of 561.40p, the shares gained 59.6% on price alone, against 47.6% for the FTSE 100. The path was three acts. In 2022 the stock climbed to 563.50p in November 2022, with RSI at 95.0 in March 2022, the most overbought reading in the window. From 2023 to early 2025 it slid, reaching a five-year low of 244.40p in April 2025 with RSI at 13.8, the most oversold reading. Then it more than doubled: from that low to the August 2026 month-end of 595.60p, the window high, is +143.7%. January 2026 alone added 22.8%, the best month in the window; on February 5 Rio Tinto said it would not make an offer for Glencore. The monthly RSI is now 63.0, and the 10-month moving average, about 550p, sits just below the price.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Glencore is a copper growth story with a trading business that pays out when the world is disorderly. 2026 Marketing is guided above US$5B, the mines are earning a 52% copper margin, and Glencore's illustrative 2026 Adjusted EBITDA is c.US$19.7B against US$13.5B in 2025. Net debt is only 0.56x EBITDA, and c.US$3.5B is going back to shareholders this year. The copper pipeline — Alumbrera ahead of schedule, MARA approved under RIGI, KCC land access secured — targets c.1 Mt a year by end-2028 and c.1.6 Mt by 2035. The ASX listing on October 14 adds a new pool of buyers. At 16.2x trailing and 12.4x forward earnings, with 16 of 20 analysts at Buy and an average target 15% above the price, the market is not paying much for any of it.

Bear Case

The half was flattered by a war. Energy trading went from US$40M of EBIT to US$2.66B in one half, and Glencore's own guidance assumes that falls back to a c.US$3.5B group Marketing mid-point from 2027. Strip out the trading spike and the US$744M of one-off gains and tax credits, and earnings look more ordinary. Costs are rising (copper guide 203¢/lb versus 184¢ in H1), cobalt is held hostage by DRC export quotas, coal still makes up a fifth of EBITDA in a sector many investors avoid, and the legal overhang (the Jersey investigation, UK shareholder claims) has no price on it. The stock has already risen 64% in a year.

Top 3 Risks

01
Trading earnings normalise faster than expected. If Middle East supply routes reopen and volatility collapses, the energy book gives back what it gained. Glencore's own new method puts a normal year at c.US$2.8–4.2B of Marketing EBIT, against more than US$5B in 2026.
02
Copper delivery in difficult places. The growth targets lean on the DRC (export quotas, the pending Orion sale), Argentina (a new investment regime) and Chile (the Collahuasi permit ruling). Delays push out the volumes that justify the copper story.
03
Legal and ESG overhang. The Jersey investigation and UK prospectus claims could cost amounts Glencore says may be material and cannot estimate, and coal exposure keeps some investors out of the stock entirely.
Industry Commentary
Commodity trading is a volatility business
2026 WINDFALL

A trader earns on the spread between where a commodity is and where it is needed, and on the timing between buying and selling. Calm markets compress those spreads; shocks widen them. H1 2026 was the textbook case: Hormuz disruption cut supply routes, Glencore's oil and gas desk captured the regional price gaps, and Marketing EBIT more than doubled. The cost is risk — the VaR limit had to be waived for two months. Glencore's new guidance method ties normal-year trading profit to the size of its inventory and its funding costs, which makes the long-run number more predictable and makes clear that 2026 is above it.

Copper: tight concentrate, scarce new mines
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Average copper prices rose 39% period on period in H1 2026, and spot treatment charges moved further below zero — smelters are paying miners for concentrate because there is not enough of it. That is good for miners with growth and bad for smelters, and Glencore is both. Its answer is to grow mine supply: Argentina (MARA, El Pachón, Alumbrera), Peru (Coroccohuayco), the DRC and the US (NewRange). In the Rio Tinto talks its Board said the terms did not adequately value "our copper business, and its leading growth pipeline".

Coal: cash today, shrinking appeal
MIXED

The coal mines produced US$1.93B of H1 Industrial EBITDA (US$2.36B for the segment with oil refining and oil production), and the 2026 energy shock helped them: lower LNG availability sent buyers back to thermal coal. But the margin on energy coal is thin (US$17.8/t in H1) and Glencore cut Cerrejón output in 2025 in what it called an oversupplied Atlantic market. Steelmaking coal from EVR is the higher-margin piece, at an estimated US$86.4/t for 2026. For a long-term holder the question is how much coal cash funds copper growth, and how much the coal exposure weighs on the valuation.
