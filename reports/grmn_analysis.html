01 Company Overview

Garmin designs and manufactures GPS-enabled hardware across five reported segments — fitness, outdoor, aviation, marine and auto OEM — and wraps them in the Garmin Connect software ecosystem. FY2025 revenue was $7.25B, up 15.1% from $6.30B in FY2024, with the trailing-twelve-month figure through the September 26, 2026 quarter at $7.89B, up 13.6% YoY. The market values the company at $53.94B (192.70M shares at the October 29, 2026 close).

The central debate on this page is still the price, not the business, and the third quarter made the business side stronger. Q3 2026 delivered $1.99B of revenue (+12%), a 63.3% gross margin, a 30.8% operating margin, and GAAP diluted EPS of $2.80, above Garmin's own adjusted (“pro forma”) EPS of $2.76. The outdoor segment, which had shrunk 2% in Q2, grew 20% on the new fēnix 9 watches, and management raised its 2026 outlook to about $8.15B of revenue and $10.80 of pro forma EPS, up from $10.00. Two things flattered the quarter: about $33M of refunds of tariffs paid earlier, and memory chips bought before prices rose. CFO Doug Boessen told analysts on the call: “We expect higher memory costs to impact the fourth quarter, which has been factored in our full year gross margin guidance.” GRMN trades at 26.9× trailing earnings after a five-year run that took the stock from $143.60 to $279.94, and it fell 5.8% on the day of the beat before recovering 2.7% the next. Sections 04 and 07 take apart whether the earnings base or the valuation is the fragile part.

Infotainment, domain controllers and camera systems built into vehicles for carmakers — a components business sold to automotive procurement departments, which is a completely different animal from selling a $900 watch to a runner. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
VERTICAL INTEGRATION AVIATION CERTIFICATION (STCs) GARMIN CONNECT ECOSYSTEM BATTERY-LIFE ENGINEERING HELM / COCKPIT ATTACH RATES FORTRESS BALANCE SHEET FIVE-MARKET DIVERSIFICATION

Garmin's moat is engineering integration plus regulatory time, and the two work on different clocks. On the consumer side the advantage is that Garmin designs and manufactures its own devices, which is why a fēnix can run for weeks on a charge while a general-purpose smartwatch runs for a day. That is not a software feature a competitor patches in; it is silicon, power management and industrial design decided years earlier. Layered on top is Garmin Connect, where a decade of a customer's training history lives, which makes switching brands cost the user their own data. On the professional side the moat is slower and harder: every avionics box requires an STC for each airframe it goes into, and that library takes years and capital to build. The financial evidence is in the segment table: a 78% gross margin in aviation and 68% in outdoor in Q3 2026 are not the margins of a commodity manufacturer. The balance sheet reinforces all of it — no borrowings (stockanalysis.com counts $224.5M of lease obligations as debt) against $4.07B of cash and marketable securities at September 26, 2026, a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.02 — meaning Garmin can fund a product cycle, a downturn and a dividend simultaneously without asking anyone's permission.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Discretionary hardware concentration. Fitness plus outdoor is 65.0% of Q3 2026 revenue and both are one-off purchases of expensive optional goods. Garmin does sell subscriptions — on the October 28 call Pemble said Connect Plus, the paid tier of its Garmin Connect app, has grown faster than the segments it serves — and it added TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic in July 2026, but it reports none of that revenue separately, so there is no visible recurring base to cushion a consumer slowdown. A recession does not compress Garmin's margins gradually; it stops the replacement cycle.
▸
Q3's margins had help. About $33M of refunds of tariffs paid earlier lifted the Q3 gross margin; without them it would have been about 61.7% rather than 63.3% (our arithmetic on the release). Memory chips helped too. Pemble on the call: “Our results have benefited from our safety stock of memory purchased at lower prices.” He linked the memory situation to the wider build-out of AI, and Boessen said the higher costs reach the fourth quarter. Neither help repeats at the same size.
▸
Guidance implies a softer fourth quarter. Management now guides FY2026 to about $8.15B of revenue, a 61.0% gross margin and a 29.0% operating margin, against nine-month actuals of 61.8% and 28.8%. Our arithmetic on those round numbers puts Q4 revenue near $2.39B (+12%) at a gross margin near 59%, down from 63.3% in Q3 and in line with Q4 2025's 59.2%. Guidance has been raised twice this year (July and October); the question is whether it is conservative again or whether Q3 was the high point for margins.
▸
Auto OEM as a permanent drag. A 16%-gross-margin segment that lost $12M in Q3 2026, inside a 63%-gross-margin company, drags the company-wide margin down by its nature, and it ran an operating loss for FY2025 too. Automotive programme wins are lumpy and take years to turn into revenue, and Garmin has no pricing power against carmaker procurement. Revenue fell 20% in Q3; the Mercedes-Benz programme management expects to drive growth in 2027 is the next test.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCTOBER 29, 2026

RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen persistently and may be stretched; below 30 means the opposite. It says nothing about whether a business is good — only whether a price has moved a long way in one direction without pausing.

Over the five years from the October 29, 2021 close of $143.60 to the October 29, 2026 close of $279.94, GRMN returned +94.9% on price alone, against +67.5% for SPY (an S&P 500 fund) and +85.3% for QQQ (a Nasdaq-100 fund) over the same dates, price only, plus five years of a dividend now running at $4.20 a share. That headline hides two completely different stocks. The first, from late 2021 through September 2022, was a post-pandemic hardware unwind: revenue actually fell 2.5% in FY2022 and the shares fell 44.1% from $143.60 to $80.31. The second, from that low onward, is a +265.8% advance to the July 2026 peak close of $293.78, and the business improved underneath it: operating margin went from 21.15% in FY2022 to 28.82% on a trailing basis today, while revenue grew from $4.86B to $7.89B.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Five end markets, four of them growing in Q3 2026, on a 63.3% gross margin and a 30.8% operating margin — with $4.07B of cash and marketable securities and no borrowings. Outdoor came back to +20% on the fēnix 9, fitness grew 16% at a 33% operating margin, and management raised its full-year outlook in July and again in October, to $10.80 of pro forma EPS. TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic, and the Mercedes-Benz programme due in 2027, give the two weakest parts of the story (little recurring revenue, a loss-making auto unit) a path forward. Earnings quality is high: Q3 GAAP EPS of $2.80 came in above pro forma $2.76, and a 26.4% ROIC comes with no debt. The stock fell 5.8% on the day of a beat-and-raise.

Bear Case

You are paying 26.9× trailing earnings and a 2.78 PEG for a maker of discretionary hardware. Q3's 63.3% gross margin had help — about $33M of tariff refunds and memory chips bought before prices rose — and management's own guidance implies a fourth-quarter gross margin near 59%. Auto OEM lost $12M in Q3 on a 16% gross margin and shrank 20%. There is no disclosed subscription revenue to cushion a consumer slowdown, and five of nine analysts on stockanalysis.com rate it Hold, with one Sell. The rise in the P/E from 18.2× at the end of FY2022 to 26.9× today accounts for about a third of the share price's gain since then (our arithmetic: the price roughly tripled while the P/E rose about 1.5 times), and that part cannot be counted on to repeat.

Top 3 Risks

1
The valuation falls back. At 26.9× trailing earnings and a PEG of 2.78, a return to the 18–19× year-end P/E of FY2022 and FY2023 would cost roughly 30% of the share price without a single estimate being cut. This is the dominant risk and it does not depend on how well the company operates.
2
Margins drift back down. Q3's record margin had one-off help from about $33M of tariff refunds and from memory chips bought at lower prices; management expects higher memory costs from the fourth quarter. If the gross margin settles back near 59–60%, earnings growth slows even with revenue intact — and the outdoor rebound has to prove it lasts beyond the fēnix 9 launch quarter.
3
Consumer discretionary cyclicality with no recurring cushion. Fitness plus outdoor is 65.0% of Q3 2026 revenue, all of it one-off purchases of expensive optional hardware. FY2022 already demonstrated what this looks like — revenue −2.5%, operating margin down to 21.15%, shares −44%. TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic are the right idea, but they are months old and do not yet show up as a separate revenue line.
Industry Commentary
The wearables market is consolidating around two very different logics
ONGOING HEADWIND

Garmin competes against general-purpose computers worn on the wrist (Apple, Samsung) and against single-purpose sensors (Whoop, Coros, Polar). Its defence against the first is battery life and sport-specific depth; against the second it is ecosystem breadth. The July 2026 launch of CIRQA, a screen-free band, is Garmin moving onto Whoop's turf, while Apple's Ultra line has been moving onto Garmin's. The segment data shows how fast the picture swings: outdoor revenue fell 2% in Q2 2026, then grew 20% in Q3 on the fēnix 9 launch, while fitness growth slowed from 25% to 16%. A new flagship can still win back the premium adventure end of the range — the 68%-gross-margin end; the test is whether outdoor keeps growing once the launch quarter has passed.

Hardware companies are trying to buy their way into recurring revenue
EMERGING TAILWIND

Device makers are trying to add subscription revenue, which comes back every year, to one-off hardware sales, and Garmin has spent 2026 working on it: TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic acquired in July 2026 and the Moxy sensor business in September, on top of a Connect ecosystem that already holds years of user training data. Garmin already charges for extra app features through Connect Plus, which Pemble said on the October 28 call has grown faster than the segments it serves. The test is whether owners will pay for software on top of the device, and there is no disclosed subscription revenue line yet to track it.

Bull vs bear: this is a multiple argument, not a business argument
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Almost nothing in the reported data disputes that Garmin is a high-quality business: 26.4% ROIC, 0.02 debt-to-equity, 61.1% gross margin, $1.47B of free cash flow, GAAP EPS above the adjusted figure. The bulls are right about the company. The bears are not arguing with them — they are arguing that a business whose revenue grows 12–15% a year should not trade at 27× trailing earnings with a PEG of 2.78, particularly when Q3's margins had one-off help and management's own guidance implies a fourth-quarter gross margin near 59%. Bull implication: if outdoor keeps growing after the fēnix 9 launch quarter and TrainingPeaks seeds a subscription line, the valuation holds and earnings do the work — $11.35 of FY2027 consensus EPS at today's 26.9× is about $305. Bear implication: if margins fall back as memory costs come through and investors pay 22× instead, the same $11.35 is worth about $250, near the bottom of the Street's $220–$370 target range. That range is exactly this argument, expressed in dollars.
