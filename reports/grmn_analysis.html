01 Company Overview

Garmin designs and manufactures GPS-enabled hardware across five reported segments — fitness, outdoor, aviation, marine and auto OEM — and wraps them in the Garmin Connect software ecosystem. FY2025 revenue was $7.25B, up 15.1% from $6.30B in FY2024, with the trailing-twelve-month figure through the June 27, 2026 quarter at $7.67B, up 13.5% YoY. At a $52.49B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #478 globally and #11 among Swiss-domiciled companies, sitting between TE Connectivity and Honeywell in the electronics cohort.

The central debate on this page is multiple, not execution. Execution is not in question: Q2 2026 delivered $2.02B of revenue (+11%), a 62.4% gross margin, a 30.4% operating margin, and GAAP diluted EPS of $2.80 against pro forma EPS of $2.81 — a one-cent gap, which is the cleanest earnings quality you will find in consumer technology. The question is what a hardware company whose largest segment is discretionary smartwatches should be worth. GRMN trades at 28.1× trailing earnings after a five-year run that took the stock from $143.60 to $272.20, and it has already given back 13% from its August 10 closing high of $313.16 with no company-specific news in between. Sections 04 and 07 take apart whether the earnings base or the rating is the fragile part.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
VERTICAL INTEGRATION AVIATION CERTIFICATION (STCs) GARMIN CONNECT ECOSYSTEM BATTERY-LIFE ENGINEERING HELM / COCKPIT ATTACH RATES FORTRESS BALANCE SHEET FIVE-MARKET DIVERSIFICATION

Garmin's moat is engineering integration plus regulatory time, and the two work on different clocks. On the consumer side the advantage is that Garmin builds most of its own hardware — it operates its own manufacturing rather than outsourcing the whole stack — which is why a fēnix can run for weeks on a charge while a general-purpose smartwatch runs for a day. That is not a software feature a competitor patches in; it is silicon, power management and industrial design decided years earlier. Layered on top is Garmin Connect, where a decade of a customer's training history lives, which makes switching brands cost the user their own data. On the professional side the moat is slower and harder: every avionics box requires an STC for each airframe it goes into, and that library takes years and capital to build. The financial evidence is in the segment table: a 75% gross margin in aviation and 69% in outdoor are not the margins of a commodity manufacturer. The balance sheet reinforces all of it — $226.9M of total debt against $2.67B of cash, a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.03 — meaning Garmin can fund a product cycle, a downturn and a dividend simultaneously without asking anyone's permission.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Discretionary hardware concentration. Fitness plus outdoor is 61.3% of Q2 2026 revenue and both are one-off purchases of expensive optional goods. There is no subscription base to cushion a consumer slowdown — TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic, acquired July 2026, are the first serious attempt to build one, and they start from a negligible base. A recession does not compress Garmin's margins gradually; it stops the replacement cycle.
▸
Outdoor is already declining. The highest-gross-margin consumer segment fell 2% year over year in Q2 2026. Whether that is a pre-fēnix-9 air pocket or genuine share loss to Apple's Ultra line and Coros will be visible in the Q3 print on October 28. Mix shift out of a 69% gross margin segment and into a 64% one is a slow, quiet compression that headline growth hides.
▸
Guidance implies second-half margin compression. Management guides FY2026 to a 59.7% gross margin and 27.0% operating margin against Q2 actuals of 62.4% and 30.4%. That is not a rounding difference — it implies the back half runs materially below the quarter that sent the stock up 16%. Either guidance is conservative, as it has repeatedly proven to be, or the market has extrapolated a peak quarter.
▸
Auto OEM as a permanent drag. A 22% gross margin, 2% operating margin segment inside a 62% gross margin company is dilutive by construction, and it ran an operating loss for FY2025. Automotive programme wins are lumpy and take years to convert into revenue, and Garmin has no pricing power against carmaker procurement. Growth has stopped (+1% in Q2 2026) without the margin improving.

RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen persistently and may be stretched; below 30 means the opposite. It says nothing about whether a business is good — only whether a price has moved a long way in one direction without pausing.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $143.60 to the September 10, 2026 close of $272.20, GRMN returned +89.6% on price alone, plus roughly five years of a dividend now running at $4.20 a share. That headline hides two completely different stocks. The first, from late 2021 through September 2022, was a post-pandemic hardware unwind: revenue actually fell 2.5% in FY2022 and the shares fell 44.1% from $143.60 to $80.31. The second, from that low onward, is a +265.8% advance to the July 2026 peak close of $293.78, driven by something genuinely durable — operating margin went from 21.15% in FY2022 to 27.61% on a trailing basis today, while revenue compounded from $4.86B to $7.67B.

The recent action is where the interesting tension sits. The stock gapped 16.2% higher on July 29, 2026 on the Q2 print, ran to a closing high of $313.16 on August 10, and has since drifted back to $272.20 — a 13.1% decline with no company-specific news in the interval. Whatever that is, it is not a reaction to fundamentals; it is a rerating of a rerating. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Five independent end markets, four of them growing, on a 62.4% consolidated gross margin and a 30.4% operating margin — with a balance sheet carrying $2.67B of cash against $226.9M of debt. Fitness grew 25% in Q2 2026 at a 37% operating margin, the fēnix 9 launch refreshes the highest-margin consumer line for Q4, and the TrainingPeaks acquisition opens a credible path to recurring revenue. Earnings quality is close to unimprovable: GAAP EPS of $2.80 against pro forma $2.81, and 26.2% ROIC achieved with no leverage. The stock is already 13.4% below its 52-week high on no news at all.

Bear Case

You are paying 28.1× trailing earnings and a 2.99 PEG for a discretionary hardware manufacturer whose second-largest segment is already shrinking (outdoor, −2% in Q2). Company guidance implies the back half of 2026 runs at a 59.7% gross margin and 27.0% operating margin — below the quarter that produced a 16% single-day rally. There is no subscription base to cushion a consumer slowdown, auto OEM loses money at 22% gross margin, and four of eight covering analysts rate it Hold with two more at Sell. The rerating from ~18× to ~28× did the heavy lifting in the last three years, and that trick does not repeat.

Top 3 Risks

1
Multiple compression. Industry Commentary
The wearables market is consolidating around two very different logics
ONGOING HEADWIND

Garmin competes against general-purpose computers worn on the wrist (Apple, Samsung) and against single-purpose sensors (Whoop, Coros, Polar). Its defence against the first is battery life and sport-specific depth; against the second it is ecosystem breadth. The July 2026 launch of CIRQA, a screen-free band, is Garmin moving onto Whoop's turf, while Apple's Ultra line has been moving onto Garmin's. The evidence that this is live pressure rather than theory is in the segment data: outdoor revenue fell 2% in Q2 2026 while fitness grew 25% — the premium adventure end of the range is where the squeeze is showing up first, and it happens to be the 69%-gross-margin end.

Hardware companies are trying to buy their way into recurring revenue
EMERGING TAILWIND

The valuation gap between "sells devices" and "sells subscriptions" is the widest structural discount in consumer technology, and Garmin has spent 2026 attacking it: TrainingPeaks and TrainHeroic acquired in July 2026, on top of a Connect ecosystem that already holds years of user training data. The prize is real — a subscription attached to an installed base of tens of millions of devices would justify a multiple Garmin has never carried. The catch is that Garmin has historically refused to charge for software, which is part of why customers choose it. Converting goodwill into ARPU without eroding the goodwill is the hardest thing management has attempted in a decade, and there is no disclosed subscription revenue line yet to track it.

Bull vs bear: this is a multiple argument, not a business argument
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Almost nothing in the verified data disputes that Garmin is a high-quality business: 26.2% ROIC, 0.03 debt-to-equity, 60.1% gross margin, $1.60B of free cash flow, a one-cent gap between GAAP and pro forma EPS. The bulls are right about the company. The bears are not arguing with them — they are arguing that a business whose revenue grows 11–15% and whose earnings grow 18% should not trade at 28× with a PEG of 2.99, particularly when the fastest-growing segment is discretionary hardware and management's own full-year guidance implies second-half margins below the quarter just reported. Bull implication: if the fēnix 9 reaccelerates outdoor and TrainingPeaks seeds a subscription line, the multiple holds and earnings do the work — $10.13 of 2026 EPS at 28× is $284, and 2027 estimates carry it further. Bear implication: if outdoor stays negative and guidance proves literal rather than conservative, the same $10.13 at a 22× multiple is $223 — below every analyst target on the page except the low. The Street's $220–$370 range is exactly that argument, expressed in dollars.
