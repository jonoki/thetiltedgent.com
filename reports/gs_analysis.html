01 Company Overview

Goldman Sachs is a global investment bank and asset manager. It advises companies on M&A, raises money for corporations and governments, trades stocks, bonds, currencies and commodities for big investors, and lends to them. Through Asset & Wealth Management it oversees $4.10 trillion of assets under supervision (Sep 30, 2026), after $83B of net client inflows in the third quarter.

Fiscal 2025 net revenues were $58.28B, up +8.9% YoY, with net earnings of $17.18B and EPS of $51.32. 2026 is running well ahead of that: in the first nine months net revenues were $56.24B (+25.5%) and EPS $55.56 (+48.8%). The third quarter, reported on October 13, 2026, brought $18.68B of net revenues (+23% YoY) and EPS of $17.01, against $12.25 a year earlier and a $12.90 Nasdaq consensus; Equities trading revenue rose 72% to $6.44B. TTM EPS through September is $69.55. The firm is 157 years old.

Goldman's moat is not technological — it is relational, reputational and regulatory, and it compounds. Boards hire Goldman for the biggest, most contested transactions partly because hiring Goldman is itself a signal to shareholders, and every big deal makes the next one easier to win. On the markets side, the moat is scale and balance sheet: lending to hedge funds and other big traders against their positions requires the ability to hold risk and extend credit at size, which only a handful of global banks can do. That business is now one of Goldman’s largest steady earners: Equities financing alone brought in $3.37B in Q3 2026, nearly double a year earlier. Regulation cuts both ways — the CET1 capital and stress-test rules that limit Goldman's returns also make it very expensive for a newcomer to compete. Finally, the partnership culture remains an effective way to keep talent: being named partner is a prize few firms can offer, even as independent advisory boutiques bid aggressively for individual bankers.

Key Vulnerabilities

Cyclicality of the revenue base. Roughly two-thirds of revenue comes from activity levels Goldman does not control — deal volumes, issuance windows, and trading volatility. The firm went from $59.34B of net revenues in 2021 to $46.25B in 2023, a 22% decline that cut EPS by 62% (from $59.45 to $22.87). The Q2 and Q3 2026 quarters ($20.34B and $18.68B of net revenues) are the mirror image of that, and they are not a run-rate. The market already prices this in: the trailing P/E has fallen to 12.8× while earnings rose.

Private credit taking the lending. Apollo, Ares, Blackstone, Blue Owl and their peers now lend directly to large buyout-backed companies, bypassing the bank-arranged loan market Goldman underwrites. That takes share of exactly the debt-underwriting and leveraged-finance fees that historically sat in GBM. Goldman is on both sides — Goldman Sachs Alternatives is itself a large private credit manager — but the fee on managing a private credit fund is smaller, if steadier, than the fee on underwriting the loan.

Boutique talent leakage in advisory. Centerview, Evercore, PJT, Moelis and Perella Weinberg have won share of advisory fees by paying senior bankers more and carrying no balance sheet, capital charge or lending business. Every senior departure is a direct transfer of client relationships — the exact asset the moat rests on.

Regulatory and litigation exposure. Goldman's stress capital buffer, an extra capital cushion on top of the minimum, is reset each year by the Federal Reserve from its own stress-test models. The firm also carries recurring legal exposure — 1MDB, consumer-lending practices, and periodic trading-conduct matters.

Capital-rule changes

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Goldman has now beaten Nasdaq's consensus EPS by 45% and 32% in back-to-back quarters, and the stock is cheaper than before either: 12.8× trailing and 12.4× FY2027 consensus earnings, at 2.39× book while earning a 20.7% return on equity this year. Equities financing, the steadier part of trading, nearly doubled in Q3 to a record. The consumer exit is essentially done with the Apple Card deal, and the mix keeps shifting toward fees on $4.10T of client assets, with alternatives fundraising heading for about $150B this year. Goldman bought back $3.0B of stock in Q3 at an average $1,048.73; at $891 the same money retires more shares. If the cycle has legs, the market has marked the stock down for a slowdown that has not shown up in the numbers.

Bear Case

Nine-month EPS grew 49% on trading conditions nobody controls, and the market is right not to pay up for it. The investment-banking backlog fell from June, Reuters reported a 41% drop in M&A activity from the second quarter, and MarketBeat's consensus implies Q4 EPS of roughly $12, down from $17.01 in Q3 — and only 7% growth into 2027. History is unambiguous: Goldman's EPS fell 62% from 2021 to 2023. The CET1 ratio has dropped from 14.4% to 12.4% this year, leaving less room for buybacks, and 14 of 24 analysts are not Buys. Meanwhile private credit keeps taking the leveraged-finance business and boutiques keep taking advisory talent.

Top 3 Risks
01
Peak-cycle earnings meeting a de-rating multiple. The most probable way to lose money here is not a blow-up but ordinary mean reversion: trading revenue normalizes, the deal pipeline stalls, and earnings fall while the multiple stays low or keeps shrinking. That combination took 31% off the stock between January and June 2022, and it requires no crisis at all — only a quiet quarter. The 22.6% drop from the July record close shows how fast it can start.
02
Regulatory capital reversal. The deregulation thesis is embedded in the current price. Goldman's stress capital buffer is reset annually by the Federal Reserve on models the firm cannot see or contest, and the political environment that produced a softer Basel endgame proposal can change. A single adverse stress-test result would curtail buybacks, which ran at $3.0B in Q3 alone, at a time when the CET1 ratio has already fallen to 12.4%.
03
Concentrated tail risk in a $2.18T balance sheet. Prime brokerage, financing and market-making mean large exposures to single clients and positions that are invisible until they aren't — the 2021 collapse of the Archegos family office cost its banks over $10B. Equities financing, which nearly doubled in Q3, is exactly this kind of lending. Add residual office-property exposure and litigation, and the chance of a sudden large loss is higher than a 1.28 beta suggests.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
The Capital-Markets Recovery, Now Tested
TAILWIND UNDER TEST

Deal-making came back in 2025 and 2026, and Goldman's fees show it: investment banking fees were $9.34B in FY2025 and $3.22B in Q3 2026 alone, up 21% on a year earlier, with IPOs and follow-on share sales lifting equity underwriting. Q3 also brought the first warning signs. Reuters reported that M&A activity fell 41% from the second quarter to $993B, and Goldman's release says its backlog of expected banking fees fell from June and is slightly below the end of 2025. Management reads it differently: Solomon said the firm entered the quarter with its highest backlog in five years and that it remains very robust. Advisory revenue is booked when a deal closes, so the gap between those two readings will show up in fees over the next two or three quarters.

Private Credit Is Eating the Middle of the Business
ONGOING HEADWIND

Direct lenders — private credit funds that lend to companies without a bank syndicate — have grown from a niche into a major source of large buyout loans. That substitutes directly for the leveraged-finance and debt-underwriting fees that historically sat inside Goldman's GBM segment. Goldman's response is to be on both sides — Goldman Sachs Alternatives is itself a large private credit manager, part of a record $491B of alternative assets under supervision at Sep 30, 2026 — but the trade is not economically neutral. Underwriting a loan and distributing it earns a large fee on almost no capital; managing a private credit fund earns a smaller recurring fee on locked-up capital. The revenue is more durable and higher-multiple; the near-term dollars are lower. Watch the ratio of AWM management fees to GBM debt underwriting over the next eight quarters — it is the clearest single read on whether this transition is accretive.

Basel Endgame, the SCB, and the Bull-vs-Bear Fault Line
THE DECIDING VARIABLE

The 2023 Basel III endgame proposal would have raised the core capital requirements of the affected banks by an estimated 16% in aggregate (Federal Reserve) and was, for Goldman specifically, a hard limit on returns. The March 2026 re-proposal would instead keep the overall level of core capital requirements for the largest banks about where it is, and the leverage-ratio changes finalized in November 2025 help most in low-risk business such as holding Treasuries and repo lending. The bull frames this as a permanent step-up: a lower requirement means structurally higher achievable returns, more capital released into buybacks, and a case for a multiple closer to Morgan Stanley's. The bear frames it as the most reversible input in the model: the Federal Reserve resets Goldman's stress capital buffer every year from its own models, rules that ease now can tighten again, and Goldman's CET1 ratio has already fallen from 14.4% to 12.4% this year. At 2.39× book with 42% of MarketBeat's panel at Buy, the friendly version is less priced in than in the summer, but it is still a bet on policy. Everything else in this analysis — the earnings power, the moat, the AUS growth — is genuinely knowable. This one is a policy bet, and investors should size it as such.
