01 Company Overview

GSK is a London-based maker of prescription medicines and vaccines. It is what remained after the July 2022 demerger of its consumer-health arm, Haleon (Sensodyne, Advil, Panadol). It reports three businesses: Specialty Medicines (HIV through its majority-owned ViiV Healthcare, plus respiratory, immunology and cancer drugs), Vaccines (led by the shingles vaccine Shingrix) and General Medicines (inhalers such as Trelegy and Advair, plus other established medicines). FY2025 sales were £32.7B, up 7% at CER, with Core earnings per share of 172.0p (+12% CER). At a $95B market value it is a FTSE 100 constituent, listed in London with ADSs in New York.

The latest quarter (Q2 2026, reported July 28): sales of £8,409M (+5% AER and CER) and Core EPS of 50.5p (+9% CER), with the Core operating margin at 33.3%. On the per-ADS estimates MarketBeat shows, Core EPS of $1.36 beat the $1.27 consensus by 7.1% and sales of $11.14B beat $11.02B by 1.1%. Statutory (Total) EPS fell 69% to 10.8p, because GSK wrote off £1,334M on camlipixant, a chronic-cough pill it stopped developing after its CALM-1/2 Phase III results, and £371M on assets from its Alector collaboration, and booked a £486M charge for remeasuring contingent consideration. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed and tilted: sales growth of 3–5% and Core operating profit growth of 7–9%, both now at the upper half, and Core EPS growth of 7–9% at the lower half, because interest costs rise to about £800M, including the ~$10.6B Nuvalent purchase, and the tax rate to about 17.5%. Within that, the Vaccines outlook went up and General Medicines went down. The ADSs rose 3.33% on the day, but by October 2 they were 12.4% below that close.

This report turns on two numbers. The first is £40B: management's outlook for sales of more than £40 billion by 2031. The 12 brokers in GSK's own compiled consensus (effective September 3, 2026) put 2031 sales at £36.4B, more than £3.5B short. The second is 2028–2030: the period in which dolutegravir, the HIV drug inside Dovato, Triumeq, Tivicay and Juluca, loses patent protection. The same consensus has dolutegravir-based sales falling from £5.7B in 2026 to under £1B in 2031. The new CEO's answer, set out with the Q2 results, is called Accelerate Growth: £1.9B of annual savings by 2029, more than 20 Phase III trial starts in 2026 (up from 10 planned), and a run of cancer deals: Nuvalent (lung cancer, closed July 15), a multiple-myeloma antibody from Chimagen (up to $750M) and a bowel/pancreatic/lung cancer conjugate from HUTCHMED (up to $1.295B). In CEO Luke Miels's words in the Q2 release: "To fund investment in the late-stage portfolio and R&D, we are starting a 3-year cost savings programme to simplify the organisation and to reallocate capital and resources. Savings will primarily be reinvested, with some used to improve margins and profitability in the dolutegravir patent expiry period (2028-2030)."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Integrase-inhibitor HIV franchise Long-acting HIV injectables Vaccine manufacturing scale Shingrix (shingles) Global commercial reach US tariff relief to Jan 2029

GSK's moat comes in two kinds. Vaccines are the durable one: they are hard to make at scale, they are bought by governments and health systems on multi-year schedules, and they do not face generic copies the way pills do. HIV is the lucrative one, and it is temporary. ViiV's medicines are built on integrase inhibitors, and the long-acting cabotegravir injectables are its attempt to move patients onto a newer, longer-protected molecule before dolutegravir's patents run out. Q2 showed the move under way: injectables delivered 80% of HIV growth. The open question is speed. Consensus has the cabotegravir regimens at £3.3B in 2030, while it has dolutegravir regimens falling by £4.7B between 2026 and 2031.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The dolutegravir cliff. Dolutegravir regimens are forecast at £5,741M in 2026, 16.9% of consensus group sales. GSK dates the loss of exclusivity to 2028–2030, and the consensus line falls to £994M by 2031. Everything in Accelerate Growth is sized against this gap.
02
US pricing. GSK and ViiV signed an agreement with the US Administration in December 2025 and a definitive deal in April 2026. It gives them Section 232 tariff relief through January 20, 2029, in exchange for joining Medicare's voluntary "Generous Model" from June 15, 2026, with supplemental state rebate agreements expected on or before October 1, 2026. Guidance includes the expected impact. Trelegy's US decline shows how quickly a benefit-design change can reach the numbers.
03
Pipeline misses are expensive. Camlipixant was GSK's late-stage chronic-cough drug; stopping it after Phase III cost a £1,334M impairment in Q2. The replacement assets are mostly bought (Nuvalent) or licensed (Ris-Rez from Hansoh, HMPL-A830 from HUTCHMED, the Chimagen antibody), so the bill for a miss now includes the purchase price.
04
Sterling and litigation tails. GSK reports in pounds but earns about half its sales in the US. At July 20 exchange rates GSK estimated currency would cut 2026 Sterling sales growth by 2 points and Core operating profit growth by 4 points. The US Zantac litigation is mostly resolved, but about 14,000 federal cases dismissed in December 2022 are on appeal at the Eleventh Circuit, with a decision expected in H2 2026.

FX exposure: two currencies sit between GSK's accounts and the ADS price. Sales are earned mainly in dollars and euros and reported in pounds: H1 2026 grew 5% at CER but only 3% at AER, because Sterling strengthened against the dollar. Per-ADS earnings and dividends are then translated back into dollars for US holders. A stronger pound lowers GSK's reported growth and raises the dollar value of each pence of EPS, so the two effects partly offset for a US holder.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, Core vs Total: GSK guides on Core results only. In Q2 2026 Total R&D included £1,334M of camlipixant impairment and £371M of Alector-related terminations, and other operating expense included a £486M CCL remeasurement charge, partly offset by £288M of divestment income, mainly linerixibat. Second, Zantac: FY2024 Total results carried a £1.8B ($2.3B) Zantac settlement charge, which is why FY2025 Total operating profit nearly doubled (+97% AER) on a 7% rise in Core. Third, the 2022 reshaping: the Haleon demerger and the 4-for-5 share consolidation in July 2022 make Total EPS before 2023 hard to compare. StockAnalysis shows FY2022 net income of £14.96B, against £4.39B in FY2021 and £4.93B in FY2023.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

The Haleon basis step: on July 18, 2022 GSK demerged Haleon, handing holders one Haleon share for each GSK share, and the same day consolidated its own shares 4-for-5 (ADS holders received 4 new ADSs for every 5, from July 19). Yahoo books these as two adjustment factors, 0.8 for the consolidation and 1.226 for the demerger, and restates every earlier close by the net factor (×1.25 ÷ 1.226 = ×1.0196). The chart uses those restated closes, so the points before July 2022 sit about 2% above the price that actually traded, and the demerger does not show as a fall. In effect the series treats the value of the Haleon shares as if it had stayed in GSK stock. From July 2022 onward the points are the traded closes.

On that basis the ADS went from $42.33 (October 2021 month-end) to $47.03 (October 2, 2026), +11.1% on price. Over the same window SPY (S&P 500) rose +67.6% ($459.25 → $769.64) and the FTSE 100 +44.6% (7,237.6 → 10,462.0), both on price alone. GSK's roughly 4% dividend yield closes part of that gap but not most of it. The path: a flat 2021–22 near $42–45, a collapse to the window low of $29.43 in September 2022, then a slow recovery to $44.77 by May 2024 and, after a dip, a sharp rerating from $33.82 (December 2024) to a peak month-end close of $59.13 in February 2026 (+74.8%), with the 6-month RSI at 90.5, the most overbought reading in the window. Since then the ADS has given back 20.5%. The 6-month RSI is now 38.75, and the price has been below its 10-month moving average (now $51.83) since August 2026.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The quarter says the engine works. Specialty Medicines grew 14%, HIV injectables delivered 80% of HIV growth, Core EPS beat by 7%, and management moved the sales and operating-profit guides to the upper half while launching a £1.9B savings plan. The pipeline has filled quickly: an approved lung-cancer drug (Jideytro) with first-line data, a second (neladalkib) awaiting an FDA decision, Ris-Rez with a 54% cut in the risk of death in a Chinese Phase III, and bepirovirsen for hepatitis B, whose pivotal data GSK says showed unprecedented functional cure rates, awaiting US and Japanese decisions in H2 2026. At 9.9 times 2026 Core earnings, with a ~4% yield and US tariff relief agreed through January 2029, the stock prices in the dolutegravir cliff and little of what replaces it. Every target in the US panel is above the price, and Berenberg and Jefferies rate it Buy.

Bear Case

The Street does not believe the £40B. GSK's own compiled consensus has 2031 sales at £36.4B, flat from 2029, because dolutegravir regimens fall from £5.7B to about £1B and General Medicines keeps shrinking. The replacement is being bought: Nuvalent's net cost of about £7.1B came on top of June's £15.1B of net debt, Core EPS growth is now guided to the lower half because of the interest bill and a higher tax rate, and camlipixant showed in Q2 what a miss costs (£1.3B). US pricing is being renegotiated through Medicare's Generous Model, and Trelegy is already shrinking there. The price has trailed the FTSE 100 by 33 points over five years, is 20% off its February peak and sits below its 10-month average. Until the Q3 print shows the new assets carrying weight, the multiple has little reason to expand.

Top 3 Risks

01
HIV patent expiry outrunning the injectables. Consensus cuts HIV sales by 44% between 2026 and 2031. If Cabenuva and Apretude adoption slows, or the next-generation long-acting regimens are late, the 2028–2030 margin promise is at risk.
02
Execution on bought growth. Nuvalent, Chimagen, HUTCHMED and the Hansoh licence all have to work, and most are early or mid-stage. Debt rose to fund them. Another camlipixant-sized impairment would hit Total EPS and confidence in the £40B outlook.
03
US pricing, currency and courts. GSK's Generous Model agreement took effect on June 15, 2026, and supplemental state rebate agreements were expected by October 1. A strong pound cuts reported growth (−4 points on 2026 Core operating profit at July rates). The Eleventh Circuit Zantac ruling is due in H2 2026.
Industry Commentary
US drug pricing — deals instead of tariffs
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

GSK agreed terms with the US Administration: tariff relief under Section 232 through January 20, 2029, in exchange for lower prices through Medicare's voluntary Generous Model and supplemental rebates with US states. The certainty is valuable for a company that earns about half its sales in the US. The cost will show up in pricing over several years, and GSK's Q2 already showed US pricing pressure on Nucala, Ojjaara and Trelegy.

The HIV market moves to long-acting
TRANSITION

HIV treatment and prevention are moving from daily pills to injections given every one or two months: in Q2 the injectables were 35% of ViiV's US HIV sales. ViiV's most direct rival is Gilead, whose Biktarvy was the comparator in ViiV's own head-to-head study. For ViiV the shift is both a defence and an opportunity: the injectables are growing 33–39% and carry later patents than dolutegravir. In US prevention, GSK described the long-acting market as competitive, which is the pressure point to watch on Apretude.

Big pharma buys its oncology growth from China and biotech
EMERGING TAILWIND

Two of GSK's recent oncology additions came from Chinese developers (Hansoh's Ris-Rez, HUTCHMED's HMPL-A830), one from privately held Chimagen (a T-cell engager) and one from a US biotech (Nuvalent). The bull framing: Ris-Rez's survival data are the first positive Phase III overall-survival result for a B7-H3-targeted ADC in any tumour type, per GSK. The bear framing: data from Chinese patients still has to be repeated globally, which is why the PanTumor-101 data outside China, and the Phase II readouts expected through 2027, matter.
