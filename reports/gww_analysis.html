01 Company Overview

W.W. Grainger distributes MRO supplies — maintenance, repair and operating products: the gloves, motors, fasteners, lighting, abrasives, safety gear and hand tools that keep a factory, hospital, school district or municipal fleet running. It is not what anyone would call a glamorous business. It is also, on the numbers, one of the better ones in the S&P 500: FY2025 revenue of $17.94B, up 4.5% from $17.17B, and a trailing-twelve-month figure through September 2026 of $19.37B, up 9.1%, earned at a ROIC of 34.0% and a ROE of 52.3%. The stock market values it at $67.45B (46.81M shares at the November 6 close, our calculation), and it serves what it describes as more than 4.6 million customers.

The central argument of this page is that Grainger is two companies wearing one ticker, and that any single blended margin, growth rate or P/E you read about it is close to meaningless. High-Touch Solutions N.A. is the traditional sales-force-and-branch business: in Q3 2026, 40.8% gross margin, 17.1% operating margin, sales up 13.8%. Endless Assortment — Zoro in the U.S. and the separately listed MonotaRO in Japan — sells online: 31.0% gross margin, 11.7% operating margin, sales up 19.3% on a daily organic constant-currency basis (adjusted for selling days, acquisitions and exchange rates). Blend them and you get "38.8% gross margin, 16.0% operating margin, +14.9% organic growth," which describes neither business. Section 02 pulls them apart.

Why the blended number misleads. Company gross margin in Q3 2026 was 38.8% ($2,011M on $5,181M). Neither segment is anywhere near that: High-Touch ran 40.8% and Endless Assortment 31.0%. The consolidated figure is an arithmetic result of the mix, so whenever Endless Assortment grows faster in dollars, the mix alone pulls reported gross margin down even if both segments improve. Q3 was the exception that shows the point: a weaker yen held Endless Assortment to 11.9% reported growth against High-Touch's 13.8%, while in local currency it grew 19.3%. Anyone reading a falling consolidated gross margin as lost pricing power may have mistaken a mix effect for a margin effect. The gap also narrows further down the income statement: 40.8% vs 31.0% at gross margin, 17.1% vs 11.7% at operating margin, because the online business carries less operating expense per dollar of sales. Set the two side by side and the trade is explicit. High-Touch earned about 46% more operating margin per dollar of sales in Q3 and grew 13.8%. In local currency Endless Assortment grew about 1.4 times as fast, and every dollar it adds dilutes the consolidated margin percentage while adding absolute profit. That is a good trade — an online dollar at an 11.7% operating margin is worth having — but it means the correct way to value Grainger is to value each business separately and add them up (a sum of the parts), not to put one P/E on the combined earnings. It also means the consolidated 16.0%–16.2% FY2026 operating margin guide is a weighted average of two businesses guided separately (High-Touch 17.3%–17.5%, Endless Assortment 10.9%–11.1%). If you only track the blended number, you cannot tell a good quarter from a bad one.

Sum-of-the-parts hint from the market: MonotaRO alone is publicly worth about ¥1.11T — roughly $7.05B at ¥157.50 to the dollar (November 6 close) — against Grainger's total $67.45B. Grainger's stake in it is a real, separately priced asset inside the consolidated company.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
HUGE PRODUCT RANGE NEXT-DAY AVAILABILITY PROCUREMENT INTEGRATION 4.6M CUSTOMER BASE DISTRIBUTION DENSITY PRIVATE-LABEL ASSORTMENT 55-YEAR DIVIDEND RECORD

The moat is boring on purpose. When a $400M/year manufacturing plant has a bearing fail at 2am, the cost of the bearing is a rounding error against the cost of the line being down. What the plant manager buys is certainty that the part is in stock and arrives tomorrow. That converts breadth of assortment plus fulfilment reliability into genuine pricing power on individual products, because nobody price-shops a $14 fitting against downtime. Layered on top is the part competitors find hardest to replicate: procurement integration. Once Grainger's catalogue is wired into a customer's purchasing system, once vending machines sit on the customer's own shop floor dispensing gloves against a cost centre, and once a contract price file has been negotiated with a national account, switching is not a purchasing decision — it is an IT and change-management project. That is why ROIC of 34.0% is sustainable in a business with no patents and no technology edge.

Two honest qualifications. First, the moat is narrower in the long tail — the low-urgency, planned, price-shopped purchase is exactly where a search box beats a sales rep, which is why Grainger built Zoro rather than defending only High-Touch. Second, the moat protects the relationship, not the gross margin percentage: Grainger's 39.4% gross margin is below both Fastenal's 44.6% and MSC's 40.9%. Grainger's superiority shows up in ROIC and absolute scale, not in unit pricing.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Amazon Business — taken seriously, not waved away. Amazon Business attacks precisely the segment of MRO demand where Grainger's moat is thinnest: the unplanned, low-value, non-contract purchase made by someone with a corporate card and no procurement relationship. Amazon does not need Grainger's margin, has structurally lower customer acquisition cost, and has already replicated the parts that can be replicated — a search-first catalogue, direct links into customers' purchasing software, multi-user approval workflows and negotiated business pricing. Two things are worth being precise about. (a) Grainger's management did not mention Amazon Business on the Q3 2026 call, as it did not on the Q2 call, and readers can decide whether that reads as confidence or as avoidance. (b) Amazon does not disclose Amazon Business revenue, so no share or size figure is asserted anywhere on this page; anyone quoting one to you is quoting an estimate. The honest framing is directional: Grainger's own strategic response — building and scaling Zoro at an 8.4% operating margin against High-Touch's 17.1% — is itself the admission that the threat is real. You do not build a lower-margin marketplace alongside a high-margin sales business unless you believe the marketplace channel is going to take that volume whether you own it or not.
▸
Tariff and input-cost pass-through is a two-sided risk. Grainger aims to run price/cost neutral — it passes tariffs and inflation through in price rather than absorbing them. On the Q3 call interim CFO Laurie Thomson said: "Price cost was roughly neutral during the quarter," with the September price increase, which CEO D.G. Macpherson put at about 1% for the full year, helping offset continued inflation. Neutrality is a dollar concept, not a percentage one: passing through a dollar of cost as a dollar of price holds gross profit flat while diluting gross margin percentage. The tariff-refund help has also faded: Q2's 39.5% gross margin carried about 90 basis points from $43M of tariff refunds (duties charged under IEEPA, the International Emergency Economic Powers Act), while Q3's refund benefit was about 10 basis points and was largely offset by an inventory write-off after a fire at the Louisville distribution center. Q3 gross margin was 38.8% against 38.6% a year earlier. Refunds do not recur; freight and product-cost inflation, which management tied to the conflict in the Middle East, may.
▸
Mix-driven margin erosion is structural, not cyclical. In local currency Endless Assortment grows faster than High-Touch, at roughly two-thirds the operating margin. Arithmetically, success compresses the consolidated margin percentage. Macpherson confirmed on the Q2 call that large projects lower the gross margin but not the operating margin — a defensible point, but it means the reported margin line will keep sending false signals, and a market that anchors on consolidated margin will keep mispricing good quarters as bad ones and vice versa.
▸
Sensitivity to the industrial cycle. MRO demand follows industrial production, factory headcount and how busy plants are. FY2025 showed what a soft cycle does: revenue grew only +4.5% and the shares spent most of the year below their November 2024 level. The FY2026 guide of +13.0%–14.5% organic growth is the company's own forecast, and Macpherson split the year's strength on the Q3 call: "a good portion of that is market, but a good portion of that is our ability to execute as well." Distinguishing the two matters at 31.6x trailing earnings.

RSI compares the size of recent monthly gains to the size of recent monthly losses and expresses the result on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen a lot with few down months and is described as "overbought"; below 30 means the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — an overbought stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $481.41 to the November 6, 2026 close of $1,440.70, GWW returned +199.3% on price alone — a compound annual rate of about 24.9%, before the dividend — against +61.4% for SPY and +66.6% for QQQ over the same dates, also on price alone. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A 34.0% ROIC business with 4.6 million customers, a 55-year dividend-increase record and growth that is still speeding up: Q3 2026 delivered +14.9% daily organic constant currency, with High-Touch led by double-digit volume, Zoro up 25.2% and preliminary October sales up about 18%. Endless Assortment's operating margin reached 11.7%, which answers the old objection that the online business was buying growth at a loss. The FY2026 adjusted EPS guide has moved up again, to $47.00–$48.00, and consensus already looks to $51.70 for FY2027. If the September prices stick without costing volume, that FY2027 figure is reachable.

Bear Case

You are paying 31.6x trailing and 27.9x FY2027 earnings for a distributor of consumables whose consensus rating is Hold and whose average price target sits 0.4% below the price. Reported EPS growth of 102.9% is largely the 2025 U.K. loss dropping out; adjusted growth was 22.8%. Q3's gross margin was 38.8% with most of Q2's refund help gone, MonotaRO slowed to 13.6% from 24.0%, and October's 18% carries about 2 points from lapping the 2025 government shutdown. Amazon Business is structurally advantaged in exactly the long-tail purchases Zoro chases, and management did not mention it on the call. The CFO seat is still interim, and 13 of 18 analysts sit at Hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Growth led by price instead of volume. The case for the current valuation rests on more goods being sold, not higher prices. In Q1 2026 management said price and volume contributed about equally in High-Touch, and in Q3 volume led. If the September increases produce revenue but not volume, the market will read FY2026 as a pricing year rather than a year of taking share, and a price of 27.9x FY2027 earnings would be hard to hold.
02
Structural margin dilution from the mix shift. In local currency Endless Assortment grows faster at about two-thirds the operating margin. Success mechanically lowers the consolidated percentage that most investors and screens actually track. Grainger has to keep explaining that its best-performing segment is the one dragging the headline down — and explanations only work until a quarter misses.
03
Amazon Business in the long tail, plus the disclosure gap around it. Neither Amazon nor Grainger publishes the numbers that would let anyone size this properly. What is observable is that Grainger felt compelled to build a lower-margin marketplace to defend the channel, that Zoro's 8.4% operating margin is about half High-Touch's 17.1%, and that management chose not to address the competitor on its most recent earnings call. Absence of disclosure is not evidence of safety.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs as a Pass-Through Machine — and Its Limits
ONGOING HEADWIND

Grainger is close to a pure pass-through business, and management describes it in exactly those terms: it targets price/cost neutrality, and interim CFO Laurie Thomson said Q3 was "roughly neutral" after a September increase that Macpherson put at about 1% for the full year. This is the structural advantage of distribution — you do not manufacture, so an input cost increase is a repricing exercise, not a margin event. But three limits deserve to be stated plainly. First, neutrality preserves gross dollars, not gross percentage: pass through $100 of cost as $100 of price and the margin ratio falls. Second, Q2's gross margin of 39.5% included about 90 basis points from $43M of IEEPA tariff refunds, described by management as only a small portion of eighteen months of absorbed tariff cost — so the company was not fully neutral through that period, and Q3's margin of 38.8%, with about 10 basis points of refund, is the cleaner read. Third, Q2 gross margin also absorbed private-label cost headwinds, unfavourable freight and lower-margin project business, and Macpherson's point that large projects lower the gross margin but not the operating margin means the gross margin line can fall for reasons unrelated to pricing power.

The MRO Demand Cycle Has Turned
EMERGING TAILWIND

On the Q3 call Thomson said "MRO demand continues to trend positively," and Macpherson described "a sort of steady strengthening throughout the year in terms of demand." The arithmetic of the turn is visible in the quarterly sequence: revenue growth of +1.7% in Q1 2025, +5.6%, +6.1%, +4.5%, then +10.1% in Q1 2026, +10.3% in Q2 and +11.3% in Q3, with daily organic constant-currency growth reaching 14.9%. Because MRO consumption tracks industrial production and headcount rather than capital spending, this is a relatively clean read on underlying industrial activity in North America and Japan. The bull interpretation is share gain on top of a recovering market; the bear interpretation is that a cyclical business is being priced as a steady grower at the top of its cycle. The volume-versus-price split is the test: in Q1 management called the two roughly equal in High-Touch, and in Q3 High-Touch volume growth reached double digits.

Digital Distribution Is Eating the Long Tail — Bull vs Bear
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The industry's defining structural question is what happens to the unplanned, low-value, non-contract MRO purchase when a search box is as good as a sales rep. Grainger's answer — Zoro and MonotaRO — is arguably the best in the industry: MonotaRO is a separately listed Tokyo-quoted company growing 19.4% on TTM revenue of ¥379.8B, and Zoro grew 25.2% daily in Q3 with its operating margin up 260 basis points to 8.4%. The bull framing: Grainger owns both the high-touch relationship and a credible marketplace, so it captures the volume whichever way the purchase migrates, and Endless Assortment's 11.7% operating margin shows the online model can be profitable at scale rather than needing permanent support. The bear framing: the online channel earns an 11.7% operating margin against High-Touch's 17.1%, so every share point that migrates earns about a third less — and the competitor best positioned in that channel is a company that does not need to earn 11.7% at all, does not disclose what it is doing, and was not mentioned on Grainger's most recent earnings call. Both framings are consistent with the same facts. Which one you believe is, in the end, the whole investment decision on this name.
