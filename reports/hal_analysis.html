01 Company Overview

In 1919 Erle P. Halliburton built a wooden mixing box and started an oil-well cementing business in Duncan, Oklahoma, with a borrowed wagon, a team of mules and a pump. A century later the company he founded is one of the three big names in oilfield services, alongside SLB and Baker Hughes. Halliburton does not own oil; it rents out know-how, crews and equipment to the companies that do. FY2025 revenue was $22.18B, down 3.3% from $22.94B in FY2024, and GAAP EPS fell to $1.50 from $2.83, dragged by impairments and other charges ($748M in the first nine months of 2025 alone). Its history is not quiet: the 1998 Dresser Industries merger, the 2007 separation of KBR, Dick Cheney's tenure as CEO (1995–2000), the cement job on Deepwater Horizon, and a $3.5B breakup fee paid to Baker Hughes when their 2016 merger was called off. HAL has been an S&P 500 member since the index's launch in March 1957.

The story of 2026 is a service company caught between two geopolitical currents and one weak home market. The first current is Venezuela, where Halliburton opened in 1940: the stock jumped in January as the market priced a return of Western service work, on September 21 the company signed memoranda of understanding (agreements to work together, not contracts) with Eneva and WESCA, and on the Q3 call COO Shannon Slocum said Halliburton is open for business there and will have a dozen electric submersible pumps (the motors that lift oil up a well) delivered there this quarter. The second is the Middle East conflict. "We are another three months into one of the largest energy disruptions in history," Jeff Miller said in the Q3 release. Middle East/Asia revenue was $4.02B in the first nine months of 2026, down 8% on a year earlier, but in Q3 it rose 8% sequentially to $1.41B, back to its level of a year before, on more work in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait. The weak market is North America: revenue there fell 9% year over year in Q3 on less fracking work in the US. The shares ran from a $28.26 close at the end of 2025 to $42.30 at the end of April and have given back 19.6% since, to $33.99. The central debate is management's first view of 2027 — mid-single-digit revenue growth and an operating margin of about 15%, some 200 basis points higher, with no help from US fracking — against a quarter in which the fracking division's margin did not move and the company booked $107M of severance to cut costs.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale in North American fracking Century of cementing know-how Global base network (Venezuela since 1940) Integrated well-construction contracts Three-player oligopoly at the top Proprietary tools & reservoir software

The moat is scale and presence. Oilfield service is a business where the customer pays for a crew to show up, in a remote place, with the right equipment, and not to make a mistake that costs a well. Only a handful of companies can do that across dozens of countries at once — and Halliburton has had bases in Venezuela since 1940, which is why it is first in line if work reopens there. Recent wins show the model: an integrated contract from bp for the first appraisal campaign at the Bumerangue field off Brazil (August 25, 2026) and a multi-year bundled drilling, well-construction and completions contract from Eni for the Cronos gas development off Cyprus (September 15, 2026). The weakness is that none of this protects price. When producers cut budgets, three well-capitalised competitors fight over fewer jobs, and margins show it: the adjusted operating margin was 12.4% in the first nine months of 2026, down from 13.7% a year earlier, and management is now cutting headcount to rebuild it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A price-taker in a cyclical market. Halliburton's customers set their budgets off the oil price and their own return targets. Revenue shrank 3.3% in FY2025 and gross margin fell from 18.75% to 15.71%; trailing gross margin is now 14.97%. The company cannot will demand into existence.
02
North America is 38% of revenue and shrinking. Q3 2026 North America revenue of $2.16B was down 9% YoY. The fracking fleet is the part of the business with the highest operating leverage (profits swing more than revenue) — which cuts both ways.
03
Geopolitics is now in the income statement. Middle East/Asia revenue is down 8% over the first nine months, and the COO expects inflationary headwinds in the region to persist. The Venezuela opportunity, on the other side of the ledger, rests on memoranda of understanding and early equipment orders — not yet a large business.
04
The smallest of the big three. At $28.32B of market value, Halliburton is roughly a third the size of SLB (about $76B) and half the size of Baker Hughes ($55.59B). Both rivals have larger non-oilfield or international businesses; of the three, Halliburton depends most directly on well activity.

Notable One-Off Items

Charges and credits pull GAAP and adjusted profit apart in both directions. Q3 2026 carried a $107M pre-tax charge: $107M of severance tied to cutting headcount worldwide, $25M of inventory write-downs and $14M of fixed-asset write-offs, offset by a $36M gain on investments and $3M of other items. After tax it cost $84M, which is why GAAP EPS was $0.50 and adjusted EPS $0.60. Q2 2026 went the other way: a $95M net credit (a $64M investment gain and other credits, less a $17M loss on a business sale) lifted GAAP EPS to $0.64 against adjusted $0.55. The SAP S/4 upgrade spending ($42M in Q3) stays inside adjusted profit, and its wind-down is part of management's 2027 margin plan. Buybacks were $250M in Q3 and $558M over nine months, with about $1.6B left on the authorisation, per the CFO.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 14.5x consensus 2026 earnings and 11.7x 2027 earnings for a direct bet on well activity, just as management has put a number on 2027: mid-single-digit revenue growth and an operating margin of about 15%, without any recovery in US fracking ("To us, that recovery is purely upside," Miller said). International revenue grew 9% YoY in Q3, the Middle East turned up 8% sequentially, Latin America grew 13% and Europe/Africa 23% YoY, and Venezuela is reopening to a company that has had bases there since 1940. Free cash flow of $1.60B TTM is a 5.6% yield, the dividend takes only 28.5% of earnings, and the company bought back $250M of stock in Q3 with about $1.6B of authorisation left. Consensus expects EPS to rise 23.8% in 2027, and the average target of $43.40 is 27.7% above the price.

Bear Case

Service companies are paid last and cut first. North America fell 9% YoY in Q3, the fracking division's margin did not budge against guidance for a 125–175 basis-point gain, and the company booked $107M of severance to cut its workforce. Roughly half of the 2027 margin goal is cost-cutting, not demand. Company-defined free cash flow was only $156M in Q3 as receivables grew, and consensus sees 2026 revenue up only 0.7% with EPS down 3.3%. The 2026 rally was built on geopolitics: a Venezuela opening that is still memoranda of understanding and early orders, and a Middle East conflict that raises the oil price while raising costs in the region where Halliburton's drilling work is growing. The stock sits below its 10-month average, and revenue has not grown since FY2023's $23.02B (TTM $22.48B).

Top 3 Risks

01
Customer budget cuts. Halliburton's revenue is its customers' capital spending. A lower oil price, or producers choosing buybacks over drilling, flows straight through to fleet utilisation and pricing — as FY2025's 3.3% revenue decline and Q3 2026's 9% fall in North America showed.
02
Middle East escalation. Middle East/Asia revenue is down 8% over the first nine months of 2026, and the COO expects inflationary headwinds in the region to persist. Crews and equipment in a conflict zone are both a cost and a safety exposure.
03
The 2027 plan slips. The ~15% margin target leans on cost savings, a flat North America and continued international growth. A further fracking slowdown, or a stall in Venezuela, where sanctions, payment risk and politics can all intervene, would leave the target to the cost cuts alone.
Industry Commentary
Oil up, activity down: the Gulf paradox
TWO-WAY RISK

For an oil producer, a Middle East war can be a windfall. For a service company, it is a paradox: the oil price may rise, but work in the region is disrupted and costs climb. Halliburton's Middle East/Asia revenue fell 8% over the first nine months of 2026, then recovered 8% sequentially in Q3 to $1.41B on more activity in Saudi Arabia and Kuwait. Miller frames the disruption as a source of years of work — he expects restoring production, rebuilding inventories and strengthening energy security to drive multi-year demand growth — while the COO warns that the region's cost inflation will persist for some time.

North American shale: shrinking, and priced as upside
ONGOING HEADWIND

The US fracking market that made Halliburton's last decade is no longer a growth engine. Q3 2026 North America revenue fell 5% sequentially and 9% YoY to $2.16B, mainly on lower pressure pumping in US Land, and moving crews to new customers at better prices cost more downtime than management expected. Management has stopped counting on it: the 2027 outlook assumes a flat North America, and Miller calls any recovery in fracking "purely upside." That makes this a business being run for cash and price discipline, not growth.

The international cycle — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull framing: international revenue of $3.54B in Q3 2026 was up 9% YoY, the Drilling and Evaluation margin jumped to 15.0%, and a pipeline of deepwater work (bp in Brazil, Eni in Cyprus) and Venezuela is arriving while the Gulf recovers. The bear framing: SLB and Baker Hughes, at roughly 2.7x and 2x Halliburton's market value, are chasing the same international contracts, and the region with the most upside is also the one with the most war risk. The way to judge it is not the P/E; it is whether the Q4 margin guidance lands, whether the 2027 cost savings arrive, and whether the Venezuela memoranda turn into signed work.
