01 Company Overview

Huntington Bancshares is a Columbus, Ohio bank holding company, tracing its origins to 1866, that operates The Huntington National Bank: consumer and commercial deposits, lending, payments, mortgage banking, wealth management and capital markets, through branches, ATMs, digital channels and call centres. FY2025 revenue was $7.69B, up 10.7%; the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 2026 is $9.83B, up 31.0% on $7.50B a year earlier (stockanalysis.com). That jump is bought, not built. It comes from two acquisitions: Veritex Holdings, closed October 20, 2025 (adding $9.3B of loans and $10.5B of deposits) and Cadence Bank, closed February 1, 2026 (adding $36.9B of loans and $43.5B of deposits). Total assets went from $204.2B at end-2024 to $287.8B at September 30, 2026. At $31.69B of market capitalisation Huntington ranks #310 in the S&P 500.

The central debate on this page is whether Huntington bought scale or bought risk, and the fall quarter tilted it. The bull version: a Midwest deposit franchise now has branches in Texas and the Southeast, and the combined bank earns a 14.6% return on tangible common equity (16.6% adjusted) while trading at about 1.7 times tangible book value (price divided by the book value of the bank minus goodwill: $15.73 against $9.47). The bear version: tangible book value per share is lower than at end-2025 ($9.89 then; $9.55 in March, $9.65 in June and $9.47 in September), common equity tier 1 (CET1), the core capital cushion, is 10.1%, and on September 16 management lowered its outlook, citing lower-than-expected loan volumes and sustained high short-term rates raising deposit costs (Barclays conference slides). Every number in Section 04 is chosen to let you decide which reading the evidence supports.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
$223.8B average deposits Midwest franchise since 1866 Texas + Southeast entry 25.9% fee income mix 1.75% ACL coverage 0.31% net charge-offs Switching costs in treasury

A bank's moat is its deposits, and Huntington's average $223.8B were built over generations in Ohio, Michigan and the industrial Midwest, where branch density and long relationships have historically let a bank pay depositors less. The Veritex and Cadence deals were designed to put that funding to work in Texas and the Southeast, where loan demand and population growth are higher. Treasury-management and commercial-card relationships are sticky: moving a mid-market company's payables and receivables is a multi-quarter project, not a rate decision. What is not a moat is anything on the asset side. A commercial loan is a commodity priced in a market with 323 listed regional banks plus private lenders, and the September guidance cut says as much: management's slides named lower-than-expected loan volumes and higher deposit costs as the reasons.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The margin depends on rates and competition Huntington does not control. On Sep 16 management cut its 2026 net interest income growth outlook to about 35% from 39–43% and its 2027 EPS range to $1.75–$1.83 from $1.90–$1.93 (Huntington's Barclays conference slides, 8-K of the same date; the earlier EPS range as reported by Zacks Equity Research). The same day the Federal Reserve raised its policy rate by a quarter point, its first increase since 2023. At a 3.25% NIM on average earning assets of $255.3B, a 25-basis-point compression is roughly $640M of annual revenue (our calculation), more than a fifth of a quarter's revenue.
02
Capital is the thinnest part of the story. CET1 is 10.1% (10.0% in Q2) and tangible common equity to tangible assets is 6.9%, down from 7.1%. Tangible book value per share fell again, to $9.47 from $9.65. Huntington repurchased $100M of stock in Q3 and $409M so far this year (release). CET1 of about 10% leaves less room to absorb credit losses without slowing the buyback or, eventually, the dividend.
03
Credit is drifting the wrong way from a low base. Net charge-offs rose to 0.31% of loans from 0.25% in Q2 (0.22% a year ago); CFO Zachary Wasserman attributed the rise mainly to one commercial credit (Q3 call transcript, Motley Fool). Nonperforming assets reached $1,722M, 0.90% of loans, up from 0.85% in Q2 and 0.60% a year earlier. The ACL is 1.75%, down from 1.78%. The commercial loans bought in the two deals have had few quarters to season.
04
Loans are not growing. Average loans fell 0.4% in Q3 as commercial real estate and consumer loans declined, partly offset by commercial and industrial loans. A bank earns its margin on loans, so a book that shrinks while deposit costs stay high is the setup behind the lower outlook.
05
Integration is mostly behind it, but the proof comes in Q4. The Cadence systems conversion completed in mid-June 2026. On the Q3 call Steinour said "Cost synergies have been achieved as planned," and the CFO said core expenses should be flat in Q4 versus Q3. Acquisition costs still ran $117M pre-tax in Q3.

Notable one-off items. Huntington recorded $117M of pre-tax acquisition-related expenses in Q3 2026 (Q2: $152M) and an $8M pre-tax adjustment on acquired equity securities. That is the whole reason reported EPS of $0.33 and adjusted EPS of $0.38 differ, and why reported ROTCE of 14.6% becomes 16.6% adjusted and noninterest expense of $1,772M becomes $1,655M adjusted. Treat the adjusted figures with care: acquisition costs are one-time for a given deal, but a bank that closed two deals in fifteen weeks has made "one-time" a recurring line. A second distortion runs the other way: TTM net income rose 10.2% while TTM EPS fell 15.4%, because the share count rose to fund the deals. Per-share earnings are what a shareholder owns, and they went backwards.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a year.

Five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 monthly close of $14.84 to the October 26, 2026 close of $15.73, HBAN gained +6.0% in price over five years, roughly 1.2% a year (monthly closes, price only, dividends not included). Dividends make up most of the return: twenty quarterly dividends of $0.155 over the window add $3.10 a share, which takes the five-year return to about +26.9% (price plus dividends, not reinvested; our calculation from Yahoo Finance dividend history). The price path was anything but calm: the stock fell 35.0% from $14.84 to the October 2023 monthly close of $9.65, then rose 86.6% to the November 2024 close of $18.01, the highest month-end close of the window. No month has closed above that level in the 23 months since. Beta of 0.97 understates how violent the middle of that path was.

Relative performance. HBAN is up 0.6% over 52 weeks, from the Oct 27, 2025 close of $15.64 to $15.73 (Yahoo Finance daily closes, our calculation). HBAN is not a NASDAQ-100 constituent: that index excludes financial companies, which rules out banks. The S&P 500 is therefore the benchmark, and a sector index such as the KBW Regional Banking Index would be the more informative second comparison.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The arithmetic of the last two years, stated plainly. Huntington's total assets went from $204.2B (end-2024) to $225.1B (end-2025) to $287.8B (September 2026). Deposits went from $162.4B to $176.6B to $224.7B, net loans from $127.8B to $147.1B to $188.2B, and shareholders' equity from $19.7B to $24.3B to $32.2B (Huntington's quarterly financial supplements for Q4 2025 and Q3 2026). Average loans and deposits in Q3 were $188.4B and $223.8B. Two lines did not grow: TTM EPS fell to $1.21 from $1.43 a year earlier, and tangible book value per share fell from $9.89 at end-2025 to $9.47 in September. Goodwill rose from $6.0B to $9.5B. That is the shape of a balance sheet bought rather than built: dilution now in exchange for earnings later.

What the operating numbers say. Set the acquisitions aside and Q3 2026 is a decent quarter for a decent bank. ROA of 1.01% clears the traditional one-percent mark. ROTCE of 14.6% (16.6% adjusted) is above any reasonable estimate of the cost of equity, which is the test of whether a bank creates value. The 3.25% margin rose 4 basis points. Fee income is 25.9% of revenue, with payments ($211M), capital markets ($122M) and wealth ($131M) each material. At 13.0 times trailing earnings and about 1.7 times tangible book, it is not an expensive stock. But the direction of travel was down in the quarter on most lines: revenue, fee income, ROTCE, tangible book value and credit quality all moved the wrong way from Q2.

Where the argument sits. Two things are true at once. The efficiency ratio of 61.2% is mediocre, and the adjusted 59% only gets there by excluding $117M of integration cost. And CET1 of 10.1% with 6.9% tangible equity to tangible assets means the capital cushion was spent on the deals. Against that, management says the cost savings are on plan and has put a date on the payoff: 2027 EPS of $1.75–$1.83. The forward P/E of 9.0× 2027 consensus earnings against a trailing 13.0× is the market's price for that promise, and the September cut shows the promise can move. Management has already lowered it once, on Sep 16, citing higher deposit costs and lower loan volumes than it had planned.

The credit question is starting to get an answer. Huntington took on $46.2B of loans underwritten by two other institutions within fifteen weeks. Purchase accounting marks such loans to fair value at closing, which resets the loss history. Net charge-offs of 0.31% (one commercial credit, per the CFO) and nonperforming assets of 0.90% of loans, up from 0.60% a year ago, are the first signs of what the combined book looks like. They are still low in absolute terms. Whether the acquired commercial and commercial-real-estate loans behave like Huntington's own will take until 2027 to judge.

Bull Case

A deposit franchise built in the Midwest has been put to work in Texas and the Southeast through two deals that added $46.2B of loans and $54.0B of deposits. The combined bank earns a 14.6% return on tangible common equity (16.6% adjusted) and 1.01% on assets with net charge-offs at 0.31%, trades at 13.0× trailing and 9.0× 2027 consensus earnings and pays a 3.94% dividend. Management says cost savings are on plan, reaffirmed 2027 EPS of $1.75–$1.83 on the Oct 22 call, and expects the margin to hold at about 3.25% in Q4. The average analyst target of $18.81 implies 19.6% upside with no sell ratings on the panel. If the savings land, an investor is buying a bank earning above its cost of equity at a single-digit forward multiple.

Bear Case

Everything good in the numbers was purchased. TTM EPS is down 15.4% while net income is up 10.2%, so shareholders paid for the growth in dilution. Tangible book value per share is below its end-2025 level ($9.89 then, $9.47 in September), CET1 is a thin 10.1% and the 61.2% efficiency ratio is well off best-in-class. Management lowered its outlook on Sep 16 only about five weeks before the Q3 print, loans are shrinking, charge-offs and nonperforming assets are rising, and Wolfe Research downgraded the stock to Hold four days after results. The stock has not closed a month above its November 2024 high, and $46.2B of loans written by someone else will not season until 2027, with any reserve build coming out of a capital base that has little spare room.

Top 3 Risks

01
The outlook is cut again. The 2027 EPS range has already come down once, to $1.75–$1.83. It assumes $150 million of extra cost savings and a 50–60% deposit beta (the share of rate rises Huntington has to pass on to depositors). Higher deposit costs or tighter loan pricing than that would pull the number down again.
02
Acquired credit seasons badly. $46.2B of loans from two other lenders, marked to fair value at closing. Net charge-offs rose to 0.31% from 0.25% in Q2, and nonperforming assets are at $1,722M. A faster rise would land on a 10.1% CET1 ratio with little cushion.
03
Rates and competition squeeze the margin. At a 3.25% NIM on roughly $255B of earning assets, a 25-basis-point compression is about $640M of annual revenue, more than a fifth of a quarter's total. Deposit competition returning, or a flatter yield curve, would erase the acquisition benefit faster than cost savings can replace it, and Huntington controls neither.
Industry Commentary
Regional Bank Consolidation Has Actually Arrived
EMERGING TAILWIND
Huntington's two closings, Veritex on October 20, 2025 and Cadence on February 1, 2026, fifteen weeks apart, show large regional deals getting done. The sector remains fragmented: 323 listed US regional banks share $1.92T of market capitalisation and $570.05B of revenue (stockanalysis.com, October 2026). Scale matters more than it used to, because technology spend, compliance cost and deposit-gathering all have fixed-cost characteristics. The stock's reaction to the first closing (the Oct 2025 drop above) shows the market asks for proof of the payoff.
Deposits Are No Longer Free, and Everyone Knows It
ONGOING HEADWIND
The March 2023 crisis changed depositor behaviour: money-market funds, high-yield digital accounts and instant transfers turned a sticky liability into a shoppable one. HBAN's own chart records the repricing, a 26.9% fall in a single month in March 2023. Huntington's cost of deposits was 1.90% in Q3 2026, up 2 basis points from Q2, and the September outlook cut named higher deposit costs and lower loan volumes. Fifth Third's shares closed 4.1% lower the day Huntington cut its outlook. Management's plan assumes about 50–60% of rate moves pass through to deposit costs (Q3 call transcript, Motley Fool).
Midwest Funding, Sunbelt Lending — The Whole Trade
STRUCTURAL REPOSITIONING
The strategic logic of both acquisitions is geographic: gather deposits where competition for them is low and population is flat, lend where loan demand is high and population is growing. Texas and the Southeast supply the lending growth; Ohio, Michigan and the industrial Midwest supply the cheaper funding. Bull framing: this is a durable advantage a competitor can only copy through its own acquisitions, and it explains how a bank with a 61.2% efficiency ratio still earns 14.6% on tangible common equity. Bear framing: the trade only works if the Midwest deposits stay put and the acquired Sunbelt loans behave, and the Q3 numbers show total loans shrinking by 0.4% while commercial real estate runs down by design. Neither half has been tested through a downturn.
