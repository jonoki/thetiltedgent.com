01 Company Overview

HDFC Bank is India's largest private-sector bank and, since absorbing its former parent, the mortgage lender HDFC Ltd, on July 1, 2023, one of the largest balance sheets in the country: ₹45,778bn (≈$482B at ₹94.95 per dollar) of total assets and ₹33,274bn of deposits at September 30, 2026, served through 9,706 branches. By market value it is #3 in India (behind Reliance Industries and Bharti Airtel), at $114.0B (our calculation: 15.42B shares ÷ 3 per ADR × the $22.18 close). The home market is the NSE, where the stock is a heavyweight of the Nifty 50; the NYSE line trades as ADS, each representing three ₹1 equity shares. The bank reports in Indian rupees under Indian GAAP, fiscal year ending March; the most recent reported period is Q2 FY2027 (July–September 2026), announced on Saturday, October 17, 2026.

The post-merger story used to be a funding squeeze: HDFC Ltd brought a mortgage book funded by bonds and borrowings, not deposits, and borrowings were 21% of total liabilities in September 2023. They are 10.7% at September 30, 2026 (11% in June), and deposits are now outrunning loans: period-end deposits +18.8% year on year against gross advances +16.3%. The gross-advances-to-deposits ratio computed from the bank's own figures is 96.8% (₹32,195bn ÷ ₹33,274bn), against 96.5% in June. What has not recovered is the cost of that funding: CASA is 31.6% of deposits (32% in June, 38% in 2023) and the NIM on total assets fell to 3.16% from 3.26% in June, so standalone profit grew only 4.7% to ₹195.1bn. The stock also carries a governance shock — the chairman's resignation in March 2026 and a US securities class action filed in August 2026 — and, since the print, a change at the top: Sashidhar Jagdishan's tenure as CEO ended on October 26 and Anup Bagchi, until then CEO of ICICI Prudential Life Insurance, took over on October 27. The ADR went from $36.54 at end-2025 to a $22.18 close on October 30, 2026, the lowest month-end close in the five-year window.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

9,706-branch deposit franchise Clean book: GNPA 1.11% CET1 17.1% Mortgage book + cross-sell base Insurance / AMC / NBFC subsidiaries Payments & cards scale Nifty 50 index weight

HDFC Bank's moat was never cleverness; it was discipline plus distribution. The branch network grew from 9,455 (March 2025) to 9,706 (September 2026), and on the Q2 call (October 17) Jagdishan said the distribution, "which has doubled over the last 5 years, will start to mature as we move to the next 5 years" — maturing branches are what bring in low-cost deposits. The merger traded some of that edge for scale: it doubled the mortgage book and brought a bond-funded liability side the bank is still digesting. Asset quality is the part of the moat that has held — GNPA 1.11% at September 30, and 0.87% excluding agriculture — and capital is abundant at 18.9% total CRAR. The vulnerability is on the liability side, in governance since March 2026 and, since October 2026, in leadership: a new CEO and a deputy MD who is leaving.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Governance and US litigation. Chairman Atanu Chakraborty resigned in March 2026, citing "happenings and practices within the bank" not "in congruence with my personal values and ethics" (resignation letter disclosed in the bank's March 18 exchange filing, per ETV Bharat); the ADR fell 7.28% to $26.62 on March 18, 2026. On June 26 the bank disclosed an external review by Wilson Sonsini Goodrich & Rosati and Wadia Ghandy & Co, which "did not identify any basis for the Statement" (Business Today, June 26). A US securities class action was reported filed in the Southern District of New York (filing date August 13, 2026 per law-firm notices from Kaplan Fox and Levi & Korsinsky) for a class period of July 17, 2023 to May 26, 2026; The Hans India reported the suit and the bank's response that it is "without merit". Allegations centre on roughly ₹45 crore (≈$4.7M) of payments to the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation that allegedly topped up interest on large deposits and were booked as sponsorship. The rupee sum is small; the question it raises about deposit-gathering practices is not. Deadline to ask to lead the suit: October 13, 2026 (passed). The CEO handover on October 27 and the deputy MD's planned exit on December 31 add leadership change to the governance questions.
02
New CEO, departing deputy. Anup Bagchi became MD & CEO on October 27, 2026 after Sashidhar Jagdishan's tenure ended on October 26; Deputy MD Kaizad Bharucha leaves on December 31, 2026, subject to RBI approval of his request. The bank says Bagchi comes from ICICI Prudential Life Insurance, where he has been CEO since June 2023. A new leader means new priorities on a balance sheet still being repaired.
03
Funding cost. The deposit gap has been closed with time deposits and wholesale money, not CASA. NIM on total assets fell to 3.16% in Q2 from 3.26% in Q1 (3.32% on interest-earning assets). Goldman Sachs called the fall larger than it expected and J.P. Morgan tied it to the FCNR deposit impact (as summarised by Business Today, October 19). On the Q2 call the CFO said the cost of funds stayed flat at about 4.4% instead of falling because of "the mix of the FCNR deposits that came in", and that he sees the margin as "at the bottom".
04
Profit lagging the balance sheet. Wholesale lending (+16.4% at September 30), the lowest-spread part of the book, is growing alongside small and mid-market loans (+23.5%) and retail (+11.7%). Profit is lagging: Q2 standalone profit grew 4.7% (about 12.6% excluding treasury trading gains) against loans +16.3% and deposits +18.8%.
05
Accounting regime change. Indian banks move to an expected-credit-loss (ECL) provisioning method, which books likely loan losses earlier, from April 1, 2027 under the RBI's final directions of April 2026. The Q2 call did not put a figure on the one-time transition charge.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 close of $35.96 (bonus-adjusted; $71.91 unadjusted) to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $22.18, the ADR lost 38.3% of its dollar price (price only, dividends not included). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The merger's funding hole has been refilled: deposits +18.8%, gross advances +16.3%, loan-to-deposit ~97%, borrowings down from 21% to 10.7% of liabilities, and management says the margin is at its low point. Asset quality is clean (GNPA 1.11%), capital is deep (CET1 17.1%), and the bank still earns about a 1.8% ROA. You pay ~13.5x trailing earnings and ~1.8x book for India's #3 company by market value. A new chairman is in place, a new CEO arrived on October 27 with a record running ICICI Prudential Life, the lawsuit concerns a ₹45 crore matter, and all four ADR analysts' targets on StockAnalysis's panel sit above the price.

Bear Case

The funding gap closed at a price: CASA fell from 38% to 31.6%, time deposits are 68% of the total, and NIM is 3.16% — so net interest income grew 9.7% while loans grew 16.3% and profit 4.7%. Growth is coming from corporate and mid-market loans at thin spreads, and the ROE is 13.5% against 16–17% pre-merger. On top sits a governance question: a chairman who resigned citing ethics, a US suit over how large deposits were won, and now a new CEO and a departing deputy MD. For a dollar investor the rupee's 21% five-year fall adds a second drag the NSE holder never sees.

Top 3 Risks

01
Governance and litigation. The March 2026 chairman resignation and the August 2026 SDNY class action (class period July 2023 – May 2026) mark the 2026 fall in the share price. The CEO handover on October 27 and the deputy MD's exit on December 31 add leadership change. Further disclosures, a regulatory finding, or a settlement are all possible.
02
Margin stuck below the pre-merger norm. If CASA does not recover toward the 38–40% of the merger period and before it, NIM stays near 3.2% on total assets (3.16% in Q2) and earnings grow slower than the balance sheet — already happening: Q2 standalone profit +4.7% (about +12.6% excluding treasury trading gains) against loans +16.3%.
03
Currency. Revenue and dividends are in rupees; the ADR is in dollars. ₹74.87 → ₹94.95 per USD over five years cost a US holder about 21% before any change in the business. ECL provisioning from April 2027 is a fourth, smaller risk.
Industry Commentary
The deposit war — households moving money out of savings accounts
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Every large Indian bank is fighting for the same deposits. On the Q2 call (October 17) then-CEO Jagdishan said "the incremental accretion to low-cost current and savings accounts in the system is becoming smaller and smaller due to the fact that there is a structural shift that is happening in the household savings behavior." For HDFC Bank, which needs deposits to replace maturing borrowings, this is the binding constraint on margin. The policy response — the RBI's FCNR(B) foreign-currency deposit swap facility, open for deposits taken between June 8 and August 31, 2026 — helped: HDFC Bank reported about US$11.5bn raised through it (business update, October 4, 2026).

Governance scrutiny of Indian banks listed in the US
ONGOING HEADWIND

Because HDFC Bank has NYSE-listed ADRs, it is exposed to US securities law in a way its purely domestic peers are not. The August 2026 complaint alleges that about ₹45 crore paid to the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation effectively added 2.51 percentage points of interest (6.01% total) on large deposits and was recorded as sponsorship of a road-safety campaign. These are allegations, not findings. Leadership is turning over at the same time: Anup Bagchi became MD and CEO on October 27, 2026, and Deputy MD Kaizad Bharucha leaves on December 31, 2026. The dollar amount is immaterial to a bank earning about ₹195bn a quarter; the materiality question is whether it reveals a wider practice in how bulk deposits are priced, at exactly the moment the bank depends on deposit growth.

Credit demand and the India growth proxy — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

On the Q2 call (October 17) then-CEO Jagdishan said "we have a good demand across segments, whether it's corporate, whether it's MSME or retail"; gross advances grew 16.3% in the year to September 30. The bull framing: HDFC Bank is the most liquid dollar route into Indian credit growth, the merger digestion is largely done, and a price of 13.5 times earnings for a bank with clean loans and a 1.8% ROA already allows for the governance shock and more. The bear framing: being tied to India's growth cuts both ways — growth is currently being bought with expensive deposits and thin-spread corporate loans, the dollar translation has eaten a fifth of the value over five years, and until the litigation resolves the ADR carries a risk the NSE line does not. The way to settle it is not the P/E; it is two more quarters of CASA and NIM.
