01 Company Overview

HDFC Bank is India's largest private-sector bank and, since absorbing its former parent, the mortgage lender HDFC Ltd, on July 1, 2023, one of the largest balance sheets in the country: ₹43,975bn (≈$459B) of total assets and ₹31,708bn of deposits at June 30, 2026, served through 9,694 branches. By market value it is #2 in India (behind Reliance Industries) and #185 globally at $121.5B, per companiesmarketcap. The home market is the NSE, where the stock is a heavyweight of the Nifty 50; the NYSE line trades as ADS, each representing three ₹1 equity shares. The bank reports in Indian rupees under Indian GAAP, fiscal year ending March; the most recent reported period is Q1 FY2027 (April–June 2026).

The premise supplied for this report — HDB as an India growth proxy whose story is the post-merger loan-to-deposit squeeze — is partly out of date. The squeeze was real: HDFC Ltd brought a mortgage book funded by bonds and borrowings, not deposits, and borrowings were 21% of total liabilities in September 2023. They are 11% today, and deposit growth (+14.7% YoY, period-end) is now running level with loan growth (+15.4%). The gross-advances-to-deposits ratio computed from the bank's own balance sheet is 96.5% at June 2026 (96.0% a year earlier) — the funding gap has largely been refilled. What has not recovered is the cost of that refill: CASA fell from 38% to 32% and the NIM on total assets sits at 3.26%. The stock, meanwhile, has been hit by something the premise does not mention: a governance shock — the chairman's resignation in March 2026 on ethical grounds and a US securities class action filed in August 2026 — that took the ADR from $36.54 at end-2025 to a $22.70 month-end low in August 2026.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

9,694-branch deposit franchise Clean book: GNPA 1.17% CET1 17.4% Mortgage book + cross-sell base Insurance / AMC / NBFC subsidiaries Payments & cards scale Nifty 50 index weight

HDFC Bank's moat was never cleverness; it was discipline plus distribution. The branch network grew from 9,455 (March 2025) to 9,694 (June 2026), and management says branch productivity is rising as the investments of "the last five, six years" mature — branch productivity is what turns into low-cost deposits. The merger traded some of that edge for scale: it doubled the mortgage book and brought a bond-funded liability side the bank is still digesting. Asset quality is the part of the moat that has held — GNPA 1.17%, and 0.91% excluding agriculture — and capital is abundant at 19.6% total CRAR. The vulnerability is on the liability side and, since March 2026, in governance.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Governance and US litigation. Chairman Atanu Chakraborty resigned in March 2026, citing "happenings and practices within the bank" not "in congruence with my personal values and ethics" (resignation letter disclosed in the bank's March 18 exchange filing, per ETV Bharat); the ADR fell 7.28% to $26.62 on March 18, 2026. A US securities class action was reported filed in the Southern District of New York (filing date August 13, 2026 per law-firm notices from Kaplan Fox and Levi & Korsinsky) for a class period of July 17, 2023 to May 26, 2026; The Hans India reported the suit and the bank's response that it is "without merit". Allegations centre on roughly ₹45 crore (≈$4.7M) of payments to the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation that allegedly topped up interest on large deposits and were booked as sponsorship. The rupee sum is small; the question it raises about deposit-gathering practices is not. Lead-plaintiff deadline: October 13, 2026.
02
Funding cost. The deposit gap has been closed with time deposits and wholesale money, not CASA. NIM on total assets is 3.26%; an analyst on the call described margins as "almost down to 3.4 odd percent" on interest-earning assets. Non-retail deposit rates "have continued to remain elevated" (CEO).
03
Mix-driven margin dilution. Corporate lending (+18.6%) at spreads the CEO calls "very thin" is growing faster than retail (+7.2%). Loan growth bought this way lifts the balance sheet faster than earnings: management concedes adjusted profit growth of 9.8% is "still lower than the overall balance sheet growth".
04
Accounting regime change. Indian banks move to an expected-credit-loss (ECL) provisioning method from April 1, 2027. The CFO says the bank's existing provisions look "adequate and sufficient" under ECL, but a one-time transition charge was not quantified on the call.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The merger's funding hole has been refilled: deposits +14.7%, gross advances +15.4%, loan-to-deposit ~96%, borrowings down from 21% to 11% of liabilities with ₹40,000–50,000 crore more maturing into cheaper deposits. Asset quality is clean (GNPA 1.17%), capital is deep (CET1 17.4%), and the bank still earns a 1.85% ROA. You pay ~15x trailing earnings and ~1.8x book for India's #2 company by market value. A new chairman is in place, the lawsuit concerns a ₹45 crore matter, and all four ADR analysts' targets sit above the price.

Bear Case

The funding gap closed at a price: CASA fell from 38% to 32%, wholesale deposits rose to 20%, and NIM is 3.26% — so net interest income grew 6.7% while loans grew 15%. Growth is coming from corporate loans at "very thin" spreads, retail is growing 7%, and the ROE is 13.8% against 16–17% pre-merger. On top sits a governance question: a chairman who resigned citing ethics and a US suit over how large deposits were won. For a dollar investor the rupee's 22% five-year fall adds a second drag the NSE holder never sees.

Top 3 Risks

01
Governance and litigation. The March 2026 chairman resignation and the August 2026 SDNY class action (class period July 2023 – May 2026) are the specific cause of the 2026 de-rating. Further disclosures, a regulatory finding, or a settlement are all possible; none is quantifiable from sources fetched.
02
Margin stuck below the pre-merger norm. If CASA does not recover toward the 38–40% management targets and corporate lending keeps outgrowing retail, NIM stays near 3.3% on total assets and earnings grow slower than the balance sheet — which management concedes is already happening (9.8% adjusted profit growth vs ~15% loan growth).
03
Currency. Revenue and dividends are in rupees; the ADR is in dollars. ₹74.34 → ₹95.82 per USD over five years cost a US holder about 22% before any change in the business. ECL provisioning from April 2027 is a fourth, smaller risk.
Industry Commentary
The deposit war — households moving money out of savings accounts
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Every large Indian bank is fighting for the same deposits. An analyst on the Q1 FY27 call pointed out that the RBI's own Financial Stability Report says the correlation between rates and CASA "is breaking down", and the CFO agreed that household savings patterns have shifted "over the last three years more so". System liquidity has been volatile: the CFO cited an average surplus of ₹2.08 trillion last quarter but a range from ₹5.5 trillion to a deficit of ₹0.43 trillion. For HDFC Bank, which needs deposits to replace maturing borrowings, this is the binding constraint on margin. The policy response — an FCNR foreign-currency deposit window and swap facilities — is a help, and the bank says it will be "a significant portion of the system" in mobilising it.

Governance scrutiny of Indian banks listed in the US
ONGOING HEADWIND

Because HDFC Bank has NYSE-listed ADRs, it is exposed to US securities law in a way its purely domestic peers are not. The August 2026 complaint alleges that about ₹45 crore paid to the Maharashtra State Road Development Corporation effectively added 2.51 percentage points of interest (6.01% total) on large deposits and was recorded as sponsorship of a road-safety campaign. These are allegations, not findings. The dollar amount is immaterial to a bank earning ₹190bn a quarter; the materiality question is whether it reveals a wider practice in how bulk deposits are priced, at exactly the moment the bank depends on deposit growth.

Credit demand and the India growth proxy — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The CEO says "there is a very healthy credit demand that we are seeing in the system", pointing to mid-market lending under ECLGS 5.0 and an advances book growing 15%. The bull framing: HDFC Bank is the most liquid dollar route into Indian credit growth, the merger digestion is largely done, and a 15x multiple on a clean-book, 1.85%-ROA franchise prices in the governance shock and more. The bear framing: being a "growth proxy" cuts both ways — growth is currently being bought with expensive deposits and thin-spread corporate loans, the dollar translation has eaten a fifth of the value over five years, and until the litigation resolves the ADR carries a risk the NSE line does not. The way to settle it is not the P/E; it is two more quarters of CASA and NIM.
