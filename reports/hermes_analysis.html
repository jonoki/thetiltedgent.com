01 Company Overview

Start with two numbers that point in opposite directions. In the first nine months of 2026 Hermès grew revenue 5.5% at constant exchange rates to €12,260M, and in the first half it kept a recurring operating margin of 41.0%. Over the same stretch its share price kept falling. The house behind the Birkin and Kelly bags closed at €1,268.50 on October 27, 2026, down 53.7% from its February 2025 month-end peak of €2,737 and 40.2% since the start of 2026. The business has slowed. It has not broken. What changed is the price the market will pay for it.

The premise to check is that Hermès is a throttled supply story: it makes its leather goods by hand in French workshops, adds capacity slowly, and so sells everything it makes at prices it sets. The numbers still support that. Leather Goods and Saddlery grew 10.2% at constant currency in the first nine months of 2026, speeding up to 10.8% in the third quarter, and in Q1 sales in Hermès's own stores rose 7% even as tourist flows slowed. What weakened is the edge of the business: Asia excluding Japan, now 42% of sales, grew only 1.6% over nine months and not at all in the third quarter; the Middle East fell; and currencies cut about €310M from reported revenue, leaving reported growth at 2.9%. At 29.5x trailing earnings the stock is still priced as a rare compounder. The question for the next year is whether 6% constant-currency growth deserves that.

The latest print. Before the Paris open on October 22, 2026, Hermès reported third-quarter revenue of €4,096M, up 4.3% at constant exchange rates and 5.5% reported. Analysts polled by Visible Alpha had expected 5.2% growth (Reuters), and FactSet's consensus was €4.13B (Agefi-Dow Jones). Leather goods grew 10.8%, but Asia excluding Japan was flat, ready-to-wear and accessories fell 2.0% and the Middle East fell 4.5%. Reuters called it Hermès's weakest quarterly growth since early 2020. The company again gave no numbers for the outlook: it "confirms an ambitious goal for revenue growth at constant exchange rates" over the medium term. Axel Dumas said: "Despite a complex environment, Hermès continues its profitable growth and maintains its course." The shares fell 4.94% that day, from €1,356.50 to €1,289.50, and another 3.96% the next.

The last full results. On July 29, 2026 Hermès reported second-quarter sales up 6.7% at constant currency to €4.1B, which Reuters described as in line with expectations, and a 41% operating margin that was better than expected. The misses were in the lines investors watch most: Asia-Pacific excluding Japan grew 2.5% against the 3.3% expected in a Visible Alpha consensus, and leather goods 10% against 10.8% (Reuters, July 29, 2026). On the call Axel Dumas said: "I see a situation that has stabilized in China, I do not see an improvement." The shares fell 11.0% that day, their worst day in more than 15 years according to Reuters. Hermès gives no numerical guidance: it "confirms an ambitious goal for revenue growth at constant exchange rates" over the medium term (H1 release). Finance chief Eric du Halgouët told the call that operating investments "will reach EUR 1 billion across the year as a total", and said of currency hedging: "A negative impact, which will be quite strong across the year."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Hand-made, capacity-limited supply 25 French leather workshops Own-store distribution Pricing power Family control (SCA) €12.9B net cash

The moat is controlled supply. Leather goods are made by hand in French workshops that are announced years before they open, so output cannot jump when demand does. Hermès sells through its own stores and, at wholesale, through concession stores in places such as airports (Q1 release). That is how leather goods grew 10.2% at constant currency in the first nine months of 2026 in a soft luxury market, with a group margin above 40%. The family structure protects the model: the family-owned active partner controls the company, and the family holding company H51 was created in 2011 to counter LVMH, which had built a stake of about 17% in 2010 (Wikipedia). The weakness is the same feature seen from the other side. Hermès cannot add volume to make up for a slower region or a weaker currency, so when Asia slows and the euro strengthens, reported growth slows with it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The multiple is still high. At 29.5x trailing earnings Hermès is still priced as a rare compounder. The share price has halved from its peak while diluted EPS went from €43.87 (FY2024) to €43.00 (TTM), so almost all of the fall has come from the multiple. If growth settles at mid-single digits, there is no rule that says the multiple stops near 30. RBC made the same point when it downgraded the stock to Sector Perform from Outperform: the growth premium that justified Hermès's valuation premium over the sector is converging (Investing.com, August 18, 2026).
02
Asia excluding Japan is 42% of sales and stopped growing in Q3. The region grew 1.6% at constant currency over the first nine months of 2026 and 0.0% in the third quarter. The Q3 release said Greater China "was stable over the period in a more difficult context", and Reuters reported the finance chief calling the Chinese market "very difficult". Analysts cutting targets in September 2026 cited China: Citi pointed to continued weak demand in China, and Bernstein to a "more subdued" Chinese demand backdrop (MarketScreener, September 21 and 30, 2026; the Bernstein phrase is from MarketScreener's headline).
03
Middle East and tourism. The Other area, mainly the Middle East, fell 6% in Q1, 4.2% for the half and 4.5% in Q3. The Q1 release said the region was "significantly impacted by recent geopolitical developments in the region from March onwards", and France fell 3% in Q1 as tourist flows slowed.
04
Currency and the French tax charge. About €310M of revenue was lost to currencies over the first nine months of 2026. Profits also carry France's exceptional contribution on the profits of large companies: without it, H1 net profit would have been €2.5B instead of €2.24B.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the headline numbers. First, France's exceptional contribution on the profits of large companies: Hermès reports profit both with and without it. FY2025 group net profit was €4.52B as reported and €4.86B before the contribution (+5.5%). In H1 2026 it was €2.24B as reported and €2.5B before it. On our arithmetic the charge was roughly €270M in the half (an estimate: 30.7% × €8,163M less €2,238M). Second, currency: about €310M of revenue lost in the first nine months of 2026 (more than €360M at the half) and €515M in FY2025. Third, dividends: FY2024 included a €10.00 exceptional dividend, so the drop to €18.00 for FY2025 is not a cut in the ordinary payout, which rose from €16.00. Buybacks are small. Hermès bought 94,846 shares for €160M in H1 2026, dedicated to employee share plans, and the Q3 release gives the same figure for the first nine months, and its issued share count has been 105,569,412 since at least January 2025.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business is intact. Revenue grew 6.1% at constant currency in H1 2026, leather goods close to 10%, own-store sales held up through the Middle East shock, and the recurring operating margin stayed at 41.0%. Adjusted free cash flow rose 18% and the house sits on €12.9B of net cash with essentially no debt. The stock has halved while earnings stayed roughly flat, and ten of the twelve broker targets disclosed since mid-September sit above the price. Leather goods, the core, sped up to 10.8% at constant currency in Q3, and management says it sells all it makes. New workshops through 2030 lift the supply ceiling. If Asia stabilises and currencies stop subtracting, reported growth catches up with underlying growth.

Bear Case

Growth has slowed from 8.9% at constant currency in FY2025 to 4.3% in Q3 2026, its weakest quarter since early 2020 according to Reuters, and brokers are cutting further: Bernstein's 2027 organic growth forecast went from 10.5% to 7.6%. Asia excluding Japan, 42% of sales, did not grow in Q3, and ready-to-wear and accessories shrank. UBS and Goldman Sachs rate the stock Sell, and Berenberg cut it to Hold after the quarter. Price increases are expected to be smaller in 2027. Even after the fall, 29.5x trailing earnings is a premium multiple for mid-single-digit growth, and the stock's results-day reactions (−8.2% in April, −11.0% in July, −4.9% in October) show the market punishing each sign of slowing. Family control means there is no takeover floor under the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further de-rating on slower growth. The fall so far has come from the multiple, not the earnings. Q3 growth came in at 4.3% at constant currency, below the 5.2% analysts expected (Visible Alpha poll, per Reuters). If the full-year results on February 12, 2027 show the slowdown continuing, the market may keep resetting the multiple toward what it pays other luxury houses.
02
Asia and China demand. The largest region did not grow in Q3, and the September target cuts at Citi and Bernstein both cited China. A further slowdown there outweighs strength in the Americas and Japan.
03
Currency and geopolitics. About €310M of revenue was lost to currencies over the first nine months of 2026, and the Middle East shock cut both regional sales and the tourist flow into Paris. Neither is within Hermès's control.
Industry Commentary
A sector-wide de-rating
HEADWIND

Over the year to October 27, 2026, Kering fell 45.3%, Hermès 42.5% and LVMH 36.5%, while the CAC 40 slipped 4.3%. In September alone, on month-end closes, Hermès fell 15.5%, LVMH 14.0% and Kering 12.1%. Over five years Hermès still beat LVMH and Kering by a wide margin. Over the last year only Kering fell further, even though Hermès's own growth rate is still positive at constant currency.

Jewellery is the bright spot
WHERE GROWTH IS

Richemont, built around jewellery, rose 1.7% over the year and 45.1% over five years in Swiss francs. HSBC kept a Buy on it on September 30 while cutting Hermès, forecasting 17% Q3 organic growth for Richemont's jewellery division (MarketScreener). Hermès's own jewellery and home line grew 11.2% at constant currency in FY2025 and 3.3% in the first nine months of 2026 (−1.1% in Q3), but at 13% of sales it is too small to change the group's growth rate.

Pricing after the boom
OPEN QUESTION

The question for 2027 is how much more price clients will accept. Hermès's finance chief told the H1 call that next year's price increase "will be slightly lower than the one that we applied this year", and Bernstein expects leather-goods price increases to moderate in FY2027. For Hermès, whose volumes are capped by its workshops, a smaller price increase means slower revenue growth in leather goods, its largest sector.
