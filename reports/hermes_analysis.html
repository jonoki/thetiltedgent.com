01 Company Overview

Start with two numbers that point in opposite directions. In the first half of 2026 Hermès grew revenue 6.1% at constant exchange rates to €8,163M and kept a recurring operating margin of 41.0%. Over the same stretch its share price kept falling. The house behind the Birkin and Kelly bags closed at €1,299.50 on October 2, 2026, down 52.5% from its February 2025 month-end peak of €2,737 and 38.8% since the start of 2026. The business has slowed. It has not broken. What changed is the price the market will pay for it.

The premise to check is that Hermès is a throttled supply story: it makes its leather goods by hand in French workshops, adds capacity slowly, and so sells everything it makes at prices it sets. The numbers still support that. Leather Goods and Saddlery grew 9.8% at constant currency in H1 2026, and in Q1 sales in Hermès's own stores rose 7% even as tourist flows slowed. What weakened is the edge of the business: Asia excluding Japan, now 43% of sales, grew only 2%; the Middle East fell; and currencies cut more than €360M from reported revenue, leaving reported growth at just 1.6%. At 30.2x trailing earnings the stock is still priced as a rare compounder. The question for the next year is whether 6% constant-currency growth deserves that.

The latest print. On July 29, 2026 Hermès reported second-quarter sales up 6.7% at constant currency to €4.1B, which Reuters described as in line with expectations, and a 41% operating margin that was better than expected. The misses were in the lines investors watch most: Asia-Pacific excluding Japan grew 2.5% against the 3.3% expected in a Visible Alpha consensus, and leather goods 10% against 10.8% (Reuters, July 29, 2026). On the call Axel Dumas said: "I see a situation that has stabilized in China, I do not see an improvement." The shares fell 11.0% that day, their worst day in more than 15 years according to Reuters. Hermès gives no numerical guidance: it "confirms an ambitious goal for revenue growth at constant exchange rates" over the medium term (H1 release). Finance chief Eric du Halgouët told the call that operating investments "will reach EUR 1 billion across the year as a total", and said of currency hedging: "A negative impact, which will be quite strong across the year."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Hand-made, capacity-limited supply 25 French leather workshops Own-store distribution Pricing power Family control (SCA) €12.9B net cash

The moat is controlled supply. Leather goods are made by hand in French workshops that are announced years before they open, so output cannot jump when demand does. Hermès sells through its own stores and, at wholesale, through concession stores in places such as airports (Q1 release). That is how leather goods grew 9.8% at constant currency in H1 2026 in a soft luxury market, with a group margin above 40%. The family structure protects the model: the family-owned active partner controls the company, and the family holding company H51 was created in 2011 to counter LVMH, which had built a stake of about 17% in 2010 (Wikipedia). The weakness is the same feature seen from the other side. Hermès cannot add volume to make up for a slower region or a weaker currency, so when Asia slows and the euro strengthens, reported growth slows with it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The multiple is still high. At 30.2x trailing earnings Hermès is still priced as a rare compounder. The share price has halved from its peak while diluted EPS went from €43.87 (FY2024) to €43.00 (TTM), so almost all of the fall has come from the multiple. If growth settles at mid-single digits, there is no rule that says the multiple stops at 30. RBC made the same point when it downgraded the stock to Sector Perform from Outperform: the growth premium that justified Hermès's valuation premium over the sector is converging (Investing.com, August 18, 2026).
02
Asia excluding Japan is 43% of sales and growing 2%. In H1 2026 the region grew 2.4% at constant currency and fell 1.2% reported, and Axel Dumas told the H1 call that he saw China stabilised but not improving. Analysts cutting targets in September 2026 cited China: Citi pointed to continued weak demand in China, and Bernstein to a "more subdued" Chinese demand backdrop (MarketScreener, September 21 and 30, 2026; the Bernstein phrase is from MarketScreener's headline).
03
Middle East and tourism. The Other area, mainly the Middle East, fell 6% in Q1 and 4.2% for the half. The Q1 release said the region was "significantly impacted by recent geopolitical developments in the region from March onwards", and France fell 3% in Q1 as tourist flows slowed.
04
Currency and the French tax charge. More than €360M of revenue was lost to currencies in H1 2026. Profits also carry France's exceptional contribution on the profits of large companies: without it, H1 net profit would have been €2.5B instead of €2.24B.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the headline numbers. First, France's exceptional contribution on the profits of large companies: Hermès reports profit both with and without it. FY2025 group net profit was €4.52B as reported and €4.86B before the contribution (+5.5%). In H1 2026 it was €2.24B as reported and €2.5B before it. On our arithmetic the charge was roughly €270M in the half (an estimate: 30.7% × €8,163M less €2,238M). Second, currency: more than €360M of revenue lost in H1 2026 and €515M in FY2025. Third, dividends: FY2024 included a €10.00 exceptional dividend, so the drop to €18.00 for FY2025 is not a cut in the ordinary payout, which rose from €16.00. Buybacks are small. Hermès bought 94,846 shares for €160M in H1 2026, dedicated to employee share plans, and its issued share count has been 105,569,412 since at least January 2025.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

From the September 2021 month-end close of €1,196.50 to the October 2, 2026 close of €1,299.50, Hermès gained 8.6% on price. Adding back the €90.00 of dividends paid over the period (not reinvested) makes it about +16.1%. Over the same window the CAC 40 rose 21.1% on price. The endpoints hide a full round trip. The stock rose more than two and a half times from its June 2022 low of €1,067 to a €2,737 month-end peak in February 2025, then gave back 86% of that gain. The October 2 close is the lowest point on the chart since September 2022.

The 6-period monthly RSI peaked at 82.7 in March 2024 and now reads 13.4, the lowest in the window. Every reading from March 2026 to the October 2 close, eight in a row, has been below 25: deeply oversold by this measure for seven months. The 10-month moving average is about €1,627.70, and the price is 20.2% below it. Oversold readings describe how fast the stock has fallen, not where it stops.

Relative performance

Against its home index Hermès has lagged: +8.6% over five years against +21.1% for the CAC 40, and −39.4% over one year against −2.0% (price only, Yahoo Finance closes, our calculation). Against luxury peers the picture splits by product. Over five years Hermès beat LVMH (−39.0%) and Kering (−65.9%) but trailed Richemont (+77.4% in Swiss francs), the jewellery-led group. Over the last year it fell the most of the four: LVMH −30.3%, Kering −26.7%, Richemont +12.5%. The pattern says the market is no longer paying Hermès a premium for being the safest name in a weak sector. Its 1.46 beta against the CAC 40 also shows the stock has moved more than its index in both directions.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business is intact. Revenue grew 6.1% at constant currency in H1 2026, leather goods close to 10%, own-store sales held up through the Middle East shock, and the recurring operating margin stayed at 41.0%. Adjusted free cash flow rose 18% and the house sits on €12.9B of net cash with essentially no debt. The stock has halved while earnings stayed roughly flat, and every broker target disclosed in late September sits above the price (€1,470–€1,750). JPMorgan and DZ Bank both judged the H1 figures better than expected. New workshops through 2030 lift the supply ceiling. If Asia stabilises and currencies stop subtracting, reported growth catches up with underlying growth.

Bear Case

Growth has slowed from 8.9% at constant currency in FY2025 to about 6% in 2026, and brokers are cutting further: Bernstein's 2027 organic growth forecast went from 10.5% to 7.6%. Asia excluding Japan, 43% of sales, is growing about 2%, and Dumas told the H1 call he sees China stabilised, not improving. RBC downgraded the stock in August on a narrowing growth premium over peers. Price increases are expected to be smaller in 2027. Even after the fall, 30x trailing earnings is a premium multiple for mid-single-digit growth, and the stock's results-day reactions (−8.2% in April, −11.0% in July) show the market punishing each sign of slowing. Family control means there is no takeover floor under the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Further de-rating on slower growth. The fall so far has come from the multiple, not the earnings. If Q3 (October 22) confirms growth near 5%, as HSBC and Barclays expect, the market may keep resetting the multiple toward what it pays other luxury houses.
02
Asia and China demand. The largest region is barely growing, and the September target cuts at Citi and Bernstein both cited China. A further slowdown there outweighs strength in the Americas and Japan.
03
Currency and geopolitics. More than €360M of revenue was lost to currencies in H1 2026, and the Middle East shock cut both regional sales and the tourist flow into Paris. Neither is within Hermès's control.
Industry Commentary
A sector-wide de-rating
HEADWIND

Over the year to October 2, 2026, Hermès fell 39.4%, LVMH 30.3% and Kering 26.7%, while the CAC 40 slipped 2.0%. In September alone, on month-end closes, Hermès fell 15.5%, LVMH 14.0% and Kering 12.1%. Over five years Hermès still beat LVMH and Kering by a wide margin. Over the last year it fell the most of the three, even though its own growth rate is still positive at constant currency.

Jewellery is the bright spot
WHERE GROWTH IS

Richemont, built around jewellery, rose 12.5% over the year and 77.4% over five years in Swiss francs. HSBC kept a Buy on it on September 30 while cutting Hermès, forecasting 17% Q3 organic growth for Richemont's jewellery division (MarketScreener). Hermès's own jewellery and home line grew 11.2% at constant currency in FY2025 and 5.4% in H1 2026, but at 13% of sales it is too small to change the group's growth rate.

Pricing after the boom
OPEN QUESTION

The question for 2027 is how much more price clients will accept. Hermès's finance chief told the H1 call that next year's price increase "will be slightly lower than the one that we applied this year", and Bernstein expects leather-goods price increases to moderate in FY2027. For Hermès, whose volumes are capped by its workshops, a smaller price increase means slower revenue growth in leather goods, its largest sector.
