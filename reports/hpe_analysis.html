01 Company Overview

Hewlett Packard Enterprise sells the plumbing of corporate computing: servers, storage, enterprise networking gear and the financing to pay for it. Fiscal 2026 (year to 31 October 2026) revenue was $46.7B, up 36.3%, and GAAP net earnings were $4.4B, or $3.07 a share, a year after GAAP earnings per share of −$0.04. The fourth quarter alone brought in $14.5B, up 50%. HPE's market value is $53.72B at the November 25 close (1.316B shares, our calculation), and the stock is up 91.1% over the past year against 6.2% for the S&P 500 (SPY), on price alone, yet 44.5% below its October 9 high of $73.56.

Two things happened at once, and the central task of this page is to keep them apart. The $14B Juniper Networks acquisition closed in July 2025 after HPE settled a Department of Justice antitrust suit, roughly doubling the networking business and bringing in a high-margin product line. At the same time, HPE became a large seller of AI systems, a business that adds revenue at a lower gross margin than the company average. In the fourth quarter gross margin reached 38%, which CFO Marie Myers put down to favourable pricing in Cloud & AI. On the call she said that will not last: she expects gross margin to moderate in the second half of fiscal 2027 as AI server volumes rise and component costs climb faster than prices. Growth in AI orders is shown on this page separately from the margin story on purpose, because more of it makes the margin line look worse, not better.

AI systems are not a reportable segment (they live inside the Server line of Cloud & AI), but they drive the whole debate, so they are broken out here. Q4 FY2026, per the CFO on the call: AI systems orders of $4.5B, up more than 130%, against revenue of $2.5B, up by triple digits, so orders ran 1.8 times sales (the book-to-bill). For the year AI systems revenue was $5.8B, up 28%, and the AI systems backlog was up nearly 90%. Company-wide orders rose 88% in the quarter. Two deals she named, the AMD Helios rack-scale systems and HPE's Oracle deals, are "much more backend loaded", meaning their revenue arrives later in fiscal 2027 rather than early. The margin point: HPE expects gross margin "to moderate in the second half as AI server volumes increase, including the ramp of Helios," and said that reflects "both the higher mix of AI servers and costs increasing faster than pricing." So read any quarter here on two tracks: orders and backlog for demand, Networking mix and pricing for margin. The physical limit is parts: CEO Antonio Neri said HPE expects "supply of memory, NAND, and many other critical components to remain constrained" until at least the second half of calendar 2028.

The one number to watch: AI systems revenue ÷ total revenue. At $2.5B of $14.5B in Q4 it is roughly 17%; for the full year it was $5.8B of $46.7B, about 12%. If the backlog converts on the schedule the CFO described, that share rises through fiscal 2027 and the reported gross margin falls even if nothing goes wrong operationally.

HPE Financial Services leases and finances IT equipment for customers. It is a lending book, and lending books are funded with debt by design. This matters more than its 6% revenue share suggests, because it muddies every debt ratio computed from the consolidated balance sheet. At 31 October 2026 HPE reported $18.65B of borrowings ($3.65B short-term, $15.0B long-term) against $5.75B of cash, a consolidated debt-to-equity of 0.68 (our calculation from the release). Reading that as debt carried by the server and networking business is wrong: a meaningful share of those borrowings funds a book of customer leases and is repaid from those leases. Management's preferred figure is net leverage of 1.4× at year-end, after it used cash to retire $1.25B of notes. Corporate Investments & Other, largely HPE Labs and early-stage software, lost $29M on $234M of revenue (a −12.4% operating margin): a rounding error on profit. Earlier in the year HPE completed the sale of its H3C stake on 28 May 2026 for $1.357B, removing a long-standing China exposure and funding debt reduction.

Read this instead of D/E: net leverage 1.4× (company-stated), $12.9B of borrowings net of cash (our calculation), and $4.50B of fiscal 2026 free cash flow.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
#2 US Enterprise WLAN Juniper Mist AI Ops Installed Base + Captive Financing $3.5B+ ARR, Up 12% Sovereign & Government Access Full-Stack Compute + Network Q4 Orders +88%

The core advantage is not technological, it is positional. HPE is one of very few vendors that can quote a customer a GPU cluster, the switching and routing network around it, the storage behind it, the operating software above it and the lease that pays for it, on one contract, with one support relationship. Dell can match the compute and the financing but not the networking depth; Cisco and Arista can match the networking but not the server scale; Supermicro can match the AI rack and little else. The Juniper acquisition is what turned this from a slide into a fact: routing revenue of $893M and data-centre networking of $397M in a single quarter are lines HPE did not have two years ago. The AMD Helios systems are the clearest example, which Neri described on the Q4 call as HPE's rack-scale compute and liquid cooling combined with Juniper's Ethernet switching. Layered on top, GreenLake (HPE's pay-as-you-go platform, where customers pay for capacity used rather than buying equipment outright) sits inside HPE's recurring revenue, which ended fiscal 2026 at more than $3.5B of ARR for the whole company, up 12%, from software, services and subscriptions (the CFO on the Q4 call). Answering a question on GreenLake, Neri said "we exit 2026 with a $3.5 billion ARR, which obviously is growing very rapidly."

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The DOJ settlement armed a competitor. To close Juniper, HPE agreed, in the settlement the DOJ announced on 28 June 2025, to divest its global Instant On campus and branch WLAN business within 180 days to a DOJ-approved buyer, and to run an auction licensing Juniper's AI Ops for Mist source code on a perpetual, non-exclusive basis, with optional transitional support and personnel transfers. HPE paid $14B for Juniper and then handed a rival a permanent licence to the software layer that made Mist valuable. Whoever bought that licence competes with HPE using HPE's own code, forever.
02
AI revenue dilutes the margin. The Q4 non-GAAP operating margin was 16.9% and Cloud & AI earned 18.4%, helped by pricing. For fiscal 2027 HPE guides Cloud & AI to about 14%, and the CFO expects gross margin to moderate in the second half as AI servers ramp and costs rise faster than prices. The stock fell 16.3% in the next session, a drop TheStreet tied to that warning.
03
Component supply is the limit. Neri expects memory, NAND and other critical components to stay scarce. Favourable pricing lifted the Q4 margin; the CFO now expects costs to rise faster than prices. A growing backlog looks bullish on a slide and is a cash and cancellation risk in practice: an order is not a sale, and an AI order that waits a year in a market where GPU prices reset is a negotiation waiting to happen.
04
Big deals, late in the year. The CFO said the Helios and Oracle deals are much more backend loaded. A fiscal 2027 that depends on a few large systems landing in its second half leaves less room for a delayed shipment. When a handful of customers decide when to spend, the revenue line moves with them.

Notable One-Off Items. Three distortions sit inside these numbers. In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a stock can stay overbought for a long time while the fundamentals catch up.

Five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 month-end close of $14.35 to the November 25, 2026 close of $40.82, HPE returned +184.5% on price alone, a compound annual rate of about 23.3% before dividends. Over the same dates SPY, the S&P 500 fund, rose 57.3% and QQQ, the Nasdaq-100 fund, 61.0%, both also on price. But the shape is everything. For the first three and a half years of that window the stock did essentially nothing: it closed at $14.35 in November 2021 and $15.43 in March 2025, a total move of +7.5% over forty months, with a trough at $11.98 in September 2022. Every dollar of the return was made after March 2025. The stock went from $23.81 at end-March 2026 to a $63.89 month-end close in September 2026, +168% in six months, touched an intraday high of $73.56 on 9 October, and has since given back 36.1% from the September close. Anyone who owned HPE as a cheap, dull, dividend-paying hardware company for four years was right about the dullness, then saw it nearly triple in six months and lose more than a third of that peak in two.

Relative performance. Over the year to November 25, 2026, HPE's +91.1% price return beat SPY (+6.2%) and QQQ (+4.1%) by a wide margin. Against its peers (52-week price change at the same close, StockAnalysis) it sits behind Dell (+133.5%) and ahead of Arista (+34.2%), Cisco (+23.6%), Nvidia (−18.2%) and Supermicro (−30.0%). The day-to-day picture is rougher: at $40.82 the stock is 28.2% below its 50-day average of $56.88 and 5.1% below its 200-day average of $43.00, and its 14-day RSI on daily closes is 36.8, weak but not at the 30 level usually read as oversold. A 1.47 beta and two consecutive sessions of +17.4% and −16.3% around the print describe a stock that moves on every hint about AI margins.

Beat, raise, and a lower price
08 Summary & Key Risks

Two businesses, opposite margin signs, one stock. Start at the top of the Q4 FY2026 income statement. Revenue $14,547M, up 50.3%. Gross profit $5,527M, a 38.0% GAAP gross margin, up from 33.5% a year earlier; on HPE's non-GAAP basis it was 38.2%, which the CFO said was up 180 basis points (1.8 percentage points), driven by favourable pricing in Cloud & AI. Cloud & AI's operating margin doubled to 18.4% from 9.3%, on $11.3B of revenue. That is an extraordinary quarter for a hardware company, and management's own guidance does not expect it to hold. AI systems produced $2.5B of revenue in the quarter, and HPE's fiscal 2027 guidance takes Cloud & AI back to about a 14% operating margin as more AI servers ship and component costs rise faster than prices. Networking, the steadier profit, earned a 22.1% margin on $3.1B, including $893M of routing and $397M of data-centre networking that did not exist inside HPE two years ago.

Which means the growth and the margin now point in opposite directions. AI systems orders were $4.5B in Q4 against $2.5B of AI revenue, a book-to-bill of 1.8×, and the AI systems backlog ended the year up nearly 90%. Company-wide orders rose 88%. Every one of those numbers is a reason to own the stock and a reason the gross margin falls. Networking, the margin engine, grew 12.3% in Q4 and is guided to the high teens to low 20s in fiscal 2027: faster than before, still far slower than AI. Run those trends forward and the mix shifts toward the low-margin line by construction. HPE's own guidance says so: fiscal 2027 revenue up 27–35% but non-GAAP operating profit up only 23–30%. On 24 November the stock fell 16.3% in one session, a drop TheStreet tied to the CFO's margin warning.

What Juniper actually bought, and what it cost. $14B, closed July 2025, and the price included more than cash. To get the DOJ to settle before trial, HPE agreed to divest its entire global Instant On campus and branch WLAN business within 180 days to a DOJ-approved buyer, and to auction a perpetual, non-exclusive licence to Juniper's AI Ops for Mist source code, with optional transitional support and personnel transfers to make the licensee a viable competitor. The DOJ's own framing was that Cisco was #1, HPE #2 and Juniper #3 in US enterprise WLAN, and that the combination would leave Cisco and HPE holding over 70% of the market. So HPE bought the #3 player, sold a business, and permanently licensed the software that made the target differentiated. The strategic logic still holds — routing of $893M and data-centre networking of $397M in one quarter are real, and so is a record $1.2B of Networks-for-AI orders in Q4 — but the acquisition delivered a smaller competitive moat than the headline price suggests, and a ROIC of 10.60% (StockAnalysis) is the visible cost of that. The quantified return is $800M of annual run-rate cost savings by the end of fiscal 2028, raised in September from at least $600M.

On leverage — read the right number. Consolidated debt-to-equity is 0.68 on $18.65B of borrowings at 31 October 2026. Reading that as a statement about the operating business is a mistake, because HPE Financial Services is an in-house leasing and financing arm: it writes customer leases and funds them with matching borrowings, much as a bank or a carmaker's finance arm does. Consolidating it inflates both debt and assets without telling you anything about the servers-and-switches business. The figure to use is management's own: net leverage of 1.4× at year-end, below its 2× target, after HPE used cash to retire $1.25B of notes. On that basis HPE is moderately, not aggressively, indebted, and the $1.357B of H3C proceeds in May 2026 helped get it there.

GreenLake and its run-rate. The pay-as-you-go platform is the piece of the story that would justify a software company's valuation. On the Q4 call the CFO put total company annualised revenue run-rate (ARR) above $3.5B at the end of fiscal 2026, up 12%, from software, services and subscriptions; $3.5B is about 7.5% of the year's $46.7B of revenue. Asked about GreenLake, Neri said the ARR "obviously is growing very rapidly". ARR is the right measure (a customer count grows when you sign small accounts, whereas ARR tells you what they actually spend), and at 12% it grew about a third as fast as total revenue (+36.3%): AI hardware, not subscriptions, is doing the growing, and the "HPE is becoming a subscription business" argument has yet to show up in the numbers.

Valuation — and the honest version of the cheapness. HPE trades at 13.3× trailing GAAP earnings and 7.9× the midpoint of its fiscal 2027 non-GAAP EPS guidance, with a PEG of 0.23. Every one of those looks cheap and every one of them needs a caveat. The trailing P/E uses a year whose strong Q4 margin management expects to moderate; the forward P/E uses non-GAAP EPS of $5.05–5.35, against GAAP guidance of $3.68–3.98, and assumes the margin squeeze stays inside the guidance; and the PEG is flattered by growth measured from a loss year. Against the peer median trailing P/E of 18.8× (StockAnalysis, November 25), HPE is cheaper than Cisco (25.1×) and Arista (44.0×) and a little dearer than Dell (12.5×) and well above Supermicro (4.9×): the market pays for networking margins and marks down AI-server assemblers, and puts HPE near the server end. $4.50B of fiscal 2026 free cash flow against a $66.61B enterprise value (market value plus borrowings minus cash; our calculation) is about 14.8× EV/FCF; against the fiscal 2027 guidance of at least $5.5B it is about 12.1×. That is the hardest version of the valuation to flatter, and it is cheap for a company growing this fast. At $40.82 against a $66.30 average target and a $73.56 52-week high, the price says investors doubt the margin more than the analysts do.

Bull Case

HPE is one of very few vendors that can sell the compute, the network, the storage and the financing for an AI deployment on one contract, and the numbers say customers are buying it that way: $4.5B of AI systems orders against $2.5B of revenue in Q4, an AI systems backlog up nearly 90%, company-wide orders up 88%, and a record $1.2B of Networks-for-AI orders. Fiscal 2027 guidance calls for 27–35% revenue growth, $5.05–5.35 of non-GAAP EPS and at least $5.5B of free cash flow, at least 75% of it returned to shareholders; the dividend just rose 19% and the buyback authority is about $7.3B. Juniper savings are now targeted at $800M. At 7.9× guided non-GAAP earnings, about 12× guided free cash flow and net leverage of 1.4×, the price assumes the margin squeeze is far worse than management's own guidance.

Bear Case

The CFO said Cloud & AI's Q4 operating margin was "supported by strong favorable pricing dynamics and disciplined cost management", and management already expects the margin to fade: gross margin moderates in the second half of fiscal 2027, Cloud & AI's operating margin falls from 18.4% to about 14%, and costs are rising faster than prices. Profit grows slower than revenue by HPE's own guidance (non-GAAP operating profit +23–30% on revenue +27–35%). Networking, the profit engine, grew 12.3% at a 22.1% margin, short of the mid-to-high 20s it targets. Big AI deals are backend-loaded, components stay scarce, and the $14B Juniper price included a forced divestiture and a perpetual source-code licence handed to a competitor. The stock is 44.5% below its October high, has a beta of 1.47, and six of the ten analysts listed above who updated after the print cut their targets.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margins squeezed by the mix. Growth in AI systems mechanically lowers gross margin. HPE already guides Cloud & AI to about 14% in fiscal 2027 from 18.4% in Q4; if AI revenue grows faster than planned while Networking stays near its 12% Q4 pace, the blended margin falls further even with flawless execution, and the forward P/E that makes the stock look cheap is built on the guidance holding.
02
Backlog that does not convert on today's terms. Scarce memory and other components delay revenue; costs are rising faster than prices; and AI hardware prices reset quickly. A large, backend-loaded backlog built on a few big deals is a negotiating position for the buyer, not a guaranteed revenue schedule for the seller.
03
The Juniper give-back compounding. The Instant On divestiture removes a product line and the perpetual AI Ops for Mist licence puts Juniper's differentiating software into a competitor's hands permanently. If that licensee builds a credible WLAN alternative while Cisco defends from above and Arista attacks the data centre, the 22.1% Networking operating margin — the single most valuable number in this company — is the thing that erodes instead of rising to the mid-to-high 20s HPE targets.
  Industry Commentary
Memory Is the New Bottleneck
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
For two years the binding constraint on AI infrastructure was GPU supply. It has moved. On the November call Neri said HPE expects memory, NAND and many other critical components to remain constrained until at least the second half of calendar 2028. Read alongside HPE's AI systems backlog, up nearly 90% at the end of fiscal 2026 while the year's AI systems revenue rose 28%, it points to orders running ahead of the parts. Pricing helped in Q4 (the CFO credited favourable pricing for its 38% gross margin) but is turning: she expects costs to rise faster than prices in fiscal 2027, squeezing a line that already carries below-average gross margins. Every server maker — HPE, Dell, Supermicro, Lenovo — needs the same scarce components, so none of them can stand out on supply, and they compete on price at the customer.
Antitrust Now Prices Networking Deals
ONGOING HEADWIND
The HPE-Juniper settlement is now the template for enterprise networking consolidation, and its terms were unusually invasive. The DOJ did not merely require a divestiture — it required HPE to auction a perpetual, non-exclusive licence to the acquired company's AI Ops for Mist source code, with personnel transfers to make the licensee competitive. That is a remedy designed to manufacture a rival out of the acquirer's own intellectual property. Any future buyer of a networking asset now has to underwrite the possibility of paying full price and then arming a competitor with the crown jewels. It raises the effective cost of consolidation across the sector, and it means the remaining independent networking vendors are worth less to a strategic buyer than a pure market-share calculation would suggest.
Networks-for-AI: Where the Margin Actually Is
EMERGING TAILWIND
The industry has spent three years discovering that the GPU is the cheapest-to-differentiate part of an AI cluster and the network is the hardest. Arista trades at 44× trailing earnings on $11.9B of revenue (StockAnalysis, November 25) because the market has worked this out. HPE's version is a record $1.2B of Networks-for-AI orders in Q4, bookings for the year that Neri put "in the mid-$2 billion or so", $1.7B of backlog at year-end and a networking growth target of high teens to low 20s for fiscal 2027. Bull framing: if HPE can attach high-margin Juniper networking to every low-margin AI rack it ships (the Helios systems are built that way), the blended margin problem shrinks and the company earns a networking company's valuation rather than a server maker's. Bear framing: the attach is not yet visible in the blend: Networking revenue grew 12.3% in Q4 while AI systems orders grew more than 130%. Until the networking grows as fast as the compute, HPE is a server company with a good networking division, and the market prices those at Dell's 12.5× earnings, not Arista's 44×.
