01 Company Overview

HP Inc. is the half of the old Hewlett-Packard garage that kept the name. In 2015 Hewlett-Packard Company split in two: the servers, storage and services business left as Hewlett Packard Enterprise, and the PCs-and-printers stub renamed itself HP Inc. and stayed in the S&P 500 under the historic HPQ ticker. Today it sells laptops, desktops and workstations (Personal Systems) and printers plus the ink and toner that feed them (Printing). FY2026 (year to October 31, 2026) revenue was $60.5B, up 9.5%, with diluted EPS of $2.62 under standard accounting rules (GAAP), per HP's results release.

2026 has been a strange year for this stock, and the strangeness is the story. Revenue is accelerating — up 9.5% for the year and 9.3% to $16.0B in the fourth quarter (to October 31, 2026) — yet Personal Systems units fell 29% in that quarter. HP is selling far fewer PCs for a lot more money each: the memory and storage chips inside them have become much more expensive, HP has passed the cost on to customers, and buyers are moving to pricier AI PCs. Even so, the PC business kept only 3.0 cents of operating profit per dollar of sales. Management now expects the PC market to shrink by a high-single to low-double-digit percentage in units in calendar 2027, a steeper fall than the mid-single-digit decline it assumed in September. The leadership question is settled: on November 4 HP named Emma Walmsley, GSK's chief executive from 2017 to 2025, as CEO from January 4, 2027. Interim CEO Bruce Broussard, in the job since Enrique Lores left on February 3, 2026, then becomes Chair. The stock closed February at $18.99 — its lowest month-end of the five-year window — and has since risen 69% to $32.15, including an 11.49% jump the day after the Q4 beat. The question at $32.15: is HP's earnings power genuinely improving, or is it enjoying a price-driven revenue bump while the number of machines it sells shrinks underneath?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Printer installed base → supplies annuity Global brand & channel reach Commercial PC fleet relationships Procurement scale in memory & chips Z workstations franchise Cash generation (FY2026 FCF $3.3B)

HP's moat is narrower than its brand. In PCs it competes on scale and procurement — being big enough to secure memory supply when it is scarce. That is a real advantage in a shortage but not a pricing moat: the product is largely interchangeable with Lenovo's and Dell's. The genuine moat is in printing, where millions of installed printers generate a recurring stream of proprietary supplies. The vulnerability is that this installed base is shrinking slowly, and the moat protects the stream only as long as people keep printing.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Component cost is setting the margin, not HP. Q4 gross margin fell to 17.5% from 20.2% (calculated), and the PC margin to 3.0% from 4.6% in Q3 despite price increases. The CFO expects memory and storage costs to keep rising in fiscal 2027, more slowly; if they rise faster, the promised margin recovery slips.
02
Units are falling while prices rise. PC units −29% in Q4. In September HP assumed industry PC units would fall roughly mid-single digits in calendar 2027 (8-K, September 21, 2026); on the Q4 call the CFO projected a fall of high single to low double digits. Price-driven revenue growth reverses if component costs ease and PC prices follow them down.
03
Leadership handover. Emma Walmsley starts on January 4, 2027, after the fiscal 2027 guide was set; her operating career was at GSK and L'Oréal, per the 8-K. UBS flagged after the print that the new chief does not start until early 2027 (Proactive Investors).
04
Thin balance-sheet cushion. Book equity is barely positive at $527M after years of buybacks, the current ratio is 0.81, and net debt is $4.88B (StockAnalysis). Fine while free cash flow runs at $3B a year (fiscal 2027 guide: $2.9–$3.1B); less comfortable if a margin squeeze meets a unit decline.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 12.3x trailing GAAP earnings and about 10x fiscal 2027 consensus for a business that produced $3.3B of free cash flow in fiscal 2026 — an 11.4% FCF yield — while paying a 3.73% dividend (just raised to $0.31 a quarter) and retiring nearly 3% of its shares a year. Q4 beat on revenue and EPS, commercial PC revenue grew 23%, and AI PCs reached nearly 60% of shipments. Management guides fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS of $3.15–$3.45, expects the PC margin back in its long-term range before the fiscal year ends, and says HP is on track for about $1B of yearly cost savings (gross) by the end of fiscal 2028 (CFO, Q4 call). A permanent CEO who ran GSK for nearly nine years arrives in January.

Bear Case

The revenue growth is price, not demand: PC units fell 29% in the quarter Personal Systems revenue rose 14%. Gross margin fell anyway, to 17.5% from 20.2%, and the PC margin to 3.0%. HP now expects the 2027 PC market to shrink by high single to low double digits, worse than it assumed in September; the $0.26 of tariff refunds in fiscal 2026 will not repeat, and free cash flow is guided below fiscal 2026's $3.3B. The average target is 14% below the price, six analysts rate it Sell, and 9.26% of the float was short at the November 13 settlement. Book equity is barely positive, and the new CEO's career has been in pharmaceuticals and consumer goods (GSK, L'Oréal), not PCs. A 69% rally off the low on a business with falling units and falling margins asks a lot of a recovery that has not happened yet.

01
The memory squeeze outlasts fiscal 2027. Q4 gross margin 17.5% vs 20.2%, the PC margin 3.0%. The CFO expects memory and storage costs to keep rising, more slowly; if they climb faster, the PC margin's recovery to its long-term range slips and the earnings under a 12.3x multiple shrink.

Industry Commentary
The memory squeeze on PC makers
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

On the Q4 call CFO Karen Parkhill said HP expects "memory and storage costs to continue to increase, but at a lesser rate than what we saw last fiscal year." For a PC maker, memory is a cost it cannot design around: every machine needs it. HP's response has been to secure supply and raise prices, accepting fewer units — the right move in a shortage, and the reason revenue (+14% in Personal Systems) and units (−29%) moved in opposite directions in Q4. Intel's PC warning on October 30, which hit Dell, Hewlett Packard Enterprise and Super Micro as well as HP (24/7 Wall St.), shows the squeeze is industry-wide.

2027: a smaller PC market
ONGOING HEADWIND

HP's September 21, 2026 8-K put a number on the cycle: "consistent with current third-party industry forecasts," industry PC units were assumed to fall roughly mid-single digits in calendar 2027, an assumption the company called "fluid" and "subject to change." Two months later it changed: on the Q4 call the CFO said HP is "projecting the PC unit TAM to decline high single to low double digits in calendar year 2027" (TAM, total addressable market: the whole industry's sales). She expects the market's revenue to stay "roughly flat," as higher prices and pricier models make up for fewer units. The AI-PC upgrade has so far shown up as higher prices; the rest of it has to come from a smaller market.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $32.15 HPQ trades at 12.3x trailing GAAP EPS and an 11.4% free-cash-flow yield on fiscal 2026 (calculated). The bull framing: that is a cash machine priced for decline, where a normal memory cycle and a cost programme could lift earnings above a consensus that already expects a dip. The bear framing: the Street's own numbers put fair value below the price — a $27.55 average target, $29 median — and the multiple is low because the earnings are cyclical and were flattered in fiscal 2026 by pricing and tariff refunds. The way to judge it is not the P/E; it is whether the PC margin climbs back from 3.0% during fiscal 2027, and what the new CEO does in her first months.
