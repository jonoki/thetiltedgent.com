01 Company Overview

Hormel is the company behind a pantry's worth of American brand names — SPAM, Skippy, Planters, Applegate, Jennie-O, Hormel Natural Choice, Hormel Black Label, Columbus and Wholly, among more than thirty others. It was founded in 1891 in Austin, Minnesota, it is still headquartered there, and it has paid a quarterly dividend without interruption since going public in 1928. FY2025 (year ended October 2025) net sales were $12.1B, but GAAP diluted EPS was only $0.87 against $1.37 adjusted, after $234M of non-cash impairment charges in the fourth quarter.

The story of the last five years is a slow leak rather than a blow-out. The stock closed September 2021 at $41.00 and closed September 21, 2026 at $20.46 — half its price, and the lowest point in the 61-point monthly series charted below. Revenue went nowhere over that span ($11.39B in FY2021, $12.15B trailing), gross margin slipped from 17.0% to 15.7%, and the company spent the period digesting a large acquisition: Planters, bought from Kraft Heinz in 2021 for $3.35B (per Hormel's own newsroom). Now it is in the middle of a full reset — an interim CEO since 2025, a new CEO taking over on October 26, 2026, a new CFO who started September 8, 2026, and a portfolio being pruned (Justin's, the whole-bird turkey business and Brazil's Ceratti all sold or agreed to be sold in FY2026). The central question for the stock: is Hormel a durable brand house going through a messy clean-up, or a slow-growth meat-and-snacks business whose margins are structurally lower than they used to be? At 14.3x adjusted earnings with a 5.7% dividend yield, the market is currently leaning toward the second answer.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Century-old household brands SPAM · Skippy · Planters Foodservice operator relationships Foundation control (46.8%) 60 straight dividend increases Modest leverage (D/E 0.36)

The moat is shelf position and habit. A shopper reaching for SPAM or Skippy is not running a price comparison, and a restaurant that has built a menu around a pre-cooked Hormel protein does not switch suppliers over a few cents a pound. The Foundation's near-majority stake adds a second layer: management does not have to answer to an activist, and the dividend — the February 2026 payment was the 390th consecutive quarterly one — has a long record to protect. The vulnerability is that brand power only protects the premium part of the portfolio. A large chunk of Hormel's volume has been commodity turkey, private-label nuts and other lines where the brand is absent and the price is set by the market, and that is exactly where Q3's volume went missing.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Earnings quality is noisy. TTM GAAP EPS is $0.62 against roughly $1.43 adjusted (our sum of reported adjusted quarters). Q4 FY2025 carried $234M of impairments; Q3 FY2026 carried a $56M divestiture loss, a $48M impairment and a $38M litigation settlement. Each is explainable. Together, four quarters of "one-off" charges start to look like a cost of the strategy.
02
Volume is shrinking. Q3 volume fell 7.4%. Some of it is deliberate portfolio pruning; some is weak demand for private-label snack nuts and a consumer the company itself describes as "under pressure". Until the pruning ends, it is hard to tell the two apart from outside.
03
Leadership turnover all at once. A new CEO (October 26, 2026) and a new CFO (September 8, 2026) arrive together, after an interim CEO and an interim CFO. New teams often reset guidance and take charges in their first year.
04
Payout ratio on GAAP earnings is above 100%. The $1.17 annualised dividend is 189% of TTM GAAP EPS and about 82% of TTM adjusted EPS (our calculations). The dividend is covered by free cash flow ($780.9M TTM against roughly $644M a year at the current rate, our estimate: 550.35M shares × $1.17), but the cushion is thinner than a 60-year record suggests.

Notable One-Off Items

Almost the entire gap between GAAP and adjusted results is portfolio clean-up. In Q4 FY2025, $234M of non-cash impairments (mainly a minority investment in the International segment and Retail intangible assets) turned the quarter into a $0.10 diluted loss per share. In Q3 FY2026, a $56M loss on the Brazil divestiture, a $48M impairment on the Indonesian minority investment and a $38M litigation settlement took GAAP diluted EPS to $0.11 against $0.37 adjusted, and pushed the effective tax rate to 42.3%. Nine-month FY2026 GAAP EPS was $0.72 (−25.8%) while adjusted EPS was $1.11 (+5.7%). Management's FY2026 guidance keeps the two tracks apart: $1.06–$1.12 GAAP, $1.45–$1.51 adjusted.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 14.3x trailing adjusted earnings and 13.7x this year's consensus for a company with some of the most durable brands in American food, a 5.72% dividend yield backed by 60 consecutive annual increases, a controlling shareholder that will defend the payout, and modest leverage (D/E 0.36). Adjusted margins are rising — 9.0% in Q3 against 8.4% — and free cash flow is up to $780.9M TTM from $534.4M in FY2025. The charges that crushed GAAP earnings are the cost of pruning low-margin lines, and pruning is supposed to shrink sales before it lifts margins. A new CEO who ran Applegate and the Retail business, plus a CFO from Tyson, get a clean slate. Every one of ten analysts' targets sits above the price.

Bear Case

Revenue has gone nowhere since FY2022 ($12.46B then, $12.15B trailing), gross margin has fallen from 17.4% to 15.7%, and Q3 volume fell 7.4%. "Portfolio shaping" is a phrase that has now covered impairments, a divestiture loss and a litigation charge in consecutive reporting periods; GAAP EPS of $0.62 means the dividend is 189% of reported earnings. The company itself describes its consumer as "under pressure", and named weak private-label snack nuts and commodity turkey as drags. Two new executives arriving together is a classic setup for a "kitchen-sink" reset in the first guidance. A stock with a 0.31 beta has still halved: low volatility has not meant low risk.

Top 3 Risks

01
Top-line credibility. The sales guide has been cut once already (to $12.1–$12.2B from $12.2–$12.5B), and the stock fell 10.25% the day it happened. Another miss on sales would test the thesis that the decline is deliberate pruning rather than lost demand.
02
A first-year reset under new management. A CEO starting October 26 and a CFO starting September 8 will set FY2027 guidance together. New teams frequently lower expectations and take further charges; the $1.55 FY2027 consensus EPS is the number at risk.
03
Dividend growth, not dividend safety. A cut looks unlikely given the Foundation's stake and $780.9M of TTM free cash flow, but with adjusted payout near 82% the latest increase was just 1%. For a stock owned largely for its dividend, a streak kept alive by token raises is a slow-burn risk to the valuation.
Industry Commentary
A pressured shopper and the private-label question
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Hormel's own words for the backdrop are "a consumer environment that remains under pressure." Its Q3 numbers show what that looks like inside one company: Retail volume down 9%, weak private-label snack nuts, lower commodity-based pricing in parts of the portfolio. A wire report carried by WSAU framed the sales cut against broader packaged-food headwinds from inflation and tight consumer budgets. Hormel's strategic answer is to lean into branded, premium lines — SPAM, Applegate and Hormel chili all grew in the quarter — and walk away from volume it cannot price. That is the right move for margins and the wrong one for reported sales, at least for a while.

Protein input costs cut both ways
ONGOING SWING FACTOR

Hormel is a meat company underneath the brands, and commodity prices move its results in both directions. In Q3, favourable pork input costs supported Foodservice profit, while lower commodity-based pricing dragged Retail sales. Commodity turkey was named as a drag, which is one reason the whole-bird turkey business was sold. Selling commodity exposure should make earnings steadier over time, at the price of a smaller revenue base.

Foodservice as the growth leg
EMERGING TAILWIND

Twelve straight quarters of organic growth, a 14.4% segment margin and products that save restaurant labour make Foodservice the part of Hormel that looks least like a struggling grocery brand. Ghingo said Foodservice "once again outperformed industry trends, supported by the strength of our solutions-based offerings and operator partnerships." The bull framing: as Retail is pruned, Foodservice becomes a bigger share of profit and the group's margin rises with it. The bear framing: Foodservice growth was only 2% organic in Q3, and restaurant traffic is exposed to the same pressured consumer. It can carry the story; it cannot yet carry the whole company.
