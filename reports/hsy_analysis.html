01 Company Overview

The Hershey Company makes and sells confectionery and snack products in the United States and internationally, through three reported segments: North America Confectionery, North America Salty Snacks and International. The brand stable runs from Hershey's and Reese's to Kisses, Kit Kat and, on the salty side, SkinnyPop, Dot's Pretzels and the recently acquired LesserEvil. FY2025 net sales were $11.69B, up 4.4% on FY2024's $11.20B, and the twelve months to September 27, 2026 brought $12.26B, up 6.7% on the twelve months before. At a $33.3B market capitalisation (our calculation: 200.98M common and Class B shares on the 10-Q cover × the October 30 close) the company ranks #306 in the S&P 500 on October 30 — second among listed confectioners behind Mondelez at $82.2B, and more than ten times the next, Tootsie Roll, at $2.8B.

The central debate here is not demand. It is input cost. Hershey's gross margin went from 47.4% in FY2024 to 33.55% in FY2025 — a 1,387 basis point collapse (13.9 percentage points; a basis point is a hundredth of a point) that took EPS from $10.92 to $4.34 in a single year on rising revenue. That is a cocoa story, and cocoa is now working the other way: Q3 2026 reported gross margin was 46.1%, against 32.6% a year earlier, and 42.7% on the company's adjusted basis. The same quarter showed the cost of the recovery: prices up about 11 points, volume down about 10, and a cut to the 2026 organic sales outlook as management described households strained by gas prices and interest rates. The question this page puts to the reader is how much of the earnings recovery is already in the $166 share price, and whether shoppers come back as price increases fade. A second, quieter question sits underneath it: the Hershey Trust casts roughly 79% of the votes, which removes the takeover bid that often puts a floor under a beaten-up consumer-staples stock.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Century-old brand equity US shelf & checkout dominance Seasonal ritual franchises Pricing power (13pts realised in Q3) Trust-controlled: no forced sale Scale procurement & hedging desk ROE 38.0% / ROIC 20.8%

The core advantage is that Hershey's, Reese's and Kisses are not really products, they are defaults. That shows up arithmetically: in Q3 2026 North America Confectionery pushed through about 13 points of net price realisation and lost about 11 points of volume, expanding segment margin by 1,400bp in the process. A brand without a moat that raises price 13% in a snack category nobody needs does not come out of it with higher profit. Layer on the Halloween/Easter/Christmas franchises — demand that recurs on the calendar rather than on the promotion cycle — and you have a business that can absorb an extraordinary input shock and still be standing. The evidence for the moat is precisely the fact that FY2025 gross margin fell 1,387bp and the company still earned $883M of net income on record revenue.

Key Vulnerabilities

A.
Single-commodity exposure with no substitute. Chocolate requires cocoa, cocoa grows in a narrow equatorial band concentrated in West Africa, and there is no reformulation that keeps the product recognisably Hershey's. FY2025 proved the sensitivity: gross margin 47.42% → 33.55%, EPS $10.92 → $4.34, on revenue that grew 4.4%. Profit swings far more than sales when cocoa moves: the whole earnings line rides on one farm commodity.
B.
Pricing power is a stock, not a flow. Thirteen points of price in a quarter spends brand goodwill; it is not a repeatable income stream. The roughly −10 point consolidated volume decline in Q3 is the receipt. If cocoa deflates and competitors pass it through faster, Hershey either gives price back (margin) or holds it and keeps losing volume (share). In October management cut its 2026 organic sales outlook to about 3%, from 3%–3.5%, and described shoppers strained by gas prices and interest rates.
C.
The diversification leg is underperforming. North America Salty Snacks grew +2.3% organic in Q3 while segment margin fell 590bp to 12.1%, with most reported growth bought via LesserEvil. A hedge that is margin-dilutive and organically flat is not yet functioning as a hedge, and it competes against Frito-Lay's own delivery network to stores, which Hershey cannot copy.
D.
GLP-1 and the "less snacking" secular question. Confectionery is the archetypal impulse category, and the long-run demand question raised by appetite-suppressing drugs hangs over the whole group. No single quarter answers it, and it sits behind every volume number on this page.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has risen quickly and may be stretched ("overbought"); below 30 it has fallen quickly and may be washed out ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The worst input-cost shock in the company's modern history has passed through the P&L, and the stock is 30% below its February close while gross margin expands. FY2025's $4.34 EPS was the trough; TTM EPS is already $8.84, and consensus sees $8.66 of adjusted EPS in 2026 and $9.53 in 2027 (StockAnalysis), putting HSY on 17.4× FY2027 earnings with a 3.51% dividend covered about 1.9× by $2.12B of free cash flow. Management raised its 2026 EPS outlook again in October and still aims for a mid-40s gross margin over the long term — and because Hershey buys cocoa ahead, lower cocoa prices reach the income statement with a lag. A franchise earning 20.8% ROIC on the way back from the bottom, priced below the confectioners' average multiple, is an earnings rebound in a business whose demand barely moves with the economy.

Bear Case

Strip out the hedge gains and Q3's 46.1% gross margin becomes 42.7% — still below FY2024's 47.42%. The growth is price, not units: about 11 points of pricing against about −10 points of volume left organic sales up 0.8%, and management cut its 2026 organic outlook to about 3% while describing households strained by gas prices and interest rates. It also said 2027 sales growth could land in the lower part of its 2%–4% range. The diversification leg is not yet working — Salty Snacks grew 2.3% organically with margin down 590bp — and International's loss widened to $30.0M. Fifteen of 23 analysts sit on Hold, seven of the eight post-print actions on StockAnalysis's table cut targets, and TD Cowen downgraded to Hold. And the one thing that usually rescues a stalled staple, a takeover, is structurally impossible: the Trust casts ~79% of the votes. Owning it is a bet on the cocoa price, with no buyer waiting to pay a premium if that bet goes wrong.

01
Cocoa reverses again. The entire 2026–27 earnings recovery rests on a soft commodity subject to West African weather, disease and political risk. FY2025 showed the downside sensitivity precisely: a 1,387bp gross margin hit took EPS down 60% in one year on rising revenue. Hedging smooths the path, it does not change the destination — and the same forward-buying that delays the pain also delays the relief.
02
Elasticity breaks. Volume was down about 10 points consolidated and about 11 points in North America Confectionery in Q3 2026, and management says convenience-store candy sales weakened in September and October. If the consumer response to shelf prices worsens — or if competitors pass cocoa deflation through to price faster than Hershey does — the company faces a genuine choice between defending margin and defending share, and the Halloween/holiday season is where that shows up first.
03
No takeover floor, and a lasting discount for the control structure. Hershey Trust Company held 54,612,012 Class B shares at December 31, 2025 — each carrying 10 votes to the common's 1 — entitling it to cast approximately 79% of all votes. Common holders elect only one-sixth of the Board as a class. No acquirer can buy this company without the Trust's consent, and Pennsylvania's interest in the Milton Hershey School has historically weighed against a sale. Practically, that means the strategic bid that often arrives for an underperforming $33B staple will not arrive, and minority holders have limited recourse if capital allocation disappoints.
Industry Commentary
Cocoa — the input that rewrote the income statement
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The effect of cocoa costs is extraordinary for a company of this vintage: consolidated gross margin fell from 47.42% in FY2024 to 33.55% in FY2025, operating margin from 26.72% to 12.58%, and diluted EPS from $10.92 to $4.34 — all on net sales that grew 4.4% to $11.69B. The quarterly path makes the shape clearer still: gross margin ran 33.66% (Q1'25), 30.45% (Q2'25), 32.61% (Q3'25), 37.04% (Q4'25), 39.39% (Q1'26), 45.32% (Q2'26) and 46.14% (Q3'26). That is a V, and the right-hand side of it is steeper than the left.

The discipline: reported Q3 2026 gross margin of 46.1% includes $113.4M of hedge gains marked to market. On the adjusted basis it is 42.7% (+1,090bp). Use the second number.
Hedging, forward coverage and the timing of relief
ONGOING HEADWIND

Hershey buys cocoa ahead through hedges, which is why its cost of goods and the spot market are never the same thing at the same time. On the July call chief executive Kirk Tanner said the company had good visibility into cocoa prices falling in 2027, and that margin improvement does not rest on that alone; the then-CFO, Steve Voskuil, said its hedges leave room to benefit if prices fall further. In October his successor, Dave Hulays, restated the long-run aim of a mid-40s gross margin. Two second-order effects follow. First, the hedging lag means 2026's commodity prices are largely a 2027 margin event, so the consensus $9.53 of 2027 EPS is the number carrying the thesis. Second, the gains and losses on those hedges, valued at market prices each quarter, are a visible, volatile line inside reported gross margin: $113.4M in Q3 2026, about 3.5 points of sales. Pricing remains the other lever, and it is easing: about 13 points of net price realisation in North America Confectionery and about 4 points internationally in Q3.

The Trust, the votes, and the missing takeover premium
STRUCTURAL — CUTS BOTH WAYS

Hershey's dual-class structure is not a founder's vanity arrangement; it is a charitable trust's endowment. At December 31, 2025, Hershey Trust Company — wholly owned by the Milton Hershey School Trust — held 54,612,012 Class B shares as trustee and was "entitled to cast approximately 79% of all of the votes" on matters requiring both classes to vote together. Class B carries 10 votes per share against common's 1, converts to common share-for-share at any time, and is paid a smaller dividend: the common's is set 10% higher than the Class B's ($1.452 against $1.320 a quarter). Common holders, voting separately as a class, elect one-sixth of the Board. The Trust keeps selling small blocks of common stock under a pre-arranged trading plan — its Form 4 filed October 28, 2026 shows 30,000 shares sold on October 26–28, leaving 486,119 common shares held directly — while the Class B block stays intact: an endowment spreading its money around, not a holder giving up control.

Bull reading: a permanent, mission-driven anchor shareholder insulates management from activist pressure to over-lever, over-promise or cut the brand investment that makes 13 points of pricing possible. It is a real reason this company could absorb a 1,387bp margin shock without panic restructuring, and the dividend has a protector with an institutional need for it. Bear reading: the chance of a takeover is effectively zero. A $33B staple trading 30% below its February close, with an underperforming snacks arm and a loss-making international segment, would normally attract a bid from a rival or a buyout firm — and any such approach dies on the Trust's 79%. History reinforces this: Pennsylvania's attorney-general has an established interest in the Milton Hershey School's endowment, which has historically made a sale of the company a public-policy question rather than a purely commercial one. Minority holders here own the economics of a great franchise and almost none of the control, and must therefore be paid in cash flow rather than in the hope of a premium.
