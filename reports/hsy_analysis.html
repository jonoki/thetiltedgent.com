01 Company Overview

The Hershey Company makes and sells confectionery and snack products in the United States and internationally, through three reported segments: North America Confectionery, North America Salty Snacks and International. The brand stable runs from Hershey's and Reese's to Kisses, Kit Kat and, on the salty side, SkinnyPop, Dot's Pretzels and the recently acquired LesserEvil. FY2025 net sales were $11.69B, up 4.4% on FY2024's $11.20B, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through Q2 2026 is $12.16B, up 7.7%. At a $35.0B market capitalisation the company sits roughly #720 globally — second in the listed confectioners industry behind Mondelez at $79.6B, and ahead of every other public pure-play by an order of magnitude.

The central debate here is not demand. It is input cost. Hershey's gross margin went from 47.4% in FY2024 to 33.55% in FY2025 — a 1,387 basis point collapse that took EPS from $10.92 to $4.34 in a single year on rising revenue. That is a cocoa story, and cocoa is now working the other way: Q2 2026 reported gross margin was 45.32%, up 1,480bp year over year. The question this page puts to the reader is how much of the recovery is already in the $174 share price, and how much of the remaining upside depends on a commodity nobody at Hershey controls. A second, quieter question sits underneath it: the Hershey Trust casts roughly 79% of the votes, which permanently removes the takeover bid that normally puts a floor under a beaten-up staples name.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Century-old brand equity US shelf & checkout dominance Seasonal ritual franchises Pricing power (14pts realised) Trust-controlled: no forced sale Scale procurement & hedging desk ROE 32.8% / ROIC 17.7%

The core advantage is that Hershey's, Reese's and Kisses are not really products, they are defaults. That shows up arithmetically: in Q2 2026 North America Confectionery pushed through 14 points of net price realisation and lost only 10 points of volume, expanding segment margin by 830bp in the process. A brand without a moat that raises price 14% in a discretionary snack category does not come out of it with higher profit. Layer on the Halloween/Easter/Christmas franchises — demand that recurs on the calendar rather than on the promotion cycle — and you have a business that can absorb an extraordinary input shock and still be standing. The evidence for the moat is precisely the fact that FY2025 gross margin fell 1,387bp and the company still earned $883M of net income on record revenue.

Key Vulnerabilities

A.
Single-commodity exposure with no substitute. Chocolate requires cocoa, cocoa grows in a narrow equatorial band concentrated in West Africa, and there is no reformulation that keeps the product recognisably Hershey's. FY2025 proved the sensitivity: gross margin 47.42% → 33.55%, EPS $10.92 → $4.34, on revenue that grew 4.4%. The entire earnings line is a leveraged bet on one soft commodity.
B.
Pricing power is a stock, not a flow. Fourteen points of price in a quarter is a drawdown on brand equity, not an annuity. The −8 point consolidated volume decline is the receipt. If cocoa deflates and competitors pass it through faster, Hershey either gives price back (margin) or holds it and keeps bleeding volume (share). Management's own framing — volumes improve only "as commodity inflation eases and pricing elasticities normalize" — concedes the trade.
C.
The diversification leg is underperforming. North America Salty Snacks grew +0.6% organic while segment margin fell 500bp to 16.1%, with essentially all reported growth bought via LesserEvil. A hedge that is margin-dilutive and organically flat is not yet functioning as a hedge, and it competes against a route-to-market (Frito-Lay) that Hershey cannot replicate.
D.
GLP-1 and the "less snacking" secular question. Confectionery is the archetypal impulse category, and the structural demand debate around appetite-suppressant drugs sits over the whole group. Nothing in the fetched sources quantifies an impact on Hershey specifically, so no number is asserted here — but it is the reason a 3.3% dividend yield in a defensive staple is not being bid away.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The worst input-cost shock in the company's modern history has already passed through the P&L, and the stock is 27% below its February high while gross margin expands. FY2025's $4.34 EPS is the trough; consensus sees $8.49 in 2026 and $9.87 in 2027, putting HSY on 18.7× forward earnings with a 3.34% dividend covered 1.9× by $2.23B of free cash flow. The CFO has stated the company has "good visibility into cocoa deflation" into 2027 with hedges structured to "participate in further deflation" — and because Hershey forward-buys, that benefit lands in the income statement with a lag the market has not yet had to see. A franchise earning 17.7% ROIC at the bottom of its cycle, priced at the industry-average multiple on depressed earnings, is the definition of a cyclical recovery in a non-cyclical wrapper.

Bear Case

Strip out the derivative gains and Q2's heroic 45.3% gross margin becomes 41.6% — an improvement, but a fraction of the headline, and still below FY2024's 47.42%. The growth is price, not units: 12 points of pricing against −8 points of volume is a shrinking business charging more, and that trade has a limit. The diversification leg is failing on its own terms — Salty Snacks grew 0.6% organically with margin down 500bp — while International lost money outright. Fourteen of 23 analysts sit on Hold and four of the five most recent actions were target cuts. And the one thing that usually rescues a stalled staple, a takeover, is structurally impossible: the Trust casts ~79% of the votes. You are underwriting a commodity forecast with no strategic put.

Top 3 Risks

01
Cocoa reverses again. The entire 2026–27 earnings recovery rests on a soft commodity subject to West African weather, disease and political risk. FY2025 showed the downside sensitivity precisely: a 1,387bp gross margin hit took EPS down 60% in one year on rising revenue. Hedging smooths the path, it does not change the destination — and the same forward-buying that delays the pain also delays the relief.
02
Elasticity breaks. Volume is already down roughly 8 points consolidated and 10 points in North America Confectionery. If the consumer response to shelf prices worsens — or if competitors pass cocoa deflation through to price faster than Hershey does — the company faces a genuine choice between defending margin and defending share, and the Halloween/holiday season is where that shows up first.
03
No takeover floor, and a permanent governance discount. Hershey Trust Company held 54,612,012 Class B shares at December 31, 2025 — each carrying 10 votes to the common's 1 — entitling it to cast approximately 79% of all votes. Common holders elect only one-sixth of the Board as a class. No acquirer can buy this company without the Trust's consent, and Pennsylvania's interest in the Milton Hershey School has historically weighed against a sale. Practically, that means the strategic bid that normally arrives for an underperforming $35B staple will not arrive, and minority holders have limited recourse if capital allocation disappoints.
Industry Commentary
Cocoa — the input that rewrote the income statement
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

No commentary on this page asserts a cocoa price level, because none was verified from the pages fetched. What is verified is the effect, and it is extraordinary for a company of this vintage: consolidated gross margin fell from 47.42% in FY2024 to 33.55% in FY2025, operating margin from 26.72% to 12.58%, and diluted EPS from $10.92 to $4.34 — all on net sales that grew 4.4% to $11.69B. The quarterly path makes the shape clearer still: gross margin ran 33.66% (Q1'25), 30.45% (Q2'25), 32.61% (Q3'25), 37.04% (Q4'25), 39.39% (Q1'26) and 45.32% (Q2'26). That is a V, and the right-hand side of it is steeper than the left. Management's own framing of the supply backdrop is that "inventories are healthier, supply is more diversified, and the industry is much more agile" after the historic surpluses of the 2023–24 cycle.

The discipline: reported Q2 2026 gross margin of 45.32% includes derivative mark-to-market gains. Excluding them it is 41.6% (+350bp). Use the second number.
Hedging, forward coverage and the timing of relief
ONGOING HEADWIND

Hershey forward-buys cocoa, which is why its cost of goods and the spot market are never the same thing at the same time. The company did not publish specific forward-coverage percentages or hedge horizons in the Q2 2026 release, and none are invented here. The statements that were made on the call are these: the CFO said the company has "good visibility into cocoa deflation next year" even if futures stay where they are, and separately that there is "good visibility into cocoa deflation for 2027" — while cautioning that the business is not "solely resting on cocoa deflation by itself." On structure, management said "our hedging strategies we use will allow flexibility to participate in further deflation as the markets normalize" — i.e. the book is not locked so tightly that a falling market passes the company by. Two second-order effects follow. First, the hedging lag means 2026's commodity conditions are largely a 2027 margin event, so the consensus $9.87 of 2027 EPS is the number actually carrying the thesis. Second, mark-to-market on those same derivatives is now a visible, volatile line inside reported gross margin — it added roughly 370bp in Q2 2026 alone. Pricing remains the other lever: ~14 points of net price realisation in North America Confectionery and ~10 points internationally, with second-half gross margin improvement guided to "just below 400 basis points," a modest trim from the prior projection.

Not verified: specific hedge ratios, tonnage under contract, or forward coverage percentages — not disclosed in the release and not stated on the pages fetched.
The Trust, the votes, and the missing takeover premium
STRUCTURAL — CUTS BOTH WAYS

Hershey's dual-class structure is not a founder's vanity arrangement; it is a charitable trust's endowment. At December 31, 2025, Hershey Trust Company — wholly owned by the Milton Hershey School Trust — held 54,612,012 Class B shares as trustee and was "entitled to cast approximately 79% of all of the votes" on matters requiring both classes to vote together. Class B carries 10 votes per share against common's 1, converts to common share-for-share at any time, and receives dividends 10% lower than the common. Common holders, voting separately as a class, elect one-sixth of the Board. In July 2026 the Trust sold 30,000 common shares (roughly 2% of its ~1.3M directly-held common position) while leaving the Class B block entirely intact — the pattern of a diversifying endowment, not a retreating control holder.

Bull reading: a permanent, mission-driven anchor shareholder insulates management from activist pressure to over-lever, over-promise or cut the brand investment that makes 14 points of pricing possible. It is a real reason this company could absorb a 1,387bp margin shock without panic restructuring, and the dividend has a protector with an institutional need for it. Bear reading: takeover optionality is effectively zero. A $35B staple trading at a 27% discount to its own six-month-ago price, with an underperforming snacks arm and a loss-making international segment, would normally attract a strategic or a private-equity bid — and any such approach dies on the Trust's 79%. History reinforces this: Pennsylvania's attorney-general has an established interest in the Milton Hershey School's endowment, which has historically made a sale of the company a public-policy question rather than a purely commercial one. Minority holders here own the economics of a great franchise and almost none of the control, and must therefore be paid in cash flow rather than in the hope of a premium.
