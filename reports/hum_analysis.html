01 Company Overview

Humana is the second-largest Medicare Advantage (MA — privately administered Medicare plans paid a risk-adjusted per-member capitation by the federal government) carrier in the United States, operating two reported segments: Insurance, which houses individual and group MA, stand-alone Part D prescription drug plans, state-based Medicaid contracts, the TRICARE military services contract and specialty benefits; and CenterWell, a care-delivery arm spanning senior-focused primary care clinics, home health and pharmacy. FY2025 revenue was $129.66B, up 10.1% from $117.76B, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $145.68B, up 18.3%. At a $48.12B market capitalisation it ranks roughly #524 globally and about #357 in the US.

The central debate is not whether Humana can grow — it just grew individual MA membership 23.4% year over year to 6.45 million, an extraordinary number in a business where 3% is a good year. It is whether that growth was bought. Humana is simultaneously absorbing a large Star Ratings revenue penalty for Bonus Year 2026, carrying a consolidated benefit ratio guided to 92.75%, and telling investors full-year 2026 adjusted EPS will be at least $9.00 versus $17.91 already banked in the first half. The stock has re-rated +131% off its March 2026 low on the membership story. Sections 04 and 07 test whether the earnings are following.

Humana holds the TRICARE East military health contract and a large stand-alone Part D prescription drug book, plus dental, vision and other specialty benefits. Neither TRICARE nor PDP membership was disclosed in the release fetched for this report, so no counts are given. What is measurable here is the administrative leverage: the consolidated operating cost ratio fell from 11.0% to 9.8% GAAP (10.9% to 9.7% adjusted) year over year — 120 basis points of adjusted improvement, driven by centralising eleven utilisation-management markets into a single team and expanding outsourcing. On ~$146B of revenue, 120bp is a very large number, and it is the single clearest piece of evidence that the turnaround is operational and not just enrolment-driven.

Why this card matters: at a 92.75% benefit ratio, the operating cost ratio is essentially the entire controllable margin lever. Watch it every quarter.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
MEDICARE SCALE — #2 IN MA CENTERWELL CARE DELIVERY VALUE-BASED PROVIDER NETWORK DISTRIBUTION & BROKER REACH RISK-ADJUSTMENT DATA DEPTH TRICARE FEDERAL FRANCHISE REGULATORY BARRIER TO ENTRY

The core advantage is a closed loop that very few carriers have built: Humana underwrites the member, then owns the primary care clinic, the home-health visit and the pharmacy that treat the member. In a capitated business, owning the cost side is worth more than owning the premium side, because the insurer's profit is the residual. Management's own attribution of Q2 cost favourability to lower inpatient utilisation among value-based care members is the mechanism in one sentence: keep the senior out of the hospital, and the 91.2% benefit ratio becomes 89%. Layer on the regulatory barrier — MA is a licensed, county-by-county, bid-driven market with multi-year approval cycles — and the competitive set is effectively five national carriers, not fifty.

The problem is that the same regulator that builds the moat also sets the price, and it has recently been setting it against Humana.

Key Vulnerabilities

A
Star Ratings are a single-point regulatory dependency. Humana's MA Star Ratings significantly declined in 2025. The company sued CMS over the ratings; the court rejected the challenge in October 2025 and an appeal is pending. The resulting Bonus Year 2026 revenue hole is the stated reason FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided down to at least $9.00 in a year when revenue is growing 18%. Management now targets Stars revenue per member per month 10% above the peer median and top-quartile status by 2028 — a three-year repair on a metric scored annually by a third party.
B
Growth bought at an unproven price. Adding 1.2 million individual MA members in twelve months in a market where rivals were shrinking invites the obvious question: did Humana win the members other carriers did not want? The full-year benefit ratio guide of 92.75% against a Q2 print of 91.2% implies a second half meaningfully worse than the first. If the 2026 cohort runs hot, the membership win becomes a margin problem in 2027.
C
Medical cost trend is running 7–8%. Management described medical and pharmacy cost trend of 7% to 8% as "tracking as expected" — but that is the level of trend that broke the sector's margins in 2024. Bids are filed roughly a year ahead of the plan year; a 200bp trend surprise cannot be repriced until the following January.
D
Deliberate 2027 shrinkage. Humana plans targeted exits affecting roughly 600,000 members in 2027, pruning lower-performing plans. That is disciplined — but it means the 2026 membership headline is not a run-rate, and reported growth will decelerate sharply even before organic trends are considered. Management cites a 40% recapture rate from the 2025 exits as the offset.

Above 70 suggests the stock has risen fast enough that buyers may be exhausted ("overbought"); below 30 suggests the opposite ("oversold"). It describes momentum, not value — a stock can stay overbought for months.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $463.16 to $400.73 on September 10, 2026, HUM has delivered a price return of −13.5%. That single number hides one of the more violent round trips in large-cap healthcare. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Humana has taken 1.2 million individual MA members — 23.4% growth — while competitors retrenched, and has done it while cutting the adjusted operating cost ratio 120bp to 9.7%. The Q2 benefit ratio of 91.2% came in as guided, and management attributes cost favourability to lower inpatient utilisation among CenterWell value-based members, which is the vertical-integration thesis actually working. If Stars recover toward the 2028 top-quartile target and MA pretax margin reaches the stated 3%, earnings on a now-much-larger revenue base ($145.68B TTM, growing 18%) are far above the $9.15 consensus for 2026 — and the trailing P/E of 37.85 is being paid on a trough, not a peak.

Bear Case

The stock has risen 131% off the March low on a membership number, not an earnings number. Net margin is 0.88% — below the 1.57% industry average — ROIC is 8.13%, and FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided down to at least $9.00 despite 18% revenue growth. Full-year benefit ratio guidance of 92.75% against a 91.2% Q2 implies a much worse second half, and 600,000 members are being deliberately exited in 2027. Star Ratings repair is a three-year project scored annually by a regulator that has already beaten Humana in court. Goldman's $280 target — 30% below the current price — is simply the current earnings capitalised at a normal multiple.

Top 3 Risks

01
Star Ratings do not recover on schedule. The Bonus Year 2026 headwind is already in the numbers; the risk is that the autumn 2026 and 2027 CMS releases fail to restore quality bonus revenue, extending a two-year earnings hole into four. Management's own timeline for top-quartile status is 2028 — that is a long time to hold a 37.85x multiple.
02
The 2026 membership cohort runs hot. Adding 1.2 million members in a year when rivals were shrinking carries obvious adverse-selection risk. Full-year benefit ratio guidance of 92.75% already implies second-half deterioration; a print above 93.0% would reframe the entire growth story as mispriced risk, and 2027 bids are already filed.
03
Policy and rate risk on a single-payer customer. Substantially all of Humana's revenue ultimately comes from government programmes — CMS for Medicare, states for Medicaid, the Department of Defense for TRICARE. Annual rate notices, risk-adjustment audit methodology (RADV), and any legislative move against MA benchmark payments are all exogenous, unhedgeable, and capable of resetting the earnings power of the business in a single announcement.
Industry Commentary
The Star Ratings regime has become the sector's dominant earnings variable
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Quality bonus payments attach to Medicare Advantage contracts rated 4 stars or better, and they are large enough to swing a carrier's entire margin. Humana is the case study: its ratings significantly declined in 2025, it sued CMS, lost in October 2025, and is appealing — and the consequence is that FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided to at least $9.00, a year-over-year decline, in a year when revenue grew 18.3% and membership grew 23.4%. No amount of commercial execution offsets that. The remediation is measurable but slow: management reports running 5% ahead of last year's quality improvement rate on key HEDIS measures and targets Stars revenue per member per month 10% above the peer median by 2028. For investors, this means the most important line in any Humana model is not premium growth or the benefit ratio — it is a score published by a regulator each autumn.

Medical cost trend at 7–8% is the level that broke the sector in 2024
ONGOING HEADWIND

Management described medical and pharmacy cost trend of 7% to 8% as tracking in line with expectations. That is the crux of the sector's problem: MA bids are filed roughly a year ahead of the plan year, so a carrier that underestimates trend by 200bp cannot reprice until the following January and eats the difference in between. The 2024 sector-wide de-rating — Humana's own EPS fell from $20.00 in FY2023 to $9.98 in FY2024 on 10.7% revenue growth — was exactly this mechanism. The industry's aggregate net profit margin of 1.57% leaves essentially no buffer: a single point of unanticipated trend on a 92% benefit ratio wipes out most of the earnings. Humana's structural answer is CenterWell, and the Q2 attribution of favourability to lower inpatient utilisation among value-based members is the first concrete evidence it is doing something.

Competitive retrenchment has handed share to whoever stayed — bull and bear read it differently
EMERGING TAILWIND

The 2024–25 margin collapse pushed the large carriers to exit unprofitable MA counties and trim benefits. Humana leaned the other way and grew individual MA membership from 5.23 million to 6.45 million in twelve months, lifting Q2 revenue 26.2% and Insurance segment revenue to $39.14B. The bull reading: this is a durable share gain in a structurally growing market — seniors keep aging in, MA penetration keeps rising, and Humana bought share at the bottom of the cycle with administrative costs falling 120bp at the same time. When Stars normalise, that share converts to disproportionate earnings on a $146B base. The bear reading: in insurance, the members nobody else wants are usually the members nobody else wants. Humana is already pruning 600,000 of them for 2027, and the full-year benefit ratio guide of 92.75% against a 91.2% Q2 says management expects the second half to be worse. At 37.85x trailing earnings with a 0.88% net margin, the price already assumes the bull reading is correct.
