01 Company Overview

Humana is the second-largest Medicare Advantage (MA — privately administered Medicare plans paid a risk-adjusted per-member capitation by the federal government) carrier in the United States, operating two reported segments: Insurance, which houses individual and group MA, stand-alone Part D prescription drug plans, state-based Medicaid contracts, the TRICARE military services contract and specialty benefits; and CenterWell, a care-delivery arm spanning senior-focused primary care clinics, home health and pharmacy. FY2025 revenue was $129.66B, up 10.1% from $117.76B, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 2026 is $154.19B, up 22.0%. At a $50.83B market capitalisation it ranks #243 in the S&P 500.

The central debate is not whether Humana can grow — it grew individual MA membership 23.3% year over year to 6.46 million at September 30, 2026. It is whether that growth was bought, and when the earnings follow. 2026 still carries a large Star Ratings revenue penalty, an Insurance benefit ratio guided to 92.75%, and full-year adjusted EPS guidance of at least $9.50 (raised from $9.00 on November 6). What changed this autumn is the repair: on October 9 Humana said 95% of its Medicare Advantage members are in plans rated 4 stars or better for 2027, the ratings that set bonus payments in 2028. The stock is up +144% from its March 2026 month-end low. Sections 04 and 07 test whether the earnings can catch up.

Humana sells Medicare Advantage plans to individuals aging into Medicare or switching from traditional fee-for-service. CMS pays a risk-adjusted capitation per member per month; Humana keeps the spread between that payment and the medical costs it incurs, minus administrative expense. Individual MA membership rose from 5,237.3 thousand at September 30, 2025 to 6,456.9 thousand at September 30, 2026, and management kept its full-year growth expectation of approximately 25% — a share grab executed while several competitors were retrenching from unprofitable counties. Claims costs climb through the year in Medicare Advantage, and the guidance said so: the Q3 Insurance benefit ratio of 93.7% came in better than the "slightly above 94 percent" Humana had guided, against 91.1% a year earlier; the company expects the fourth quarter just above 95.5%, which lands the year on its unchanged 92.75% ±25bp guide. Margin profile: thin by design — management's stated aim is a sustainable pretax margin of at least 3% in individual MA in 2028, which tells you where it is now.

CenterWell is Humana's answer to Optum: own the clinic, own the home-health visit, own the pharmacy, and capture the margin that would otherwise leak to a third-party provider — while using value-based care models to bend utilisation. By September 30 it had added 146,800 senior primary care patients year-to-date, +30%, to about 638,000 across 399 centres; pharmacy revenue was up 18% year-to-date. The strategic value shows up not in CenterWell's own P&L but in the Insurance benefit ratio: in July management attributed Q2 cost favourability to lower inpatient utilisation among value-based care members. The offsetting reality is that most of the segment's revenue is intersegment ($5.09B of eliminations in Q3) — this is not an independent growth business, it is an internal supply chain. The ownership changed too: in October Humana moved nearly 150 clinics into a new joint venture majority-owned by BlackRock (through HPS), a deal announced on October 13 that closed the same month, replacing its partnership with private-equity firm Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe. The new venture has no put option — no right for the partner to force Humana to buy its stake — and CenterWell may buy the clinics back from 2028. Humana is also selling its minority stake in the home-health business Gentiva (about $900M, expected to close in the fourth quarter).

Humana holds the TRICARE East military health contract and a large stand-alone Part D prescription drug book, plus dental, vision and other specialty benefits. Spreading overhead across more members is showing up: the consolidated operating cost ratio fell to 11.3% GAAP in Q3 2026 from 12.6% a year earlier (11.2% from 11.8% adjusted), and to 10.3% adjusted for the first nine months, 0.8 percentage points lower than a year earlier; in the Insurance segment alone the nine-month GAAP ratio fell 1.1 points to 7.4%. In July management credited centralising eleven utilisation-management markets into a single team and more outsourcing. On about $154B of revenue, each tenth of a point is roughly $150M a year, and this is the clearest evidence that the turnaround is operational and not just enrolment-driven.

Why this card matters: at a 92.75% benefit ratio, the operating cost ratio is essentially the entire controllable margin lever. Watch it every quarter. Membership by line is not broken out in the release.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
MEDICARE SCALE — #2 IN MA CENTERWELL CARE DELIVERY VALUE-BASED PROVIDER NETWORK DISTRIBUTION & BROKER REACH RISK-ADJUSTMENT DATA DEPTH TRICARE FEDERAL FRANCHISE REGULATORY BARRIER TO ENTRY

The core advantage is a closed loop that very few carriers have built: Humana underwrites the member, then owns the primary care clinic, the home-health visit and the pharmacy that treat the member. In a capitated business, owning the cost side is worth more than owning the premium side, because the insurer's profit is the residual. Management's own attribution of Q2 cost favourability to lower inpatient utilisation among value-based care members is the mechanism in one sentence: keep the senior out of the hospital, and the benefit ratio falls. Layer on the regulatory barrier — MA is a licensed, county-by-county, bid-driven market with multi-year approval cycles — and the competitive set is a handful of national carriers.

The problem is that the same regulator that builds the moat also sets the price. For two years its quality scores went against Humana; the 2027 ratings, published in October 2026, went the other way.

Key Vulnerabilities

A
Star Ratings are a single-point regulatory dependency. Humana's MA Star Ratings significantly declined in 2025. The company sued CMS over the ratings; the court rejected the challenge on October 14, 2025 and Humana has appealed. The resulting Bonus Year 2026 revenue hole is the stated reason FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided down (now at least $9.50) in a year when revenue is growing more than 20%. The October 2026 ratings repaired most of it on paper: 95% of MA members in 4-star-or-better plans for 2027, with six contracts at 4.5 stars, and Humana said it beat its goal of top-quartile Stars revenue per member for Bonus Year 2028. The dependency has not gone away — the score is re-set every October, and Humana itself plans its 2028 products assuming results only around top quartile, not this year's level.
B
Growth bought at an unproven price. Adding 1.2 million individual MA members in twelve months in a market where rivals were shrinking invites the obvious question: did Humana win the members other carriers did not want? Three quarters in, the answer leans Humana's way — the Q3 benefit ratio of 93.7% beat guidance and management said cost trend for new and existing members is in line with or better than its high-single-digit assumption — but the quarter also had less help from reserve releases: favourable prior-period development was $67M, against $275M a year earlier. If the 2026 cohort runs hot, the membership win becomes a margin problem in 2027.
C
Medical cost trend is running high single digits. In July management put medical and pharmacy cost trend at 7% to 8%, and in November said it is running in line with or better than that high-single-digit assumption — but that is the level of trend that broke the sector's margins in 2024. Bids are filed roughly a year ahead of the plan year; a 200bp trend surprise cannot be repriced until the following January.
D
Deliberate 2027 shrinkage. Humana said in July it plans targeted exits affecting roughly 600,000 members in 2027, pruning lower-performing plans. That is disciplined — but it means the 2026 membership headline is not a run-rate. Management cites a 40% recapture rate from the 2025 exits as the offset, and on November 6 Humana said sign-ups and recapture were running ahead of plan: it now expects Medicare Advantage membership to grow in 2027, though by less than in 2026 (Healthcare Dive, November 6, 2026).

Notable One-Off Items. There are three, and they dominate the reported numbers. (1) The clinic joint-venture options. Humana's old CenterWell clinic partnerships gave its private-equity partner the right to sell its stake to Humana, and changes in the value of those put and call options run through GAAP earnings. In Q3 they added $358M pretax ($2.94 a share), more than the whole gap between GAAP EPS of $1.33 and the adjusted loss of $0.70; restructuring costs and other items pulled the other way. With the BlackRock joint venture replacing them, Humana expects a non-cash reversal of about $1.2B of that liability, which is why FY2026 GAAP guidance went from "at least $6.52" to "at least $15.09" while adjusted rose only to "at least $9.50". Read the GAAP figure for 2026 as inflated by this, not as earnings power. (2) The Bonus Year 2026 Star Ratings headwind. This is not a one-off in the accounting sense — it is a real revenue reduction flowing through 2026 premium — but it is non-recurring in the sense that it reverses if and when ratings recover. It is the stated reason adjusted EPS falls year over year (from $17.14 in FY2025) despite revenue growth of more than 20%. (3) Q4 2025's loss. Humana reported −$6.61 diluted EPS on $32.5B of revenue in the December 2025 quarter. Any TTM figure on this page still contains that quarter; TTM EPS of $10.28 will move sharply when Q4 2026 replaces it — upward, because the $1.2B put-liability reversal lands in GAAP, even though Humana expects another seasonal adjusted loss in the fourth quarter. Do not mistake that arithmetic for a turnaround.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY  ⚠ AS OF NOVEMBER 9, 2026
RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 suggests the stock has risen fast enough that buyers may be exhausted ("overbought"); below 30 suggests the opposite ("oversold"). It describes momentum, not value — a stock can stay overbought for months.

Over the five years from the November 2021 month-end close of $419.71 to $423.22 on November 9, 2026, HUM has delivered a price return of +0.8%. That single number hides one of the more violent round trips in large-cap healthcare. The stock peaked at $558.08 in October 2022 — the moment when Medicare Advantage was still being valued as a secular grower with pricing power — and bottomed at a $173.39 month-end close in March 2026, a −68.9% drawdown. From that low it has risen +144% in a little over seven months. Over the last 52 weeks it traded between $163.11 and $456.50 (intraday), a high 180% above the low; the beta of 0.64 is, in this context, a statistical artefact rather than a description of risk.

The shape of the chart maps almost perfectly onto regulatory events rather than business events. The 2023–24 collapse began with the January 2024 medical-cost guidance shock, deepened through the October 2024 Star Ratings decline, and ground lower through 2025 as the Bonus Year 2026 revenue hit was quantified. The 2026 recovery began in April, accelerated through the Q1 report, held through the summer on the membership surge and lower operating costs, and took its next step on October 9, when the 2027 Star Ratings came in far better than a year earlier. Momentum is extended: the 6-period monthly RSI reads about 76, above the 70 overbought line, and November is the seventh straight month-end above the 10-month moving average, the longest run in this five-year window (the previous one was six months, August 2022 to January 2023).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Humana has taken 1.2 million individual MA members — 23.3% growth — while competitors retrenched, cut its adjusted operating cost ratio 0.8 points over nine months, and beat its own benefit-ratio guidance in Q3. The Stars repair has already happened on paper: 95% of MA members in 4-star-or-better plans for 2027, beating Humana's own top-quartile goal for the 2028 bonus year. If MA pretax margin reaches the stated 3% in 2028, earnings on a much larger revenue base ($154.19B TTM, growing 22%) sit far above the $9.33 consensus for 2026 — the $17.36 consensus for 2027 already says so — and the trailing P/E of 41.17 is being paid on a trough, not a peak.

Bear Case

The stock has risen 144% off the March low on membership and a quality score, not on earnings. Net margin is 0.81% — below the 2.37% industry average — ROIC is 6.34%, and FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided down to at least $9.50 despite revenue growth above 20%. Q3 was a loss on an adjusted basis, Q4 is guided to a benefit ratio above 95.5%, reserve releases are shrinking, and 600,000 members are being deliberately exited in 2027. Star Ratings are re-scored every October by a regulator that has already beaten Humana in court. The panel's $280 low target sits 34% below the current price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Star Ratings do not recover on schedule. The autumn 2026 release restored the 2028 bonus, but Humana itself says this year's result includes a one-time outperformance and plans 2028 products assuming only about top-quartile results. The October 2027 release sets 2029 bonuses; a slip there would reopen the earnings hole the market has just stopped pricing, at a 41.17× trailing multiple.
02
The 2026 membership cohort runs hot. Adding 1.2 million members in a year when rivals were shrinking carries obvious adverse-selection risk. Q3 came in better than guided, but the fourth quarter carries the year's highest expected benefit ratio (just above 95.5%) and reserve releases have shrunk from $275M to $67M a quarter; a full-year Insurance ratio above the 93.0% top of the guide would reframe the growth story as mispriced risk, and 2027 bids are already filed.
03
Policy and rate risk on a single-payer customer. Substantially all of Humana's revenue ultimately comes from government programmes — CMS for Medicare, states for Medicaid, the Department of Defense for TRICARE. Annual rate notices, risk-adjustment audit methodology (RADV), and any legislative move against MA benchmark payments are all exogenous, unhedgeable, and capable of resetting the earnings power of the business in a single announcement.
Industry Commentary
The Star Ratings regime has become the sector's dominant earnings variable
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Quality bonus payments attach to Medicare Advantage contracts rated 4 stars or better, and they are large enough to swing a carrier's entire margin. Humana is the case study: its ratings significantly declined in 2025, it sued CMS, lost in October 2025, and is appealing — and the consequence is that FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided to at least $9.50, a year-over-year decline, in a year when trailing revenue grew 22.0% and individual MA membership grew 23.3%. Then the score flipped: in October 2026 Humana said 95% of its MA members are in 4-star-or-better plans for 2027, while across the industry the share of MA drug plans at 4 stars or above fell to about 37% from 44% (Healthcare Dive, from CMS data), and the stock gained 11.56% in a day. For investors, this means the most important line in any Humana model is not premium growth or the benefit ratio — it is a score published by a regulator each autumn.

Medical cost trend at 7–8% is the level that broke the sector in 2024
ONGOING HEADWIND

Management described medical and pharmacy cost trend of 7% to 8% in July, and in November said it is running in line with or better than that high-single-digit assumption. That is the crux of the sector's problem: MA bids are filed roughly a year ahead of the plan year, so a carrier that underestimates trend by 200bp cannot reprice until the following January and eats the difference in between. The 2024 sector-wide sell-off — Humana's own EPS fell from $20.00 in FY2023 to $9.98 in FY2024 on 10.7% revenue growth — was exactly this mechanism. The industry's aggregate net profit margin of 2.37% leaves essentially no buffer: a single point of unanticipated trend on a 92% benefit ratio wipes out most of the earnings. Humana's structural answer is CenterWell, and the July attribution of favourability to lower inpatient utilisation among value-based members was the first concrete evidence it is doing something; the Q3 benefit ratio beating guidance is the second.

Competitive retrenchment has handed share to whoever stayed — bull and bear read it differently
EMERGING TAILWIND

The 2024–25 margin collapse pushed the large carriers to exit unprofitable MA counties and trim benefits. Humana leaned the other way and grew individual MA membership from 5.24 million to 6.46 million in twelve months, lifting Q3 revenue 26.1% and Insurance segment revenue to $39.08B. The bull reading: this is a durable share gain in a structurally growing market — seniors keep aging in, MA penetration keeps rising, and Humana bought share at the bottom of the cycle with administrative costs falling as a share of revenue at the same time. When Stars normalise, that share converts to disproportionate earnings on a $154B base. The bear reading: in insurance, the members nobody else wants are usually the members nobody else wants. Humana is already pruning 600,000 of them for 2027, and the fourth-quarter benefit ratio guide just above 95.5% shows how much of the year's cost lands late. At 41.17× trailing earnings with a 0.81% net margin, the price already assumes the bull reading is correct.
