01 Company Overview

Hyundai sells about four million cars a year — Hyundai and Genesis badges, petrol, hybrid, electric and hydrogen — from plants in Korea, the US, India and elsewhere, and lends money to many of the people who buy them. In 2025 it booked ₩186.3 trillion of revenue and ₩11.5 trillion of operating profit, a 6.2% margin. In the second quarter of 2026 revenue hit a quarterly record of ₩49.2 trillion (+1.9%), but operating profit fell 20.8% to ₩2.85 trillion as a fire at a parts supplier stopped some of its most profitable models, raw-material costs rose and discounts grew. Hybrids were the bright spot: a record 187,661 sold, 18.9% of the total.

For a few months in 2026 the stock market stopped pricing Hyundai as a carmaker. Boston Dynamics, the Hyundai Motor Group robotics company, put its humanoid robot Atlas on stage at CES in January, and the shares rose 13.8% on January 7 alone (The Korea Times). By June 1 they had traded as high as ₩783,000, more than 2.6 times the 2025 year-end close of ₩296,500. Then the car business reasserted itself: falling sales, a weak second quarter, the first full-scale strike in a decade, a stronger won, and a run of broker target cuts. On October 2 the shares closed at ₩346,500 — 55.7% below that intraday high, but still 57.5% above a year earlier. The question for this report is how much of the robot story survives in a price that is now back near eleven times trailing earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Hybrid + EV + ICE + hydrogen lineup 6%+ US share, 5 straight quarters US plants (HMMA, HMGMA) Captive finance arm Group supply chain (Mobis, Kia) Boston Dynamics robotics

Carmaking is a scale business, and Hyundai's advantage is breadth. It sells every kind of powertrain, so when US buyers swung to hybrids — the company cites high fuel prices, and the CFO pointed on the call to what he called the abolition of the US IRA — it could ship more hybrids rather than rebuild its lineup; that is how US share rose to 6.3% in a flat market. It builds cars inside the US at its Alabama (HMMA) and Georgia (HMGMA) plants, which matters more with a 15% tariff on imports, and the group has committed $26B of new US investment over 2025–2028, per the investor-day deck. Its own lender finances a large share of the cars it sells. The weakness is that none of this makes it unusually profitable: a 5–6% operating margin is the margin of a company that competes on price and volume.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Tariffs take a third of the profit. The ₩4.11T tariff cost in 2025 compares with ₩11.47T of operating profit that year. Local production cuts the cost only as fast as new US capacity comes on.
02
Losing ground at home. Domestic sales fell 10.8% in the first half; in April Kia briefly overtook Hyundai as Korea's best-selling carmaker for the first time in nearly three decades, and Tesla's first-half Korean sales rose 192.2% to 56,139 against Hyundai's 39,575 EVs (The Korea Times). In September, domestic sales were 25.7% lower than a year earlier.
03
Labour. The union staged its first full-scale strike in a decade on August 21. Industry estimates put the disruption at 55,200 vehicles and ₩2.3T of lost sales (Korea JoongAng Daily). The deal ratified on August 31 includes a bonus of 400% of monthly base pay plus ₩12.7M and 15 shares per worker, and the union had sought job guarantees as robots and AI come onto assembly lines (Korea Herald).
04
The robot premium is fragile. NH Investment & Securities wrote in July that "progress is slower than the market had expected" on humanoids and autonomous driving (Seoul Economic Daily). Kiwoom's Shin Yoon-chul said foreign ownership had fallen from over 35% in January to below 25%, "a historically unusual level of foreign capital outflow" (The Korea Times).

Notable One-Off Items

Four items shape the recent figures. First, the supplier fire: engine-valve supply to Hyundai's largest Korean plant was cut by a fire at a supplier — Korea Investment & Securities' Kim Chang-ho named it as the Ahn Safety Industry Daejeon plant (Seoul Economic Daily) — and a separate fire at a Hyundai Mobis plant in India briefly stopped Hyundai's Indian factory. The CFO said substitute valves were in use across all products by May and that the company aims to recover the lost output in the second half. Second, currency: a weaker won raised revenue by about ₩2.6T in Q2, but a higher quarter-end exchange rate raised provisions and cut the operating-profit benefit to ₩0.24T. Third, the strike falls in Q3, which has not been reported yet. Fourth, the Indian listing: Hyundai sold 17.5% of Hyundai Motor India in 2024, when the subsidiary listed in India (first trading day October 22, 2024); the sale added ₩3.07T to capital surplus rather than to profit, and Hyundai still owns 82.5%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The robot premium is mostly gone, so the price pays for the car business: 11.2 times trailing earnings, 8.3 times this year's consensus, a 2.9% dividend yield with a ₩10,000 floor and a 35%+ payout policy. The first half was hit by things that should not repeat — a supplier fire, inventory clearance before new models, a raw-material spike the CFO says is easing — and the second half brings a new Grandeur, Elantra, Tucson and IONIQ 3. US share keeps rising on hybrids. If the margin gets back into the 6.3–7.3% guidance range and Atlas production starts on time in 2028, today's price includes the robots almost for free.

Bear Case

The second half started badly: wholesale sales fell 16% in September, and the strike cost an estimated 55,200 cars. Meeting guidance needs a second-half margin of roughly 7–9% after 5.6% in the first half, while the 15% US tariff, Chinese EVs in Europe and Tesla in Korea press on price. Brokers are still cutting, and the consensus target of ₩641,504 assumes a recovery the reported numbers do not yet show. The robot business is years from revenue, and NH says it is moving slower than the market expected. Foreign ownership fell from over 35% in January to below 25% by July (Kiwoom).

Top 3 Risks

01
Guidance miss. The CFO kept the 6.3–7.3% margin guidance on July 23: "we are maintaining our annual operating profit margin guidance of 6.3% to 7.3% without downward revision from our initial guidance at the beginning of the year" (English interpretation of the call, EarningsCall transcript). The strike came a month later. A cut at the Q3 results would be the first change to the 2026 guidance.
02
Currency turns from help to hurt. A weak won lifted Q2 revenue by about ₩2.6T. Citi and Samsung Securities both cut on a stronger won, and Citi cut its FY2026–2028 profit estimates by 6–10% on it.
03
Losing share at home and in Europe. Domestic sales were down 16.3% for the first nine months of 2026, and Europe wholesale fell 10.9% in Q2. The CFO said incentives were raised in Q2 in part because of the aggressive entry of Chinese EVs into Europe. Meritz sees Hyundai's competitive position deteriorating in Korea, the US and India.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs and the move to build in America
CURRENT HEADWIND

Korean cars entering the US now pay a 15% tariff (The Korea Times). Hyundai's answer is to build more where it sells: US plants in Alabama and Georgia, a group commitment of $26B of new US investment from 2025 to 2028, a new low-carbon Hyundai steel mill in Louisiana and an 80% parts-localisation target for 2030, per the August investor-day deck. Korea Investment & Securities' Kim Chang-ho expects lower tariff costs in the second half to help earnings (Seoul Economic Daily). The arithmetic is still daunting: 2025's ₩4.11T tariff bill was more than a third of operating profit.

Hybrids win, EVs stall, China competes
MIXED

With high fuel prices (company deck) and what the CFO called the abolition of the US IRA, buyers have moved to hybrids — Hyundai's global EV sales fell 12.7% in Q2 while hybrids rose 11.3%, according to the call. A carmaker that sells every powertrain can follow demand; one that bet only on EVs cannot. The competition has changed shape, though: Chinese EV makers are pushing into Europe, where Hyundai raised incentives, and Tesla, selling mostly the China-built Model Y, outsold Hyundai's whole EV range in Korea in the first half (The Korea Times). The bull framing: hybrids carry better margins and Hyundai has the lineup. The bear framing: price competition is spreading to every market Hyundai depends on.

Physical AI and the re-rating that didn't stick
STRUCTURAL OPTION

For half a year, investors valued Hyundai as a robotics company that happens to build cars. The company leans into the framing — its own profile now says it is "accelerating its transformation beyond mobility through advanced robotics and physical AI" — and its investor-day deck shows robotics, on-demand mobility and software-defined vehicles among the industry's profit pools of 2035. The market has since asked for proof: Kiwoom's Shin Yoon-chul said "the catalyst that brings foreign investors back is unlikely to be expectations for new businesses" (The Korea Times). Hana's Song Sun-jae lists what would bring the premium back: Boston Dynamics' robot application centre, a production entity, a parts supply chain and the SDV Pace Car (Seoul Economic Daily). Until then, the stock trades on cars.
