01 Company Overview

Interactive Brokers is an automated electronic broker that executes, clears and settles trades in stocks, options, futures, foreign exchange, bonds, metals and cryptocurrencies for individual and institutional clients worldwide, and lends against those positions. In fiscal 2025 it produced $6.205 billion of net revenues, up 20% from $5.185 billion in 2024, on a pretax profit margin of 77% — a level no other listed brokerage comes close to. Trailing-twelve-month (TTM) revenue is $6.84 billion, +21.3% YoY, and the company ranks roughly #385 globally by market capitalization at a $42.7B quoted market cap.

Two structural facts govern how you read every number on this page. First, revenue mix: in FY2025, net interest income (NII — the spread the broker earns between what it collects on customer margin loans and segregated cash and what it pays customers on their credit balances) was $3.563 billion, or 57.4% of net revenues, versus $2.149 billion (34.6%) from commissions. IBKR is therefore as much a leveraged position on the level of short-term interest rates as it is a bet on trading activity. Second, share structure: the publicly traded entity, IBKR Group, Inc., holds only a minority economic interest in the operating partnership, IBG LLC. At June 30, 2026 total equity was $22.3 billion — $5.9 billion attributable to IBKR Group, Inc. shareholders and $16.3 billion to noncontrolling interests held by founder Thomas Peterffy and insiders through IBG Holdings LLC. The public company's economic share of the operating business is therefore roughly 26%. Everything you see quoted — the $42.7B market cap, the $2.52 EPS, the $1.13B net income, the 16.47% net margin — is on the public Class A basis, not the whole-company basis. Revenue and pretax income, by contrast, are reported for the entire operating company. Mixing the two is the single most common error made with this stock.

On a look-through basis the arithmetic is different: MarketBeat's ownership filings imply roughly 1.70 billion total shares across all classes (a 6,230,136-share position is disclosed as 0.367% of the company), of which only 453.08 million are publicly traded Class A. At $94.33 that implies a whole-company equity value of roughly $160 billion — our derived estimate, not a reported figure. Read the quoted $42.7B as "the public slice," and the 77% pretax margin as "the whole pie."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
77% PRETAX MARGIN $2.1M REVENUE / EMPLOYEE SELF-CLEARING SCALE GLOBAL MULTI-CURRENCY LOWEST ALL-IN COST $10.3B EXCESS CAPITAL OWNER-OPERATOR ALIGNMENT

The moat is arithmetic, not narrative. IBKR generated $6.84 billion of TTM revenue with 3,265 employees — roughly $2.1 million of revenue and $285 million of customer equity per employee (our computation from disclosed figures). Schwab produces $26.03 billion with 33,000 people, about $0.79 million per head. That gap is the entire story of the 77% pretax margin, which management noted in July 2026 was the seventh consecutive quarter above 70%. The model is a single global clearing and risk platform that onboards accounts, runs margin calculations, routes orders, lends securities and computes interest with almost no human intervention; management explicitly described extending AI into compliance, customer service and onboarding to keep headcount growth below revenue growth. Because incremental accounts and incremental balances arrive at near-zero marginal cost, every dollar of new NII or commission drops through at a rate that no full-service or advice-led competitor can match. Layer on self-clearing (no third-party clearer taking a cut), a genuinely global multi-currency book that lets the firm serve non-US customers domestic brokers cannot, and $10.3 billion of excess regulatory capital, and the result is a low-cost producer that can price below everyone and still out-earn them. Distribution through introducing brokers, RIAs, hedge funds and prop firms adds balances without a retail marketing budget.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Interest-rate concentration. Roughly 57% of FY2025 net revenue and a larger share of pretax income came from net interest income. Management's own disclosure is that each 25 bp cut in fed funds removes about $81 million of annual NII, with another $38 million at risk per 25 bp of non-USD rate movement. Net interest margin has already compressed from 2.07% to 1.93% year over year and segregated-cash yields from 3.86% to 3.32%. Balance growth has masked this so far; if account and margin growth decelerate while rates fall, both legs give at once.

2. Control and share-structure concentration. Public Class A holders own roughly 26% of the operating company's economics; Thomas Peterffy and insiders hold the $16.3 billion noncontrolling interest and effective voting control. Minority holders cannot force a change in strategy, capital allocation, dividend policy or succession. The dividend payout ratio is 13.9% and the yield 0.37% — capital retention is a founder's decision, not a board negotiation. Succession is a live question given the founder's age and continued presence on earnings calls.

3. Correlated cyclicality. The three growth engines — DARTs, customer equity and margin loans — are not independent. A sustained equity bear market simultaneously cuts trade counts, shrinks the $930.3 billion equity base and forces deleveraging of the $108.5 billion margin book, which is itself up 67% YoY and therefore the most cyclically extended balance on the sheet. Margin lending has historically been the fastest line to contract in a drawdown.

4. Multi-jurisdiction regulatory surface. Growth is deliberately global — Korea entry, European crypto, prediction markets with Kalshi and CME, SpaceX pre-IPO access in Europe — and each new venue adds a regulator. Newer products (crypto, event contracts, pre-IPO access) sit in the least-settled areas of securities and commodities law. Domestically, the SEC transaction fee rate increase effective April 4, 2026 is a reminder that the fee structure is set externally. An effective tax rate of 8.59% at the public entity is a function of the pass-through structure, not of tax planning that would survive a structural change.

Notable One-Off Items

Currency diversification marks are the recurring "non-recurring" item. IBKR holds a fixed basket of currencies as a treasury policy, and the mark runs through Other Income. In 2Q2026 that produced a $26 million gain, driving the wedge between GAAP net revenues of $1.90B and adjusted $1.88B, and GAAP pretax income of $1.46B versus $1.44B adjusted. The sign reverses: FY2025 GAAP EPS of $2.22 exceeded adjusted $2.19, while FY2024 GAAP $1.73 was below adjusted $1.76. Any year-over-year EPS comparison that ignores this is measuring FX, not the brokerage.

The 4-for-1 stock split restates all per-share history. Announced April 15, 2025, record date June 16, 2025, distributed June 17, 2025, with split-adjusted trading from June 18, 2025. Every price and EPS figure on this page is split-adjusted. Pre-split headlines and older analyst price targets (you will still find $147–$214 targets from 2025 in search results) are four times the current scale and are not comparable.

The 8.59% effective tax rate is structural, not a one-off benefit. Most of the operating company's income is allocated to noncontrolling interests and taxed at the member level, so the public entity's tax line looks anomalously low. It is not a credit that will reverse, but it is also not a margin the company controls.

Regulatory fee changes. The SEC transaction fee rate increase effective April 4, 2026 lifted reported regulatory fee revenue in 2Q2026; it is substantially pass-through and should not be read as margin. No material write-downs, litigation charges, impairments or acquisitions were disclosed in FY2025 or in the first half of FY2026 — management said on the July 2026 call that nothing in the acquisition pipeline "stood out as worthy of pursuing yet."

Five-year trajectory. On a split-adjusted basis IBKR closed September 2021 at $15.19 and trades at $94.33 today — a total price return of roughly +521%, or about 44% compounded annually over five years (dividends excluded; at a 0.37% yield they are immaterial to the comparison). The path was not smooth. The stock spent all of 2022 going nowhere and bottomed at $13.46 in June 2022, below where it started the window, as the market repriced everything with a multiple. The entire return has been generated since early 2024, when three things compounded at once: account growth accelerated past 30%, the fed funds rate stayed high enough to make $180B+ of customer credits genuinely valuable, and margin balances began the run that has taken them to $108.5 billion. From the December 2023 close of $20.45 to today the stock is up more than fourfold in under three years.

Relative performance. Over the last twelve months IBKR returned +49.12% against the NASDAQ-100's +24.68% one-year total return (Invesco QQQ Trust) — roughly double the benchmark. Against its own sector the gap is wider: IBKR's market capitalization rose +53.8% YoY while Charles Schwab's rose +11.9%. Among the four public brokers in our comparison set, IBKR's 16.7% share of combined revenue is far smaller than Schwab's 63.7%, but it is the only one delivering Schwab-scale growth at Schwab-beating margins. The Capital Markets industry as a whole trades at a 29.54 average P/E; IBKR's 36.95 is the market paying up for that combination.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Account growth is the compounding machine: 5.19 million customers, up 34% year over year, is far faster than any US peer, and each account arrives at near-zero marginal cost onto a platform already earning a 77% pretax margin — the highest in the brokerage industry by a wide margin. Those accounts drag balances with them: customer equity $930.3B (+40%), margin loans $108.5B (+67%), credits $182.4B (+27%), DARTs 4.82M (+36%). Global reach is the differentiator competitors cannot copy quickly — Korea, European crypto, prediction markets and pre-IPO access all add DARTs and balances without adding meaningful headcount to a 3,265-person firm generating $2.1M of revenue per employee. And the rate risk that anchored the bear case has not shown up: the Fed has held at 3.50%–3.75% for five straight meetings and the market now prices a hike as more likely than a cut.

Bear Case

Roughly 57% of revenue is net interest income, and the price of that income is already falling: net interest margin compressed to 1.93% from 2.07% and segregated-cash yields to 3.32% from 3.86%. Only volume growth hid it. Management's own math says each 25 bp of fed funds cuts is worth $81 million of annual NII, plus $38 million per 25 bp of non-USD rates — a return to a genuine easing cycle removes a large, high-incremental-margin block of profit that commissions cannot replace. Meanwhile the multiple has already re-rated: 36.95x trailing earnings against a 29.54 industry average and a 32.51 NASDAQ-100, after a 49% twelve-month move that has left the price essentially at the sell-side's target cluster (MarketBeat's average target implies just 2.3% upside). You are paying a growth multiple for a business whose largest revenue line is a rate call you do not control.

Top 3 Risks
01
Net interest income compression on a falling-rate path. With ~57% of revenue and a larger share of pretax income coming from NII, and disclosed sensitivity of $81M per 25 bp of fed funds plus $38M per 25 bp of non-USD rates, a 100 bp easing cycle removes roughly $324M of annualized USD NII before any offsetting balance growth — against FY2025 pretax income of $4.77B. The margin compression is already visible in the 1.93% net interest margin.
02
Concentration of control in Thomas Peterffy. Public Class A holders own roughly 26% of the operating company's economics — $5.9B of $22.3B total equity — with $16.3B of noncontrolling interest and effective voting control held by the founder and insiders. Minority shareholders have no practical mechanism to influence capital allocation, dividend policy, strategy or succession, and founder succession remains an unresolved overhang.
03
Correlated cyclical exposure at a re-rated multiple. DARTs, customer equity and margin loans all contract together in a drawdown, and the margin book — up 67% year over year to $108.5B — is the most cyclically extended balance on the sheet. At 36.95x trailing earnings and a beta of 1.34, the stock carries both operating and multiple risk into any market correction, as the −24% move from January to March 2025 demonstrated.
  Industry Commentary
The rate path has stopped falling — and that changes the story
EMERGING TAILWIND
The standard bear thesis on IBKR is NII compression as rates fall. The live data does not currently support it. The Fed cut three consecutive times in September, October and December 2025, reaching a target range of 3.50%–3.75% — and has held there through five straight meetings into July 2026. At that July meeting three FOMC members dissented in favor of raising the policy rate by 25 bp, and Trading Economics reports markets pricing roughly a 77% probability of an increase in September 2026, with the fed funds rate projected to trend toward 4.25% in 2027. Against management's disclosed sensitivity of $81 million of annual NII per 25 bp of fed funds, the near-term skew is a tailwind, not a headwind. That said, the sensitivity is symmetric and the underlying compression is real: net interest margin has already fallen to 1.93% from 2.07% and segregated-cash yields to 3.32% from 3.86%. The risk did not disappear — it got deferred, and the market has repriced the stock as though it were resolved.
Balance growth is outrunning spread compression — for now
EMERGING TAILWIND
The mechanism that let IBKR grow NII 23% to $1.06 billion in 2Q2026 while its net interest margin fell 14 basis points is pure volume. Margin loans rose 67% to $108.5 billion, customer credits 27% to $182.4 billion, and customer equity 40% to $930.3 billion, on an account base up 34% to 5.19 million. Securities lending added an estimated $343 million of net revenue, +37%. This is the correct way to underwrite the business: NII per dollar of balance is a rate call, but the number of dollars is a franchise call, and the franchise has been compounding above 30% for years. The vulnerability is that the two are not independent in a drawdown — margin balances up 67% in a year are a leveraged bet on the equity market as much as a lending book, and they contract fastest when prices fall.
The multiple has already re-rated — bull vs bear from here
ONGOING HEADWIND
The bull framing: at 36.95x trailing and 31.40x forward earnings, IBKR trades at a modest premium to a 32.51 NASDAQ-100 while growing revenue 21.3% and EPS 32.6%, with a PEG of 1.56, a 77% pretax margin and the fastest account growth in the peer set. Barclays at $114 and BMO at $110 are underwriting continued 30%-plus account compounding and a rate floor. The bear framing: the stock is up 49.12% in twelve months and market cap is up 53.8%; it sits 3.6% below its 52-week high and, on MarketBeat's $96.89 average target, has 2.3% of implied upside — the sell side has caught up to the price rather than leading it. KBW initiated at Market Perform with a $75 target in April 2026 and has not moved it through two beats, which is a valuation view, not an execution view. The resolution is the September FOMC meeting and the October 15 print: if rates hold or rise while accounts keep compounding above 30%, the premium is defensible; if the Fed resumes cutting into decelerating account growth, a 57%-NII revenue mix at 37x earnings is an uncomfortable place to be.
