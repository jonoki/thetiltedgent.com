01 Company Overview

Interactive Brokers is an automated electronic broker that executes, clears and settles trades in stocks, options, futures, foreign exchange, bonds, metals and cryptocurrencies for individual and institutional clients worldwide, and lends against those positions. In fiscal 2025 it produced $6.205 billion of net revenues, up 20% from $5.185 billion in 2024, on a pretax profit margin of 77% — and 78% in the third quarter of 2026. Trailing-twelve-month (TTM) revenue is $7.06 billion, +18.7% YoY, and the public company's market capitalization is $38.7B at the October 21 close (453.08M Class A shares, the count StockAnalysis carries, × $85.33).

Two structural facts govern how you read every number on this page. First, revenue mix: in FY2025, net interest income (NII — the spread the broker earns between what it collects on customer margin loans and segregated cash and what it pays customers on their credit balances) was $3.563 billion, or 57.4% of net revenues, versus $2.149 billion (34.6%) from commissions. IBKR is therefore as much a leveraged position on the level of short-term interest rates as it is a bet on trading activity. Second, share structure: the publicly traded entity, IBKR Group, Inc., holds only a minority economic interest in the operating partnership, IBG LLC. At September 30, 2026 total equity was $23.3 billion — $6.2 billion attributable to IBKR Group, Inc. shareholders and $17.0 billion to noncontrolling interests (our subtraction of the $6.215 billion attributable to the public company from the $23.251 billion total) held by founder Thomas Peterffy and insiders through IBG Holdings LLC. The public company's economic share of the operating business is therefore roughly 27%. Everything you see quoted — the $38.7B market cap, the $2.62 EPS, the $1.18B trailing-twelve-month net income, the 16.68% net margin — is on the public Class A basis, not the whole-company basis. Revenue and pretax income, by contrast, are reported for the entire operating company. Mixing the two is an easy mistake to make with this stock.

On a look-through basis the arithmetic is different: if the public company's ~27% share of the operating company's book equity also described its share count, total shares across all classes would be roughly 1.70 billion, of which only 453.08 million are publicly traded Class A. At $85.33 that implies a whole-company equity value of roughly $145 billion — our derived estimate, not a reported figure. Read the quoted $38.7B as "the public slice," and the 77–78% pretax margin as "the whole pie."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
78% PRETAX MARGIN $2.1M REVENUE / EMPLOYEE SELF-CLEARING SCALE GLOBAL MULTI-CURRENCY LOW ALL-IN COST OWNER-OPERATOR ALIGNMENT

The moat is arithmetic, not narrative. IBKR generated $7.06 billion of TTM revenue with 3,286 employees — roughly $2.1 million of revenue and $294 million of customer equity per employee (our computation from disclosed figures). Schwab produces $27.06 billion with 33,000 people, about $0.82 million per head. That gap is the entire story of the 78% pretax margin, which management called the eighth consecutive quarter above 70% on the October 2026 call. The model is a single global clearing and risk platform that onboards accounts, runs margin calculations, routes orders, lends securities and computes interest with almost no human intervention; management has described extending AI into compliance, customer service and onboarding to keep headcount growth below revenue growth. Because incremental accounts and incremental balances arrive at near-zero marginal cost, every dollar of new NII or commission drops through to pretax profit at a high rate. Layer on self-clearing (no third-party clearer taking a cut) and a genuinely global multi-currency book that lets the firm serve non-US customers domestic brokers cannot, and the result is a low-cost producer that can charge low prices and still earn a 78% pretax margin. Distribution through introducing brokers, RIAs, hedge funds and prop firms adds balances without a retail marketing budget.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Interest-rate concentration. Roughly 57% of FY2025 net revenue came from net interest income. Management's own disclosure is that a 25 bp move in fed funds changes annual NII by about $83 million — a cut removes it, a hike adds it — with another $35 million at stake per 25 bp of non-USD rate movement. Net interest margin was 1.99% in 3Q2026 against 2.16% a year earlier. Balance growth has masked this so far; if account and margin growth slow while rates fall, both legs give at once.

2. Control and share-structure concentration. Public Class A holders own roughly 27% of the operating company's economics; Thomas Peterffy and insiders hold the $17.0 billion noncontrolling interest and effective voting control. Minority holders cannot force a change in strategy, capital allocation, dividend policy or succession. The board has just raised the quarterly dividend 71%, from $0.0875 to $0.15 ($0.60 a year), and Peterffy said on the call the company is trying to catch up with its guideline of paying 0.5% to 1% of market value — a reminder that the payout is a founder's decision, not a board negotiation. Succession is a live question given the founder's continued presence on earnings calls.

3. Correlated cyclicality. The three growth engines — DARTs, customer equity and margin loans — are not independent. A sustained equity bear market simultaneously cuts trade counts, shrinks the $964.7 billion equity base and forces deleveraging of the $105.2 billion margin book, which is up 36% YoY and therefore among the most cyclically extended balances on the sheet. It has already shown it can move down: it was $108.5 billion at June 30. Margin lending can contract fastest in a drawdown. The third quarter gave a small preview of the trade-count leg: DARTs fell 11% from the second quarter.

4. Multi-jurisdiction regulatory surface. Growth is deliberately global — Korea entry, European crypto, prediction markets with Kalshi and CME, the SpaceX IPO offered to European clients — and each new venue adds a regulator. Newer products (crypto, event contracts, IPO access) sit in the least-settled areas of securities and commodities law. Domestically, the higher SEC Section 31 transaction fee rate lifted IBKR's regulatory fees by $30 million in 3Q2026, a reminder that the fee structure is set externally. The effective tax rate at the public entity was about 9.9% in 3Q2026 ($148 million of tax on $1,496 million of pretax income; our calculation) — a function of the pass-through structure, not of tax planning that would survive a structural change.

Notable One-Off Items

Currency diversification marks are the recurring "non-recurring" item. IBKR holds a fixed basket of currencies as a treasury policy, and the mark runs through Other Income. In 3Q2026 the release shows a $40 million gain in Other Income and a $44 million loss in other comprehensive income, a net $4 million drag on comprehensive earnings, and a wedge between GAAP net revenues of $1.91B and adjusted $1.87B and between GAAP pretax income of $1.50B and adjusted $1.45B (GAAP EPS $0.69, adjusted $0.67). The sign of the wedge changes from year to year: FY2025 GAAP EPS of $2.22 exceeded adjusted $2.19, while FY2024 GAAP $1.73 was below adjusted $1.76. Any year-over-year EPS comparison that ignores this is measuring FX, not the brokerage.

The 4-for-1 stock split restates all per-share history. Announced April 15, 2025, record date June 16, 2025, distributed June 17, 2025, with split-adjusted trading from June 18, 2025. Every price and EPS figure on this page is split-adjusted. Pre-split headlines and older analyst price targets (you will still find $147–$214 targets from 2025 in search results) are four times the current scale and are not comparable.

The roughly 9.9% effective tax rate is structural, not a one-off benefit. Most of the operating company's income is allocated to noncontrolling interests and taxed at the member level, so the public entity's tax line looks anomalously low ($148 million of tax on $1,496 million of pretax income in 3Q2026; our calculation). It is not a credit that will reverse, but it is also not a margin the company controls.

Regulatory fee changes. The higher SEC Section 31 transaction fee rate (effective April 4, 2026) lifted execution, clearing and distribution fees by 46% to $134 million in 3Q2026, including a $30 million increase in regulatory fees; it is substantially pass-through and should not be read as margin.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Account growth is the compounding machine: 5.58 million customers, up 35% year over year, and each account arrives at near-zero marginal cost onto a platform already earning a 78% pretax margin. Those accounts drag balances with them: customer equity $964.7B (+27%), margin loans $105.2B (+36%), credits $186.2B (+20%), DARTs 4.27M (+18%). Global reach is the differentiator competitors cannot copy quickly — Korea, European crypto, prediction markets and IPO access all add DARTs and balances without adding meaningful headcount to a 3,286-person firm generating $2.1M of revenue per employee. The rate risk that anchored the bear case has turned: the Fed raised its target range to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16, and each 25 bp is worth about $83 million of annual NII. The board also raised the quarterly dividend 71%, to $0.15.

Bear Case

Roughly 57% of FY2025 revenue was net interest income, and the price of that income is falling: net interest margin was 1.99% in 3Q2026 against 2.16% a year earlier, and only volume growth hid it. Management's own math says each 25 bp move in fed funds is worth $83 million of annual NII, plus $35 million per 25 bp of non-USD rates — so a return to an easing cycle would remove a large, high-incremental-margin block of profit that commissions cannot replace. Trading activity also cooled: DARTs of 4.27 million were 11% below the second quarter and commissions of $602 million were 10.5% below its record $673 million, while the margin book ($105.2 billion) sits below June's $108.5 billion. Earnings per share only matched consensus ($0.67 adjusted) and the shares fell 3.88% the next session. At 32.57 times trailing earnings you are still paying a growth multiple for a business whose largest revenue line is a rate call you do not control.

Top 3 Risks
01
Net interest income compression on a falling-rate path. With ~57% of revenue coming from NII, and disclosed sensitivity of $83M per 25 bp of fed funds plus $35M per 25 bp of non-USD rates, a 100 bp easing cycle removes roughly $332M of annualized USD NII before any offsetting balance growth — against FY2025 pretax income of $4.77B. The margin compression is already visible in the 1.99% net interest margin (2.16% a year earlier).
02
Concentration of control in Thomas Peterffy. Public Class A holders own roughly 27% of the operating company's economics — $6.2B of $23.3B total equity — with $17.0B of noncontrolling interest and effective voting control held by the founder and insiders. Minority shareholders have no practical mechanism to influence capital allocation, dividend policy, strategy or succession, and founder succession remains an unresolved overhang.
03
Correlated cyclical exposure at a growth multiple. DARTs, customer equity and margin loans all contract together in a drawdown, and the margin book — up 36% year over year to $105.2B — is among the most cyclically extended balances on the sheet. At 32.57x trailing earnings and a beta of 1.37, the stock carries both operating and multiple risk into any market correction, as the −24% move from January to March 2025 demonstrated.
  Industry Commentary
The Fed has started raising rates again — and that changes the story
EMERGING TAILWIND
The standard bear thesis on IBKR is NII compression as rates fall. The live data does not currently support it. The Fed cut three consecutive times in September, October and December 2025, reaching a target range of 3.50%–3.75%, held there through the July 2026 meeting (where three FOMC members dissented in favor of raising), and then raised the range by 25 bp to 3.75%–4.00% on September 16, 2026. The next meeting is October 27–28. Against management's disclosed sensitivity of $83 million of annual NII per 25 bp of fed funds, the near-term skew is a tailwind, not a headwind. That said, the sensitivity is symmetric and the underlying compression is real: net interest margin was 1.99% against 2.16% a year earlier, as interest paid on customer cash balances grew. The risk did not disappear — the sensitivity works in both directions.
Balance growth is outrunning spread compression — for now
EMERGING TAILWIND
The mechanism that let IBKR grow NII 19% to $1.154 billion in 3Q2026 while its net interest margin fell 17 basis points (to 1.99% from 2.16%) is pure volume. Margin loans rose 36% to $105.2 billion, customer credits 20% to $186.2 billion, and customer equity 27% to $964.7 billion, on an account base up 35% to 5.58 million. Management estimated total securities lending revenue at $426 million, +36%. This is the correct way to underwrite the business: NII per dollar of balance is a rate call, but the number of dollars is a franchise call, and account growth is still above 30%. The vulnerability is that the two are not independent in a drawdown — margin balances up 36% in a year are a leveraged bet on the equity market as much as a lending book, and they contract fastest when prices fall. The third quarter showed a softer side: DARTs fell to 4.27 million from 4.82 million in the second quarter.
The multiple has re-rated lower — bull vs bear from here
ONGOING HEADWIND
The bull framing: at 32.57x trailing and 26.8x forward earnings (StockAnalysis next-twelve-month basis, restated to the banner-date close), IBKR trades well below where it did in August (38.96x trailing), while growing revenue 18.7% and EPS 26.5% on a trailing basis, with a PEG of 1.22 and a 78% pretax margin. Barclays at $117 and Deutsche Bank at $104 are underwriting continued account compounding and a rate floor. The bear framing: trading volumes are softer than in the second quarter, earnings only matched consensus, and the shares fell 3.88% the day after the print despite a 71% dividend raise; UBS cut its target to $97 on October 20. The resolution is the October 27–28 FOMC meeting and the 4Q2026 print: if rates hold or rise while accounts keep compounding above 30%, the premium is defensible; if the Fed resumes cutting into decelerating account growth, a 57%-NII revenue mix at 33x earnings is an uncomfortable place to be.
