01 Company Overview

ICICI began in 1955 as a development lender set up at the initiative of the World Bank, the Government of India and Indian industry. ICICI Bank was incorporated in 1994 as part of the group, and in 2002 the parent merged into the bank. ICICI itself was the first Indian company to list on the New York Stock Exchange, in 1999; the bank's own ADS followed in March 2000, each worth two ₹2 shares. At September 30, 2026 the bank had ₹18.3 trillion of loans, ₹20.5 trillion of deposits and ₹27.3 trillion of standalone assets (results filing) and 7,656 branches (investor presentation), and by market value it ranks behind HDFC Bank and ahead of State Bank of India at $105.7B. The home line trades on the NSE as ICICIBANK. The bank reports in rupees under Indian GAAP for a year ending March; the latest reported period is Q2 FY2027 (July–September 2026), released on Saturday, October 17, 2026 (board meeting from 9:40 a.m. to 2:15 p.m., analyst call at 5:00 p.m. India time), so the first NYSE session to react was Monday, October 19.

The quarter paired fast growth with a thinner margin. Loans grew 30.0% year on year (24.1% leaving out loans funded by the new foreign-currency deposits explained in Section 02), profit after tax rose 20.1% to ₹148.43bn, about 4.2% above the LSEG estimate of ₹142.51bn that NiftyTrader reported, and the gross NPA ratio fell to 1.20% from 1.58% a year earlier. But the net interest margin fell to 4.18% from 4.36% in Q1 (4.28% once a one-off interest benefit on a tax refund is removed) and 4.30% a year earlier, and CFO Anindya Banerjee said the full effect would show in Q3: "we will see a further moderation in margins," with a recovery expected from Q4. That is a step away from the July call, where margins were guided to stay in a range. The ADR had climbed 6.1% in the two sessions before the print ($28.27 on October 14 to $29.99 on October 16), closed $29.48 on October 19 (−1.70%), and ended October 30 at $29.45 — 7.3% above the previous edition's $27.44, 1.8% below the pre-print close and 14.0% under its May 2025 month-end peak. CEO Sandeep Bakhshi, in the job since October 2018, began a new two-year term on October 4, 2026, approved by shareholders at the August 21 AGM.

FCNR(B) deposits are foreign-currency deposits from non-resident Indians. Under an RBI swap facility the bank can take them in and lend against them, and its international branches have done so: the difference between 30.0% total loan growth and 24.1% without those loans is this book. Bakhshi said on the Q2 call: "The bank has mobilized gross FCNR (B) deposits of about USD 18 billion pursuant to RBI swap facility." The cost is margin: the bank's presentation puts the overseas margin at 1.07% against a domestic margin of 4.32% (4.45% in Q1), which is how a growing dollar book pulls the overall 4.18% down. Within the retail and rural book, farmer finance was ₹323bn (+3.7%) at September 30, beside the ₹558bn of jewel loans (presentation). The RBI-directed ₹12.83bn provision on agricultural priority-sector loans taken in Q3 FY2026 is still held; Banerjee said of a possible write-back, "hopefully, we should have an update on that over the next 2 quarters."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

₹20.5 trillion deposit franchise NIM 4.18% Net NPA 0.30% ₹131bn contingency buffer CET1 16.01% AMC, insurance & broking arms Digital platforms (iLens, InstaBIZ)

ICICI's edge is the combination of a cheap, granular deposit base and underwriting that has kept bad loans low while the book grew. Return on assets was 2.26% in Q2 (annualised; 2.49% in Q1), and it sits on top of a ₹131bn contingency provision — about 0.7% of advances — that is not tied to any loan and is there to absorb a bad year. Bakhshi frames the strategy as growing profit before tax excluding treasury, and the numbers follow it: that measure grew 23.4% to ₹199.54bn, with fee income up 20.1% to ₹77.95bn. The vulnerabilities are the price of that growth and the rules it runs under.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth outrunning the cycle. Loans +30.0% (+24.1% leaving out foreign-currency-funded loans), business banking +33.8%, domestic corporate +30.6%, jewel loans +79.4%. Banerjee said the bank has the capital and funding to keep growing where the risk and reward are attractive: "we have the capital and funding for that." Fast-growing small-business and gold-loan books are where the next credit cycle would show first.
02
Regulatory friction. The RBI-directed ₹12.83bn provision on mis-classified farm loans is still held, with an update promised within two quarters. In late September 2026 the bank received two GST show-cause notices (disclosed September 25 and October 1; the larger arrived September 30) over services to customers maintaining minimum balances — ₹229.1 crore (about ₹2.29bn) plus interest and penalty, and ₹16.8 crore including interest and penalty — small against quarterly profit of ₹148bn, but the same theme of fees under scrutiny.
03
Margin squeeze, now under way. The margin fell 18 basis points (hundredths of a percentage point) in a quarter to 4.18%. Banerjee: "the full impact of these on the average balance sheet will come in Q3, and we will see a further moderation in margins," and "from Q4, I would expect margins to come back in a pretty healthy way." About 57% of domestic loans reset with the RBI repo rate or other external benchmarks, so rate cuts reprice assets faster than deposits.
04
Accounting regime change. India's move to expected-credit-loss (ECL) provisioning will require provisions on Stage 2 loans (loans that have become riskier but are not yet overdue) that, in Banerjee's words on the July call, are "currently not made by any bank". On the one-off hit: "On the net worth, we will not really have any impact." The recurring cost was not quantified.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items shape the Q2 FY2027 comparison. Treasury swung to a loss of ₹2.30bn from a gain of ₹2.20bn a year earlier (₹1.51bn in Q1) — which is why profit before tax excluding treasury (+23.4% to ₹199.54bn) grew faster than reported profit before tax (+20.4% to ₹197.25bn). Margin: Q1's 4.36% included 8 basis points of interest on a tax refund; without it Q1 was 4.28%, so the underlying fall to 4.18% is about 10 basis points, not 18. Provisions were ₹8.51bn against ₹9.14bn a year earlier and ₹12.60bn in Q1; the tax charge was 24.7% of profit before tax against 22.6% in Q1, which had included a ₹4.46bn write-back. The ₹12.83bn RBI-directed farm-loan provision taken in Q3 FY2026 is still held.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The numbers of a well-run bank: 2.26% ROA, 16.6% ROE, NIM 4.18%, net NPAs 0.30%, CET1 16.01% and a ₹131bn buffer that belongs to no loan. Loans grew 30.0% (24.1% leaving out the foreign-currency-funded book) while provisions fell 6.9% year on year, profit beat the LSEG estimate by about 4.2%, and gross NPAs fell to 1.20%. At 17x trailing earnings and 2.6x book it is priced as a quality bank, every one of 42 NSE analysts rates it a buy, the CEO's term is renewed, and LIC has permission to buy up to 9.99%.

Bear Case

The margin fell 18 basis points in one quarter to 4.18%, and management says Q3 will be lower still, with a recovery promised only from Q4. Funding 30% loan growth took about $18B of foreign-currency deposits whose lending earns a margin near 1%, and a majority of domestic loans reprice with the repo rate, so rate cuts bite the margin first. The fastest-growing books — business banking, domestic corporate, gold loans — are the least tested, and the RBI has already found compliance problems in the farm book. Unanimous buy ratings leave little room for upgrades, and for a dollar holder the rupee's 21% five-year fall cut a +74.3% rupee gain to +39.2%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Credit cycle in the fast lanes. Business banking +33.8%, domestic corporate +30.6%, jewel loans +79.4%. Today's credit cost of about 0.2% of loans is far below management's own normal of up to 0.50%. A turn in small-business or gold-loan conditions would show here first.
02
Margin. It fell to 4.18% from 4.36%, management guided to a further fall in Q3, and 57% of domestic loans are linked to external benchmarks. The foreign-currency-funded book earns a far thinner margin than rupee lending.
03
Currency. Earnings and dividends are in rupees; the ADS is in dollars. ₹74.87 → ₹94.95 per USD over five years cost a US holder about 21% before any change in the business — the dollar dividend per ADS fell from $0.257 (August 2025) to $0.249 (August 2026) even as the rupee dividend rose from ₹11 to ₹12 per share.
Industry Commentary
Indian credit growth is back — and the banks are funding it from abroad
EMERGING TAILWIND

ICICI's loans grew 30.0% year on year (24.1% leaving out the foreign-currency-funded book), and on the Q2 call Banerjee rejected the idea that corporate growth was just that money parked temporarily. The RBI's swap facility for FCNR(B) foreign-currency deposits let ICICI raise about $18B, and it issued about $3.55B of US-dollar bonds in July–August. The bull reading: a growth cycle with a funding valve, led by the banks with the cleanest books. The bear reading: funding growth with dollar deposits and dollar bonds adds balance-sheet size faster than margin, and the margin fell 18 basis points in the quarter.

A supervisor that reads the fine print
ONGOING HEADWIND

The RBI's annual supervisory review led to a ₹12.83bn provision against farm loans classed as priority-sector lending on terms that were not fully compliant, and the bank still holds it; tax authorities are pursuing GST on minimum-balance services; and the switch to expected-credit-loss provisioning, discussed on the July call, will raise recurring provisions across the sector by requiring them on Stage 2 loans. For ICICI the sums are small against ₹148bn of quarterly profit. The pattern matters more than any one item: priority-sector farm lending and fee-earning retail accounts are both under regulatory and tax scrutiny.

Competition from state banks
STRUCTURAL

An analyst on the July call noted that public-sector banks have used up much of their liquidity and loan-to-deposit buffers. Banerjee's response was to point to ICICI's own franchise: "Our belief is that our franchise should deliver more," adding that some loans are being quoted at rates ICICI will not match. With State Bank of India at $93.5B of market value, behind ICICI's $105.7B, the contest for corporate and deposit market share is between two models: the state bank's balance sheet against the private bank's pricing discipline.
