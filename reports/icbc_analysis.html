01 Company Overview

ICBC has topped The Banker's ranking of the world's 1,000 biggest banks for fourteen years running. At June 30, 2026 it held RMB57.07 trillion of assets (about US$8.5 trillion at the Oct 2 rate), RMB31.99 trillion of customer loans and RMB39.17 trillion of customer deposits, served through 15,168 outlets to more than 15 million corporate and 780 million personal customers. It earns its living the old-fashioned way: about three-quarters of operating income is net interest income, the gap between what it charges borrowers and what it pays savers. In the first half of 2026 that came to RMB341.2 billion, up 8.8%, and operating income rose 9.1% to RMB446.2 billion. Net profit rose a slimmer 4.5% to RMB176.5 billion (3.3% for shareholders of the parent), because credit losses jumped 22.1%.

The central fact about ICBC is the shape of its margin. The net interest margin fell every year from 2.11% in 2021 to 1.28% in 2025 as China cut lending rates; in the first half of 2026 it held at 1.29% because deposit costs fell faster (1.15% from 1.45%) than loan yields (2.64% from 2.92%). President Liu Jun told the Aug 28 results meeting that this cushion is temporary: "the support from repricing to net interest margin will gradually weaken." The second fact is ownership. Two arms of the Chinese state own 65.9% of the shares, and on Sep 6, 2026 the Bank agreed to sell up to RMB100 billion of new A shares to the Ministry of Finance (RMB70 billion) and China National Tobacco Corporation with four of its provincial companies (RMB30 billion) to top up its core capital — approved by shareholders on Sep 29. The H shares in Hong Kong trade at 0.57x book value and about 6.3x trailing earnings, and still rose 30.1% in the year to Oct 2 while the Hang Seng Index fell 12.1%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

RMB39.2T deposit franchise State shareholders (65.9%) 15,168 outlets 780M+ personal customers Payments & payroll plumbing RMB clearing bank in 12 countries CET1 13.21%

ICBC's moat is cheap, sticky funding at a scale no private competitor can match. RMB39.2 trillion of customer deposits — more than half of them from households — cost it 1.15% on average in the first half, and payroll flows alone exceeded RMB3 trillion in six months. Behind that sits 65.9% state ownership — and in 2026 the state is putting in new capital. The same feature caps the upside. A bank that describes itself as "the main force in serving the real economy" and "the ballast stone in maintaining financial stability" lends where policy points — manufacturing, technology, green and inclusive finance — and prices its loans to the national interest rate cycle, not to maximise its own return on equity.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The margin has lost 82 basis points since 2021. NIM went from 2.11% in 2021 to 1.29% in the first half of 2026; return on equity from 12.15% to 8.63%. The 2026 stabilisation came from deposits repricing lower, and management itself says that support "will gradually weaken" as fewer high-rate time deposits come up for renewal.
02
Household credit is turning. The personal NPL ratio rose 19 basis points in six months and the personal impairment charge rose 4.6-fold. Mortgages — 17.9% of the loan book — saw their NPL ratio rise from 1.06% to 1.29%. Corporate credit is improving, but it is the household book that has been shrinking and souring at the same time.
03
Capital is being drawn down and refilled at a discount. The CET1 ratio slid from 14.10% (end-2024) to 13.57% (end-2025) to 13.21% (June 2026) as risk-weighted assets grew 5.5% in six months. The fix is the RMB100 billion placement, priced off the A-share market — illustratively at RMB7.82 a share, against book value of RMB11.12. Selling new shares below book trims book value per share for existing holders, by about 1% on our calculation.
04
Policy comes first. The Board's outlook lists resolving risks in property, small financial institutions and local government debt among its priorities. When the state needs a big bank to absorb a problem, ICBC is the first call — and the cost of that lands in the income statement before it shows up anywhere else.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 6.3x trailing earnings and 0.57x book value for the bank at the top of The Banker's world ranking, with a 4.7% trailing dividend yield and a payout that rose to 31% at the interim. Revenue growth came back in 2026 (+9.1% in the half, after +1.9% in 2025) because deposit costs are falling faster than loan yields, and the NPL ratio is drifting down with 2.18x coverage. The state is putting RMB70 billion of fresh equity in alongside RMB30 billion from China National Tobacco — about 34 basis points of CET1 on our calculation. Even the lowest target in the S&P Global panel (HK$7.72) is above the current price.

Bear Case

The margin has fallen from 2.11% to 1.29% in five years and management says its 2026 cushion — repricing old deposits lower — is running out. Profit grew 4.5% on revenue growth of 9.1% because credit costs rose 22%, and the household book is where it hurts: personal NPLs up 19 basis points in six months, the segment's profit halved. CET1 has fallen from 14.10% to 13.21% in eighteen months, and the fix is new shares sold at roughly 0.7x book, diluting existing holders. ROE of 8.63% on a bank that must lend where policy points does not obviously deserve more than its current multiple; the 30% rally has already taken the stock to about 4% below its 52-week high.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margin compression resumes. Each basis point of NIM on RMB53.4 trillion of average interest-earning assets is worth about RMB5.3 billion a year of net interest income (our calculation). If loan yields keep falling after deposit repricing is spent, growth in net interest income — 8.8% in the half — stalls.
02
Household credit losses keep climbing. Mortgage NPLs rose from 1.06% to 1.29% and card NPLs from 4.61% to 5.37% in six months, and the personal impairment charge went from RMB13.8 billion to RMB64.3 billion. Another half like that would eat most of the revenue gain.
03
Policy and dilution. The placement adds about 3.6% to the share count at the illustrative price, below book value, and the Board's outlook names property, small financial institutions and local government debt as risks to resolve. H shares are 24.35% of the total; the controlling shareholders are the state.
Industry Commentary
The deposit-repricing cushion is wearing thin
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Chinese bank margins have fallen for years as the central bank cut lending benchmarks to support growth; ICBC's NIM went from 2.11% in 2021 to 1.28% in 2025. In 2026 the decline paused because the cost of deposits fell 30 basis points in a year as multi-year time deposits signed at higher rates rolled over at lower ones. President Liu Jun was explicit at the results meeting that this is a one-time tailwind: "as the volume of existing time deposits reaching maturity declines and the interest-rate gap between old and new products narrows further, the support from repricing to net interest margin will gradually weaken." His stated answer is to "further tap the potential for reducing liability costs, and work to establish a stable trend in net interest margin" — a target, not a number. For a reader, the margin line in the October and next March results is the single best test of whether 2026's revenue recovery lasts.

Recapitalising a state bank — who pays
ONGOING

The Sep 6 placement is the state topping up the capital of a bank it already controls. The Ministry of Finance takes RMB70 billion; China National Tobacco Corporation and four of its provincial companies take RMB30 billion and sign a strategic cooperation agreement. The issue price is pegged to the A-share market (illustratively RMB7.82), well above where the H share trades in renminbi terms but below book value of RMB11.12. The capital gain to the bank is about 34 basis points of CET1; the cost to existing shareholders is about 3.6% more shares and roughly 1% off book value per share, on our calculation from the announcement's illustrative figures. Hong Kong investors marked the stock down 2.47% the next session; it then closed September at HK$7.71, its highest month-end in five years.

The dividend trade on Chinese banks
TAILWIND

ICBC pays out about 30% of profit, now twice a year, and Board Secretary Tian Fenglin told investors the Bank has paid "more than RMB1.64 trillion" in cash dividends since its 2006 listing — "the listed company with the highest cumulative dividend amount in the A-share market." At HK$7.455 the H share yields 4.7% on the past year's payments. The move was sector-wide: all four big state banks' H shares rose between 24% and 39% in the year to Oct 2 while the Hang Seng Index fell 12%. The flip side of a re-rating is a lower starting yield — at the HK$3.41 low of October 2022 a share cost less than half today's price, with a dividend of similar size.
