01 Company Overview

Intercontinental Exchange operates global exchanges, clearing houses, and the data and workflow technology that sits underneath them. It owns the New York Stock Exchange, the ICE Brent crude and Dutch TTF natural gas benchmark complexes, a fixed-income pricing and index franchise, and — through Ellie Mae and Black Knight — the software rails on which a large share of US residential mortgages are originated and serviced. Full-year 2025 net revenues were $9.931 billion, up 7% YoY, which management described as the company's 20th consecutive year of record revenues. Third-quarter 2026 net revenues were $2.765 billion, up 15%, and trailing-twelve-month net revenue (FY2025 less the first nine months of 2025 plus the first nine months of 2026) is $10.91 billion. On July 30, 2026 ICE also agreed to buy MarketAxess, the electronic bond-trading platform, for $167 per share in cash (see Section 06).

At a $94.36 billion market capitalisation (Oct 30 close × 558.86 million shares), ICE ranks #124 in the S&P 500, below CME Group ($108.50B) and well above Nasdaq ($58.95B), MSCI ($44.57B) and Cboe ($35.01B) — all our calculation at the Oct 30 close. One structural point governs every revenue figure on this page: ICE reports both a gross revenue line and a net revenue line after transaction-based expenses — pass-through items such as Section 31 fees, liquidity rebates and routing costs on the NYSE cash-equities business. Only the net figure is comparable across exchange operators, and every segment number here is stated net.

ICE Mortgage Technology is the combination of Ellie Mae/Encompass (the loan origination system, acquired 2020 for ~$11B) and Black Knight (MSP servicing platform, Empower, data and analytics — closed September 2023 for ~$11.7B). Q3 2026 revenue of $549M grew 4%: Servicing Software $229M (+6%), Origination Technology $189M (+1%), Data & Analytics $71M (+6%), Closing Solutions $60M (+4%). The segment's adjusted operating margin was 40%.

The segment's structure matters more than its growth rate. Servicing (MSP) is a per-loan, contractually recurring fee that rises as loans stay on book — it is the ballast. Origination and Closing Solutions are transaction-linked and scale with US mortgage origination volume, which has been depressed since 2022 by high mortgage rates and the resulting reluctance of existing homeowners to move. That means the segment is currently earning close to a trough contribution while carrying the full cost base and intangible amortisation of two large acquisitions. Origination growth slowed to +1% in Q3 (from +5% in Q2) and Closing Solutions to +4% (from +14%), so the share gains seen in Q2 did not repeat.

The Black Knight contribution and its cost. Black Knight is the largest reason ICE carries so much debt: $23.3B on the September 30, 2026 balance sheet (short-term $2.96B plus long-term $20.35B) against $936M of unrestricted cash. That total rose in August 2026 when ICE sold $3.75B of new bonds to help fund the MarketAxess purchase. Debt is also the main source of the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings (see Section 04) through interest and acquisition amortisation.

ICE's moat is unusually literal: it owns the reference prices other people's contracts are written against. ICE's 2025 annual report calls its flagship Brent crude contract the cornerstone of a global oil network of more than 800 related crude and refined oil products, and TTF is the main benchmark for European gas. Once a benchmark is embedded in physical supply contracts, indices and structured products, trading migrates to the venue that clears it and stays there — a competing venue must not only match the fee, it must persuade thousands of counterparties to move open positions simultaneously. The 114 million contracts of record open interest (+20% YoY) reported in January 2026 is the moat expressed as a number: those are committed positions with margin posted at ICE Clear, and unwinding them to move venues is expensive. The same dynamic in a different form governs Mortgage Technology, where Encompass and MSP are the core record-keeping systems built into lenders' and servicers' compliance work; replacement is a multi-year project with regulatory risk attached, which is why servicing revenue grew even through the worst origination environment in a decade. Layered on top, roughly half of revenue is contractually recurring subscription and connectivity income that grows independently of trading activity — and the data those venues generate is a proprietary input ICE resells at very high margin (operating margin 53.2% TTM per StockAnalysis; adjusted 61% in Q3 2026 per ICE).

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Cyclicality dressed as a growth story. Half of revenue is transaction-based and depends on volatility ICE does not control. The last two quarters made this concrete: energy revenue fell 13% to $518M in Q2 because the year-earlier quarter was inflated by Middle East hedging demand, then rose 25% to $603M in Q3. Investors who pay a high price-to-earnings multiple for a business with this swing built in get hurt in the quarters that follow a spike. The stock's recent record is mixed rather than weak: +13.7% over the year to Oct 30, 2026 against +11.2% for the S&P 500 (price only, both), but +4.3% year to date against +10.8%.

2. Mortgage Technology is a leveraged bet on the rate cycle that has not paid off yet. ICE paid roughly $23B combined for Ellie Mae and Black Knight and the segment now produces about $2.2B of revenue a year growing around 4%. Origination Technology revenue of $189M in Q3 2026 (+1%) reflects an origination market suppressed by high rates and homeowners' reluctance to give up cheaper existing mortgages. The acquisition debt is permanent; the revenue recovery is contingent. If mortgage rates stay elevated through 2027, this is about 20% of revenue growing at low-single digits against $23.3B of total debt (which also now includes the MarketAxess financing), and the return on the deal never clears its cost of capital.

3. Regulatory and antitrust exposure across every segment. ICE operates licensed exchanges and systemically important clearing houses in multiple jurisdictions; fee structures, market-data pricing and clearing capital requirements are all live regulatory topics. The Black Knight transaction itself only closed after an FTC review that required divestitures. The MarketAxess purchase has cleared the US antitrust waiting period (early termination on October 21, 2026) and MarketAxess stockholders approved it on October 29, but other approvals are still outstanding. The October 2025 Polymarket investment adds a new and genuinely unsettled regulatory surface: prediction markets sit at the boundary of CFTC event-contract rules and state gaming law.

4. Returns on capital are structurally below peers. ROIC of 8.75% and ROE of 14.40% sit below CME (12.55% / 16.13%) and Nasdaq (10.87% / 17.33%) (StockAnalysis, S&P Global data). The cause is the goodwill and intangibles carried from serial M&A. ICE is a better business than that ROIC implies, but it is a worse capital allocator than a single-venue operator like CME — and a $6.0B equity purchase of MarketAxess adds to the goodwill the market will want to see earn its keep.

Notable One-Off Items

Acquisition amortisation is the dominant recurring "adjustment." In Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.67 versus adjusted $2.02 — a $0.35 per-share gap (the Q2 gap was $0.21), mostly amortisation of intangibles acquired with Black Knight and Ellie Mae plus integration and deal costs. For the first nine months of 2026 GAAP EPS was $5.85 versus adjusted $6.27. The full-year 2025 gap was wider: GAAP EPS $5.77 versus adjusted $6.95, a $1.18 per-share (20%) difference. Q3 2026 operating margin was 51% GAAP versus 61% adjusted. ICE's fourth-quarter guidance shows the same wedge: operating expenses of $1,317–1,327M GAAP versus $1,082–1,092M adjusted, about $235M a quarter (roughly $0.9B a year if that pace held). Investors should decide deliberately which basis they underwrite; the amortisation is non-cash but it is the accounting recognition of $23B of real cash spent on two acquisitions.

FY2021 is not comparable. Reported FY2021 net income of $4,058M exceeded that year's operating income of $3,551M, and FY2021 EPS of $7.18 was followed by $2.58 in FY2022. The distortion is non-operating investment gains, not trading performance. Any five-year EPS CAGR anchored on 2021 is misleading; the clean operating progression is operating income of $3.55B (2021) → $3.73B → $3.97B → $4.43B → $4.98B (2025) on StockAnalysis's (S&P Global) definition. On ICE's own reported basis, operating income was $4.929B in 2025 and about $5.70B over the twelve months to September 2026 (FY2025 $4.929B − nine months 2025 $3.692B + nine months 2026 $4.466B).

Antitrust-driven divestitures. The Black Knight acquisition closed in September 2023 only after an FTC process that required ICE to divest overlapping mortgage assets (Black Knight's Empower loan-origination system and Optimal Blue pricing engine, sold to Constellation under the consent order the FTC proposed on August 31, 2023 and made final on November 3, 2023), which reduced the acquired revenue base relative to the announced deal.

The Polymarket investment is an equity stake, not consolidated revenue. ICE committed up to $2 billion in cash at an approximately $8 billion pre-investment valuation (announced October 7, 2025). It invested $1 billion in October 2025 and a further $600 million on March 27, 2026, and said that with up to $40 million of shares to be bought from existing holders its obligations under the arrangement would be complete. ICE stated the investment was not expected to materially affect 2025 results or capital-return plans. Expect it to show up below operating profit, as ICE's share of Polymarket's results or as changes in the stake's value, which can swing GAAP earnings for reasons unrelated to ICE's own business, and as a data-distribution revenue opportunity rather than a trading-fee one.

MarketAxess is not in these numbers yet. The $167-a-share cash purchase (about $6.0B of equity) has not closed, so none of MarketAxess's revenue or debt is in the figures above. The $3.75B of bonds ICE sold in August 2026 for it are, however, already in the debt total. ICE guides its fourth-quarter share count to 558–563 million and 2026 capital spending to about $1,050M.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Roughly half of ICE's $10.91B of trailing net revenue is contractually recurring, and with the Q3 results management set its FY2026 recurring-growth guidance at 9% for Exchanges and 8% for FIDS. On top of that sits a transaction business with record open interest of 114 million contracts (+20%), energy revenue back to +25% growth, and a mortgage business earning close to its low point that gets a lift if rates fall. You are paying a 20.64 forward P/E (FY2026 adjusted EPS) and a 1.32 PEG — below the exchange peer average — for $5.28B of trailing free cash flow, a $4.0B buyback authorisation, and consensus at Buy with an average target of $192.20 (+13.8%).

Bear Case

ICE has trailed the S&P 500 over five years and year to date, and the reason shows up in the returns: ROIC of 8.75% and ROE of 14.40%, below both CME and Nasdaq, on a balance sheet carrying $23.3B of debt against $936M of cash — before the roughly $6.0B MarketAxess purchase is paid for with more borrowing. The company spent roughly $23B on Ellie Mae and Black Knight to buy a segment now growing about 4%, and the gap between GAAP and adjusted operating expenses runs about $235M a quarter. A 23x trailing multiple on a business growing revenue 9–15% with returns near its cost of capital is not obviously cheap, and Q3's energy jump (+25%) follows a quarter when the same line fell 13%.

01
The mortgage cycle does not turn. Origination Technology revenue of $189M a quarter (+1% in Q3) is being earned in a depressed market; if mortgage rates remain elevated through 2027, about 20% of revenue grows at low-single digits while the $23.3B of debt and the acquisition amortisation remain fixed. This is the risk that converts a hoped-for recovery into a sunk cost.

A calm year in oil and European gas removes the earnings upside the share price assumes, regardless of how good the recurring half looks.

Deal, regulatory and capital-spending risks adding up. ICE runs systemically important clearing houses and licensed exchanges in multiple jurisdictions, has an antitrust history in mortgage technology, has committed up to $2B to Polymarket — a prediction-market platform operating inside an unsettled CFTC/state-gaming perimeter — and has agreed a $6.0B cash purchase of MarketAxess that still needs further approvals and is financed with debt. A serial acquirer with 8.75% ROIC deploying more capital into new areas is the single item most likely to keep the valuation discount in place.
  Industry Commentary
Energy market volatility is the revenue engine — and the comparison problem
CYCLICAL — CUTS BOTH WAYS
Energy was ICE's best line in FY2025 at $2.182B, +16% YoY, and the structural picture keeps improving: record total open interest of 114 million contracts (+20% YoY) as of January 26, 2026, with total oil OI 18.6M (+17%), ICE Brent 7.5M (+27%), EU natural gas 6.3M (+20%) and TTF 5.8M (+20%). Open interest is the deepest measure of franchise health — it is capital committed and margined at ICE Clear.

Q2 2026 energy revenue was $518M, down 13% YoY, because Q2 2025 was inflated by Middle East–driven hedging. Q3 2026 energy revenue was $603M, up 25%, with Brent and gas activity back above the year-earlier level. This is the core interpretive challenge with ICE: the transaction half of the business generates its best revenue in the worst weeks for the world, and every such quarter creates a comparison that must be matched twelve months later. Investors who extrapolate a strong quarter are systematically wrong; investors who extrapolate a weak one are equally wrong in the other direction. The honest read is that the energy franchise is structurally larger than it was a year ago while its quarterly revenue still swings with events.
The mortgage cycle as an embedded call option on lower rates
EMERGING TAILWIND
Mortgage Technology produced $2.101B in FY2025 (+4%) and $549M in Q3 2026 (+4%) — Servicing Software $229M (+6%), Origination Technology $189M (+1%), Data & Analytics $71M (+6%), Closing Solutions $60M (+4%). Read the composition rather than the total. Servicing is a per-loan recurring fee that benefits from loans staying on book, so it has grown right through the downturn. Origination and Closing are transaction-linked and are being earned against a US origination market suppressed since 2022 by high mortgage rates and the reluctance of homeowners with cheap existing mortgages to move.

The option structure is what matters: the platform, the sales force and the integration cost are all already in the base. Extra origination volume is very profitable because it costs little extra to process. The strike price on this option is the mortgage rate; the premium is the $23.3B of total debt and the acquisition amortisation ICE pays every year regardless. It is genuinely valuable and genuinely uncertain, and Q3's +1% origination growth shows the recovery has not arrived.
Exchange consolidation and data monetisation — where the bull and bear cases actually meet
STRUCTURAL — CUTS BOTH WAYS
The industry's twenty-year arc has been exchanges buying data and analytics businesses to convert cyclical trading fees into subscription revenue: ICE bought NYSE, Ellie Mae and Black Knight; Nasdaq bought Adenza; LSEG bought Refinitiv; Deutsche Börse bought SimCorp. ICE is furthest along this path and the results are visible — recurring revenue of $5.056B in FY2025 (50.9% of net revenue), growing 7% in Q3 2026, and a Fixed Income & Data Services segment guided to 8% recurring growth. The Polymarket investment is one expression of exactly this strategy: ICE committed up to $2B and became the institutional distributor of Polymarket's event-driven data, buying a data stream rather than a trading venue. The MarketAxess purchase is another: a $6.0B bet on bond trading, where ICE's own platform earns $34M a quarter.

The bull framing: half the company is now a data business with exchange-quality margins, growing high-single digits with contracted visibility, attached to a benchmark franchise nobody can dislodge — and it trades at a 20.64 forward multiple and a 1.32 PEG while MSCI, a pure data business, trades at 33.02 trailing. The bear framing: that transformation was bought, not built. It cost roughly $23B of acquisitions and left $23.3B of debt, an ROIC of 8.75% below both CME and Nasdaq, and a gap between GAAP and adjusted operating costs of about $235M a quarter. CME has a $108.50B market cap on $6.96B of revenue with a 0.15 debt-to-equity ratio and a 63.5% net margin; ICE has a $94.36B market cap on $10.91B of revenue with 0.80 debt-to-equity and a 38.1% net margin. Whether ICE deserves to close that valuation gap is entirely a question of whether the mortgage business recovers and whether the next acquisition is better than the last two.
