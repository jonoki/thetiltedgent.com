01 Company Overview

Intercontinental Exchange operates global exchanges, clearing houses, and the data and workflow technology that sits underneath them. It owns the New York Stock Exchange, the ICE Brent crude and Dutch TTF natural gas benchmark complexes, a fixed-income pricing and index franchise, and — through Ellie Mae and Black Knight — the software rails on which a large share of US residential mortgages are originated and serviced. Full-year 2025 net revenues were $9.931 billion, up 7% YoY, which management described as the company's 20th consecutive year of record revenues; trailing-twelve-month revenue has since reached $10.56 billion (+9.0%).

At an $88.27 billion market capitalisation, ICE is the 140th largest US-listed company and roughly the 277th largest company globally, sitting just below CME Group ($95.99B) and well above Nasdaq ($53.86B), MSCI ($41.10B) and Cboe ($29.32B). One structural point governs every revenue figure on this page: ICE reports both a gross revenue line and a net revenue line after transaction-based expenses — pass-through items such as Section 31 fees, liquidity rebates and routing costs on the NYSE cash-equities business. Only the net figure is economically meaningful or comparable across exchange operators, and every segment number here is stated net.

ICE Mortgage Technology is the combination of Ellie Mae/Encompass (the loan origination system, acquired 2020 for ~$11B) and Black Knight (MSP servicing platform, Empower, data and analytics — closed September 2023 for ~$11.7B). Q2 2026 revenue of $557M grew 5%: Servicing $226M (+2%), Origination $197M (+5%), Data & Analytics $69M (+6%), Closing Solutions $65M (+14%).

The segment's structure matters more than its growth rate. Servicing (MSP) is a per-loan, contractually recurring fee that rises as loans stay on book — it is the ballast. Origination and Closing Solutions are transaction-linked and scale with US mortgage origination volume, which has been structurally depressed since 2022 by high mortgage rates and the resulting lock-in effect on existing homeowners. That means the segment is currently earning close to a trough contribution while carrying the full cost base and intangible amortisation of two large acquisitions. Closing Solutions' +14% growth is the clearest evidence that ICE is still taking wallet share within a small market.

The Black Knight contribution and its cost. Black Knight is the single largest reason ICE carries $20.53B of total debt against $1.07B of cash — an enterprise value of $107.73B versus an $88.27B market cap. Recurring vs Transaction Revenue — the quality argument
RECURRING 50.7%
TRANSACTION 49.3%
Q2 2026: recurring revenue $1.353B, +8% YoY; transaction revenue $1.313B, +2% YoY. FY2025: recurring $5.056B (+5%, 50.9% of net revenue); transaction $4.875B (+10%, 49.1%).

The mix has held right around half-and-half for two years, but the growth leadership has flipped: in FY2025 transaction revenue grew twice as fast as recurring; in Q2 2026 recurring grew four times as fast. That is the crux of the quality case. Roughly half of ICE's revenue is subscription-like — data feeds, connectivity, index licences, MSP servicing fees, listings annual fees — and does not require volatility to grow. Management's FY2026 guidance calls for high-single-digit Exchange recurring growth and 7–8% FIDS recurring growth. The transaction half is the volatility-levered call option on top.

ICE's moat is unusually literal: it owns the reference prices other people's contracts are written against. Brent underpins the pricing of a large majority of internationally traded crude (a company-stated figure, treat as an estimate), and TTF plays the same role for European gas. Once a benchmark is embedded in physical supply contracts, indices and structured products, liquidity migrates to the venue that clears it and stays there — a competing venue must not only match the fee, it must persuade thousands of counterparties to move open positions simultaneously. The 114 million contracts of record open interest (+20% YoY) reported in January 2026 is the moat expressed as a number: those are committed positions with margin posted at ICE Clear, and unwinding them to move venues is expensive. The same dynamic in a different form governs Mortgage Technology, where Encompass and MSP are systems of record embedded in lender and servicer compliance workflows; replacement is a multi-year project with regulatory risk attached, which is why servicing revenue grew even through the worst origination environment in a decade. Layered on top, roughly half of revenue is contractually recurring subscription and connectivity income that grows independently of trading activity — and the data those venues generate is a proprietary input ICE resells at very high incremental margin (operating margin 52.4% TTM, adjusted 61% in Q2 2026).

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Cyclicality dressed as a growth story. Half of revenue is transaction-based and depends on volatility ICE does not control. Q2 2026 made this concrete: energy revenue fell 13% to $518M purely because the year-ago quarter had been inflated by Middle East hedging demand. A quarter of high volatility pulls forward revenue and creates a comparison that must be lapped. Investors who pay a growth multiple for a business with an embedded volatility beta get hurt in the lapping quarters — which is a fair description of the last twelve months, over which the stock returned −10.5% against the S&P 500's +20.9%.

2. Mortgage Technology is a leveraged bet on the rate cycle that has not paid off yet. ICE paid roughly $23B combined for Ellie Mae and Black Knight and the segment now produces $2.1B of revenue growing 4–5%. Origination revenue of $197M in Q2 2026 reflects an origination market suppressed by high rates and homeowner lock-in. The acquisition debt is permanent; the revenue recovery is contingent. If mortgage rates stay elevated through 2027, this is 21% of revenue compounding at low-single digits against $20.5B of debt, and the return on the deal never clears its cost of capital.

3. Regulatory and antitrust exposure across every segment. ICE operates licensed exchanges and systemically important clearing houses in multiple jurisdictions; fee structures, market-data pricing and clearing capital requirements are all live regulatory topics. The Black Knight transaction itself only closed after an FTC review that required divestitures (historical context — not verified against a live source in this build). The October 2025 Polymarket investment adds a new and genuinely unsettled regulatory surface: prediction markets sit at the boundary of CFTC event-contract rules and state gaming law.

4. Returns on capital are structurally below peers. ROIC of 8.61% and ROE of 14.09% sit below CME (12.16% / 15.80%) and Nasdaq (10.43% / 16.52%). The cause is the goodwill and intangibles carried from serial M&A. ICE is a better business than that ROIC implies, but it is a worse capital allocator than a single-venue operator like CME, and the market is entitled to price that difference.

Notable One-Off Items

Acquisition amortisation is the dominant recurring "adjustment." In Q2 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.69 versus adjusted $1.90 — a $0.21 per-share gap, roughly $118M of after-tax adjustments in a single quarter, overwhelmingly amortisation of intangibles acquired with Black Knight and Ellie Mae plus residual integration costs. The full-year gap is wider: FY2025 GAAP EPS $5.77 versus adjusted $6.95, a $1.18 per-share (20%) difference. Similarly, Q2 2026 operating margin was 52% GAAP versus 61% adjusted, and operating expenses $1.3B GAAP versus $1.0B adjusted. FY2026 expense guidance shows the same wedge: $5.140–5.180B GAAP versus $4.190–4.230B adjusted — an ~$950M annual add-back. Investors should decide deliberately which basis they underwrite; the amortisation is non-cash but it is the accounting recognition of $23B of real cash spent on two acquisitions.

FY2021 is not comparable. Reported FY2021 net income of $4,058M exceeded that year's operating income of $3,551M, and FY2021 EPS of $7.18 was followed by $2.58 in FY2022. The distortion is non-operating investment gains, not trading performance. Any five-year EPS CAGR anchored on 2021 is misleading; the clean operating progression is operating income of $3.55B (2021) → $3.73B → $3.97B → $4.43B → $4.98B (2025) → $5.53B TTM.

Antitrust-driven divestitures. The Black Knight acquisition closed in September 2023 only after an FTC process that required ICE to divest overlapping mortgage assets, which reduced the acquired revenue base relative to the announced deal. This is historical context and was not re-verified from a live source in this build — verify against the 2023 10-K before relying on it.

The Polymarket investment is an equity stake, not consolidated revenue. ICE committed up to $2 billion in cash at an approximately $8 billion pre-money valuation (announced October 7, 2025), with a further ~$600M tranche reported in March 2026 taking the funded amount to roughly $1.64B before completion of the $2B pledge. ICE stated the investment was not expected to materially affect 2025 results or capital-return plans. Expect it to show up as an equity-method or fair-value line that can inject non-operating volatility into GAAP earnings, and as a data-distribution revenue opportunity rather than a trading-fee one.

Litigation: no individually material litigation charge was identifiable from the sources retrieved for this build. Absence of evidence here is not evidence of absence — check the contingencies note of the most recent 10-Q.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 suggests the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 suggests heavy selling and a possible washout. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. ICE closed September 2021 at $114.82 and the August 19, 2026 session at $157.24 — a price gain of +36.9% over five years, or roughly +45% on a total-return basis including dividends. Annualised, that is a 7.78% five-year total return against the S&P 500's 13.39% — the index compounded at nearly double ICE's rate, meaning $100 in ICE became about $145 while $100 in the S&P 500 became about $187. The underperformance is not a recent phenomenon: over ten years ICE compounded at 12.25% versus 15.24% for the index. Over the last twelve months the gap widened sharply — ICE −10.50% against the S&P 500's +20.87%, and year-to-date −2.24% against +13.38%.

The shape of the path matters more than the endpoints. The stock spent 2022 and most of 2023 dead money in a $90–$120 band — the market marking down a company that had just announced an $11.7B mortgage acquisition into the fastest rate-hiking cycle in forty years. It then re-rated almost continuously from the September 2022 low of $90.35 to the July 2025 high close of $184.83, a +105% run driven by record energy activity and a market willing to underwrite the mortgage recovery. The last thirteen months have given roughly half of that back. The June 2026 monthly close of $123.11 — a 17% single-month drawdown from May's $147.85, and within $1.32 of the 52-week low of $121.79 — was the capitulation point; the stock has since recovered 27.7% off that close.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Roughly half of ICE's $10.56B of TTM net revenue is contractually recurring, and that half just accelerated to +8% YoY in Q2 2026 while management guides FIDS recurring revenue to 7–8% and Exchange recurring revenue to high-single digits for FY2026. On top of that sits a transaction business with record open interest of 114 million contracts (+20%) and a mortgage franchise earning near-trough economics that becomes a free call option the moment rates fall. You are paying a 19.05 forward P/E and a 1.46 PEG — below the exchange peer set and below the index — for $5.08B of free cash flow, a $4.0B buyback authorisation, and consensus at Strong Buy with an average target of $185.93 (+18.25%).

Bear Case

ICE has underperformed the S&P 500 over one, five and ten years, and the reason shows up in the returns: ROIC of 8.61% and ROE of 14.09%, below both CME and Nasdaq, on a balance sheet carrying $20.53B of debt against $1.07B of cash. The company spent roughly $23B on Ellie Mae and Black Knight to buy a segment now producing $2.1B growing 4–5%, and the adjustment wedge required to make earnings look clean is ~$950M a year. Energy revenue fell 13% in the most recent quarter. If mortgage rates stay high and energy comparisons stay difficult, a 22x trailing multiple on a business compounding revenue at 7–9% with sub-cost-of-capital returns is not obviously cheap.

Top 3 Risks
01
The mortgage cycle does not turn. Origination revenue of $197M/quarter is being earned in a depressed market; if mortgage rates remain elevated through 2027, 21% of revenue compounds at low-single digits while the $20.5B of acquisition debt and ~$950M of annual amortisation remain fixed. This is the risk that converts a call option into a sunk cost.
02
Volatility normalisation in energy. Q2 2026 energy revenue of $518M was down 13% YoY simply because the prior-year quarter was inflated by geopolitical hedging. Roughly half of group revenue is transaction-linked and structurally unforecastable. A calm year in oil and European gas removes the earnings upside the multiple is discounting, regardless of how good the recurring half looks.
03
Regulatory and capital-allocation risk compounding. ICE runs systemically important clearing houses and licensed exchanges in multiple jurisdictions, has an antitrust history in mortgage technology, and has now committed up to $2B to Polymarket — a prediction-market platform operating inside an unsettled CFTC/state-gaming perimeter with reported ongoing scrutiny. A serial acquirer with 8.61% ROIC deploying $2B into a new regulatory frontier is the single item most likely to keep the valuation discount in place.
  Industry Commentary
Energy market volatility is the revenue engine — and the comparison problem
ONGOING HEADWIND
Energy was ICE's best line in FY2025 at $2.182B, +16% YoY, and the structural picture keeps improving: record total open interest of 114 million contracts (+20% YoY) as of January 26, 2026, with total oil OI 18.6M (+17%), ICE Brent 7.5M (+27%), EU natural gas 6.3M (+20%) and TTF 5.8M (+20%). Open interest is the deepest measure of franchise health — it is capital committed and margined at ICE Clear.

And yet Q2 2026 energy revenue was $518M, down 13% YoY. The reconciliation is that Q2 2025 was distorted upward by Middle East–driven hedging, and lapping it produces a decline even as the underlying position base grows. This is the core interpretive challenge with ICE: the transaction half of the business generates its best revenue in the worst weeks for the world, and every such quarter creates a comparison that must be lapped twelve months later. Investors who extrapolate volatility-quarter revenue are systematically wrong; investors who extrapolate lapping-quarter revenue are equally wrong in the other direction. The honest read is that the energy franchise is structurally larger than it was a year ago and cyclically weaker than it was a year ago at the same time.
The mortgage cycle as an embedded call option on lower rates
EMERGING TAILWIND
Mortgage Technology produced $2.101B in FY2025 (+4%) and $557M in Q2 2026 (+5%) — Servicing $226M (+2%), Origination $197M (+5%), Data & Analytics $69M (+6%), Closing Solutions $65M (+14%). Read the composition rather than the total. Servicing is a per-loan recurring fee that benefits from loans staying on book, so it has grown right through the downturn. Origination and Closing are transaction-linked and are being earned against a US origination market suppressed since 2022 by high mortgage rates and the lock-in effect that keeps existing homeowners with sub-4% mortgages from moving.

The option structure is what matters: the platform, the sales force and the integration cost are all already in the base. Incremental origination volume flows through at very high contribution margin because there is no incremental cost to process it. Closing Solutions growing 14% at trough volumes tells you ICE is still winning share while it waits. The strike price on this option is the mortgage rate; the premium is the $20.5B of debt and ~$950M annual amortisation ICE pays every year regardless. It is genuinely valuable and genuinely uncertain, and the market is currently assigning it close to zero — which is precisely why the forward P/E is 19.05 rather than the mid-20s the recurring-revenue profile would otherwise support.
Exchange consolidation and data monetisation — where the bull and bear cases actually meet
STRUCTURAL — CUTS BOTH WAYS
The industry's twenty-year arc has been exchanges buying data and analytics businesses to convert cyclical trading fees into subscription revenue: ICE bought NYSE, Ellie Mae and Black Knight; Nasdaq bought Adenza; LSEG bought Refinitiv; Deutsche Börse bought SimCorp. ICE is furthest along this path and the results are visible — recurring revenue of $5.056B in FY2025 (50.9% of net revenue) accelerating to +8% in Q2 2026, and a Fixed Income & Data Services segment guided to 7–8% recurring growth. The Polymarket investment is the newest expression of exactly this strategy: ICE committed up to $2B and became the institutional distributor of Polymarket's event-driven data, buying a data stream rather than a trading venue.

The bull framing: half the company is now a data business with exchange-quality margins, growing high-single digits with contracted visibility, attached to a benchmark franchise nobody can dislodge — and it trades at a 19.05 forward multiple and a 1.46 PEG while MSCI, a pure data business, trades at 30.96 trailing. The bear framing: that transformation was bought, not built. It cost roughly $23B of acquisitions and left $20.53B of debt, an ROIC of 8.61% below both CME and Nasdaq, and an annual GAAP-to-adjusted wedge of about $950M. CME built a $95.99B market cap on $6.76B of revenue with a 0.15 debt-to-equity ratio and a 63.4% net margin; ICE assembled an $88.27B market cap on $10.56B of revenue with 0.69 debt-to-equity and a 38.3% net margin. Whether ICE deserves to close that valuation gap is entirely a question of whether the mortgage option pays and whether the next acquisition is better than the last two.
