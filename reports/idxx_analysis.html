01 Company Overview

IDEXX Laboratories is a leading supplier of diagnostics to veterinary clinics for pets — point-of-care blood chemistry, hematology and immunoassay analysers, the consumables those analysers use, a global reference-laboratory network, and the practice-management software that sits underneath it. It also runs a small water-testing business and a livestock, poultry and dairy business. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $4.304 billion, up 10.4% year over year, with net income of $1.059 billion and diluted EPS of $13.08. Over the four quarters to September 2026 (TTM) revenue is $4.66B (+11.9%) and diluted EPS is $14.84.

The central debate is simple to state and hard to resolve. IDEXX runs a razor-and-razorblade model: place an analyser once, then sell the tests that run on it, at high margin, for a decade. The third quarter showed that engine still working — CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue grew 11.0% organically in Q3 2026 (organic means excluding currency moves and acquisitions), and premium instrument placements reached about 5,780 units. But the volume underneath it is still shrinking: US same-store clinical visits fell an estimated 1.8% in Q3 2026, after 1.3% in Q2, and worse than the roughly −1.5% management had assumed for the second half. Growth is coming from more testing per visit, price and a bigger installed base rather than from more pets walking through the door. On November 2 the market sided with the engine: the stock rose 21.9% on the day of the print, from $551.21 to $671.64, and closed at $672.04 on November 3. At 45.3× trailing earnings, the price again assumes the visit decline is a cycle — post-COVID normalisation plus a squeezed consumer — and not a permanent reset. The shares sit 34.2% above the 52-week intraday low of $500.61 (September 11, 2026) and 12.7% below the high of $769.98 (November 26, 2025).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Closed consumable ecosystem
Reference-lab density
Practice software lock-in

Assay menu breadth
Regulatory method approval (Water)

The core advantage is that IDEXX sells a workflow, not a product. A clinic that adopts Catalyst chemistry, ProCyte hematology and inVue Dx cytology, sends its send-out work to the IDEXX reference lab, and runs Cornerstone or ezyVet practice-management software has three separate switching costs stacked on top of each other: proprietary consumables that only run on IDEXX hardware, staff retrained on IDEXX result formats, and patient records living in IDEXX software. That is why ROIC is 47.0% and ROE is 68.9%, and why gross margin was 62.9% in Q3 2026, up 1.1 points on the year. The September purchase of CoVet, which writes up a vet's consultation notes, adds one more piece of software a clinic would have to replace to leave. The razor is sold at modest margin — sometimes placed on subscription — and the blade is sold forever. The vulnerability is that this machine converts visits into revenue, and visits are now shrinking.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Declining US clinical visit volume. Same-store visits fell an estimated 1.8% in Q3 2026, after 1.3% in Q2, against management's second-half assumption of about −1.5%. US diagnostics recurring revenue still grew about 11 points faster than visits (CFO Andrew Emerson on the Q3 call), but that offset from testing, price and menu is finite: if visits decline persistently, per-visit diagnostic intensity has to keep rising just to hold flat, and there is a ceiling on how many tests a veterinarian will run on one animal.
02
Mars' vertical integration. Mars owns Antech (reference labs), Heska (point-of-care analysers), and the VCA and Banfield clinic networks. That combination lets a competitor route diagnostic volume to itself inside owned clinics — a channel IDEXX cannot win on product merit — and it puts a well-capitalised private owner behind a rival platform in both of IDEXX's core delivery models.
03
Valuation risk. At 45.3× trailing and 40.0× 2027 consensus earnings against 9.3–10.0% guided organic growth for 2026, the price assumes years of double-digit earnings growth. A PEG of about 3.2 leaves little cushion. The 21.9% jump on November 2 shows how far the price moves on one quarter's evidence about visits and growth; it can move as far the other way.
04
Leadership transition and execution risk. Mike Erickson became CEO on May 12, 2026, with Jay Mazelsky staying on as Executive Chair until May 2027 (2026 proxy). The new team inherits a business whose growth now comes from price, test mix and new tests rather than more visits — a hard shift to manage without straining the clinic relationships the moat rests on. Its first full-year guide, for 2027, is still to come.

The five-year trajectory is a round trip. From the November 2021 month-end close of $608.07 to the November 3, 2026 close of $672.04, IDXX has gained 10.5% — and it pays no dividend, so that is the total return. Over a similar span revenue grew from $3.22B (FY21) to $4.30B (FY25) and diluted EPS from $8.60 to $13.08: earnings rose 52%, the share price far less. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The sell-the-tests engine is intact and proving it in poor conditions: CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue grew 11.0% organically in Q3 while US clinic traffic fell an estimated 1.8%. About 5,780 premium instrument placements in one quarter — about 1,900 of them inVue Dx — mean test revenue in 2027 and beyond. With 47% ROIC, 69% ROE, $1.30B of free cash flow and $804.7M of buybacks in the first nine months of 2026, the business earns far more on its capital than most companies, and management raised its revenue and EPS guidance again, to $14.96–$15.16. If visits merely stabilise, earnings growth can catch up with a 45.3× trailing P/E that is already well below the roughly 71× FY2021 earnings the shares fetched in late 2021.

Bear Case

Growth is coming from price and test mix on a shrinking volume base, and visits fell faster in Q3 (−1.8%) than in Q2 (−1.3%). After a 21.9% one-day jump the stock trades at 45.3× trailing earnings and a PEG of about 3.2 — a price that assumes years of double-digit growth from roughly 10% organic revenue growth. The 2026 operating-margin guide was trimmed to 32.1–32.3%. Mars owns Heska's point-of-care analysers, Antech's reference labs and thousands of VCA and Banfield clinics that can keep their testing in-house. And a CEO in the job since May has yet to give his first full-year guide.

Top 3 Risks

01
Structural, not cyclical, visit decline. If US same-store visits settle into a persistent −1% to −2% annual run rate, IDEXX must find 11–12 points of price, mix and share gain every year just to deliver 10% recurring growth. That is achievable for a few years and very hard for a decade.
02
A lower P/E. At 40.0× 2027 consensus earnings and a PEG of about 3.2, a fall to 25× earnings on the midpoint of 2026 guidance (about $15.06) implies roughly $377 per share — about 44% below the current price — with no damage to the business required. The price paid is the main risk.
03
Mars keeps testing inside its own clinics. Heska plus Antech plus VCA and Banfield gives one private competitor an integrated stack from analyser to clinic. Diagnostic volume inside those clinics is effectively unwinnable, and it caps IDEXX's addressable share of a market that is no longer growing on volume.

The post-COVID pet-visit hangover
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2020–2021 pet-adoption surge pulled a cohort of animals into the veterinary system all at once and inflated visit volumes far above trend. Those animals are now mid-life, and the households that acquired them are absorbing higher costs of living — on the Q2 call IDEXX's CFO linked weak wellness visits to inflation and to gas and housing costs. Same-store US visits fell an estimated 1.8% in Q3 2026, after 1.3% in Q2, against management's assumption of about −1.5% for the second half — a worsening, not a recovery. Whether that resolves as a pure affordability cycle or as a permanent reset in how often a pet sees a vet is the question the entire animal-health sector is now priced against.
Consolidation of the diagnostic channel under Mars
ONGOING HEADWIND
Mars' animal-health arm has assembled the only credible end-to-end alternative to IDEXX: Antech in reference labs, Heska in point-of-care instruments, and VCA and Banfield in clinic ownership. That vertical structure removes a meaningful slice of the market from competitive bidding and gives a well-capitalised private owner the ability to price instruments aggressively and recover economics downstream. IDEXX's countermeasure is depth rather than breadth — an assay menu and software layer that corporate groups find expensive to unwind — software, services and imaging grew 9.9% organically in Q3 2026, in line with the reference labs at 10.2%, and the September CoVet purchase adds AI note-taking to that software layer. The software is not a side business; it is what keeps clinics from leaving.
Diagnostic intensity per visit — the bull/bear fulcrum
EMERGING TAILWIND
The fact that links the two cases is arithmetic: in the US, recurring diagnostics revenue grew 9.6% in Q3 2026 while visits fell an estimated 1.8%, which means about 11 points of growth came from what happens inside each visit (CFO Andrew Emerson put the gap at about 1,100 basis points, or 11 percentage points, on the Q3 call) — more panels ordered, a richer menu, higher price, and new tests like inVue Dx cytology that did not exist as a point-of-care test a few years ago. The bull reading is that vets testing more per pet is a long-running trend with years to run: an ageing pet population, about 5,780 premium placements in Q3 expanding the base, and international CAG recurring revenue growing 13.6% organically from a lower starting level. The bear reading is that testing per visit cannot rise forever to cover falling volume, and that once new tests stop adding growth the growth rate drifts toward the visit trend — at which point 45.3× trailing earnings is a hard price to defend. Reported revenue growth eased from 14.3% in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 to 9.7% in Q2 and held at 9.9% in Q3 2026; whether it holds near 10% is what the next prints will show.
