01 Company Overview

IDEXX Laboratories is the dominant supplier of diagnostics to the companion-animal veterinary industry — point-of-care blood chemistry, hematology and immunoassay analysers, the consumables those analysers consume, a global reference-laboratory network, and the practice-management software that sits underneath it. It also runs a small but very high-quality water-testing franchise and a livestock/poultry/dairy business. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $4.304 billion, up 10.4% year over year, with net income of $1.059 billion and diluted EPS of $13.08. On a TTM basis revenue is $4.55B (+12.8%) and EPS is $14.22. Global market-cap rank is roughly #619.

The central debate is simple to state and hard to resolve. IDEXX runs a textbook razor/razorblade model: place an analyser once, harvest consumable revenue at high margin for a decade. That engine is still working — CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue grew 10.3% organically in Q2 2026. But the volume underneath it is shrinking: US same-store clinical visits fell an estimated 1.3% in Q2 2026, and management guided to roughly −1.5% in the second half. Every dollar of growth is now coming from price, test mix and installed-base expansion rather than more pets walking through the door. If the visit decline is cyclical — post-COVID normalisation plus a squeezed consumer — the compounding story survives and a 37.6× trailing multiple is defensible. If it is structural, IDEXX is a mid-single-digit volume business wearing a premium-compounder valuation, and the 29% drawdown from the November 2025 high is the market beginning to reprice it. The stock closed at $535.38, within 3.2% of its 52-week low.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Closed consumable ecosystem
Reference-lab density
Practice software lock-in
Multi-year clinic contracts
Assay menu breadth
Regulatory method approval (Water)

The core advantage is that IDEXX sells a workflow, not a product. A clinic that adopts Catalyst chemistry, ProCyte hematology and inVue Dx cytology, sends its send-out work to the IDEXX reference lab, and runs Cornerstone or ezyVet practice-management software has three separate switching costs stacked on top of each other: proprietary consumables that only run on IDEXX hardware, staff retrained on IDEXX result formats, and patient records living in IDEXX software. That is why ROIC is 47.2% and ROE is 74.2%, and why gross margin ran at roughly 64% on a comparable basis in Q2 2026. The razor is sold at modest margin — sometimes placed on subscription — and the blade is sold forever. The vulnerability is that this machine converts visits into revenue, and visits are now shrinking.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Declining US clinical visit volume. Same-store visits fell an estimated 1.3% in Q2 2026 and are guided to roughly −1.5% in the second half. IDEXX is offsetting this with utilisation, price and menu, but the offset is finite: if visits decline persistently, per-visit diagnostic intensity has to keep rising just to hold flat, and there is a ceiling on how many tests a veterinarian will run on one animal.
02
Mars' vertical integration. Mars owns Antech (reference labs), Heska (point-of-care analysers), and the VCA and Banfield clinic networks. That combination lets a competitor route diagnostic volume to itself inside owned clinics — a channel IDEXX cannot win on product merit — and it puts a well-capitalised private owner behind the #2 platform in both of IDEXX's core delivery models.
03
Valuation compression risk. At 37.6× trailing and 34.2× forward earnings against 8.5–9.7% guided organic growth, the multiple embeds an assumption of durable double-digit compounding. A PEG of 2.47 leaves almost no cushion. The stock has already lost roughly 29% from its November 2025 closing peak with earnings still growing — that is pure de-rating, and de-ratings can run further than the fundamentals justify.
04
Leadership transition and execution risk. A CEO handoff completed in May 2026 and the EVP of Global Strategy and Commercial role was eliminated in April 2026. New management inherits a business where the growth algorithm has shifted from volume to price and mix — the hardest kind of transition to execute without disturbing the customer relationship that the whole moat rests on.

The five-year trajectory is a round trip and then some. From the October 2021 close of $666.14 to the September 4, 2026 close of $535.38, IDXX has lost 19.6% — and it pays no dividend, so that is the total return. Over the same window, revenue grew from $3.22B (FY21) to $4.30B (FY25) and EPS from $8.60 to $13.08. Earnings rose 52%; the share price fell 20%. That entire gap is multiple compression: from roughly 55× trailing at the 2021 peak to 37.6× today. This has never been a story about the business breaking. It is a story about what a market is willing to pay for high-quality, decelerating growth once the discount rate stops being zero.

Within that, there have been two genuine cycles. The 2022 bear market took the stock from $666 to $325.80 by September 2022 — a 51% drawdown — as rate-driven de-rating hit every high-multiple compounder simultaneously. It then round-tripped back above $750 by November 2025 on the strength of the inVue Dx launch and reaccelerating CAG recurring growth. The 2026 decline is different in character: it has come with good earnings, driven by the visit-volume guidance and the leadership transition rather than by rates. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The razorblade annuity is intact and proving it in the worst possible conditions: CAG Diagnostics recurring revenue grew 10.3% organically while US clinic traffic fell 1.3%. Nearly 5,300 premium instrument placements in a single quarter — 1,600 of them inVue Dx — are pre-loaded consumable revenue for 2027 and beyond. With 47.2% ROIC, 74.2% ROE, $1.25B of free cash flow and a 2.4% annual reduction in share count, this is one of the highest-quality compounders in healthcare, now available at 34× forward earnings versus the 55× it commanded in 2021. If visits merely stabilise, the multiple has a long way back up.

Bear Case

Growth is being manufactured from price and test mix on a shrinking volume base, and management itself guides visits lower in the second half. Organic growth has already stepped down to ~9%, with a 2.47 PEG at 37.6× trailing earnings — you are paying a compounder multiple for mid-single-digit real volume. Mars now owns both the #2 point-of-care platform (Heska) and the #2 reference-lab network (Antech), plus thousands of clinics that can route their own diagnostics. Add a fresh CEO and an eliminated strategy role, and the stock has already fallen 29% from its peak while earnings rose — the market is telling you the multiple, not the model, was the mistake.

Top 3 Risks

01
Structural, not cyclical, visit decline. If US same-store visits settle into a persistent −1% to −2% annual run rate, IDEXX must find 11–12 points of price, mix and share gain every year just to deliver 10% recurring growth. That is achievable for a few years and very hard for a decade.
02
Further multiple compression. At 34.2× forward and a PEG of 2.47, a de-rating to 25× forward earnings on FY26 guidance of ~$14.80 implies roughly $370 per share — about 31% below the current price — with no deterioration in the business required. The valuation is the position risk.
03
Mars channel foreclosure. Heska plus Antech plus VCA and Banfield gives one private competitor an integrated stack from analyser to clinic. Diagnostic volume inside those clinics is effectively unwinnable, and it caps IDEXX's addressable share of a market that is no longer growing on volume.

Industry Commentary

The post-COVID pet-visit hangover
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2020–2021 pet-adoption surge pulled a cohort of animals into the veterinary system all at once and inflated visit volumes far above trend. Those animals are now mid-life, and the households that acquired them are absorbing higher costs of living — IDEXX's CFO explicitly linked wellness-visit softness to "inflationary dynamics and challenges with gas and housing prices." Same-store US visits fell an estimated 1.3% in Q2 2026, and guidance is for approximately −1.5% in the second half — a deterioration, not a recovery, in the trend. The industry is running its third consecutive year of negative-to-flat traffic. Whether that resolves as a pure affordability cycle or as a permanent reset in how often a pet sees a vet is the question the entire animal-health sector is now priced against.
Consolidation of the diagnostic channel under Mars
ONGOING HEADWIND
Mars' animal-health arm has assembled the only credible end-to-end alternative to IDEXX: Antech in reference labs, Heska in point-of-care instruments, and VCA and Banfield in clinic ownership. That vertical structure removes a meaningful slice of the market from competitive bidding and gives a well-capitalised private owner the ability to price instruments aggressively and recover economics downstream. IDEXX's countermeasure is depth rather than breadth — an assay menu and software layer that corporate groups find expensive to unwind — which is why veterinary software and imaging grew ~12% organically in Q2 2026, ahead of the reference-lab line at ~10%. The software attach is not a side business; it is the retention mechanism.
Diagnostic intensity per visit — the bull/bear fulcrum
EMERGING TAILWIND
The reconciling fact between the two cases is arithmetic: recurring revenue grew 10.3% organically while visits fell 1.3%, which means roughly 11–12 points of growth came from what happens inside each visit — more panels ordered, richer menu, higher price, new modalities like inVue Dx cytology that simply did not exist as a point-of-care test three years ago. The bull reading is that this is a secular medicalisation trend with years to run: an ageing installed pet population, 5,265 premium placements a quarter expanding the base, and international CAG recurring growing ~12% against a far lower penetration baseline. The bear reading is that intensity per visit is a finite resource being consumed to paper over volume decline, and that once menu expansion laps itself the growth rate converges to the visit trend — at which point 37.6× trailing earnings is not a defensible price. Six quarters of decelerating reported growth (14.3% in Q4 2025 to 9.7% in Q2 2026) is currently more consistent with the bear reading than the bull one.
