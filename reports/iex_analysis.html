01 Company Overview

IDEX is a collection of small, specialised engineering businesses that make the parts nobody sees and everybody needs: pumps and metering equipment, precision fluidics, filtration, fire and rescue equipment, band clamps and dispensing machines. It started in 1988, when Kohlberg Kravis Roberts bought several divisions of Houdaille Industries, and it has grown ever since by buying more niche businesses — the largest recent one being Mott, a filtration business bought for about $1 billion in 2024. It reports three segments: Health & Science Technologies (HST), Fluid & Metering Technologies (FMT) and Fire & Safety/Diversified Products (FSDP). FY2025 revenue was $3.46B, up 5.8%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $6.41.

For most of the last three years this was a waiting room. Revenue went sideways — $3.18B in FY2022, $3.27B in FY2023, $3.27B in FY2024 — and the stock slid from a $244.02 month-end in March 2024 to $162.76 in September 2025, a 33% drawdown in a business that never lost money. Then the orders turned. In Q2 2026 IDEX booked a record $1.07B of orders, up 28% organically, led by a 47% jump in HST orders tied to data centers, semiconductors and space & defense. The Q3 results, released October 28, 2026, showed the orders turning into sales: sales of $951.7M, up 8% organically (Q2: +5%), orders of $991.2M (+13%), adjusted EPS of $2.33 (+15%) and a third guidance raise in a row. HST organic sales grew 13%, which the release attributes mainly to AI-related demand for data center power and semiconductors. An order is a promise and revenue is a delivery; in Q3 more of the promise was delivered, while order growth cooled from Q2's pace. The stock closed October 30, 2026 at $233.84, up 43.7% from the September 2025 month-end low ($233.84 ÷ $162.76 − 1). The central question: is this an AI-infrastructure growth business wearing an industrial's clothes, or a cyclical order bulge that the market has already paid 32x trailing earnings for?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Designed-in components Replacement & consumable revenue Niche market positions Growth by acquisition 44.9% gross margin 16 years of dividend growth

The moat is smallness in the right place. An IDEX valve or filter is a tiny share of the cost of the instrument, tool or truck it sits in, but once it is designed in, swapping it means re-qualifying the whole machine. That is why a company making pumps and clamps earns a 44.9% gross margin and a 21.4% operating margin. The replacement side deepens it: management says about 50% of HST's semiconductor revenue is now recurring, helped by the growth of Mott's filtration business. The vulnerability is that niche positions do not create growth on their own — FY2023 and FY2024 revenue were both about $3.27B — so growth has to be bought (acquisitions) or borrowed from a hot end market.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The growth is concentrated in one cycle. Data centers, semiconductors and space & defense were more than a third of HST revenue at the Q2 call, and HST again produced most of Q3's growth ($52.5M of $72.9M). Spending on chip plants and data centers is famous for lumpy orders; a 47% order jump (Q2) can reverse just as fast, and group order growth already cooled to +13% in Q3.
02
More than half the company grows more slowly. FMT (+4% organic, mostly from price) and FSDP (+6%) together are 54.5% of Q3 segment sales, against HST's +13%. Lower volumes in chemicals, agriculture and energy sit in FMT.
03
Acquisition-driven model carries leverage and integration risk. Borrowings were $1.75B against $602.1M of cash at September 30, 2026 (Q3 release); StockAnalysis's total debt of $1.78B gives net debt of about $1.18B, or 1.71x debt/EBITDA. Mott cost about $1 billion. Buying growth works until a deal disappoints.
04
The valuation already assumes it works. At 32.5x trailing GAAP EPS and a 2.02 PEG, the stock is priced for the order surge to become sales and margin, which Q3 began to show. Another good quarter is needed to hold the price.

Notable One-Off Items

Two things to know when reading the earnings. First, GAAP versus adjusted: Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.95 and adjusted was $2.33 — a gap of $0.38 a quarter, so the $8.91–$8.96 FY2026 guide is well above what GAAP will show (the forward P/E above is on the adjusted consensus; the trailing P/E is GAAP). Second, Q3 carried $3.4M of restructuring expenses and asset impairments, $2.2M of it in HST, where the release ties the restructuring-related charges to the expected closure of a facility. Q2 had included a $0.08 per share net benefit from refunds of tariffs collected under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). The company repurchased $74.6M of stock in Q3 ($77M in Q2) and paid $54.0M in dividends — at that buyback pace, roughly 1.7% of the market cap a year.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Orders lead sales, and in Q3 sales caught up: +8% organic, with orders of $991.2M (+13%) still 1.04x sales. The growth is in HST, where adjusted EBITDA margin rose 110bps to 28.8%, and all three segments grew organically, the two slow ones faster than in Q2. Management raised guidance for the third quarter running: FY2026 adjusted EPS of $8.91–$8.96 and organic sales growth of 6.5–6.8%. If HST's data-center, semiconductor and space & defense exposure keeps growing while FMT and FSDP hold their Q3 pace, the $8.95 FY2026 EPS consensus rises toward $9.76 for 2027, and today's 26x forward multiple looks reasonable for a business with a 45% gross margin, 18% FCF margin and 16 straight years of dividend increases. Every analyst on StockAnalysis's panel rates it Buy or Hold.

Bear Case

You are paying 32.5x trailing GAAP earnings and a 2.02 PEG for a company whose FY2025 GAAP EPS ($6.41) was lower than FY2022's ($7.71). The growth is still concentrated: HST supplied about 72% of Q3's dollar sales growth, while FMT's +4% was mostly price. A third of HST was tied to data-center, semiconductor and space & defense demand at the Q2 call, the most cyclical corner of the market, and order growth has already cooled from +28% in Q2 to +13% in Q3, with book-to-bill down from 1.16x to 1.04x. Net debt is about $1.18B, growth has historically come from acquisitions, and adjusted EPS runs about $0.38 a quarter above GAAP. The stock is 43.7% off its low and 4.1% below its 52-week high, priced for the good news to continue.

Top 3 Risks

01
HST order reversal. The rally rests on HST's order surge. Group order growth already cooled to +13% in Q3 from +28% in Q2; if Q4 orders fall back further, the market will read Q2 as a one-off and the stock has room to fall.
02
Industrial softness spreading. Lower volumes in chemicals, agriculture and energy already weigh on FMT, and FMT's Q3 growth was mostly price. A broader industrial slowdown hits the two segments that make up 54.5% of the sales base.
03
Valuation leaves little room. 32.5x trailing, 26.1x forward on adjusted estimates, and a 3.9% free-cash-flow yield. The lowest analyst target ($228) is only 2.5% below the price, so even the bears aren't forecasting much downside — which is exactly when a miss hurts most.
Industry Commentary
AI infrastructure reaches the component makers
KEY DRIVER

Data-center and semiconductor spending usually shows up first in chips and servers. IDEX shows it one layer down: filters, fluidics and precision parts for power and cooling systems and for chip-making tools. By Q4 2025 the company was pointing to rising HST orders from AI-related projects, including data-center power solutions and semiconductor applications; at the Q2 2026 call those markets plus space & defense were more than a third of HST revenue; and in the Q3 release HST's 13% organic sales growth was attributed mainly to AI-related demand for data center power and semiconductors. For a component supplier, the benefit arrives as orders first, sales a few quarters later — the pattern in the Q2 orders and Q3 sales.

Industrial demand outside AI is mixed
ONGOING HEADWIND

Outside the AI-linked niches, the Q3 2026 release names lower volumes in chemical, agriculture and energy markets in FMT, offset by higher volumes in municipal water, semiconductor and industrial markets; price, not volume, was the main named driver of FMT's organic growth, and price/cost headwinds weighed on gross and FSDP margins. Tariffs were a factor too: Q2 included an $0.08 per share benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds, a reminder that trade policy is moving IDEX's reported earnings in both directions.

The valuation question
DEBATE

At 32.5x trailing GAAP earnings and 26.1x forward adjusted estimates, IEX is priced as a steady, high-quality business rather than a cyclical one. The bull framing: the business has always had high margins, and it finally has a growth engine to match; paying up for rising margins and a 1.04x book-to-bill is paying for visible earnings. The bear framing: the same stock traded at $162.76 in September 2025 on similar margins, and the difference is one cycle's worth of orders. How to tell which is right: watch HST sales and orders and FMT volume growth over the next two to three quarters. If both hold, the price-to-earnings ratio is earned; if HST fades and FMT stays mostly price, it isn't.
