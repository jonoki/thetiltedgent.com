01 Company Overview

IDEX is a collection of small, specialised engineering businesses that make the parts nobody sees and everybody needs: pumps and metering equipment, precision fluidics, filtration, fire and rescue equipment, band clamps and dispensing machines. It started in 1988, when Kohlberg Kravis Roberts bought several divisions of Houdaille Industries, and it has grown ever since by buying more niche businesses — the largest recent one being Mott, a filtration business bought for about $1 billion in 2024. It reports three segments: Health & Science Technologies (HST), Fluid & Metering Technologies (FMT) and Fire & Safety/Diversified Products (FSDP). FY2025 revenue was $3.46B, up 5.8%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $6.41.

For most of the last three years this was a waiting room. Revenue went sideways — $3.18B in FY2022, $3.27B in FY2023, $3.27B in FY2024 — and the stock slid from a $244.02 month-end in March 2024 to $162.76 in September 2025, a 33% drawdown in a business that never lost money. Then the orders turned. In Q2 2026 IDEX booked a record $1.07B of orders, up 28% organically, led by a 47% organic jump in HST orders tied to data centers, semiconductors and space & defense — markets that management says are now more than a third of HST revenue. Sales lagged at +5% organic, which is normal: an order is a promise, revenue is a delivery. The stock closed September 21, 2026 at $222.53, up 36.7% from the September 2025 low. The central question: is this an AI-infrastructure growth business wearing an industrial's clothes, or a cyclical order bulge that the market has already paid 32x trailing earnings for?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Spec-in components Replacement & consumable revenue Niche market positions Serial acquirer playbook 44.7% gross margin 16 years of dividend growth

The moat is smallness in the right place. An IDEX valve or filter is a tiny share of the cost of the instrument, tool or truck it sits in, but once it is designed in, swapping it means re-qualifying the whole machine. That is why a company making pumps and clamps earns a 44.7% gross margin and a 21.1% operating margin. The replacement side deepens it: management puts Mott's recurring revenue at about 50%. The vulnerability is that niche positions do not create growth on their own — FY2023 and FY2024 revenue were both about $3.27B — so growth has to be bought (acquisitions) or borrowed from a hot end market.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The growth is concentrated in one cycle. Data centers, semiconductors and space & defense are now more than a third of HST revenue, and HST produced nearly all of Q2's growth. Semiconductor and data-center capex is famous for lumpy orders; a 47% order jump can reverse just as fast.
02
Two-thirds of the company is flat. FMT (+1% organic) and FSDP (−1%) together are 55% of Q2 segment sales. Weakness in agriculture, chemicals, energy, dispensing, European rescue and China all sit here.
03
Acquisition-driven model carries leverage and integration risk. Total debt is $1.89B against $621.3M of cash — net debt of $1.26B, or 1.87x debt/EBITDA. Mott cost about $1 billion. Buying growth works until a deal disappoints.
04
The valuation already assumes it works. At 32.0x trailing GAAP EPS and a 2.04 PEG, the stock is priced for the order surge to become sales and margin. A good quarter is needed just to hold the price.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Orders lead sales, and orders just posted a record $1.07B, up 28% organically, a 1.16x book-to-bill. The growth is in HST, the segment where margins are rising fastest (+270bps), and the CFO says the growth mix is gross-margin accretive — Q2 gross margin was 46.3%. Management raised every line of 2026 guidance and is raising capex to meet demand. If HST's data-center, semiconductor and space & defense exposure keeps compounding while FMT and FSDP simply stop shrinking, the $8.83 FY2026 EPS consensus rises toward $9.63 for 2027 and today's 25x forward multiple looks reasonable for a business with a 44–46% gross margin, 18% FCF margin and 16 straight years of dividend increases. Every analyst on StockAnalysis's panel rates it Buy or Hold.

Bear Case

You are paying 32x trailing GAAP earnings and a 2.04 PEG for a company whose FY2025 GAAP EPS ($6.41) was lower than FY2022's ($7.71). The growth is narrow: HST did the work while FMT (+1%) and FSDP (−1%) — 55% of sales — stood still. A third of HST is now tied to data-center and semiconductor capex, the most cyclical corner of the market, and a 47% order jump can be a pull-forward as easily as a new level. Net debt is $1.26B, growth has historically come from acquisitions, and adjusted EPS runs about $0.39 a quarter above GAAP. The stock is 36.7% off its low and priced for the good news to continue.

Top 3 Risks

01
HST order reversal. The rerating rests on HST's +47% order quarter. If Q3 orders fall back sharply, the market will read Q2 as a one-off and the multiple has room to fall.
02
Industrial softness spreading. Agriculture, chemicals, energy, dispensing, European rescue and China are already weak in FMT and FSDP. A broader industrial slowdown hits the two segments that make up most of the sales base.
03
Valuation leaves little room. 32.0x trailing, 25.2x forward on adjusted estimates, and a 3.9% free-cash-flow yield. The lowest analyst target ($215) is only 3.4% below the price, so even the bears aren't forecasting much downside — which is exactly when a miss hurts most.
Industry Commentary
AI infrastructure reaches the component makers
KEY DRIVER

Data-center and semiconductor spending usually shows up first in chips and servers. IDEX shows it one layer down: filters, fluidics and precision parts for power and cooling systems and for chip-making tools. By Q4 2025 the company was pointing to rising HST orders from AI-related projects, including data-center power solutions and semiconductor applications; by Q2 2026 those markets plus space & defense were more than a third of HST revenue. For a component supplier, the benefit arrives as orders first, sales a few quarters later — which is exactly the pattern in the Q2 numbers.

Short-cycle industrial demand is still soft
ONGOING HEADWIND

Outside the AI-linked niches, the Q2 2026 call listed weakness in agricultural, chemical, energy, dispensing, European rescue and China markets, with strength in water, mining and India. Price, not broad volume, was the main named driver of FMT's Q4 2025 organic growth. Tariffs were a factor too: Q2 included an $0.08 per share benefit from IEEPA tariff refunds, a reminder that trade policy is moving IDEX's reported earnings in both directions.

The valuation question
DEBATE

At 32.0x trailing GAAP earnings and 25.2x forward adjusted estimates, IEX is priced as a quality compounder rather than a cyclical. The bull framing: the business has always had compounder margins, and it finally has a growth engine to match; paying up for a 1.16x book-to-bill and rising margins is paying for visible earnings. The bear framing: the same stock traded at $162.76 a year ago on the same margins, and the difference is one cycle's worth of orders. How to tell which is right: watch HST orders and FMT organic growth over the next two to three quarters. If both hold, the multiple is earned; if HST fades and FMT stays flat, it isn't.
