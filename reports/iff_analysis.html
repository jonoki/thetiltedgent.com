01 Company Overview

You have never bought anything from IFF, and you have used its products today. It designs the smell of the detergent, the flavour system inside the soft drink, the enzymes in the washing powder and the probiotic in the yoghurt — sold not to you but to the consumer-goods companies whose names are on the label. FY2025 revenue was $10.89B, down from $11.48B in FY2024 as businesses were sold, and the year ended in a GAAP net loss of $361M (EPS −$1.41), driven by a $1.15B goodwill impairment on Food Ingredients booked in Q1 2025. At $84.41 the company is worth $21.14B, ranking #392 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation.

The third quarter, reported after the close on November 3, 2026 (call on November 4): continuing sales were $1.94B, up 4% reported and 5% on a comparable currency-neutral basis (excluding exchange-rate moves and divested businesses), and adjusted operating EBITDA was $403M (20.8% margin), up 6% comparable currency neutral. Adjusted EPS excluding amortisation was $0.83 against consensus of $0.81 on the Nasdaq calendar ($0.82 in another source). Reported (GAAP) EPS from continuing operations was a loss of $0.50, after a $236M loss on assets held for sale and a $23M goodwill impairment, both on the Botanicals & Food Enhancements business that IFF is selling. IFF raised the low end of its 2026 ranges, but CEO Erik Fyrwald said fourth-quarter profit should be softer, mainly in Health & Biosciences, because of higher costs and reinvestment. On the call, CFO Michael DeVeau said the fourth-quarter margin should fall from the third, partly because of seasonality as well as those costs. The shares fell 4.9% on November 4 ($92.18 → $87.67, close to close); 247 Wall St. attributed the after-hours drop to the fourth-quarter warning.

The story here is a company shrinking on purpose. IFF built itself into a giant through two big deals — Frutarom for $7.1B in 2018 and the $26.2B merger with DuPont's Nutrition & Biosciences unit, completed February 1, 2021 — and spent the next five years taking the result apart. Pharma Solutions went to Roquette on May 1, 2025 at an enterprise value of up to $2.85B (~13x EBITDA). On May 29, 2026 IFF agreed to sell Food Ingredients — texturants, emulsifiers, plant-based and other specialty ingredients — to funds advised by CVC Capital Partners for about $4.3B (~10x EBITDA), keeping a ~10% stake. What remains is three businesses: Taste, Scent and Health & Biosciences, guided to $7.5–7.6B of 2026 sales after the third quarter (the range was $7.4–7.6B before it). The central question: is the smaller IFF a better business at a fair price, or the same leverage problem with fewer levers left to pull?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Specification lock-in Formula libraries & sensory know-how Industrial fermentation scale Concentrated global industry Small cost in customer's product Regulatory dossiers

The economics of flavours and fragrances rest on one asymmetry: the ingredient is a tiny share of the customer's cost, and a large share of the customer's risk. Nobody switches the scent of a best-selling detergent to save a fraction of a cent per bottle and then discover that shoppers notice. Once a formula is "in the brief", it tends to stay for the life of the product, and the industry is concentrated among a handful of global houses — IFF, Givaudan, DSM-Firmenich and Symrise — which keeps price competition in check. The vulnerability is that the market has valued IFF's position below its largest peer's: Givaudan is worth $45.84B against IFF's $21.14B.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage has set the agenda for five years. Total debt is $6.31B against $519M of cash — net debt of $5.79B at September 30, 2026. Every asset sale since 2021 has been, at least in part, a way to pay down debt. At 2.75x the balance sheet is no longer a crisis, but the ratio rose from 2.5x at Q2 and it still shapes what management can do on buybacks, M&A and dividends.
02
Stranded costs and separation execution. About $100M of overhead is left behind when Food Ingredients goes. The sale is not expected to close until around the end of Q2 2027, a long wait, during which the two businesses keep sharing systems and people.
03
Health & Biosciences is slowing. The margin leader grew sales 1% currency neutral in Q3 and its adjusted EBITDA margin slipped to 24.0% from 25.0% in Q2. Management said fourth-quarter profit would be softer, particularly there, because of higher costs and reinvestment. If it persists, the best segment stops carrying the group.
04
GAAP earnings are thin and noisy. Trailing net income is $182M on $10.85B of revenue (StockAnalysis) and the dividend payout ratio on GAAP earnings is 227.08%. Q3 2026 GAAP EPS from continuing operations was a loss of $0.50, and the company booked $34M of regulatory costs in the quarter ($115M in nine months), mainly provisions and legal fees for ongoing investigations of its fragrance businesses. Adjusted figures look far healthier; the gap is amortisation from the acquisitions plus impairments, deal costs and these provisions. Investors pay on the adjusted number and have to trust it.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items dominate the reported history. First, the $1.15B goodwill impairment on Food Ingredients in Q1 2025, which produced a Q1 2025 GAAP loss of $1.02B (−$3.98 per share in IFF's 10-Q) and drove FY2025 into a net loss. Second, the gain on the Pharma Solutions sale, which lifted Q2 2025 net income to $599M ($2.33 per share) — the comparison base that makes later quarters look like a collapse when they are not. Third, a $488M gain on debt extinguishment in 2025 from tender offers, per IFF's Q4 2025 release. Fourth, the Q3 2026 charges: a $236M loss on assets held for sale and a $23M goodwill impairment, both on the Botanicals & Food Enhancements business being sold, which together turned the quarter's GAAP result into a $0.50 per share loss from continuing operations while adjusted EPS ex-amortisation was $0.83. IFF's February 11, 2026 results release showed a $374M net loss ($1.46 per share); the 10-K filed on February 27 reported $361M ($1.41), the figure used here.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 9, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $142.17 to the November 9, 2026 settled close of $84.41, IFF lost 40.6% of its price ($84.41 ÷ $142.17 − 1; price only, dividends not included). The shape is a long slide and a partial recovery: from a $150.65 month-end peak in December 2021 to a $61.54 trough in September 2025 — down 59% — then back up 37.2% to today's price as the portfolio reset took shape. The October 2026 month-end close of $93.02 was the highest since October 2024; the stock then fell 9.3% to the November 9 close, with the post-results drop in between.

The 6-period monthly RSI captures the turn. It fell to 13.7 at the September 2025 low — the most oversold reading in the five-year series, below even the 20.0 of the September 2022 sell-off — after seven of eight monthly closes from February to September 2025 came in lower, the only exception a 1.1% April uptick. It recovered to 70.2 at the August 2026 close and 74.0 at October, and stands at 55.6 on November 9. The 10-month moving average, about $80.67, sits below the price: unlike 2023–2025, the longer trend is pointing up.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The portfolio work is done. What remains is three businesses of similar size, all growing — Q3 2026 continuing sales up 5% currency neutral, with Scent profit up 10% — with adjusted EBITDA margins from 18.6% to 24.0% and a continuing gross margin of 42.5%. Both sales fetched respectable prices (~13x EBITDA for Pharma Solutions, ~10x for Food Ingredients), which says the parts were worth more than the market thought. Food Ingredients proceeds of ~$3.8B fund more than $1B of debt reduction and a $2.5B buyback, taking leverage to 2.0–2.5x. IFF raised the low end of its 2026 guidance at Q3. On consensus, EPS grows 22.2% in 2027 to $3.87, putting the stock at about 21.8x that figure — and a market value still well below Givaudan's, for a business finally structured to close that gap.

Bear Case

A smaller company with the same debt is not obviously a better one. Net debt is $5.79B today (2.75x, up from 2.5x at Q2) and the cash to cut it does not arrive until around mid-2027; meanwhile ~$100M of overhead has to be removed from a continuing EBITDA base of $1.55–1.60B. Management has already warned that fourth-quarter profit will be softer, mainly in Health & Biosciences, whose sales grew just 1%. GAAP earnings are thin — a 1.7% net margin, a 227% payout ratio, a $0.50 GAAP loss per share in Q3 — and investors are asked to pay on adjusted numbers. The stock has rallied 37.2% from its September 2025 low and trades at 14.8x EV/EBITDA, above the ~10x CVC is paying for the division it is selling. Much of the recovery may already be in the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Deal and separation execution. The Food Ingredients sale is not expected to close until around the end of Q2 2027. The debt-reduction plan, the buyback and the leverage target all depend on the ~$3.8B arriving roughly on time and on price.
02
Stranded costs eat the margin story. ~$100M of overhead left behind is roughly 6% of guided 2026 continuing EBITDA ($100M ÷ $1.55–1.60B). If it is not cut quickly, the smaller IFF reports lower margins than management's picture of the smaller company.
03
Fourth-quarter profit and the regulatory bill. Management expects softer Q4 profit, particularly in Health & Biosciences, on higher costs and reinvestment. Separately, regulatory costs for the fragrance investigations were $34M in Q3 and $115M for nine months, and the Q2 release said the Middle East conflict held back fine fragrance.
Industry Commentary
Portfolio simplification across flavours & fragrances
EMERGING TAILWIND

IFF is not alone in narrowing its focus. Its own disposals — Pharma Solutions at ~13x EBITDA and Food Ingredients at ~10x — show that buyers, including private equity, will pay for the lower-margin ingredient businesses these groups accumulated. For the sellers, the payoff is a cleaner mix around taste, scent and biosciences, where specification lock-in and formula know-how support pricing. IFF's own shares are up 36.15% over the trailing 52 weeks (StockAnalysis).

Consumer demand and geopolitics
ONGOING HEADWIND

Flavours and fragrances sit behind consumer brands, so they feel shopper weakness with a lag, as customers run down inventories and delay new formulas. IFF's Q2 2026 release said the Middle East conflict held back fine fragrance, and on the August call the CEO said consumer demand had been resilient so far this year but could change. On the Q3 call he said consumers in the US and Europe had cut spending on dietary supplements. The CFO's line on the Q2 call — "The world remains volatile, which is reflected in our full year guidance range" — explains why the 2026 continuing guide is a range; after Q3 it is $100M wide in sales ($7.5–7.6B).

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
KEY DEBATE

IFF trades at 14.8x trailing EV/EBITDA, about 26.7x FY2026 consensus EPS and 21.8x FY2027. The bull framing: after the sale, IFF is a three-segment company with every segment growing, and the market has yet to price the balance-sheet repair and buyback that the proceeds pay for; analysts' average target of $98.24 is 16.4% above the price. The bear framing: the stock has already rallied 37.2% off the low, trades above the ~10x multiple paid for the division it is selling, still carries $5.79B of net debt until the cash arrives, and management has told the market Q4 profit will be softer. The way to settle it is not the P/E — which is distorted by amortisation and restatements — but two numbers reported every quarter: currency-neutral growth in the continuing business and net leverage. The next read is the Q4 report in early 2027 (estimated).
