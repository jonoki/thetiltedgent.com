01 Company Overview

You have never bought anything from IFF, and you have used its products today. It designs the smell of the detergent, the flavour system inside the soft drink, the enzymes in the washing powder and the probiotic in the yoghurt — sold not to you but to the consumer-goods companies whose names are on the label. FY2025 revenue was $10.89B, down from $11.48B in FY2024 as businesses left the perimeter, and the year ended in a GAAP net loss of $361M (EPS −$1.41), driven by a $1.15B goodwill impairment on Food Ingredients booked in Q1 2025. At $83.60 the company is worth $21.33B, ranking #1,115 globally by market capitalisation.

The story here is a company shrinking on purpose. IFF built itself into a giant through two big deals — Frutarom for $7.1B in 2018 and the $26.2B merger with DuPont's Nutrition & Biosciences unit, completed February 1, 2021 — and spent the next five years taking the result apart. Pharma Solutions went to Roquette on May 1, 2025 at an enterprise value of up to $2.85B (~13x EBITDA). On May 29, 2026 IFF agreed to sell Food Ingredients — texturants, emulsifiers, plant-based and other specialty ingredients — to funds advised by CVC Capital Partners for about $4.3B (~10x EBITDA), keeping a ~10% stake. What remains is three businesses: Taste, Scent and Health & Biosciences, guided to $7.4–7.6B of 2026 sales. The central question: is the smaller IFF a better business at a fair price, or the same leverage problem with fewer levers left to pull?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Specification lock-in Formula libraries & sensory know-how Industrial fermentation scale Concentrated global industry Small cost in customer's product Regulatory dossiers

The economics of flavours and fragrances rest on one asymmetry: the ingredient is a tiny share of the customer's cost, and a large share of the customer's risk. Nobody switches the scent of a best-selling detergent to save a fraction of a cent per bottle and then discover that shoppers notice. Once a formula is "in the brief", it tends to stay for the life of the product, and the industry is concentrated among a handful of global houses — IFF, Givaudan, DSM-Firmenich and Symrise — which keeps pricing rational. The vulnerability is that the market has valued IFF's position below its largest peer's: Givaudan is worth $37.88B against IFF's $21.33B. In our reading, the heavier balance sheet IFF carried out of its acquisitions explains much of that gap.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage has set the agenda for five years. Total debt is $6.28B against $569M of cash — net debt of $5.71B. Every asset sale since 2021 has been, at least in part, a deleveraging trade. At 2.5x the balance sheet is no longer a crisis, but it still shapes what management can do on buybacks, M&A and dividends.
02
Stranded costs and separation execution. About $100M of overhead is left behind when Food Ingredients goes. The sale is not expected to close until around the end of Q2 2027, a long window for a carve-out, during which two businesses share systems and people.
03
Profit growth lags sales growth in Scent. Scent sales grew 8% currency neutral in Q2 while its EBITDA grew 5%. If mix or input costs keep eating the operating leverage, the fastest-growing segment stops being the best source of profit growth.
04
GAAP earnings are thin and noisy. TTM net income is $277M on a 2.57% net margin and the dividend payout ratio on GAAP earnings is 147.87%. Adjusted figures look far healthier; the gap is amortisation from the acquisitions plus impairments and deal costs. Investors pay on the adjusted number and have to trust it.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The portfolio work is done. What remains is three businesses of similar size, all growing — Q2 2026 continuing sales up 6% currency neutral — with adjusted EBITDA margins from 18% to 25% and gross margin up to 43.7%. Both disposals cleared at respectable multiples (~13x for Pharma Solutions, ~10x for Food Ingredients), which says the parts were worth more than the market thought. Food Ingredients proceeds of ~$3.8B fund more than $1B of debt reduction and a $2.5B buyback, taking leverage to 2.0–2.5x. On consensus, EPS grows 20.9% in 2027 to $3.96, putting the stock at about 21x forward — and a market value still well below Givaudan's, for a business finally structured to close that gap.

Bear Case

A smaller company with the same debt is not obviously a better one. Net debt is $5.71B today and the cash to cut it does not arrive until around mid-2027; meanwhile ~$100M of stranded costs have to be removed from a continuing EBITDA base of $1.53–1.60B. GAAP earnings are thin — a 2.6% net margin, a 147.87% payout ratio — and investors are asked to pay on adjusted numbers. Scent is growing sales faster than profit. The stock has already rallied 35.8% from its September 2025 low and trades at 14.2x EV/EBITDA, above the ~10x CVC is paying for the division it is selling. The easy re-rating may already be in the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Deal and separation execution. The Food Ingredients sale is not expected to close until around the end of Q2 2027. The deleveraging plan, the buyback and the leverage target all depend on the ~$3.8B arriving roughly on time and on price.
02
Stranded costs eat the margin story. ~$100M of overhead left behind is roughly 6–7% of guided 2026 continuing EBITDA. If it is not cut quickly, the smaller IFF reports lower margins than the pro-forma pitch.
03
Consumer and geopolitical demand. Management cited Middle East conflict hurting fine fragrance and general macro pressure on consumer demand. IFF sells into consumer staples and prestige beauty; volumes follow the shopper, with a lag.
Industry Commentary
Portfolio simplification across flavours & fragrances
EMERGING TAILWIND

IFF is not alone in narrowing its focus. Its own disposals — Pharma Solutions at ~13x EBITDA and Food Ingredients at ~10x — show that buyers, including private equity, will pay for the lower-margin ingredient businesses these groups accumulated. For the sellers, the payoff is a cleaner mix around taste, scent and biosciences, where specification lock-in and formula know-how support pricing. The market has rewarded the move sector-wide in 2026: per companiesmarketcap.com, DSM-Firmenich's market value is up 29.73% this year and Symrise's up 27.38%.

Consumer demand and geopolitics
ONGOING HEADWIND

Flavours and fragrances sit behind consumer brands, so they feel shopper weakness with a lag through customer destocking and reformulation delays. On the Q2 2026 call IFF named two specific pressures: Middle East conflict weighing on fine fragrance, and macroeconomic pressure on consumer demand. The CFO's line — "The world remains volatile, which is reflected in our full year guidance range" — explains why the 2026 continuing guide is a range of $200M in sales.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
KEY DEBATE

IFF trades at 14.2x trailing EV/EBITDA, about 25.5x FY2026 consensus EPS and 21.1x FY2027. The bull framing: after the sale, IFF is a three-segment company with every segment growing, and the market has yet to price the balance-sheet repair and buyback that the proceeds pay for; the Street's average target of $95.66 is 14.4% above the price. The bear framing: the stock has already rallied 35.8% off the low, trades above the ~10x multiple paid for the division it is selling, and still carries $5.71B of net debt until the cash arrives. The way to settle it is not the P/E — which is distorted by amortisation and restatements — but two numbers reported every quarter: currency-neutral growth in the continuing business and net leverage.
