01 Company Overview

Illumina sells the machines that read DNA, and — more importantly — the chemistry those machines burn through on every run. It is the dominant supplier of next-generation sequencing: analysts quoted by Reuters in June 2026 put its share of a roughly $7.3B global gene-sequencing market at about 70%. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $4.49B (+4.9%), of which recurring consumables are the bulk: in Q2 2026, consumables were $852M of $1,159M in revenue. GAAP EPS over the trailing twelve months is $5.37.

This is a turnaround story, and the stock has told it at full volume. Five years ago Illumina closed September 2021 at $405.61. Then came the GRAIL years: Illumina bought the cancer-screening company, booked a $4.40B net loss in FY2022 and further net losses of $1.16B and $1.22B in FY2023 and FY2024, and ended by spinning GRAIL back out on June 24, 2024 (one GRAIL share per six Illumina shares, 85.5% distributed). China then added Illumina to its Unreliable Entity List on February 4, 2025 and banned imports of its sequencers on March 4, 2025. The stock hit a five-year month-end low of $77.60 in April 2025 — 81% below the October 2021 month-end.

Since then it has tripled. China lifted the import ban effective November 10, 2025 (Illumina stays on the list). Q1 and Q2 2026 beat guidance, full-year guidance went up, and S&P Dow Jones announced on September 4, 2026 that Illumina would rejoin the S&P 500 effective before the open on September 21, 2026, replacing Builders FirstSource. The settled close of $239.62 on September 18 — the index-rebalance session — is 209% above the April 2025 low and 83% above the December 2025 close. The question now is simple to state and hard to answer: at 44.6x trailing earnings for a company guiding to $4.60–$4.64B of revenue, how much of the clinical-sequencing recovery is already in the price, just as Roche launches the first serious rival platform?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base → consumables annuity Clinical test validation lock-in ~70% share of sequencing (analyst est.) 20 years of known error profiles Software & informatics ecosystem Scale manufacturing (US & Singapore)

The moat is not the machine; it is everything built on top of it. A clinical lab that has validated a cancer panel on Illumina chemistry would have to re-validate it to switch. Evercore analyst Vijay Kumar put it plainly to Reuters in June 2026: "You know exactly what you're dealing with Illumina for 20 years, the software, the kind of error profiles. The bar has to be very, very high." A genomics lab head in Zurich compared it to switching from Mac to Android. That stickiness is why a consumables base with ~67% of revenue can grow while instrument prices come under attack.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A credible rival just arrived, at half the price. Roche launched the Axelios 1 on June 30, 2026 at a $750,000 US list price against a NovaSeq X list of roughly $985,000–$1.25M, targets about 100 units in year one and is aiming for more than CHF 1B of annual sales long term. Research labs — which switch more easily than clinical labs — are the first battleground.
02
China is a policy lever, not a market. Greater China revenue fell 11.1% YoY to $56M in Q2 2026. Illumina remains on the Unreliable Entity List; the import ban was imposed in March 2025 as a trade countermeasure and lifted in November 2025 after US–China talks. It can be re-imposed the same way.
03
Gross margin is leaking. Non-GAAP gross margin fell from 69.4% to 68.2% in Q2 2026. The 10-Q lists tariffs (mainly on imports from the Singapore plant), a mix shift toward instruments, lower-margin SomaLogic revenue, and higher memory-chip and freight costs.
04
GRAIL is gone but not finished. The 10-Q still lists contingent value rights issued in the GRAIL deal and "the risk of additional litigation." The Icahn Partners derivative suit over the acquisition was settled with no payment; the Delaware Chancery Court holds a dismissal hearing on November 2, 2026, and the dismissal is without prejudice to other shareholders.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, strategic-investment gains and losses: Illumina kept 14.5% of GRAIL at the spin-off and marks it and other holdings to market through the income statement. That swung Q2 2025 GAAP EPS up by $0.65 and Q2 2026 by $0.15; Illumina sold $104M of its GRAIL stake in H1 2026. GAAP EPS therefore moves with GRAIL's share price, not just with sequencing. Second, the FY2022–FY2024 net losses ($4.40B, $1.16B, $1.22B) shrank shareholders' equity to $2.84B, which inflates ROE and the P/B of 12.75. Third, acquisition accounting for SomaLogic: acquisition-related items — intangible amortisation, the SomaLogic inventory step-up and deal costs, net of $32M of gains on the contingent-consideration liability — were a $0.23 adjustment to H1 2026 EPS. Management's non-GAAP EPS ($1.31 in Q2 2026) strips these out and is the number the guidance and consensus use.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $405.61 to the September 18, 2026 settled close of $239.62, ILMN's quoted price fell 40.9%. That overstates the loss slightly: holders on June 13, 2024 also received one GRAIL share for every six Illumina shares, and on digrin's GRAIL-adjusted series the five-year decline is 39.3%. The GRAIL shares themselves, if kept, would add their own value on top; that was not quantified here. The shape is a V with a very long left side: from the October 2021 month-end of $415.06 to the April 2025 month-end of $77.60 the stock lost 81%, then more than tripled (+209%) in seventeen months.

The 6-period monthly RSI now prints 90.0 — the highest reading in the five-year series, above the 86.6 of August 2026 and far from the 12.1 at the June 2022 low. Price is 45% above its 10-month moving average of roughly $165.76. Both say the same thing: momentum is as one-sided as it has been in five years. That is not a forecast of a fall — index inclusion, two guidance raises and a China reopening are real — but it does mean the stock has priced a lot of good news in a short time, right as it absorbed a one-off wave of index-fund buying.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Illumina is the default operating system of DNA sequencing, with an estimated ~70% share and an installed base that buys consumables on every run. The GRAIL distraction is gone, China has reopened (partly), and the business has re-accelerated: Q2 2026 revenue +9.5%, +8.1% excluding China, guidance raised twice, ROW organic growth now guided above 5% and management targeting high-single-digit revenue growth by 2027 with double-digit-to-teens EPS growth. Clinical testing — oncology, prenatal, rare disease — is becoming the majority of consumables demand, and clinical customers do not switch easily. Add $931M of free cash flow, ~$1.78B of buyback authority and new structural demand from S&P 500 index funds, and a premium multiple for a monopoly-like franchise returning to growth is defensible.

Bear Case

At $239.62 you pay 44.6x trailing and 44.7x FY2026 consensus earnings for a company that grew revenue 4.9% over the last twelve months and shrank it in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025. The average analyst target is $202.65, 15% below the price, and monthly RSI is 90 — the most overbought reading in five years — just after a one-off wave of index buying. Roche has launched a rival at roughly half NovaSeq X's list price, gross margin is slipping on tariffs, mix and chip costs, and China can close again at a policy decision's notice. If Q3 disappoints, there is a long way down to where the fundamentals were priced a year ago.

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation after an index-driven run. 44.6x trailing earnings, a 3.89 PEG, a 2.6% free-cash-flow yield and a price above all but one of the recent sell-side targets. Index inclusion is a one-time flow; once it is absorbed, the multiple has to be carried by earnings alone.
02
Competition on price. Roche's Axelios 1 lists at $750,000 against roughly $985,000–$1.25M for NovaSeq X, with a CHF 1B+ long-term ambition. Even without share loss, a credible alternative limits Illumina's pricing on the consumables that make up two-thirds of revenue.
03
Policy: China, tariffs and NIH funding. Illumina stays on China's Unreliable Entity List; tariffs on imports from its Singapore plant are already in the gross margin; and the 10-Q names reductions in NIH funding as a demand risk for US research customers. None of these is in management's control.
Industry Commentary
The platform war finally starts
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For most of two decades the sequencing market has been Illumina and a set of niche challengers. Roche's Axelios 1, built on its Sequencing by Expansion technology and launched June 30, 2026, is the first rival backed by a diagnostics giant with its own global clinical sales force. It is priced to take share ($750K list vs NovaSeq X at roughly $985K–$1.25M), it launched with named academic and clinical partners (Hartwig Medical Foundation, Broad Clinical Labs) and single-cell compatibility through 10x Genomics, and Roche is guiding to ~100 units in year one. Evercore's Vijay Kumar framed the optimistic case for both sides — that Roche could "gain share in an expanding market rather than making this 'a knife fight' for a limited pool of customers." The pessimistic case for Illumina is that the knife fight happens in consumables pricing, where the margin lives.

Geopolitics as a line item
ONGOING HEADWIND

Illumina is a case study in how trade policy turns into P&L. China used it as a countermeasure to US tariffs — Unreliable Entity List in February 2025, import ban in March 2025 — then lifted the ban effective November 10, 2025 after US–China talks, while keeping the listing that makes every instrument sale subject to government approval. Greater China revenue is now $56M a quarter, 4.8% of the total and still falling (−11.1% YoY in Q2 2026). Meanwhile US tariffs on imports from Illumina's Singapore manufacturing site, Middle East-related freight and energy costs, and memory-chip inflation are all named in the 10-Q as gross-margin drags for 2026. The company reports "ROW" growth excluding China precisely because China has become unforecastable.

Clinical sequencing becomes the base load
EMERGING TAILWIND

The structural shift under the stock price is who is buying the reagents. Per Zacks, clinical end-markets generated the majority of Illumina's sequencing consumables revenue in Q1 2026, and clinical consumables demand excluding China grew about 20% for a second straight quarter. Clinical demand is driven by tests that get reimbursed and written into care guidelines — oncology profiling, prenatal screening, rare-disease diagnosis — and it is far less exposed to research-grant cycles than academic demand. The bull framing: Illumina is turning from a cyclical research-tools vendor into a clinical infrastructure company, and deserves a multiple that reflects the stickiness. The bear framing: the market has already paid that multiple — 44.6x trailing earnings — before the first year of real platform competition has played out. The way to adjudicate is not the P/E; it is clinical consumables growth and non-GAAP gross margin over the next four quarters, both reported every quarter.
