01 Company Overview

Illumina sells the machines that read DNA, and — more importantly — the chemistry those machines burn through on every run. It is the dominant supplier of next-generation sequencing: analysts quoted by Reuters in June 2026 put its share of a roughly $7.3B global gene-sequencing market at about 70%. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $4.54B (+5.9%), of which recurring consumables are the bulk: in Q3 2026, sequencing and microarray consumables were $841M of $1,129M in revenue. GAAP EPS over the trailing twelve months is $5.81.

This is a turnaround story, and the stock has told it at full volume. At the start of this report's five-year window Illumina closed November 2021 at $365.33. Then came the GRAIL years: Illumina bought the cancer-screening company, booked a $4.40B net loss in FY2022 and further net losses of $1.16B and $1.22B in FY2023 and FY2024, and ended by spinning GRAIL back out on June 24, 2024 (one GRAIL share per six Illumina shares, 85.5% distributed). China then added Illumina to its Unreliable Entity List on February 4, 2025 and banned imports of its sequencers on March 4, 2025. The stock hit a five-year month-end low of $77.60 in April 2025 — 80% below the December 2021 month-end of $380.44, the high of the window.

Since then it has roughly tripled. China lifted the import ban effective November 10, 2025 (Illumina stays on the list). Every quarter of 2026 has come in above Illumina's own guidance, full-year guidance was raised in July and again in October, and S&P Dow Jones Indices announced on September 4, 2026 that Illumina would rejoin the S&P 500 effective before the open on September 21, 2026, replacing Builders FirstSource. The stock kept climbing into the third-quarter results, closing at $290.06 on October 29. Then it fell 18.6% on October 30, the day after a quarter that beat guidance and raised the full-year outlook again, but whose reported revenue of $1,129M was below the $1.15B FactSet estimate cited on MarketScreener. The settled close of $238.21 on November 3 is 207% above the April 2025 low and 82% above the December 2025 close. The question is the same as before the print, at a lower price: at 41.0x trailing earnings for a company now guiding to $4.64–$4.66B of revenue, how much of the clinical-sequencing recovery is already in the price, as Roche's rival platform ramps up?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base → consumables annuity Clinical test validation lock-in ~70% share of sequencing (analyst est.) 20 years of known error profiles Software & informatics ecosystem Scale manufacturing (US & Singapore)

The moat is not the machine; it is everything built on top of it. A clinical lab that has validated a cancer panel on Illumina chemistry would have to re-validate it to switch. Evercore analyst Vijay Kumar put it plainly to Reuters in June 2026: "You know exactly what you're dealing with Illumina for 20 years, the software, the kind of error profiles. The bar has to be very, very high." A genomics lab head in Zurich compared it to switching from Mac to Android. That stickiness is why a consumables base with ~67% of revenue can grow while instrument prices come under attack.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A credible rival just arrived, at half the price. Roche launched the Axelios 1 on June 29, 2026 at a $750,000 US list price against a NovaSeq X list of roughly $985,000–$1.25M, targets about 100 units in year one and is aiming for more than CHF 1B of annual sales long term. Research labs — which switch more easily than clinical labs — are the first battleground.
02
China is a policy lever, not a market. Greater China revenue was $59M in Q3 2026, up from $52M a year earlier but only 5.2% of the total, after an 11.1% fall in Q2. Illumina remains on the Unreliable Entity List; the import ban was imposed in March 2025 as a trade countermeasure and lifted in November 2025 after US–China talks. It can be re-imposed the same way.
03
Costs keep pressing on gross margin. Non-GAAP gross margin excluding tariff refunds was 69.4% in Q3 2026, 0.2 points above a year earlier, after falling from 69.4% to 68.2% in Q2. The Q2 10-Q listed tariffs (mainly on imports from the Singapore plant), a mix shift toward instruments, lower-margin SomaLogic revenue, and higher memory-chip and freight costs; on the Q3 call CEO Jacob Thaysen still named electronic component pricing, shipping costs and tariffs as big headwinds. The Q3 GAAP gross margin of 73.5% is lifted by a one-off $79M tariff-refund benefit.
04
GRAIL is gone but not finished. Illumina still carried a $36M liability at September 27, 2026 for the contingent value rights issued in the GRAIL deal (Q3 10-Q). The Icahn Partners derivative suit over the acquisition was settled with no payment; the Delaware Court of Chancery set a hearing on its dismissal for November 2, 2026, and the dismissal does not cover claims by other shareholders.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the reported figures. First, strategic-investment gains and losses: Illumina kept 14.5% of GRAIL at the spin-off and marked it to market through the income statement. That swung Q2 2025 GAAP EPS up by $0.65 and Q2 2026 by $0.15. During 2026 it sold the stake: its marketable equity securities fell to $2M at September 27 from $215M at the start of the year, which the Q3 10-Q attributes to the GRAIL sale. Its venture-fund holdings still run through GAAP EPS ($31M of losses in Q3 2026), so GAAP EPS moves with more than sequencing. Second, tariff refunds: after the Supreme Court held in February 2026 that the emergency-powers law behind some US tariffs (IEEPA) does not authorise them, Illumina booked a $79M benefit to cost of revenue in Q3 2026 for refunds it expects and cut revenue by $37M for the part it expects to pass back to customers (Q3 10-Q). Non-GAAP EPS was $1.62 with the refunds and $1.44 without; the headline figure and the guidance exclude them. Third, the FY2022–FY2024 net losses ($4.40B, $1.16B, $1.22B) shrank shareholders' equity, which stood at $3.15B at September 27, 2026; that inflates ROE and the price-to-book ratio of 11.51 (StockAnalysis). Fourth, acquisition accounting for SomaLogic: acquisition-related items — intangible amortisation, the SomaLogic inventory step-up and deal costs, net of $32M of gains on the contingent-consideration liability — were a $0.23 adjustment to H1 2026 EPS. Management's non-GAAP EPS ($1.44 in Q3 2026, excluding tariff refunds) strips these out and is the number the guidance uses.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Illumina is the default operating system of DNA sequencing, with an estimated ~70% share and an installed base that buys consumables on every run. The GRAIL distraction is gone, China has reopened (partly), and every 2026 quarter has beaten guidance: Q3 revenue grew 7.6% before tariff refunds, clinical sales grew 14% outside Greater China, more than 80 NovaSeq X machines were placed, and full-year guidance went up again, to $4.64–$4.66B of revenue and $5.40–$5.45 of non-GAAP EPS. Clinical testing — oncology, prenatal, rare disease — is becoming the majority of consumables demand, and clinical customers do not switch easily. Add $1,058M of free cash flow, ~$1.77B of buyback authority, S&P 500 index funds as permanent holders and a price 23% below its October high, and a premium multiple for a dominant franchise returning to growth is defensible.

Bear Case

At $238.21 you pay 41.0x trailing and 43.5x FY2026 consensus earnings for a company whose reported revenue grew 4.2% last quarter and 5.9% over the last twelve months, after shrinking in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025. Reported Q3 revenue came in under the FactSet estimate, instrument sales fell as Europe weakened, and the market answered a beat-and-raise quarter with an 18.6% one-day fall. Roche has launched a rival at roughly half NovaSeq X's list price, chip, freight and tariff costs are named headwinds, and China can close again at a policy decision's notice. The average analyst target, $261.60, is less than 10% above the price, and the consensus has slipped to Hold.

Valuation after the run into S&P 500 membership. 41.0x trailing earnings, a 3.69 PEG and a 2.9% free-cash-flow yield, even after the October 30 fall. Index inclusion was a one-time wave of buying; from here the multiple has to be carried by earnings alone.

03

Industry Commentary
The platform war finally starts
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For most of two decades the sequencing market has been Illumina and a set of niche challengers. Roche's Axelios 1, built on its Sequencing by Expansion technology and launched June 29, 2026, is the first rival backed by a diagnostics giant with its own global clinical sales force. It is priced to take share ($750K list vs NovaSeq X at roughly $985K–$1.25M), it launched with named academic and clinical partners (Hartwig Medical Foundation, Broad Clinical Labs) and single-cell compatibility through 10x Genomics, and Roche is guiding to ~100 units in year one. Evercore's Vijay Kumar framed the optimistic case for both sides, telling Reuters that Roche could gain share in an expanding market rather than making this "a knife fight" for a limited pool of customers. The pessimistic case for Illumina is that the knife fight happens in consumables pricing, where the margin lives.

Geopolitics as a line item
ONGOING HEADWIND

Illumina is a case study in how trade policy turns into P&L. China used it as a countermeasure to US tariffs — Unreliable Entity List in February 2025, import ban in March 2025 — then lifted the ban effective November 10, 2025 after US–China talks, while keeping the listing that makes every instrument sale subject to government approval. Greater China revenue was $59M in Q3 2026, 5.2% of the total, up 13.5% on a year earlier after an 11.1% fall in Q2. Meanwhile US tariffs on imports from Illumina's Singapore manufacturing site, Middle East-related freight and energy costs, and memory-chip inflation were all named in the Q2 10-Q as gross-margin drags for 2026. The tariff story also runs the other way: after the Supreme Court held in February 2026 that the IEEPA tariffs were not authorised, Illumina booked $79M of expected refunds in Q3. The company reports "ROW" growth excluding China precisely because China has become unforecastable.

Clinical sequencing becomes the base load
EMERGING TAILWIND

The structural shift under the stock price is who is buying the reagents. Per Zacks, clinical end-markets generated the majority of Illumina's sequencing consumables revenue in Q1 2026, and clinical consumables demand excluding China grew about 20% for a second straight quarter; on the Q3 call, management put clinical sales growth outside Greater China at 14%. Clinical demand is driven by tests that get reimbursed and written into care guidelines — oncology profiling, prenatal screening, rare-disease diagnosis — and it is far less exposed to research-grant cycles than academic demand. The bull framing: Illumina is turning from a cyclical research-tools vendor into a clinical infrastructure company, and deserves a multiple that reflects the stickiness. The bear framing: the market has already paid that multiple — 41.0x trailing earnings, even after October's fall — before the first year of real platform competition has played out. The way to adjudicate is not the P/E; it is clinical consumables growth and non-GAAP gross margin over the next four quarters, both reported every quarter.
