01 Overview

The Fed's own definition: "The federal funds rate is the interest rate charged by banks to borrow from each other overnight." The effective rate is what those loans actually cost. In the New York Fed's words, the EFFR "is calculated as a volume-weighted median of overnight federal funds transactions", reported by domestic banks and U.S. branches of foreign banks. It is the rate the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) targets when it sets policy.

3.88% (Friday, October 2, 2026, on $118 billion of loans) sits inside the 3.75–4.00% target range the FOMC set on September 16, 2026, its first increase since July 2023.

Who sets the target
The FOMC
Eight scheduled meetings a year; decisions at 2 p.m. Eastern on the second day (federalreserve.gov).
Who publishes it
New York Fed
About 9:00 a.m. ET for the prior business day, with the 1st–99th percentiles and the day's volume.
Can you buy it?
Not directly
It is built from loans reported by banks. Individuals use money market funds or T-bills (Section 05).
Why it matters
Anchor rate
The Fed: range changes "influence short-term interest rates for other financial instruments".
02 How It's Priced

The EFFR is the overnight end of the curve. On October 2, 2026 every Treasury tenor yielded more (3-month 4.19%, 10-year 5.28%); a year earlier the 3-month to 7-year points were below the 4.09% EFFR.

Floor, ceiling and the day's trades

The FOMC sets a range; the Fed "steers the federal funds rate into its target range primarily by changing the interest rate paid on reserve balances and the interest rate of its overnight reverse repurchase facility." An ON RRP counterparty "should be unwilling to invest funds overnight with another counterparty at a rate below the ON RRP rate", which sets a floor.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reinvestment risk. The overnight rate follows each FOMC decision within a day.
02
Inflation risk. The Fed's 2026 PCE inflation median is 3.7%, against a 3.88% overnight rate before tax.
03
Not directly investable. Funds that earn short rates hold other assets and charge fees (Section 05).
04
Where it sits in the range can shift. From January 2022 to mid-September 2025 the EFFR sat 7–8 basis points above the range's floor; from November 28, 2025 it sat 12–14 above.
04 Key Figures

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

When the FOMC raises the range or holds it high. Cash at overnight rates picks up an increase within a day, and there is almost no price to lose.

When it does poorly

When the FOMC cuts. Income drops the day after a decision (0.07% at end-2021), with no long-dated holding to gain from falling yields. After inflation and tax the return can be negative.

Top 3 Risks

01
Rate cuts. The SEP longer-run median is 3.2%, below today's 3.875% midpoint; overnight income would follow any move down.
02
Inflation. The 2026 PCE median of 3.7% leaves 0.18 pp between it and the 3.88% EFFR, before tax.
03
The product, not the rate. Investor.gov: mutual funds "are not guaranteed or insured by the FDIC or any other government agency."

Where it sits in the range
POLICY

From late September 2025 the EFFR moved up from 7–8 basis points above the range floor to 14 above by November 28, 2025 (3.89%, 1 below IORB). The September 2026 directive has the Desk buy Treasury bills when appropriate "to maintain an ample level of reserves".
