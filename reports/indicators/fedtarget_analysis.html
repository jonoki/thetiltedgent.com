01 What It Measures

This is the interest rate the Federal Reserve aims for, set as a band a quarter of a percentage point wide (0.25 pp; pp = percentage point). Its committee, the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC), picks a range for the federal funds rate, the rate banks charge one another for overnight loans, and then uses its own rates to keep that market rate inside the band. Since September 17, 2026 the range is 3.75% to 4.00%.

The Fed's definition: "The federal funds rate is the interest rate at which depository institutions lend balances at the Federal Reserve to other depository institutions overnight." FRED, which carries the two ends of the range as daily series, describes the top end: "This series represents upper limit of the federal funds target range established by the Federal Open Market Committee. The data updated each day is the data effective as of that day." The chart in Section 07 plots that upper limit (FRED DFEDTARU, percent, not seasonally adjusted); the lower limit is FRED DFEDTARL.

02 How It's Built

Who sets it. The FOMC has twelve voting members: "the seven members of the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System; the president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York; and four of the remaining eleven Reserve Bank presidents, who serve one-year terms on a rotating basis." The 2026 committee is chaired by Kevin Warsh (federalreserve.gov, About the FOMC, updated October 7, 2026). The September 16, 2026 decision passed 12–0.

How often. Eight scheduled meetings a year; a decision is announced in a statement on the meeting's second day (September's was released at 2:00 p.m. EDT) and takes effect the next day. The committee can also act between meetings: in 2020 it met unscheduled on March 2 and March 15, and the range fell effective March 4 (to 1.00–1.25%) and March 16 (to 0–0.25%).

How it is held in place. The range is a target, not a rate anyone is charged. The Fed steers the market rate into it with administered rates, listed in the Implementation Note issued with each statement. Interest on reserve balances (IORB), set by the Board, is what the Fed pays banks on the reserves they hold with it; the overnight reverse repo facility (ON RRP) sets a floor, since a counterparty "should be unwilling to invest funds overnight with another counterparty at a rate below the ON RRP rate"; standing repo (SRP) operations "limit upward pressure and help provide a ceiling on rates".

03 Strengths & Limits

The range is the Fed's policy stance in one line. Every published effective federal funds rate (EFFR) from October 1, 2021 to October 7, 2026 sat inside the range in force that day (1,258 business days; our count from FRED EFFR, DFEDTARL and DFEDTARU).

Key Limitations

01
A band, not a rate. It says where the overnight rate should be, not where it trades. On October 7, 2026 the EFFR was 3.88%, 0.13 pp above the lower limit (our arithmetic).
02
Moves in steps. It changes only at decisions, usually by 0.25 pp, so it stays flat for months: 3.50–3.75% held for 280 days, December 11, 2025 to September 16, 2026, through five meetings.
03
Not the whole of policy. The Fed also acts through its securities holdings, which the range does not show: the September 2026 directive has the Desk buy Treasury bills when appropriate "to maintain an ample level of reserves".
04
Nominal, not real. It is not adjusted for inflation. The 3.875% midpoint is 0.175 pp above the median FOMC projection of 3.7% inflation for 2026 in the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index (fourth quarter over fourth quarter; Summary of Economic Projections, September 16, 2026; our arithmetic).
05
The path ahead is a projection, not a promise. The SEP's rate projections are those of individual participants, each "based on his or her assessment of appropriate monetary policy".

05 What Moves It

One thing moves the range: an FOMC vote. The committee acts for the Fed's "dual mandate" of maximum employment and price stability, and holds a 2 percent goal for inflation. On September 16, 2026 it said: "The Committee decided to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, in support of the Federal Reserve's dual mandate." The statement added: "Inflation remains elevated. Today's policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal."

The minutes, released October 7, 2026, give the reasons: participants "generally emphasized that inflation remained elevated while the labor market appeared to be near full employment", and "all participants viewed a higher target range for the federal funds rate as appropriate." For August, Fed staff estimated 12-month PCE inflation at 3.8% and core PCE (excluding energy and many food prices) at 3.4%; under the new method BEA said it would adopt at the end of September, the estimates were 3.6% and 3.2%.

The Fed's own projection

The Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) of September 16, 2026 puts the median end-2026 midpoint at 4.1%, against today's 3.875%. Of 18 participants, 12 placed it at 4.125%, the midpoint of a 4.00–4.25% range, 4 at 4.375% and 2 at 3.875%, today's level.

08 Why It Matters

The Fed: "Changes in the federal funds rate trigger a chain of events that affect other short-term interest rates, foreign exchange rates, long-term interest rates, the amount of money and credit, and, ultimately, a range of economic variables, including employment, output, and prices of goods and services."

The links are mechanical at the short end. Overnight market rates sit inside the range (the EFFR and SOFR, both 3.88% on October 7, 2026). The bank prime loan rate, "one of several base rates used by banks to price short-term business loans" (H.15), was 3.00 pp above the upper limit on every business day since January 2019 but one, September 17, 2025, when it moved a day before the range did (our count from FRED DPRIME and DFEDTARU). Treasury yields price the expected path: on October 7, 2026 the 3-month Treasury yielded 4.22% and the 2-year 4.77%, both above the 4.00% top of the range (H.15, constant maturity). Money market funds and T-bill funds such as SGOV hold short-dated paper, so their yields track these short-term rates. At the last three changes, the EFFR and prime moved on the day the new range took effect (FRED EFFR, DPRIME).

When it rises

IORB and the Fed's other rates move up the day the new range takes effect, and the EFFR and prime have moved that same day. Variable-rate loans priced off prime cost more; savings in money market funds and short T-bills earn more, about $25 a year per $10,000 for each 0.25 pp (simple interest). Bond prices fall as yields rise, and the higher rate used to discount future profits weighs on share prices.

When it falls

The same chain in reverse: borrowing on prime and short-term loans gets cheaper, cash yields drop, and existing bonds gain in price as yields fall. From October 2021 to March 2022 the upper limit was 0.25%, and overnight cash earned close to nothing.
