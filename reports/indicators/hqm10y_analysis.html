01 Overview

This is a yardstick, not a bond or a fund: the 10-year point on a yield curve that the Treasury fits each business day to the prices of high-quality corporate bonds in dollars. FRED's notes: "The HQM yield curve uses data from a set of high quality corporate bonds rated AAA, AA, or A that accurately represent the high quality corporate bond market." It is a spot rate, "the yield on a bond that gives a single payment at that maturity" (FRED).

It exists for pensions. Treasury's papers on a corporate spot curve "for Pension Discounting" date from 2005, and the HQM version serves the Pension Protection Act of 2006 (PPA). Treasury's 2011 explainer: "Segment rates required by the PPA are derived from the monthly average spot rates." FRED's notes: it projects yields "out to 100 years maturity to get discount rates for long-dated pension liabilities."

In September 2026 the 10-year rate averaged 5.86%, the highest since October 2023 (6.11%) and 0.87 points above the 10-year Treasury (4.99%).

02 How It's Priced

The HQM rate is the Treasury rate plus a credit spread, which widens with maturity: HQM par yields against Treasury par yields, the September spread was 0.37 points at 2 years, 0.56 at 5, 0.78 at 10 and 0.94 at 30.

How the curve is built

Treasury's explainer: "The spot rates are chosen to be the rates that give the best statistical fit to all the prices in the bond set." Two adjustment terms blend AAA, AA and A bonds into one curve of "market-weighted average quality." On an upward-sloping curve the spot rate sits above the par yield (5.86% vs 5.77%).

03 Strengths & Vulnerabilities
Published by the U.S. Treasury AAA, AA and A bonds only Maturity held at exactly 10 years Monthly since January 1984

A rate for every maturity lets a pension plan discount each year's payments at its own rate.

Key Vulnerabilities (for an owner of bonds like these)

01
Rate risk. A 10-year bond at the 5.77% par yield has a modified duration of 7.52: a 1-point rise cuts its price by about 7.2%.
02
Spread risk. The spread over the 10-year Treasury ranged from 0.78 points (December 2024) to 1.85 (October 2022).
03
Downgrade risk. A bond cut to BBB or lower drops out of the curve's bond set; its owner keeps it, and its price falls.

08 Summary & Key Risks

When it does well

For a holder of bonds like these: when Treasury yields fall and the spread holds or narrows. A 1-point drop adds about 7.9% to the illustrative bond, on top of a 5.77% coupon. Inflation below the 2.35% breakeven or a lower Fed path tend to do this.

When it does poorly

When yields rise: a 1-point rise costs about 7.2%, more than a year of coupons. Upside inflation surprises, a higher policy path (2026 median 3.8% in June, 4.1% in September) or wider spreads, as in October 2022, tend to do this.

Top 3 Risks

01
Rising yields. −19.7% for a 3-point rise (full repricing).
02
Spread widening. A return to October 2022's 1.85-point spread would add 0.98 points with Treasuries unchanged.
03
Inflation above the breakeven. 2.35% priced over ten years against a 3.7% Fed median for 2026.
Rates Commentary
Splitting the 5.86%
DECOMPOSITION

5.86% = 4.99% 10-year Treasury + 0.87 points of spread; the Treasury part is a 2.64% real (TIPS) yield plus 2.35 points of breakeven inflation. Of the 0.94-point rise over the year, the Treasury yield accounts for 0.87 and the spread for 0.07.

Why a monthly average
METHOD

Treasury's 2011 explainer: "The monthly average reduces the possible volatility in the curve that could come from special behavior at month-end." The cost is lag. The 10-year Treasury rose from 4.96% on September 22 to 5.29% on September 30; the month-end HQM rate, 6.17%, ended 0.31 points above the average.
