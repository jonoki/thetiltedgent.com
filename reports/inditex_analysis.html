01 Company Overview

Inditex is the Spanish group behind Zara, along with Zara Home, Lefties, Pull&Bear, Massimo Dutti, Bershka, Stradivarius and Oysho — eight store brands selling in 215 markets through 5,444 stores (July 31, 2026) and their online shops. In the fiscal year to January 31, 2026 (Inditex calls it FY2025) sales were €39,864M, up 3.2% as reported and up 7.0% in constant currency — that is, before translating foreign sales back into weaker-or-stronger euros. Net income rose 6.0% to a record €6,220M, or €1.996 per share. The first half of the current year (February to July 2026) kept the pace: sales €19,755M (+7.6%, +9.2% in constant currency) and net income €2,980M (+6.8%). Costs were the soft spot: operating expenses rose 8.3%, faster than sales, so operating profit (EBIT) grew 7.6%, no faster than sales. On the results call on September 9 CEO Óscar García Maceiras said disruptions in the Middle East had raised transport and input costs in the half. Management kept its 2026 guidance unchanged from June: a stable gross margin (±50 bps), about 5% more gross selling space, a currency drag of about 1% on sales and around €2.3B of ordinary capex.

What makes Inditex different is speed and control. It buys a significant part of its production close to its headquarters in Spain in short series, sources within the season and ships from centralised logistics platforms, so it can react to fashion trends as they arise. The financial signature of that model is unusual for a retailer: a gross margin of 58.3%, a pre-tax margin of 20.1%, €10.4B of net cash and a dividend that took 88% of FY2025 earnings. The market pays for that: at €53.24 the shares trade at 25.9 times trailing earnings, for a business whose net income grew 6.0% last year and 6.8% in the first half. The central question is whether Zara itself — 70% of sales but up only 1.0% as reported in FY2025 and 4.8% in the first half of 2026 — can keep the group growing while the smaller brands do the running.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

In-season, close-to-home sourcing Centralised logistics platform Zara brand reach (215 markets) Eight-brand diversification Integrated store + online stock Net cash €10.4B Prime-location flagships

The moat is a process, not a patent. A retailer that orders a whole season months in advance has to discount whatever misses. Inditex keeps a significant part of procurement close to Spain in short series and sources within the season, which the company says lets it adapt its offer to changing trends. The evidence is in the numbers: a 58.3% gross margin, a 40% return on capital employed in FY2025, and negative working capital — suppliers are paid after customers have paid, so growth funds itself (operating working capital was −€6,965M at July 31, 2026). Copying the model means copying the logistics platform and the supplier network at once.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Zara is growing slowest. The concept that makes 70% of sales and of pre-tax profit grew 1.0% in FY2025 and 4.8% in the first half of 2026 in euros, while the group grew 3.2% and 7.6%. The smaller brands cannot carry the group for long: all five together are under 30% of sales.
02
Currency eats growth. Inditex sells in more than 200 markets and reports in euros. In FY2025 constant-currency growth of 7.0% became 3.2% as reported; management expects about a −1% currency effect on 2026 sales at current rates.
03
Fully priced quality. At 25.9 times trailing earnings and 24.5 times the consensus estimate for the year to January 2027, the shares already pay for steady growth. Net income grew 6.0% in FY2025 and 6.8% in the first half; any slip in the gross margin, guided only to "a stable gross margin (+/-50 bps)" for 2026, falls straight through to a richly valued profit line.
04
A controlled company. The founder controls 59.294% of the votes and the free float is about 35.6%. Minority shareholders get a high payout but no say over strategy, succession or capital allocation; the board has one executive director.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things shape the reported figures. First, the logistics programme: Inditex spent an extra €900M a year in FY2024 and FY2025 to expand logistics capacity, which is why capex ran at €2.7B; ordinary capex for 2026 is guided to about €2.3B, plus close to €200M of extraordinary spending on corporate facilities. Second, leases: under IFRS store rents sit below EBITDA as depreciation and interest, so EBITDA (€11,267M in FY2025) overstates cash earnings; the company's free-cash-flow figure adds back €1,834M of FY2025 lease payments. Third, the bonus dividend: €0.55 of the €1.75 FY2025 dividend is a bonus on top of the 60% ordinary payout, so the headline yield includes a discretionary part. The releases carry no adjusted-earnings figure; the numbers above are IFRS as published.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Inditex is a retailer with a 40% return on capital, a 58% gross margin, €10.4B of net cash and €1M of financial debt, that added 5.3% gross space in 2025, guides about 5% for 2026, and still pays out 88% of earnings. Constant-currency growth is accelerating — 7.0% in FY2025, 9.2% in the first half of 2026 and +9% for the start of Autumn/Winter — and Bershka, Stradivarius and Oysho grew 17–21% in the first half while opening new markets, including Bershka in the US and Brazil. If currency stops subtracting, reported growth converges on constant-currency growth, and a company growing earnings near the 8.7% consensus expects for this year, with this balance sheet, can grow into a mid-20s multiple. 20 of 24 analysts rate it a Buy, with an average target 13.4% above the price.

Bear Case

You are paying 25.9 times trailing earnings for 6–7% net income growth. Zara — 70% of sales and profit — grew 1.0% in euros last year and 4.8% in the first half, and the brands doing the growing are too small to carry the group. The gross margin is guided only to stable, so there is little room for profit to outgrow sales — and in the first half operating expenses rose 8.3%, faster than sales, while management pointed to higher transport and input costs. The stock has trailed the IBEX 35 by about 50 points over five years, the founder controls the votes, and the low analyst target of €41.50 sits 22% below the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Zara's slowdown. The core concept grew 1.0% in FY2025 and 4.8% in the first half of 2026 in euros. If it stalls, no combination of Bershka, Stradivarius and Oysho can keep the group near its 7–9% constant-currency pace.
02
Valuation leaves no room. 25.9x trailing and 24.5x forward earnings, a PEG of 2.8 and a 3.5% free-cash-flow yield. A quarter of flat margin with slower sales would leave the multiple, not the business, to absorb the disappointment; the shares fell 3.6% on a first half in which profit grew 6.8%.
03
Currency and consumer demand outside Europe. Translation cost 3.8 points of growth in FY2025 (7.0% constant currency vs 3.2% reported) and is guided at about −1% for 2026. Asia & rest of world fell to 15.0% of sales in the first half from 16.0%. On the September 9 call Inditex's investor-relations director said geopolitical conditions were hurting sales in the Middle East, where about 480 stores trade under franchise, though sales there had improved since the first quarter.
Industry Commentary
Speed as a business model
STRUCTURAL

The fashion industry's oldest problem is guessing demand months ahead. Inditex's answer — source a significant part close to home, in short series and within the season — is the core of its model. Its recent investment has gone into quality of space rather than quantity: fewer, larger stores (store count down 6% over three years, sales up 22%), heavy investment in logistics (€900M a year extra in 2024 and 2025) and technology in the stores, from assisted checkouts to soft-tag security, planned for 90% of products in the Spring/Summer 2026 collections. The result shows in the gross margin, which rose to 58.3% in FY2025 and 58.7% in the first half of 2026. The next layer is software: at the July 2026 shareholders' meeting García Maceiras said "Artificial Intelligence at Inditex is embedded across the organisation, driving productivity, operational efficiency, technological development and customer experience".

The euro problem
ONGOING HEADWIND

About half of Inditex's sales are in Europe outside Spain, but a third come from the Americas, Asia and the rest of the world, and all of it is reported in euros. In FY2025 the gap between constant-currency growth (7.0%) and reported growth (3.2%) was 3.8 points; in the first half of 2026 it narrowed to 1.6 points (9.2% vs 7.6%), and management guides about −1% for the full year at current rates. The underlying business is growing faster than the reported line suggests — but shareholders are paid in euros.

A cash machine that pays it out
SHAREHOLDER RETURNS

Inditex generated €4,686M of free cash flow in FY2025 (company definition) and paid €5,235M in dividends, more than it produced, drawing net cash down from €11,495M to €10,958M. The dividend has risen every year in the window covered by the chart — from €0.35 per half-yearly payment in 2021 to €0.875 per half in 2026.
