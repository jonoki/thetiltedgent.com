01 Company Overview

Infineon makes the chips that move and switch electricity — power semiconductors in silicon, silicon carbide and gallium nitride — plus the microcontrollers, sensors and security chips that sit around them. By its own presentation (citing TechInsights and Omdia) it is the world's #1 supplier of automotive chips (12.8% share), power discretes and modules (17.3%) and microcontrollers (23.1%). Fiscal 2026 (to September 30, 2026) revenue was €16.35B, up 12%, with a Segment Result of €3.22B (19.7% margin) — a new annual high, just above the €16.31B of fiscal 2023, though still well short of that year's 27% margin.

The story of 2026 is that the chip cycle turned and AI arrived at the same time. Every AI server rack needs power conversion from the grid down to the processor. Infineon's dedicated AI power revenue reached €1.7B in fiscal 2026 and its whole data-center business about €2.2B; for fiscal 2027 it guides that data-center figure to close to €4B. The September quarter set a record at €4.706B (+19% year on year, 23.8% Segment Result margin), and the first fiscal-2027 outlook calls for about €20B of revenue, more than 20% growth, at a mid-twenties margin. The share price went the other way: from a €38.00 close on March 31, 2026 to €81.67 on June 30 (+115% in a quarter), then down to €49.88 on November 13, 38.9% below that June close, as brokers marked down chip-sector valuations (JPMorgan cited doubts about how long and how durable the AI-driven cycle will be). At that price the stock trades on 44x trailing IFRS earnings and 18x the street's fiscal-2027 adjusted EPS (consensus of November 2, before the outlook). The debate has moved from whether fiscal 2027 delivers to what the market will pay for it.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

#1 in power semis (17.3%) #1 in automotive chips (12.8%) #1 in microcontrollers (23.1%) Silicon, SiC and GaN in-house Own fabs: Germany, Austria, Malaysia >€10B capacity agreements 300 mm GaN in Villach

The moat is breadth plus capacity. In AI power, Infineon sells the whole chain from the grid connection to the processor's voltage regulator, in all three materials — silicon carbide for high-voltage conversion, gallium nitride for dense intermediate stages (it is extending its collaboration with Nvidia to gallium nitride intermediate bus converters), silicon at the chip — and makes them in its own fabs. When supply is short, owning cleanroom space is the advantage: the Smart Power Fab in Dresden opened in 2026 and is ramping, silicon carbide capacity is being expanded in Kulim, Malaysia, and in late October Infineon committed about €1.1B through 2034 to 300 mm gallium nitride production in its existing Villach cleanrooms, with volume production planned by the end of 2027. Customers committing volumes and prepayments to reserve capacity for several years — more than €10B of agreements, signed and in negotiation — is the clearest sign that the position is valued. In cars, a microcontroller designed into a platform tends to stay for its life, which is why automotive share moves slowly in either direction.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The market is paying less for chip earnings. At €49.88 the stock is 44x trailing IFRS EPS of €1.14 and 18x consensus fiscal-2027 adjusted EPS of €2.84 (Vara Research, November 2) — down from 70x and 23x at the October 2 price of the previous edition, even though the outlook rose. Five of the seven brokers that published after the print cut their targets, most citing lower valuations across the sector; JPMorgan pointed to doubts about how long and how durable the AI-driven cycle will be. While that mood lasts, good results alone may not lift the price.
02
The pace of the AI build-out. Bernstein cut its target on uncertainty about how fast AI data centers are built in the US, and Reuters linked the November 5 chip selloff partly to concerns that closer scrutiny of data-center construction could slow AI spending. Data-center power is guided to nearly double to close to €4B in fiscal 2027, so a slower build-out would hit the fastest-growing division first. UBS (Neutral, €55) has also linked Infineon's profitability outlook to growing competition in China.
03
Cash flow lags the growth. Investment of €3.0–3.5B planned for fiscal 2027 (€2.6B in fiscal 2026) leaves free cash flow guided at about €1.8B reported and €2.2B adjusted, below the €2.26B and €2.74B in the November 2 consensus. Net debt was €4.71B at September 30. If demand slows while the new fabs ramp, their fixed costs stay.
04
IFRS profit trails the headline margin. Charges outside the Segment Result — share-based pay and acquisition amortisation above all — came to €1.01B in fiscal 2026, so IFRS EPS of €1.14 was about a third below adjusted EPS of €1.73, and the September quarter's €0.40 fell short of the €0.52 consensus. In Automotive, the refocused high-voltage drivetrain business will still slightly dilute growth and margin in fiscal 2027, per the CEO.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, acquisition accounting and share-based pay: in fiscal 2026, €437M of acquisition-related amortisation and other costs and €396M of share-based payment sat outside the Segment Result, which is why adjusted EPS (€1.73) is 52% above IFRS EPS (€1.14). Second, restructuring and the high-voltage automotive refocus: €116M of restructuring charges in the year, and the refocus cost about one point of Group margin, per the CFO. Third, cash flow: the €2.18B Marvell automotive Ethernet payment fell in fiscal 2025 and the €564M ams OSRAM payment in Q4 FY2026, so the adjusted free cash flow the company targets (€1.925B in fiscal 2026) gives the cleaner view of underlying cash generation.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Infineon is the largest supplier in the two chip markets that matter most for electrification — cars and power — and a principal supplier of power delivery for AI data centers at a time when demand outruns supply. Fiscal 2026 ended on a record €4.71B quarter at a 23.8% Segment Result margin, and the company guides fiscal 2027 to about €20B of revenue, more than 20% growth, close to €4B of it from data centers, at a mid-twenties margin. Customers have signed or are negotiating more than €10B of multi-year capacity agreements with prepayments, and Automotive is guided to mid-teens growth — the part of the outlook Deutsche Bank's analyst called the biggest positive surprise. Jefferies and Berenberg think the guide is conservative. At €49.88 the stock is on 18x the pre-outlook consensus for fiscal-2027 adjusted EPS, and all seven brokers that published after the print have targets above the price (average €77.6).

Bear Case

The stock more than doubled in three months on expectations, then lost about 40% of its June month-end value before the November results were out — the market is pricing how long the AI cycle lasts, not the last quarter. Brokers cut targets after a record quarter because chip valuations fell across the sector, and that can continue whatever Infineon reports. Trailing IFRS earnings put it on 44x; charges outside the Segment Result came to €1.01B in fiscal 2026, and IFRS EPS missed consensus in Q4. Investment rises to €3.0–3.5B in fiscal 2027, so free cash flow is guided below consensus, and net debt was €4.71B at September 30. Growth this year is supply-limited and depends on new capacity ramping on time; if the US data-center build-out slows, as Bernstein flags, the fastest-growing division takes the hit first.

Top 3 Risks

01
The sector sets the price, not the quarter. Five of the seven brokers that published after the print cut their targets, most citing lower valuations across the chip sector; JPMorgan pointed to doubts about how long and how durable the AI-driven cycle will be. If investors keep paying less for chip earnings, the stock can fall even while Infineon meets its guide.
02
Execution on capacity. Management says fiscal-2027 growth will be supply-limited, a function of ramping new capacity: the fourth building in Dresden, silicon carbide in Kulim, 300 mm gallium nitride in Villach (volume production planned by the end of 2027) and back-end sites in Bangkok and Melaka, plus outside manufacturing partners. Delays cap revenue; if demand turns first, the cost of unused capacity — guided to fall to about €350M in fiscal 2027 from just under €650M — would rise again.
03
AI spending and China. Data-center power revenue is guided to nearly double to close to €4B, and Bernstein cut its target on uncertainty about the pace of US AI data-center build-outs. In cars, management says its China business grew twice as fast as the division in fiscal 2026 in a supply-constrained local market, while UBS has linked Infineon's profitability outlook to rising Chinese competition.
Industry Commentary
AI data centers are rewiring power delivery
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Ever-growing processing capability needs energy-efficient power delivery, and every conversion step between the grid and the chip wastes energy. That pushes operators toward wide-bandgap materials — silicon carbide for the high-voltage stages, gallium nitride for dense intermediate stages — toward 800-volt rack architectures and power delivered vertically beneath the processor, and toward suppliers that can deliver the whole chain at volume. Infineon's aim is more chip content per kilowatt across that chain: it is extending its Nvidia collaboration to gallium nitride intermediate bus converters, and in August 2026 it announced the purchase of C2i Semiconductors, a Bangalore designer of digital power controllers for vertical power delivery. The result shows in the restated Power Systems division: Q4 revenue +48% year on year at a 30.1% margin. Management also expects inference and task-coordination workloads to lift growth in ordinary server processors, which need power too.

Where in the chip cycle are we?
KEY DEBATE

Management's reading on November 10 was an upcycle "fully on track": lead times still stretching, customer and distributor inventories at or slightly below target, customers securing supply further out and supply tightening. Markets were less sure. On November 5 European chip stocks fell sharply as investors reassessed the sector after the US midterm elections, amid concerns that closer scrutiny of data-center construction could slow AI spending (Reuters). After the results, five of seven brokers cut Infineon targets on lower sector valuations while keeping their ratings. Berenberg and Jefferies read the fiscal-2027 guide as conservative; UBS found it less conservative than it had expected. The disagreement is less about the direction of the cycle than about what to pay for it.

Cars: more chips per vehicle in a muted market
RECOVERING

The CEO describes "an overall muted car market", so automotive chipmakers grow through content. Software-defined vehicles — central computers, zone controllers and Ethernet in place of dozens of separate control boxes — are the content driver, and Infineon says carmakers are starting to roll out such architectures in all major regions; it reports a new domain-control design win at a leading Japanese carmaker. Electric-car adoption is rising in Europe, India and Southeast Asia on higher fuel costs and more affordable models, per management, and its refocused high-voltage drivetrain business has turned up. Customers are also rebuilding their chip inventories. Infineon guides Automotive to mid-teens growth in fiscal 2027, and Deutsche Bank's analyst called the automotive outlook the biggest positive surprise of the results.
