01 Company Overview

Infosys is a Bengaluru IT services firm founded in Pune in 1981 by seven founders, which in March 1999 became the first Indian company listed on Nasdaq. Its American shares now trade on the NYSE, one ADS for each rupee share listed on the NSE and BSE in India. In the fiscal year to March 31, 2026 it booked $20.16B of revenue, up 4.6% in dollars and 3.1% in constant currency, with a 20.3% operating margin, $3.31B of net profit and $3.73B of free cash flow. North America supplied 56.1% of revenue and Europe 32.1%; India itself only 2.9%.

The business is still growing; the share price is not. The ADS closed 2025 at $17.82 and June 2026 at $10.49, a 41% fall in six months, and at $11.04 it sits 50.4% below its September 2021 close. The reason is visible in the company's own words. On the July 23, 2026 earnings call, CFO Jayesh Sanghrajka described the pressure every renewal now carries: "On the back of AI, there is an additional deflation or the AI led deflation as we call it. So that is a headwind that is there." The same day Infosys trimmed its FY2027 growth range to 1.5%–3.0% in constant currency from 1.5%–3.5%, and that range includes about 1.7 points from two acquisitions. The quarter itself missed the company's own plan: "Q1 revenue growth was lower than our expectations," the CFO said, naming a one-off 50 basis point hit from a client's program termination, soft volumes, and client productivity demands with high competitive intensity "resulting in softer increase in price versus our expectations." The question for an owner is simple to state: is a 13.6x earnings multiple with a 4.7% dividend yield cheap for a business that still converts more than 100% of its profit to cash, or fair for one whose organic growth is heading toward zero?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Offshore delivery scale (328,062 people) 41 clients above $100M a year Run-the-business switching costs Net cash balance sheet Large-deal engine ($14.9B FY26 TCV) Topaz AI platform and partnerships

The durable advantage is cost and embeddedness at scale. A bank or carmaker that has handed Infosys its application maintenance, testing and infrastructure for a decade would have to rebuild that knowledge to leave, and few clients want the operational risk. The financial result is a business that turned FY2026 net profit of $3.31B into $3.73B of free cash flow, returned over ₹37,500 crore to shareholders for FY2026 through dividends and a ₹18,000 crore buyback, and still held $3.9B of cash and investments at June 30. Europe grew revenue 12.8% in dollars in FY2026 while North America grew 1.2%. The vulnerability is that embeddedness protects the relationship, not the rate card.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
AI-led deflation at every renewal. Clients have always asked for productivity savings when contracts renew; the CFO told the July call that AI adds a further cut on top and that it reaches beyond large deals: "That is not only on the large deal portion, that is also there on the non-large deal portion." Revenue is people × hours × rate, and AI tools attack the hours.
02
Organic growth near zero. The FY2027 range of 1.5%–3.0% includes about 1.7 points from the Optimum and Stratus acquisitions, which leaves roughly −0.2% to +1.3% from the existing business (our arithmetic from the CFO's breakdown). The same breakdown names a further 0.75–1 point drag from work shifting offshore, where it bills at lower rates.
03
One client can move the year. The revised range carries slightly over 1 point of impact from a large European manufacturing client cutting spend, together with deals Infosys declined on returns. With the top 10 clients at 19.9% of revenue, a second such cut would show up in the full-year number.
04
A leadership handover in a hard year. Salil Parekh hands over to Ashiss Kumar Dash on April 1, 2027, in the middle of the industry's pricing reset, and the FY2028 outlook will be the first under him.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the comparisons. First, India's new Labour Codes, notified on November 21, 2025, forced a one-time $143M increase in gratuity and leave liabilities in Q3 FY2026, cutting that quarter's reported operating margin by 2.8 points (18.4% reported against 21.2% adjusted); FY2026 operating margin was 20.3% reported and 21.0% adjusted. Second, the buyback: the board approved on September 11, 2025 a tender-offer repurchase of 10 crore shares (2.41% of the equity) at ₹1,800 each, ₹18,000 crore in all, and FY2026 cash flow shows $2,006M spent on it — at a price 74% above the October 1 close of ₹1,035. Third, acquisitions: Stratus Global ($74M, April 21) and Optimum Achieve ($365M, May 4, 2026) add roughly 1.7 points to FY2027 revenue growth, so reported growth this year flatters the organic trend.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 13.6x trailing and 12.8x forward earnings for a business with a 20%+ operating margin, a 32% return on equity, net cash, and free cash flow that ran at 116.5% of net profit last quarter. The 4.7% dividend yield and an 8.6% free-cash-flow yield pay you to wait. Large-deal wins of $14.9B in FY2026 and $3.6B in Q1, with most of it net new, say clients are still signing, and since the print Investec, Metsä Group, Knorr-Bremse and ABN AMRO have awarded or renewed work; utilisation rose to 84.9%; and the headline that put AI revenues at 8.2% in Q1 says the new work is arriving. Accenture's October 1 report — 7% local-currency growth and a 3%–6% FY2027 guide — is the first sign the sector's demand trough may be behind it. The ADS already fell 41% in six months; a stable second half and a smooth CEO handover could be enough for a rerating toward Accenture's 14.7x.

Bear Case

Strip out the 1.7 points from acquisitions and FY2027 guidance implies organic growth of roughly −0.2% to +1.3% in constant currency. The CFO calls AI-led deflation a headwind that now reaches every renewal, not just large deals, and said Q1 fell short of the company's own expectations on soft volumes and weaker pricing as well as a one-off termination. J.P. Morgan, HSBC and CLSA all cut to Hold after the print. Headcount fell, attrition rose to 13.0%, one European manufacturing client took over a point of growth with it, and eleven of fourteen analysts sit on Hold with a median target only 2.2% above the price. A low multiple on falling growth is how a structurally shrinking business looks on the way down, and a leadership change lands in April 2027 in the middle of it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Price deflation from AI. Management names it explicitly as a headwind on both large and smaller deals. If the productivity AI delivers is passed to clients faster than Infosys wins new AI work, revenue per client falls even while volumes hold.
02
Guidance risk at Q2. The FY2027 range was trimmed once in July after a weaker Q1. On the company's own breakdown, organic growth is already −0.2% to +1.3%; a second cut on October 23 would push that range further toward zero or below.
03
Currency and the ADS premium. A US holder owns a rupee business: the rupee's fall from ₹74 to ₹96 per dollar since 2021 cost ADS holders about 12 points more than rupee holders lost. The ADS also traded 2.6% above the NSE share at the October 2 exchange rate, a premium that can close.
Industry Commentary
AI-led deflation meets the effort-priced model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The CFO's account on the July 23 call is the clearest statement of the problem from inside the industry: "whenever the deal comes up for renewal, we always used to have the additional productivity ask from the client, which is how traditionally this industry has been. On the back of AI, there is an additional deflation or the AI led deflation as we call it." Infosys's answer is to sell AI work itself — the Topaz platform and, in the CEO's words, "strategic partnerships established with all leading AI companies". The scoreboard is the gap between the two: AI revenue — 8.2% of total revenue in Q1 — is, in the CFO's words, "growing at a strong double-digit sequentially over the last many quarters", against deflation on the legacy book. Read against Q1's total constant-currency growth of 2.4%, the deflation is still winning. CEO Salil Parekh's outlook on the call held both halves: "Overall, we see a good pipeline for AI services, and that gives us a good view for continued AI work with our clients. Outside of that, we continue to see the macro-environment remaining uncertain."

Accenture as the sector's weathervane
SECTOR SIGNAL

The largest one-day move in the Infosys ADS since February came on Accenture's results day, not its own: −9.7% on June 18, when Accenture cut its fiscal 2026 range to 3%–4%. Then came +5.5% on October 1, when it reported 7% local-currency growth and guided fiscal 2027 to 3%–6%. Investors are treating the whole group as one trade on enterprise IT budgets. That cuts both ways: Infosys gets credit for a peer's recovery before showing one itself, and it will be marked down with the group if the recovery stalls.

Cash returns as the floor
SUPPORT

Infosys paid out more than ₹37,500 crore to shareholders for FY2026 through dividends and the buyback, and FY2026 cash flow shows $2,133M of dividends and $2,006M of buybacks against $3,733M of free cash flow. A 4.7% trailing yield and an 8.6% free-cash-flow yield put a cash return under the shares that few growth stocks offer. The catch: the buyback was done at ₹1,800 a share, 74% above the October 1 NSE close — a reminder that capital returns support a price, they do not set it.
