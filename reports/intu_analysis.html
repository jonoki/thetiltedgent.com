01 Company Overview

Intuit builds financial software for small and mid-market businesses and for consumers: QuickBooks (accounting, payroll and payments), Mailchimp (marketing), TurboTax (consumer tax preparation), Credit Karma (consumer credit and lending marketplace) and ProConnect/Lacerte (professional tax). It is the dominant franchise in US small-business accounting and consumer DIY tax filing, and it monetises that position through an unusually high-margin subscription model: TTM revenue of $20.93B carries an gross margin of 80.79% and free cash flow of $7.76B (a 37.1% FCF margin).

The most recent completed fiscal year, FY2025 (ended July 31, 2025), produced revenue of $18.83B, up 15.63% YoY, with net income of $3.87B and EPS of $13.67. Intuit ranks roughly #235 globally and #117 in the United States by market capitalisation at $101.19B. That ranking is itself the story: Intuit was worth $219B in July 2025 and has since shed more than half its value, becoming the worst performer in the S&P 500 in 2026 as the market repriced the risk that generative AI commoditises tax and bookkeeping software. The operating business has not deteriorated — it is the terminal-value assumption that broke.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
General-Ledger Switching Cost Accountant Channel Brand (TurboTax / QuickBooks) Proprietary Financial Data Expert Network at Scale Embedded Payments & Payroll Regulatory / Filing Compliance

Intuit's moat has historically rested on three legs, and the market is currently repricing all three. First, switching costs: once a small business's chart of accounts, bank feeds, payroll history and payment rails live inside QuickBooks, migration is a multi-week accounting project rather than a software swap — which is why QuickBooks Online Accounting could grow revenue 22% on higher effective prices without a customer revolt. Second, the accountant channel: roughly the entire US small-business accounting profession is trained on Intuit's file formats, and ProTax exists largely to keep it that way. Third, brand and compliance: filing a tax return is a low-frequency, high-consequence transaction where trust dominates price, which is exactly Goodarzi's "customers buy confidence, not code" argument, backed by the observation that customers spend at least 7x more on human experts than on software. The strategic response to AI is therefore not to compete on model quality but to bundle model output with a credentialed human — TurboTax Live at 53% of TurboTax revenue, and consumption-priced AI-plus-expert services in the August 2026 platform launch. Whether that reframing holds is the entire investment debate; at 13.8x forward earnings, the market is currently pricing meaningful doubt.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Generative AI commoditises the DIY value proposition. The core historical product — explaining a deduction, classifying an expense, summarising a tax rule — is precisely what a general-purpose language model now does for free. Goldman Sachs downgraded on June 2, 2026 with analyst Gabriela Borges warning that "Intuit will likely be hit with lower average revenue and lower market share as competition increases over the next two years," naming Prime Meridian, Perplexity Tax and Chime Tax as emerging AI-native entrants, and cutting the price target from $519 to $276. Management has already conceded share loss in the ~$5B DIY segment.
▸
Consumer concentration and extreme seasonality. TurboTax accounts for roughly a quarter of revenue and, per press reporting, a comparable share of operating income — and virtually all of it is earned in a ten-week window. Online paying units are guided to grow just 2%, so revenue growth of ~7% is almost entirely price and mix. Total IRS filers are expected to fall ~30bps (~2 million units), the sharpest industry contraction since the post-COVID season. A single bad filing season translates directly into a broken fiscal year.
▸
Tax-policy and free-file risk. Any expansion of government-provided free filing — the IRS Direct File programme and the legacy Free File Alliance — attacks the low end of TurboTax's funnel exactly where management already reports the most price sensitivity (filers earning under $50,000). Note: the current legislative and administrative status of IRS Direct File for the 2027 filing season could not be verified from a live source within this report's search budget and is deliberately not stated here. It should be treated as an unquantified but real tail risk, not a resolved question.
▸
Mailchimp underperformance and integration drag. Mailchimp revenue declined slightly year-over-year and was named as a specific target of the 17% workforce reduction and organisational "rightsizing," with churn and small-customer acquisition cited as problems. Excluding Mailchimp, GBS grew 17% versus 15% reported and the Online Ecosystem grew 22% versus 19% — a roughly 200–300bps drag on the company's best segment, plus continuing intangible amortisation from the acquisition.
▸
Securities litigation overhang. Multiple securities class actions allege Intuit overstated TurboTax strength and issued unreliable FY2026 tax guidance over a class period running August 22, 2025 to May 20, 2026. No damages figure has been established. Separately, Intuit has a documented history of regulatory scrutiny over TurboTax "free" advertising claims; specific current-status details on those matters were not verifiable here.

Notable One-Off Items

There are three material non-recurring or non-cash distortions to be aware of, and they are large.

1. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is enormous. FY2026 guidance is for GAAP operating income of $5.705–5.725B against non-GAAP operating income of $8.784–8.804B — a gap of roughly $3.08B, about 14.4% of guided revenue. At the EPS line, GAAP $15.79–15.84 versus non-GAAP $23.80–23.85 is a gap of roughly $8.00 per share, or 34% of the non-GAAP number. That wedge is principally stock-based compensation plus acquisition-related intangible amortisation from Credit Karma (2020) and Mailchimp (2021). The precise split between SBC and amortisation was not disclosed in the sources verified for this report — but a company buying back stock at $1.6B per quarter while excluding equity compensation from headline earnings is, in substance, using cash to fund a cost it does not report. Prefer the GAAP series and the free cash flow line.

2. Restructuring charges of $300–340M. The 17% workforce reduction (~3,000 roles) announced May 20, 2026 carries estimated charges of $300–340M, landing largely in Q4 FY2026 — the quarter being reported August 25, 2026. Expect a visibly depressed GAAP Q4. The CFO stated the majority of the resulting cost savings flow to the bottom line rather than being reinvested, which should make FY2027 margins look artificially strong against an FY2026 base carrying the charge.

3. Litigation. Securities class actions covering the August 22, 2025 – May 20, 2026 class period allege overstated TurboTax strength and unreliable FY2026 guidance. No accrual or damages estimate was verifiable. Intuit also has a well-documented history of regulatory action over TurboTax "free" advertising claims; the current status of those matters could not be confirmed from a live source here and is therefore not quantified.

4. Capital return is genuinely large. $1.6B of buybacks in Q3 FY2026 alone, a new $8B repurchase authorisation, and a dividend raised 15% to $1.20 quarterly. Buyback yield is 1.15% and total shareholder yield 2.45%. Against a $101.19B market cap, the $8B authorisation is ~7.9% of shares — meaningful support, and management is buying at multiples it has not seen in a decade.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 usually means the stock has run hot and buyers may be exhausted; below 30 usually means it has been sold hard and may be due a bounce. It describes momentum, not value.

Five-year trajectory. On a monthly-close basis Intuit went from $539.51 in September 2021 to $369.92 on August 24, 2026 — a price return of −31.4% over five years, against a period in which the NASDAQ-100 roughly doubled. Including dividends the total return is modestly better but still deeply negative in relative terms. That headline conceals two entirely different regimes. From September 2021 the stock rode the post-pandemic software bid to a monthly-close high of $652.30 in November 2021, then gave back 41% into the rate-shock trough of $385.44 in June 2022. It then compounded steadily for three years — $389 (Dec 2022) → $625 (Dec 2023) → $628 (Dec 2024) — before a spectacular final melt-up to an all-time monthly close of $787.63 in June 2025, when market capitalisation reached roughly $219B.

The second regime is the 2026 collapse. From $662.42 at the December 2025 close the stock lost 25% in January 2026 alone (to $498.92) as the "SaaS-pocalypse" narrative — accelerated by Anthropic's Claude model release in February 2026 — spread through application software. It kept bleeding through the spring, then broke on May 21, 2026, falling more than 20% in one to two sessions after the Q3 print cut TurboTax guidance and announced a 17% workforce reduction. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying an 80.8% gross-margin franchise with 20.6% ROIC, 15% revenue growth and a 7.7% free-cash-flow yield at 13.8x forward earnings and a 0.91 PEG — a valuation Intuit has not seen in a decade. The growth engines are working: QuickBooks Online Accounting +22%, Online Ecosystem +22% ex-Mailchimp, QuickBooks Advanced plus Intuit Enterprise Suite ~+38% with IES contracts accelerating 37% quarter-over-quarter, Credit Karma +19%, and TurboTax Live +36% to 53% of TurboTax revenue while attacking a $37B assisted-tax category. Management has taken $300–340M of restructuring pain to cut 17% of headcount with the CFO explicitly committing the savings to the bottom line, and is buying back stock under an $8B authorisation (~7.9% of the float) at the lowest multiple in years.

Bear Case

The market is not mispricing Intuit — it is pricing a genuine terminal-value problem. Generative AI reproduces the core DIY value proposition (explain the deduction, classify the expense, summarise the rule) at near-zero marginal cost, and management has already conceded share loss in the ~$5B DIY segment and acute price sensitivity below $50,000 of income. TurboTax online paying units grow just 2%; all ~7% revenue growth is price and mix, which is exactly the kind of growth that snaps when a free substitute appears. Total IRS filers are contracting ~30bps. Goldman sees "lower average revenue and lower market share... over the next two years." Meanwhile GAAP earnings are 34% below the non-GAAP headline on ~$3.08B of annual add-backs, and free-file policy remains an unquantified tail risk on the most profitable ten weeks of the year.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI substitution in consumer tax and bookkeeping. This is the risk that halved the stock and it is unresolved. Emerging AI-native filers named by Goldman — Prime Meridian, Perplexity Tax, Chime Tax — attack the price-sensitive end of a category where Intuit already reports share loss and only 2% unit growth. If the 2027 filing season shows visible unit defection rather than the current price-and-mix growth, the ~25% of revenue and comparable share of operating income sitting in Consumer re-rates a second time, and the mid-market/Enterprise Suite offset is nowhere near large enough to fill the hole yet.
02
A disappointing FY2027 guide on August 25, 2026. The print lands within 24 hours of this snapshot, after a +41.7% rally off the June low that has taken the stock past Morgan Stanley's $335 target and close to Bank of America's $400. Positioning is stretched, 4.54% of the float is short, and Q4 GAAP results will be distorted by the $300–340M restructuring charge. A soft FY2027 revenue or margin guide into that setup produces asymmetric downside — the same asymmetry that produced a −20%+ two-session move in May.
03
Tax-policy shock to the consumer franchise. Any expansion of government free filing or a change to the Free File framework strikes precisely the sub-$50,000 filer cohort management identified as its weakest flank, in a business earning nearly all its money in ten weeks. The current status of IRS Direct File could not be verified for this report and is therefore unquantified — which is itself the risk: it is a binary policy variable that can be repriced by an administrative announcement, with no warning and no offsetting hedge.
  Industry Commentary
AI agents versus the small-business software stack
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The defining question in application software in 2026 is whether vertical SaaS franchises are moats or interfaces. The "SaaS-pocalypse" trade accelerated after Anthropic's Claude model release in February 2026, and Intuit — the most AI-exposed consumer franchise in the group — became its clearest expression, losing roughly $131B of market value between July 2025 and May 2026 while revenue grew 15%. Intuit's counter-argument is empirical rather than rhetorical: its own AI agents already process recommendations across 50+ million transactions weekly, TurboTax Live grew 36% to 53% of TurboTax revenue, and customers demonstrably spend 7x more on human experts than on software — so the durable margin pool may sit in the assisted layer AI cannot yet credential, not the software layer it is commoditising. The August 2026 consumption-based pricing for AI and expert services is the direct test of that thesis. Until the 2027 filing season produces unit data, this is unresolvable from the outside.

Small-business formation, filer counts and the shrinking top of funnel
ONGOING HEADWIND

Intuit's two largest businesses both depend on unit formation, and both are seeing it stall. Total IRS filers are expected to decline approximately 30 basis points, roughly 2 million units — described by management as the most significant industry-wide contraction since the post-COVID tax season — even as e-files rise 1%, meaning the mix is shifting digital while the pie shrinks. On the business side, growth is being carried by existing customers paying more: QuickBooks Online Accounting's 22% growth was attributed to higher effective prices, customer growth and mix-shift, in that order, and management signalled it will "take pricing actions at the higher end of our portfolio." Price-led growth against flat-to-shrinking units is durable only while switching costs hold. The genuine bright spot is upmarket: the mid-market businesses Intuit now serves are themselves "growing north of 30 percent," which is why Enterprise Suite matters so much more than its current revenue contribution suggests.

The mid-market land grab — where the incremental TAM actually is
EMERGING TAILWIND

Intuit Enterprise Suite is the bull case's load-bearing wall. QuickBooks Advanced plus IES grew approximately 38% in Q3 FY2026, IES contract growth accelerated 37% quarter-over-quarter, and the direct sales force is scaling 30% with improving productivity. The product now carries multi-entity, multi-currency, AI-driven bookkeeping and Intuit Intelligence Chat — an explicit run at the mid-market ERP tier where incumbents charge far more and where AI-native competition is far less mature than in consumer tax. Bull framing: if IES compounds near 38% for two more years it becomes a multi-billion-dollar business growing three times faster than the consolidated company, which alone justifies re-rating from 13.8x forward earnings, and the consumer question becomes a valuation footnote rather than the thesis. Bear framing: Consumer is roughly a quarter of revenue and profit today while IES is a rounding error — the arithmetic simply does not work yet, and the market is right to demand two more years of proof before paying a franchise multiple. The August 25 FY2027 guide is the first instalment of that proof.
