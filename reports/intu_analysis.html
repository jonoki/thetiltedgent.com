01 Company Overview

Intuit builds financial software for small and mid-market businesses and for consumers: QuickBooks (accounting, payroll and payments), Mailchimp (marketing), TurboTax (consumer tax preparation), Credit Karma (consumer credit and lending marketplace) and ProConnect/Lacerte (professional tax). It is the dominant franchise in US small-business accounting and consumer DIY tax filing, and it monetises that position through an unusually high-margin subscription model: TTM revenue of $21.92B carries a gross margin of 80.63% and free cash flow of $9.05B (a 41.3% FCF margin).

The last full fiscal year, FY2026 (ended July 31, 2026), produced revenue of $21.4B, up 14% YoY, GAAP operating income of $5.9B (+20%) and GAAP EPS of $16.46 (+20%); non-GAAP EPS was $24.27. The fiscal year did its job. The August guide is where the story moved: FY2027 revenue growth of 9–10% (cut to 8–9% in November), TurboTax at 2–3%, and a management team that now says it is “deliberately shifting our execution and investments towards accelerating customer acquisition and market share growth” (Goodarzi on the Q4 call, per Investing.com). Intuit was worth $219B in July 2025; at $72.73B on November 23, 2026 ($275.46 × 264.02M shares outstanding on November 12, from the 10-Q; our calculation) it is worth about a third of that, and by June it was the worst performer in the S&P 500 in 2026. Until August the damage was almost all in what investors would pay for each dollar of earnings — the market doubting how long tax and bookkeeping software can keep earning in an AI world. The August print added something the market can point to in the company's own numbers: slower growth, chosen on purpose, to win back customers lost on price.

The first quarter of FY2027 (August–October 2026, reported November 19) split the company in two. The small-business side did what Intuit promised: revenue rose 12% to $4.354B, above guidance, Global Business Solutions grew 15% and QuickBooks Online Accounting 19%, and GAAP EPS rose 63% to $2.59. Credit Karma, the consumer credit marketplace, grew only 9%; Goodarzi said its “performance was below our expectations, reflecting the impact of higher interest rates on our growth” (call transcript, Investing.com), and Intuit cut the full-year revenue guide to 8–9% growth, from 9–10%, while keeping its profit guidance. The same day it agreed to sell Mailchimp, the email-marketing business it bought in 2021 for about $12B (Marketing Dive), to Bending Spoons for $3.68B, with the cash earmarked for buybacks. The stock fell 12.77% on November 20, to $270.44, and closed at $275.46 on November 23, 19.2% below the previous edition.

The incremental TAM is mid-market. QuickBooks Advanced plus Intuit Enterprise Suite (IES) grew approximately 38% in Q3 FY2026, with IES contract growth accelerating 37% quarter-over-quarter and the direct sales force scaling 30%. Management stated that the mid-market businesses it now serves are "growing north of 30 percent." On the Q4 call it added that mid-market customers grew 28% and that Enterprise Suite annualized revenue passed $145 million, about four times the prior year (per Investing.com's transcript summary); on the Q1 FY2027 call Goodarzi put it at $177 million, with mid-market customers up 30% (Investing.com transcript and summary). IES is Intuit's attempt to move upmarket into the ERP tier — multi-entity, multi-currency, AI-driven bookkeeping, and "Intuit Intelligence Chat" — and it is the single most important structural offset to the consumer-tax problem. AI agents already run recommendations across 50+ million transactions weekly, and the August 2026 platform expansion introduces consumption-based pricing for AI and human expert services, most heavily adopted at the Advanced and Plus tiers. On October 15, 2026 Intuit agreed to buy Sequence, whose AI software automates pricing, billing, collections and revenue recognition, to add those tools to Enterprise Suite and QuickBooks Online Advanced; terms were not disclosed (Intuit release). Billing complex contracts is, in the words of Ashley Still, who runs Intuit’s small-business and mid-market group, “one of the biggest reasons they choose an ERP” — the all-in-one finance and operations software larger firms buy.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
General-Ledger Switching Cost Accountant Channel Brand (TurboTax / QuickBooks) Proprietary Financial Data Expert Network at Scale Embedded Payments & Payroll Regulatory / Filing Compliance

Intuit's moat has historically rested on three legs, and in 2026 the market has marked all three down. First, switching costs: once a small business's chart of accounts, bank feeds, payroll history and payment rails live inside QuickBooks, migration is a multi-week accounting project rather than a software swap — which is why QuickBooks Online Accounting could grow revenue 22% on higher effective prices without a customer revolt. Second, the accountant channel: roughly the entire US small-business accounting profession is trained on Intuit's file formats, and ProTax exists largely to keep it that way. Third, brand and compliance: filing a tax return is a low-frequency, high-consequence transaction where trust dominates price, which is exactly Goodarzi's "customers buy confidence, not code" argument, backed by the observation that customers spend at least 7x more on human experts than on software. The strategic response to AI is therefore not to compete on model quality but to bundle model output with a credentialed human — TurboTax Live at 53% of TurboTax revenue, and consumption-priced AI-plus-expert services in the August 2026 platform launch. The FY2026 print tested the brand leg and found a crack: by management's own account price, not product, is now the main reason customers leave TurboTax, and the FY2027 plan answers with cheaper entry points rather than higher prices. The switching-cost leg looks intact — QuickBooks Online Accounting grew 20% in Q4 and 19% in Q1 FY2027. The new crack is Credit Karma, which has no switching cost at all: it is paid by lenders and insurers for qualified applications, so higher interest rates (Goodarzi’s explanation) or pressure from AI tools (the risk Piper Sandler and RBC point to) hit revenue straight away. Whether the moat holds at a lower price point is the investment debate; at 12.0x consensus FY2027 earnings the market is pricing deep doubt.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Generative AI commoditises the DIY value proposition. The core historical product — explaining a deduction, classifying an expense, summarising a tax rule — is precisely what a general-purpose language model now does for free. Goldman Sachs downgraded on June 2, 2026 with analyst Gabriela Borges warning that "Intuit will likely be hit with lower average revenue and lower market share as competition increases over the next two years," naming Prime Meridian, Perplexity Tax and Chime Tax as emerging AI-native entrants, and cutting the price target from $519 to $276. Management has already conceded share loss in the ~$5B DIY segment.
▸
Consumer concentration and extreme seasonality. TurboTax accounts for roughly a quarter of revenue and, per press reporting, a comparable share of operating income — and virtually all of it is earned in a ten-week window. FY2026 TurboTax revenue grew 7% almost entirely on price and mix; FY2027 is guided to just 2–3% as Intuit cuts entry prices to win customers back, and Credit Karma, the other consumer business, is now guided to only 3–7%. Total IRS filers were expected to fall about 0.3% in the 2026 season, the sharpest industry contraction since the post-COVID season. A single bad filing season translates directly into a broken fiscal year.
▸
Tax-policy and free-file risk. Any expansion of government-provided free filing — the IRS Direct File programme and the legacy Free File Alliance — attacks the low end of TurboTax's funnel exactly where management already reports the most price sensitivity (filers earning under $50,000). In November 2025 the IRS said Direct File would not be offered in the 2026 filing season and that no future launch date had been set (Associated Press, as reported by CPA Practice Advisor, November 5, 2025), so the threat is a future revival rather than a live programme.
▸
The Mailchimp sale has to close. Mailchimp, bought in 2021 for about $12B (Marketing Dive), stopped growing: its own segment since August 1, it fell 1% to $313M in Q1 FY2027 and is guided to $1.256–1.266B, −1% to 0% for the year. On November 19 Intuit agreed to sell it to Bending Spoons for $3.68B, of which about $368M is reinvested in Bending Spoons shares, subject to adjustments, US antitrust clearance and other regulatory approvals, with closing expected in early 2027 and an outside date of August 19, 2027 (8-K). Until it closes, the guidance still includes Mailchimp, and the buybacks the cash is meant to fund wait on the deal.
▸
Securities litigation overhang. Two securities class actions, filed on July 10 and August 17, 2026 in federal court in Northern California (Intuit 10-Q), allege that Intuit overstated its competitive advantages and growth and that its FY2026 TurboTax revenue-growth guidance was unreliable, according to the investor law firm Kessler Topaz Meltzer & Check, whose notice gives a class period of February 25, 2025 to June 1, 2026. No damages figure has been established. Separately, Intuit has a documented history of regulatory scrutiny over TurboTax "free" advertising claims.

Notable One-Off Items

Five items distort the reported figures or are about to change them, and they are large.

1. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap just got much smaller — by definition. On the old basis FY2026 GAAP EPS of $16.46 sat $7.81 below non-GAAP EPS of $24.27, a wedge of about 32% made up mainly of stock-based compensation and amortisation from the Credit Karma (2020) and Mailchimp (2021) acquisitions. From August 1, 2026, "share-based compensation expense will no longer be excluded from Intuit's non-GAAP financial measures." The FY2027 guide carries $2.020B of it ($5.81 per share), and the remaining gap is small: GAAP operating income $7.408–7.490B vs non-GAAP $8.063–8.145B (a $655M difference), and GAAP EPS $20.12–20.36 vs non-GAAP $22.88–23.12 ($2.76, about 12%). This is the honest way to report, and it matters for reading the reaction: the $27.30 FY2027 EPS consensus the guide "missed" was set before Intuit announced the change on August 25 (its May 20 Q3 release still excluded share-based compensation), and sits 12% above FY2026's old-basis $24.27. Adding back $5.81 puts the guide at $28.69–28.93 on the old basis — our arithmetic. Per Investing.com's summary of the call, management targets share-based compensation of 8% of revenue by fiscal 2030. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying an 80.6% gross-margin franchise with 22.7% ROIC and a 12.4% free-cash-flow yield at 12.0x consensus FY2027 earnings (our calculations at $275.46). The small-business engine that carries the valuation just accelerated rather than slowed: GBS +15%, the Online Ecosystem +20%, QuickBooks Online Accounting +19%, mid-market customers +30% and Enterprise Suite at $177M of annualized revenue. Intuit beat its own Q1 guide on every line and kept its full-year operating-income and EPS guidance despite the Credit Karma cut. Selling Mailchimp removes a business that had stopped growing and hands $3.31B of cash to buybacks; with the $6.4B of authorisation left after $1.5B repurchased in Q1, that is about 13% of the market cap (our calculation). Credit Karma’s problem, if Goodarzi is right that it is interest rates, is cyclical, and the stock now prices it as permanent.

Bear Case

The bear case stopped being a forecast in August and gained a second leg in November. Intuit now guides 8–9% revenue growth, TurboTax 2–3% and Credit Karma 3–7%, down from 20% last year, with Q2 revenue guided up only 6–7%; analysts at Piper Sandler and RBC also point to pressure on Credit Karma from AI tools, not only rates. The CEO says price is the number one reason customers leave TurboTax and that the company "fell short of our new customer targets" — so the moat that let Intuit raise prices is the thing now being tested, and the fix is cheaper entry points, which dilutes ARPU. Mailchimp is going for $3.68B, less than a third of the roughly $12B Intuit paid in 2021, a reminder that its big acquisitions have not all held their value. Goldman sees "lower average revenue and lower market share... over the next two years." On this view 12.0x FY2027 earnings is not cheap for a company that went from 14% growth delivered in FY2026 to 8–9% guided for FY2027, a guide already cut once, and free-file policy remains an unquantified tail risk on the most profitable ten weeks of the year.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI substitution in consumer tax and bookkeeping. This is the risk that halved the stock and it is unresolved. Emerging AI-native filers named by Goldman — Prime Meridian, Perplexity Tax, Chime Tax — attack the price-sensitive end of a category where Intuit already reports share loss and now names price as the main reason customers leave. If the 2027 filing season shows unit defection even after the entry-price cuts, the ~25% of revenue sitting in TurboTax gets marked down again, and the mid-market/Enterprise Suite offset is nowhere near large enough to fill the hole yet.
02
The customer-acquisition reset buys neither units nor growth. FY2027 is a deliberate trade: lower entry prices (QuickBooks Free, Credit Karma Tax) and more acquisition spend in exchange for customer growth. If the 2027 filing season shows lower ARPU without a unit recovery, Intuit gets the worst of both — the 2–3% TurboTax guide becomes a ceiling, and the GBS reset to 13–14% (with a 10–15% three-year range cited on the Q4 call, per Investing.com) looks like the start of a slide rather than a pause. The first test came early: Credit Karma, the other half of the consumer plan, is now guided to 3–7% growth. The Hold targets named since the print, $292–$320, only 6–16% above the share price after the November cuts, says many analysts are still waiting for evidence.
03
Tax-policy shock to the consumer franchise. Any expansion of government free filing or a change to the Free File framework strikes precisely the sub-$50,000 filer cohort management identified as its weakest flank, in a business earning nearly all its money in ten weeks. The IRS said in November 2025 that Direct File would not run in the 2026 season and set no date for its return, so the risk is a future revival: a policy decision that could come with little warning and that Intuit cannot hedge.
  Industry Commentary
AI agents versus the small-business software stack
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The defining question in application software in 2026 is whether specialised subscription software keeps its customers or becomes a mere front end that AI tools can replace. The "SaaS-pocalypse" trade accelerated after Anthropic's Claude model release in February 2026, and Intuit — the most AI-exposed consumer franchise in the group — became its clearest expression, losing roughly $150B of market value between June 2025 and June 2026 (our estimate) while FY2026 revenue grew 14%. Intuit's counter-argument is empirical rather than rhetorical: its own AI agents already process recommendations across 50+ million transactions weekly, TurboTax Live grew 37% to 53% of TurboTax revenue, and customers demonstrably spend 7x more on human experts than on software — so the durable margin pool may sit in the assisted layer AI cannot yet credential, not the software layer it is commoditising. The August 2026 consumption-based pricing for AI and expert services is the direct test of that thesis. The FY2026 print sharpened the question: management now names price, not product quality, as the main reason customers leave TurboTax — which is what commoditisation looks like from the inside. Until the 2027 filing season produces unit data, the answer is unresolvable from the outside. In October the trade partly reversed: CNBC reported on October 16 that the iShares software ETF (IGV) had gained more than 13% over three months while the semiconductor ETF (SOXX) fell more than 4%, as fears that AI would make software obsolete faded. Intuit rose 23.7% in the month, then gave it back in November, when the AI question reached a business the market had not been watching: analysts at Piper Sandler and RBC pointed to pressure on Credit Karma from AI tools (Investing.com, November 20), while Goodarzi pointed to higher interest rates.

Small-business formation, filer counts and the shrinking top of funnel
ONGOING HEADWIND

Intuit's two largest businesses both depend on unit formation, and both are seeing it stall. Total IRS filers were expected to fall about 0.3% in the 2026 season — described by management as the most significant industry-wide contraction since the post-COVID tax season — even as e-files rise 1%, meaning the mix is shifting digital while the pie shrinks. On the business side, growth is being carried by existing customers paying more: In Q3, QuickBooks Online Accounting's 22% growth was attributed to higher effective prices, customer growth and mix-shift, in that order. By the Q4 call the emphasis had flipped to customer acquisition, with a free QuickBooks tier as the entry point. Price-led growth against flat-to-shrinking units is durable only while switching costs hold. The genuine bright spot is upmarket: the mid-market businesses Intuit now serves are themselves "growing north of 30 percent," which is why Enterprise Suite matters so much more than its current revenue contribution suggests.

The mid-market land grab — where the incremental TAM actually is
EMERGING TAILWIND

Intuit Enterprise Suite is the bull case's load-bearing wall. QuickBooks Advanced plus IES grew approximately 38% in Q3 FY2026, and on the Q4 call management put mid-market customer growth at 28% and Enterprise Suite annualized revenue above $145 million, about four times the prior year (per Investing.com); one quarter later those figures were 30% and $177 million (Q1 FY2027 call, per Investing.com). The product now carries multi-entity, multi-currency, AI-driven bookkeeping and Intuit Intelligence Chat — an explicit run at the mid-market ERP tier where incumbents charge far more and where AI-native competition is far less mature than in consumer tax. Bull framing: if IES keeps growing near 38% for two more years it becomes a multi-billion-dollar business growing three times faster than the consolidated company, which alone would justify a higher price than 12.0x forward earnings, and the consumer question becomes a valuation footnote rather than the thesis. Bear framing: Consumer is roughly a quarter of revenue and profit today while IES is a rounding error — the arithmetic simply does not work yet, and the market is right to demand two more years of proof before paying a premium valuation. The FY2027 guide of 13–14% growth for GBS ex-Mailchimp did not supply that proof; Q1’s 15% and the sale of Mailchimp make GBS a cleaner read, and the quarterly Enterprise Suite disclosures now have to do the rest.
