01 Company Overview

Intuit builds financial software for small and mid-market businesses and for consumers: QuickBooks (accounting, payroll and payments), Mailchimp (marketing), TurboTax (consumer tax preparation), Credit Karma (consumer credit and lending marketplace) and ProConnect/Lacerte (professional tax). It is the dominant franchise in US small-business accounting and consumer DIY tax filing, and it monetises that position through an unusually high-margin subscription model: TTM revenue of $21.45B carries a gross margin of 80.98% and free cash flow of $8.66B (a 40.4% FCF margin).

The fiscal year just reported, FY2026 (ended July 31, 2026), produced revenue of $21.4B, up 14% YoY, GAAP operating income of $5.9B (+20%) and GAAP EPS of $16.46 (+20%); non-GAAP EPS was $24.27. The fiscal year did its job. The guide is where the story moved: FY2027 revenue growth of 9–10%, TurboTax at 2–3%, and a management team that now says it is “deliberately shifting our execution and investments towards accelerating customer acquisition and market share growth” (Goodarzi on the Q4 call, per Investing.com). Intuit was worth $219B in July 2025; at $81.27B on September 21, 2026 ($304.12 × 267.24M shares, our calculation) it has lost well over half of that, and by June it was the worst performer in the S&P 500 in 2026. Until August the damage was almost all multiple — the market doubting the terminal value of tax and bookkeeping software in an AI world. The August print added something the market can now point to in the company's own numbers: slower growth, chosen on purpose, to buy back customers lost on price.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
General-Ledger Switching Cost Accountant Channel Brand (TurboTax / QuickBooks) Proprietary Financial Data Expert Network at Scale Embedded Payments & Payroll Regulatory / Filing Compliance

Intuit's moat has historically rested on three legs, and the market is currently repricing all three. First, switching costs: once a small business's chart of accounts, bank feeds, payroll history and payment rails live inside QuickBooks, migration is a multi-week accounting project rather than a software swap — which is why QuickBooks Online Accounting could grow revenue 22% on higher effective prices without a customer revolt. Second, the accountant channel: roughly the entire US small-business accounting profession is trained on Intuit's file formats, and ProTax exists largely to keep it that way. Third, brand and compliance: filing a tax return is a low-frequency, high-consequence transaction where trust dominates price, which is exactly Goodarzi's "customers buy confidence, not code" argument, backed by the observation that customers spend at least 7x more on human experts than on software. The strategic response to AI is therefore not to compete on model quality but to bundle model output with a credentialed human — TurboTax Live at 53% of TurboTax revenue, and consumption-priced AI-plus-expert services in the August 2026 platform launch. The FY2026 print tested the brand leg and found a crack: by management's own account price, not product, is now the main reason customers leave TurboTax, and the FY2027 plan answers with cheaper entry points rather than higher prices. The switching-cost leg looks intact — QuickBooks Online Accounting still grew 20% in Q4. Whether the moat holds at a lower price point is the investment debate; at 12.7x consensus forward earnings the market is pricing meaningful doubt.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Generative AI commoditises the DIY value proposition. The core historical product — explaining a deduction, classifying an expense, summarising a tax rule — is precisely what a general-purpose language model now does for free. Goldman Sachs downgraded on June 2, 2026 with analyst Gabriela Borges warning that "Intuit will likely be hit with lower average revenue and lower market share as competition increases over the next two years," naming Prime Meridian, Perplexity Tax and Chime Tax as emerging AI-native entrants, and cutting the price target from $519 to $276. Management has already conceded share loss in the ~$5B DIY segment.
▸
Consumer concentration and extreme seasonality. TurboTax accounts for roughly a quarter of revenue and, per press reporting, a comparable share of operating income — and virtually all of it is earned in a ten-week window. FY2026 TurboTax revenue grew 7% almost entirely on price and mix; FY2027 is guided to just 2–3% as Intuit cuts entry prices to win customers back. Total IRS filers are expected to fall ~30bps (~2 million units), the sharpest industry contraction since the post-COVID season. A single bad filing season translates directly into a broken fiscal year.
▸
Tax-policy and free-file risk. Any expansion of government-provided free filing — the IRS Direct File programme and the legacy Free File Alliance — attacks the low end of TurboTax's funnel exactly where management already reports the most price sensitivity (filers earning under $50,000). Note: the current legislative and administrative status of IRS Direct File for the 2027 filing season could not be verified from a live source within this report's search budget and is deliberately not stated here. It should be treated as an unquantified but real tail risk, not a resolved question.
▸
Mailchimp underperformance and integration drag. Mailchimp revenue declined slightly year-over-year and was named as a specific target of the 17% workforce reduction and organisational "rightsizing." From FY2027 it is its own reporting segment, guided to $1.256–1.266B, −1% to 0% — a ~$1.26B business that is no longer growing, carved out of the GBS headline so it stops diluting it. In Q3 FY2026 the drag was visible: GBS grew 17% ex-Mailchimp versus 15% reported. The acquisition also leaves continuing intangible amortisation.
▸
Securities litigation overhang. Multiple securities class actions allege Intuit overstated TurboTax strength and issued unreliable FY2026 tax guidance over a class period running August 22, 2025 to May 20, 2026. No damages figure has been established. Separately, Intuit has a documented history of regulatory scrutiny over TurboTax "free" advertising claims; specific current-status details on those matters were not verifiable here.

Notable One-Off Items

There are three material non-recurring or non-cash distortions to be aware of, and they are large.

1. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap just got much smaller — by definition. On the old basis FY2026 GAAP EPS of $16.46 sat $7.81 below non-GAAP EPS of $24.27, a wedge of about 32% made up mainly of stock-based compensation and amortisation from the Credit Karma (2020) and Mailchimp (2021) acquisitions. From August 1, 2026, "share-based compensation expense will no longer be excluded from Intuit's non-GAAP financial measures." The FY2027 guide carries $2.020B of it ($5.81 per share), and the remaining gap is small: GAAP operating income $7.408–7.490B vs non-GAAP $8.063–8.145B (a $655M difference), and GAAP EPS $20.12–20.36 vs non-GAAP $22.88–23.12 ($2.76, about 12%). This is the honest way to report, and it matters for reading the reaction: the $27.30 FY2027 EPS consensus the guide "missed" may have been on the old basis. Adding back $5.81 puts the guide at $28.69–28.93 — our arithmetic, and only valid if that consensus still excluded SBC, which we could not confirm. Per Investing.com's summary of the call, management targets share-based compensation of 8% of revenue by fiscal 2030.

2. Restructuring landed at $293M. The 17% workforce reduction announced May 20, 2026 cost $293M in fiscal 2026, below the $300–340M range estimated in May. It hit Q4: GAAP operating income was $475M and GAAP EPS $1.34, against $4.03 non-GAAP. FY2027's 26–27% guided GAAP operating-income growth is flattered by lapping that charge.

3. Litigation. Securities class actions covering the August 22, 2025 – May 20, 2026 class period allege overstated TurboTax strength and unreliable FY2026 guidance. No accrual or damages estimate was verifiable, and the status was not re-checked for this edition. Intuit also has a well-documented history of regulatory action over TurboTax "free" advertising claims; current status unconfirmed.

4. Capital return is large and growing. Intuit repurchased $5.5B of stock in FY2026, up 96%, has $7.9B of authorisation remaining, and raised the quarterly dividend 15% to $1.38. Against an $81.27B market cap the remaining authorisation is about 9.7% of the company (our calculation) — bought at 9.4x free cash flow.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 usually means the stock has run hot and buyers may be exhausted; below 30 usually means it has been sold hard and may be due a bounce. It describes momentum, not value.

Five-year trajectory. On a monthly-close basis Intuit went from $539.51 in September 2021 to $304.12 on September 21, 2026 — a price return of −43.6% over five years. Including dividends the total return is modestly better but still deeply negative in relative terms. That headline conceals two entirely different regimes. From September 2021 the stock rode the post-pandemic software bid to a monthly-close high of $652.30 in November 2021, then gave back 41% into the rate-shock trough of $385.44 in June 2022. It then compounded steadily for three years — $389 (Dec 2022) → $625 (Dec 2023) → $628 (Dec 2024) — before a spectacular final melt-up to an all-time monthly close of $787.63 in June 2025, when market capitalisation reached roughly $219B.

From $662.42 at the December 2025 close the stock lost 25% in January 2026 alone (to $498.92) as the "SaaS-pocalypse" narrative — accelerated by Anthropic's Claude model release in February 2026 — spread through application software. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying an 81.0% gross-margin franchise with 22.9% ROIC, 14% revenue growth just delivered and a 10.7% free-cash-flow yield at 12.7x consensus forward earnings (our calculations at $304.12). The quarter itself beat. The FY2027 guide looks worse than it is: it now charges $5.81 a share of stock compensation that FY2026 excluded, guides GAAP operating income up 26–27%, and deliberately spends on customer acquisition after a year in which price drove customers away — a management team choosing units over ARPU before a competitor forces it. The growth engines still work: Credit Karma +20%, QuickBooks Online Accounting +23% for the year, TurboTax Live 53% of TurboTax, Enterprise Suite annualized revenue past $145M and about four times the prior year. And the company returned $5.5B through buybacks in FY2026 with $7.9B of authorisation left — about 9.7% of the market cap.

Bear Case

The bear case stopped being a forecast in August. Intuit itself now guides 9–10% growth, TurboTax 2–3%, Credit Karma down to 11–13% from 20%, and a Mailchimp segment that no longer grows. The CEO says price is the number one reason customers leave TurboTax and that the company "fell short of our new customer targets" — so the moat that let Intuit raise prices is the thing now being tested, and the fix is cheaper entry points, which dilutes ARPU. Generative AI still reproduces much of the DIY value proposition at near-zero marginal cost; Goldman sees "lower average revenue and lower market share... over the next two years." On this view 12.7x forward earnings is not cheap for a business whose own growth guide just dropped from 14% to 9–10%, and free-file policy remains an unquantified tail risk on the most profitable ten weeks of the year.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI substitution in consumer tax and bookkeeping. This is the risk that halved the stock and it is unresolved. Emerging AI-native filers named by Goldman — Prime Meridian, Perplexity Tax, Chime Tax — attack the price-sensitive end of a category where Intuit already reports share loss and now names price as the main reason customers leave. If the 2027 filing season shows unit defection even after the entry-price cuts, the ~25% of revenue sitting in TurboTax re-rates again, and the mid-market/Enterprise Suite offset is nowhere near large enough to fill the hole yet.
02
The customer-acquisition reset buys neither units nor growth. FY2027 is a deliberate trade: lower entry prices (QuickBooks Free, Credit Karma Tax) and more acquisition spend in exchange for customer growth. If the 2027 filing season shows lower ARPU without a unit recovery, Intuit gets the worst of both — the 2–3% TurboTax guide becomes a ceiling, and the GBS reset to 13–14% (with a 10–15% three-year range cited on the Q4 call, per Investing.com) looks like the start of a slide rather than a pause. The Hold cluster at $300–$315 says the Street is waiting for that evidence.
03
Tax-policy shock to the consumer franchise. Any expansion of government free filing or a change to the Free File framework strikes precisely the sub-$50,000 filer cohort management identified as its weakest flank, in a business earning nearly all its money in ten weeks. The current status of IRS Direct File could not be verified for this report and is therefore unquantified — which is itself the risk: it is a binary policy variable that can be repriced by an administrative announcement, with no warning and no offsetting hedge.
  Industry Commentary
AI agents versus the small-business software stack
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The defining question in application software in 2026 is whether vertical SaaS franchises are moats or interfaces. The "SaaS-pocalypse" trade accelerated after Anthropic's Claude model release in February 2026, and Intuit — the most AI-exposed consumer franchise in the group — became its clearest expression, losing roughly $131B of market value between July 2025 and May 2026 while revenue grew 15%. Intuit's counter-argument is empirical rather than rhetorical: its own AI agents already process recommendations across 50+ million transactions weekly, TurboTax Live grew 36% to 53% of TurboTax revenue, and customers demonstrably spend 7x more on human experts than on software — so the durable margin pool may sit in the assisted layer AI cannot yet credential, not the software layer it is commoditising. The August 2026 consumption-based pricing for AI and expert services is the direct test of that thesis. The FY2026 print sharpened the question: management now names price, not product quality, as the main reason customers leave TurboTax — which is what commoditisation looks like from the inside. Until the 2027 filing season produces unit data, the answer is unresolvable from the outside.

Small-business formation, filer counts and the shrinking top of funnel
ONGOING HEADWIND

Intuit's two largest businesses both depend on unit formation, and both are seeing it stall. Total IRS filers are expected to decline approximately 30 basis points, roughly 2 million units — described by management as the most significant industry-wide contraction since the post-COVID tax season — even as e-files rise 1%, meaning the mix is shifting digital while the pie shrinks. On the business side, growth is being carried by existing customers paying more: In Q3, QuickBooks Online Accounting's 22% growth was attributed to higher effective prices, customer growth and mix-shift, in that order. By the Q4 call the emphasis had flipped to customer acquisition, with a free QuickBooks tier as the entry point. Price-led growth against flat-to-shrinking units is durable only while switching costs hold. The genuine bright spot is upmarket: the mid-market businesses Intuit now serves are themselves "growing north of 30 percent," which is why Enterprise Suite matters so much more than its current revenue contribution suggests.

The mid-market land grab — where the incremental TAM actually is
EMERGING TAILWIND

Intuit Enterprise Suite is the bull case's load-bearing wall. QuickBooks Advanced plus IES grew approximately 38% in Q3 FY2026, and on the Q4 call management put mid-market customer growth at 28% and Enterprise Suite annualized revenue above $145 million, about four times the prior year (per Investing.com). The product now carries multi-entity, multi-currency, AI-driven bookkeeping and Intuit Intelligence Chat — an explicit run at the mid-market ERP tier where incumbents charge far more and where AI-native competition is far less mature than in consumer tax. Bull framing: if IES compounds near 38% for two more years it becomes a multi-billion-dollar business growing three times faster than the consolidated company, which alone justifies re-rating from 12.7x forward earnings, and the consumer question becomes a valuation footnote rather than the thesis. Bear framing: Consumer is roughly a quarter of revenue and profit today while IES is a rounding error — the arithmetic simply does not work yet, and the market is right to demand two more years of proof before paying a franchise multiple. The FY2027 guide of 13–14% growth for GBS ex-Mailchimp did not supply that proof; the quarterly Enterprise Suite disclosures now have to.
