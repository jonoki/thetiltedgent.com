01 Company Overview

The name on the ticker is 150 years old; the company behind it is six. In 1859 Robert Gardner started a governor company in Quincy, Illinois, making speed controls for steam engines. It became Gardner Denver, was bought by KKR in 2013 for $3.9B, and returned to the public market in 2017. On February 29, 2020 it merged with the industrial segment of Ingersoll-Rand plc in a Reverse Morris Trust, took the Ingersoll Rand name and the IR ticker, and joined the S&P 500 before the open on March 3, 2020. The old Ingersoll-Rand, keeping its HVAC business, became Trane Technologies. So any long-run "IR" chart from before March 2020 is a different company — this report starts in November 2021.

What it sells is unglamorous and hard to do without: compressors, vacuum pumps, blowers, and precision pumps and fluid-handling equipment that sit inside factories, water plants, pharma lines, power stations and data centers. FY2025 revenue was $7.65B, up 5.75%; trailing-twelve-month revenue is $8.05B. The story since 2020 has been steady growth built two ways: a large installed base that throws off service revenue, plus a steady diet of bolt-on acquisitions — on the Q3 2026 call CEO Vicente Reynal said eight deals have closed or been signed so far in 2026, with seven more at the letter-of-intent stage. The market paid up for that growth through 2024 (a $104.17 month-end close in November 2024) and spent most of the next two years marking it down, to a $71.64 month-end in May 2026. Q3 2026 changed the tone: organic orders jumped 18% after rising 1.6% in Q2, and at $82.73 the stock is 20.6% below that peak, against 30.6% below at the last edition.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed-base aftermarket Mission-critical, low-cost-share equipment Bolt-on M&A engine Brand heritage since 1859 Investment-grade balance sheet (Baa1) ~95% FCF conversion guide (ex-recoveries)

The moat is the cost-share asymmetry. A compressor or a metering pump costs little next to the production line it serves, and when it fails the line stops. Customers don't shop hard on price for the spare part that restarts a pharma batch. That's why gross margin has stayed above 43% since FY2024 (43.81% in FY2024, 43.61% in FY2025, 43.09% TTM), and why the company can fund acquisitions from its own cash: $1.52B of TTM free cash flow, including $187.5M of one-time ILC Dover recoveries. The weakness is that the moat protects margins on existing equipment, not demand for new equipment. When factories stop expanding, orders slow — organic orders grew just 1.6% in Q2 2026 before jumping 18% in Q3, which shows how much a few large projects move the number.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth that is partly bought. Q3 revenue +6% reported, +4% organic; the FY2026 guide of 5.5–7.0% reported includes about 2.5 points from acquisitions and 1 point from currency. The goodwill left over from deals can be written down: H1 2025 carried $265.8M of goodwill and intangible impairments. The ILC Dover settlement recoveries now coming in are a one-time offset, not a repeatable profit.
02
China pricing. In Q2 the CFO said China was where inflation was hardest to offset with price, and Reynal tied that to overcapacity; in Q3 the CFO said pricing there is still a headwind but stabilizing. ITS margin without tariff refunds was still down 120 basis points from a year earlier, which the release puts down mainly to inflation.
03
Valuation still assumes steady growth. The stock trades at 23.4x GAAP trailing EPS, an EPS figure lifted by the one-time ILC Dover recovery; on the $3.64 midpoint of adjusted EPS guidance it is 22.7x, and EV/EBITDA is about 17.5x on TTM figures (calculated below). Those are multiples for a company that grows through its cycle. If growth stalls, the P/E has further to fall.

Notable One-Off Items

The biggest item now runs the other way. Q3 2026 GAAP results include a $380.1M net recovery from settlement agreements with insurance carriers and other third parties over the ILC Dover transaction: gross recoveries of about $425.1M, of which $187.5M was collected in Q2 and Q3 and the rest is expected in Q4. The company leaves it out of adjusted earnings, which is why Q3 GAAP EPS ($1.73) sits far above adjusted ($1.07). Second, IEEPA tariff refunds arrived in Q3: adjusted EPS was $1.07 with them and $1.01 without, adjusted EBITDA $584M against $552M, and the full-year guide excludes them. Third, the 2025 base is low: $265.8M of goodwill and intangible impairments in H1 2025 cut FY2025 GAAP EPS to $1.45. The adjusted EPS guide of $3.58–$3.70 is the cleaner measure of earnings power. Fourth, Q3 capital use: $350M of share repurchases and $90M of M&A.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business hasn't broken, and Q3 showed demand coming back. Revenue rose every year from $5.15B in FY2021 to $7.65B in FY2025, gross margin has stayed above 43% since FY2024, and net leverage is down to 1.6x. Organic orders jumped 18% in Q3 with a 1.14 book-to-bill, management raised the adjusted EPS guide to $3.58–$3.70, and the board added $2B to the buyback. Seven more deals are at the letter-of-intent stage and about $238M of ILC Dover recoveries is still to come in Q4. At about 22.7x the guidance midpoint, orders that keep growing could close more of the 20.6% gap to the 2024 peak.

Bear Case

Strip out the one-offs and the quarter is less dramatic. Without tariff refunds, adjusted EBITDA rose 1% on 6% more revenue, and ITS margin fell 120 basis points on inflation. One roughly $195M data-center order is about 8% of the quarter's orders. Growth is still partly bought: the guide includes about 2.5 points from acquisitions. ROIC is 8.99%, a thin return for a company whose strategy is paying for other companies, and the $265.8M impairment in 2025 showed that some past deals were overpriced. At 23.4x GAAP trailing earnings that include a one-time recovery, the stock is priced for steady growth at a time when much of its growth comes from acquisitions.

Top 3 Risks

01
Orders that don't last. Q3 organic orders +18% followed +1.6% in Q2, and one roughly $195M data-center order is part of the jump. If orders fall back in Q4, reported with results expected in February, the raised organic guide of +2% to +3.5% comes under pressure.
02
M&A execution and impairment. Seven more deals at the letter-of-intent stage means capital keeps flowing into acquisitions. Single-digit ROIC and the 2025 write-down show what overpaying costs.
03
ITS margin pressure. ITS is 78% of revenue, and without tariff refunds its margin fell 120 basis points from a year earlier to 27.8%, mainly on inflation, though it rose 100 basis points from Q2. If that pressure persists, group margin falls with it.
