01 Company Overview

The name on the ticker is 150 years old; the company behind it is six. In 1859 Robert Gardner started a governor company in Quincy, Illinois, making speed controls for steam engines. It became Gardner Denver, was bought by KKR in 2013 for $3.9B, and returned to the public market in 2017. On February 29, 2020 it merged with the industrial segment of Ingersoll-Rand plc in a Reverse Morris Trust, took the Ingersoll Rand name and the IR ticker, and joined the S&P 500 before the open on March 3, 2020. The old Ingersoll-Rand, keeping its HVAC business, became Trane Technologies. So any long-run "IR" chart from before March 2020 is a different company — this report starts in September 2021.

What it sells is unglamorous and hard to do without: compressors, vacuum pumps, blowers, and precision pumps and fluid-handling equipment that sit inside factories, water plants, pharma lines and power stations. FY2025 revenue was $7.65B, up 5.75%; trailing-twelve-month revenue is $7.94B. The story since 2020 has been a machine for compounding: a large installed base that throws off service revenue, plus a steady diet of bolt-on acquisitions — management said on the Q2 2026 call that 11 transactions are under letter of intent. The market paid up for that machine through 2024 (a $104.17 month-end close in November 2024) and has spent the 21 months since marking it down. At $72.31 the stock is 30.6% below that peak even though every annual revenue line since has gone up.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed-base aftermarket Mission-critical, low-cost-share equipment Bolt-on M&A engine Brand heritage since 1859 Investment-grade balance sheet (Baa1) ~95% FCF conversion guide

The moat is the cost-share asymmetry. A compressor or a metering pump costs little next to the production line it serves, and when it fails the line stops. Customers don't shop hard on price for the spare part that restarts a pharma batch. That's why gross margin has held above 42% (42.82% TTM, 43.61% in FY2025), and why the company can fund acquisitions from its own cash: $1.22B of TTM free cash flow. The weakness is that the moat protects margins on existing equipment, not demand for new equipment. When factories stop expanding, orders slow — organic orders grew just 1.6% in Q2 2026.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth that is partly bought. Reported revenue +9%, organic +4.1%; FY2026 guide 4.5–6.5% reported against +1% to +3% organic. Most of this year's expected growth comes from acquisitions and currency rather than organic sales, and the goodwill left over from them can be written down. H1 2025 carried $265.8M of goodwill and intangible impairments.
02
China pricing. Management calls China the hardest pricing market because of overcapacity. Its "transitory" view has not been proven yet, and it is the main reason given for ITS margin falling 180bps.
03
Valuation still assumes compounding. Even after the fall, the stock trades at 29.8x GAAP trailing EPS and 19.7x forward, with EV/EBITDA near 15.8x on TTM figures (calculated below). Those are multiples for a company that grows through its cycle. If growth stalls, the multiple has further to fall.

Notable One-Off Items

The biggest distortion is the $265.8M of goodwill and intangible impairments in H1 2025. It cut FY2025 GAAP EPS to $1.45 and makes the trailing EPS jump 88% as it drops out of the TTM window. Neither figure reflects underlying earnings power; the company's adjusted EPS guide of $3.45–$3.57 (expected near the high end) is the cleaner number. Second, tariff refunds: management expects IEEPA tariff refunds in H2 2026 and has excluded them from guidance. Any refund would be upside, but also a one-off. Third, Q2 2026 capital use: $240M of share repurchases and $110M of M&A.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business hasn't broken; the multiple has. Revenue rose every year from $5.15B in FY2021 to $7.65B in FY2025, gross margin held above 42%, free cash flow has run at about $1.2B a year, and net leverage is only 1.7x with a fresh Moody's upgrade to Baa1. Management says July orders grew low-double-digit to mid-teens organically and that delayed projects are restarting. It has 11 deals under LOI and possible tariff refunds that guidance excludes. At about 20.6x the midpoint of FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance, with every analyst target above the price, a single quarter of organic orders re-accelerating could reverse much of the 30.6% drawdown.

Bear Case

Organic orders grew 1.6%. Most of this year's expected growth comes from acquisitions and currency: the guide is 4.5–6.5% reported against 1–3% organic. Adjusted EBITDA rose 2% on 9% more revenue, and ITS margin fell 180bps on China pricing. ROIC is 8.47% and ROE 9.47%, which are thin returns for a company whose strategy is paying for other companies. A $265.8M impairment in 2025 showed that some past deals were overpriced. At 29.8x GAAP trailing earnings, the stock is still valued as a compounder at a time when its growth depends on acquisitions.

Top 3 Risks

01
Organic order stall. Q2 organic orders +1.6% with book-to-bill about 1.0x. If the July rebound management described doesn't appear in Q3 results on October 29, the FY2026 organic guide of +1% to +3% comes under pressure.
02
M&A execution and impairment. Eleven deals under LOI means capital keeps flowing into acquisitions. Single-digit ROIC and the 2025 write-down show what overpaying costs.
03
ITS margin pressure. ITS is 79% of revenue, and its margin fell 180bps to 26.8% on China pricing and investment spending. If management is wrong that the pressure is "transitory," group margin falls with it.
