01 Company Overview

Gartner sells an opinion — and for more than four decades corporate technology buyers have paid a premium for it. Founded in 1979 by Gideon Gartner and David Stein to tell companies what to make of IBM's product line, it is now the research-and-advisory firm behind the Magic Quadrant and the hype cycle. The business is overwhelmingly subscription: in the first nine months of 2026 the Insights segment (research seats and advisory access) was 82.6% of revenue, with Conferences and Consulting making up almost all of the rest. FY2025 revenue was $6.50B, up 3.67%, and diluted EPS was $9.65, down 39.69% from an FY2024 figure of $16.00. Over the last four reported quarters it was $14.03.

The stock is the story. Gartner closed January 2025 at $542.83; it closed June 2026 at $129.62 — a 76% fall at month-end prices for a company that never stopped being profitable. Two single days did most of the damage: August 5, 2025 (about −27.6%), when contract-value growth slowed from 7% to 5%, and February 3, 2026 (about −20.9%), after FY2025 consulting revenue of $552.5M fell below the guidance the company had given through the year. Layered over both was a bigger question: if generative AI can summarise a market, rank vendors and answer an analyst-style question on demand, what is a $300,000-a-year research relationship worth? Then the Q2 2026 print on August 4, 2026 sent the stock up 22.61% in a day, and October added 22.65% more. On November 3, 2026 Gartner beat on earnings and raised its full-year guidance, and the stock fell 13.19% that day: contract value grew faster for a third straight quarter, but only 2.5%. At $207.19 it is 60% above the June month-end and still 62% below the January 2025 one. The central debate: is AI a substitute for Gartner's product, or the biggest topic its clients have ever wanted help with?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brand as a buying standard Magic Quadrant vendor dependency Prepaid subscription model Peer networks of CIOs & functional leaders Conference convening power 70.2% gross margin

The moat was never the information — it was the signature. A CIO who buys the vendor in the top-right of the Magic Quadrant has cover if the project fails; a vendor that is left out of it has a sales problem. That two-sided dependency is why Gartner can charge an average contract of $300,000 to $350,000 a year (the CFO's rough figure on the Q3 call), and why it collects the cash before it delivers the service. The vulnerability is that generative AI makes the information layer close to free, and in a budget squeeze the research seat looks like a discretionary line item. What has to hold is the signature.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
AI as a substitute. The bear case in one line: a model that can summarise a vendor landscape in seconds erodes the reason to pay for a research seat. Management argues the opposite — that AI is the top topic clients want advice on — and wallet retention improved again in Q3 2026. But CV growth of 2.5% says the argument has only begun to show up in new selling: on the Q3 call new business was down 4% year on year in Global Technology Sales and 3% in Global Business Sales.
02
Government and budget exposure. Federal contract value was the single largest drag through 2025. Federal is now 2–3% of contract value, per the CFO on the Q3 2026 call, so the direct hit is mostly behind the company — but budget pressure elsewhere is not: on the same call he pointed to the war in the Middle East and new tech layoffs, as reported by The Motley Fool.
03
Credibility and litigation. A securities class action filed in March 2026 in the District of Connecticut alleges, in the amended complaint filed July 27, 2026 according to co-lead counsel Cohen Milstein, that Gartner, its CEO and its CFO misled investors about demand for its services and the threat from AI between July 30, 2024 and February 2, 2026. Outcome and cost are unknown; the more immediate damage is that guidance is now discounted.
04
Negative equity and a buyback bigger than cash flow. Book value is −$34.67M at September 30, 2026 after years of buybacks, and debt is about $3.0B against $1.63B of cash (Q3 10-Q). Leverage is modest against EBITDA (gross debt under two times trailing EBITDA, per the CFO), but the buyback has outrun the cash the business makes: $1.7B over the last four quarters against $1.29B of trailing free cash flow.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort year-on-year comparisons. First, the Digital Markets sale: Q3 2025 carried $55.2M of revenue from Capterra, Software Advice and GetApp that Q3 2026 does not, so reported revenue understates the underlying trend — hence the company's "adjusted revenues" measure. Second, the buyback: 0.9M shares repurchased for $138M in Q3 2026 and 7.8M shares for $1.22B in the first nine months, with $1.1B of authorisation remaining at September 30; weighted diluted shares fell 14.7% year-on-year (75.0M to 63.9M), which is why adjusted EPS rose 30.1% on adjusted revenue up 2.4%. Third, Q3 2025 carried a $150M goodwill impairment, so the +613% jump in GAAP diluted EPS ($0.47 to $3.35) overstates the change; and FY2024's $1,254M net income ($16.00 EPS) is well above the years either side of it, so the headline −39.69% FY2025 EPS decline overstates the deterioration.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 13.8x consensus 2026 earnings for a 70.2%-gross-margin subscription business that turns more than all of its profit into cash — $1.29B of TTM free cash flow against $954M of net income, a 9.9% yield — and uses it to retire shares at a rate of 11.56% a year. The two numbers that broke the stock have turned: CV growth has accelerated for three consecutive quarters, to 2.5%, wallet retention improved again in Q3, and federal is down to 2–3% of contract value. If AI turns out to be the topic that fills Gartner's Symposium halls rather than the tool that empties its research seats, a return to mid-single-digit CV growth on a share count shrinking this fast compounds per-share earnings in the low-to-mid teens — management's own target, restated on the Q3 call, is adjusted EPS growth above 12% a year, compounded over three years from 2025.

Bear Case

CV growth of 2.5% is acceleration from almost nothing, and outside the federal business it slowed, to 2.8% from 3.3% in Q2, with the CFO saying it will not get back to 4% by year-end. Wallet retention is still below 100%, new business fell in both sales channels (−4% and −3%), and adjusted EPS rose 30.1% on adjusted revenue up 2.4% — margins and buybacks are doing the work that growth used to do. The AI question is unresolved and asymmetric: if a model can deliver 80% of a research seat's value for a fraction of the price, renewal conversations get harder every year. Add negative equity, a buyback that outran free cash flow over the last four quarters, a securities class action and a Street that has two-thirds of its ratings on Hold with an average target below the price, and 14.8x trailing earnings is not obviously cheap for a company whose reported revenue fell 1.3% in Q3.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI substitution of the research seat. The thesis behind a 76% month-end peak-to-trough fall. Management says AI is the most requested topic among clients; the proof will be CV growth, not attendance at conferences.
02
Contract value stalls again. CV of $5.3B grew 2.5% in Q3 2026. A reversal in the next print would undo the rebound since June, and the history of this stock since August 2025 is that CV disappointments are priced in a single session.
03
Guidance credibility. The consulting shortfall against guidance in FY2025 is now the subject of litigation. The FY2026 guide is framed as floors ("at or above", on the Q3 call); a miss against a floor would be punished harder than a miss against a range.
Industry Commentary
Generative AI vs. the paid-research model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Gartner's product is judgement packaged as research, delivered through analysts and documents. Generative AI attacks the packaging directly: summarising a market, comparing vendors and drafting a recommendation are exactly what language models do well. Management's answer on the Q3 2026 call was that AI is creating demand rather than destroying it — Hall said "AI continues to be the most requested topic across our client base," and that Gartner's research guides "more than 75,000 senior executives on the best way to get value from AI." Both readings are consistent with the data so far: retention is improving and CV growth has picked up for three quarters, but only to 2.5%. What separates them is whether new-business selling recovers, and new business was still down in Q3 (4% in Global Technology Sales, 3% in Global Business Sales, year on year, per the call).

Budget pressure: federal, macro, and non-AI tech
PERSISTENT HEADWIND

Federal clients were the largest single drag through 2025; federal is now 2–3% of contract value, per the CFO on the Q3 2026 call, so that drag is now small. Outside it, growth slowed in Q3 (ex-federal CV +2.8%, from 3.3% in Q2), and the CFO pointed to pressure from the war in the Middle East and new tech layoffs, as reported by The Motley Fool. The tech-vendor client base — the companies that buy Magic Quadrant placement and conference exhibition space — remains exposed to how much of their own budgets AI spending crowds out.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $207.19 Gartner trades at about 10.4x TTM EBITDA and a 9.9% free-cash-flow yield, on our calculation. The bull framing: a business with a 77.6% contribution margin in its core segment, prepaid by clients, does not need growth to be worth more than this — it needs growth to stop falling, which it has for three quarters. The bear framing: a 10% FCF yield is what the market charges for a franchise whose long-run future is in question. The adjudicator is the same as in every quarter since August 2025: FX-neutral contract value growth, and whether wallet retention crosses back above 100%.
