01 Company Overview

Gartner sells an opinion — and for more than four decades corporate technology buyers have paid a premium for it. Founded in 1979 by Gideon Gartner and David Stein to tell companies what to make of IBM's product line, it is now the research-and-advisory firm behind the Magic Quadrant and the hype cycle. The business is overwhelmingly subscription: in Q2 2026 the Insights segment (research seats and advisory access) was 77.0% of revenue, with Conferences and Consulting making up the rest. FY2025 revenue was $6.50B, up 3.67%, and diluted EPS was $9.65, down 39.69% from an FY2024 figure of $16.00.

The stock is the story. Gartner closed January 2025 at $542.83; it closed June 2026 at $129.62 — a 76% fall at month-end prices for a company that never stopped being profitable. Two single days did most of the damage: August 5, 2025 (about −27.6%), when contract-value growth slowed from 7% to 5%, and February 3, 2026 (about −20.9%), after FY2025 consulting revenue of $552M fell below the guidance the company had maintained through the year and the 2026 outlook called for only about 2% FX-neutral revenue growth. Layered over both was a bigger question: if generative AI can summarise a market, rank vendors and answer an analyst-style question on demand, what is a $400,000-a-year research relationship worth? Then the Q2 2026 print on August 4, 2026 sent the stock up 22.61% in a day. At $183.32 it is 41% above the June month-end and still 66% below the January 2025 one. The central debate: is AI a substitute for Gartner's product, or the biggest topic its clients have ever wanted help with?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Brand as a buying standard Magic Quadrant vendor dependency Prepaid subscription model Peer networks of CIOs & functional leaders Conference convening power 69.6% gross margin

The moat was never the information — it was the signature. A CIO who buys the vendor in the top-right of the Magic Quadrant has cover if the project fails; a vendor that is left out of it has a sales problem. That two-sided dependency is why Gartner can charge enterprise clients an average of $414,000 of contract value a year, and why it collects the cash before it delivers the service. The vulnerability is that generative AI makes the information layer close to free, and in a budget squeeze the research seat looks like a discretionary line item. What has to hold is the signature.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
AI as a substitute. The bear case in one line: a model that can summarise a vendor landscape in seconds erodes the reason to pay for a research seat. Management argues the opposite — that AI is the top topic clients want advice on — and Q2 2026 retention improved. But CV growth of 1.7% says the argument has not yet shown up in new selling.
02
Government and budget exposure. Federal contract value has been the single largest drag, with management citing government efficiency initiatives and evolving trade policies in explaining the Q4 2025 results (and, as reported by TIKR, funding changes for state, local and education clients). Federal CV is now about $125M, so the direct hit is mostly behind the company — but budget pressure elsewhere is not.
03
Credibility and litigation. A securities class action filed March 17, 2026 in the District of Connecticut alleges management misrepresented CV growth and consulting revenue prospects over February 4, 2025 – February 2, 2026. Outcome and cost are unknown; the more immediate damage is that guidance is now discounted.
04
Negative equity and rising debt. Book value is −$167.35M after years of buybacks, total debt is $3.33B against $1.49B of cash, and net borrowing over the last twelve months was $520.46M. Leverage is modest against EBITDA (1.8x gross per the company), but the buyback is now partly debt-funded.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 12.7x consensus 2026 earnings for a 69.6%-gross-margin subscription business that converts almost all of its profit to cash — $1.29B of TTM free cash flow, an 11.1% yield — and uses it to retire shares at a rate of 8.83% a year. The two numbers that broke the stock have turned: CV growth has accelerated for two consecutive quarters, wallet retention rose to 98.2%, and the federal drag is largely in the base. If AI turns out to be the topic that fills Gartner's Symposium halls rather than the tool that empties its research seats, a return to mid-single-digit CV growth on a share count shrinking this fast compounds per-share earnings in the low-to-mid teens — management's own framing, as summarised in a write-up of the Q2 call, is adjusted EPS compound growth above 12% over the next three years.

Bear Case

CV growth of 1.7% is acceleration from almost nothing. Wallet retention is still below 100%, GBS new business fell 5%, Consulting shrank 8.8%, and the company trimmed its adjusted revenue floor even as it raised profit guidance — margin and buybacks are doing the work that growth used to do. The AI question is unresolved and asymmetric: if a model can deliver 80% of a research seat's value for a fraction of the price, renewal conversations get harder every year. Add negative equity, a partly debt-funded buyback, a pending securities class action and a Street that has two-thirds of its ratings on Hold with targets at the price, and 16.8x trailing earnings is not obviously cheap for a company whose reported revenue grew 0.7%.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI substitution of the research seat. The thesis behind a 76% month-end peak-to-trough fall. Management says AI is the most requested topic among clients; the proof will be CV growth, not attendance at conferences.
02
Contract value stalls again. CV of $5.28B grew 1.7% in Q2 2026. A reversal in the November 3 print would undo the August re-rating, and the history of this stock since August 2025 is that CV disappointments are priced in a single session.
03
Guidance credibility. The consulting shortfall against guidance in FY2025 is now the subject of litigation. The FY2026 guide is framed as floors ("at least", "no less than"); a miss against a floor would be punished harder than a miss against a range.
Industry Commentary
Generative AI vs. the paid-research model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Gartner's product is judgement packaged as research, delivered through analysts and documents. Generative AI attacks the packaging directly: summarising a market, comparing vendors and drafting a recommendation are exactly what language models do well. Management's answer on the Q2 2026 call was that AI is creating demand rather than destroying it — Hall said Gartner connects "more than 80,000 business leaders looking to get value from AI," along with more than 10,000 CIOs and IT organisations and about 5,000 technology providers. Both readings are consistent with the data so far: retention is improving and CV is growing, but slowly. What separates them is whether new-business selling recovers, and GBS new business was still down 5% in Q2.

Budget pressure: federal, tariffs, and non-AI tech
EASING HEADWIND

Explaining the Q4 2025 results, the CEO pointed to DOGE initiatives affecting federal clients, tariff-related uncertainty, funding changes for state, local and education clients, and struggles among technology vendors outside AI (as reported by TIKR). Federal CV has since shrunk to about $125M and returned to positive net contract value increase in Q2 2026, so the largest single drag is now small. The tech-vendor client base — the companies that buy Magic Quadrant placement and conference exhibition space — remains exposed to how much of their own budgets AI spending crowds out.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $183.32 Gartner trades at about 9.7x TTM EBITDA and an 11.1% free-cash-flow yield, on our calculation. The bull framing: a business with a 77.5% contribution margin in its core segment, prepaid by clients, does not need growth to be worth more than this — it needs growth to stop falling, which it has for two quarters. The bear framing: an 11% FCF yield is what the market charges for a franchise whose terminal value is in question. The adjudicator is the same as in every quarter since August 2025: FX-neutral contract value growth, and whether wallet retention crosses back above 100%.
