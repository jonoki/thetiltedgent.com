01 Company Overview

Illinois Tool Works is a diversified industrial manufacturer that operates 88 decentralized divisions in 49 countries (2025 10-K) across seven reporting segments — Automotive OEM, Food Equipment, Test & Measurement and Electronics, Welding, Polymers & Fluids, Construction Products, and Specialty Products. FY2025 revenue was $16.04 billion, up 0.9% YoY with organic growth flat, while GAAP operating margin reached 26.3% and after-tax ROIC hit 29.3% — well above the roughly 16% operating margin and 11% ROIC of the peer averages in Section 04 (our approximate estimates).

That margin structure, not revenue growth, is the entire investment case. ITW ranks #163 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization, at $79.16 billion. It is one of the market's longest-running dividend growers: on August 7, 2026 the Board raised the dividend 7% to $6.88 a share a year (dividend yield 2.47%), which ITW calls its 63rd consecutive annual increase. Trailing twelve-month revenue is $16.65 billion (nine months of 2026 are up 5.0% on 2025), as the two capital-spending-linked segments accelerated in the third quarter: Test & Measurement and Electronics grew 15.8% organically and Welding 6.9% (about 15% without the tornado).

7-SEGMENT DIVERSIFICATION

How 80/20 actually works — and why it is the moat. Most companies cite the Pareto principle as a slogan. ITW operationalizes it as a mandatory management system applied division by division. The starting point is a hard segmentation of every division's revenue: the roughly 20% of customers and products that generate ~80% of revenue are designated the "80," and everything else is the "20." The "80" gets dedicated resources — its own production lines, its own inventory policy, simplified order-to-cash, direct engineering attention, and preferential lead times. The "20" is systematically de-emphasized: slow-selling product variants are discontinued, low-volume customers are moved to distribution or repriced, and complexity is removed from the plant floor. "Front-to-back" means the same segmentation is pushed through every function — not just sales and marketing at the front end, but production scheduling, supply chain, purchasing, warehousing and back-office administration behind it. The result is a manufacturing footprint that runs long production runs of a small number of high-volume parts, with structurally lower changeover cost, lower working capital, and far less overhead per revenue dollar than a peer serving the full long tail.

Two mechanisms then compound it. Customer-Back Innovation (CBI) requires divisions to develop products from directly observed customer problems rather than internal technology pushes; ITW's 2025 10-K calls it the most impactful driver of high-quality organic growth through the cycle. CBI added 2.4% to revenue growth in FY2025 and 3% in the first half of 2026 (CEO Christopher O'Herlihy, Q2 call), and on the Q3 2026 call, as summarised by StockStory, he said it contributed more than 3%. Decentralized entrepreneurship keeps the 88 divisions operating as small independent businesses with their own profit-and-loss accounts, their own general manager and direct customer ownership, so 80/20 decisions get made by people close enough to the customer to know which 20% can be shed without damage. Corporate provides the framework and capital allocation; it does not run the divisions.

The financial signature of this is unmistakable. ITW's FY2025 operating margin was 26.3% and its Q3 2026 margin 27.8%, against a peer-average estimate near 16% in Section 04. After-tax ROIC of 29.3% for FY2025 (30.3% annualized for Q3 2026) is the more telling number: it means the business needs remarkably little capital to produce its profit, which is what allows ITW to return effectively all of its free cash flow. In FY2025 every one of the seven segments earned above 21%, and five earned above 27%; in Q3 2026 six of the seven earned above 28% (Automotive OEM, at 20.1%, is the exception). Management targets a 30% enterprise operating margin by 2030 and guides enterprise initiatives to add more than 100 bps to margin in 2026 (they added 130 bps in Q3). Layered on top: products designed into customer platforms (auto components, construction anchoring systems) carry multi-year specification lock-in that makes switching a re-engineering exercise, not a purchasing decision; the Food Equipment installed base generates recurring service revenue (up 5% in Q2 2026 while equipment sales fell 2%); and seven uncorrelated end markets meant Construction Products could fall 5.1% organically in FY2025 without breaking the consolidated margin.

Vulnerability 01
Organic Growth Ceiling
The same discipline that produces the margin caps the growth. Deliberately shedding the "20" means walking away from revenue, and ITW's revenue record shows it: $15.93B (FY22) → $16.11B → $15.90B → $16.04B (FY25) — essentially flat over three years, with organic growth of 0.0% in FY2025. Organic growth for the first nine months of 2026 was 2.9% and Q3 alone 3.8%, inside the 3–4% full-year guidance but coming off a low base and driven by two capital-spending-linked segments. Investors paying 24.85× trailing earnings are underwriting margin expansion and buybacks, not top-line growth.
Vulnerability 02
Automotive OEM Concentration
The largest segment (20.5% of FY2025 revenue) is tied to global light-vehicle production, which management expected to fall ~2% in 2026 (Q2 call). Q3 2026 Auto OEM organic growth was −2.1% and its margin fell 170 bps to 20.1%. The EV transition cuts both ways: EVs drop the parts specific to petrol engines, so ITW's growth depends on winning new content platform by platform, and the net outcome will not be knowable in advance. It is also the lowest-margin segment, so an auto-led recovery is dilutive to blended margin.
Vulnerability 03
Tariff & Price/Cost Timing
ITW offset the FY2025 tariff impact in dollar terms through pricing and supply chain actions, but a dollar-for-dollar price offset is margin-dilutive by arithmetic — adding equal dollars to both revenue and cost lowers the percentage. ITW's Q2 release said timing lags between inflation and price increases modestly diluted margin; in Q3 it said price increases more than offset higher raw material costs and margin rose 40 bps. On the Q2 call management said it did not expect any material recovery from tariff refunds. Any further tariff escalation restarts the lag.
Vulnerability 04
Balance Sheet Leverage & the Buyback Math
Aggressive buybacks have shrunk book equity to $3.14B at September 30, 2026, so debt-to-equity reads 2.98× and ROE reads 101.65% — both flattered by a denominator that has been bought back rather than by superior underlying returns. Total debt is $9.37B ($2.22B short-term, $7.15B long-term) against $809M of cash, and the current ratio is 1.29. In August ITW sold $1.5B of 4.650% notes due 2029, with the proceeds intended to repay part of its commercial paper. Q3 operating income of $1,177M was about 14× Q3 interest expense of $83M, so coverage is comfortable, but the structure leaves less cushion than headline ROIC implies, and the payout ratio is 61.4%.

Notable One-Off Items. Several distortions are worth isolating before extrapolating any of the figures above:

1. FY2024 GAAP EPS is not a clean baseline. FY2024 diluted EPS of $11.71 sits materially above FY2025's $10.49 (ITW's Feb 3, 2026 release). Most of that gap is two one-time items in 2024, per ITW's February 5, 2025 results release: $1.26 a share from the sale of its equity interest in Wilsonart and $0.30 from a LIFO inventory accounting change. Excluding them, FY2024 EPS was $10.15. Using FY2024 as the base makes FY2025 look like an earnings decline when operating performance was roughly flat.

2. Tariffs and price/cost timing. ITW's FY2025 pricing and supply chain actions offset the tariff impact in dollar terms, which is margin-dilutive by arithmetic. Its Q2 release said timing lags modestly diluted margin; its Q3 release said price increases more than offset higher raw material costs. On the Q2 call management said it did not expect any material recovery from tariff refunds, so the absorbed cost should be modeled as permanent.

3. The Welding tornado. Late-July tornado damage at two Welding facilities reduced Q3 revenue by an estimated $40M, about 1 percentage point of company growth; without it company organic growth would have been about 5% rather than 3.8%. Business-interruption insurance largely offset the lost operating income, and ITW expects the future impact to be immaterial. Watch Q4 Welding for the catch-up: management said on the call, per StockStory, that orders are running ahead of revenue.

4. Buybacks. ITW repurchased $750M of shares in Q2 and $187M in Q3, against about $1.5B guided for the year; on Aug 7 the Board added a new $6 billion authorization. Diluted shares averaged 285.3M in Q3, 2.2% fewer than the 291.7M a year earlier (buyback yield 1.98% per StockAnalysis).

5. Tax rate. Q3 2026 EPS grew 6.8% even though the effective tax rate was 24.4% against 21.8% in Q3 2025, which had a net $27M one-off tax benefit. The nine-month 2026 reported rate of 23.3% includes a $34M one-off benefit in Q1 (mainly a U.S. tax audit resolution); excluding it the rate is 24.3%.

6. M&A and currency. The Q3 release attributes 0.3% of the 4.4% revenue growth to an acquisition and 0.3% to currency; organic growth was 3.8%. Currency is a tailwind that reverses mechanically if the dollar strengthens.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

ITW earns a 26.61% trailing operating margin (27.8% in Q3) and a 29.29% ROIC — roughly 11 points and 2.7× the peer estimates — because 80/20 front-to-back is a genuine, decades-refined operating system rather than a slogan, and it produces those returns across all seven segments simultaneously. The FY2025 organic flatline looks cyclical: in Q3 2026 Test & Measurement/Electronics grew 15.8% organically, Welding 6.9% (about 15% without the tornado) and Polymers & Fluids 8.2%, and guidance was raised again. Layer on enterprise margin initiatives toward a 30% 2030 goal, free cash flow of about 100% of net income, ~$1.5B of buybacks plus a new $6B authorization, and 63 straight years of dividend increases, and a 24.2× forward multiple is a reasonable price for one of the highest-quality industrial franchises in the market.

Bear Case

Revenue was $15.93B in FY2022 and $16.04B in FY2025 — three years of essentially zero growth, with FY2025 organic growth at exactly 0.0%. The 80/20 discipline that generates the margin structurally caps the top line, because shedding the unprofitable 20% means shedding revenue. At 24.85× trailing earnings and a 3.01 PEG, investors are paying a growth multiple for a business whose total return depends on buybacks and a 2.47% dividend. Three of sixteen analysts rate it Strong Sell against one Strong Buy; MarketBeat's average target of $276.33 sits below the current price, and JPMorgan's October downgrade removed the Street-high target. Margin may be near a ceiling — three of seven segments saw margin decline year over year in Q3 (Automotive OEM −170 bps, Construction −170, Specialty −90) despite record operating income — the largest segment is tied to auto builds expected to fall, and the shortfall against revenue estimates in Q3 shows growth is not accelerating everywhere.

01
Valuation with no growth cushion. At 24.85× trailing and 24.24× forward earnings on a business whose revenue grew about 0.7% in total across three fiscal years (FY2022 to FY2025), there is little margin of safety if the 2026 organic re-acceleration proves to be a one-year cyclical bounce. If investors paid 22× this year's expected earnings ($11.49) instead of 24.2×, the shares would be about $252.78, 9.3% lower, before any earnings disappointment.
02
Margin ceiling risk. Management's 30% by-2030 target requires about 3.4 more percentage points of margin on top of the 26.61% trailing base. In Q3 2026 three of seven segments saw operating margin fall year over year — Automotive OEM −170 bps, Construction −170, Specialty Products −90 — even as the consolidated figure rose 40 bps on 130 bps from enterprise initiatives. If price/cost pressure persists or the capital-spending upswing fades, the bull thesis loses its engine.
03
Cyclical and auto concentration. Automotive OEM is 20.5% of FY2025 revenue and its organic sales fell 2.1% in Q3 against global light-vehicle production management expected to decline ~2% in 2026. Combined with Construction Products' housing exposure (−0.8% organic in Q3) and the capital-spending sensitivity of the two fastest-growing segments, a genuine industrial downturn would hit growth and margin at the same time — and the balance sheet, with $9.37B of debt, $809M of cash and a 1.29 current ratio, has less absorptive capacity than the headline ROIC suggests.
  Industry Commentary — Macro Themes Driving ITW
Industrial Production & the Short-Cycle Split
ONGOING HEADWIND
The industrial economy is not moving as one block, and ITW's seven segments make that unusually legible. Capital-expenditure-linked businesses have inflected sharply: in Q3 2026 Test & Measurement/Electronics grew 15.8% organically and Welding 6.9% (about 15% without the tornado), against −1.4% and +2.0% in all of FY2025; Polymers & Fluids added 8.2%. Short-cycle, consumption-linked and auto-linked businesses have not: Food Equipment +0.5%, Automotive OEM −2.1%, Construction Products −0.8% and Specialty Products −1.2%. The two capital-spending-linked segments are about 31% of Q3 revenue (the three growing segments together about 43%), so a company-wide 3.8% organic growth rate is carried by less than half of the base. A durable industrial recovery requires the rest to follow; if it does not, 2026's growth is a narrow capital-spending bounce that fades.
Tariffs, Pricing and Reshoring
ONGOING HEADWIND
Tariffs hit ITW twice and in opposite directions. The direct cost was offset in dollar terms in FY2025 through pricing and supply chain actions, but a dollar-for-dollar offset is mathematically margin-dilutive; ITW's Q2 release said timing lags modestly diluted margin, and its Q3 release said price increases more than offset higher raw material costs. On the Q2 call management said it did not expect material recovery from tariff refunds. Structurally, ITW is better placed than most: on the Q1 2025 call, as summarised by StockStory, CEO Christopher O'Herlihy said over 90% of its production is in the markets it serves, so less of its revenue crosses a tariffed border. The offsetting tailwind is reshoring — North American factory construction and equipment investment feed Welding, Test & Measurement/Electronics and commercial Construction Products; North America grew 6.0% organically in Q3.
Auto Build Rates, EV Mix — and the Bull/Bear Verdict
MIXED — THE SWING FACTOR
Management expected global light-vehicle production to fall ~2% in 2026 (Q2 call), and Automotive OEM organic growth was −2.1% in Q3. ITW targets beating global builds by 200–300 bps through content gains; on the Q2 call CFO Michael Larsen pointed to its growing business with Chinese EV makers and said EVs are now almost 20% of global production. EVs also drop the parts specific to petrol engines, so the net content outcome is decided platform by platform and not knowable in advance.

Bull framing: Auto OEM is 20.5% of FY2025 revenue at a 20.1% Q3 margin. If the other six segments carry growth while auto stays flat, mix alone lifts blended margin toward the 30% goal — the market is currently paying little for auto recovery.

Bear framing: auto is the largest segment and it is shrinking organically into a declining production base, while the segments that are growing are the most capital-spending-cyclical in the portfolio. That combination means the growth ITW is being credited for in 2026 is precisely the growth most likely to reverse in a downturn, and 24.85× trailing earnings leaves little set aside for that outcome.
