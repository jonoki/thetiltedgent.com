01 Company Overview

Two years ago Jacobs was half a government contractor. In September 2024 it spun its Critical Mission Solutions and Cyber & Intelligence government-services businesses out and merged them with privately held Amentum, handing shareholders one Amentum share for every Jacobs share (record date September 23, distribution September 27, 2024). What was left is an engineering and consulting firm that plans, designs and manages infrastructure and advanced facilities — water systems, transport, power grids, semiconductor fabs, pharmaceutical plants and, increasingly, data centres — plus PA Consulting, the UK consultancy it took full ownership of on March 20, 2026.

The remaining company is growing into its new shape. FY2025 (ended September 26, 2025) delivered $12.0B of gross revenue, $8.7B of adjusted net revenue (+5.3%) and adjusted EPS of $6.12 (+15.9%). By June 2026 backlog had reached a record $28.9B, up 27% year over year, and management had raised its fiscal-2026 guidance for the third quarter running. The catch is on the GAAP line: trailing EPS is only $2.86, weighed down by Amentum mark-to-market losses in FY2025 and more than $330M of PA-transaction compensation charges in each of the last two quarters. So the stock screens at 50x trailing earnings and about 20x this year's adjusted estimate. Which of those two numbers you believe is most of the investment case.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

$28.9B record backlog Multi-decade public-client relationships Mission-critical facility know-how Global delivery (~43,000 staff) PA Consulting advisory front-end Asset-light, fee-based model

Jacobs' edge is trust on hard projects. A water authority rebuilding a treatment plant, or a chipmaker commissioning a fab, pays for a firm that has done it before without incident. Frameworks and program-management roles tend to renew. The stock market cares mainly because much of the work is cost-reimbursable design and management, so Jacobs puts little of its own balance sheet at risk on the build itself. The moat is narrow in one specific way. Environment Analyst reports that Tetra Tech, AECOM, WSP and Jacobs together held just over 40% of US environmental and sustainability consulting in 2024, down 2.0 points on 2023. Clients keep a short list, and Jacobs is always on it, but so are the others.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
GAAP and adjusted earnings have split apart. Adjusted EPS guidance is $7.20–$7.30. GAAP trailing EPS is $2.86. The gap comes from $227.3M of Amentum mark-to-market losses in FY2025, $214.9M and $237.5M of subsidiary-compensation charges in Q2 and Q3 FY2026, and PA employee-consideration costs. The market sold the May print on this gap despite an adjusted beat.
02
Gross revenue overstates the growth. Trailing revenue is up 20.1%, but net revenue guidance is 9.5–10.0%. Pass-through-heavy full-program work adds revenue dollars without much margin. The trailing operating margin is 5.93% on gross revenue, while the adjusted EBITDA margin is 15.2% on net revenue.
03
More leverage after the PA buyout. The upfront payment was 80% cash, funded from cash on hand and debt. Total debt is $4.05B against $1.18B of cash, debt/equity is 1.25 and net leverage is 1.8x. That is within the sub-2.0x target but no longer the clean balance sheet of the pre-deal company.
04
Concentration in the data-centre cycle. Data centres and semiconductors are driving the 24% growth in Life Sciences & Advanced Manufacturing, and AI-linked work is 11% of net revenue. If hyperscale capex pauses, the fastest-growing part of the backlog is the one that slows.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

Why the early part of the chart is scaled

On September 27, 2024 every Jacobs shareholder received one Amentum share per Jacobs share. Jacobs stock gave up that value in one step. ICLUBcentral records Amentum's first price as $25.67 and puts the tax-basis split at 85.833% Jacobs / 14.167% Amentum, which implies a Jacobs price of about $155.5 just before the separation. Jacobs then opened ex-distribution at about $131 (Yahoo's Sep 30, 2024 open of $129.88 is itself scaled by the later 1.01 factor). A raw price chart would show that drop as a ~16% crash, when the value had simply moved into a second stock. The chart uses Yahoo Finance's monthly closes, which treat the spin as a 1197:1000 split-type adjustment on September 30, 2024, plus a smaller 101:100 adjustment on May 16, 2025. The second one is the record date for a special dividend of the final 7,299,065 Amentum shares released from escrow, after which Jacobs owned no Amentum stock. Every month before those dates is divided by the factor, so the line is continuous. It is not dividend-adjusted. The cost is that pre-spin points are lower than what the stock actually printed: August 2024 shows $124.80, while the raw close was about $149.6 (digrin "Real price").

On that consistent basis, J went from $109.62 at the September 2021 close to $143.76 on September 21, 2026, +31.1%. That figure credits shareholders with the value of the Amentum shares, but not with the later performance of those shares or with dividends. The 6-period monthly RSI reads 60.1, down from 68.0 at the end of August. It is neutral-to-firm, not stretched. Its low point in the window was 28.7 in September 2022, and it reached 31.4 in May 2026. The 10-month moving average sits near $133.59, below the price, so the long-term trend turned back up during the summer rally.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Strip out the separation and deal noise and you have a firm guiding adjusted EPS up about 19% this year to $7.20–$7.30, with the consensus at $8.26 for FY2027. Backlog is a record $28.9B, up 27%, with a 1.4x trailing book-to-bill. The data-centre backlog has doubled, AI-linked work is already 11% of net revenue, and the adjusted EBITDA margin rose 109bps to 15.2%. At $143.76 that is about 19.8x this year's adjusted earnings and 17.4x next year's. The GAAP charges are tied to a one-time buyout. When they roll off, the headline P/E collapses toward the adjusted one, and a high-margin consultancy is fully in the numbers. No analyst target sits below the current price.

Bear Case

GAAP EPS is $2.86 and the stock is at 50x it. "Adjusted" has excluded Amentum losses, then PA compensation charges, then PA tax items, and investors are being asked to look through all of them. Gross revenue growth of 20% is mostly pass-through, and net revenue grows about 10%. The PA buyout added debt (D/E 1.25) just as PA's own revenue stalled on UK government delays. The growth engine is data-centre capex, which is cyclical and concentrated. The May 2026 slide shows how fast sentiment turns when GAAP and guidance disappoint together.

Top 3 Risks

01
Earnings quality. The gap between $2.86 GAAP TTM EPS and $7.20–$7.30 adjusted guidance rests on charges management calls non-recurring. If PA-related compensation or tax items keep appearing into FY2027, the market may stop crediting the adjusted number.
02
Data-centre and advanced-manufacturing cycle. That is where the fastest backlog growth sits (Life Sciences & Advanced Manufacturing +24%). A hyperscaler or semiconductor capex pause would hit the part of the book the multiple is paying for.
03
PA Consulting execution and leverage. Jacobs paid £1.216B upfront, mostly in cash and debt, at 13.0x EBITDA for a business whose Q3 revenue was flat because of UK government-driven delays. Deleveraging to ~1.5x by end-FY27 depends on PA getting back to growth.
Industry Commentary
The AI build-out as an engineering story
EMERGING TAILWIND

Data centres, chip fabs and the power to run them all have to be planned, permitted and designed before anyone pours concrete. Jacobs' Q3 numbers show that in the order book: data-centre backlog "roughly doubled", the pipeline "tripled", and AI-ecosystem work rose to 11% of net revenue from 10% a quarter earlier. The same demand shows up in Energy & Power, which grew double digits. This is the reason backlog is growing faster than revenue.

A crowded top table in US environmental consulting
ONGOING HEADWIND

Environment Analyst (April 16, 2026) finds that Tetra Tech, AECOM, WSP and Jacobs held just over 40% of a US environmental and sustainability consulting market above $25B in 2024. That share was down 2.0 points on 2023, so consolidation has plateaued. Jacobs' own position dropped after it sold the Critical Mission Solutions business, and WSP kept buying (POWER Engineers in 2024, TRC completed in February 2026). Jacobs' water and environmental work grew only about 1% in Q3. That is the slow end of the portfolio.

From contractor to consultant
STRATEGIC SHIFT

In two years Jacobs has shed its government-services arm (to Amentum), exited its last Amentum shares (May 2025) and bought out PA Consulting (March 2026). The intended result is a higher-margin, advice-led business sitting at the front of the asset lifecycle, from strategy through design to program management. Management says it is on track to reach or exceed its FY2029 targets. Until the deal charges clear, investors have to take the margin case partly on trust.
