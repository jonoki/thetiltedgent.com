01 Company Overview

Johnson Controls engineers, manufactures, installs and services the systems that keep large buildings running — heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), fire detection and suppression, electronic security, refrigeration, and the building-management software that ties them together — sold into data centres, hospitals, schools, airports and commercial offices across the Americas, EMEA and Asia-Pacific. Fiscal 2026 (year ended September 30, 2026 — note the September fiscal year-end: "FY2026" is the year ending Sept 30, 2026, not the calendar year) produced revenue of $25.67B, up 8.8% on the continuing-operations basis (8% organic), and adjusted EPS of $5.16, up 37%. At the October 28, 2026 close the company was worth $95.3B.

The framing that matters: this is no longer a conglomerate. After selling the Residential & Light Commercial HVAC business to Bosch on August 1, 2025, JCI is a deliberately narrowed pure-play commercial-buildings company. The investment question is therefore not "can it grow into new markets" but "can a historically under-margined operator execute" — a simplification and margin story, judged on delivery. On the evidence of the September 2026 quarter, the last of fiscal 2026, it is delivering: organic growth (revenue growth excluding acquisitions, divestitures and currency) of 12%, orders up 44%, adjusted EBIT margin (operating profit before interest and tax, excluding one-off items) up 300 basis points (3.0 percentage points) to 18.3%, and a record $23.0B backlog. Fiscal 2027 guidance calls for 11–12% organic growth and adjusted EPS of $6.15–$6.30.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED BASE ANNUITY CODE & SAFETY CERTIFICATION TYCO / SIMPLEX BRANDS SPEC-IN AT DESIGN STAGE FIELD SERVICE DENSITY $23.0B BACKLOG NVIDIA-CERTIFIED CDU

JCI's moat is not technological, it is positional and physical. A chiller or a fire-suppression system is specified by an engineer at the design stage of a building, installed by a licensed contractor, inspected under local life-safety code, and then serviced for two decades by whoever holds the parts, the drawings and the commissioning data. That sequence creates switching costs that have nothing to do with product superiority: replacing a Simplex fire panel means re-permitting, and re-commissioning a Metasys building-management system means re-mapping thousands of control points. The economic expression of this is the service line — $7.97B in fiscal 2026, 31.0% of revenue — and the $23.0B backlog, up 43% organically, which is contracted, funded work. Field density compounds it: a technician network dense enough to guarantee four-hour response in a hospital or a data hall is a fixed-cost asset a smaller competitor cannot replicate regionally. What JCI has not historically had is the conversion of that position into peer-level margin, and that gap — not the moat — is the whole debate.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The margin gap versus Trane is real but narrowing. On trailing-twelve-month operating margin (stockanalysis.com), JCI earns 15.60% against Trane's 17.80% — a 220 basis point gap at broadly similar scale ($25.67B revenue for JCI versus $22.21B for Trane). The gap on returns is wider: ROIC of 15.13% versus 26.58%, and ROE of 21.73% versus 36.64%. JCI's adjusted EBIT margin rose 300bp to 18.3% in Q4 FY2026 (15.9% for the year), and Trane's market cap ($98.67B) is now only slightly above JCI's ($95.31B), both at the October 28 close.
02
Data-centre concentration cuts both ways. On the July 2026 call CFO Marc Vandiepenbeeck said data-centre revenue for fiscal 2026 "is probably going to land in the high teens" as a share of sales, and put it at roughly one-third of company revenue within three to five years. That is the growth engine, and it is also a single-customer-class exposure to the capital budgets of the largest cloud companies, which have historically moved in step and can be deferred quickly. An Americas backlog up 54% is only as good as the customers behind it.
03
Thin liquidity, though cash generation has improved. A current ratio of 0.90 and quick ratio of 0.61 mean short-term bills exceed short-term assets, and cash of $618M against total debt of $9.28B (September 30, 2026) is thin for a company that has spent $5.0B on buybacks since the Bosch sale and $1.5B more in fiscal 2026. Cash generation, by contrast, has turned: fiscal 2026 free cash flow of $3.41B exceeded net income of $2.85B.
04
EMEA is not fixed. Three percent organic growth in the quarter and a 14.7% margin for fiscal 2026, in a segment that already carries the highest service mix, suggest cost and pricing problems, not a cyclical dip. About a fifth of revenue earned 14.7% for the year while the Americas earned 20.0% and APAC 19.3%.

Notable One-Off Items

The largest distortion in the last edition's trailing figures has now dropped out. On August 1, 2025 Johnson Controls completed the sale of its Residential & Light Commercial (R&LC) HVAC business to the Bosch Group, comprising the North America ducted business and JCI's 60% interest in the Johnson Controls-Hitachi Air Conditioning joint venture. Headline transaction value was $8.1B; net cash proceeds to JCI after taxes and transaction expenses were approximately $5.0B, and the entire $5.0B was committed to an accelerated share repurchase (a buyback in which a bank delivers the shares up front and is paid over time). The gain landed in the September 2025 quarter (discontinued operations added $2.40 to fiscal 2025 diluted EPS), which is why fiscal 2025 diluted EPS was $5.03. With fiscal 2026 complete, that quarter has left the trailing year: fiscal 2026 diluted EPS is $4.68 in total, but $4.72 from continuing operations against $2.63 a year earlier. The release also records a $70M gain on the sale of the ADT Mexico security business in fiscal 2026, and $331M of net cash from divestitures for the year.

What this means for the multiple, stated plainly. The trailing P/E is now 33.6× ($157.35 ÷ $4.68), and stockanalysis.com's 33.65 agrees, so the source conflict the last edition described (one earnings base with the Bosch gain, one without) is gone. On fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS of $5.16 the stock trades at 30.5×, and on the $6.31 fiscal 2027 consensus at 24.9×. The honest read: a premium multiple that already prices in a good deal of the margin recovery, held up by guidance for another 20%-plus rise in adjusted EPS. In the September quarter the gap between GAAP EPS ($1.60) and adjusted EPS ($1.66) was made of $95M of transformation costs, $87M of restructuring and $30M of transaction costs, partly offset by a $90M discrete tax benefit, a $57M mark-to-market gain and $23M of related tax effects (company reconciliation). Prior-year revenue figures are restated for discontinued operations, so fiscal 2025's $23.60B is not comparable with pre-divestiture reports; the adjusted effective tax rate was about 17% for the quarter and the year.

The Irish domicile. Johnson Controls International plc is headquartered in Cork, Ireland, and is an Irish-domiciled plc rather than a US corporation. The practical effect is a structurally lower tax rate than a comparable US-domiciled industrial: the TTM effective rate is 15.65% (stockanalysis.com) and the adjusted rate for fiscal 2026 about 17% (release), against a US federal statutory rate of 21% before state taxes. That is worth roughly a low-single-digit percentage of net income annually versus a US-domiciled peer — though note that Trane Technologies plc is also Irish-domiciled (effective rate 18.99%), so this is a shared advantage within the peer group rather than a JCI-specific edge, and it does not explain any part of the margin gap. The domicile carries a corresponding risk: it is exposed to changes in international tax treatment, and Irish plcs have periodically drawn US political attention.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The simplification is done and the operating fix is visibly working: 12% organic growth and 44% order growth in Q4, a record $23.0B backlog up 43% organically, adjusted EBIT margin up 300bp to 18.3%, and fiscal 2027 guidance of 11–12% organic growth and $6.15–$6.30 of adjusted EPS. Management expected data centres to be high-teens of fiscal 2026 revenue, with a stated path to a third of the company, the Alloy cold-plate business and the NVIDIA-certified CDU add liquid-cooling content, and 31% of fiscal 2026 revenue was higher-margin service on an installed base that grows with every systems win. Free cash flow of $3.41B exceeded GAAP net income, and on next year's consensus the multiple falls to 24.9×.

Bear Case

Almost the entire five-year return arrived in the last twenty-two months, and the stock now trades at 33.6× trailing GAAP EPS and 30.5× fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS — a full price for a company still earning a 15.60% operating margin against Trane's 17.80% and 15.13% ROIC against 26.58%. The gap has not closed; it has begun to narrow. Service fell to 29.5% of Q4 sales as equipment grew faster, the current ratio is below 1, and 10 of 22 analysts rate it Hold or Sell; after the beat Citi raised its target by $2 and RBC held at $165, both at Hold. EMEA — about a fifth of sales — grew 3% organically in Q4 and earned 14.7% for the year.

Top 3 Risks

1
Data-centre spending concentration. Management expected data centres to be high-teens of FY2026 revenue and targets one-third within three to five years, with Americas backlog up 54% on that demand. Data-centre capital budgets are set by a handful of large cloud companies that tend to move together; a synchronised pause would hit orders, backlog conversion and the entire growth narrative at once.
2
Multiple compression if margin convergence stalls. The stock is priced for the gap to Trane to keep closing: on trailing earnings it already trades at Trane's multiple. A quarter of flat segment margin — particularly in EMEA, which earned 14.7% for fiscal 2026 on the highest service mix in the company — would remove the reason investors paid for a one-year gain of 40.2% against SPY's 12.4%.
3
Balance-sheet fragility meets cyclicality. A current ratio of 0.90, quick ratio of 0.61, $618M of cash against $9.28B of debt, and a beta of 1.28 — after spending the $5.0B of Bosch proceeds and $1.5B more in fiscal 2026 on buybacks rather than paying down debt. If the construction cycle turns while backlog conversion slows, there is little cushion.
  Industry Commentary
AI thermal management is rewriting commercial HVAC demand
EMERGING TAILWIND
High-density AI compute cannot be air-cooled economically, which turns a commodity building service into engineered infrastructure. JCI's CEO put it as "this is the age of thermal management." In Q4 FY2026 orders rose 44% organically — "driven by continued data center demand", in the CFO's words (Investing.com transcript) — with Americas backlog up 54%; the company has a $1B+ CDU pipeline with NVIDIA certification (July call), the Alloy Enterprises cold-plate business, a new absorption chiller design that management says cuts cooling electricity by about 44%, and a modular data-centre partnership with Armada. Management expected data centres to be high-teens of FY2026 revenue (July call), with a target of ~one-third within three to five years. This is a genuine step-change in the addressable market for exactly the equipment JCI makes — and the reason a slow-growth industrial rose 114% over the chart's five years.
The peer margin gap — and the conglomerate discount that came with it
ONGOING HEADWIND
Against Trane Technologies — the closest comparable, similarly Irish-domiciled, similarly exposed to applied HVAC and data centres — JCI earns 15.60% operating margin versus 17.80%, 15.13% ROIC versus 26.58%, and 21.73% ROE versus 36.64% (stockanalysis.com, trailing twelve months), on more revenue ($25.67B versus $22.21B) and about twice the headcount (87,000 versus 44,000). Trane's market cap is still a little larger: $98.67B versus $95.31B at the October 28 close (shares × close, our calculation). That gap was $14B at the last edition; the conglomerate discount — the market paying JCI less for more revenue because it did not trust the conversion — has mostly gone. Is the operating gap closing? At the margin line, yes. Q4 FY2026 adjusted EBIT margin rose 300bp to 18.3%, above Trane's trailing GAAP operating margin of 17.80% (different bases: adjusted against GAAP); for the full year it was 15.9%. On returns on capital, no: Trane's ROIC is still nearly twice JCI's, and with the trailing multiples now level, the rest of the case has to be paid for in earnings.
Bull versus bear, framed as one question
THE DECIDING VARIABLE
Everything on this page reduces to whether service revenue mix rises faster than systems revenue over the next three years. In Q4 FY2026 service was 29.5% of sales growing 8% organic, while products and systems grew 13% organic on orders up 44% — so the mix is currently moving the wrong way, and every incremental dollar of lower-margin equipment installed is a claim on future high-margin service. The bull reads that as a loaded spring: a $23.0B backlog converting into an installed base that carries a 15-to-25-year service annuity, with attach rates management says are higher in data centres than across the fleet. The bear reads it as dilution: a company winning volume at data-centre pricing, booking it as growth, and hoping the service attach arrives before the construction cycle turns and before investors stop paying 30× adjusted earnings for the promise. The evidence to watch is not revenue or orders — it is the service-revenue percentage in each quarterly release. Fiscal 2026 closed at 31.0%; if it is not visibly above that and rising by FY2028, the bull case did not happen.
