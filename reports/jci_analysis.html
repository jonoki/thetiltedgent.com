01 Company Overview

Johnson Controls engineers, manufactures, installs and services the systems that keep large buildings running — heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), fire detection and suppression, electronic security, refrigeration, and the building-management software that ties them together — sold into data centres, hospitals, schools, airports and commercial offices across the Americas, EMEA and Asia-Pacific. Fiscal 2025 (year ended September 30, 2025 — note the September fiscal year-end: "FY2026" is the year ending Sept 30, 2026, not the calendar year) produced revenue of $23.60B, up 2.8% year-on-year on the continuing-operations basis; trailing-twelve-month revenue through the June 2026 quarter has since accelerated to $25.00B, up 6.8%. The company ranks roughly #299 in the world by market capitalisation at $83.7B.

The framing that matters: this is no longer a conglomerate. After selling the Residential & Light Commercial HVAC business to Bosch on August 1, 2025, JCI is a deliberately narrowed pure-play commercial-buildings company. The investment question is therefore not "can it grow into new markets" but "can a historically under-margined operator execute" — a simplification and margin story, judged on delivery. On the evidence of the June 2026 quarter it is delivering: organic growth (revenue growth excluding acquisitions, divestitures and currency) of 10%, orders up 27%, and adjusted EBIT margin up 260 basis points to 17%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED BASE ANNUITY CODE & SAFETY CERTIFICATION TYCO / SIMPLEX BRANDS SPEC-IN AT DESIGN STAGE FIELD SERVICE DENSITY $21.0B BACKLOG NVIDIA-CERTIFIED CDU

JCI's moat is not technological, it is positional and physical. A chiller or a fire-suppression system is specified by an engineer at the design stage of a building, installed by a licensed contractor, inspected under local life-safety code, and then serviced for two decades by whoever holds the parts, the drawings and the commissioning data. That sequence creates switching costs that have nothing to do with product superiority: replacing a Simplex fire panel means re-permitting, and re-commissioning a Metasys building-management system means re-mapping thousands of control points. The economic expression of this is the service line — $2,018M, 30.5% of revenue — and the $21.0B backlog, up 32% organically, which is contracted, funded work. Field density compounds it: a technician network dense enough to guarantee four-hour response in a hospital or a data hall is a fixed-cost asset a smaller competitor cannot replicate regionally. What JCI has not historically had is the conversion of that position into peer-level margin, and that gap — not the moat — is the whole debate.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The margin gap versus Trane is real and still open. On trailing-twelve-month GAAP operating margin, JCI earns 14.20% against Trane's 17.80% — a 360 basis point gap at broadly similar scale ($25.00B revenue for JCI versus $22.21B for Trane). The gap on returns is wider still: ROIC of 12.79% versus 26.58%, and ROE of 14.33% versus 36.64%. The gap is narrowing at the margin line — JCI's adjusted EBIT margin rose 260bp to 17% in Q3 FY2026 — but on the trailing full-company numbers Trane still converts a smaller revenue base into a larger market cap ($97.99B versus $83.74B).
02
Data-centre concentration cuts both ways. Management put data centres at "high teens as a percentage of total fiscal 2026 revenue" and targets roughly one-third of company revenue within three to five years. That is the growth engine, and it is also a single-customer-class exposure to hyperscaler capital budgets, which have historically moved in step and can be deferred quickly. A backlog up 40% in the Americas is only as good as the counterparties behind it.
03
Balance-sheet and cash-conversion strain. Current ratio of 1.00 and quick ratio of 0.67 leave no working-capital cushion, and total cash of $641M against total debt of $9.48B is thin for a company that just spent $5.0B on buybacks. Trailing free cash flow of $1.66B against $3.58B of reported net income shows how much of the accounting profit is non-cash divestiture gain rather than distributable cash.
04
EMEA is not fixed. One percent organic growth and 20 basis points of margin improvement in a segment that already carries the highest service mix suggests structural cost and pricing problems, not a cyclical dip. Roughly a fifth of revenue is currently earning 14.3% while the other two segments earn 21%.

Notable One-Off Items

There is one distortion large enough to change how every trailing per-share figure on this page should be read. On August 1, 2025 — inside the trailing-twelve-month window — Johnson Controls completed the sale of its Residential & Light Commercial (R&LC) HVAC business to the Bosch Group, comprising the North America ducted business and JCI's 60% interest in the Johnson Controls-Hitachi Air Conditioning joint venture. Headline transaction value was $8.1B; net cash proceeds to JCI after taxes and transaction expenses were approximately $5.0B, and the entire $5.0B was committed to an accelerated share repurchase programme (visible in the 6.86% buyback yield and in shares outstanding falling to 605.74M). The bulk of the accounting gain landed in the September 2025 quarter, which is why FY2025 net income jumped to $3,291M and diluted EPS to $5.03 from $1,705M and $2.52 in FY2024 — a 99.6% EPS "increase" that was overwhelmingly a one-off, not operating improvement. A further $70M gain on the sale was recognised through the first nine months of FY2026.

What this means for the multiple, stated plainly. TTM net income of $3.58B and diluted EPS of $5.79 include that gain. This report states a trailing P/E of 23.9× ($138.24 ÷ $5.79). Note a genuine source conflict: stockanalysis.com displays a trailing P/E of 39.61 for JCI, which implies an earnings base of roughly $2.11B — i.e. it appears to strip out the divestiture gain. Both numbers are internally consistent with their own earnings definitions; neither is wrong. The MarketBeat-family data (via The Cerbat Gem, August 31, 2026) shows 24.23× on a $139.33 price, consistent with the GAAP-inclusive basis used here. The honest read: against FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance of ~$5.05, JCI trades at roughly 27.4× — a premium multiple that already prices in a good deal of margin recovery. Two further items: prior-year revenue figures have been restated for discontinued operations (FY2025 revenue of $23.60B is not comparable to pre-divestiture reported figures), and the effective tax rate moved to ~17% in Q3 and nine-month FY2026 from ~12% a year earlier, a five-point headwind to reported earnings growth.

The Irish domicile. Johnson Controls International plc is headquartered in Cork, Ireland, and is an Irish-domiciled plc rather than a US corporation. The practical effect is a structurally lower tax rate than a comparable US-domiciled industrial: the TTM effective rate is 19.43% and the current-quarter rate ~17%, against a US federal statutory rate of 21% before state taxes. That is worth roughly a low-single-digit percentage of net income annually versus a US-domiciled peer — though note that Trane Technologies plc is also Irish-domiciled (effective rate 18.99%), so this is a shared advantage within the peer group rather than a JCI-specific edge, and it does not explain any part of the margin gap. The domicile carries a corresponding risk: it is exposed to changes in international tax treatment, and Irish plcs have periodically drawn US political attention.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The simplification is done and the operating fix is visibly working: 10% organic growth, 27% order growth, a record $21.0B backlog up 32% organically, and adjusted EBIT margin up 260bp to 17% in a single year under a new CEO. Data centres are already high-teens of revenue with a stated path to a third of the company, the CDU pipeline exceeds $1B with NVIDIA certification in hand, and 30.5% of revenue is already higher-margin service on an installed base that is growing with every systems win. A PEG of 1.18 and a 6.86% buyback yield mean you are not paying a fancy price for it.

Bear Case

Almost the entire five-year return arrived in the last eighteen months, and the stock now trades at roughly 27× FY2026 adjusted EPS — a full price for a company still earning 14.20% operating margin against Trane's 17.80% and 12.79% ROIC against 26.58%. The gap has not closed; it has begun to narrow. Trailing GAAP EPS of $5.79 is inflated by the Bosch gain and free cash flow is less than half reported net income. Half the analyst community rates it Hold or Sell, the two most recent bank updates were $2 and $7 target raises on a blowout quarter, and EMEA — a fifth of revenue — grew 1% with 20bp of margin improvement.

Top 3 Risks

1
Hyperscaler capex concentration. Data centres are high-teens of FY2026 revenue and targeted at one-third within three to five years, with Americas backlog up 40% on that demand. Data-centre capital budgets are set by a handful of buyers who move together; a synchronised pause would hit orders, backlog conversion and the entire growth narrative at once.
2
Multiple compression if margin convergence stalls. The stock is priced for the gap to Trane to keep closing. A single quarter of flat segment margin — particularly in EMEA, where 20bp of improvement on the highest service mix in the company is already a warning — would remove the reason investors paid a 22-point one-year performance premium over Trane.
3
Balance-sheet fragility meets cyclicality. A current ratio of exactly 1.00, quick ratio of 0.67, $641M of cash against $9.48B of debt, and a beta of 1.32 — after spending $5.0B of divestiture proceeds on buybacks rather than deleveraging. If the construction cycle turns while backlog conversion slows, there is no cushion and limited flexibility to buy the dip in its own shares again.
  Industry Commentary
AI thermal management is rewriting commercial HVAC demand
EMERGING TAILWIND
High-density AI compute cannot be air-cooled economically, which turns a commodity building service into engineered infrastructure. JCI's CEO put it as "this is the age of thermal management." The company reports applied HVAC growing at high-teens rates, systems orders +40%, a $1B+ CDU pipeline with NVIDIA certification and first shipments in Q4 FY2026, a new absorption chiller design cutting cooling electricity by 44%, and a modular data-centre partnership with Armada. Data centres are high-teens of FY2026 revenue with a target of ~one-third within three to five years. This is a genuine step-change in the addressable market for exactly the equipment JCI makes — and the reason a slow-growth industrial re-rated 103% in five years.
The peer margin gap — and the conglomerate discount that came with it
ONGOING HEADWIND
Against Trane Technologies — the closest comparable, similarly Irish-domiciled, similarly exposed to applied HVAC and data centres — JCI earns 14.20% operating margin versus 17.80%, 12.79% ROIC versus 26.58%, and 14.33% ROE versus 36.64%, on more revenue ($25.00B versus $22.21B) and twice the headcount (87,000 versus 44,000). Trane's market cap is nonetheless larger at $97.99B versus $83.74B. That is the conglomerate discount in one line: the market has been paying JCI less for more revenue because it did not trust the conversion. Is the gap closing? At the margin line, yes — but only recently and only partially. Q3 FY2026 adjusted EBIT margin rose 260bp to 17%, which on an adjusted basis is now within touching distance of Trane's GAAP operating margin. On returns on capital, no: half of Trane's ROIC is not a gap that closes in a year, and the residual discount is best read as the market waiting for two or three more quarters of proof.
Bull versus bear, framed as one question
THE DECIDING VARIABLE
Everything on this page reduces to whether service revenue mix rises faster than systems revenue over the next three years. Today service is 30.5% of revenue growing 7% organic, while systems grows 11% organic with orders up 40% — so the mix is currently moving the wrong way, and every incremental dollar of low-margin equipment installed is a claim on future high-margin service. The bull reads that as a loaded spring: a $21.0B backlog converting into an installed base that carries a 15-to-25-year service annuity, with attach rates management says are already improving. The bear reads it as dilution: a company winning volume at data-centre pricing, booking it as growth, and hoping the service attach arrives before the construction cycle turns and before investors stop paying 27× adjusted earnings for the promise. The evidence to watch is not revenue or orders — it is the service-revenue percentage in each quarterly release. If it is not visibly above 30.5% and rising by FY2028, the bull case did not happen.
