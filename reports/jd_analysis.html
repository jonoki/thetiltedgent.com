01 Company Overview

JD.com sells to Chinese shoppers mostly as a retailer, not a marketplace: it buys the goods, holds them in its own warehouses and delivers them through its own logistics arm, JD Logistics. That is why product sales were 77.1% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, with electronics and home appliances alone at 45.6%. The company calls itself "a leading supply chain-based technology and service provider". It reports in renminbi (RMB) on a calendar year. FY2025 revenue was RMB1,309.1B, up 13.0%, but net income attributable to shareholders halved to RMB19.6B from RMB41.4B, because a new food-delivery business inside the New Businesses segment lost RMB46.6B of operating profit over the year. This report covers the Nasdaq ADS; the same company trades in Hong Kong as 9618.

The latest quarter, Q2 2026 (April–June), reported August 13, 2026, ran the two halves of the story in opposite directions. Revenue fell 2.9% to RMB346.4B, which the release put down "primarily due to a high base effect", and electronics and home appliances dropped 11.8%. Profit went the other way: operating income of RMB4.5B against a RMB0.9B loss a year earlier, net income attributable of RMB7.1B (+15.4%), and non-GAAP diluted earnings of RMB6.29 per ADS against RMB4.97. The food-delivery losses are shrinking from their 2025 peak, and the core retail business posted a 4.6% operating margin. The market sold it anyway: the ADS closed down 7.3% on the release day and finished at $25.78 on October 2, 22% below the July 31 close of $33.01. At that price the market values JD at about $34.6B. Its cash and short-term investments, net of borrowings, come to about RMB165.2B, or $24.6B. The debate is whether that cash, and a retail business earning 4–6% margins, belongs to shareholders, or whether a supplier-funded balance sheet and heavy promotional spending make it look richer than it is.

What an ADS holder actually owns

The share you buy in New York is an ADS of JD.com, Inc., a Cayman Islands company, not a share of a Chinese company. The 20-F says so plainly: "Investors in our ADSs or Class A ordinary shares are not purchasing equity interest in the consolidated variable interest entities in the Chinese mainland but instead are purchasing equity interest in a holding company incorporated in the Cayman Islands, and may never directly hold equity interests in the consolidated variable interest entities in the Chinese mainland." Chinese law restricts foreign ownership of some internet businesses, so those licensed entities are controlled by contract rather than owned: the VIE structure. At JD the contract-controlled entities are a small part of the business: the 20-F puts their external revenues at 6.8% of consolidated net revenues in 2025; most of the business runs through subsidiaries JD owns through equity. A second layer matters more for valuation: several big units have outside shareholders, which is why the June 30, 2026 balance sheet carries RMB63.3B of non-controlling interests. Part of the consolidated cash belongs, in economic terms, to those minority holders.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Owned logistics network First-party inventory = authenticity Electronics & appliance scale Supplier payables float (RMB229B) Net cash ≈ RMB165B Brand partners (Costco, CHANEL)

The moat is trust delivered fast. Because JD buys and stocks most of what it sells, a shopper buying a phone or a refrigerator knows it is genuine and when it will arrive; that is why a brand like Costco picked JD as its only official e-commerce partner in China and why the release leans on "guaranteed product authenticity" in luxury beauty. The cost of that moat is a balance sheet full of goods: RMB114.6B of inventory at June 30, 2026, funded largely by RMB229.0B owed to suppliers. That float is a competitive advantage too, since suppliers finance JD's working capital, but it is also the reason the headline cash pile should not be read as spare money. The vulnerability is that the moat protects the order, not the margin: in a market with Alibaba, PDD and Meituan all fighting for the same wallet, JD's retail margin has never been above the mid-single digits in the data shown here.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Electronics is a cycle, not a trend. Electronics and home appliances are 45.6% of revenue and fell 11.8% in Q2 2026 against a high base. When the biggest category contracts, the group contracts: total revenue fell 2.9% even with general merchandise and services growing. The release does not say when the base effect ends.
02
The food-delivery bill is smaller, not gone. New Businesses still lost RMB9.9B in Q2 2026 — about 73% of what JD Retail earned in the same quarter. Management describes the trend as narrowing; the Q2 release gives no date or target for breakeven.
03
Thin margins leave no room for a price war. TTM net margin is 1.1% and TTM operating margin 0.1% on RMB1.31 trillion of revenue. A one-point swing in gross margin is worth roughly RMB13B a year, close to JD's entire trailing net income of RMB14.8B.
04
Structure and control. Minority holders own RMB63.3B of the equity in consolidated units, part of the business runs through contract-controlled VIEs, and Class B shares carry 20 votes each. Outside ADS holders have economic exposure with little say over how the cash is spent.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items separate JD's reported profit from its operating reality. First, non-operating income: in Q2 2026 "others, net" added RMB4.3B and the share of results of equity investees RMB2.6B, against operating income of RMB4.5B, so more than half of pre-tax income of RMB10.7B came from outside operations. Second, tax: Q2 2025 carried a small tax benefit while Q2 2026 paid RMB2.0B, which flatters last year's comparison. Third, buybacks: JD repurchased 183.2M Class A shares (91.6M ADS) for about US$3.0B in 2025, 6.3% of shares, all cancelled, and another 69.9M shares (34.9M ADS) for US$1.0B in the first half of 2026, 2.5% of the shares outstanding at the end of 2025. US$1.0B of authorisation remained at June 30, 2026. Diluted weighted ADS count fell to 1.41B in Q2 2026 from 1.49B a year earlier, which lifts per-ADS figures relative to total profit. Fourth, a regulatory fine: on April 17, 2026 China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) penalised a JD consolidated entity, among other e-commerce platforms, about RMB635M, finding that JD had not properly checked the qualifications of third-party decorated-cake sellers and order-transfer service providers on its platform, in breach of the Food Safety Law. JD adds the fine back in its first-half non-GAAP figures.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $78.28 to the October 2, 2026 close of $25.78, the ADS lost 67.1% of its price. Adding back the $4.64 per ADS of cash dividends paid over the window ($1.26, $0.62, $0.76, $1.00, $1.00) without reinvestment gives roughly −61%. Over the same dates the Hang Seng fell 5.5% and the S&P 500 rose 67.7%. The shape matters: a two-year slide from the November 2021 high of $84.11 to a January 2024 low of $22.55 (−73.2%), two violent but short rallies (September 2024 and July 2026), and a 2026 that has given back each one.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 39.0, below neutral but not oversold. Its five-year low was 22.6 in October 2022 and its high 73.1 in October 2024. The 10-month moving average, about $28.28, sits above the price, and the 3-month average ($26.87) is falling: the July rally has been fully unwound, and the trend is down, though not stretched.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 17.2x trailing GAAP earnings and 10.9x non-GAAP for a company whose trailing numbers still carry most of a RMB46.6B food-delivery loss that is now shrinking quarter by quarter. Net cash of about $24.6B covers 71% of the $34.6B market cap; the dividend yields 3.9%; buybacks retired 6.3% of the shares in 2025, and JD bought back 2.5% more in the first half of 2026; and trailing free cash flow of $4.69B is a 13.5% yield. The core retail business is holding a 4.6% margin through a revenue decline, which suggests JD is not buying sales with price, and marketplace and logistics revenues are growing. If New Businesses losses keep narrowing toward breakeven and electronics laps its high base, earnings rise sharply off a low base, and 32 of 36 analysts, with an average target of $39.44, are positioned for that.

Bear Case

The cash is not spare. It sits beside RMB229.0B owed to suppliers and RMB63.3B of minority interests, and management has shown it will spend it: RMB46.6B went into New Businesses in 2025 and RMB20.2B more in the first half of 2026. Revenue is now falling, led by a category that is nearly half the business, and the TTM operating margin is 0.1%. More than half of Q2 pre-tax income came from investment and equity-investee gains rather than operations. Control sits with Class B shares carrying 20 votes each. The stock has fallen 67% in five years while its home index fell 5.5%. Strong Buy ratings slipped from 29 in April to 25 in September 2026, and both target changes since the print were cuts.

Industry Commentary
The food-delivery war: what it cost JD
EASING, NOT OVER

JD's reported numbers show the scale of its 2025 push into food delivery, a business it entered in a market where Meituan is the listed incumbent and Alibaba competes too. The New Businesses segment lost RMB1.3B in Q1 2025 and then RMB14.8B, about RMB15.7B and RMB14.8B in the next three quarters, for RMB46.6B over the year, more than double JD's FY2025 net income of RMB19.6B. In 2026 the losses are falling, to RMB10.4B in Q1 and RMB9.9B in Q2, and the release credits "improved operational efficiency and revenue diversification." Management frames this as efficiency rather than retrenchment: the Q2 release says JD Food Delivery "maintained high-quality development". The Q3 2026 figure, set against the RMB15.7B of Q3 2025, is the cleanest test of that.

First-party retail and the margin trade-off
ONGOING HEADWIND

A first-party retailer owns the inventory risk, so its profit depends on what it can charge above cost. JD's Q2 2026 gross margin of 17.1% rose from 15.9% because the mix shifted toward marketplace and marketing revenue (+8.3%) and away from electronics (−11.8%). That is the right direction for margins, but it comes with a shrinking top line, and the electronics category is where JD's scale advantage is greatest. The release attributes the revenue decline to a high base. Neither the Q2 2026 nor the full-year 2025 release gives numeric revenue or profit guidance.

The valuation gap — cash, buybacks and the "China discount"
THE CORE DEBATE

At $25.78 JD trades at 17.2x trailing GAAP earnings, 10.9x non-GAAP, a 3.9% dividend yield and a 13.5% trailing free-cash-flow yield, with net cash of about $24.6B against a $34.6B market cap. The bull framing: strip out net cash and the operating business is priced at about $10B for a retailer that earned RMB28.4B of segment operating profit in Retail in the first half alone, while management spent US$3.0B on buybacks in 2025 and pays about US$1.4B a year in dividends. The bear framing: the cash is supplier float and partly belongs to minority holders, the controlling shareholder decides what happens to it, and 2025 showed it can be spent on promotions for new businesses rather than handed back. The Hang Seng fell 5.5% over five years while JD fell 67.1%: the discount is specific to JD, not just to China. What would settle it: New Businesses losses near zero, or a capital return large enough to show the cash belongs to shareholders.
