01 Company Overview

JD.com sells to Chinese shoppers mostly as a retailer, not a marketplace: it buys the goods, holds them in its own warehouses and delivers them through its own logistics arm, JD Logistics. That is why product sales were 75.3% of third-quarter 2026 revenue, with electronics and home appliances alone at 41.3%. The company calls itself "a leading supply chain-based technology and service provider". It reports in renminbi (RMB) on a calendar year. FY2025 revenue was RMB1,309.1B, up 13.0%, but net income attributable to shareholders halved to RMB19.6B from RMB41.4B, because a new food-delivery business inside the New Businesses segment lost RMB46.6B of operating profit over the year. This report covers the Nasdaq ADS; the same company trades in Hong Kong as 9618.

The latest quarter, Q3 2026 (July–September), reported November 17, 2026, put revenue back into growth: up 3.5% to RMB309.6B, after a 2.9% fall in the second quarter. Electronics and home appliances were nearly flat (−0.7%), while general merchandise rose 8.1% and marketplace and marketing revenue 9.6%. Profit kept rising: operating income of RMB5.2B against a RMB1.1B loss a year earlier, net income attributable of RMB6.5B (+23%), and non-GAAP diluted earnings of RMB5.88 per ADS against RMB3.73. The food-delivery losses shrank again and the core retail business held a 5.8% operating margin. It was still less than the market wanted: on Nasdaq's adjusted basis JD earned $0.77 per ADS against a $0.89 consensus, and the ADS closed down 7.7% on the release day and finished at $24.92 on November 18, 24.5% below the July 31 close of $33.01. Five days before the results, on November 12, JD completed its takeover of Ceconomy, the German parent of the MediaMarkt and Saturn electronics chains. At $24.92 the market values JD at about $33.5B. Its cash and short-term investments, net of borrowings, come to about RMB141.0B, or $21.1B. The debate is whether that cash, and a retail business earning 4–6% margins, belongs to shareholders, or whether a supplier-funded balance sheet and heavy spending on new ventures make it look richer than it is.

What an ADS holder actually owns

The share you buy in New York is an ADS of JD.com, Inc., a Cayman Islands company, not a share of a Chinese company. The 20-F says so plainly: "Investors in our ADSs or Class A ordinary shares are not purchasing equity interest in the consolidated variable interest entities in the Chinese mainland but instead are purchasing equity interest in a holding company incorporated in the Cayman Islands, and may never directly hold equity interests in the consolidated variable interest entities in the Chinese mainland." Chinese law restricts foreign ownership of some internet businesses, so those licensed entities are controlled by contract rather than owned: the VIE structure. At JD the contract-controlled entities are a small part of the business: the 20-F puts their external revenues at 6.8% of consolidated net revenues in 2025; most of the business runs through subsidiaries JD owns through equity. A second layer matters more for valuation: several big units have outside shareholders, which is why the September 30, 2026 balance sheet carries RMB64.4B of non-controlling interests. Part of the consolidated cash belongs, in economic terms, to those minority holders.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Owned logistics network First-party inventory = authenticity Electronics & appliance scale Supplier payables float (RMB198B) Net cash ≈ RMB141B Brand partners (Costco, CHANEL, ZARA)

The moat is trust delivered fast. Because JD buys and stocks most of what it sells, a shopper buying a phone or a refrigerator knows it is genuine and when it will arrive; that is why a brand like Costco picked JD as its only official e-commerce partner in China and why the Q2 2026 release leans on "guaranteed product authenticity" in luxury beauty. The cost of that moat is a balance sheet full of goods: RMB116.2B of inventory at September 30, 2026, funded largely by RMB197.6B owed to suppliers. That float is a competitive advantage too, since suppliers finance JD's working capital, but it is also the reason the headline cash pile should not be read as spare money. The vulnerability is that the moat protects the order, not the margin: in a market with Alibaba, PDD and Meituan all fighting for the same wallet, JD's retail margin has never been above the mid-single digits in the data shown here.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Electronics is a cycle, not a trend. Electronics and home appliances are 41.3% of revenue. They fell 11.8% in Q2 2026 against a high base, were still down 0.7% in Q3, and are down 7.5% over the first nine months. When the biggest category shrinks, the group stalls: nine-month revenue grew only 1.6% even with general merchandise up 9.5%. The release does not say when the comparison with last year's strong sales stops weighing on growth, and Ceconomy adds a second electronics retailer, in Europe.
02
The food-delivery bill is smaller, not gone. New Businesses still lost RMB9.6B in Q3 2026, about 64% of what JD Retail earned in the same quarter, and only RMB0.3B less than in Q2. Management describes the trend as narrowing; the Q3 release gives no date or target for breakeven.
03
Thin margins leave no room for a price war. TTM net margin is 1.2% and TTM operating margin 0.6% on RMB1.32 trillion of revenue. A one-point swing in gross margin is worth roughly RMB13B a year, close to JD's entire trailing net income of RMB16.0B.
04
Structure and control. Minority holders own RMB64.4B of the equity in consolidated units, part of the business runs through contract-controlled VIEs, and Class B shares carry 20 votes each. Outside ADS holders have economic exposure with little say over how the cash is spent.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items separate JD's reported profit from its operating reality. First, non-operating income: in Q3 2026 "others, net" added RMB2.3B and the share of results of equity investees RMB2.2B, against operating income of RMB5.2B and interest expense of RMB0.7B, so about 43% of pre-tax income of RMB9.0B came from outside operations (in Q2 it was more than half). Second, tax: Q3 2026 income tax expense was RMB1.6B against RMB0.3B a year earlier, which flatters last year's comparison. Third, buybacks: JD repurchased 183.2M Class A shares (91.6M ADS) for about US$3.0B in 2025, 6.3% of shares, all cancelled, and another 69.9M shares (34.9M ADS) for US$1.0B in the first half of 2026, 2.5% of the shares outstanding at the end of 2025. US$1.0B of authorisation remained at June 30, 2026; the Q3 release reports no repurchases, and 2,691M shares were outstanding at September 30. Diluted weighted ADS count was 1.41B in Q3 2026 against 1.48B a year earlier, which lifts per-ADS figures relative to total profit. Fourth, regulatory and impairment charges: on April 17, 2026 China's State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) penalised a JD consolidated entity, among other e-commerce platforms, about RMB635M, finding that JD had not properly checked the qualifications of third-party decorated-cake sellers and order-transfer service providers on its platform, in breach of the Food Safety Law; Q3 2026 carried RMB0.6B of goodwill and long-lived asset impairments. JD adds both back in its non-GAAP figures.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 14.7x trailing GAAP earnings and 9.3x non-GAAP for a company whose trailing numbers still carry about RMB44.6B of New Businesses losses (Q4 2025 through Q3 2026), losses that are shrinking quarter by quarter. Net cash of about $21.1B covers 63% of the $33.5B market cap; the dividend yields 4.0%; buybacks retired 6.3% of the shares in 2025, and JD bought back 2.5% more in the first half of 2026; and trailing free cash flow of $2.82B is an 8.4% yield. Revenue grew again in Q3, the core retail business held a 5.8% margin, and marketplace and logistics revenues are growing. Ceconomy brings more than 1,000 European stores for EUR1,335M. If New Businesses losses keep narrowing toward breakeven and electronics stops being measured against last year's unusually strong sales, earnings rise sharply off a low base, and 31 of 35 analysts, with an average target of $37.10, are positioned for that.

Bear Case

The cash is not spare. It sits beside RMB197.6B owed to suppliers and RMB64.4B of minority interests, and management has shown it will spend it: RMB46.6B went into New Businesses in 2025, RMB29.8B more in the first nine months of 2026, and EUR1,335M on Ceconomy in November. Electronics, about 41% of revenue, is still shrinking, the TTM operating margin is 0.6%, and Q3 free cash flow was −RMB23.9B. About 43% of Q3 pre-tax income came from outside operations, mainly investment and equity-investee gains. Control sits with Class B shares carrying 20 votes each. The stock has fallen 70% over the five-year chart while its home index was roughly flat (−0.8%). Strong Buy ratings slipped from 27 in June to 24 in November 2026, and four of the eight calls after the Q3 print cut their targets.

Capital allocation to new fronts. Food delivery, Joybuy in Europe and Jingxi absorbed RMB46.6B of operating losses in 2025 and RMB29.8B in the first nine months of 2026, and JD has now paid EUR1,335M for control of Ceconomy, a European electronics retailer. Ceconomy adds European electronics demand to the same bet.

Changes in US–China rules on listed Chinese companies affect the ADS directly; the Hong Kong line (9618) is the fallback listing.
Industry Commentary
The food-delivery war: what it cost JD
EASING, NOT OVER

JD's reported numbers show the scale of its 2025 push into food delivery, a business it entered in a market where Meituan is the listed incumbent and Alibaba competes too. The New Businesses segment lost RMB1.3B in Q1 2025 and then RMB14.8B, RMB15.7B and RMB14.8B in the next three quarters, for RMB46.6B over the year, more than double JD's FY2025 net income of RMB19.6B. In 2026 the losses are falling, to RMB10.4B in Q1, RMB9.9B in Q2 and RMB9.6B in Q3, and the Q3 release credits "enhanced operational efficiency and revenue diversification." Marketing expenses fell 26.2% in Q3, which the release puts down "primarily due to the optimization of promotional spending on new business initiatives." The year-on-year cut is large (RMB15.7B to RMB9.6B); the step from Q2 to Q3 was only RMB0.3B, and the release gives no breakeven date.

First-party retail and the margin trade-off
ONGOING HEADWIND

A first-party retailer owns the inventory risk, so its profit depends on what it can charge above cost. JD's Q3 2026 gross margin of 18.1% rose from 16.9% because the mix shifted toward marketplace and marketing revenue (+9.6%) and general merchandise (+8.1%) while electronics was flat (−0.7%). That is the right direction for margins, and in Q3 it came with modest top-line growth (+3.5%). But electronics is where JD's scale advantage is greatest, and with Ceconomy JD now also owns a large electronics retailer in Europe. Neither the Q3 2026 nor the full-year 2025 release gives numeric revenue or profit guidance.

The valuation gap — cash, buybacks and the "China discount"
THE CORE DEBATE

At $24.92 JD trades at 14.7x trailing GAAP earnings, 9.3x non-GAAP, a 4.0% dividend yield and an 8.4% trailing free-cash-flow yield, with net cash of about $21.1B against a $33.5B market cap. The bull framing: strip out net cash and the operating business is priced at about $12B for a retailer that earned RMB43.5B of segment operating profit in Retail in the first nine months alone, while management spent US$3.0B on buybacks in 2025 and pays about US$1.4B a year in dividends. The bear framing: the cash is supplier float and partly belongs to minority holders, the controlling shareholder decides what happens to it, and 2025 showed it can be spent on promotions for new businesses rather than handed back; in November part of it went to Ceconomy. The Hang Seng was roughly flat (−0.8%) over the five-year chart while JD fell 70.4%: the discount is specific to JD, not just to China. What would settle it: New Businesses losses near zero, or a capital return large enough to show the cash belongs to shareholders.
