01 Company Overview

Jack Henry started in 1976 in Monett, Missouri, when Jack Henry and Jerry Hall rented a former engine repair shop for $40 a month and began writing software for small banks. Fifty years later it still lives in Monett and still sells to the same kind of customer: roughly 7,200 banks and credit unions, most of them community institutions too small to build their own technology. It runs their core (SilverLake and CIF 20/20 for banks, Symitar for credit unions), moves their payments, and sells the digital-banking, lending and fraud tools that bolt on top. Fiscal 2026 (year to June 30) revenue was $2.544B, up 7.1%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $6.98, up 11.9%.

This is a business built on the most boring promise in finance: the ledger will post tonight, the same as every night. Banks switch core providers about as often as people move house, and when they do it is a multi-year project. That inertia is Jack Henry's whole edge, and the fiscal 2026 numbers show it working — management reported a record 58 competitive core wins, 14 of them institutions above $1 billion in assets. And yet the stock closed September 21, 2026 at $151.56, below where it stood five years earlier ($164.06 at the end of September 2021), and 17% below its December 2025 close of $182.48. The question this report keeps asking: when a company keeps winning and the price keeps not moving, is the market missing something, or is it pricing a ceiling on growth that the company itself signalled at its September 15 investor day?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Core-conversion switching cost Cross-sell into 7,200+ institutions Recurring processing revenue Community-bank focus Near-zero debt (D/E 0.04) 22 straight years of dividend increases

The moat is the conversion. Moving a bank off one core and onto another means migrating every account, every loan and every integration, retraining every teller, and betting the institution's reputation on one weekend cutover. Clients do it rarely, which means a core provider's revenue behaves more like an annuity than like software sales. Jack Henry's differentiation inside that is who it serves: community banks and credit unions, where its service reputation has let it take share from larger rivals — the CEO said on the fiscal 2026 call, per the call highlights, that the company is taking share from all of its competitors rather than from one. The vulnerability sits at the other end of the same annuity: the client base shrinks every time a small bank is bought by a bigger one, and a good year of wins can be offset by a wave of mergers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth is steady, not accelerating — and management said so. Going into the September 15, 2026 investor day, management had already said fiscal 2027 revenue growth should be similar to the 7% of fiscal 2026, with a step-up only in fiscal 2028 and 2029 as signed deals are implemented (per management's remarks at the Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology conference on September 10, 2026, as reported by Investing.com). The investor day was the first time it put numbers on those later years; the stock fell 6.37% that day. The specific FY28–FY29 targets are not reproduced here because they were not confirmed on a company document or a primary source. A company priced near 21x earnings needs either acceleration or margin expansion; the guide offered neither in the near term.
02
Margins stall in fiscal 2027. Q4 FY26 GAAP operating income fell 12.2% and GAAP EPS 10.2%, on lower deconversion fees and lower-margin implementation work (per the call highlights). The FY27 GAAP operating-margin guide is 24.5–24.7%, below FY26's 25.0%, and AI innovation, cyber infrastructure and a data-centre consolidation project were among the cost pressures discussed on the call.
03
Trust is the product, and it was tested. On August 31, 2026 the company disclosed a cybersecurity incident: a voice-phishing ("vishing") attack attributed to ShinyHunters reached "a limited portion of our internal, non-production corporate environment," and personal data for "fewer than 10 clients" was impacted. The company said the incident "is not financially material" and that no client-facing or core systems were accessed. For a vendor whose pitch is reliability, reputational cost can exceed financial cost — and it lands in the middle of the core-win sales cycle.
04
Client consolidation. Every acquisition of a Jack Henry client by a bank on another platform is a lost relationship. Deconversion fees soften the blow in the year they are paid and then disappear, as the drop from $42.8M to a guided ~$23M shows.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 21.7x trailing and 20.9x forward earnings for a company with 22 consecutive years of dividend increases, almost no debt (D/E 0.04), ROIC above 22%, a record 58 core wins, and a sticky client base whose switching cost is a multi-year conversion. Management bought $164M of stock at an average of $140 in the June quarter, and every price target on the board — the lowest is $170 — sits above the price. Deconversion fees make fiscal 2027 GAAP growth look slower than the business is; cloud conversions and signed-but-not-yet-live deals are what management says will lift growth in FY28 and FY29. If the core wins convert on schedule, the stock is back in the lower half of a five-year range while earnings are at a five-year high.

Bear Case

The company told you what it is: fiscal 2027 growth about the same as fiscal 2026's 7%, acceleration deferred to FY28–FY29, with GAAP operating margin guided down in FY27 and EPS guided up only 5–6%. At over 21x trailing earnings that is not cheap for mid-single-digit EPS growth. The Q4 quarter already showed the weak spots — core segment revenue +1.9%, GAAP operating income −12.2%. A 6.21% short float says a meaningful minority expects de-rating to continue, the August cybersecurity incident hands competitors a talking point in exactly the sales cycles that produce core wins, and bank consolidation keeps shrinking the addressable client count. Five years of flat-to-down price despite rising EPS is what multiple compression looks like, and nothing in the guide says it has finished.

Top 3 Risks

01
Multiple compression without growth acceleration. The investor day confirmed a gradual growth path rather than a step change, and the market sold 6.37% on it. If FY27 lands at the guide and no faster, the stock has to earn its multiple on a 5–6% EPS growth year.
02
Security and reputation. The August 2026 incident was contained to a non-production environment and judged not financially material by the company, but a second incident — or evidence that the first cost core wins — would hit the one asset that justifies the switching-cost moat: trust.
03
Margin investment cycle. AI development, cyber infrastructure and data-centre consolidation spending are the stated reasons FY27 margins will not step up. If those costs run longer than a year, the operating leverage the bull case needs moves further out.
Industry Commentary
The core-processing annuity — and its leak
STRUCTURAL

Core processors sell the ledger a bank cannot run without, on long contracts, with conversion costs that make leaving a board-level decision. That makes the revenue durable. The leak is consolidation: when a client bank is acquired, its accounts usually move to the acquirer's platform, and the processor collects a one-time deconversion fee on the way out. Jack Henry's $42.8M of fiscal 2026 deconversion revenue, guided to about $23M in fiscal 2027, is the visible trace of that process. Growth for a processor in this market is therefore wins minus losses — which is why the company leads with competitive core wins, and why 58 of them in a year is the number that matters most.

AI: a cost now, a velocity gain later
ONGOING HEADWIND

Per the call highlights, Jack Henry has 22 AI-enabled products in market with more than 20 planned, has hired nine "AI coaches" to train over 2,000 associates, and says AI's benefits tend to arrive across several fiscal years through faster development. The near-term effect is spend: AI innovation costs and frontier-model security were among the cost and risk items discussed on the call, alongside the guidance that FY27 margins will not step up significantly. For a vendor to community banks, AI is less a threat to the product than a feature race — the risk is falling behind larger rivals with bigger research budgets, not being replaced.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

JKHY trades at about 15.2x EV/EBITDA and 21.7x trailing earnings (our calculations at $151.56), with a 1.61% dividend yield and a free-cash-flow yield of 5–6.5% depending on the definition. The bull framing: that is a fair-to-cheap price for a near-unlevered, 23%-ROIC compounder whose five-year price decline came while EPS rose; every analyst target is above the price, and management is buying stock. The bear framing: the multiple was set when the market expected acceleration, and the company has now said acceleration is gradual and years away. A steady 7% grower with flat near-term margins may simply be worth what it trades at. The adjudicator is not the P/E; it is whether core wins turn into faster non-GAAP revenue growth in FY28, as management says it expects.
