01 Company Overview

Johnson & Johnson is the world's largest diversified healthcare company, operating two segments since the 2023 spin-off of its consumer business (Kenvue): Innovative Medicine (pharmaceuticals — 64% of sales) and MedTech (surgical, cardiovascular, orthopaedic and vision devices — 36%). The company generated FY2025 revenue of $94.2B, up 6.0% YoY, and is guiding to roughly $101.1B in 2026 — which would make it the first pharma-led company to cross $100B in annual sales. J&J ranks #1 globally in healthcare by market cap alongside Eli Lilly, holds a rare AAA credit rating (one of only two US corporates), and is a Dividend King with 64 consecutive years of dividend increases.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Scale & Diversification 26 Billion-Dollar Platforms $17B/yr R&D Engine AAA Credit Rating 64-Yr Dividend King Physician Switching Costs Global Regulatory Expertise

J&J's moat is breadth plus balance sheet. It is the only company with top-3 positions in both pharmaceuticals and medical devices, with 26 platforms each generating $1B+ in annual sales — no single product exceeds ~15% of revenue even with Darzalex at $14.4B. Its AAA credit rating (shared only with Microsoft among US corporates) lets it outspend rivals through cycles: ~$17B of annual R&D plus $30B+ of M&A since 2022 (Abiomed, Shockwave, Intra-Cellular, Halda) executed without stressing leverage (D/E ~0.58). In devices, Ethicon consumables and Biosense Webster's installed mapping systems create genuine switching costs — an EP lab trained on CARTO mapping re-orders J&J catheters for years. In pharma, the moat is narrower and patent-dependent, but J&J's commercial infrastructure (the reason Protagonist and Legend Biotech partnered rather than went alone) is itself an asset: it can take an in-licensed molecule to $5B faster than nearly anyone.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Patent cliffs are a treadmill. Stelara's collapse (−41% in FY25, from a $10.4B peak) shows how fast biologics erode post-LOE. Darzalex, now 15% of company revenue, faces key patent expiries in the early 2030s, and Xarelto was selected for Medicare price negotiation. The pipeline must replace ~$10B+ of at-risk revenue every cycle just to stand still.
▸
Litigation overhang. The July 2026 $5.5B ovarian-cancer talc settlement resolves ~76,000 claims but excludes mesothelioma cases and future filings; three failed bankruptcy attempts preceded it. Legal costs have consumed billions of FCF and the brand damage is permanent in consumer memory even post-Kenvue.
▸
Tech disruption in EP. Boston Scientific's Farapulse PFA system has taken meaningful ablation share since 2024, forcing Biosense Webster into a defensive launch cycle (Varipulse, dual-energy ThermoCool). If PFA becomes the default energy source, J&J's ~40%+ EP share could compress materially.
▸
Policy & pricing. IRA Medicare negotiation (Stelara, Xarelto, Imbruvica already selected), potential "most-favored-nation" pricing rules, and pharma-specific tariff proposals all target the US price umbrella that funds ~55% of J&J's Innovative Medicine revenue.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP results are heavily distorted right now. The July 28, 2026 talc settlement (~$5.5B, resolving ~76,000 ovarian-cancer claims, ~$3B cash expected in 2027; the Q2 10-Q carried a ~$3.7B present-value reserve) follows three rejected bankruptcy attempts, including the $8B-plus plan thrown out in March 2025. Acquisition accounting also weighs on reported earnings: Intra-Cellular Therapies ($14.6B, closed April 2025) and Halda Therapeutics (~$3.05B, late 2025, a $0.10 EPS drag) added amortization and acquired-IPR&D charges, alongside $228M of FY25 restructuring (orthopaedics/surgery rationalization) and ~$300M of Q2 2026 orthopaedics-separation costs. Net effect: trailing GAAP EPS ($8.63) understates run-rate earning power (adjusted 2026 guide: $11.68). Treat trailing P/E and net-income-derived ratios with caution.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

The first $100B pharma-led company, with oncology compounding 20%+ (Darzalex/Carvykti toward $50B by 2030), the Stelara cliff already absorbed, Icotyde and Tremfya rebuilding immunology, and the talc discount finally lifted. The ortho separation and MedTech cardio (Abiomed/Shockwave) add margin and growth optionality, all on an AAA balance sheet with 64 years of dividend growth — a defensive that suddenly grows like a compounder.

▼ Bear Case

At 23× forward earnings — the top of its decade range and above the S&P 500's ~22× — JNJ is priced for flawless execution while offering ~7-8% EPS growth and a PEG near 3. The talc settlement still needs 95% claimant acceptance and excludes mesothelioma; Darzalex's early-2030s LOE is the next cliff; Boston Scientific is eating the EP franchise; and IRA/MFN pricing policy directly targets its US profit pool. Reversion to a historical ~16-17× multiple implies 25%+ downside with the dividend yielding only 2%.

01
Valuation / multiple reversion. The 2025-26 re-rating accounts for most of the 5-year return; at ~23× forward with a 0.23 beta crowd positioned in it as a bond proxy, any growth disappointment or rate shock hits the multiple harder than the estimates.
02
Litigation tail risk. If talc settlement participation falls short of 95%, litigation resumes; mesothelioma claims, future filings, and other product liability (e.g., pelvic mesh legacy) keep an irreducible legal line-item in the model.
03
Policy shock to US drug pricing. Medicare negotiation rounds, most-favored-nation proposals, and pharma tariffs could compress US net prices on Xarelto, Stelara, Imbruvica and eventually Darzalex — a direct hit to the segment generating ~64% of revenue at the highest margins.
  Industry Commentary — What Matters Right Now
US Drug-Pricing Policy & Pharma Tariffs
ONGOING HEADWIND

The IRA's negotiated Medicare prices are now phasing in — J&J products Stelara, Xarelto and Imbruvica were in the first wave — and subsequent selection rounds reach deeper into big-pharma portfolios each year. Layered on top: "most-favored-nation" international reference pricing proposals and sector-specific tariff threats on imported drugs. J&J's $55B four-year US manufacturing commitment is partly genuine capacity, partly political insurance. The industry-wide effect is a slow compression of the US price umbrella that funds roughly half of global pharma economics; for J&J specifically, every 1% of US net price is roughly $350-400M of high-margin revenue.

The Biosimilar/LOE Treadmill Accelerates
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Stelara's 41% single-year collapse is the template for what happens to $10B biologics post-exclusivity — faster and deeper erosion than the small-molecule era, as PBMs (pharmacy benefit managers) force-switch to biosimilars. Across the industry, ~$300B of branded revenue faces LOE by 2030 (Keytruda 2028 is the headline event; Darzalex follows in the early 2030s). This is why J&J paid $14.6B for Intra-Cellular, $3B for Halda, and partnered on Icotyde — replacement pipelines are now bought as much as built, and the market is rewarding companies (J&J, AbbVie) that pre-funded the cliff while punishing those that didn't.

MedTech: Procedure Volumes Strong, Innovation Cycles Decide Share
EMERGING TAILWIND

Surgical and cardiac procedure volumes remain healthy on aging demographics, and the GLP-1 "fewer surgeries" scare of 2023 has largely been disproven — if anything, healthier patients qualify for more procedures. But within categories, technology transitions are brutal: pulsed-field ablation flipped EP share to Boston Scientific in two years, robotics keeps compounding for Intuitive, and intravascular lithotripsy (Shockwave) created a $1B+ category from scratch. Bull framing: J&J owns two of the best growth assets (Shockwave, Abiomed), is counterpunching in PFA, and the ortho spin concentrates the portfolio on exactly these high-innovation categories. Bear framing: if Varipulse can't hold the EP lab and Ottava arrives years behind Intuitive's installed base, MedTech becomes a ~4% grower that dilutes the pharma story — and the bull case rests almost entirely on oncology execution.
