01 Company Overview

Johnson & Johnson is the world's largest diversified healthcare company, operating two segments since the 2023 spin-off of its consumer business (Kenvue): Innovative Medicine (pharmaceuticals — 64% of sales) and MedTech (surgical, cardiovascular, orthopaedic and vision devices — 36%). The company generated FY2025 revenue of $94.2B, up 6.0% YoY, and after its Q3 report on October 13 is guiding to $101.2B in 2026 (midpoint), which would put annual sales above $100B for the first time. The third quarter showed the split inside the company: drug sales grew 7.7% while MedTech grew 2.5%, and the cost of two early-stage drug deals pushed adjusted EPS down 7.5%. J&J ranks #17 in the S&P 500 by market cap, holds a rare AAA credit rating (S&P rates only two US public companies AAA: J&J and Microsoft), and is a Dividend King with 64 consecutive years of dividend increases.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Scale & Diversification 29 Billion-Dollar Platforms $15B/yr R&D Engine AAA Credit Rating 64-Yr Dividend King Physician Switching Costs Global Regulatory Expertise

J&J's moat is breadth plus balance sheet. It is one of the few companies with large businesses in both prescription drugs and medical devices, and 29 of its products and platforms each generate $1B+ in annual sales (company remarks, Oct 13, 2026) — no single product exceeds ~15% of revenue even with Darzalex at $14.4B. Its AAA credit rating (S&P gives it to only one other US public company, Microsoft) lets it outspend rivals through cycles: $14.7B of R&D in FY2025 plus $30B+ of M&A since 2022 (Abiomed, Shockwave, Intra-Cellular, Halda, and Firefly Bio in 2026) executed without stressing leverage (D/E ~0.58). In devices, Ethicon consumables and Biosense Webster's installed mapping systems create genuine switching costs — an EP lab trained on CARTO mapping re-orders J&J catheters for years. In pharma, the moat is narrower and patent-dependent, but J&J's commercial infrastructure (the reason Protagonist and Legend Biotech partnered rather than went alone) is itself an asset: it can take an in-licensed molecule to $5B faster than nearly anyone.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Patent cliffs are a treadmill. Stelara's collapse (−41% in FY25, from about $10.4B the year before) shows how fast biologics erode post-LOE. Darzalex, now 15% of company revenue, faces key patent expiries in the early 2030s, and Xarelto was selected for Medicare price negotiation. The pipeline must replace ~$10B+ of at-risk revenue every cycle just to stand still.
▸
Litigation overhang. The July 27, 2026 proposal commits $5.5B to settle the ~76,000 remaining ovarian-cancer talc claims, but only if at least 95% of claimants take part; J&J says it had already settled about 95% of filed mesothelioma suits. Three failed bankruptcy attempts preceded it, and the charge cut Q3 2026 GAAP EPS to $0.09. Legal costs have consumed billions of FCF, and the brand damage outlasts the 2023 Kenvue separation.
▸
Tech disruption in EP. Boston Scientific's Farapulse PFA system has taken meaningful ablation share since 2024, forcing Biosense Webster into a defensive launch cycle (Varipulse, dual-energy ThermoCool); J&J's electrophysiology sales grew just 4.4% in Q3 2026. If PFA becomes the default energy source, J&J's ~40%+ EP share could compress materially.
▸
Policy & pricing. IRA Medicare negotiation (Stelara, Xarelto, Imbruvica already selected), potential "most-favored-nation" pricing rules, and pharma-specific tariff proposals all target US drug prices, which fund a large share of J&J's Innovative Medicine revenue.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP results are heavily distorted right now. Q3 2026 carried about $7B of charges for the proposed talc settlement, the Firefly Bio acquisition and the Sail Biomedicines collaboration (Ryan Koors, head of investor relations, on the Oct 13 call), cutting GAAP EPS to $0.09 from $2.12 a year earlier. The July 27, 2026 talc proposal ($5.5B for the ~76,000 remaining ovarian-cancer claims, a first payment of no more than $3B in 2027 and nothing more before 2028) follows three rejected bankruptcy attempts, including the $8B-plus plan thrown out in March 2025. J&J also counts purchased early-stage research (acquired IPR&D) inside its adjusted EPS: Firefly and Sail cut 2026 adjusted EPS by about $0.64 and will cut 2027's by about $1.36 (8-K, July 29, 2026). Earlier deals add amortization too: Intra-Cellular Therapies ($14.6B, closed April 2025) and Halda Therapeutics (~$3.05B, late 2025), alongside $228M of FY25 restructuring and about $0.3B of Q2 2026 orthopaedics-separation costs. Net effect: trailing GAAP EPS ($6.60) sits far below the adjusted 2026 guide ($11.10 midpoint). Treat trailing P/E and net-income-derived ratios with caution.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

Sales on course to pass $100B in 2026, with oncology compounding fast (management targets $50B by 2030), the Stelara cliff largely absorbed, Tremfya (+68% in Q3) and Icotyde rebuilding immunology, and the talc discount lifting once the settlement closes. The Q3 MedTech miss was partly temporary by management's account (an Abiomed study, the Middle East conflict), Ottava is now authorized, and the orthopaedics spin-off removes the slowest grower, all on an AAA balance sheet with 64 years of dividend growth. Guidance went up even as two deals took $0.64 off 2026 EPS: a defensive that grows like a compounder.

▼ Bear Case

At 21.6× next year's consensus earnings, slightly above the S&P 500's forward multiple, JNJ is priced for smooth execution while consensus sees only ~5% EPS growth into 2027, held down by $1.36 of deal costs (a PEG near 4). MedTech grew 2.5% in Q3, below management's own expectations; the talc settlement still needs 95% claimant participation; Darzalex's early-2030s patent expiry is the next cliff; Boston Scientific keeps taking share in EP; and US drug-pricing policy targets its biggest profit pool. A 16–17× multiple on the $11.71 2027 consensus would mean $187–$199 a share, 21–26% below the Oct 15 close, with the dividend yielding about 2%.

  Industry Commentary — What Matters Right Now
US Drug-Pricing Policy & Pharma Tariffs
ONGOING HEADWIND

The IRA's negotiated Medicare prices are now phasing in — J&J products Stelara, Xarelto and Imbruvica were in the first wave — and subsequent selection rounds reach deeper into big-pharma portfolios each year. Layered on top: "most-favored-nation" international reference pricing proposals and sector-specific tariff threats on imported drugs. J&J's $55B four-year US manufacturing commitment is partly genuine capacity, partly political insurance. The industry-wide effect is a slow squeeze on US drug prices.

The Biosimilar/LOE Treadmill Accelerates
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Stelara's 41% single-year collapse is the template for what happens to $10B biologics post-exclusivity — faster and deeper erosion than the small-molecule era, as PBMs (pharmacy benefit managers) force-switch to biosimilars. Across the industry, several of the biggest drugs lose exclusivity around the turn of the decade (Keytruda in 2028 is the headline event; Darzalex follows in the early 2030s). This is why J&J paid $14.6B for Intra-Cellular, $3B for Halda, bought Firefly Bio, signed with Sail Biomedicines and partnered on Icotyde — replacement pipelines are now bought as much as built, and the market is rewarding companies (J&J, AbbVie) that pre-funded the cliff while punishing those that didn't.

MedTech: Procedure Volumes Strong, Innovation Cycles Decide Share
EMERGING TAILWIND

Surgical and cardiac procedure volumes remain healthy on aging demographics, and the GLP-1 "fewer surgeries" scare of 2023 has largely been disproven — if anything, healthier patients qualify for more procedures. But within categories, technology transitions are brutal: pulsed-field ablation flipped EP share to Boston Scientific in two years, robotics keeps compounding for Intuitive, and intravascular lithotripsy (Shockwave) created a $1B+ category from scratch. Bull framing: J&J owns two of the best growth assets (Shockwave, Abiomed), is counterpunching in PFA, and the ortho spin concentrates the portfolio on exactly these high-innovation categories. J&J's own Q3 2026 showed the risk: MedTech grew 2.5% and electrophysiology 4.4%, and management called the result below its own standard. Bear framing: if Varipulse can't hold the EP lab and Ottava arrives years behind Intuitive's installed base, MedTech becomes a ~4% grower that dilutes the pharma story — and the bull case rests almost entirely on oncology execution.
