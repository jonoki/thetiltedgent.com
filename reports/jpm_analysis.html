03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
#1 US Deposit Franchise Fortress Balance Sheet (13.9% CET1) Full-Service Diversification $19.8B Tech Budget Scale: $5.1T Assets Regulatory Barriers to Entry Brand & Talent Magnet

JPMorgan's moat is scale compounded across every banking line. Its $2.8T of deposits (Sep 30, 2026) is cheap, sticky funding; its 2026 technology budget of $19.8B exceeds the total revenue of most regional banks; and what it calls its fortress balance sheet (CET1 of 13.9% at Sep 30, 2026 against an 11.5% requirement including buffers) lets it buy distressed rivals — First Republic in 2023 — and lean into markets when competitors retreat. Diversification is itself a moat: consumer lending income, cyclical deal fees and fee-based asset management offset each other across cycles. Post-2008 capital rules make it very hard to build a new bank of this scale. The result: a 20% ROTCE in Q3 2026 (24% for the first nine months) at a size most rivals can't match.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Succession overhang. Jamie Dimon, 70, has run the firm since 2005, and the stock trades at about 3.0x tangible book value (shareholders' equity minus goodwill and other intangible assets: $335.53 against $112.64 a share at Sep 30, 2026). The June 2026 elevation of co-presidents Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh, with Marianne Lake's retirement, starts the clock; a poorly handled transition could compress the multiple.
▸
Rate and balance sensitivity. Net interest income, the largest revenue line, rose 13% to $27.1B in Q3 2026 on higher deposit and loan balances. Management's 2027 central case of about $107B excluding Markets depends on that balance growth continuing; slower growth, deposit-cost competition or a shift in rates would undercut it.
▸
Regulatory & political exposure. As the largest G-SIB, JPMorgan carries the highest capital surcharge (4.5%). Basel III endgame re-proposals (the last round of the post-2008 bank capital rules), consumer-fee rules and political scrutiny of "too big to fail" cut both ways — today's lighter-touch regulation could reverse after a future election.
▸
Fintech & stablecoin disruption. Payments and deposits — the profit core — face encroachment from stablecoins, private credit displacing bank lending, and fintechs (Stripe, Square, Robinhood, Coinbase). JPMorgan is responding (JPMD deposit token, blockchain settlement), but disintermediation of deposit economics is the structural bear case for all banks.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

The best-run bank of its size grew tangible book value per share 7% in a year (to $112.64) while earning a 24% ROTCE over the first nine months of 2026, holds #1 positions in US retail deposits and global investment-banking fees, and still runs 2.4 points of CET1 above its requirement. Net interest income is growing again (+13% in Q3) on deposit and loan growth, with a 2027 central case of about $107B excluding Markets; AWM's $5.2T of AUM and $263B of quarterly client inflows add fees on top, and the Apple Card and the $20B QIA partnership extend the lead. If the capital held back from buybacks earns what management expects, the slowdown in buybacks pays off. At 13.4x forward earnings it still trades well below the S&P 500.

▼ Bear Case

You're paying about 3.0x tangible book value right as Q3 showed the limits: profit missed consensus, expenses rose 12%, deal fees fell 8% from Q2 and Dimon said client activity moderated. Buybacks, a steady prop under per-share earnings, were cut to $664M, and the return on whatever replaces them is unproven. The average analyst target ($365.45) implies about 9% upside with buyers and holders split 14 to 13, Dimon's succession clock is running, and a move back to 2.0x tangible book would mean a fall of about a third even if book value holds.

1.
Valuation + succession convergence. A premium valuation (about 3.0x tangible book) and a 70-year-old CEO whose succession has begun; a messy transition or abrupt exit announcement is the clearest single trigger for a lower valuation.
2.
Macro/credit turn. A genuine recession would hit all three engines at once — card losses above the ~3.2% charge-off rate management expects for 2026, a collapse in deal fees, and reserve builds (Q3 already added $539M to reserves) — as the 38% month-end fall of 2021–22 showed.
3.
NII and capital disappointment. The 2027 central case of about $107B of net interest income excluding Markets assumes balance growth continues; slower loan demand, deposit-cost competition or a shift in rates would miss it, and capital held back from buybacks would then earn less than promised.
  Industry Commentary — Money-Center Banks, October 2026
Lending Income: Balances Doing the Work
GROWTH, WITH CONDITIONS
The gain from rising rates has given way to growth from volume. JPMorgan's net interest income rose 13% in Q3 2026 to $27.1B, which the company put down to higher deposit, card and corporate-loan balances, partly offset by lower rates: firm-wide average loans rose 10% and deposits 8% from a year earlier. Its 2027 central case of about $107B excluding Markets keeps that going. The condition is that customers keep borrowing and depositing at today's pace; if loan demand slows or banks have to pay up for deposits, the sector's lending income stalls and growth again depends on fees.
Capital-Markets Upcycle + Regulatory Thaw
EMERGING TAILWIND
The deal and trading boom continued into Q3 2026 but cooled: JPM's investment-banking fees rose 14% from a year earlier yet fell 8% from Q2, markets revenue rose 15% on a 49% jump in equities while fixed income slipped 4%, and Dimon said client activity “did moderate somewhat during the quarter.” On capital, JPMorgan's stress capital buffer (the extra capital the Federal Reserve's stress test requires) is 2.5% through Sep 30, 2027, putting its CET1 requirement at 11.5% including buffers against 13.9% held at Sep 30. JPMorgan chose to use that room for lending and investment rather than buybacks this quarter. The cycle risk: deal fees are the most volatile revenue in finance, and 2026's strong comparisons get hard in 2027.
Credit Normalization & Disintermediation — Bull vs. Bear Frame
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The cycle's central question: JPM's card charge-offs ran at 2.91% in Q3 2026 against a ~3.2% expectation for the year, firm-wide net charge-offs fell $389M from a year earlier to $2.2B, yet the firm still added $539M to reserves, and private credit plus stablecoins are probing the banks' lending and deposit businesses from outside bank regulation. Bull frame: credit is behaving, and scale banks convert credit stress into share gains (First Republic proved it). Lighter regulation and loan growth could extend high returns into 2027–28. Bear frame: at about 3.0x tangible book, JPM is priced for the bull frame; if unemployment rises, charge-offs overshoot, deal fees stall, and bank valuations fall — with the most premium-priced name having the most multiple to lose.
