01 Company Overview

JPMorgan Chase is the largest US bank by assets (~$4.4T) and the world's most valuable bank by market capitalization, operating across consumer banking, credit cards, investment banking, markets, payments, commercial banking, and asset & wealth management. In FY2025 the firm generated roughly $185.8B in managed revenue (up ~3% reported; up ~7% excluding the prior-year Visa share gain) and a record $57.0B of net income ($20.02 EPS, 20% ROTCE). It ranks #1 in US deposits, #1 in global investment-banking fees (8.4% wallet share in FY2025), and is the largest US credit-card issuer by purchase volume.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
#1 US Deposit Franchise Fortress Balance Sheet (14.1% CET1) Full-Service Diversification $19.8B Tech Budget Scale: $4.4T Assets Regulatory Barriers to Entry Brand & Talent Magnet

JPMorgan's moat is scale compounded across every banking vertical. Its ~$2.5T deposit base is the cheapest funding on Wall Street; its 2026 technology budget of $19.8B exceeds the total revenue of most regional banks; and its "fortress balance sheet" (CET1 of 14.1% vs. a ~12.3% requirement) lets it buy distressed rivals — First Republic in 2023 — and lean into markets when competitors retreat. Diversification is itself a moat: consumer NII, cyclical IB fees, and fee-based AWM offset each other across cycles, which is why the firm has out-earned its cost of equity every year since 2011. Post-2008 capital rules effectively prohibit building a new bank of this scale, and 400+ AI projects generating an estimated $600M of efficiencies deepen the data advantage. The result: 20%+ ROTCE at a size where peers struggle to reach 13%.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Succession overhang. Jamie Dimon, 70, has run the firm since 2005 and is widely credited with a "Dimon premium" in the stock (~2.7x tangible book vs. ~1.6x peer average). The June 2026 elevation of co-presidents Doug Petno and Troy Rohrbaugh — and Marianne Lake's abrupt exit — starts the clock; a poorly handled transition could compress the multiple.
▸
Rate sensitivity. Management guides 2026 NII ex-Markets to ~$95B, roughly flat, absorbing a ~$2B headwind from Fed rate cuts. Faster easing or deposit-cost competition would pressure the firm's single largest revenue line, and the Q2 2026 earnings beat leaned heavily on cyclical markets revenue instead.
▸
Regulatory & political exposure. As the largest G-SIB, JPMorgan carries the highest capital surcharge (4.5%). Basel III endgame re-proposals, CFPB fee actions, and capital-one-day/political scrutiny of "too big to fail" cut both ways — current deregulatory momentum could reverse after the 2028 cycle.
▸
Fintech & stablecoin disruption. Payments and deposits — the profit core — face encroachment from stablecoins, private credit displacing bank lending, and fintechs (Stripe, Square, Robinhood, Coinbase). JPMorgan is responding (JPMD deposit token, blockchain settlement), but disintermediation of deposit economics is the structural bear case for all banks.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

The best-run bank in the world is compounding tangible book at ~10%/yr with a 23% ex-items ROTCE, #1 share in deposits, IB fees, and cards, and $25B+ of excess capital as regulators ease Basel endgame. AWM's $5.1T AUM and the capital-markets upcycle add fee growth on top of a ~$95B NII base, while the Apple Card deal and $19.8B of tech spend extend the lead. At 14.9x forward earnings — a ~30% discount to the S&P 500 for double the index's ROE quality — the premium to peers remains earned.

▼ Bear Case

You're paying an all-time-high ~2.7x tangible book — roughly double the historical big-bank average — right as the earnings mix tilts toward cyclical markets revenue, NII goes flat on Fed cuts, and card charge-offs normalize upward. The average analyst target ($373.86) implies under 4% upside, Dimon's succession clock is now visibly running, and any 1995-style multiple reversion toward 1.8–2.0x tangible book means a 25%+ de-rating even if earnings hold.

1.
Valuation + succession convergence. A record multiple resting partly on a 70-year-old CEO's premium; a messy transition or abrupt exit announcement is the single most identifiable de-rating trigger.
2.
Macro/credit turn. A genuine recession would hit all three engines at once — card losses above the ~3.6% charge-off run-rate, IB fee collapse, and reserve builds — as 2022's −35% drawdown demonstrated.
3.
NII disappointment. Faster-than-guided Fed easing or deposit repricing pressure versus the ~$95B ex-Markets guide; consensus 2027 estimates embed NII re-acceleration that is not guaranteed.
  Industry Commentary — Money-Center Banks, August 2026
The NII Plateau: Rate Tailwind Is Over
ONGOING HEADWIND
The 2022–2024 windfall from repricing assets faster than deposits has run its course. JPMorgan guides 2026 NII ex-Markets to ~$95B — flat YoY with a ~$2B embedded rate headwind — and the story is similar at BAC ($16.2B quarterly NII, +9% and decelerating) and WFC. With the Fed easing, deposit betas catching up, and loan growth in the mid-single digits, sector revenue growth now depends on fees, not spread. Banks with diversified fee engines (JPM, MS) are decoupling from spread-dependent peers; watch the 2s/10s curve — renewed steepening is the one path back to NII upside.
Capital-Markets Upcycle + Regulatory Thaw
EMERGING TAILWIND
After a three-year drought, the deal machine is back: JPM IB fees +30% and markets +35% in Q2 2026, BAC IB fees +50%, and an IPO window wide open. Simultaneously, the Basel III endgame re-proposal has been softened dramatically from the original ~19% RWA inflation, and stress-capital buffers are trending down. For the big five, that is a double windfall — more fee revenue and more distributable capital. JPM alone repurchased $6.2B in Q2 2026 and holds CET1 of 14.1% against a ~12.3% requirement. The cycle risk: IB fee pools are the most volatile revenue line in finance, and 2026 comparisons get hard in 2027.
Credit Normalization & Disintermediation — Bull vs. Bear Frame
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The cycle's central question: card charge-offs are back to ~3.6% (pre-pandemic norms), reserve builds have resumed ($149M at JPM in Q2), and private credit plus stablecoins are probing the banks' lending and deposit franchises from outside the regulatory perimeter. Bull frame: normalization is not deterioration — consumer balance sheets are solid, JPM prices for ~4% card losses, and scale banks convert credit stress into share gains (First Republic proved it). Deregulation plus a steeper curve could extend the ROTCE super-cycle into 2027–28. Bear frame: at 2.7x tangible book, JPM is priced for the bull frame; if unemployment rises past ~5%, charge-offs overshoot, IB fees stall, and the sector re-rates — with the most premium-priced name having the most multiple to lose.
