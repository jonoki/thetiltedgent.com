01 Company Overview

Keyence makes the eyes and the measuring tape of the modern factory: photoelectric, fiber-optic, laser and proximity sensors that tell a machine where a part is; machine-vision cameras that inspect it; measuring instruments, microscopes and barcode readers; PLCs; and, most recently, 3D printers built for production floors. Its annual report puts the customer base at around 400,000 customers in some 110 countries, with no single customer above 10% of sales. In the fiscal year ended March 20, 2026, net sales rose 10.4% to ¥1,169.3B, operating income rose 8.4% to ¥595.8B — an operating margin of 51.0% — and net income rose 11.7% to ¥445.2B, or EPS of ¥1,835.63. The new year has started faster. The first quarter (March 21 – June 20, 2026) brought sales +32.8% and operating income +44.7%; the second quarter, released on October 29 after the Tokyo close, went further. For the six months to September 20, 2026, sales rose 34.9% to ¥735.4B, operating income 50.0% to ¥408.3B — a 55.5% operating margin, 56.9% in the second quarter alone — and net income 49.4% to ¥298.7B (EPS ¥1,231.53). Keyence gives no sales or profit forecast; its only guidance is the dividend, and on October 29 it raised it: an interim dividend of ¥325 a share (the forecast had been ¥275) and a year-end forecast of ¥325, so ¥650 for the year to March 2027 against ¥550 the year before, all on the share count before the 5-for-1 stock split that takes effect on November 21.

More than half of every yen of sales ends up as operating profit, at a company that does not own most of its production — its own risk disclosures describe products made at "cooperating factories" under a fabless system — and that sells through its own direct sales force. That margin is more than double each of the three listed automation peers compared in section 04. The surprise is the five-year record: from the October 2021 month-end close of ¥68,510 to the November 9, 2026 close of ¥71,580, Keyence gained just 4.5% while the Nikkei 225 gained 103.4%. All of that gain, and more, came in 2026: the stock was down 17.3% from October 2021 to December 2025, then rose 26.3% to November 9, with its two biggest daily gains of the year on the sessions after the April and July results. It has fallen 10.8% since the previous edition's October 2 close of ¥80,270 and is 18.6% below its August 6 high, and the Nikkei 225 fell 6.0% on November 6 alone. The question this report turns on: is the acceleration the start of a new factory-investment upturn, or a peak flattered by a weak yen? The company says currency added roughly ¥26.1B to sales and ¥18.9B to operating income in the June quarter alone.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

54% operating margin Fabless production Direct sales force ~400,000 customers, ~110 countries No customer >10% of sales Steady new-product cadence ¥3.2T cash & securities, no debt

The moat shows up in one number before any other: an 84.6% gross margin over the last twelve months on products that are boxes of optics and electronics. Customers are paying for what the sensor does on their line — fewer defects, faster changeovers, less downtime — not for its parts. Three habits keep it that way. Production is mostly outsourced to "cooperating factories" under Keyence's own quality control, so capital goes into design and selling rather than plants (capital spending was ¥28.4B last year against ¥430.7B of operating cash flow). It sells through its own direct sales force, which the annual report names as a growth priority, keeping the company close to the engineers who specify its products. And the product list keeps turning over: the 2026 annual report highlights a new PLC, an all-in-one fluorescence microscope, 3D printers for production sites, linear cylinder sensors and smart valves. With some 400,000 customers and none above 10% of sales, no single buyer has the leverage to push prices down. The weakness is the mirror image: a business this concentrated on factory capital spending rises and falls with it, and a share price that already assumes excellence has little room for an ordinary year.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The price still assumes the best. At ¥71,580 the stock trades at 31.9× trailing earnings and 4.7× book value, against 26.1× for the average of three listed peers. The five-year chart shows what a high starting price can cost: the stock fell 17.3% from October 2021 to December 2025 while the Nikkei 225 rose 74.2%.
02
The yen flatters the latest numbers. About ¥18.9B of Q1's ¥57.8B operating-income increase came from currency, by the company's estimate. Keyence's own sensitivity table says each ¥1 move in the dollar shifts annual operating income by about ¥1.1B, each ¥1 in the euro by about ¥0.5B, and each ¥0.1 in the yuan by about ¥1.0B. A stronger yen would turn this tailwind around.
03
It depends on factory spending. Every sale depends on manufacturers deciding to automate, inspect or expand. When that slows, so does Keyence: Japan grew just 0.3% in the quarter to December 20, 2025 and Europe 0.2% in the quarter to September 20, 2025. Asia's growth of 33.9% in local currency in the June 2026 quarter is the kind of number that reverses when factory spending turns down.
04
A balance sheet that is mostly cash and securities. Cash, deposits and securities totalled ¥3.21T at September 20, 2026 (¥698.5B of cash and deposits, ¥803.6B of securities held as current assets, ¥1,691.8B of long-term investment securities and ¥20.4B of long-term time deposits) — about 18.5% of the market value and 81% of total assets of ¥3.96T, of which 93.7% is funded by equity. Keeping that much capital idle drags return on equity down to 13.5% despite 50%-plus operating margins. The dividend forecast has gone from ¥350 two years ago to ¥550 and now ¥650 a share; buybacks have been negligible (treasury purchases of ¥15M last year).

Notable One-Off Items

Two items below the operating line move the profit figures. First, currency: the year to March 2026 booked a foreign-exchange gain of ¥16.3B against a ¥4.2B loss the year before, which is why pre-tax income grew 13.3% while operating income grew 8.4%; the six months to September 2026 swung the other way, to a ¥2.0B foreign-exchange loss from a ¥1.6B gain a year earlier. Second, interest and dividend income on the cash pile: ¥14.0B for the six months (¥7.1B a year earlier), against ¥16.4B for the whole of the year to March 2026. Neither is part of the core business, and both can reverse. At the operating level there were no unusual items: Keyence's income statements show no restructuring or impairment lines in either period.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of ¥68,510 to the November 9, 2026 close of ¥71,580, Keyence rose 4.5% on price. Dividends of ¥1,925 a share went ex over the same window — about another 2.8% of the starting price, before any reinvestment. That is a thin return for a business with 50%-plus operating margins, and the reason is the starting point: October 2021 sat just below the December 2021 high. The stock then spent four years going sideways, and every month-end above ¥70,000 (late 2021, early 2024) gave way. The break came in 2026, when two results releases in April and July took the stock from ¥54,860 at the end of March to ¥83,660 in August, the highest month-end of the window. It has slipped back since: ¥80,480 at the end of September, ¥78,000 at the end of October and ¥71,580 on November 9.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 45.8 at the November 9 close, down from 76.2 at the August month-end — the highest reading in the five-year series — and well above the series low of 18.5 at the June 2022 trough. The 10-month moving average sits at about ¥74,800, 4.5% above the price and still rising: the stock has slipped under it after the summer's overbought heat.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Keyence just posted first-half sales growth of 34.9% and operating income growth of 50.0%, with September-quarter sales up about 36.8%, faster than the June quarter's 32.8%. The margin structure — 86% gross, 56.9% operating in the September quarter — is unmatched among listed automation peers, the balance sheet holds about ¥3.2T of cash and securities with no debt, and the dividend forecast went from ¥550 to ¥650 a share. President Tetsuya Nakano's June 2026 letter to shareholders expects "growing demand driven by ongoing technological innovation as well as increasing needs for automation, quality improvement, and energy efficiency." Fourteen of sixteen analysts rate it a buy, with an average target of ¥100,406 (+40.3%). If the overseas push keeps growth in the high teens, today's 31.9× P/E falls quickly as profits grow.

Bear Case

The stock spent 2021–2025 proving that quality does not protect a stock bought at a peak price: +4.5% in five years against a Nikkei up 103.4%. Today it trades at 31.9× trailing and 29.1× forward earnings (consensus EPS for the year to March 2027), with a 0.9% dividend yield and a 2.3% free-cash-flow yield, after three exceptional quarters. Part of the growth is currency — ¥18.9B of the June quarter's operating profit by the company's own estimate — and the strongest region, Asia, is the most exposed to swings in factory spending. Nakano's own letter flags the backdrop: "the global economy requires continued close monitoring of policy trends in various countries, in addition to concerns over a potential economic downturn driven by rising geopolitical risks." A strong yen, a turn in electronics capital spending or simply a return to 10% growth would leave the price with nothing to stand on; the shares fell 10.8% between October 2 and November 9 despite the results. Meanwhile about ¥3.2T of cash and securities sits idle, buybacks are negligible, and ROE is held to 13.5%.

Top 3 Risks

01
A high price for the earnings. At 31.9× trailing earnings and 4.7× book, the stock prices in sustained high growth. The chart shows the cost of paying a high price at the start: −17.3% from October 2021 to December 2025.
02
A stronger yen. The dollar averaged ¥159 in the June quarter against ¥146 a year earlier. Reverse that and, on the company's sensitivity of about ¥1.1B of annual operating income per yen, the dollar alone would take roughly ¥14B off operating income — before the euro and yuan.
03
The factory-spending cycle. Keyence has no recurring software or service base to cushion a downturn in factory investment. The quarters to September 20 and December 20, 2025 showed how quickly Japan and Europe can slow to near zero; Asia's June 2026 quarter, +33.9% in local currency, is the most exposed to a reversal.
Industry Commentary
Every region sped up in the June quarter
EMERGING TAILWIND

Keyence's local-currency growth table is a clean read on global factory spending because the company sells to so many manufacturers in so many industries. Over the five quarters to June 2026, overseas growth went 11.4% → 10.4% → 13.3% → 16.4% → 24.0%, and Japan went 5.2% → 3.8% → 0.3% → 8.6% → 19.9%. The annual report describes "continued capital investment, particularly in the manufacturing sector," and points to demand from "automation, quality improvement, and energy efficiency." When a supplier to some 400,000 manufacturers sees every region speed up together, it is a signal about the cycle, not only about Keyence. The company's own account of the June quarter, in the Japanese text of its July 28, 2026 earnings release, says the same thing region by region: manufacturing-led capital spending continued; the Americas stayed firm across a wide range of industries; Asia kept growing, led by the semiconductor and electronics industries; Europe showed signs of recovery; and capital spending in Japan was on a recovering trend.

The weak yen — tailwind with an expiry date
ONGOING HEADWIND RISK

With 70% of sales overseas, Keyence gains when the yen weakens, and its own sensitivity table puts numbers on it. In the June 2026 quarter the yen averaged 159 to the dollar (146 a year earlier), 185 to the euro (162) and 23.2 to the yuan (20.1). The company puts the effect at roughly ¥26.1B of sales and ¥18.9B of operating income for that quarter. That is real cash in yen, but it is not demand. Over the full year to March 2026 currency barely mattered (12.9% local-currency overseas growth against 13.5% reported); in the June quarter it was 15 points of the overseas growth rate.

Quality at a price — how Keyence compares
VALUATION

Against three listed automation peers, Keyence's operating margin (53.8% over the last twelve months) is more than double Fanuc's (23.2%), SMC's (24.1%) and Cognex's (25.0%), and its gross margin (84.6%) leads all three. Its trailing P/E of 31.9 sits above Fanuc (25.0) and SMC (18.2) and below Cognex (35.2). The bull framing: a business that converts more than half of sales into operating profit with no debt deserves a large premium, and growth this fast shrinks it within a year or two. The bear framing: the stock returned 4.5% over five years against 103.4% for the Nikkei 225 while running the same business model — the margins were never the problem; the price paid was.
