01 Company Overview

Kraft Heinz makes and sells packaged food under a portfolio of legacy American and international brands — Heinz ketchup, Kraft cheese and macaroni, Oscar Mayer, Philadelphia, Capri Sun, Lunchables, Ore-Ida, Jell-O, Velveeta, Kool-Aid — through grocery retail, foodservice and emerging-market channels. FY2025 revenue was $24.94B, down 3.5% from $25.85B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through the September 26, 2026 quarter is $24.78B, down 1.5%. The company ranks #325 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation at $29.13B — a number worth sitting with, because in 2017 this company launched a $143B takeover bid for Unilever. It is now worth about a fifth of that bid.

This is the classic "cheap or broken?" name in US large-cap staples, and it is the epilogue to the 3G Capital experiment — the thesis that a food conglomerate could be run permanently on zero-based budgeting (every cost justified from scratch each year), and that brand equity was a fixed asset you could harvest rather than something you had to keep feeding. The stock trades at 0.81× book value (its net worth on its own accounts), yields 6.50%, and generated $3.53B of free cash flow in the last twelve months. It also lost $3.47B under standard accounting rules (GAAP) over the same twelve months, because it wrote off $7.35B of goodwill and intangibles in the June 2026 quarter alone. The central question on this page is whether a 6.5% yield backed by real cash flow compensates you for owning a portfolio whose accounting value the company itself keeps marking down, in categories whose volumes keep shrinking. The third quarter beat a low bar and lifted the profit forecast, but the break-up remains paused; what the new CEO has done instead is start selling pieces, beginning with North American coffee.

Kraft Heinz announced in September 2025 a plan to separate into two companies, provisionally "Global Taste Elevation Co." (sauces, spreads, condiments, international) and "North American Grocery Co." On February 11, 2026, alongside Q4 2025 results, new CEO Steve Cahillane paused the separation and redirected the company toward an investment programme in marketing, sales, R&D and price, set at $600M then and $700M now. The Q3 2026 10-Q still lists "the pause of work related to the separation" as a risk, and separation costs still ran at $24M in the quarter and $90M for the nine months. No separation has completed, no new company names have been finalised and no new tickers exist. The February guidance that came with the pause was organic net sales down 1.5% to 3.5% and adjusted EPS of $1.98–$2.10. August improved that to organic −0.5% to −2.0% and $2.03–$2.09; November moved it to organic −0.5% to −1.25% and $2.07–$2.13.

How to read it: a split is the usual way to stop a shrinking business weighing on a growing one's share price. Pausing it says management wants the operations fixed first, a defensible view that also removes the most obvious near-term reason for the shares to be valued more highly. Selling coffee for a $650M enterprise value while keeping a 30% stake is the small version of the same idea, and Cahillane presents it as a step toward "a more growth-oriented portfolio."
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
HEINZ CONDIMENT FRANCHISE ESTABLISHED SHELF SPACE HEINZ PACKETS IN RESTAURANTS MANUFACTURING SCALE WIDE DISTRIBUTION HEINZ IN EMERGING MARKETS MATURE, CASH-RICH BUSINESS

The real moat here is narrower than the brand list suggests, and it is worth separating the two halves of the portfolio. Heinz — specifically ketchup and the wider condiments and sauces franchise — is a genuine brand asset: shoppers think of it first, it is a cheap purchase people do not comparison-shop hard, and in foodservice it comes close to a switching cost, because a restaurant that puts Heinz on the table is signalling something to its customers and cannot swap it silently. That is why the proposed break-up put condiments in the "Taste Elevation" bucket and why that bucket was expected to be valued more highly. The Kraft half is different. Processed cheese, cold cuts, boxed dinners and lunch kits are categories where store brands have closed most of the quality gap, where the retailer decides how far each brand's price sits above its own store brand, and where the consumer trend — less processed, higher protein, fewer additives, smaller portions — runs directly against the product. The balance sheet has been telling this story for years: goodwill has fallen from $31.30B at FY2021 to $19.65B at the September 2026 quarter, and other intangibles from $43.54B to $32.33B. That is $22.9B less brand and goodwill value on the books in under five years, of which $16.7B is the impairments booked in the first nine months of 2025 and of 2026 alone. Goodwill plus intangibles still stand at $51.98B against total assets of $72.23B, which is 72% of the balance sheet and 1.78× the entire $29.13B market capitalisation. A share price below book value suggests investors already expect further write-downs.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Volume is still falling, and price has stopped helping. In Q3 2026 organic net sales were price flat, volume and mix −1.1 points, and in North America price −0.8, volume and mix −2.4. Management says it will need to raise prices next year; "I do feel like '27 will require price," Cahillane said on the call, with inflation still rising. Raising prices into falling volumes widens the gap to store brands, the same trade that has weakened the franchise for years.
▸
The write-downs keep coming. The $15.4B write-down of February 2019 (Kraft and Oscar Mayer brand values; Bloomberg, February 21, 2019) was supposed to be the reset. It was not. Kraft Heinz took a $9.3B non-cash impairment in Q2 2025, driving a quarterly loss of more than $7.8B and an FY2025 net loss of $5.85B, then a further $7.35B in Q2 2026 ($2.44B goodwill plus $4.91B intangibles) driving a $5.46B quarterly loss, and another $72M in Q3. Impairments use no cash and look backward, but a company that keeps reducing its own estimate of its brands' future cash flows is telling you those cash flows keep coming in below plan.
▸
The largest shareholder may be leaving. About 325M of Berkshire Hathaway's shares (27.5% of the company) were registered for potential resale in January 2026. Until that block is dealt with, every rally faces a known possible seller, and an exit would be a public verdict on the merger Berkshire helped put together in 2015.
▸
Small sales declines become large profit declines. Q3 2026 North American sales fell 3.4% while segment adjusted operating income fell 10.5%. Full-year 2026 guidance is for adjusted operating income, before currency moves, down 16% to 18% on organic sales down only 0.5% to 1.25%. Heavy fixed costs plus reinvestment mean small revenue declines produce large profit declines, and the reinvestment is spending management could cut, which would flatter next year's earnings while doing nothing for the brands. Jefferies also noted that Q3 profit was helped by the timing of retailer-promotion and marketing spending, which reverses in Q4.

Notable One-Off Items. This company's GAAP income statement is currently unusable without adjustment, and saying so is not an apology for management. Three items dominate. (1) The Q2 2026 impairment of $7,352M — $2,441M goodwill and $4,911M other intangibles — turned a quarter with $6,262M of sales and a 34.1% adjusted gross margin into a $5,460M net loss and $(4.60) diluted EPS, against $0.56 of adjusted EPS. (2) The Q2 2025 impairment of $9.3B, which produced a quarterly loss of more than $7.8B and drove the FY2025 net loss of $5,846M. (3) The February 2019 write-down of $15.4B against the Kraft and Oscar Mayer brands (Bloomberg, February 21, 2019). None of these consumed cash. Free cash flow rose each year from $1,553M in FY2022 to $3,661M in FY2025 before slipping to $3,532M TTM. But cumulative non-cash write-offs of this size are the balance-sheet record of a merger thesis that did not work, and the remaining $51.98B of goodwill and intangibles is the size of the exposure that has not yet been marked. Q3 2026 itself was clean by comparison, with $72M of impairments.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOVEMBER 6, 2026

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the security has risen persistently and is conventionally called "overbought"; below 30 it has fallen persistently and is called "oversold". This is a six-month version, so it describes multi-month momentum, not day-trading signals — and in a stock that has spent five years in a downtrend, sub-30 readings have marked pauses in the decline rather than bottoms.

The five-year picture is a rise, a plateau and a long slide. From the November 2021 close of $33.61 to $24.61 on November 6, 2026, the price fell 26.8%. Add the $7.60 of dividends paid over the period (19 quarterly payments of $0.40 by ex-date, not reinvested) and the total return is about −4%. Over the same dates SPY rose 61.4% and QQQ 66.6% on price (Yahoo Finance closes): a very large shortfall against the market, with the dividend doing nearly all the work of keeping holders close to whole.

The shape matters more than the total. The stock actually rose through the 2022 inflation shock, peaking at $42.63 in April 2022, because packaged food was the classic way to hide from inflation: pass through higher costs, protect margins, get paid for being defensive. Month-end closes then held between roughly $31 and $41 until April 2024 ($38.61). The break came after that, and it was not a macro break. From $35.11 in September 2024 the stock fell to $25.82 by June 2025, a 26% decline in nine months with no recession. Read this as investors cutting what they would pay for processed food over the long term, with GLP-1 weight-loss drugs a commonly cited reason. It is the single most important move on this chart.

Relative performance. Against the S&P 500, the index it still belongs to, KHC has been a very poor holding over five years. The more useful comparison is the staples peer group, where KHC's problem is visibly its own. Mondelez, with $39.90B of revenue, carries an $83.57B market cap and a 20.8× P/E: 2.1× revenue. Kraft Heinz, with $24.78B of revenue, carries $29.13B: 1.18× revenue. Campbell's trades at 17.7×. Conagra, at $7.28B on $11.24B of revenue, is at 0.65× revenue and shares the same problem in North American packaged food. Investors are not marking down packaged food uniformly; they are separating global snacking and condiments from North American centre-store grocery (the packaged food in a supermarket's middle aisles), and paying more for the former. That distinction is exactly what the September 2025 split was designed to capture, and exactly why pausing it left the stock stuck.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 0.81× book and about 12× guided adjusted earnings for a business that threw off $3.53B of free cash flow in the last twelve months, at 120% conversion for the nine months. The 6.50% dividend is covered 1.9× by cash. Q3 adjusted EPS beat the Nasdaq consensus by 23%, guidance rose in both August and November, Emerging Markets grew 13.8%, debt is down $3.2B in nine months, and the coffee sale shows management will sell what does not fit. Once the Berkshire block is dealt with, the best-known potential seller is gone. If organic sales merely stabilise near zero, a share price around 15 times earnings of $2.10, plus a 6% yield, is a perfectly respectable return from here, and a restarted separation would be a free extra on top.

Bear Case

North American volume and mix fell 2.4 points in Q3 2026 and price fell 0.8, so the core business is losing on both. North American sales −3.4% produced adjusted operating income −10.5%, and FY2026 guidance is for constant-currency adjusted operating income down 16–18%. Goodwill and intangibles have shrunk by $22.9B since FY2021 and still stand at $51.98B, or 1.78× market cap. The largest shareholder of ten years has had its 27.5% registered for possible sale, 11.4% of the float is short, only one post-print call in the table is a Buy, free cash flow fell 5.2% in nine months, and inflation is now rising into a year management says will need price increases. A 6.5% yield on a business whose operating income is guided down 16–18% this year is not a value stock; it is a slow liquidation.

Top 3 Risks

01
Dividend sustainability, on earnings rather than cash. The $1.60 rate costs about $1.89B a year and is comfortably covered by $3.53B of free cash flow; that part is real. But it consumes 75–77% of guided adjusted EPS of $2.07–$2.13, and adjusted operating income is guided down 16–18%. Two more years at that rate would take adjusted EPS to about $1.45, below the dividend, though consensus has FY2027 at $2.06. The board would then choose between the dividend, the reinvestment programme and continued debt reduction. Income holders should treat the yield as adequate compensation, not as a guarantee.
02
Further impairment of the remaining $51.98B. Goodwill and intangibles are 72% of total assets and 1.78× market capitalisation. Impairment tests key off forecast brand cash flows, and those forecasts have been cut repeatedly, most heavily in 2019, 2025 and 2026. Each write-down reduces book equity (already down from $49.45B at FY2021 to $36.09B), which worsens the reported leverage ratios and can pressure the credit rating even though no cash moves. The 0.50 debt-to-equity that looks reassuring today is the most fragile number in Section 04.
03
Structural demand decline in centre-store processed food. This is the risk that makes the other two matter. GLP-1 adoption, protein-forward eating and ingredient-label scrutiny all reduce the calories consumed from exactly the categories Kraft Heinz leans on: boxed dinners, processed cheese, cold cuts, lunch kits, sweetened drinks. Volume and mix have been negative for multiple quarters. If this is a permanent change in what people eat rather than a temporary trade-down, no amount of marketing, cost cutting or restructuring fixes it, and the long-term value of the North American grocery portfolio is materially lower than any multiple currently in the price.
Industry Commentary
GLP-1 and the Long-Term Value of Processed Calories
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

GLP-1 receptor agonists (the class of injectable weight-loss and diabetes drugs including semaglutide) reduce appetite, and in doing so reduce the number of calories a user buys. The categories seen as most exposed include ones Kraft Heinz leans on: snacking, sweetened beverages, boxed convenience dinners, processed meat and cheese. The stock's fall from $35.11 in September 2024 to $25.82 in June 2025, 26% in nine months with no recession, can be read as investors already acting on this. What is unresolved is size: whether GLP-1s remove one or two percent of category volume permanently, or reset demand for centre-store grocery altogether. Kraft Heinz's Q3 2026 North American numbers show volume and mix at −2.4 points with price at −0.8, which fits either reading. GLP-1 did not come up on the Q3 2026 call. When the split was paused in February, Cahillane called most of the company's problems "fixable and within our control" (Reuters); on the Q3 call he said of Philadelphia that the category itself is under pressure.

Store Brands and the Shrinking Price Premium
ONGOING HEADWIND

Four years of packaged-food price increases have widened the gap between branded and retailer-owned products at exactly the moment when store-brand quality has become hard to distinguish in commodity categories such as shredded cheese, mayonnaise, frozen potatoes and cold cuts. The company's own numbers tell the story: in Q2 2026 it took +1.3 points of price and gave up 2.6 points of volume and mix; in Q3 price was flat and fell 0.8 points in North America. The $700M investment programme is aimed at reversing this, part marketing, part price, and it is plainly a sacrifice of margin to defend volume, which is why constant-currency adjusted operating income is guided down 16–18% on sales down only 0.5–1.25%. This is also why the 3G playbook has run out: you cannot cost-cut your way through a share loss caused by the price gap your cost-cutting funded.

The Break-Up Plan — Paused, Not Cancelled
ON HOLD

Food conglomerates have spent five years unbundling: Kellogg into Kellanova and WK Kellogg in 2023, a company run by the man now running Kraft Heinz, and Kraft Heinz's own September 2025 plan to separate "Global Taste Elevation Co." from "North American Grocery Co." The logic is visible in the peer valuations: Mondelez at 2.1× revenue and 20.8× earnings versus Kraft Heinz at 1.18× revenue. A condiments-and-international company could plausibly be valued nearer Mondelez, and the grocery company nearer Conagra. The November coffee sale is the first sign of what the pause means in practice: smaller disposals rather than one big split.

Bull vs bear, framed honestly: the bull owns a 6.5% yield covered 1.9× by free cash flow, at 0.81× book, in a business that has raised guidance twice this year and is starting to sell what does not fit, with a paused-not-cancelled break-up as a free extra and any sale by the 27.5% holder as a one-off rather than a verdict. The bear owns a North American business losing 2.4 points of volume and 0.8 of price, guiding operating income down 16–18%, carrying $51.98B of goodwill and intangibles against a $29.13B market cap, with thirteen holds and four sells against two buys. Both readings use the same numbers. The difference is entirely whether you believe the volume decline is temporary or permanent, and the next answers are the Q4 share numbers around the holidays and whether 2027's price increases stick.
