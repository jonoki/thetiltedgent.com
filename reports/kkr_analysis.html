01 Company Overview
KKR & Co. Inc. is one of the world's largest alternative asset managers, investing across private equity, real assets (infrastructure, energy and real estate), credit and liquid strategies, while also owning a life and annuity balance sheet (Global Atlantic) and a portfolio of consolidated operating companies. As of the quarter ended June 30, 2026 the firm managed $796 billion in total AUM, up 16% YoY, of which $638 billion was fee-paying AUM (+15%). Reported GAAP revenue for full-year 2025 was $25.65 billion (-2.9% versus 2024's $26.42 billion) — a figure driven largely by consolidation and mark-to-market accounting rather than by underlying fee generation.
Ranked the 235th most valuable listed company globally by market capitalization (CompaniesMarketCap, August 18, 2026) and 4th largest US-listed asset manager behind BlackRock, Blackstone and Brookfield Corporation, KKR sits inside a small oligopoly of scaled alternative managers that has captured a disproportionate share of institutional and, increasingly, individual capital. Its economics are best read through three non-GAAP measures the company itself emphasizes: fee-related earnings, total operating earnings and adjusted net income — all three of which set records in the second quarter of 2026.
Co-Chief Executive Officers
Joseph Y. Bae & Scott C. Nuttall
Verified co-CEOs. Both joined KKR in 1996, were named Co-Presidents/Co-COOs in 2017 and Co-CEOs in October 2021. Bae built KKR's Asia franchise; Nuttall built the credit, capital-markets and balance-sheet businesses. Founders Henry R. Kravis and George R. Roberts remain Co-Executive Chairmen.

KKR's moat is built on duration of capital rather than on any product. Roughly $334 billion — 42% of AUM — is perpetual capital (+16% YoY), meaning it has no contractual end date: Global Atlantic's policyholder liabilities, core private-equity and core infrastructure vehicles, listed and semi-liquid vehicles, and the balance sheet itself. Traditional drawdown funds add a further layer of stickiness with ten-year-plus lock-ups and $143 billion of uncalled commitments that will convert to fee-paying assets on KKR's schedule, not the market's. That structure means a bad quarter in markets does not produce redemptions the way it does for a mutual-fund complex. On top of it sits a genuine scale advantage: KKR raised $34.3 billion in Q2'26 alone and $133 billion over the trailing twelve months, surpassing its three-year, $300 billion fundraising target six months early at $305 billion raised since the start of 2024. Co-CEO Scott Nuttall described the industry as "increasingly K-shaped" — capital concentrating in a handful of scaled platforms while sub-scale managers struggle to raise at all. The owned insurer closes the loop: KKR originates credit assets, places them on Global Atlantic's balance sheet, and earns a spread plus a fee on capital it does not have to re-raise. Finally, the in-house capital-markets business monetizes syndication and financing on KKR's own deals, converting deal flow into fee income that a pure LP-funded manager cannot capture.
KEY VULNERABILITIES

Private-credit cycle exposure. Credit and liquid strategies are now KKR's single largest AUM bucket at $330.8B, and much of Global Atlantic's $164B of credit assets sits alongside it. Industry-wide fundraising has already cracked: global private-credit fundraising was $49.9B in Q1 2026 and direct-lending fundraising fell to $10.7B, the lowest quarterly figure in nearly three years. Over $620 billion of high-yield bonds and leveraged loans mature in 2026–2027. Spread compression, rising defaults or a marks-driven confidence shock would hit fee growth, spread income and carried interest simultaneously — the classic correlated-exposure problem for a manager that has diversified into adjacent forms of the same credit risk.

Realization dependence and a blocked exit window. Roughly $10.2 billion of gross unrealized carried interest sits on the balance sheet, but it converts to cash only through IPOs, sponsor-to-sponsor sales or strategic exits. Industry-wide there are approximately 29,000 sponsor-owned portfolio companies awaiting exits. Q3'26 monetization visibility disclosed on the July 30 call was only about $700 million. If the exit window stays narrow, performance fees stay deferred, distributable earnings stay depressed, and — more damaging — limited partners with no distributions have less capacity to fund the next flagship.

Retail channel fragility. K-Series has grown to $42B and is central to the growth story, but the wealth channel is semi-liquid by design. Peers have already been tested: retail assets are roughly 24% of Blackstone's AUM and about 40% of Blue Owl's, and record redemption requests in Q1 2026 came predominantly from high-net-worth investors. Oppenheimer flagged that "the recent wave of redemptions from retail funds has raised questions about the sustainability of the retail growth story." Nuttall has framed the volatility as a "healthy educational period," which is the optimistic reading of an untested distribution channel.

GAAP opacity and key-person concentration. KKR's reported financials consolidate funds and an insurance company, so GAAP revenue swung from $5.5B (2022) to $18.7B (2023) to $26.4B (2024) to $25.7B (2025) with no comparable change in the underlying business — an investor base that cannot model the company reliably will discount it. Separately, the firm is still culturally and commercially tied to a small group of individuals: two co-CEOs, two co-executive chairmen who are the founders, and a partnership whose deal-level relationships are not fully institutional.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Unrealized carried-interest marks. KKR carries roughly $10.2 billion of gross unrealized performance income that has not been realized in cash. Movements in this balance flow through GAAP results without any cash changing hands, and they reverse. Investors should treat any quarter's GAAP swing that traces to carry marks as noise; the cash event is the realization. Q2'26 produced $847.5 million of realized performance income — that is the number that matters.

Global Atlantic consolidation effects. Full consolidation of a life and annuity balance sheet added tens of billions of assets, liabilities and debt to KKR's GAAP statements and is the single biggest reason revenue, margins, ROE, ROIC and debt-to-equity are not comparable to a pure-play manager or to KKR's own pre-2024 history. Management disclosed that on a mark-to-market basis Insurance results would have exceeded $600M year-to-date against a ~$250M-per-quarter guided run-rate — a reminder that reported insurance earnings are an accounting convention, in both directions.

Equity-based compensation. KKR excludes equity-based comp from adjusted net income. This is standard for the peer group, but it is a real economic cost borne by shareholders through dilution; investors comparing KKR's ANI multiple to a conventional company's P/E should mentally haircut it.

M&A. The Arctos Partners acquisition added approximately $20 billion of AUM, concentrated in private equity, and therefore inflates the Q2'26 private-equity AUM growth rate of +19% relative to organic growth. Adjust for it when extrapolating.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hot and often pauses; below 30 it has been beaten up and often bounces. It says nothing about whether the business is good.

Five-year trajectory. KKR closed September 2021 at $60.88 and August 19, 2026 at $110.46 — a price return of +81.4% over five years. Over the identical window the S&P 500 (via SPY) went from $429.14 to $769.06, a price return of +79.2%. In other words, after one of the most spectacular round trips in the large-cap financials complex, KKR has delivered a market return with roughly 1.8x the market's volatility. The path was anything but linear: a 2022 credit-and-rate drawdown to $43.00 (September 2022, -46% from the October 2021 close), a violent 2023–2024 re-rating that took the stock to an all-time monthly closing high of $167.07 in January 2025 (a +289% advance from the 2022 low), and then a brutal 13-month de-rating to $87.68 in February 2026, -47.5% from the peak. The recovery since has been partial: +26% off that trough, but still -33.9% below the January 2025 high and -27.4% below the 52-week high of $152.10.

What actually changed. Almost nothing in the fundamentals explains the 2025–26 collapse. FRE per share went from $4.13 (FY2025) to $4.68 on an LTM basis; AUM went from $743.9B at December 2025 to $796B at June 2026; the firm beat a three-year fundraising target six months early. What changed was the multiple the market is willing to pay. That is why co-CEO Scott Nuttall told investors that "external perception is so disconnected from… how it feels inside the firm."

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The operating business is compounding faster than at any point in its history while the multiple has halved. LTM FRE of $4.235B (+19%), TOE of $5.544B (+19%) and ANI of $5.022B (+13%) are all records; AUM is $796B (+16%), 42% of it perpetual; $143B of dry powder converts to fees on KKR's schedule; and the $300B three-year fundraising target was beaten six months early. At $110.46 the stock trades at ~16.5x forward earnings with a 0.79 PEG — cheaper than Blackstone and Ares on forward multiples despite comparable growth. Three optionalities are unpriced: Strategic Holdings scaling toward $1.1B by 2030, K-Series compounding from a small $42B base, and Global Atlantic's mark-to-market economics running well ahead of guided GAAP earnings.

Bear Case

KKR has concentrated its growth in the one asset class the market has decided to distrust. Credit is now the largest AUM bucket at $330.8B, credit fees have tripled, and Global Atlantic holds $164B more of it — so a private-credit downcycle hits fees, spread income and carry simultaneously. The evidence is already visible: direct-lending fundraising at a near-three-year low, $620B+ of leveraged maturities in 2026–27, record retail redemptions across the peer group. Meanwhile $10.2B of unrealized carry cannot convert to cash while the exit window stays shut, GAAP earnings are so noisy that the marginal buyer cannot underwrite them, and a 1.79 beta guarantees the stock will lead any credit-driven drawdown. Five years of ownership has delivered exactly market returns for far more than market risk.

Private-credit deterioration is a triple-count exposure. Unlike a pure-play manager, KKR earns fees on credit AUM, spread income on Global Atlantic's $164B credit book, and carry on credit funds. A rise in defaults or a spread-widening event does not diversify across those three lines — it correlates them. Watch direct-lending fundraising (already at a near-three-year low of $10.7B in Q1'26) and the $620B+ 2026–27 maturity wall.

The realization window may stay shut longer than the balance sheet can wait. $10.2B of gross unrealized carry is an asset only if it converts. Q3'26 monetization visibility is just ~$700M against ~29,000 sponsor-owned companies industry-wide queued for exit. Prolonged illiquidity suppresses distributable earnings, starves limited partners of the distributions that fund the next flagship, and keeps the performance-fee multiple compressed.
03
The retail/wealth growth story is unproven under stress. K-Series is up ~70% YoY to $42B and is a core plank of the growth narrative, but semi-liquid vehicles have never been tested through a genuine credit cycle. Peers with larger retail books have already seen record redemption requests. If the channel proves procyclical — inflows in calm markets, gates in stressed ones — a meaningful share of KKR's projected FRE growth does not arrive.
  Industry Commentary
The Migration of Capital from Public to Private Markets
EMERGING TAILWIND
This is the structural thesis underneath every alternative manager, and it has not broken. Preqin projects global alternatives AUM reaching $32 trillion by 2030, with infrastructure alone approaching $3 trillion. The drivers are durable: fewer companies choosing to be public, pension and sovereign funds needing returns that public beta cannot deliver, banks retreating from balance-sheet lending under capital rules, and — newest and most important — the opening of the individual-investor channel through semi-liquid vehicles. KKR's own numbers show the pull-through: $133B raised in twelve months, $305B since the start of 2024 against a $300B three-year goal, and K-Series at $42B. The caution is that a structural tailwind and a good entry price are different things; the same thesis was used to justify 40x FRE in January 2025.
Private Credit: Fastest-Growing Asset Class, Loudest Alarm Bell
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Private credit is forecast to more than double to $4.5 trillion by 2030, with semi-liquid and evergreen structures accounting for roughly $1.4 trillion of that. But the near-term data has turned: global private-credit fundraising was $49.9B in Q1 2026 and direct lending collapsed to $10.7B, the weakest quarter in nearly three years. Regulators are increasing scrutiny of lending exposure and systemic risk, and more than $620 billion of high-yield bonds and leveraged loans mature in 2026–2027 into a higher-rate environment. For KKR specifically this is not a peripheral asset class: credit and liquid strategies are the largest AUM bucket at $330.8B, total credit AUM has gone from ~$80B to ~$300B, and Global Atlantic holds $164B of credit assets. This is the single dynamic most likely to determine the next 24 months of the share price.
The Exit Drought and the Two Multiples — Bull vs Bear
ONGOING HEADWIND
The market does not value an alternative manager with one multiple; it values two earnings streams separately. Fee-related earnings — recurring, contracted, high-margin (70% at KKR, above 65% for ten straight quarters) — are treated like a subscription business and historically command a premium multiple. Performance fees and carried interest — lumpy, market-dependent, and currently trapped as $10.2B of unrealized marks — are capitalized at a fraction of that. The 2025–26 de-rating was essentially the market cutting the multiple on the second bucket toward zero while trimming the first. At $110.46 KKR trades at roughly 23.6x LTM FRE per share ($4.68), 18.0x LTM TOE per share ($6.13) and 19.9x LTM ANI per share ($5.55) — against a January 2025 peak that priced 40.5x FY2025 FRE per share, an asset-management industry weighted-average P/E of 31.6x and an S&P 500 at 28.0x. Bull implication: you are buying a 19%-growing recurring earnings stream at a discount to the index, with the carry optionality free. Bear implication: if the exit window stays shut, that carry never converts, FRE growth decelerates against tough 2026 fundraising comps, and 23.6x FRE turns out to be the fair multiple for a cyclical rather than a compounder. Roughly 29,000 sponsor-owned companies are waiting to be sold; the reopening of that queue is the event both cases hinge on.
