01 Company Overview

KLA Corporation is the dominant supplier of process control and yield management equipment to the semiconductor industry — the inspection and metrology (measurement) tools that find defects and verify dimensions at every critical step of chip manufacturing. It is not a "chip maker" but the referee of chip making: roughly 56% of every dollar spent on semiconductor process control worldwide goes to KLA, a share roughly four to five times its nearest competitor and one of the most concentrated positions in capital equipment.

Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) revenue was $13.58B, up 11.7% YoY from $12.16B, with GAAP net income of $4.83B (+18.9%) and a 35.6% net margin. The fourth quarter set a company record at $3.66B (+15.2% YoY). At a ~$223B market capitalization KLA is still up roughly 85% over twelve months — but it is now 44% below the June 2026 peak, having given back well over $150B of market value since June as investors de-rated the whole AI/advanced-packaging capital expenditure ("capex") equipment complex. All share figures reflect the 10-for-1 stock split completed in 2026; share counts of ~1.31 billion and per-share values are split-adjusted throughout. The split was a 10-for-1 forward split effective June 12, 2026; the header price, the 52-week range and the Section 07 series are all on the same post-split basis.

Contracted maintenance, spares, upgrades and productivity services on an installed base of roughly 30,000+ systems, the large majority still in active production because process-control tools stay in fabs for decades and get re-purposed to trailing-edge nodes rather than scrapped. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
~56% PROCESS-CONTROL SHARE 30,000+ INSTALLED SYSTEMS YIELD-DATA LOCK-IN OPTICAL + E-BEAM BREADTH RETICLE INSPECTION NEAR-MONOPOLY 61% GROSS MARGIN ~$3B RECURRING SERVICE ANNUITY NODE-QUALIFICATION REFERENCE TOOL

KLA's moat is best understood as a reference-standard problem rather than a technology lead. When a foundry qualifies a new node, the yield-learning data set — the defect signatures, the overlay fingerprints, the process-window maps — is generated on KLA tools and encoded in KLA's analytics. Switching inspection vendors mid-node means re-baselining that entire data set, at a moment when every day of yield-ramp delay costs hundreds of millions of dollars. The economics are brutally asymmetric: process control is only ~13% of WFE spending, so a customer trying to save 20% on inspection risks the other 87% of its capital producing scrap. That asymmetry is why KLA has held a 50%+ share for two decades, why it sustains a gross margin north of 61% while the equipment peer group averages ~46%, and why its share has actually risen as nodes got harder. Reinforcing it: KLA is the only vendor with credible depth across broadband-plasma optical inspection, laser scattering, e-beam review and multiple metrology modalities, so it can sell an integrated inspection strategy rather than a point tool. And each installed system seeds a decade-plus service annuity that competitors cannot dislodge without displacing the hardware first.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. China concentration meeting an active export-control regime. The FY2026 10-K reports China at 30% of total revenue in fiscal 2026 (~$4.07B) — still the single largest geography, ahead of Taiwan at ~26% (est.) — but that is a reported decline from 33% in FY2025 and 43% in FY2024, replacing the ~31% trailing estimate in the previous edition. International revenue was ~87% of the total. A large slice of that is trailing-edge domestic capacity that Washington has repeatedly restricted, and the September-2025 expansion of Entity List coverage to majority-owned affiliates widened the licensing surface. KLA also faces the mirror risk: Beijing's push to indigenise inspection and metrology, where domestic vendors like SMEE-adjacent startups and Skyverse are being funded specifically to attack KLA's position. A policy shock here is the fastest way to take several hundred basis points off growth.

2. Cyclicality dressed up as secular growth. Management raised the 2026 WFE outlook to the low-$150B range (mid-20s% growth) and pointed to ~$190B for 2027. Those are excellent numbers — and they are also the definition of a cycle peak forming. KLA's revenue fell only ~7% peak-to-trough in the 2023–24 downturn, but the stock fell far more. At 46.7x trailing the shares still discount continuation rather than normalisation — though the August–September de-rating has taken roughly ten turns of trailing P/E out of that assumption. Any 2027 WFE revision toward flat would compress both the earnings estimate and the multiple simultaneously.

3. Encroachment at the edges of the franchise. Onto Innovation has been taking share in advanced-packaging and panel-level metrology at ~25% growth, ASML is pushing HMI e-beam and YieldStar deeper into in-line metrology where it can bundle with lithography, and Applied Materials continues to invest in eBeam and its cSAM/SEMVision lines. None of these threatens the reticle or broadband-plasma core, but advanced packaging — precisely the fastest-growing part of KLA's TAM at ~$1.1B and +70% — is the least defended part of the franchise, with lower barriers and more credible challengers.

4. Input-cost inflation from the memory shortage it benefits from. KLA's own tools consume DRAM, NAND and specialty components, and management flagged that memory procurement costs reduced gross margin by roughly 100 basis points, with the pressure potentially persisting into 2027. This is an unusual and underrated bear point: the same memory supercycle driving order growth is taxing the margin line, and it partly explains why a record quarter still produced a violently negative stock reaction.

Notable One-Off Items

There is no single large write-down distorting FY2026, but four items materially affect comparability. (1) Memory input-cost inflation. Management explicitly flagged roughly 100 basis points of gross-margin drag from DRAM/NAND and component procurement costs, with the possibility it persists into 2027 — a recurring-looking headwind that did not exist a year ago. (2) Export-control effects. KLA has absorbed restricted-shipment and licensing impacts across the last three fiscal years; these show up as forgone revenue and periodic inventory/reserve adjustments rather than a clean one-line charge, so year-over-year China comparisons should be treated as noisy. The FY2026 10-K has since been filed and reports China at 30% of revenue in FY2026, 33% in FY2025 and 43% in FY2024 — a three-year decline that is itself the clearest quantification of the control regime's cumulative effect. (3) Buyback-driven EPS accretion. Share count fell ~1.34% over the past year, adding roughly a point to EPS growth and — more importantly — compressing book equity such that the 87.5% ROE overstates underlying profitability by a wide margin. Shareholder yield of ~1.78% (dividend + net buyback) is the cleaner capital-return figure. (4) Tax rate. KLA's effective rate has run in the low-to-mid teens on foreign-derived intangible income benefits; any change in that treatment would flow straight to EPS. (5) The 10-for-1 stock split completed in 2026 makes all pre-2026 per-share history non-comparable unless adjusted — every figure on this page is split-adjusted.

Five-year trajectory. From a $33.10 split-adjusted close in September 2021, KLAC returned roughly +416% through September 1, 2026 — a compound annual rate near 39%, comfortably ahead of the S&P 500's roughly 14–15% annualised and ahead of the broad semiconductor index over the same window. But the path was anything but smooth. The stock spent the whole of 2022 in a de-rating, bottoming at $29.70 in September 2022 as the post-COVID digital-demand hangover collided with the October 2022 China export controls. It then compounded almost without interruption from early 2023 to mid-2024 on the first leg of AI capex, stalled through the second half of 2024 as WFE spending digested, and re-accelerated violently from late 2025. The final year is where the character of the stock changed entirely: from roughly $92 in August 2025 to a $301.71 monthly close in June 2026 — a 3.3x in ten months, which is not how a 61%-gross-margin capital equipment company normally behaves. The July 2026 reversal to $182.82, a partial recovery to $208.25 by August 12, and then a second leg down to $175.45 at August month-end and $170.89 on September 1 is best read as the market discovering it had discounted 2027 in advance — and, in the August leg, doing so to the entire semi-cap group at once rather than to KLA specifically.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

KLA owns ~56% of a market that is structurally growing faster than the equipment industry it sits inside: process control has drifted from ~11% to ~13% of WFE as gate-all-around, backside power and High-NA EUV multiply the number of things that can go wrong on a wafer. Management's raised outlook — low-$150B WFE in 2026, ~$190B in 2027 — combined with advanced-packaging process control at ~$1.1B and +70% growth, supports a multi-year revenue compounding rate well above the 11.7% FY2026 print (the September quarter is guided to ~+25%). Layer on a ~$3B recurring service annuity growing 13–15%, a 61%+ gross margin, 67% ROIC and a 23% payout ratio, and this is one of the highest-quality franchises in technology hardware — now available 44% below its June high, at 31.4x forward earnings against a consensus target that rose while the stock fell.

Bear Case

At 46.7x trailing and 31.4x forward earnings, KLAC still trades at close to double its own decade-long average multiple on what may be peak-cycle earnings. The July 28 reaction is the tell: record revenue, a strong guide and a raised industry outlook produced a 16.3% two-day decline, because the price already embedded the good news. Meanwhile the memory supercycle is cutting both ways — roughly 100 basis points of gross-margin drag from KLA's own component procurement, potentially persisting into 2027. China at a reported 30% of FY2026 revenue sits under an actively tightening export-control regime and a well-funded indigenisation push, free cash flow converted at only 78% of net income as inventory built, and the 87.5% ROE is a buyback artefact rather than evidence of improving returns. A single downward 2027 WFE revision compresses the estimate and the multiple at the same time.

Top 3 Risks
01
Valuation / cycle-timing risk. A 31.4x forward multiple on earnings that assume ~$190B of 2027 WFE leaves a thin cushion — thicker than the 38.2x of three weeks ago, but thin. If 2027 WFE consensus drifts toward $165–175B, both the EPS estimate and the multiple compress together — and the June-to-September 44% drawdown demonstrated exactly how fast that repricing happens in this name, twice, without a single downward revision to company guidance.
02
China policy risk, in both directions. A reported 30% of FY2026 revenue came from China, down from 33% and 43% in the two prior years. Tighter US export controls or licensing denials remove revenue immediately; Chinese retaliation or accelerated domestic substitution in inspection and metrology removes it permanently. This is the least modellable and highest-variance item in the story.
03
Margin structure under input-cost pressure. The ~100bps of memory-driven gross-margin drag management flagged is a new phenomenon and may not be transitory. KLA's premium rests substantially on a 61%+ gross margin; if input costs keep the reported figure below the 62.5% guide while advanced packaging (a structurally lower-margin mix) grows fastest, the quality argument that justifies the multiple weakens.
  Industry Commentary
Process-Control Intensity Is Rising Faster Than WFE Itself
EMERGING TAILWIND
This is the core structural argument for owning KLA rather than a broad equipment basket. Every leading-edge architecture change — gate-all-around transistors, backside power delivery, High-NA EUV patterning, hybrid bonding — increases the number of measurement and inspection steps per wafer, because each adds a new class of defect that cannot be caught downstream. Process control has consequently drifted from roughly 11% of WFE spending a decade ago toward ~13% today, implying a ~$20B market on the low-$150B WFE base management guided for 2026 and roughly $25B on the ~$190B 2027 framing. Advanced packaging is the sharpest expression of it: KLA guided that line to ~$1.1B in calendar 2026, growth above 70% YoY, because a defective chiplet assembly destroys multiple known-good die at once, making inspection economics far more compelling than at wafer level. If this intensity trend holds, KLA grows a few points faster than WFE through the cycle — which is precisely what the premium multiple is paying for.
The Memory Supercycle Cuts Both Ways
ONGOING HEADWIND
HBM4 and conventional DRAM capacity additions from Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron are the marginal driver of the 2027 WFE step-up, and KLA guided September-quarter process-control systems revenue to roughly 73% foundry/logic and 27% memory — leaving room for memory mix to climb. But the same shortage that drives orders is inflating KLA's own bill of materials: management explicitly flagged that memory and component procurement costs reduced gross margin by roughly 100 basis points, with the possibility that pressure carries into 2027. A capital equipment vendor being squeezed by the cost of the very components its customers are ramping is an unusual dynamic, and it is under-discussed relative to its earnings impact. It also partially explains the July reaction: gross margin guidance of 62.5% ±1pt looked fine in isolation, but the commentary around why it was not higher mattered more.
Export Controls and the 30% China Question
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
China contributed a reported 30% of fiscal 2026 revenue (~$4.07B) per the FY2026 10-K — the largest single geography, ahead of Taiwan (~26%) and Korea (~15%), both estimated — and down from 33% in FY2025 and 43% in FY2024. That exposure sits inside a control regime that has been ratcheted in every year since October 2022, most recently through the expansion of Entity List coverage to majority-owned affiliates, which widened the number of end customers requiring licences. The counter-pressure is equally real: Beijing is directly funding domestic inspection and metrology vendors to displace KLA at trailing-edge nodes, where the technical bar is lowest.

Bull framing: a stable-to-constructive licensing environment lets China revenue hold near current dollar levels while leading-edge logic, memory and advanced packaging carry growth — in which case KLA compounds mid-to-high teens through 2027 and today's 31x forward multiple resolves into something closer to 23x within two years. Bear framing: a tightening — or a Chinese retaliation aimed at US equipment vendors — takes a high-single-digit percentage off revenue with almost no offsetting cost reduction, at the same moment 2027 WFE optimism is being marked down. The distribution of outcomes here is genuinely bimodal, which is exactly why 29 analysts produced a target range spanning $175 to $325.
