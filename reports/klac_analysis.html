01 Company Overview

KLA Corporation is the dominant supplier of process control and yield management equipment to the semiconductor industry — the inspection and metrology (measurement) tools that find defects and verify dimensions at every critical step of chip manufacturing. It is not a "chip maker" but the referee of chip making: roughly 56% of every dollar spent on semiconductor process control worldwide goes to KLA, a share roughly four to five times its nearest competitor and one of the most concentrated positions in capital equipment.

Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) revenue was $13.58B, up 11.7% YoY from $12.16B, with GAAP net income of $4.83B (+18.9%) and a 35.6% net margin. The first quarter of fiscal 2027 (to September 30, 2026) set another record at $4.14B, up 28.9% YoY, and KLA guided the December quarter to $4.7B ±$200M. At a ~$206B market value (1.30 billion shares at October 26 × the $157.91 close) the stock is up 30% over twelve months, but it is 48.6% below its June 2026 high: it has lost close to $190B of market value since the June 30 close (our calculation) as investors marked down the whole group of companies that sell equipment for AI chip production. All per-share figures reflect the 10-for-1 stock split of June 2026 (holders received nine extra shares for each share held after the close on June 11; split-adjusted trading began June 12); the header price, the 52-week range and the Section 07 series are all on the same post-split basis.

Contracted maintenance, spares, upgrades and productivity services on KLA's large installed base of systems, the large majority still in active production because process-control tools stay in fabs for decades and get re-purposed to trailing-edge nodes rather than scrapped. Service revenue is largely subscription-like: it rose from $2.33B in fiscal 2024 to $3.13B in fiscal 2026 (10-K). 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
~56% PROCESS-CONTROL SHARE LARGE INSTALLED BASE YIELD-DATA LOCK-IN OPTICAL + E-BEAM BREADTH RETICLE INSPECTION NEAR-MONOPOLY 61.5% GROSS MARGIN (TTM) ~$3B RECURRING SERVICE ANNUITY NODE-QUALIFICATION REFERENCE TOOL

KLA's moat is best understood as a reference-standard problem rather than a technology lead. When a foundry qualifies a new node, the yield-learning data set — the defect signatures, the overlay fingerprints, the process-window maps — is generated on KLA tools and encoded in KLA's analytics. Switching inspection vendors mid-node means rebuilding that entire data set, at a moment when every day of yield-ramp delay costs hundreds of millions of dollars. The economics are brutally asymmetric: process control is only ~13% of WFE spending, so a customer trying to save 20% on inspection risks the other 87% of its capital producing scrap. That asymmetry is why KLA holds more than half the market and why it sustains a gross margin north of 61% while the equipment peer group averages ~46%. Reinforcing it: KLA is the only vendor with credible depth across broadband-plasma optical inspection, laser scattering, e-beam review and multiple metrology modalities, so it can sell an integrated inspection strategy rather than a single tool. And each installed system seeds a decade-plus service annuity that competitors cannot dislodge without displacing the hardware first.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. China concentration meeting an active export-control regime. The FY2026 10-K reports China at 30% of total revenue in fiscal 2026 ($4.05B) — still the single largest geography, ahead of Taiwan at 26.8% — but that is a reported decline from 33% in FY2025 and 43% in FY2024. International revenue was ~87% of the total. On the October call CFO Bren Higgins said China ticked up a little in the September quarter and put it at 27% to 29% of revenue for the December quarter, falling to the low 20s percent in 2027 as growth elsewhere picks up. A large slice of that is trailing-edge domestic capacity that Washington has repeatedly restricted. A September 2025 rule extending US export blacklist (Entity List) restrictions to companies majority-owned by listed firms would widen the set of Chinese customers that need a licence; it has been suspended since November 10, 2025 and returns on November 10, 2026 unless the suspension is extended. KLA also faces the mirror risk: Beijing's push to build its own inspection and metrology suppliers, such as Skyverse, to replace US tools. A policy shock here is the fastest way to take several percentage points off growth.

2. A cycle that looks like permanent growth. In October management raised its 2026 forecast for wafer-equipment spending to the mid-$150B range and said it expects the market to exceed $200B in 2027. Those are excellent numbers — and they are also what the top of a cycle looks like. KLA's revenue fell only ~7% peak-to-trough in the 2023–24 downturn, but the stock fell far more. At 39.6x trailing earnings the shares still assume the upswing continues, although the 48.6% fall from the June high has taken much of that hope out of the price. Any cut to 2027 spending forecasts would lower the earnings estimate and the price investors are willing to pay per dollar of earnings at the same time.

3. Encroachment at the edges of the franchise. Onto Innovation has been taking share in advanced-packaging and panel-level metrology at ~25% growth, ASML is pushing HMI e-beam and YieldStar deeper into in-line metrology where it can bundle with lithography, and Applied Materials continues to invest in eBeam and its cSAM/SEMVision lines. None of these threatens the reticle or broadband-plasma core, but advanced packaging — the fastest-growing part of KLA's market, on track for $1.1B or more in 2026 — is the least defended part of the franchise, with lower barriers and more credible challengers.

4. Input-cost inflation from the memory shortage it benefits from. KLA's own tools consume DRAM, NAND and specialty components. On the October call Higgins said gains elsewhere were "being offset by persistently high memory costs for our system image processing requirements", and the December-quarter guide for non-GAAP gross margin, 62.5% ±1 point, sits just below the 62.7% just reported. This is an unusual and underrated bear point: the same memory boom driving KLA's orders is squeezing its margin, even in record quarters.

Notable One-Off Items

There is no single large write-down distorting the last twelve months, but five items affect comparisons. (1) Memory input-cost inflation. DRAM/NAND and component costs are weighing on gross margin: in October CFO Bren Higgins described "persistently high memory costs" and said work to use memory more efficiently, together with KLA's usual pricing, should offset the headwind over time — a drag that did not exist a year ago. (2) Export-control effects. KLA has absorbed restricted-shipment and licensing impacts across the last three fiscal years; these show up as forgone revenue and periodic inventory/reserve adjustments rather than a clean one-line charge, so year-over-year China comparisons should be treated as noisy. The FY2026 10-K has since been filed and reports China at 30% of revenue in FY2026, 33% in FY2025 and 43% in FY2024 — a three-year decline that is itself the clearest quantification of the control regime's cumulative effect. (3) Buybacks lift EPS. Diluted shares in the September quarter were 1.0% fewer than a year earlier, adding about a point to EPS growth and keeping book equity small, so the 80.6% ROE overstates underlying profitability by a wide margin. Dividends plus buybacks over the last twelve months, $3.79B, equal 1.8% of the $205.8B market value — the cleaner capital-return figure. (4) Tax rate. The September-quarter effective rate was 15.1% ($275.5M of tax on $1,824.1M of pre-tax income), including a $20.2M charge under the global minimum-tax rules ("Pillar Two") that KLA excludes from non-GAAP EPS; any change in tax treatment flows straight to EPS. (5) The 10-for-1 stock split of June 2026 makes older per-share history non-comparable unless adjusted — every figure on this page is split-adjusted.

Five-year trajectory. From a $37.28 split-adjusted close at the end of October 2021, KLAC returned +323.6% through October 30, 2026 on price alone — about 33.5% a year compounded, against +64.6% for the S&P 500 ETF (SPY) and +78.8% for the Nasdaq-100 ETF (QQQ) over the same dates (Yahoo closes, dividends excluded). But the path was anything but smooth. The stock spent the whole of 2022 falling, bottoming at $30.26 in September 2022 as the post-COVID digital-demand hangover collided with the October 2022 China export controls. It then compounded almost without interruption from early 2023 to mid-2024 on the first leg of AI spending, stalled through the second half of 2024 as chipmakers paused equipment spending, and re-accelerated violently from late 2025. The final year is where the character of the stock changed entirely: from roughly $87 in August 2025 to a $301.71 monthly close in June 2026 — a 3.5x in ten months, which is not how a 61%-gross-margin capital equipment company normally behaves. Since then the path has been a series of swings for the whole chip-equipment group: a July 2026 month-end of $182.82, $175.45 at the end of August, a rebound to $194.93 at the end of September, and a 29.6% slide over three weeks of October before the October 30 close of $157.91. KLA's revenue, earnings and guidance rose through all of it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

KLA owns ~56% of a market that is structurally growing faster than the equipment industry it sits inside: process control has drifted from ~11% to ~13% of WFE as gate-all-around, backside power and High-NA EUV multiply the number of things that can go wrong on a wafer. Management's raised outlook — wafer-equipment spending in the mid-$150B range in 2026 and above $200B in 2027 — combined with advanced-packaging process control on track for $1.1B or more in 2026, supports years of revenue growth well above the 11.7% of fiscal 2026: the September quarter grew 28.9%, and December is guided to $4.7B. Layer on a ~$3B recurring service business growing 14%, a 61%+ gross margin, ~70% ROIC and a 23% payout ratio, and this is one of the highest-quality businesses in technology hardware — now available 48.6% below its June high, at 27.5x fiscal-2027 consensus earnings, against an average analyst target of $221.42.

Bear Case

At 39.6x trailing and 27.5x forward earnings, KLAC still trades well above its own historical 18–25x on what may be peak-cycle earnings. The last two prints are the tell: in July record revenue and a raised outlook met a 10.8% fall the next session, and in October a beat and a higher guide produced a one-day gain that was gone the session after, as the whole group sold off. The price is following the sector's mood about AI spending more than KLA's own numbers. Meanwhile the memory boom is cutting both ways — "persistently high memory costs", in the CFO's words, are weighing on KLA's own gross margin. China at a reported 30% of FY2026 revenue sits under export controls that have tightened repeatedly since 2022 and a state-funded push to replace US tools, free cash flow converted at only 77% of net income as inventory rose to $4.09B, and the 80.6% ROE is a product of buybacks rather than evidence of improving returns. A single downward revision to 2027 spending forecasts would cut both the earnings estimate and the price investors pay per dollar of earnings at the same time.

Top 3 Risks
01
Valuation / cycle-timing risk. A 27.5x forward P/E on earnings that assume more than $200B of 2027 wafer-equipment spending leaves a thin cushion. If 2027 forecasts drift toward $165–175B, the EPS estimate and the price investors pay per dollar of it fall together — and the 48.6% fall from the June high, including a 29.6% slide in three weeks of October, shows how fast that can happen in this stock without any cut to company guidance.
02
China policy risk, in both directions. A reported 30% of FY2026 revenue came from China, down from 33% and 43% in the two prior years. Tighter US export controls or licensing denials remove revenue immediately; Chinese retaliation or accelerated domestic substitution in inspection and metrology removes it permanently. This is the least modellable and highest-variance item in the story.
03
Margin structure under input-cost pressure. The memory-chip cost pressure management describes is new and may not be temporary. KLA's premium valuation rests substantially on a 61%+ gross margin; if input costs keep the reported figure below the 62.5% non-GAAP guide for longer than management expects, the quality argument that justifies that valuation weakens.
  Industry Commentary
Process-Control Intensity Is Rising Faster Than WFE Itself
EMERGING TAILWIND
This is the core structural argument for owning KLA rather than a broad equipment basket. Every leading-edge architecture change — gate-all-around transistors, backside power delivery, High-NA EUV patterning, hybrid bonding — increases the number of measurement and inspection steps per wafer, because each adds a new class of defect that cannot be caught downstream. Process control has consequently drifted from roughly 11% of WFE spending a decade ago toward ~13% today, implying a ~$20B market on the mid-$150B of 2026 spending management now expects and $26B or more if 2027 tops $200B (our arithmetic at 13%). Advanced packaging is the sharpest expression of it: KLA says that line is on track for $1.1B or more in calendar 2026, growing at nearly twice the rate of the packaging market, because a defective chiplet assembly destroys multiple known-good die at once, making inspection economics far more compelling than at wafer level. If this trend holds, KLA grows a few points faster than wafer-equipment spending through the cycle — which is precisely what its premium valuation is paying for.
The Memory Supercycle Cuts Both Ways
ONGOING HEADWIND
HBM4 and conventional DRAM capacity additions from Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron are a main driver of the 2027 spending step-up, and the mix is already moving: for the December quarter KLA guides revenue from chipmaking customers to about 60% foundry/logic and 40% memory. But the same shortage that drives orders is inflating KLA's own bill of materials: on the October call CFO Bren Higgins pointed to "persistently high memory costs for our system image processing requirements" and said work to use memory more efficiently, together with KLA's usual pricing, should offset them over time. A capital equipment vendor being squeezed by the cost of the very components its customers are ramping is an unusual dynamic, and it is under-discussed relative to its earnings impact. The December-quarter guide of 62.5% ±1 point non-GAAP gross margin is no higher than the 62.7% just reported, even with revenue guided up about 14%.
Export Controls and the 30% China Question
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
China contributed a reported 30% of fiscal 2026 revenue ($4.05B) per the FY2026 10-K — the largest single geography, ahead of Taiwan (26.8%) and Korea (13.5%) — and down from 33% in FY2025 and 43% in FY2024. That exposure sits inside a control regime that has been tightened repeatedly since October 2022. The latest step, a September 2025 rule extending Entity List restrictions to companies majority-owned by listed firms, would widen the number of customers requiring licences; it was suspended for a year from November 10, 2025 and returns on November 10, 2026 unless the suspension is extended. The counter-pressure is equally real: Beijing is directly funding domestic inspection and metrology vendors to displace KLA at trailing-edge nodes, where the technical bar is lowest.

Bull framing: a stable-to-constructive licensing environment lets China revenue hold near current dollar levels while leading-edge logic, memory and advanced packaging carry growth — in which case KLA grows in the mid-to-high teens through 2027 and today's 27.5x forward P/E falls quickly as earnings catch up with the price. Bear framing: a tightening — or a Chinese retaliation aimed at US equipment vendors — takes a high-single-digit percentage off revenue with almost no offsetting cost reduction, at the same moment 2027 WFE optimism is being marked down. The outcomes here split sharply between good and bad, which is one reason 29 analysts' targets span $175 to $325 (S&P Global range).
