01 Company Overview

The Coca-Cola Company is the world's largest non-alcoholic beverage business, selling roughly 33.8 billion unit cases in 2025 across more than 200 countries through a network of independent bottling partners. Its economic engine is a concentrate model: Coca-Cola sells syrups and concentrates (about 59% of revenue) to bottlers who handle the capital-intensive work of filling, packaging and distribution, which is why the company converts a $47.9B revenue base into a comparable operating margin above 31% while owning relatively little manufacturing.

FY2025 net operating revenues were $47,941M, up 2% reported and up 5% on an organic basis, with unit case volume flat and price/mix contributing +4%. That mix inverted in 2026: unit case volume grew 5% in both the second and the third quarter while price/mix slowed to +2% and then +1%. Third-quarter revenue, reported on October 27, 2026, was $13,380M, up 7% reported and 7% organic, with comparable EPS of $0.91 (+11%). Management raised full-year 2026 guidance for the second quarter running, to at least 5% organic revenue growth, at least 11% comparable EPS growth and at least $13.0B of free cash flow. At ~$395B market cap KO ranks #26 in the S&P 500 and is a member of the very small club of firms with a 64-year streak of consecutive annual dividend increases (a "Dividend King", the name for a company with 50 or more straight years of increases).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Brand Equity Global Bottling System Distribution Density Concentrate Economics Shelf & Cooler Space Marketing Scale Pricing Power

Coca-Cola's moat is best understood as a distribution asset wearing a brand's clothes. The brand is what gets a consumer to pay a premium for flavored sugar water; the bottling system — thousands of independent partners, tens of millions of coolers and vending points, and route density that reaches roughly 200 countries — is what makes it physically impossible for a challenger to reach the same consumer at the same cost. The concentrate structure then captures most of the economic rent: Coca-Cola sells the high-margin syrup and lets partners absorb the capital intensity, which is why Latin America ran a 59% segment operating margin in Q3 2026 while the company-owned Bottling Investments Group ran 13%. The result is a business generating 41% ROE and 21% ROIC on a 62% gross margin, with the durability to raise its dividend for 64 consecutive years through every recession, currency crisis and consumer-taste cycle since 1963. The moat is wide but it is not growing: it protects the profit pool far better than it expands it, which is exactly why the stock trades on multiple and dividend rather than on growth.

Health, sugar policy and GLP-1 displacement. Sugar taxes, front-of-pack warning labels and reformulation mandates now exist across Latin America, Europe and parts of Asia, and the US policy conversation around added sugar and artificial dyes has intensified. Structurally more important is GLP-1 adoption, which suppresses appetite for exactly the impulse-consumption occasions that drive full-sugar CSD volume. Coca-Cola's answer so far has worked — Coke Zero Sugar +16% and water +8% in Q3 2026 — but the mix shift moves the company toward lower-price, lower-margin liquid.

The IRS transfer-pricing case. This is the single largest quantified vulnerability on the balance sheet and it is not fully accrued. Coca-Cola already paid $6B in back taxes and interest for 2007–2009 after losing in Tax Court; the Eleventh Circuit heard its appeal on June 25, 2026, and no ruling had been issued by the Q3 10-Q (filed Oct 28, 2026). A loss would extend the same methodology to 2010–2025 and could add up to ~$14B more (the company's estimate as of Dec 31, 2025) — about 3.5% of market cap, or roughly $3.25 per share — taking total exposure toward $20B, and it would also raise the ongoing effective tax rate above the guided 19.7%.

Volume dependence on emerging markets at dilutive prices. The growth arithmetic now requires India, Africa, Southeast Asia and China to supply units. Q2 2026 showed the cost: Asia Pacific delivered +8% volume for +2% organic revenue because its price/mix fell 9%, mainly on unfavorable mix and affordability initiatives, and the region's price/mix was still -2% in Q3. Every incremental case is worth less than the average case already in the base, so unit growth flatters volume headlines while diluting revenue per case.

Currency and input-cost translation risk. About 60% of revenue is earned outside the US ($28.8B of $47.9B in 2025, per the 10-K). Currency was a multi-year drag through 2023–2025 and has now flipped to a ~3-point tailwind to 2026 comparable EPS — which means the current earnings trajectory is partly borrowed from FX and would reverse on a dollar rally. Aluminum, PET resin, sweetener and juice concentrate costs add a second, less controllable layer.

Notable One-Off Items

KO's recent reported figures are unusually distorted, and reading trailing metrics without adjustment will mislead:

IRS transfer-pricing liability (~$6B paid, up to ~$14B more at risk). The $6B paid in 2024 is already reflected; the incremental 2010–2025 exposure (about $14B, the company's estimate as of Dec 31, 2025) is a potential amount, not a recorded liability; the company carried a $512M tax reserve at that date. The Eleventh Circuit heard the appeal on June 25, 2026; no ruling had been issued by the Q3 10-Q. Interest on the IRS deposit produced a $57M tax benefit in Q3 2026. This is the largest single binary item in the story.

fairlife contingent consideration — $6.1B paid in Q1 2025. This one payment cut FY2025 free cash flow to $5.3B from a normalized level above $10B. It is the reason FY2025 FCF looks broken and TTM FCF of $14.3B looks inflated; FY2026 guidance of at least $13.0B is the cleaner run-rate.

FY2025 non-cash charges and gains. A $960M BODYARMOR trademark impairment in Q4 2025, a $1,274M charge on the Coca-Cola Beverages Africa held-for-sale classification, offset by a $1,952M gain on the sale of Coca-Cola Consolidated shares. FY2025 reported operating margin of 28.7% versus comparable 31.2% captures the net drag; the 2024 comparison base (21.2% reported) was itself distorted by the prior-year fairlife remeasurement.

Currency has flipped sign. After several years as a headwind, FX is now guided as a ~1-point tailwind to revenue and a ~3-point tailwind to comparable EPS in 2026 (about 4 points in Q4 alone). Comparable EPS growth of at least 11%, against at least 9% on a currency-neutral basis excluding acquisitions and divestitures, means about a quarter of this year's headline growth is currency translation, not operations.

fairlife ransomware attack (July 2026). An attack disclosed on July 16, 2026 (Form 8-K) temporarily suspended fairlife's US production; on July 27 the company said the majority of production had resumed at its four US plants and that it did not expect a material impact on its results.

Q3 2026 pension charge. A $192M non-U.S. pension settlement charge and special termination benefits were booked in Q3 and excluded from comparable results.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Volume growth has genuinely returned — +5% unit cases in both Q2 and Q3 2026, with Coca-Cola Zero Sugar up 16% — which converts KO from a pure pricing story back into a volume-plus-price compounder, and KFC's ~3,700 US restaurants move over from PepsiCo from January 2027. Currency has flipped from a multi-year drag to a ~3-point EPS tailwind, refranchising continues to lift consolidated margin (Q3 comparable operating margin 32.9%, up from 31.9%), and at least $13.0B of guided free cash flow comfortably funds a 64-year dividend growth streak. With a beta of 0.32, ~41% ROE and ~21% ROIC, KO is the highest-quality way to own defensiveness at a moment when index returns are concentrated in a handful of AI names.

Bear Case

You are paying 26.1x 2027 consensus earnings and a 3.7 PEG for mid-to-high-single-digit underlying EPS growth — a premium to the beverage peer group. About 3 points of the guided 11%-plus comparable EPS growth in 2026 is currency translation that reverses on a dollar rally, and 2027 starts with a flagged headwind of about 8% to revenue and 2% to EPS from acquisitions and divestitures, plus a slight currency headwind. Price/mix has slowed to +1%, with Asia Pacific at -2%, meaning growth is migrating to markets worth less per case. Sitting behind all of it is a potential IRS liability of up to ~$14B, not recorded on the balance sheet — about $3.25 per share — with the Eleventh Circuit ruling still pending.

Top 3 Risks
01
Adverse Eleventh Circuit ruling in the IRS transfer-pricing case. A loss extends the disputed methodology across 2010–2025 and could add up to ~$14B to the $6B already paid, roughly 3.5% of market cap, while permanently pushing the effective tax rate above the guided 19.7%. The appeal was heard on June 25, 2026 and no ruling had been issued by the Q3 10-Q; the incremental exposure is a company estimate, not a recorded liability.
02
Multiple compression on a steady dividend stock at 26x forward earnings. KO's five-year history shows exactly this mechanism twice — September 2023 and December 2024 — when rising real yields and a stronger dollar took 13–14% out of the stock in a few months without any change in fundamentals. At a 2.31% dividend yield against a 26.1x forward multiple, there is little valuation cushion if rates rise.
03
Structural volume erosion from GLP-1 adoption and sugar regulation, compounded by dilutive emerging-market mix. The company's growth now depends on markets where revenue per case is materially lower — Asia Pacific's price/mix was -2% on +6% volume in Q3 2026, after +8% volume for +2% organic revenue in Q2 — while developed-market full-sugar CSD occasions face appetite suppression and expanding sugar-tax and warning-label regimes. Zero-sugar strength (+16%) mitigates but does not neutralise the mix drag.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
GLP-1 Adoption and the Health-Shift Repricing of Liquid Calories
ONGOING HEADWIND
The market first priced GLP-1 risk into beverages in September 2023, and KO's monthly close of $55.98 that month remains its lowest month-end close since November 2021. Three years on, the evidence is mixed rather than catastrophic: full-sugar sparkling still grew, Trademark Coca-Cola rose 5% in Q3 2026, and Coca-Cola Zero Sugar grew 16% — consumers are substituting within the portfolio rather than leaving it. But the substitution is not margin-neutral. Zero-sugar, water, sports, coffee and tea (water, sports, coffee and tea grew 5% together in Q3) carry lower revenue per case than premium full-sugar single-serve, and the regulatory overlay — sugar taxes across Latin America and Europe, front-of-pack warning labels, and the intensified US conversation about added sugar and dyes that produced the cane-sugar Coke line — pushes in the same direction. The correct framing is not "does Coke lose volume," it is "does Coke keep the volume at a lower revenue and margin per case."
Currency Reversal and Commodity Pressure — a Tailwind Borrowed, Not Earned
EMERGING TAILWIND
With about 60% of revenue earned abroad, KO is heavily exposed to currency moves, and 2026 is the first year in several where that leverage works in shareholders' favour: guidance embeds a ~1-point tailwind to revenue and ~3-point tailwind to comparable EPS, which is why comparable EPS growth of at least 11% sits above currency-neutral growth, excluding acquisitions and divestitures, of at least 9%. The Latin America segment shows it in the raw — Q3 revenue +25% reported versus +16% organic, and operating income +29%. The discipline required here is to remember that about a quarter of this year's headline earnings growth is translation. A dollar rally of the kind seen in Q4 2024 would reverse it within two quarters, and the December 2024 chart shows exactly what the stock does when that happens. Commodity inputs — aluminium, PET resin, sweetener, juice concentrate — are a second-order pressure that price/mix has so far absorbed, with Q3 gross margin still up about 170bps year on year, to 63.2%.
Defensive Positioning Inside an AI-Concentrated Market — the Bull/Bear Frame
REGIME-DEPENDENT
The S&P 500's gains have been led by a small group of AI-linked giants. KO at 27.1x trailing / 26.1x forward with a beta of 0.32 is the mirror image of that trade: no AI capex exposure, no hyperscaler customer concentration, and earnings that have compounded through every macro regime since 1963. The bull implication: if the AI-led leadership breaks or breadth reverts, KO is one of a handful of $300B+ businesses with genuinely uncorrelated earnings, a 64-year dividend growth record and 21% ROIC — and it has already demonstrated the pattern, outperforming the index over the last twelve months (+30.7% price vs +12.4% for SPY). The bear implication: if the AI cycle keeps delivering, KO is a 26x forward multiple on high-single-digit underlying EPS growth, helped by FX, in a market where the alternative compounds faster — and over the chart's five years its price return already trails SPY's by about 5 points. Owning KO here is a position on market regime as much as on Coca-Cola.
