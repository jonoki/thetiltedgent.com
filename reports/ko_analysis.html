01 Company Overview

The Coca-Cola Company is the world's largest non-alcoholic beverage business, selling roughly 33.8 billion unit cases in 2025 across more than 200 countries through a network of independent bottling partners. Its economic engine is a concentrate model: Coca-Cola sells syrups and concentrates (about 59% of revenue) to bottlers who handle the capital-intensive work of filling, packaging and distribution, which is why the company converts a $47.9B revenue base into a comparable operating margin above 31% while owning relatively little manufacturing.

FY2025 net operating revenues were $47,941M, up 2% reported and up 5% on an organic basis, with unit case volume flat and price/mix contributing +4%. That mix inverted in 2026: second-quarter revenue of $13.38B rose 7% reported and 6% organic on +5% unit case volume and only +2% price/mix — the strongest Trademark Coca-Cola volume quarter in roughly 17 years excluding the pandemic rebound, helped by FIFA World Cup activation. Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to ~5% organic revenue growth, 9–10% comparable EPS growth and ~$12.4B free cash flow. At ~$373B market cap KO is roughly the 18th-largest NYSE-listed company and a member of the very small club of firms with a 64-year streak of consecutive annual dividend increases (a "Dividend King" many times over).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Brand Equity Global Bottling System Distribution Density Concentrate Economics Shelf & Cooler Space Marketing Scale Pricing Power

Coca-Cola's moat is best understood as a distribution asset wearing a brand's clothes. The brand is what gets a consumer to pay a premium for flavored sugar water; the bottling system — thousands of independent partners, tens of millions of coolers and vending points, and route density that reaches roughly 200 countries — is what makes it physically impossible for a challenger to reach the same consumer at the same cost. The concentrate structure then captures most of the economic rent: Coca-Cola sells the high-margin syrup and lets partners absorb the capital intensity, which is why Latin America runs a 64% segment operating margin while the company-owned Bottling Investments Group runs 6%. The result is a business generating 42% ROE and 20% ROIC on a 62% gross margin, with the durability to raise its dividend for 64 consecutive years through every recession, currency crisis and consumer-taste cycle since 1963. The moat is wide but it is not growing: it protects the profit pool far better than it expands it, which is exactly why the stock trades on multiple and dividend rather than on growth.

Health, sugar policy and GLP-1 displacement. Sugar taxes, front-of-pack warning labels and reformulation mandates now exist across Latin America, Europe and parts of Asia, and the US policy conversation around added sugar and artificial dyes has intensified. Structurally more important is GLP-1 adoption, which suppresses appetite for exactly the impulse-consumption occasions that drive full-sugar CSD volume. Coca-Cola's answer so far has worked — Coke Zero Sugar +16%, water/sports/tea +6% — but the mix shift moves the company toward lower-price, lower-margin liquid.

The IRS transfer-pricing case. This is the single largest quantified vulnerability on the balance sheet and it is not fully accrued. Coca-Cola already paid $6B in back taxes and interest for 2007–2009 after losing in Tax Court; oral arguments in its Eleventh Circuit appeal were heard in late June 2026, with a decision expected in roughly 6–12 months. A loss would extend the same methodology to 2010–2025 and could add up to ~$14B more, taking total exposure toward $20B — about 3.7% of market cap, or roughly $3.25 per share, and it would also raise the ongoing effective tax rate above the guided 19.9%.

Volume dependence on emerging markets at dilutive prices. The growth arithmetic now requires India, Africa, Southeast Asia and China to supply units. Q2 2026 showed the cost: Asia Pacific delivered +8% volume for +2% organic revenue because India carried a 9-point price/mix drag from affordability packs. Every incremental case is worth less than the average case already in the base, so unit growth flatters volume headlines while diluting revenue per case.

Currency and input-cost translation risk. Roughly two-thirds of revenue is earned outside the US. Currency was a multi-year drag through 2023–2025 and has now flipped to a ~3-point tailwind to 2026 comparable EPS — which means the current earnings trajectory is partly borrowed from FX and would reverse on a dollar rally. Aluminum, PET resin, sweetener and juice concentrate costs add a second, less controllable layer.

Notable One-Off Items

KO's recent reported figures are unusually distorted, and reading trailing metrics without adjustment will mislead:

IRS transfer-pricing liability (~$6B paid, up to ~$14B more at risk). The $6B paid in 2024 is already reflected; the incremental 2010–2025 exposure is not accrued and is explicitly excluded from the guided 19.9% underlying tax rate. Eleventh Circuit oral arguments were heard in late June 2026 with a ruling expected within 6–12 months. This is the largest single binary item in the story.

fairlife contingent consideration — $6.1B paid in Q1 2025. This one payment cut FY2025 free cash flow to $5.3B from a normalized level above $10B. It is the reason FY2025 FCF looks broken and TTM FCF of $14.3B looks inflated; FY2026 guidance of ~$12.4B is the cleaner run-rate.

FY2025 non-cash charges and gains. A $960M BODYARMOR trademark impairment in Q4 2025, a $1,274M charge on the Coca-Cola Beverages Africa held-for-sale classification (accumulated negative currency translation), offset by a $1,952M gain on the sale of Coca-Cola Consolidated shares. FY2025 reported operating margin of 28.7% versus comparable 31.2% captures the net drag; the 2024 comparison base (21.2% reported) was itself distorted by the prior-year fairlife remeasurement.

Currency has flipped sign. After several years as a headwind, FX is now guided as a ~1-point tailwind to revenue and a ~3-point tailwind to comparable EPS in 2026. Reported EPS growth of 9–10% versus currency-neutral 7–8% means roughly a third of this year's headline growth is translation, not operations.

fairlife technology disruption (July 2026). A production/IT outage temporarily interrupted fairlife supply; the company reported substantial restoration by July 27, 2026. Impact was largely contained within the quarter but suppressed juice/dairy/plant volume to +2%.

KO closed September 2021 at $52.47 and the August 12, 2026 session at $86.71 — a +65.3% price return, or roughly +83% total return once the ~$9.60 per share of dividends paid over the window is included (about a 12.9% annualized total return). Monthly RSI above 70 in a staple usually resolves through time rather than through a sharp drawdown, but it does argue against chasing at these levels with the average analyst target only ~9% higher.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Volume growth has genuinely returned — +5% unit cases in Q2 2026 with the best Trademark Coca-Cola quarter in ~17 years — which converts KO from a pure pricing story back into a volume-plus-price compounder. Currency has flipped from a multi-year drag to a ~3-point EPS tailwind, refranchising continues to lift consolidated margin toward the mid-30s (Q2 comparable operating margin 35.6%), and ~$12.4B of guided free cash flow comfortably funds a 64-year dividend growth streak. With a beta of 0.34, 42% ROE and 20% ROIC, KO is the highest-quality way to own defensiveness at a moment when index returns are concentrated in a handful of AI names.

Bear Case

You are paying 25.3x forward earnings and a 3.4 PEG for 7–8% currency-neutral EPS growth — a premium to both the beverage peer group and, on a growth-adjusted basis, the S&P 500. Roughly a third of headline 2026 EPS growth is FX translation that reverses on a dollar rally, and the Q2 volume surge was flattered by FIFA World Cup activation that does not repeat. Price/mix has already decelerated to +2% with India running a 9-point drag, meaning growth is migrating to markets worth less per case. Sitting behind all of it is an unaccrued IRS liability of up to ~$14B — about $3.25 per share — with an appellate ruling due within twelve months.

Top 3 Risks
01
Adverse Eleventh Circuit ruling in the IRS transfer-pricing case. A loss extends the disputed methodology across 2010–2025 and could add up to ~$14B to the $6B already paid, roughly 3.7% of market cap, while permanently pushing the effective tax rate above the guided 19.9%. The decision is expected within 6–12 months of the late-June 2026 oral arguments and is not reflected in current estimates.
02
Multiple compression on a bond-proxy at 25x forward earnings. KO's five-year history shows exactly this mechanism twice — September 2023 and December 2024 — when rising real yields and a stronger dollar took 13–14% out of the stock in a few months without any change in fundamentals. At a 2.44% dividend yield against a 25.3x forward multiple, the valuation cushion is thinner than at any point in the five-year window.
03
Structural volume erosion from GLP-1 adoption and sugar regulation, compounded by dilutive emerging-market mix. The company's growth now depends on markets where revenue per case is materially lower — Asia Pacific delivered +8% volume for +2% organic revenue — while developed-market full-sugar CSD occasions face appetite suppression and expanding sugar-tax and warning-label regimes. Zero-sugar strength (+16%) mitigates but does not neutralise the mix drag.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
GLP-1 Adoption and the Health-Shift Repricing of Liquid Calories
ONGOING HEADWIND
The market first priced GLP-1 risk into beverages in September 2023, and KO's monthly close of $55.98 that month remains the five-year low. Three years on, the evidence is mixed rather than catastrophic: full-sugar sparkling still grew, Trademark Coca-Cola rose 5% in Q2 2026, and Coca-Cola Zero Sugar grew 16% — consumers are substituting within the portfolio rather than leaving it. But the substitution is not margin-neutral. Zero-sugar, water, sports and tea (the +6% growth cluster) carry lower revenue per case than premium full-sugar single-serve, and the regulatory overlay — sugar taxes across Latin America and Europe, front-of-pack warning labels, and the intensified US conversation about added sugar and dyes that produced the cane-sugar Coke line — pushes in the same direction. The correct framing is not "does Coke lose volume," it is "does Coke keep the volume at a lower revenue and margin per case."
Currency Reversal and Commodity Pressure — a Tailwind Borrowed, Not Earned
EMERGING TAILWIND
With roughly two-thirds of revenue earned abroad, KO is one of the most FX-levered large caps in the S&P 500, and 2026 is the first year in several where that leverage works in shareholders' favour: guidance embeds a ~1-point tailwind to revenue and ~3-point tailwind to comparable EPS, which is why reported EPS growth of 9–10% sits above currency-neutral growth of 7–8%. The Latin America segment shows it in the raw — Q2 revenue +16% reported versus +5% organic, and operating income +23%. The discipline required here is to remember that roughly a third of this year's headline earnings growth is translation. A dollar rally of the kind seen in Q4 2024 would reverse it within two quarters, and the December 2024 chart shows exactly what the stock does when that happens. Commodity inputs — aluminium, PET resin, sweetener, juice concentrate — are a second-order pressure that price/mix has so far absorbed, with gross margin still expanding 120bps in Q2 to 60.5%.
Defensive Positioning Inside an AI-Concentrated Market — the Bull/Bear Frame
REGIME-DEPENDENT
The S&P 500 trades at ~28x with its top ten holdings at ~38% of the index and NVIDIA alone near 8%. KO at 26.0x trailing / 25.3x forward with a beta of 0.34 is the mirror image of that trade: no AI capex exposure, no hyperscaler customer concentration, and earnings that have compounded through every macro regime since 1963. The bull implication: if the AI-led leadership breaks or breadth reverts, KO is one of a handful of $300B+ businesses with genuinely uncorrelated earnings, a 64-year dividend growth record and 20% ROIC — and it has already demonstrated the pattern, outperforming the index over the last twelve months (+23.7% price vs ~+22%). The bear implication: if the AI cycle keeps delivering, KO is a 25x forward multiple on 7–8% currency-neutral EPS growth, funded partly by FX, in a market where the alternative compounds faster — and its five-year record already shows a ten-point shortfall to the index. Owning KO here is a position on market regime as much as on Coca-Cola.
