01 Company Overview

A note on the calendar before anything else. Kroger's fiscal year ends on the Saturday nearest January 31, so every "fiscal 2025" figure below refers to the 52 weeks ended January 31, 2026, and "fiscal 2026" is the year ending January 30, 2027 — the year currently in progress. Data aggregators disagree with the company's own labelling (StockAnalysis calls the year ended Jan 31, 2026 "FY 2026"); this page uses Kroger's convention and states the period-end date wherever it matters.

Kroger is the largest pure-play food and drug retailer in the United States, running combination food-and-drug stores, multi-department stores, marketplace stores and price-impact warehouses under roughly two dozen banners, plus supermarket fuel centres, pharmacies and a manufacturing arm. Fiscal 2025 (ended Jan 31, 2026) revenue was $147.64B, up 0.35% from $147.12B; trailing-twelve-month revenue through the May 2026 quarter was $148.65B, up 1.14%. At $34.91B of market value it ranks roughly #735 globally by market capitalisation — a company with the revenue of a superpower and the market cap of a mid-cap software firm.

That gap is the whole story, and it is not a flaw. Grocery is a ~2% net margin business by design. Kroger's fiscal 2025 operating margin was 3.20% and its net profit margin on a trailing basis is 0.73%. Those are not signs of a broken company; they are the physics of selling perishable food at high volume and low mark-up. The right way to underwrite Kroger is not margin expansion but identical sales excluding fuel, gross margin rate ex-fuel, the OG&A rate, and how much of the share count management retires each year. The central debate on this page is whether the current combination — identical sales decelerating to +0.2% while alternative profit and digital carry the margin — is a durable model or a treadmill that stops working the moment traffic turns negative.

Identical sales excluding fuel (sales from stores open without expansion or relocation for five full quarters, stripping out fuel) rose just 0.2% in Q2 FY2026, against +3.4% a year earlier; year-to-date the figure is +0.6% versus +3.3%. That deceleration is the single most important fact on this page, and management attributed it to identifiable, mostly transitory drags: a cyclospora outbreak in fresh produce cost roughly 30 basis points as shoppers avoided fruit generally, not only the implicated items; declining egg prices took another ~30bps; and pharmacy carried an unfavourable 138bp impact from Inflation Reduction Act drug-price negotiation, with generic conversion costing 50bps more. Strip those out and the underlying number is respectable — but "strip those out" is the bull's sentence, not the tape's. Adjusted eCommerce sales grew 20% year over year in Q2 FY2026 — the adjustment strips out exited fulfilment operations, the sale of Vitacost and the discontinued Ship Marketplace, so the comparison is like-for-like. Two things make this the most interesting line in the business. First, growth of 20% against a company-wide identical-sales figure of 0.2% means digital is essentially the only source of volume growth Kroger has. Second, management stated that eCommerce reached profitability for the second consecutive quarter when retail media revenue is included — a materially different claim from "eCommerce is profitable," and the qualifier deserves to be read carefully. Delivery and pickup economics in grocery are brutal: picking a 40-item basket of perishables is labour-intensive, substitution and refund rates are high, and basket margins are thin before any fulfilment cost. Kroger's answer has been to route the shortfall through advertising dollars rather than through the basket.

Watch item: if "profitable including retail media" ever reverts to "approaching profitability," the margin bridge that is currently offsetting flat identical sales comes apart.

Kroger Precision Marketing profit grew 24% in Q2 FY2026 — note that the company disclosed the growth in profit, not revenue, which is itself informative about which number management thinks flatters the story. Retail media is the structural answer to a 2% net margin: Kroger already knows, at household level, what tens of millions of loyalty-card shoppers buy, and it sells that targeting to the consumer-packaged-goods companies whose products sit on its own shelves. The incremental margin on an ad impression is close to 100%, against roughly 2% on a can of soup. This is the one line in the business that can move consolidated operating profit without moving a single pallet, and it is why "grocery is low-margin" is an incomplete description of what Kroger now is. It is also why the gross margin rate ex-fuel rose 13bps in a quarter when identical sales were essentially zero.

Pharmacy is the clearest example of why Kroger's headline comp understates the underlying business. The Inflation Reduction Act (US legislation that lets Medicare negotiate prices on selected high-cost drugs) mechanically lowers the dollar value of scripts Kroger dispenses without changing the number of prescriptions filled or the customers walking through the door. The company quantified the hit at an unfavourable 138 basis points on identical sales, with accelerating conversion of branded drugs to generics costing over 50bps more. Pharmacy is also a traffic driver and a margin mix benefit — management cited "favourable pharmacy mix" as one of the positives in the gross margin bridge. So the same business is simultaneously depressing the sales line and helping the margin line, which is exactly the kind of thing a single headline comp figure buries.

Read-through: the IRA drag is a price effect, not a demand effect. It annualises out; it does not compound.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

DENSITY & SCALE 84M+ HOUSEHOLD DATA RETAIL MEDIA FLYWHEEL OWN-BRAND PORTFOLIO PHARMACY NETWORK REAL-ESTATE FOOTPRINT FOOD MANUFACTURING

The core advantage is not price — Walmart, Costco and Aldi all beat Kroger on basket cost in most markets — it is the conversion of purchase data into a second, high-margin revenue stream. A regional grocer with 200 stores cannot sell meaningful targeted advertising; Kroger, with a loyalty programme covering the large majority of its sales, can tell a cereal manufacturer precisely which households bought the competitor's product last month and charge for the privilege of reaching them. That is why Kroger Precision Marketing profit grew 24% in a quarter when identical sales grew 0.2%. The second advantage is more prosaic and more durable: route density. Distribution cost per case falls with store density in a region, which is why Kroger's strategy has consistently been to buy share in markets it already occupies — most recently the $1.65B Giant Eagle acquisition announced July 1, 2026, covering roughly 197 supermarkets and 11 pharmacies in the Ohio Valley and mid-Atlantic, regions adjacent to Kroger's Cincinnati base.

Key vulnerabilities:

Structural share loss to mass and discount
Kroger competes for the same grocery dollar as Walmart, Costco, Amazon and the hard discounters, all of which have either lower cost structures, higher-margin adjacent businesses, or both. Identical sales of +0.2% against a broadly stable consumer is not obviously a pricing decision; it can equally be read as share leaking away one trip at a time. Kroger does not disclose national share, so the leak is not directly observable in its filings.
Capital allocation credibility
In Q3 fiscal 2025 Kroger took a $2.6B impairment against its automated fulfilment network — the Ocado-built customer fulfilment centres — producing a GAAP loss of $2.02 per share against adjusted EPS of $1.05, a $3.00-per-share charge. That was a write-off of a multi-year strategic bet, and it is the reason trailing GAAP earnings look far worse than the business does. Investors are now being asked to fund a $1.65B acquisition from the same management framework.
Regulatory exposure on the margin line
The Inflation Reduction Act drug-pricing provisions are already costing 138 basis points of identical sales. Pharmacy reimbursement, labour law and antitrust review of store acquisitions are all live policy risks for a company whose entire net margin is roughly 2% — a policy change that moves costs by 1% of sales moves half the earnings.
Food-safety and fresh execution
The cyclospora outbreak in fresh produce cost roughly 30 basis points of identical sales in a single quarter and, per the CFO, is expected to keep consumers "cautious about buying produce" — which is why full-year guidance came down. Fresh is where grocers differentiate and where reputational damage is fastest and least controllable. Shrink (spoilage and theft) was separately flagged as a gross-margin headwind.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has risen relentlessly and is described as "overbought"; below 30 it has fallen relentlessly and is "oversold". It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — an overbought stock can keep rising and an oversold one can keep falling. Computed here on monthly closes, so each point summarises half a year of trading.

Five years of KR is a story in three acts. From the October 2021 close of $40.02 the stock ran to $57.37 by March 2022 — a 43% move in five months — as food inflation went vertical and grocers were repriced as the rare businesses that benefit from rising shelf prices. That was act one, and it was not about Kroger. Act two was the merger limbo: from the October 2022 announcement of the Albertsons deal the stock spent more than two years dead money, bottoming at a $43.14 monthly close in February 2023 and still only $61.15 by December 2024, because no one could underwrite a company whose shape depended on a regulator. Act three began when the deal died: freed of the overhang, KR ran to a $72.21 close in April 2025 and a cycle-high $72.36 in March 2026 on the strength of buybacks, retail media and defensive rotation. From that high it has fallen 21.3% to the September 10, 2026 close of $56.95, with the decisive break in June 2026 — a $68.24 close in February to $55.53 in June, an 18.6% drawdown in four months as the Q1 report reset expectations and the Giant Eagle deal reopened the capital-allocation question.

Total price return over the five years to September 10, 2026 is +42.3% ($40.02 → $56.95), before dividends — which, at a yield that has run in the 2–3% area with 20 consecutive annual increases, add meaningfully to the total. Over the trailing twelve months the picture is far worse: the 52-week price change is −12.78%, and the close sits just 5.2% above the 52-week low of $54.15 and 25.6% below the high of $76.58.

Relative performance. Precise S&P 500 and NASDAQ-100 total-return figures for the matching periods were not sourced from the pages fetched and are therefore not quoted here — stating them from memory would be exactly the kind of number that should not appear on a page like this. What can be said from verified data: KR's beta of 0.41 means the stock is structurally decoupled from index moves, so index-relative performance tells you less about Kroger than it does about the market's appetite for defensives. Within its own industry the read is cleaner and unflattering: the StockAnalysis Grocery Stores group trades at a 37.7x average P/E against KR's 33.1x, and Sprouts Farmers Market — one-sixteenth of Kroger's revenue — grew +7.2% against Kroger's +1.14% and commands a market cap of $6.75B on $9.00B of sales, a price-to-sales ratio roughly three times Kroger's 0.24x. Albertsons, the closest true comparable at $83.23B of revenue, is valued at just $5.81B. The market is paying for grocery growth and refusing to pay for grocery scale.

Range-bound into the catalyst
08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying $148.65B of the most recession-resistant revenue in the economy for $34.91B, at roughly 11x reaffirmed FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance and an ~8% free cash flow yield, with a 2.74% dividend raised for 20 consecutive years. The GAAP optics are wrecked by a one-time $2.6B impairment that says nothing about the run-rate. Underneath, the parts that matter are working: FIFO gross margin ex-fuel up 13bps, eCommerce up 20% and profitable including media, retail-media profit up 24%, adjusted EPS up 5%. Management is retiring ~2.9% of the share count per quarter at these prices with leverage at 1.91x against a 2.30–2.50x target — meaning the buyback can get bigger, not smaller. Flat comps plus aggressive buybacks plus mix shift into high-margin media is a perfectly serviceable mid-single-digit EPS compounder, and it is not priced as one.

Bear Case

Identical sales of +0.2% against +3.4% a year ago, with full-year guidance cut to 0.2%–0.8% from 1.0%–2.0%, is a company losing volume. Management's explanations — cyclospora, egg prices, the IRA — are each real and each conveniently transitory, but the pattern of needing three separate excuses is itself the signal. The OG&A rate rose 33bps on wage and healthcare investment that will not reverse, and adjusted FIFO operating profit actually fell year over year ($1,076M vs $1,091M) — the EPS growth came from the share count, not the business. On a ~0.7% net margin there is no cushion: a 1% cost shock halves earnings. And the Albertsons suit for the $600M break fee plus damages is an unquantified liability against $2.87B of annual free cash flow, with a management team that just wrote off $2.6B of its last strategic bet and is now spending $1.65B on the next one.

Top 3 risks:

01
Identical sales do not recover. If the +0.2% comp reflects share loss to Walmart, Costco and the discounters rather than produce contamination and drug pricing, the entire model breaks — because on a 2% net margin there is no operating leverage to offset volume decline, and the buyback only works while free cash flow holds.
02
The Albertsons litigation lands badly. Albertsons is seeking the $600M termination fee plus unspecified damages in the Delaware Court of Chancery, with the case still in discovery and no trial date stated on any source fetched. An adverse outcome would consume a meaningful fraction of a year's free cash flow and, worse, would validate the allegation that Kroger did not genuinely pursue antitrust clearance — which has implications for how regulators and counterparties treat the Giant Eagle deal.
03
Capital allocation repeats itself. The $2.6B automation write-off in Q3 fiscal 2025 was a full impairment of a multi-year strategic investment. The same framework is now deploying $1.65B on Giant Eagle, closing in 2027 with divestitures attached. Integration risk in grocery is not theoretical — it is labour contracts, banner conversions and distribution rerouting, all executed on a 2% margin with no room for error.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2% margin ceiling
Kroger's trailing net profit margin is 0.73% and the entire Grocery Stores industry averages 0.56%. Even in a clean year — FY2024, ended Feb 1, 2025 — Kroger netted $2.645B on $147.12B, or 1.80%. This is not underperformance; it is the structure of selling perishable goods at high frequency against competitors who will match any price cut. It means every operating decision is a decision about a handful of basis points, and it is why gross margin rate ex-fuel and the OG&A rate are the two numbers management talks about first. In Q2 FY2026 those went in opposite directions: gross margin +13bps, OG&A +33bps. That 20bp net deterioration on ~$30B of ex-fuel sales is roughly $60M a quarter — real money against $1.076B of adjusted FIFO operating profit.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Policy inside the comp
The Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare drug-price negotiation is costing Kroger 138 basis points of identical sales — more than seven times the 0.2% comp it actually printed. Accelerating generic conversion adds over 50bps. Neither reflects a single lost customer; both are price effects on a pharmacy business that remains a traffic driver and, per management, a favourable margin mix. The analytical point generalises: in a business this thin, the reported top line is a poor proxy for the health of the franchise, and the gap between "sales down" and "business worse" has rarely been wider than it is in grocery right now.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Retail media as the second P&L
Kroger Precision Marketing profit grew 24% in a quarter when identical sales grew 0.2%, and eCommerce reached profitability for a second consecutive quarter when media revenue is included. Bull framing: a near-100%-incremental-margin advertising business bolted onto 403,000 employees' worth of physical distribution is a genuine re-rating candidate — it is why the gross margin rate ex-fuel rose in a flat-volume quarter, and it is the only mechanism by which a 2%-margin retailer becomes something else. Bear framing: media is currently plugging a hole rather than compounding on top of a healthy core; if the qualifier "including retail media" is doing the work in the eCommerce profitability claim, then the flywheel is a crutch. Both readings are consistent with the same disclosed facts, which is precisely why the Street is split 11 Strong Buy to 13 Hold.
