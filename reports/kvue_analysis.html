01 Company Overview

Kenvue is the consumer-health business Johnson & Johnson separated out in 2023 — the Tylenol, Neutrogena, Band-Aid, Listerine, Aveeno, Zyrtec and Johnson's portfolio, carved away from J&J's pharmaceutical and medtech engine and floated on the NYSE on May 4, 2023 at $22.00 a share. It sells over-the-counter medicines, skin and hair care and everyday health staples in essentially every market on earth, with roughly 22,000 employees run out of Summit, New Jersey. FY2025 revenue was $15.12B, down 2.1% from $15.46B in FY2024; the trailing-twelve-month figure has since recovered to $15.41B, up 1.8%. A market capitalisation of $34.17B places Kenvue around #742 globally.

The central debate on this page is unusual, because for a Kenvue shareholder today the fundamentals are arguably the second-order question. On November 2, 2025 Kenvue and Kimberly-Clark signed a definitive merger agreement; both shareholder bases approved it on January 29, 2026; the US Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period expired on February 4, 2026; and the companies expect to close in Q4 2026, subject to remaining foreign regulatory approvals. Kenvue shareholders are contracted to receive 0.14625 Kimberly-Clark shares plus $3.50 in cash per Kenvue share, ending up with roughly 46% of the combined company on a fully diluted basis. That means KVUE today trades substantially as a merger-arbitrage instrument stapled to Kimberly-Clark's share price, with an overlay of unresolved litigation. This report covers the operating business honestly — because if the deal breaks, that business is what is left — while being explicit about which numbers are really driving the quote.

A note on what this page does not claim. Two live issues touch Kenvue directly, and both are reported here only as the sources fetched actually state them. First, the acetaminophen litigation: a Yahoo Finance article dated September 7, 2026 states that "the Second Circuit vacated the prior acetaminophen judgment in July 2026 and remanded the litigation for further proceedings." That is a procedural development — a vacated judgment and a remand — and this page does not characterise any outcome, liability or damages beyond it. Second, the same article states Kenvue retains responsibility for talc-related liabilities outside the US and Canada. Neither Kenvue's Q2 2026 earnings release nor its Q2 trade-press coverage, as fetched, addressed the acetaminophen litigation at all.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
CENTURY-OLD BRAND EQUITY PHARMACY SHELF SPACE FDA MONOGRAPH / REGULATORY MOAT GLOBAL DISTRIBUTION SCALE 58% GROSS MARGIN NON-DISCRETIONARY DEMAND 0.43 BETA

The core advantage is that Kenvue owns a set of nouns. Band-Aid is what people call an adhesive bandage; Tylenol is what a large share of Americans call acetaminophen. That linguistic capture translates directly into pricing power and shelf position — retailers cannot build a pain-relief aisle without the anchor brand, which in turn funds the advertising that maintains the capture. The financial signature of the moat is visible in the numbers: a 58.4% gross margin on products that are chemically commoditised, a 19.6% operating margin, a beta of 0.43 reflecting demand that does not track the business cycle, and ROIC of 12.35% against a capital base that is mostly goodwill and brands rather than factories. The regulatory layer reinforces it: OTC drug approval is slow and expensive, and Kenvue's new fixed-dose Tylenol/naproxen combination carries protection through 2029.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Litigation overhang the company does not control. The acetaminophen litigation is live: per a September 7, 2026 Yahoo Finance article, the Second Circuit vacated the prior acetaminophen judgment in July 2026 and remanded for further proceedings. That is a reopening, not a resolution, and nothing on this page should be read as predicting an outcome. Separately, the same source states Kenvue retains talc-related liabilities outside the US and Canada. For a company with $34B of market value and $1.66B of TTM net income, an open-ended legal tail is a structurally different kind of risk from a competitive one — it is not manageable by operating better.
02
Volume is not growing in the biggest segment. Self Care, 40% of the mix, posted −0.6% volume in Q2 2026 and grew only because price went up 1.2%. Price-led growth in a category with credible private-label substitutes has a ceiling, and consumers trading down at the pharmacy shelf is exactly the behaviour that shows up as negative volume first and negative organic growth second.
03
Merger execution and break risk. The Kimberly-Clark transaction has shareholder approval and US antitrust clearance but still needs foreign regulatory approvals to close in Q4 2026. Because the consideration is mostly stock — 0.14625 KMB shares plus $3.50 cash — KVUE holders carry Kimberly-Clark's equity risk without owning it yet, and a break would return them to a standalone consumer-health company that has spent a year not guiding, not investing for independence, and losing management attention to integration planning.
04
Margin pressure from tariffs and input inflation. The Q2 2026 release attributes gross-margin decline to "inflation, tariffs, as well as unfavorable transactional foreign exchange." The Yahoo Finance piece quantifies roughly $80M in annualised tariff exposure. Against a $250M restructuring programme targeting ~$200M of annualised savings, the tariff line eats a meaningful fraction of the self-help before it reaches the P&L.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The operating business is genuinely turning: three consecutive quarters of organic growth, gross margin up from 55.96% in FY2023 to 58.44% TTM, ROIC of 12.35%, and Skin Health and Beauty finally growing on both price and volume with Neutrogena gaining household penetration for a third straight quarter. Forward P/E of 14.7× against trailing 20.9× says the market already accepts that restructuring charges are masking earnings power. And the downside is unusually well-defined: a signed merger with shareholder approval, US antitrust clearance and a Q4 2026 target close, at 0.14625 Kimberly-Clark shares plus $3.50 cash, with an analyst low target of $18.00 sitting above the current $17.79 price.

Bear Case

You are buying a legal tail with a consumer-goods business attached. The acetaminophen judgment was vacated and remanded in July 2026 — the litigation is open, not closed — and Kenvue retains ex-US/Canada talc liabilities. Meanwhile 40% of revenue (Self Care) is growing on price while losing volume, Essential Health is buying volume by conceding price, the payout ratio is 98.82% with a current ratio of 1.01, and ~$80M of annualised tariff exposure eats a large slice of the $200M restructuring benefit. If the merger breaks on a foreign regulatory objection, holders are returned to a standalone company that has spent a year not guiding and not being managed for independence, with the $14.02 low only twelve months behind it.

Industry Commentary
Consumer-health consolidation has reached its endgame
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Kenvue exists because Johnson & Johnson decided in 2023 that consumer health did not belong inside a pharmaceutical company. Three years later, Kimberly-Clark has decided it does belong inside a household-products company — the merger agreement signed November 2, 2025 values the combination such that Kenvue holders take roughly 46% of the combined entity. That round trip is the industry's verdict on standalone consumer health at Kenvue's scale: $15.1B of revenue growing at roughly 1–2% organic does not support the marketing, innovation and litigation-absorption budget that global OTC and skin-care brands require on their own. The strategic-review question the market asked through 2024 and 2025 has been answered, and the answer was sale rather than separation. Note precisely what this page does not say: no separation or break-up of the Kenvue portfolio into its three segments is documented in any source fetched for this report — the transaction on the table is a whole-company merger.

Tariffs and input inflation are compressing a margin that was just repaired
ONGOING HEADWIND

Kenvue spent two years rebuilding gross margin from 55.96% (FY2023) to 58.37% (FY2025) — a 241-basis-point repair achieved through pricing and supply-chain work. Q2 2026 gave some of it back: reported gross margin of 58.2%, with the release attributing the decline to "inflation, tariffs, as well as unfavorable transactional foreign exchange." The quantified tariff drag is roughly $80M annualised, against a $250M restructuring charge intended to produce $200M of annualised savings. In other words, close to half the self-help programme's run-rate benefit is being consumed by trade policy the company cannot influence. For a business whose products are manufactured and sold across every major region, transactional FX compounds the problem: the gap between reported and organic growth in Q2 2026 was positive (currency helped the top line) while FX simultaneously hurt the margin — a translation tailwind masking a transaction headwind.

What the two cases actually hinge on
EMERGING TAILWIND

Strip the noise and the bull and bear cases for KVUE at $17.79 turn on one binary and one trend. The binary is whether the Kimberly-Clark merger closes in Q4 2026 as planned. If it does, the KVUE price today is simply a discounted claim on 0.14625 KMB shares plus $3.50 — the analyst low target of $18.00 above the market price implies the desk thinks that discount is modest and the probability high. If it breaks on a foreign regulatory objection, the floor that has held since November 2025 disappears and the $14.02 fifty-two-week low becomes relevant again. The trend is volume. Kenvue grew net sales 3.0% in Q2 2026, but only Skin Health and Beauty grew units; Self Care lost 0.6% of volume and Essential Health bought its 1.9% volume gain with 0.8% of price. A consumer-staples business that grows by charging more for fewer units is not compounding — it is harvesting. The bull needs the Tylenol/naproxen launch and the Neutrogena penetration gains to convert into positive volume across the portfolio. The bear only needs the status quo to persist, plus one adverse turn in a litigation that was reopened in July 2026.
