01 Company Overview

In 1946 Laurence Tisch talked his parents into putting $125,000 into a resort hotel in Lakewood, New Jersey. Eighty years later the family's vehicle is a $22.2B holding company that owns three things you can name and one you probably can't: about 92% of CNA Financial, a large US commercial property-and-casualty insurer; 100% of Boardwalk Pipelines, a natural-gas and liquids pipeline and storage business; 100% of Loews Hotels & Co, including its joint-venture hotels at Universal Orlando; and about 53% of Altium Packaging, a maker of rigid plastic packaging. Along the way it has sold or spun off tobacco (Lorillard, 2008), watches (Bulova, 2008), a CBS stake (1995) and gas exploration (HighMount, 2014). Offshore drilling went differently: Diamond Offshore filed for Chapter 11 in April 2020 and came off Loews’s books, and Noble absorbed it in September 2024.

That history is the product. Loews is not really an insurer or a pipeline company; it is a capital allocator with a controlling family that buys assets when they are unloved, sells them when they are not, and — in between — shrinks its own share count. Shares outstanding fell from 248.42M at the end of 2021 to 202.9M at September 30, 2026, an 18.3% reduction, while book value per share rose from $71.84 to $90.24. The stock sits at $109.71, 1.22x book and 1.09x book excluding AOCI. The central question for this stock has never changed: how wide is the gap between what the parts are worth and what the market pays for the whole — and is management closing it by buying back stock below that value?

The third quarter, reported on November 2, 2026, was the weakest of the year. Net income fell to $306M ($1.50 a share) from $504M ($2.43), almost all of it at CNA, whose contribution dropped to $180M from $371M after a $125M after-tax charge from its annual review of long-term care reserves (old policies that pay for nursing-home and home care). The company says higher bond yields should almost entirely offset that charge over time. Boardwalk earned $85M (vs $94M) and Loews Hotels $16M (vs a $3M loss). There was no numerical guidance. The results came out before the market opened; the stock ended that day up 0.02% and fell 1.23% the next day, November 3.

The market is not giving Boardwalk and the hotels away. But it is pricing them without much enthusiasm — and every share the parent buys back at this price buys a slice of CNA, the cash and the unquoted businesses at the same time.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Permanent, family-controlled capital Parent net cash ~$2.6B Insurance float (CNA) Pipeline assets hard to replicate Universal Orlando JV position Long buyback record (−18.3% shares since 2021)

The moat here is not any single business — it is the structure. A controlling family with a long horizon can hold a cyclical insurer through bad underwriting years, wait for gas pipelines to re-contract at better rates, and fund hotel development without a quarterly vote from the market. CNA's insurance float — premiums collected now and paid out as claims later, invested in between — adds a steady stream of investment income. The parent's $4.4B of cash and investments against $1.8B of debt means it is never a forced seller. The vulnerability is the mirror image: nobody outside the family can make management close the discount, so the discount can outlast your holding period.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
CNA's underwriting is slipping. Nine-month 2026 combined ratio 98.6% versus 95.1% a year earlier; underlying 94.4% versus 91.7%. Q1 included $100M of unfavourable prior-year development, which CNA tied to excess casualty (liability cover that sits above a primary policy) and professional liability claims from recent years; in Q3 those lines were unfavourable again but offset by better results elsewhere, leaving prior-year development flat on the combined ratio. The earnings remarks also describe a competitive market with no meaningful pricing improvement expected near term.
02
Concentration in one quoted asset. Roughly $58 of the $109.71 share price is simply the CNA stake at its market price. A 10% move in CNA's shares moves Loews's value by about $5.83 per share before anything else changes.
03
The discount has no expiry date. Holding-company discounts close through break-ups, sales or relentless buybacks. The family controls the first two and has so far chosen the third. That works — but slowly, and only while the parent keeps generating cash to buy with.
04
Almost no Wall Street analysts cover it. MarketBeat tracks a single rating (Weiss Ratings) and no price target; StockAnalysis shows no analyst estimates. Fewer eyes means fewer outside voices pushing the price up or down — and no analyst is pushing the case for closing the discount.
05
Long-term care reserves. CNA's annual review raised these reserves by $173M before tax in Q3 2026, a $125M hit to Loews. Management says higher bond yields should offset the charge over time and that reserves have plateaued; if claim costs rise faster than assumed, the charge can repeat.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $109.71 you are paying 1.09x book ex-AOCI for a company that has grown reported book value per share by 26% since 2021 while retiring 18.3% of its shares. Strip out the CNA stake at market (~$58) and parent net cash (~$13) and the market is valuing Boardwalk, Loews Hotels and the Altium stake at about $7.8B of equity — for businesses that produced $1.25B of EBITDA in the first nine months of 2026, with hotels growing adjusted EBITDA 25–53% a quarter. Every buyback at this discount increases the per-share claim on those assets. The family owns about a fifth of the company and has every incentive to keep doing it.

Bear Case

More than half the value is one listed insurer whose underwriting is deteriorating — combined ratio 98.6% for the nine months versus 95.1%, with $100M of extra reserves for claims from recent years in Q1 and a $173M pre-tax long-term care reserve increase in Q3. Single-digit ROE (8.21%) is exactly what earns a ~1x book multiple, so the "discount" may be a fair price. Holding-company discounts can persist for decades; with the family in control there is no activist route to a break-up, no dividend to speak of, and effectively no analyst coverage to push the argument. Trailing EPS has already slipped to $7.23 from $8.16 at the last edition, as the weak Q3 replaced a strong one.

Top 3 Risks

01
CNA reserve and catastrophe risk. The combined ratio moved from 98.4% to 102.2% in Q1, from 94.1% to 96.5% in Q2 and from 92.8% to 97.0% in Q3. Further adverse development in excess casualty or professional liability, or a heavy hurricane quarter, hits both CNA's share price and Loews's earnings at once.
02
Capital-allocation risk through the succession. The thesis relies on management continuing to buy back stock at a discount and not overpay for the next acquisition. The parent holds $4.4B of cash and investments; how it is spent under the new CEO is the long-run swing factor.
03
Rates and credit in the investment portfolio. Loews's group net investment income ($2.09B over nine months, against $2.07B) is cushioning earnings while CNA's underwriting weakens, though it dipped to $713M in Q3 from $743M. Lower yields or credit losses would remove the cushion; higher yields cut book value through AOCI, as 2022 showed.
Industry Commentary
Commercial P&C: reserves on recent casualty years
KEY CONSTRAINT

CNA's 2026 shows the usual pattern when liability claims run ahead of pricing: premiums still growing (net written +3% in Q3), investment income steady, but claims from recent years in excess casualty and professional liability coming in above what was reserved in Q1 and unfavourable again in Q3 (offset by better results elsewhere). The underlying combined ratio was up 2.9 points year-on-year in Q3 (94.2% vs 91.3%) and 2.7 points for nine months (94.4% vs 91.7%), and the earnings remarks describe a competitive market. For Loews this is the main earnings risk, because CNA is 61% of the operating subsidiaries' nine-month contribution.

Gas transportation: re-contracting at higher rates
STEADY TAILWIND

Boardwalk's first-half growth came from higher contracting rates on gas transportation, per the company; in Q3 higher operating expenses outweighed it, while the revenue backlog rose by nearly $600M. Pipeline revenue is mostly contracted, so it moves slowly in both directions — which is exactly what a holding company with a cyclical insurer wants beside it.

The holding-company discount — bull and bear framing
THE CORE DEBATE

The bull framing: Loews is a discount that management is actively harvesting — 18.3% fewer shares since 2021, book value per share up 26%, and a parent balance sheet with $2.6B of net cash to keep going. The bear framing: a holding company that earns an 8% ROE, pays almost no dividend and cannot be forced to break itself up deserves to trade near book, and it does. The way to settle it is not the P/E; it is the trend in book value per share ex-AOCI ($95.89 at year-end 2025 → $97.20 → $99.27 → $100.67 at the last three quarter-ends) against the share price. If the first keeps rising and the second doesn't, the discount widens — and the buyback becomes more valuable.
