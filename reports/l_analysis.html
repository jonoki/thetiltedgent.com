01 Company Overview

In 1946 Laurence Tisch talked his parents into putting $125,000 into a resort hotel in Lakewood, New Jersey. Eighty years later the family's vehicle is a $21.8B holding company that owns three things you can name and one you probably can't: about 92% of CNA Financial, a large US commercial property-and-casualty insurer; 100% of Boardwalk Pipelines, a natural-gas and liquids pipeline and storage business; 100% of Loews Hotels & Co, including its joint-venture hotels at Universal Orlando; and, per Wikipedia, a 53% stake in Altium Packaging. Along the way it has sold or spun off tobacco (Lorillard, 2007), watches (Bulova, 2007), a CBS stake (1995), gas exploration (HighMount, 2014) and offshore drilling (Diamond Offshore, absorbed by Noble in September 2024).

That history is the product. Loews is not really an insurer or a pipeline company; it is a capital allocator with a controlling family that buys assets when they are unloved, sells them when they are not, and — in between — shrinks its own share count. Shares outstanding fell from 248.42M at the end of 2021 to 204.43M at June 30, 2026, a 17.7% reduction, while book value per share rose from $71.84 to $93.52. The stock sits at $106.75, just 1.14x book and 1.08x book excluding AOCI. The central question for this stock has never changed: how wide is the gap between what the parts are worth and what the market pays for the whole — and is management closing it by buying back stock below that value?

Sum of the parts: what the market is paying for

The quick way to read a holding company is to value what's quoted and see what's left for the rest. Everything below uses figures from sources fetched this session; the arithmetic is ours, not the company's.

~$58.19 / sh
92% × $12.93B CNA market cap = $11.90B, ÷ 204.43M Loews shares. Moves one-for-one with CNA's share price.

~$12.72 / sh

Implied for everything else
~$35.84 / sh
$106.75 − $58.19 − $12.72 = $35.84, or about $7.3B of equity value for Boardwalk, Loews Hotels and the Altium stake combined. Those two businesses produced $900M of EBITDA in H1 2026 alone ($639M + $261M) — before their own debt, which was not retrieved.
Book value per share
$93.52
$99.27 excluding AOCI (June 30, 2026), up from $95.89 at year-end 2025. Price-to-book ex-AOCI: $106.75 ÷ $99.27 = 1.08x.

Read the implied figure with care: it is equity value, and the subsidiaries carry their own borrowings (consolidated debt is $8.94B against $1.8B at the parent). The market is not giving Boardwalk and the hotels away. But it is pricing them without much enthusiasm — and every share the parent buys back at this price buys a slice of CNA, the cash and the unquoted businesses at the same time.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Permanent, family-controlled capital Parent net cash ~$2.6B Insurance float (CNA) Pipeline assets hard to replicate Universal Orlando JV position Long buyback record (−17.7% shares since 2021)

The moat here is not any single business — it is the structure. A controlling family with a long horizon can hold a cyclical insurer through bad underwriting years, wait for gas pipelines to re-contract at better rates, and fund hotel development without a quarterly vote from the market. The parent's $4.4B of cash and investments against $1.8B of debt means it is never a forced seller. The vulnerability is the mirror image: nobody outside the family can make management close the discount, so the discount can outlast your holding period.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
CNA's underwriting is slipping. H1 2026 combined ratio 99.4% versus 96.3% a year earlier; underlying 94.5% versus 92.0%. Q1 included $100M of unfavourable prior-year development, which CNA tied to excess casualty and professional liability lines in recent accident years. Reserve additions on recent years are the kind of news that tends to repeat.
02
Concentration in one quoted asset. Roughly $58 of the $106.75 share price is simply the CNA stake marked to market. A 10% move in CNA's shares moves Loews's value by about $5.80 per share before anything else changes.
03
The discount has no expiry date. Holding-company discounts close through break-ups, sales or relentless buybacks. The family controls the first two and has so far chosen the third. That works — but slowly, and only while the parent keeps generating cash to buy with.
04
Almost no sell-side coverage. MarketBeat tracks a single rating (Weiss Ratings) and no price target; StockAnalysis shows no analyst estimates. Fewer eyes means fewer forced re-ratings — the discount has no natural catalyst from the Street.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $53.93 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $106.75, Loews roughly doubled — +97.9% on price. The dividend adds almost nothing ($0.25 a year); the return came from book value compounding and the share count shrinking underneath it. The path was unusually smooth for a stock with an insurer inside it: one sharp drawdown into September 2022 ($49.84, the low of the window), then a long, stair-stepped climb to a $116.01 month-end high in July 2026.

The 6-period monthly RSI spent much of 2024–2025 above 70 — a stock grinding higher with few down months — and peaked at 88.2 in March 2024. It has now cooled to 49.8, neutral, after the August and September pullback. The 10-month moving average is about $108.81, just above the current price: the long uptrend has paused rather than broken, and the stock is sitting on the underside of its own trend line for the first time in a while.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $106.75 you are paying 1.08x book ex-AOCI for a company that has compounded book value per share by 30% since 2021 while retiring 17.7% of its shares. Strip out the CNA stake at market (~$58) and parent net cash (~$13) and the market is valuing Boardwalk, Loews Hotels and the Altium stake at about $7.3B of equity — for businesses that produced $900M of EBITDA in the first half of 2026 alone, with hotels growing EBITDA 26–53% a quarter. Every buyback at this discount increases the per-share claim on those assets. The family owns about a fifth of the company and has every incentive to keep doing it.

Bear Case

More than half the value is one listed insurer whose underwriting is deteriorating — combined ratio 99.4% for the half versus 96.3%, with $100M of adverse reserve development on recent accident years. Single-digit ROE (9.31%) is exactly what earns a ~1x book multiple, so the "discount" may be a fair price. Holding-company discounts can persist for decades; with the family in control there is no activist route to a break-up, no dividend to speak of, and effectively no analyst coverage to push the argument. TTM EPS growth of 30% flatters an easy comparison and is unlikely to repeat at that rate.

Top 3 Risks

01
CNA reserve and catastrophe risk. The combined ratio moved from 98.4% to 102.2% in Q1 and from 94.1% to 96.5% in Q2. Further adverse development in excess casualty or professional liability, or a heavy hurricane quarter, hits both CNA's share price and Loews's earnings at once.
02
Capital-allocation risk through the succession. The thesis relies on management continuing to buy back stock at a discount and not overpay for the next acquisition. The parent holds $4.4B of cash and investments; how it is spent under the new CEO is the long-run swing factor.
03
Rates and credit in the investment portfolio. Net investment income ($761M in Q2 2026) is carrying CNA's earnings growth while underwriting weakens. Lower yields or credit losses would remove the cushion; higher yields cut book value through AOCI, as 2022 showed.
Industry Commentary
Commercial P&C: reserves on recent casualty years
KEY CONSTRAINT

CNA's 2026 is a textbook casualty-cycle quarter pair: premiums still growing (net written +4%), investment income rising, but losses on recent accident years in excess casualty and professional liability coming in above what was reserved. Underlying combined ratio up 2.5 points year-on-year in Q2 and for the half. For Loews this is the main earnings risk, because CNA is 59% of the operating subsidiaries' contribution.

Gas transportation: re-contracting at higher rates
STEADY TAILWIND

Boardwalk's growth in both 2026 quarters came from higher contracting rates on gas transportation, per the company. Pipeline revenue is mostly contracted, so it moves slowly in both directions — which is exactly what a holding company with a cyclical insurer wants beside it.

The holding-company discount — bull and bear framing
THE CORE DEBATE

The bull framing: Loews is a discount that management is actively harvesting — 17.7% fewer shares since 2021, book value per share up 30%, and a parent balance sheet with $2.6B of net cash to keep going. The bear framing: a holding company that earns a 9% ROE, pays almost no dividend and cannot be forced to break itself up deserves to trade near book, and it does. The way to adjudicate is not the P/E; it is the trend in book value per share ex-AOCI ($95.89 → $97.20 → $99.27 over the last three reported quarter-ends) against the share price. If the first keeps rising and the second doesn't, the discount widens — and the buyback becomes more valuable.
