01 Company Overview

Every time a doctor orders a blood panel, a thyroid test or an A1C, somebody has to draw the tube, drive it to a lab, run it and send back a number. Labcorp is one of the companies that does that at national scale. It also runs a second, smaller business: central-lab and drug-development testing for pharma and biotech. FY2025 revenue was $13.95B, up 7.25%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $10.46 and adjusted EPS of $16.44.

The company you see today is the product of two structural changes, and both matter for anything you read off a long-run chart. On June 30, 2023 Labcorp spun off its clinical-development business, Fortrea (now NASDAQ: FTRE), handing shareholders one Fortrea share for every Labcorp share; Fortrea paid Labcorp about $1.6B in cash at closing, most of which went into buybacks and debt paydown. Then on May 17, 2024 it completed a holding-company reorganisation: the new parent, Labcorp Holdings Inc., became the successor issuer to Laboratory Corporation of America Holdings, still under the ticker LH. The reorganisation left no price adjustment. The spin-off did — see the note on the chart in Section 07.

The story now is a steady business that keeps beating its own plan. On October 28, 2026 Labcorp reported third-quarter revenue of $3.81B, up 6.9%, and adjusted EPS (earnings per share before one-off and acquisition-related items) of $4.78, above the $4.65 analysts expected on Nasdaq's consensus. It raised its 2026 revenue and adjusted EPS guidance and signed three more lab acquisitions. The stock rose 8.8% that day and closed at $368.16 the next, 0.8% below the $371.00 52-week intraday high set on October 28. The cloud over it is Medicare: on September 21 CMS, the agency that runs Medicare, proposed 2027 lab payment rates that lower what it pays for most tests, and the CFO puts the 2027 impact at about $125 million before any offset.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

National logistics & draw-site network Payer contracts & in-network status Scale cost per test Hospital outreach-lab roll-up Specialty test menu $8.92B biopharma backlog

The moat is density. A lab test is a low-price, high-volume product, and the fixed costs sit in couriers, draw sites, instruments and payer contracting. The operator with the most tubes per route has the lowest cost per result. The acquisition engine is how Labcorp keeps adding density: a hospital sells its outreach lab and gets cash plus a service contract, and Labcorp gets volume it can run through a network that already exists. The weak spot is price. Much of that price is set by Medicare and commercial payers rather than by Labcorp, which leaves the company exposed to fee-schedule decisions it does not control.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reimbursement is set by others. Medicare, Medicaid and commercial payers are named clients. Their fee schedules decide much of the "price" in price/mix, so a cut would go straight to margin. On September 21, 2026 CMS proposed 2027 Medicare rates that lower payment on 1,171 of about 1,500 lab test codes, with any one test's cut capped at 15% a year through 2029; final rates are due in November. Labcorp said in an 8-K the next day that the data behind the rates do not fully reflect the commercial lab market.
02
Biopharma cyclicality. BLS depends on drug-company R&D budgets and biotech funding. Orders are strong now (book-to-bill 1.13 in Q3), but Early Development revenue grew only 0.4% in the quarter.
03
Leverage from deals. Debt is 2.56 times a year's EBITDA (operating profit before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation), and net debt is about $6.51B on StockAnalysis's measure; the Q3 release shows $5.85B of debt against $261.8M of cash. Three more acquisitions are waiting to close, and a company that grows partly by acquisition needs its balance sheet to stay open.
04
GAAP versus adjusted. Q3 2026 diluted EPS was $3.22 while adjusted EPS was $4.78. A 48% gap is large, and it is repeated every quarter. The P/E in this report uses GAAP.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

This is a boring business in the good sense. Revenue grew 6.9% in Q3 2026, and full-year guidance was raised to 5.9–6.7% growth. Margins are rising in both segments (Dx +0.4 points, BLS +1.8 points in Q3), biopharma orders outran revenue (book-to-bill 1.13) and backlog is $8.92B. Management has a 2029 plan for 8.5–11.5% compound adjusted EPS growth and signed three more acquisitions it expects to add to adjusted EPS in their first year. At 19.9x FY2026 consensus adjusted earnings, a stock with a 0.85 beta that grows adjusted EPS at a low double-digit rate is not expensive. UBS, Leerink and Baird raised targets to $396–$420 after the print.

Bear Case

On GAAP earnings the stock is at 30.2x, and the gap between GAAP ($12.20 TTM) and adjusted earnings is wide and recurring. Medicare's proposed 2027 lab rates would cut payment on most test codes; the CFO puts the 2027 impact at about $125 million before offsets, and 2027 guidance does not come until February. After a +19.5% October the monthly RSI is above 70, and the average analyst target ($376.88) is only 2.4% above the price. Early Development revenue in biopharma grew just 0.4% in Q3.

Top 3 Risks

01
Reimbursement. Government and commercial payers set much of the price of a routine test. Organic price/mix added 1.3 points of Dx's 4.4% organic growth in Q3 2026, and CMS has proposed lower 2027 Medicare rates on most lab tests, so a fee-schedule cut would hit growth and margin at once.
02
Biopharma demand. Orders are strong now (book-to-bill 1.13 in Q3, 1.10 over twelve months), but the business depends on drug-company research budgets. A slowdown in trial starts would show up in BLS first, and that is the segment carrying the fastest margin expansion.
03
Valuation after the run. The stock has risen from $308.11 at the September month-end to $368.16, close to the $376.88 average target. With final Medicare rates due in November and 2027 guidance not until February, there is not much in the price to absorb a disappointment.
Industry Commentary
Hospital outreach labs: the consolidation runway
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

The quietest part of Labcorp's growth is also the most repeatable. Hospitals run "outreach" labs that test samples from outside physicians. For many systems these are sub-scale, and selling them to a national lab turns fixed cost into cash. Labcorp signed or closed 13 transactions with health systems and regional or local labs in 2025, acquisitions added +2.2% to Dx growth in Q3 2026, and after the quarter it signed three more deals: select assets of BioReference Health's lab testing business, Myriad Genetics' prenatal testing business and Millennium Health. Every deal adds volume to routes and instruments that are already paid for, which is how you get margin expansion from a business that doesn't raise prices much.

Specialty testing and AI: where the 2029 margin has to come from
EXECUTION TEST

Routine tests are a volume business at payer-set prices, and Medicare's proposed 2027 rates are a reminder of who sets them. The Investor Day plan leans on the other end: specialty tests in oncology, neurology, autoimmune disease and women's health (Labcorp launched over 130 new tests in 2025), plus AI and robotics in the labs. Specialty work lifts price/mix, and automation cuts cost per test. Delivering 0.75–1.5 percentage points of margin by 2029 needs both.
