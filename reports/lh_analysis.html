01 Company Overview

Every time a doctor orders a blood panel, a thyroid test or an A1C, somebody has to draw the tube, drive it to a lab, run it and send back a number. Labcorp is one of the companies that does that at national scale. It also runs a second, smaller business: central-lab and drug-development testing for pharma and biotech. FY2025 revenue was $13.95B, up 7.25%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $10.46 and adjusted EPS of $16.44.

The company you see today is the product of two structural changes, and both matter for anything you read off a long-run chart. On June 30, 2023 Labcorp spun off its clinical-development business, Fortrea (now NASDAQ: FTRE), handing shareholders one Fortrea share for every Labcorp share; Fortrea paid Labcorp about $1.6B in cash at closing, most of which went into buybacks and debt paydown. Then on May 17, 2024 it completed a holding-company reorganisation: the new parent, Labcorp Holdings Inc., became the successor issuer to Laboratory Corporation of America Holdings, still under the ticker LH. The reorganisation left no price adjustment. The spin-off did — see the note on the chart in Section 07.

The story now is a steady compounder asking the market for a higher multiple. At the September 10, 2026 Investor Day management put out a 2029 outlook of 5–8% compound revenue growth and 8.5–11.5% compound adjusted EPS growth, and the stock fell 4.4% that day. It has since clawed back to $320.99, 6.1% below the $341.80 52-week high set on August 27.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

National logistics & draw-site network Payer contracts & in-network status Scale cost per test Hospital outreach-lab roll-up Specialty test menu $8.73B biopharma backlog

The moat is density. A lab test is a low-price, high-volume product, and the fixed costs sit in couriers, draw sites, instruments and payer contracting. The operator with the most tubes per route has the lowest cost per result, and that is why scale players such as Labcorp and Quest dominate independent lab testing (our reading). The acquisition engine is how Labcorp keeps adding density: a hospital sells its outreach lab and gets cash plus a service contract, and Labcorp gets volume it can run through a network that already exists. The weak spot is price. Much of that price is set by Medicare and commercial payers rather than by Labcorp, which leaves the company exposed to fee-schedule decisions it does not control.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reimbursement is set by others. Medicare, Medicaid and commercial payers are named clients. Their fee schedules decide much of the "price" in price/mix, so a cut would go straight to margin. No specific pending rate change was checked for this report.
02
Biopharma cyclicality. BLS depends on drug-company R&D budgets and biotech funding. Book-to-bill of 1.03 leaves little cushion.
03
Leverage from deals. Debt/EBITDA is 2.61x and net debt is about $6.68B on StockAnalysis's measure, which appears to include leases. A company that grows partly by acquisition needs its balance sheet to stay open.
04
GAAP versus adjusted. Q2 2026 diluted EPS was $3.64 while adjusted EPS was $4.99. A 37% gap is large, and it is repeated every quarter. The P/E in this report uses GAAP.

Notable One-Off Items

Two things distort the per-share history. First, the Fortrea spin-off: figures before 2023 include the clinical-development business that left in June 2023. StockAnalysis shows FY2021 revenue of $13.14B (apparently restated to continuing operations) next to FY2021 EPS of $24.39 and net income of $2.38B, which appear to include the discontinued business. They also sit at the peak of pandemic testing. Read FY2021 against FY2025 and you will think earnings halved. They did not in any like-for-like sense. Second, the gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings: FY2025 GAAP EPS was $10.46 and adjusted was $16.44. The trailing P/E of 26.6 and the forward P/E of 17.5 differ mostly because one is GAAP and the other is adjusted, not because of growth.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

This is a boring business in the good sense. Revenue grew 7.25% in FY2025 and is guided to 5.4–6.3% in 2026. Margins are rising in both segments (Dx +50bps, BLS +130bps in Q2), biopharma backlog is $8.73B, and management has published a 2029 plan for 8.5–11.5% compound adjusted EPS growth. At 17.5x FY2026 consensus earnings, a stock with a 0.85 beta that grows EPS at a low double-digit rate and buys back stock is not expensive. Analysts raised targets straight after Investor Day, and UBS and Truist now sit at $380 and $365.

Bear Case

On GAAP earnings the stock is at 26.6x, and the gap between GAAP ($12.08 TTM) and adjusted earnings is wide and recurring. The Investor Day told the market nothing new, and the stock fell 4.4%. The 2029 targets are a range whose low end (5% revenue, 8.5% EPS) is roughly what the business already does. After a +18.8% June-to-August run the RSI is near overbought, the average target is only 7% above the price, and BLS book-to-bill of 1.03 leaves little margin if biopharma spending softens.

Top 3 Risks

01
Reimbursement. Government and commercial payers set much of the price of a routine test. Price/mix contributed half of Dx organic growth in Q2 2026, so a fee-schedule cut would hit growth and margin at once.
02
Biopharma demand. Book-to-bill of 1.03 is barely above replacement. A slowdown in trial starts would show up in BLS first, and that is the segment carrying the fastest margin expansion.
03
Valuation after the run. The stock has risen from $280.00 to $320.99 since the June month-end, with the average target at $343.31. If Q3 on October 27 is merely in line, there is not much in the price to absorb it.
Industry Commentary
Hospital outreach labs: the consolidation runway
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

The quietest part of Labcorp's growth is also the most repeatable. Hospitals run "outreach" labs that test samples from outside physicians. For many systems these are sub-scale, and selling them to a national lab turns fixed cost into cash. Labcorp signed or closed 13 such transactions in 2025, and acquisitions added +1.9% to Dx growth in Q2 2026. Every deal adds volume to routes and instruments that are already paid for, which is how you get margin expansion from a business that doesn't raise prices much.

Specialty testing and AI: where the 2029 margin has to come from
EXECUTION TEST

Routine tests are a volume business at payer-set prices. The Investor Day plan leans on the other end: specialty tests in oncology, neurology, autoimmune disease and women's health (Labcorp launched over 130 new tests in 2025), plus AI and robotics in the labs. Specialty work lifts price/mix, and automation cuts cost per test. Delivering 75–150bps of margin by 2029 needs both. The first read on whether it is working is the price/mix line, which was +1.8% in Q2 2026.
