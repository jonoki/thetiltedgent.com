01 Company Overview

L3Harris builds the parts of the defense stack that other primes buy: missile-warning and missile-tracking satellites, tactical radios and electronic-warfare systems, and — since the 2023 Aerojet Rocketdyne acquisition — solid rocket motors and propulsion. FY2025 revenue was $21.87B, up 2.5% from $21.33B; the TTM figure through the July 3, 2026 quarter is $22.93B, up 7.3% YoY, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $23.2–23.7B with ~$3.0B of free cash flow. Backlog stands at a record $42B on a 1.2× book-to-bill. The company ranks roughly #545 globally by market capitalisation.

And yet the stock is the worst-looking chart in large-cap defense. It closed at $248.37 on September 10 — −31.9% from its February 2026 monthly close of $364.54 and sitting on a fresh 52-week low of $246.51 — while the company was raising guidance. That gap is the whole report. Three things opened it: the July 29 EPS beat that the market sold anyway on capital-intensity worries, the indefinite delay of the planned Missile Solutions separation, and the August 17 departure of CEO Christopher Kubasik, who stepped down under a separation agreement following a board investigation into Code of Conduct matters — conduct the company stated was unrelated to its financial reporting, controls, customer relationships or operational performance. Sections 02 and 04 take apart whether the operating business actually deteriorated, or whether only the narrative did.

Solid rocket motors, propulsion, hypersonics and advanced missile technology — the Aerojet Rocketdyne asset bought in 2023, now three years into integration. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOLID ROCKET MOTOR DUOPOLY TACTICAL RADIO INSTALLED BASE PROGRAM-OF-RECORD LOCK-IN CLEARED FACILITIES & WORKFORCE $42B RECORD BACKLOG SDA / GOLDEN DOME PRIME POSITION 24 YEARS OF DIVIDEND GROWTH

The core advantage is that L3Harris sells into two positions almost nobody can contest. Solid rocket motors are effectively a Western duopoly with Northrop Grumman — you cannot enter without years of capital, qualification testing and a security clearance, and the Pentagon's own language, per management, has shifted from "whether we can deliver" to "how many can we produce." Tactical radios are the same idea in software form: waveforms and crypto are certified per platform, so an installed base of fielded radios is a switching cost measured in recertification cycles, not price. That is the mechanism behind CSD's 26.9% margin. Backlog of $42B against guided 2026 revenue of ~$23.45B is roughly 1.8 years of visibility, on a 1.2× book-to-bill — the order book is still growing faster than it is being consumed.

Vulnerability 01
Mix Is Diluting Margin
The fastest-growing segment is the least profitable one. SMS grew H1 revenue 15% at a 10.1% margin while CSD, at 26.0%, grew 3%. Every incremental dollar of proliferated-space revenue drags the blended rate down. Consolidated ROIC of 8.10% against ROE of 9.34% says this is a business earning close to its cost of capital, not comfortably above it.
Vulnerability 02
Capital Intensity & Cash Timing
Quadrupling THAAD and tripling PAC-3 output requires plant that does not generate revenue until late 2027. The market's reaction to the July beat was explicitly about "the size of capital investments." Free cash flow is also violently seasonal — −$194M in Q1 2026, +$771M in Q2 — so the ~$3.0B full-year guide is heavily back-loaded into Q4 and is the single most checkable claim on the page.
Vulnerability 03
Governance Discontinuity
A CEO stepping down under a separation agreement following a board investigation into Code of Conduct matters, mid-transformation, is a real risk even though the company stated the matter was unrelated to its financial reporting, controls, customer relationships or operational performance. The successor is an internal operator who ran 80% of revenue, and the board split the Chair role — both mitigants. But an abrupt exit resets the credibility clock on every multi-year commitment the prior CEO made.
Vulnerability 04
Single-Customer Concentration
The U.S. government is the dominant customer, and the appropriations calendar, continuing resolutions and supplemental funding decide the revenue line. Management expects "the highest defense budget in our country's history and a supplemental budget" for munitions — but that is an expectation about a political process, not a contract. International at 23% of revenue and rising is the diversification, and it is working, from a low base.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has been bought hard enough that momentum traders call it "overbought"; below 30, sold hard enough to be "oversold." It describes the pace of the move, not whether the price is right — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a year.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $230.54 to September 10, 2026's $248.37, LHX returned +7.7% in price — roughly 1.5% a year before dividends. That single number hides two completely different stocks. From late 2021 through September 2023 this was a dead-money defense name grinding from $230 to a cycle low of $174.12, a −24.5% drawdown, while the market questioned whether the Aerojet deal was an expensive distraction. From that low it compounded almost without interruption to a February 2026 monthly close of $364.54 — a +109.4% run on European rearmament, the Golden Dome missile-defense programme and the munitions restocking cycle. Then the last seven months gave back −31.9%, month after month, with the 10-month moving average crossing below price in spring 2026 and never recovering.

What makes the drawdown analytically interesting is that it does not line up with the fundamentals. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The operating business is doing everything asked of it and the stock is down 32%. Record $42B backlog at 1.2× book-to-bill gives ~1.8 years of revenue visibility; guidance was raised, not cut; Q2 free cash flow grew 37%; international revenue is up over 20% to 23% of the total; and L3Harris won all five AMDT-3 missile-tracking contracts against a $9B pipeline. At 20.9× the FY2026 EPS guide midpoint with a 6.5% free cash flow yield on guided ~$3.0B, a 0.96 PEG, a 2.01% yield backed by 24 years of dividend growth and short interest of 1.88%, the market is pricing execution failure in a business whose orders are contractually committed. The $12B seven-year munitions framework at guided 15–18% margins is a mix upgrade that has not yet appeared in reported results.

Bear Case

Growth is coming from the wrong places. SMS — half of revenue — grew 15% in the first half at a 9.8% and falling margin, while the 26.9%-margin radio business grew 3%. That mix drags blended profitability down even when every segment executes, and it shows up in 8.10% ROIC and 9.34% ROE: close to the cost of capital, not above it. The capital to fix it is being spent now for plant that opens in late 2027, while the missile-business separation that would have crystallised value has been shelved indefinitely. A CEO stepping down under a separation agreement after a Code of Conduct investigation adds discontinuity precisely when a multi-year capacity bet needs credible sponsorship. And every dated analyst action in the last month — Melius, Jefferies, Deutsche Bank — was a downgrade or a cut, which makes the $344.54 consensus target look like the last thing to reprice rather than a signal.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Free cash flow misses the ~$3.0B guide. Only $584M of it existed at the half, because Q1 was −$194M. That makes the full-year number a Q3–Q4 event, and free cash flow is the metric this sector's valuation is anchored to. A miss on October 29 would validate exactly the capital-intensity fear that has driven the de-rating, and would hit the multiple harder than an EPS miss.
02
SMS margin keeps eroding. At 9.8% and −60 bps YoY on half the revenue base, proliferated-space fixed-price work is structurally lower-margin than anything else L3Harris does. If it grows at 15% indefinitely, the company's blended margin falls even with flawless execution — and fixed-price contracts mean any cost overrun is absorbed, not billed.
03
Appropriations, not orders, set the ceiling. The backlog is real, but management's growth framing depends on "the highest defense budget in our country's history and a supplemental budget" — an outcome of a political process. A continuing resolution delays new starts and pushes the munitions ramp right, into a year when the capacity has already been paid for.
Industry Commentary
Munitions Capacity Is the Scarce Asset
EMERGING TAILWIND
The defense industry spent thirty years optimising for low-rate production of exquisite systems and is now being asked for volume. L3Harris's own description of the shift is the cleanest statement of it available: conversations with the Pentagon "are no longer about whether we can deliver, but how many can we produce." The commercial expression is a seven-year framework agreement worth $12B of future production revenue, under which THAAD motor output quadruples and PAC-3 nearly triples, with an automated facility opening late 2027 and management guiding framework margins to 15–18%. Solid rocket motors are a two-supplier Western market with multi-year qualification barriers, so this capacity cannot be competed away quickly. Missile Solutions grew 16% in the first half and is the smallest segment at 17.3% of revenue — the whole tailwind is still ahead of the reported numbers.
Proliferated Space Is Commoditising the Satellite
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The Space Development Agency's model — many cheap satellites on fixed-price contracts rather than a few expensive ones on cost-plus — is deliberately designed to squeeze contractor margin, and it is working. L3Harris won all five AMDT-3 constellation contracts, an unambiguous competitive win, and the segment that books them earns 9.8%, down 60 basis points year over year while growing 15%. Winning more of this work makes the company bigger and less profitable per dollar. That tension — market-share victory inside margin defeat — is the central structural issue for every prime with a space franchise right now, and for L3Harris it applies to half the revenue base. It is also why consolidated ROIC sits at 8.10%.
The Rearmament Trade Has De-Rated Before the Cycle Ended
ONGOING HEADWIND
From September 2023 to February 2026 defense re-rated on European rearmament and missile defense; LHX went from $174.12 to $364.54. Seven months later it is $248.37 despite raised guidance, a record backlog and 37% free-cash-flow growth. Multiple, not earnings, did all of the damage: roughly 30× the FY2026 guide midpoint at the peak versus 20.9× now. Bull framing: the contracted order book is unchanged, the $12B munitions framework and 15–18% framework margins are still ahead, and at a 6.5% free cash flow yield with 1.88% short interest you are buying committed revenue at a de-rated multiple while the street's own low target sits 8.3% above the price. Bear framing: a peak multiple unwinding is not a bargain until it stops, six consecutive down months and three August–September downgrades say the repricing is still in progress, and the fundamental objection — 8.10% ROIC with the fastest growth in the thinnest-margin segment — is a business-quality argument that a lower price does not answer.
