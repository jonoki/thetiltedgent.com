01 Company Overview

Lennox started in 1895 in Marshalltown, Iowa, founded by Dave Lennox, who ran a machine-repair business for the railroads. A hundred and thirty years later it is a Richardson, Texas maker of HVAC and refrigeration equipment for homes and commercial buildings across the US and Canada, sold under Lennox, Armstrong Air, Ducane, AirEase, Allied and — on the refrigeration side — Heatcraft, Bohn and Larkin. FY2025 revenue was $5.20B, down 2.7% from $5.34B, with diluted EPS of $22.79 — essentially flat on a year of shrinking sales, which tells you how much pricing and cost discipline was doing the work.

This is now two companies under one roof, and they are going in opposite directions. Home Comfort Solutions — the residential furnaces and air conditioners, 61% of Q2 2026 revenue — shrank 7% in the quarter, after a 10% fall in Q1. Building Climate Solutions — rooftop units, commercial service and refrigeration, 39% — grew 24%, after 38% in Q1, helped by acquisitions. On July 29, 2026 management cut the full-year EPS range to $23.00–$24.00 from $23.50–$25.00, and the stock closed that day at $430.02, down 20.97%. It has kept sliding since: from a $572.95 close on June 30, 2026 to $363.64 on September 21, down 36.5% in under three months, and 45.5% below the November 2024 month-end peak of $667.13. The whole debate fits in one question: is residential HVAC in a cyclical air pocket — high mortgage rates, weak housing, nervous consumers — or has Lennox's core business stepped down to a lower plateau? The market is currently pricing the second answer at 16 times trailing earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Direct-to-network distribution 130-year brand with contractors Replacement-driven demand Commercial national accounts Heatcraft refrigeration franchise ROIC 26.4%

The moat is the channel. An HVAC system is chosen by the contractor standing in your basement, not by you, and Lennox describes its own channel as a "direct-to-network distribution model" alongside its dealer and distributor networks — a closer relationship with the people who make the recommendation than a pure wholesale model gives. That, plus a mostly replacement-driven market (a failed unit gets replaced whatever the economy is doing), is why operating margin went from 13.8% in FY2021 to 20.1% in FY2025 and ROIC runs at 26.4%. The vulnerability is that a moat protects price, not volume: when fewer houses are built and homeowners repair instead of replace, the channel is still loyal — it just orders less.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Residential volume is the swing factor. Home Comfort was 60.5% of Q2 revenue and 58.8% of segment profit. On the Q2 call the CFO said lower sales volumes created about $50M of EBIT headwind in the quarter, and management pushed the expected recovery benefits into 2027.
02
Leverage is higher than it looks at first glance. Debt/equity is 1.56 and ROE is 71.8% — both flattered by a small $1.30B equity base shrunk by years of buybacks. At June 30, 2026 the company carried $1,149.2M of long-term debt plus $412.0M of commercial paper against $51.5M of cash. Interest coverage of 17x (StockAnalysis) is comfortable; the point is that there is little cash cushion if the downturn runs longer.
03
Growth is increasingly bought. Acquisitions are guided to supply about 5 of the ~8 points of 2026 revenue growth. Buying growth in the segment that is working is sensible; it also means the headline growth rate overstates what the existing business is doing, and integration risk rises with each deal.
04
Strategic choice to shed new-construction volume. Walking away from low-margin builder business protects margin but gives up volume and factory absorption now — a deliberate trade that looks smart in a recovery and expensive in a long slump.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $294.17 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $363.64, LII gained 23.6% on price (dividends excluded). That number hides a round trip. The stock fell to a $206.59 month-end in June 2022, more than tripled to $667.13 by November 2024 as gross margin climbed from 27% to 33%, and has since given back 45.5% of that peak — most of it in the last three months.

The 6-period monthly RSI (computed from the chart data) reads 30.7 — on the edge of oversold, but not at an extreme: it bottomed at 18.8 in June 2022 and was above 90 at the November 2024 peak. The 10-month moving average sits at about $478.69, 32% above the price. Momentum is weak and not yet exhausted; the chart alone offers no floor.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 16.2x trailing and 15.4x forward earnings for a business with a 33% gross margin, 26% ROIC and a dealer channel that helped take operating margin from 14% to 20% in four years. The residential slump is volume, not price: margins held at 23.7% even in a −7% quarter. Air conditioners wear out on a schedule that mortgage rates cannot change, so deferred replacement is demand postponed, not demand lost. Meanwhile commercial grew 24% with 15% organic and now out-earns residential on margin. Consensus sees EPS of $26.14 in 2027, and even the lowest target on the Street is above today's price.

Bear Case

Guidance was cut once, the recovery was pushed to 2027, and the stock kept falling for two months afterwards — the market does not believe the new range either. Residential revenue fell 8.4% in the first half, the company is deliberately shedding builder volume, and 5 of the ~8 points of 2026 growth are bought. The balance sheet has $1.56 of debt per $1 of equity and $52M of cash, with $412.0M of commercial paper outstanding at June 30 while buybacks continued. Ten of seventeen analysts rate it Hold, and a stock that re-rated on margin expansion can de-rate if the margins were partly peak-cycle pricing.

Top 3 Risks

01
A longer residential downturn. Home Comfort is 60% of revenue. If the 2027 recovery slips again, the $23.00–$24.00 guide becomes a ceiling, and each quarter of lower production adds absorption cost on top of lost volume.
02
Margin mean-reversion. Gross margin rose from 27.2% (FY2022) to 33.3% (TTM). If part of that was pricing taken in a tight market, competitors chasing volume in a soft one could claw it back — and the multiple would follow the margin.
03
Acquisition-dependent growth and a thin cash buffer. With ~5 points of growth bought and $51.5M of cash at June 30, the company is relying on integration going well and on commercial paper markets staying open.
Industry Commentary
Residential HVAC in a housing freeze
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Lennox's own read, as paraphrased from the Q2 call: elevated rates, inflationary pressures and historically low consumer confidence are holding back demand, with residential new construction down about 30% and residential unit volume down 12% in the quarter. A furnace or air conditioner is a big-ticket purchase that can often be repaired one more season instead of replaced; in a nervous economy, more homeowners choose the repair. That is why the residential decline shows up as volume, not price — and why the timing of the recovery, not its existence, is what the market is arguing about.

Commercial strength — organic and bought
MIXED SIGNAL

Building Climate Solutions is doing what residential is not: 24% growth in Q2 with 15% organic, led by national accounts, emergency replacement and service. Lennox has been adding to it by acquisition (Duro Dyne, Supco, and the Comfort-Aire and Century brands). The mix shift is real and margin-positive — commercial's 25.5% Q2 segment margin was higher than residential's 23.7% — but it is not yet big enough to carry the company when residential falls: Q2 total revenue grew just 3%.

The valuation question
OPEN QUESTION

At $363.64 Lennox trades at 16.2x trailing EPS, a 5.9% free-cash-flow yield and 12.4x EV/EBITDA (our calculations). The bull framing: this is peak-pessimism pricing for a franchise-quality business in a cyclical trough — the same stock traded at $667 when residential was fine, and houses do not stop needing cooling. The bear framing: trailing EPS of $22.51 includes the good times, the guide has already been cut once, and the stock has not found a floor. The way to decide is not the multiple; it is residential volume in the Q3 print on October 21 and whether the EPS range holds.
