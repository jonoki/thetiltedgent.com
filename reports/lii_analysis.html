01 Company Overview

Lennox started in 1895 in Marshalltown, Iowa, founded by Dave Lennox, who ran a machine-repair business for the railroads. A hundred and thirty years later it is a Richardson, Texas maker of HVAC and refrigeration equipment for homes and commercial buildings across the US and Canada, sold under Lennox, Armstrong Air, Ducane, AirEase, Allied and — on the refrigeration side — Heatcraft, Bohn and Larkin. FY2025 revenue was $5.20B, down 2.7% from $5.34B, with diluted EPS of $22.79 — essentially flat on a year of shrinking sales, which tells you how much pricing and cost discipline was doing the work.

This is now two companies under one roof, and they are going in opposite directions. Home Comfort Solutions — the residential furnaces and air conditioners, 60% of Q3 2026 revenue — grew 3% in the quarter only because of an acquisition (the CFO put organic revenue down about 1% on the call), and its segment profit fell 18% as Lennox lowered prices in some markets to hold on to customers and win back lost ones. Building Climate Solutions — rooftop units, commercial service and refrigeration, 40% — grew 24%, 15% of it organic. Management has now cut the full-year EPS range twice: to $23.00–$24.00 on July 29, 2026, and on October 28 to $21.00–$21.50 (adjusted), blaming heightened competitive pressure and persistent inflation. The stock fell 20.97% on the first cut and 10.59% on the second, and closed at $348.12 on October 29 — down 39.2% from its $572.95 close on June 30, 2026 and 47.8% below the November 2024 month-end peak of $667.13. The debate has widened. In September it was whether residential HVAC was in a temporary cyclical dip — high mortgage rates, weak housing, nervous consumers. Now it is also whether Lennox has to give up price to keep its share, which goes to the heart of the margin story. The market is pricing it at 16 times trailing earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Direct-to-network distribution 130-year brand with contractors Replacement-driven demand Commercial national accounts Heatcraft refrigeration brand ROIC 24.7%

The moat is the channel. An HVAC system is chosen by the contractor standing in your basement, not by you, and Lennox describes its own channel as a "direct-to-network distribution model" alongside its dealer and distributor networks — a closer relationship with the people who make the recommendation than a pure wholesale model gives. That, plus a mostly replacement-driven market (a failed unit gets replaced whatever the economy is doing), is why operating margin went from 13.8% in FY2021 to 20.1% in FY2025 and ROIC runs at 24.7% (StockAnalysis, trailing twelve months). The vulnerability showed in two steps this year. First, a moat protects price, not volume: when fewer houses are built and homeowners repair instead of replace, the channel is still loyal — it just orders less. Then, in Q3, price gave way too: the company says it lowered prices in select markets to defend and win back share, and Home Comfort's margin fell to 18.7% from 23.6%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Residential volume is the swing factor. Home Comfort was 59.6% of Q3 revenue and 51.3% of segment profit (our calculation from the release). On the Q3 call the CFO put its organic revenue down about 1% after about 9% in Q2, said a broad demand recovery has not yet arrived, and said the Q4 guide assumes a mid-single-digit organic sales decline.
02
Leverage is higher than it looks at first glance. Debt/equity is 1.58 and ROE is 62.8% (StockAnalysis) — both flattered by a small $1.32B equity base shrunk by years of buybacks. At September 30, 2026 the company carried $1,166.3M of long-term debt (including $319.2M due within a year) plus $455.1M of commercial paper against $43.3M of cash (release). Interest coverage of 15x (StockAnalysis) is comfortable, and the CFO put net debt at about 1.4 times adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation); the point is that there is little cash cushion if the downturn runs longer.
03
Growth is increasingly bought. Acquisitions are guided to supply about 5 of the ~6 points of 2026 revenue growth. Buying growth in the segment that is working is sensible; it also means the headline growth rate overstates what the existing business is doing, and integration risk rises with each deal.
04
Strategic choice to shed new-construction volume. Walking away from low-margin builder business protects margin but gives up volume and factory absorption now — a deliberate trade that looks smart in a recovery and expensive in a long slump.
05
An antitrust lawsuit. Since March 20, 2026 Lennox has been one of seven HVAC equipment makers named in a proposed class action in federal court in Michigan (In re HVAC Equipment Antitrust Litigation) that alleges they fixed prices. The company disputes the allegations, plans to defend itself and says it cannot estimate any possible loss (Q3 10-Q).

Notable One-Off Items

Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $6.41 and adjusted EPS $6.84 per the release. The $0.43 gap is four items: restructuring charges ($0.19 a share), acquisition costs ($0.25) and special litigation charges ($0.08), less a $0.09 gain from earlier disposals. Restructuring is new — $8.2M in Q3 and none in the first nine months of 2025 — mostly for consolidating distribution at acquired businesses and cutting overhead, with savings expected to start in 2027 (CFO, on the call). The items that distort comparisons are acquisitions (about 5 of the ~6 points of 2026 revenue growth are bought, which flatters the top line relative to the organic business) and buybacks (shares outstanding fell 1.97% year on year per StockAnalysis, supporting per-share figures). Lennox also switched how it values inventory in Q4 2025, from last-in, first-out to first-in, first-out, and restated 2024 to match (Q4 2025 release).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $299.28 to the October 29, 2026 settled close of $348.12, LII gained 16.3% on price (dividends excluded); over the same dates the SPY S&P 500 fund rose 67.5% on price. That number hides a round trip. The stock fell to a $206.59 month-end in June 2022, more than tripled to $667.13 by November 2024 as gross margin climbed from 27% to 33%, and has since given back 47.8% of that peak — most of it since June 2026.

The 6-period monthly RSI (computed from the chart data) reads 29.2 — just inside oversold territory, though not at an extreme: it bottomed at 17.3 in June 2022 and was above 90 at the November 2024 peak. The 10-month moving average sits at about $464.08, 33% above the price. Momentum is weak; the chart alone offers no floor.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 16.2x trailing and 14.6x forward (FY2027 consensus) earnings for a business with a 33% gross margin, 25% ROIC and a dealer channel that helped take operating margin from 14% to 20% in four years. Building Climate grew 24% in Q3 with 15% organic and now earns almost as much as residential on two-thirds of the revenue. Residential organic revenue fell only about 1% in Q3 after about 9% in Q2, organic inventory units are down more than 15%, the switch to the new A2L class of refrigerants is complete, and restructuring savings land in 2027. Air conditioners wear out on a schedule that mortgage rates cannot change, so deferred replacement is demand postponed, not demand lost. Management expects higher sales, profit and EPS in 2027, and consensus sees $23.86 of EPS that year.

Bear Case

Guidance has been cut twice in three months, and the second cut came with Lennox lowering prices to defend residential share: the pricing power behind the margin story is being tested. Home Comfort's margin fell to 18.7% from 23.6% in Q3, and the guide implies Q4 adjusted EPS of $3.11–$3.61 against $4.45 a year earlier. About 5 of the ~6 points of 2026 growth are bought. The balance sheet has $1.58 of debt per $1 of equity and $43M of cash, with $455.1M of commercial paper outstanding at September 30 while buybacks continued, and Lennox is one of seven HVAC makers facing a price-fixing class action. Fifteen of eighteen analysts now rate it Hold. The stock rose as margins widened; if those margins were partly pricing taken in a tight market, the share price can fall back with them.

Top 3 Risks

01
A longer residential downturn — fought on price. Home Comfort is 60% of revenue. Organic sales are still falling, the Q4 guide assumes a mid-single-digit organic decline, and Lennox is now lowering prices to keep share. If rivals answer in kind, the segment loses margin as well as volume.
02
Margins falling back. Gross margin rose from 27.2% (FY2022) to 32.6% (TTM), and Q3 came in at 31.6% against 34.1% a year earlier. If part of the rise was pricing taken in a tight market, it is now being given back — and investors would likely pay less per dollar of earnings as well.
03
Acquisition-dependent growth and a thin cash buffer. With ~5 points of growth bought and $43.3M of cash at September 30, the company is relying on integration going well and on commercial paper markets staying open.
Industry Commentary
Residential HVAC in a housing freeze
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Lennox's own read, paraphrased from the Q3 call: affordability, elevated mortgage rates and broader consumer uncertainty continue to hold back demand, and a broad recovery has not yet arrived. A furnace or air conditioner is a big-ticket purchase that can often be repaired one more season instead of replaced; in a nervous economy, more homeowners choose the repair. Through Q2 the decline showed up as volume, not price. Q3 changed that: the CEO said the company had moved to more dynamic pricing to defend its residential share, and the release blames heightened competitive pressure for margins that fell short. A shrinking market where makers fight for share on price is a harder problem than a slow one.

Commercial strength — organic and bought

Building Climate Solutions is doing what residential is not: 24% growth in Q3 with 15% organic. Acquisitions help: the release credits $13M of the segment's profit gain to them. The mix shift is real — Building Climate earned $166.5M in Q3 against Home Comfort's $175.7M on two-thirds of the revenue — but its own margin slipped to 26.2% from 27.3% as tariffs and inflation outran pricing, and it is not yet big enough to carry the company: Q3 revenue rose 10%, yet GAAP operating income fell 5%.

The valuation question
OPEN QUESTION

At $348.12 Lennox trades at 16.2x trailing GAAP EPS, a 7.0% free-cash-flow yield and 11.8x EV/EBITDA (our calculations). The bull framing: the price reflects maximum gloom about a business with lasting advantages, at the bottom of its cycle — the same stock traded at $667 when residential was fine, and houses do not stop needing cooling. The bear framing: trailing EPS of $21.54 still includes better quarters, the guide has been cut twice, and the price cuts put the margin, not just the volume, in question. The way to decide is not the multiple; it is whether the January 2027 guidance shows residential margin recovering.
