01 Company Overview

Lumentum designs and manufactures the photonics that move data with light: indium phosphide (InP) lasers — EMLs (electro-absorption modulated lasers, the transmit chip inside a data-center transceiver), CW (continuous-wave) lasers for silicon photonics, pump lasers for amplifiers and ultra-high-power lasers for co-packaged optics — plus finished 800G and 1.6T optical transceivers, optical circuit switches (OCS), telecom line systems and industrial/3D-sensing lasers. Fiscal 2026 (ended June 27, 2026) revenue was $3.014B, up 83.2%, and the exit rate was steeper still: the June quarter printed $1.006B, up 109% year over year, and the company has guided the September quarter to $1.225–1.275B. It ranks roughly #330 globally by market capitalisation at $79.05B — a company that was worth about $4B at its October 2023 trough.

This page is about one question. For most of its life Lumentum was a cyclical component supplier — a telecom-capex and Apple-3D-sensing name that traded between $40 and $100. In two years it has become an "AI second-derivative": the laser and switch vendor behind the optical links that connect GPU clusters. The stock is up roughly 10× in five years and 6.6× in twelve months. The bull case says the shift from electrical to optical interconnect is structural and Lumentum's InP fabs are a scarce asset. The bear case says this is the same component supplier, on the same hyperscaler capex cycle, at 25.8× sales and 41× next year's earnings. Sections 02, 04 and 07 take that apart with the numbers the company actually reports.

A correction to the framing this report was commissioned with: Lumentum no longer reports "Cloud & Networking" and "Industrial Tech" segments. The FY2026 results release and Q4 call disaggregate revenue by product type — Components and Systems — and neither the release nor the call transcript fetched for this report uses the old segment names. The analysis below uses the disclosure the company currently makes.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INP WAFER FABS (JAPAN ×2, GREENSBORO) EML YIELD & RELIABILITY ~1-YEAR CUSTOMER QUALIFICATION PUMP-LASER SHARE 70–80% LONG-TERM SUPPLY AGREEMENTS CHIP-TO-MODULE VERTICAL STACK OCS FIRST-MOVER SCALE

The moat is a scarce physical asset plus a slow qualification cycle. InP lasers cannot be made in a standard silicon fab; the world has a handful of high-volume InP lines, and Lumentum operates two in Japan and is bringing up a third in Greensboro, North Carolina (acquired from Qorvo, first revenue guided for early 2028, "sized for billions of dollars in annual revenue"). Even the raw substrate is constrained — management called substrate supply "a major constraint" and signed additional agreements with Japanese suppliers and AXT. On top of the fab sits yield: at 200G per lane the company says it and Broadcom face "very little competition", and the price premium it charges on CW lasers is explicitly attributed to yield and reliability. Then the customers themselves harden the position — management describes an approximately one-year qualification cycle at its largest accelerator customer, and says it has signed "multiple long-term customer agreements that helped offset planned capital expenditures", meaning hyperscalers are pre-funding capacity. Finally, owning both the chip (Components) and the module and switch (Systems) gives Lumentum a seat at the architecture table when CPO, NPO and OCS designs are being decided. What the moat is not: a guarantee of price. The 50.4% gross margin is a function of demand exceeding supply, and the company says so.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Customer concentration. The company did not name its largest customers on the call or at the Deutsche Bank conference, but said its "largest customer has taken up their orders very significantly" and that this underpins the fiscal 2028 earnings-power claim; it also referenced a ~one-year Nvidia qualification cycle. Specific ten-percent-customer percentages were not verified from the sources fetched. When one account's forecast moves the company's three-year target, that account's capex committee is the single biggest risk factor on this page.
▸
Architecture transitions can strand a product. Management's own numbers: EML share of the transmit socket drops from 70–80% at 800G to 40–50% at 1.6T as silicon photonics with CW lasers takes lanes. Further out, CPO removes the pluggable transceiver entirely — good for Lumentum's laser business, bad for its Cloud Light module business. Every generation re-opens the socket.
▸
Chinese InP capacity. Chinese transceiver leaders (Innolight, Eoptolink) are both customers and, via domestic laser fabs, potential competitors. Management's stance — Chinese vendors are "a future risk, but not an immediate threat", entering at 70–100 mW CW parts first, and "we haven't seen any impact on our numbers as yet" — is honest, and also exactly what an incumbent says two years before pricing changes.
▸
Cyclicality dressed as structure. Lumentum's 2021–2023 history is the template: telecom inventory digestion and an Apple share loss took revenue down and the stock from $106 to $39. Hyperscaler optics is a bigger, faster cycle, not a non-cycle. At 25.8× trailing sales, the market is pricing the current supply-demand imbalance as permanent.

Notable One-Off Items

One item dominates every GAAP figure on this page, and it is important to understand what it is and is not.

$7.8B non-cash loss on extinguishment of convertible notes (Q4 FY2026). After the stock rose roughly 8× in a year, Lumentum's 2026, 2028 and 2029 convertible notes were deep in the money. In the June quarter the company exchanged stock for a portion of them — retiring about $1.1B of principal, roughly 35% of the outstanding converts — and, under the accounting rules, recognised the excess of the conversion value delivered over the carrying value of the debt as a loss: $7.8B, plus $3.1M of transaction costs and $2.9M of unamortised issuance costs. No cash left the building. What did happen is real and permanent: the diluted share count went up (89.70M outstanding, up 7.18% YoY; non-GAAP diluted count ~101–102M including the remaining converts), debt fell from $2.57B to $1.64B, and stockholders' equity rose to $4.64B. So the "−$92.96 of EPS" is an accounting recognition of dilution that shareholders had already suffered economically as the stock rose. Read the GAAP loss as a footnote to the share count, not as a statement about the business.

Recurring non-GAAP adjustments are modest by the standards of high-growth tech: stock-based compensation of $191.3M in FY2026 (6.3% of revenue; $50.1M in Q4, 5.0%), amortisation of acquired intangibles of $135.7M (mostly Cloud Light and NeoPhotonics), and restructuring of $11.4M. The gap between GAAP operating margin (17.4%) and non-GAAP (29.8%) for the year is therefore explained almost entirely by SBC and amortisation, not by exotic add-backs — and in the June quarter, the GAAP operating margin was already 27.8%.

Prior-year comparison quirk: Q4 FY2025 GAAP net income of $213.3M ($2.96/share) on an operating loss of $3.2M reflects a non-operating gain that was not itemised on the pages fetched; the FY2025 base for GAAP earnings-growth comparisons is therefore not clean either. Use operating income and non-GAAP EPS for trend.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and a stock that goes up 10× spends most of that time "overbought".

Five-year trajectory — stated honestly. From the September 2021 month-end close of $83.54 to the September 4, 2026 close of $881.26, LITE returned +955%, about 60% compounded annually, with no dividend. Almost none of it happened in the first three years. The stock fell from $105.77 (Dec 2021) to a monthly-close low of $39.21 in October 2023 — a 63% drawdown — as telecom customers digested inventory and the 3D-sensing business shrank; an investor who bought at the 2021 high was underwater for more than three and a half years. From that October 2023 low to the April 2026 close of $902.32 the stock rose 23×, including a single month — February 2026, +79% — that alone added roughly $28B of market value. Over the last twelve months the 52-week change is +557%; year-to-date from $368.59 it is +139%, which the Sep 1 Yahoo Finance piece ranks among the ten best in the S&P 500. The path since April has been a wide range: $902 → $855 → $858 → $714 (July, −17%) → $915 (August, +28%) → $881. A NASDAQ-100 and S&P 500 total-return comparison over the identical window could not be verified from the sources fetched and is not stated as a number; what is safe to say is that a 10× five-year return dwarfs either index, that essentially all of the outperformance is concentrated in the 23 months since October 2024, and that the 5-year beta of 1.54 does not describe a stock that moved 17% and 28% in consecutive months.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The setup, in one paragraph. Lumentum sells the scarcest input in the AI data-center build that is not a GPU: indium phosphide lasers. Demand for optical links scales with the number of accelerators and with the bandwidth per link, so it has grown faster than the accelerator market itself — Lumentum's revenue went +58%, +65%, +90%, +109% across fiscal 2026 and is guided to roughly +134% in the September quarter. Because InP capacity is fixed in the short run, the company is repricing its backlog upward and the non-GAAP gross margin has risen 1,260 bps in a year to 50.4%, with a 40% operating margin guided next. Consensus has FY2027 revenue of $6.27B (+108%) and EPS of $21.63; management is talking about $40 of earnings power in fiscal 2028. At $881.26 the stock trades at 40.7× FY27 and 22× that FY28 number.

Cyclical supplier or structural winner? The honest answer is: it depends on what year you are asking about. The 2021–2023 chart says cyclical — a 63% drawdown driven by telecom inventory and a shrinking Apple line, in a company with the same fabs and largely the same engineers. The 2024–2026 chart says structural — a 23× rise on a genuine architectural shift from copper to light inside the data center, backed by long-term customer agreements that pre-fund capex and a qualification cycle that takes a year. Both are true. The right frame is that Lumentum is a structurally advantaged supplier into a cyclical end market: the InP moat is real and will persist; the 50% gross margin is a shortage price and will not. The Morgan Stanley bull/bear range ($450–$1,500) is the market saying exactly this — the earnings are not in dispute, the multiple a peak-cycle supplier deserves is.

What the numbers do and do not support. Supported: the growth (audited), the margin expansion (audited), the net-cash balance sheet after the convertible equitisation ($2.74B cash vs $1.67B debt), and a 16% ROIC that survives the GAAP noise. Not yet supported by disclosure: any dollar figure for OCS, CPO lasers or 3D sensing; any named-customer percentage; any third-party market-share number (the 70–80% figures are management's). The $7.8B GAAP loss is an accounting artefact of dilution that already happened — but the dilution itself is real, and the ~102M diluted share count in guidance is 14% above the 89.7M basic count.

Bull Case

Optical interconnect is the binding constraint on AI clusters and InP capacity is the binding constraint on optics. Lumentum has two fabs, a third coming, substrate agreements, a ~one-year qualification moat and customers pre-funding capex. Revenue doubles again in FY27 to ~$6.3B at a 40% operating margin; OCS, 200G EMLs and CPO lasers each add a leg so that "not all things need to go right". If $40 of FY28 EPS materialises, today's price is 22× — cheap for a 100%-grower — and the $1,400 high target is a 25× multiple on a number management has already put its name to.

Bear Case

This is a component supplier at 25.8× trailing sales, 96× EV/EBITDA and a 50% gross margin that management itself attributes to demand exceeding supply. Every generation re-opens the socket — EML share halves at 1.6T, CPO eliminates the pluggable module Cloud Light sells — and Chinese InP fabs are two years behind, not ten. One customer's order book underwrites the 2028 target. When Greensboro and the Japan expansions land in 2028–29, supply meets demand at the same moment hyperscaler capex growth decelerates, and the 2021–2023 chart replays from a far higher base. Morgan Stanley's bear case is $450, −49%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Hyperscaler capex and single-customer dependence. The 2028 earnings-power claim rests explicitly on "our largest customer" raising orders. A pause in accelerator deployment, a delay in a GPU generation (a risk Morgan Stanley names), or a shift in that customer's optics architecture would hit revenue, then pricing, then the multiple, in that order.
02
Margin normalisation as capacity arrives. A 50.4% gross margin and a 40% operating margin are shortage economics. Lumentum, Coherent, Broadcom and Chinese entrants are all adding InP capacity for 2027–2029. The stock does not need a revenue miss to de-rate; it only needs the gross margin to stop rising.
03
Valuation with no bear in the room. 22 of 26 analysts rate it Buy; the lowest target is within 7% of the price; short interest is 8% of float; monthly RSI has spent a year above 70. Consensus FY27 EPS growth of 149% is what the 0.36 PEG is built on. If that growth rate is 100% instead of 149%, the PEG is still under 1 — and the stock could still fall 30%, because the multiple, not the estimate, is the variable.
  Industry Commentary
Indium Phosphide Is the Bottleneck — for Now
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every 800G or 1.6T transceiver needs InP lasers, and InP cannot be made on a silicon line. Lumentum says it is shipping behind demand on EMLs, is "way behind" on high-power lasers, has pump lasers "effectively sold out", and that substrate — the raw wafer — is "a major constraint" it has addressed with new AXT and Japanese supply agreements. The response is capex: $167M in the June quarter alone, two Japanese fab expansions, and the Greensboro fab (first revenue early 2028, full production 2029). Industry-wide, Coherent and Broadcom are expanding too and Chinese fabs are entering at the low-power end. The constraint is real today and is the entire reason for the 50% gross margin; it is also being solved by everyone at once, with delivery dates clustered in 2028.
Every Speed Generation Re-Opens the Socket
ONGOING HEADWIND
Optics moves from 800G to 1.6T to 3.2T roughly every two years, and the architecture moves with it: pluggable transceivers, then near-packaged optics (NPO), then co-packaged optics (CPO) with external laser sources. Management's own disclosure is that EML share of the transmit socket falls from 70–80% at 800G to 40–50% at 1.6T before recovering at 3.2T; CPO, if it scales, removes the pluggable module that Cloud Light sells while multiplying demand for the ultra-high-power lasers Lumentum makes. Net, the company believes it wins each transition; the market has to underwrite that belief every 24 months. Fabrinet, as the contract manufacturer for much of the industry's module assembly, is the neutral beneficiary regardless of which architecture wins — which is why it trades at 22× forward earnings and Lumentum at 41×.
Optical Circuit Switching and the Backbone Build
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two demand sources sit outside the transceiver cycle. OCS — mirror-based switches that reroute fibre without electronics — moves from a Google-internal technology to a merchant market, and Lumentum guided its first $100M+ OCS quarter for September 2026 after doubling shipments sequentially. Separately, hyperscalers are building long-haul fibre backbones between AI sites on a scale management described as two sites that "could double total global backbone capacity built over the last decade", which is why telecom-style pump lasers are sold out and being quadrupled. Bull framing: these are second and third legs that do not depend on the 1.6T socket fight, which is what Evercore meant by "not all things need to go right". Bear framing: neither OCS nor CPO nor 3D sensing has a disclosed revenue line, so the diversification argument is, for now, a set of ramp dates; the only numbers you can audit are the two product-type lines, and both are hyperscaler-optics-driven and both are on the same capex cycle.
