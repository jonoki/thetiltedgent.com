Section 01

Company Overview

Eli Lilly is a 150-year-old Indianapolis drugmaker that has been violently re-rated into a growth stock by one molecule class: incretins. Its tirzepatide franchise — Mounjaro (type 2 diabetes) and Zepbound (obesity) — plus the newly launched oral GLP-1 pill Foundayo (orforglipron) have taken Lilly from a $28B revenue business in 2022 to $65.18 billion in full-year 2025 revenue, up 45% YoY, with guidance of $82–85 billion for 2026. It is the largest pharmaceutical company in the world by market capitalization (~$1.02 trillion), roughly 65% larger than the next-biggest healthcare company, and in November 2025 became the first healthcare business ever to cross $1 trillion in market value.

Beyond metabolic health, Lilly runs four other franchises — oncology (Verzenio, Jaypirca, Inluriyo), immunology (Taltz, Omvoh, Ebglyss), neuroscience (Kisunla, Emgality), and a legacy diabetes/insulin book — and has been aggressively buying optionality, spending roughly $10B+ on acquisitions in the last 14 months alone (Verve, Centessa, AtaiBeckley) while committing over $27 billion to U.S. manufacturing capacity. The core investment question is no longer whether Lilly wins the obesity market — it currently holds ~60% U.S. incretin share — but whether pricing erosion, oral competition, and eventual generic tirzepatide arrive faster than the market's ~32x forward multiple assumes.

Section 03

Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

IP / Patents Manufacturing Scale Regulatory Barriers Clinical Data Depth Brand Power (Mounjaro/Zepbound) Vertical Distribution (LillyDirect) R&D Reinvestment Flywheel

The moat is a peptide supply chain, not a patent. Tirzepatide's composition-of-matter protection runs into the mid-2030s, but the durable advantage is that Lilly spent five years and $27B+ building sterile fill-finish and peptide API capacity across 13 countries before demand existed. In 2023–2024 the constraint on this entire market was vials and pens, not prescriptions; Lilly is the only player that solved it at scale and can now afford to cut price aggressively — management's own framing is that every price reduction expands volume "non-linearly." Layer on the deepest clinical dataset in the class (SURPASS, SURMOUNT, ATTAIN, ACHIEVE), a small-molecule oral in Foundayo that costs a fraction of a peptide to manufacture, a triple agonist in retatrutide with best-in-class efficacy, and a direct-to-patient channel (LillyDirect) that bypasses PBM gatekeeping for cash-pay patients — and you get a business that compounds its cost advantage while competitors are still running Phase 3. The 82.8% gross margin and 41.9% ROIC are the financial signature of that position.

Key Vulnerabilities

Extreme single-franchise concentration

Mounjaro and Zepbound were 65% of Q1 2026 revenue and the seven "Key Products" were 68%. Any tirzepatide-specific event — a safety signal, a label restriction, a manufacturing interruption, an adverse patent ruling — hits roughly two-thirds of the P&L at once. Sandoz and China's Hanyu Pharma have already filed Abbreviated New Drug Applications (ANDAs) seeking generic tirzepatide, and the resulting Hatch-Waxman litigation is now a permanent overhang on the terminal value.

Drug pricing policy and MFN

Lilly has agreed to Most-Favored-Nation pricing frameworks with the U.S. administration, offering MFN prices to Medicaid patients and selling direct via TrumpRx-style channels. From July 1, 2026 Medicare Part D covers GLP-1s at $50 monthly copays — a volume tailwind, but one purchased with price. Guidance already embeds low-to-mid-teens price erosion for 2026. Medicare negotiation eligibility for the tirzepatide franchise later this decade is the single largest identifiable policy risk to consensus 2029–2031 numbers.

Oral GLP-1 competition is not going Lilly's way (yet)

Foundayo has been badly outpaced by Novo's oral Wegovy: 29,388 weekly scripts versus 171,700 as of July 31, 2026, and roughly 17,000 versus 73,000 at the comparable week-eight mark. PBM coverage arrived late (CVS Caremark only from June 1) and 45% of early volume ran through LillyDirect rather than retail. Meanwhile Structure Therapeutics' aleniglipron posted 16.3% weight loss at 44 weeks — the best oral result outside Lilly — and enters Phase 3 in Q3 2026. Orals are the volume market; losing them would cap the franchise well below current expectations.

Valuation leaves no margin for error

At ~40.8x trailing and ~32x forward earnings against a large-cap pharma group at roughly 15–18x, LLY is priced for a decade of compounding. The July 30, 2026 session — a 4% single-day drawdown on competitive headlines with no change to fundamentals — and the August 7, 2025 −14% day (its worst in 25 years, on a single Phase 3 readout) both demonstrate how much of the market cap is expectations rather than earnings.

Several non-recurring items distort the current financials and should be normalised out:

• Acquired IPR&D charges of $584M in Q1 2026 (in-process research and development written off at deal close). Lilly runs a persistently high IPR&D charge because of its acquisition cadence — Verve ($1.3B, 2025), Centessa ($6.3B, June 2026), AtaiBeckley ($2.8B, July 2026) — so these "one-offs" are arguably recurring and will suppress reported GAAP EPS again in Q2/Q3 2026.

• The FY2025 GAAP-to-non-GAAP bridge ($22.95 vs $24.21) is almost entirely IPR&D and intangible amortisation. Use non-GAAP for run-rate, GAAP for cash-adjusted reality.

The five-year trajectory

From roughly $253 in August 2021 to $1,148.84 on July 31, 2026, LLY has returned approximately +354% on price alone, or about +35% annualised — an almost unheard-of outcome for a company that was already a $250B mega-cap at the start of the window. That return is not a smooth compound: it is three distinct legs. The first (2021–mid-2023) was the market slowly pricing in Mounjaro's diabetes approval. The second (mid-2023 through 2024) was the obesity mania, when SURMOUNT data and the Zepbound launch drove the stock from ~$450 to a then-record ~$950 in a little over a year. The third (late 2025 to today) was a violent recovery from the orforglipron disappointment straight through the $1 trillion barrier.

What the chart does not show is how much of that return came in a handful of sessions. Two days — August 7, 2025 (−14%, the worst day in 25 years) and October 30, 2025 (Q3 blowout) — account for a large share of the twelve-month path. The monthly RSI has spent most of the last three years above 50 and has printed above 70 at each of the 2023, 2024 and mid-2026 peaks, each time followed by a multi-month consolidation of 15–25%.

Relative performance

Over the five-year window LLY has beaten essentially everything. Against the S&P 500 (roughly +80–90% over the same period) it has outperformed by something close to 270 percentage points. Against its own sector the gap is more extreme still: the S&P 500 Health Care sector index has been broadly flat to modestly positive over five years, weighed down by Humira's patent cliff, COVID-revenue normalisation at Pfizer and Moderna, and multi-year underperformance at Bristol Myers and Merck. Within pharma specifically, Lilly's closest analogue — Novo Nordisk — peaked in mid-2024 and has since given back the majority of its obesity-era gains, so the two stocks have diverged by well over 150 percentage points since. In the last twelve months alone LLY is up ~47% while the sector has lagged the broad market. The practical implication for a portfolio: LLY is now large enough and idiosyncratic enough that it is effectively its own factor — a 0.51 beta that carries genuine single-headline risk of 10%+ moves.

Section 08

Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

Lilly owns ~60% of the fastest-growing therapeutic category in pharmaceutical history, with a manufacturing cost position no competitor can match for years and a pipeline (retatrutide at 28.7% weight loss, orforglipron as a cheap small-molecule oral) that extends the efficacy frontier rather than defending it. Medicare Part D coverage from July 2026 converts the largest untapped patient pool into a reimbursed market. If FY26 lands at $86B and management compounds toward $120B+ by 2030 at a 48%+ performance margin, today's 32x forward multiple is roughly 15x on 2030 earnings — and the neuroscience, oncology and immunology franchises are close to free.

BEAR CASE

Two-thirds of revenue depends on one molecule facing ANDA challenges from Sandoz and Hanyu, low-to-mid-teens annual price erosion, eventual Medicare negotiation, and an oral competitor outselling Lilly's own pill roughly 6:1. The moat is manufacturing scale, which is exactly the kind of advantage that decays as rivals build capacity and as small molecules replace peptides. At 40.8x trailing earnings with 1.15% short interest and 90% Buy ratings, positioning is one-sided; a −14% day on a single Phase 3 readout in August 2025 is the template for what a genuine disappointment does to a stock priced for perfection.

Top 3 Risks

  1. Tirzepatide concentration and generic challengeMounjaro plus Zepbound were 65% of Q1 2026 revenue. Sandoz and Hanyu Pharma ANDA filings have started the patent litigation clock, and any adverse ruling, safety signal or manufacturing interruption would impair roughly two-thirds of the P&L simultaneously.
  2. Structural price erosion and drug pricing policyManagement guides to low-to-mid-teens FY26 price erosion; U.S. net price fell ~10% in Q1 excluding one-timers. Layered on MFN commitments, Medicaid MFN pricing, and future Medicare negotiation eligibility, the risk is that volume growth eventually stops outrunning price decline — at which point a 32x forward multiple has nothing to stand on.
  3. Losing the oral GLP-1 marketOrals, not injectables, are the mass-market format. Foundayo is running roughly 29,000 weekly scripts against 171,700 for oral Wegovy, and Structure Therapeutics' aleniglipron (16.3% weight loss at 44 weeks) enters Phase 3 this quarter. If Lilly's injectable dominance does not translate into oral share, the terminal size of the franchise is materially smaller than consensus assumes.

Industry Commentary

Price is now the competitive weapon, not supply

Critical constraint

For three years the binding constraint on GLP-1 revenue was manufacturing capacity — every unit made was sold. That era ended in 2026. Lilly's U.S. net realised price fell ~7% in Q1 2026 (~10% underlying), Foundayo launched at $149/month cash-pay, and Medicare Part D now covers the class at $50 copays. Management's stated view — that price cuts expand volume "non-linearly" — is a rational strategy for a low-cost producer, but it converts the industry from a supply-rationed oligopoly into a share war. Watch the gross margin line: 83.0% for FY2025 has already slipped to 82.6% in Q1 2026. Small, but directional.

Washington has permanently repriced U.S. pharma economics

Ongoing headwind

Most-Favored-Nation pricing frameworks, direct-to-consumer government channels, Medicaid MFN commitments, threatened pharmaceutical tariffs, and IRA Medicare negotiation have collectively moved the U.S. from the world's price-setter to a price-taker benchmarked against Europe. Lilly negotiated its way to a workable outcome in November 2025 — the deal was a relief rally at the time because it capped tail risk — but the structural consequence is that U.S. list-to-net spreads compress every year from here. For a franchise whose entire premium rests on a decade of U.S. pricing durability, this is the slow-moving variable that matters most for 2029–2032 estimates.

The addressable market is still expanding faster than it is being competed away

Emerging tailwind

The most important statistic in the category right now is that 80%+ of both Foundayo and oral Wegovy prescriptions are going to patients who have never taken a GLP-1. This is not share rotation; it is category creation. The 2026 market is ~$82B against roughly one billion people worldwide with obesity, and even the bearish 2030 forecast (~$95B, Goldman) versus the bullish one (~$268B, BCC Research) implies the range of outcomes is about penetration, not viability. Bull framing: in a market tripling by 2030, a 60% share holder with the best efficacy asset (retatrutide) and the cheapest manufacturing base compounds regardless of unit price — competition takes share of a much larger pie. Bear framing: if the market only reaches ~$95B while five well-funded entrants arrive with orals between 2027 and 2030, Lilly's revenue can grow while its multiple halves, which is exactly how the stock loses 40% without a single bad quarter.
