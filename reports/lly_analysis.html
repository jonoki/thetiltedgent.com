01 Company Overview

Eli Lilly is a 150-year-old Indianapolis pharmaceutical company that has become the leader in incretin therapies (GLP-1 class drugs) for diabetes and obesity. Full-year 2025 revenue reached $65.2B, up 45% YoY, driven by tirzepatide — sold as Mounjaro ($23.0B, +99%) for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound ($13.5B, +175%) for obesity. In the third quarter of 2026 revenue rose 39% to $24.4B, with Mounjaro alone at $10.7B, and management raised its 2026 revenue guidance for the third time this year, to $91–92B (from $85–87B). At roughly $1.27 trillion in market value (our calculation: 893.4M diluted shares in Q3 × the Oct 30 close), Lilly is worth about twice Johnson & Johnson (~$658B at its Oct 30 close, our calculation).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
INCRETIN DUOPOLY BEST-IN-CLASS EFFICACY $50B+ MANUFACTURING SCALE PIPELINE DEPTH (RETATRUTIDE) PATENT RUNWAY TO ~2036 LILLYDIRECT CHANNEL ORAL SMALL-MOLECULE COST EDGE

Lilly's moat is a rare pharma triple-lock: molecule superiority, manufacturing capacity, and distribution. Tirzepatide beat semaglutide head-to-head (SURMOUNT-5: ~20% vs ~14% weight loss), and that efficacy gap has translated into share — Mounjaro and Zepbound (tirzepatide) sold $14.9B in Q2 2026 against DKK 59.3B for Novo Nordisk's semaglutide brands (Ozempic, Rybelsus and Wegovy; about $9.3B at Novo's H1 average rate of DKK 6.40 per dollar, our conversion), roughly 62% of the two molecules' combined sales; in Q3 Mounjaro, Zepbound and Foundayo together sold $16.3B, and Lilly says it wrote 7 of 10 US injectable obesity and diabetes prescriptions (Novo reports its nine-month results on Nov 4, per its Q2 report). Behind it sits a capital moat competitors cannot quickly replicate: more than $50B committed to US manufacturing since 2020 (four new sites announced in Feb 2025 alone), which ended the shortage era and lets Lilly supply pharmacies freely — including its direct-to-consumer LillyDirect pharmacy — while rivals ration. Foundayo (orforglipron) adds a chemistry moat: as a synthetic small molecule (not a peptide), it is dramatically cheaper to mass-produce and needs no cold chain, positioning Lilly for price-sensitive and international markets. The US compound patent on tirzepatide runs to 2036 (2025 10-K), and the pipeline (retatrutide's 22.6% Phase 3 weight loss and its October head-to-head win over semaglutide, bimagrumab for muscle preservation) is designed to leapfrog its own franchise before anyone else does.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸Single-franchise concentration. Incretins are ~74% of revenue and effectively all of the growth. Any tirzepatide safety signal, a superior competitor molecule, or faster-than-modeled patient discontinuation (real-world dropout within 12 months is high across the class) would hit a stock priced at about 46 times the past year's earnings.
▸Pricing and policy compression. Realized prices fell 15% worldwide in Q3 2026 (−7% in the US, −29% elsewhere), mainly on Zepbound. The TrumpRx/most-favored-nation deals (Nov 2025) cap direct-to-consumer prices, and Novo cuts its US list prices for Wegovy and Ozempic to $675 a month from Jan 1, 2027. Volume must keep outrunning price — HSBC, at Reduce since its March 2026 downgrade (target raised to $940 on Sep 10), argues it won't.
▸Competitive wave 2027–2029. Novo's CagriSema (~22.7% weight loss; filed with the FDA on Dec 18, 2025, review expected in 2026), Amgen's monthly MariTide (Phase 3), Viking's VK2735, and Pfizer's amylin/GLP-1 drugs from its Metsera purchase all target Lilly's share and prices. Oral semaglutide already launched ahead of Foundayo.
▸Execution/capex risk. A $50B+ manufacturing buildout, ~$11B of capex over the last four quarters and serial IPR&D charges ($4.2B in Q3'26 alone) assume the demand curve holds; tariff policy and supply chains for drug ingredients add further operational risk.
▸Retatrutide's regulatory label. Lilly has been in court with the FDA since 2024 over whether retatrutide counts as a biologic (12 years of US market exclusivity) or a drug (five years); the case was unresolved as of August 2026, ahead of the planned Q1 2027 filing (BioSpace).

Notable One-Off Items

Q3 2026 GAAP and non-GAAP EPS both absorbed $4.57/share of acquired IPR&D charges ($4.18B), mainly from the AtaiBeckley and Curevo acquisitions — Lilly expenses the cost of buying unfinished research (acquired in-process R&D) at once, so trailing P/E overstates the "clean" multiple. Q3 also included $303M of asset impairment, restructuring and other special charges, and Q3 revenue carried about $900M of benefits, mainly $410M of Jardiance milestones (no further ones remain) and favorable adjustments to rebate and discount estimates. Acquired IPR&D totals $7.5B for the first nine months of 2026, after $2.9B in FY2025. Separately, ~$11B of capex over the last four quarters (part of a $50B+ US buildout) holds free cash flow below net income. None of these change the underlying trajectory, but all of them distort single-period comparisons.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

Lilly's tirzepatide took ~62% of its sales race with Novo's semaglutide in Q2 2026, in a market that consensus still sizes at $150B+ a year, grew revenue 39% in Q3 with ~84% gross margins, and its own pipeline (Foundayo's US prescriptions doubling in Q3, retatrutide beating semaglutide head-to-head and filing in Q1 2027) is the most likely thing to make tirzepatide obsolete. At a 1.49 PEG and 28.7x FY2027 estimates, another beat-and-raise and a cleaner Foundayo ramp take the stock toward the $1,650 Street-high targets (Citigroup, JPMorgan, BMO).

▼ Bear Case

About three-quarters of revenue rides one mechanism whose realized price fell 15% in Q3, heading into a 2027 reset (Novo's $675 list prices, MFN caps) just as CagriSema, MariTide and VK2735 arrive. If HSBC's $80–120B market size is right, a price of 46 times the past year's earnings and ~29 times FY2027 estimates would likely shrink toward ordinary drugmaker levels as volume growth slows — and Aug 7, 2025 showed this stock can lose 14% in a day on a single trial readout. At $1,417.54 it already trades above the $1,397 average analyst target.

  Industry Commentary: The GLP-1 Macro Picture
The Great GLP-1 Pricing Reset
ONGOING HEADWIND

The shortage-era pricing umbrella is folding. Lilly's realized prices fell 15% worldwide in Q3 2026 (−7% in the US, −29% elsewhere) while volume rose 53%; US sales of Wegovy injections fell 22% YoY at constant exchange rates in Q2 2026 (Novo's Q2 report); and from Jan 1, 2027 Novo cuts the US list prices of Wegovy and Ozempic to $675 a month (about −50% on Wegovy). Layer on the Nov 2025 TrumpRx/most-favored-nation framework — Zepbound self-pay prices of $299–449 a month on LillyDirect, Medicare patients paying no more than $50 a month and wider Medicaid access in exchange for price concessions (Lilly, Nov 6, 2025), with a temporary Medicare pilot covering GLP-1s for weight loss since July 2026 — and the class is converting from luxury-priced scarcity to mass-market volume. Lilly's Q3'26 numbers show volume winning so far (revenue +39% against prices −15%), but every future model now hinges on that ratio holding.

How Big Is Obesity, Really? The Market-Size Debate
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Roughly a billion people worldwide live with obesity, but bulls and bears disagree by nearly 2x on what that monetizes to: consensus models $150B+ in annual incretin revenue by the early 2030s, while HSBC's March 2026 downgrade argued $80–120B through 2032, citing price wars and real-world discontinuation (a large share of patients stop within a year). The swing factors: oral pills (Foundayo, Novo's Wegovy pill) opening price-sensitive and international markets; approvals for more conditions (sleep apnea, heart failure, kidney disease, MASH liver disease and Alzheimer's prevention); and insurance coverage, where Medicare's GLP-1 weight-loss pilot began in July 2026 — “This is a program that's really working,” Ricks said of it on CNBC's Squawk on the Street on Oct 29. Where reality lands in that $80–150B range is, mechanically, the LLY share price.

Manufacturing as Strategy — and the 2027 Competitive Wave
EMERGING TAILWIND

The defining industry lesson of 2023–2025 was that in incretins, capacity is market share — and Lilly out-built everyone, committing $50B+ to US manufacturing while tariff policy (Section 232) makes domestic capacity a regulatory asset too. For bulls: that installed base plus a small-molecule pill and a superior late-stage pipeline (retatrutide, bimagrumab) means Lilly converts the price reset into a volume land-grab, growing EPS 30%+ a year while the 2027 entrants fight for second place. For bears: ~$11B of capex over the last four quarters, $49.5B of debt (1.23 times shareholders' equity) and a share price of 46 times the past year's earnings assume the demand curve and Lilly's ~62% share against Novo both survive CagriSema, MariTide, VK2735 and a flood of cheaper orals — a bet with very little room for a single bad readout. The duopoly looks safe through ~2027; after that, the moat gets tested every year.
