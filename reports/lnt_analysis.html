01 Company Overview

Alliant Energy is a utility holding company that sells regulated electricity and natural gas in two states through two operating utilities: Interstate Power and Light (IPL) in Iowa and Wisconsin Power and Light (WPL) in Wisconsin. At September 30, 2026 it served 1,013,135 electric and 432,839 natural gas customers. FY2025 revenue was $4.36B, up 9.57%, with GAAP EPS of $3.14 and "ongoing" (company-adjusted) EPS of $3.22; over the twelve months to September 30, 2026 revenue was $4.50B and GAAP EPS $3.59. It has raised its dividend for 22 consecutive years and has set a 2027 target of $2.25 a share, up from $2.14.

For most of its history this was the quietest kind of stock: a Midwestern monopoly whose profits are set, in effect, by two state commissions multiplying an allowed return by the capital it has invested — the rate base. What changed is the customer list. Alliant's two utilities now have 3.4 gigawatts of contracted demand from data centres (Q3 2026 release), on what the CEO called three sites. Three projects are under construction — QTS and Google in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, and Meta in Beaver Dam, Wisconsin — and a second QTS campus, in Clinton, Iowa, awaits local approvals (Q3 slides, as summarised by Investing.com). The company expects peak demand to rise about 60% by 2031. On the Q3 call CEO Lisa Barton said she expected "approximately 1 additional gigawatt of contracted large load within the next six months." Serving that load means building: the October update raised the 2027–2030 capital plan by 19% to $15.9B, which the CFO said "translates to a 13% compounded annual growth rate in rate base plus construction work in progress" — from $16.9B in 2025 to $31.5B in 2030 in the Q3 slides (as summarised by Investing.com).

That is the whole hand. The stock priced it in first — the June 2026 month-end close of $76.29 was the highest of the five-year window — then fell 17.0% to a $63.30 September month-end, and won most of it back in October, closing at $72.12 on November 3, 5.5% below the June close. The debate is not whether the data centres come; three are under construction. It is whether a company with −$1.46B of trailing free cash flow (cash from operations minus capital spending), $2.7B of new shares to sell in 2027–2030 and a promise of flat Iowa electric base rates through the end of 2030 can turn 13% rate-base growth into the "8%+" yearly per-share earnings growth the CFO now expects from 2027 through 2030 — after the new shares spread the profit thinner and after whatever the regulators hold back.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Regulated franchise monopoly ~13% planned rate-base growth 3.4 GW contracted data-centre load 22 years of dividend increases 9.8% allowed ROE (Wisconsin) Low beta (0.50)

A utility does not have competitors in the usual sense; it has a franchise, a regulator and a cost of capital. Nobody else is going to string wires to a Cedar Rapids data centre, and nobody else gets to earn a regulated return on doing it. The moat is the right to invest — and the data-centre contracts have multiplied how much investing there is to do. The vulnerability sits on the other side of the same bargain: the return is granted, not earned, so it lasts only as long as the regulator believes the rates are fair. In Iowa, Alliant has promised flat electric base rates through the end of 2030; in August the commission cut its gas rates and opened a management-efficiency investigation. The franchise protects the volume. It does not protect the margin from a regulator in an affordability mood.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The plan is funded, not financed from cash. Trailing operating cash flow of $1.18B against capex of $2.64B leaves free cash flow at −$1.46B (StockAnalysis, twelve months to September 30, 2026), and it was negative in every year from FY2021 to FY2025. The gap is filled with debt (total $12.56B at September 30, debt-to-equity 1.61), up to $5.1B of DOE-guaranteed federal loans, and new shares: $2.7B in 2027–2030, ~$1.1B already arranged by forward sale. On the call the CFO described "approximately 375 basis points of estimated dilution from equity issuances" (3.75 percentage points) in the plan. Every share issued below the price the plan assumed is growth handed to someone else.
02
Iowa growth has to pay for itself. With flat retail electric base rates now committed through the end of 2030, IPL's return depends on large-load revenue arriving on time. IPL's first half fell 24.7% before a strong third quarter ($0.96 against $0.64). If a data-centre ramp slips — through supply-chain delays, late transmission upgrades or local opposition to data centres — the costs land before the revenue.
03
Customer concentration of a new kind. A 60% peak-demand increase built largely on a handful of big technology companies and data-centre operators turns a diversified residential-and-industrial utility into one with a few very large counterparties.
04
Regulatory temperature. The August 26, 2026 IUC order — a gas rate reduction, a finding on executive chartered flights and a new investigation (INU-2026-0002) — is small in dollars (more than $2.5M) and large in signal. Utilities are valued on the trust of their commissions.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported line. First, GAAP versus ongoing: nine-month 2026 GAAP EPS was $3.04 against non-GAAP $2.99; the $0.05 gap is a one-time tax benefit (re-measured deferred tax assets) that the company leaves out of its ongoing measure. Q3 alone matched at $1.52; in Q3 2025 ongoing EPS ($1.12) was above GAAP ($1.09) because of a $0.03 state-tax charge. Second, weather: temperatures added an estimated $0.05 a share in Q3 2026 against $0.03 a year earlier, and the release also lists the timing of income-tax expense among the quarter's drivers — neither has anything to do with the data-centre thesis. Third, share count: 259.36M shares were outstanding at September 30, 2026 against 257.14M at December 31, 2025, and $2.7B of new common equity is planned for 2027–2030, so per-share growth will run below net-income growth. The historical GAAP EPS series (FY2021 $2.63 → FY2025 $3.14) is not affected by stock splits; the last split was 2-for-1 in May 2016.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $54.79 to the November 3, 2026 settled close of $72.12, LNT gained 31.6% on price. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A regulated utility is a machine that turns approved investment into earnings, and Alliant's machine just got bigger again: a $15.9B plan for 2027–2030, up 19%, taking rate base toward $31.5B by 2030 at about 13% a year; 3.4 GW of contracted data-centre load, three campuses under construction, peak demand projected up 60% by 2031, and about 1 GW more expected within six months. The large-load customers let Iowa hold electric base rates flat through 2030, the best possible argument to put in front of a regulator, and DOE-guaranteed loans of up to $5.1B make part of the build cheaper to finance. Q3 beat the Nasdaq consensus by 24%, 2026 guidance moved to the top half, 2027 guidance implies 8% growth, and the CFO now expects "8%+" a year through 2030. At $72.12 you pay 19.6x 2027 consensus for a 2.97% yield that has grown for 22 years and is set to rise 5.1% in 2027.

Bear Case

The growth is real, but the per-share arithmetic is harder than the headline. Free cash flow is −$1.46B, debt is $12.56B at 1.61x equity, and the bigger plan needs $2.7B of new shares in 2027–2030, of which only ~$1.1B is arranged; 13% rate-base growth only becomes 8% EPS growth after that dilution and regulatory lag. Q3's beat leaned partly on weather and the timing of tax expense, and revenue came in a little under the forecast Investing.com cited. The rate freeze, now a year longer, means Iowa has promised customers something the data centres must deliver on schedule, and the Iowa commission cut gas rates and opened an efficiency investigation in August. After a 13.2% October, the average target is only 5.2% above the price; at 20.1x trailing GAAP earnings with 10.61% of the float sold short, the stock is priced for execution, not for slippage.

Top 3 Risks

01
Financing and dilution. A $15.9B plan on −$1.46B of free cash flow needs continuous access to debt and equity markets. About $1.6B of the $2.7B of 2027–2030 equity is still to be sold, at prices the market chooses; the DOE-guaranteed loans cover only eligible projects, up to 80% of their cost.
02
Data-centre timing. Earnings in Iowa depend on large loads ramping on schedule under a base-rate freeze that now runs to the end of 2030. Supply-chain delays, late transmission upgrades or local opposition to data centres could each slow a ramp, and any slip moves cost ahead of revenue.
03
Regulatory relationship. The August 2026 IUC order and investigation, the Wisconsin rate review due in early 2027, and individual-rate filings for data-centre customers in both states, decide how much of the growth shareholders keep. A 9.8% allowed ROE in Wisconsin is only worth what the next order says.
Industry Commentary
Data centres turn a slow utility into a growth utility
EMERGING TAILWIND

Alliant's projected demand growth moved from ~50% to 60% by 2031 within a quarter — ~50% in the Q4 2025 slides (February), 60% by the Q1 2026 update (May), reiterated at Q2 and in the Q3 release as peak demand — on 3.4 GW of contracted data-centre load at Google, QTS and Meta sites, with about 1 GW more that the CEO expects to sign within six months. For a regulated utility, load growth of that size is the rarest thing: a reason to build that also spreads fixed costs over more kilowatt-hours. Management's framing is that the growth itself pays for Iowa's rate freeze, which it has now extended to the end of 2030. The capital plan follows the load: $15.9B for 2027–2030, 19% more than the $13.4B 2026–2029 plan it replaced.

Affordability and the regulator
ONGOING HEADWIND

Wisconsin's settled rates rise 5.4% and 5.0% for electricity in 2026 and 2027, and include a pilot bill credit of $26 a month for eligible low- and moderate-income customers. Iowa's commission, in the follow-up review Iowa law requires once rates set on projected costs are in force, cut IPL's residential gas rates and opened an investigation into management efficiency. Neither is large in dollars. Both say regulators are reading the bill as closely as the balance sheet — and a utility's earned return is ultimately what its commissions allow it to keep.

Funding the build
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Free cash flow has been negative every year from FY2021 (−$587M) to FY2025 (−$1.31B), and the new capex plan peaks at $4.7B in 2028. The October update put numbers on the funding: $2.7B of new common equity in 2027–2030, about $1.1B of it already priced through forward sales, and the CFO said equity funds "approximately 40% of our incremental capital expenditures." Federal help arrived the same week: DOE-guaranteed loans of up to $5.1B, at the Treasury rate plus 0.375 percentage points, for eligible generation, storage and other projects at the two utilities. The bull framing: at ~13% rate-base growth the dilution is a small tax on a big increase. The bear framing: utilities are priced on per-share growth, and the roughly $1.6B of stock still to be sold is priced by the market on the day it is sold.
