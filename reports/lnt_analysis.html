01 Company Overview

Alliant Energy is a utility holding company that sells regulated electricity and natural gas in two states through two operating utilities: Interstate Power and Light (IPL) in Iowa and Wisconsin Power and Light (WPL) in Wisconsin. At June 30, 2026 it served 1,011,128 electric and 432,892 natural gas customers. FY2025 revenue was $4.36B, up 9.57%, with GAAP EPS of $3.14 and "ongoing" (company-adjusted) EPS of $3.22. It has raised its dividend for 22 consecutive years.

For most of its history this was the quietest kind of stock: a Midwestern monopoly whose profits are set, in effect, by two state commissions multiplying an allowed return by the capital it has invested — the rate base. What changed is the customer list. Alliant now has five executed electric service agreements with data-centre developers — Google and QTS in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, Meta in Beaver Dam, Wisconsin, a 900 MW QTS project, and a newly signed ~370 MW Iowa agreement — and management projects 60% growth in system demand by 2031, up from ~50% by 2031 in the February (Q4 2025) slides. Serving that load means building: a $13.4B capital plan for 2026–2029 that takes rate base plus construction work in progress from $16.9B (2025) to $26.2B (2029), roughly 12% a year.

That is the whole hand. The stock priced it in first — the June 2026 month-end close of $76.29 was the highest of the five-year window — and has since given back 14.8% to $64.97, near the bottom of its 52-week range. The debate is not whether the data centres come; three are under construction. It is whether a company with −$1.28B of trailing free cash flow, $2.4B of new equity to sell through 2029 and a promise of 0% Iowa electric rate increases through 2029 can convert a 12% rate-base growth rate into the "7%+" per-share earnings growth management is guiding to — without the dilution and the regulators taking the difference.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Regulated franchise monopoly ~12% planned rate-base growth 5 executed data-centre agreements 22 years of dividend increases 9.8% allowed ROE (Wisconsin) Low beta (0.53)

A utility does not have competitors in the usual sense; it has a franchise, a regulator and a cost of capital. Nobody else is going to string wires to a Cedar Rapids data centre, and nobody else gets to earn a regulated return on doing it. The moat is the right to invest — and the data-centre contracts have multiplied how much investing there is to do. The vulnerability sits on the other side of the same bargain: the return is granted, not earned, so it lasts only as long as the regulator believes the rates are fair. In Iowa, Alliant has promised flat electric rates through 2029; in August the commission cut its gas rates and opened a management-efficiency investigation. The franchise protects the volume. It does not protect the margin from a regulator in an affordability mood.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The plan is funded, not financed from cash. Trailing operating cash flow of $1.16B against capex of $2.44B leaves free cash flow at −$1.28B, and it has been negative in every year from FY2021 to FY2025. The gap is filled with debt (total $12.12B, debt-to-equity 1.61) and new shares ($2.4B through 2029, ~$1.8B already arranged by forward sale). Every share issued below the price the plan assumed is growth handed to someone else.
02
Iowa growth has to pay for itself. With 0% retail electric increases committed through 2029, IPL's return depends on large-load revenue arriving on time. First-half IPL EPS fell 24.7%. If a data-centre ramp slips — management named supply chains, transmission upgrade timing and local political pushback (data-centre moratoriums in some areas, with no current project affected) — the costs land before the revenue.
03
Customer concentration of a new kind. A 60% demand increase built largely on a handful of hyperscale and colocation customers turns a diversified residential-and-industrial utility into one with a few very large counterparties. The contract terms that protect the rest of the rate base from stranded costs were not detailed on the pages fetched.
04
Regulatory temperature. The August 26, 2026 IUC order — a gas rate reduction, a finding on executive chartered flights and a new investigation (INU-2026-0002) — is small in dollars (more than $2.5M) and large in signal. Utilities are valued on the trust of their commissions.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A regulated utility is a machine that turns approved investment into earnings, and Alliant's investment pipeline just got much bigger: rate base from $16.9B to $26.2B by 2029, five signed data-centre agreements, three campuses under construction, 60% demand growth projected by 2031, and a 2–4 GW pipeline behind that. The large-load customers let Iowa hold electric rates flat through 2029, which is the best possible argument to put in front of a regulator. Guidance is trending to the upper half, "7%+" EPS growth is guided for 2027–2029, most of the equity is already arranged, and every analyst target sits above the price. After a 14.8% pullback you are paying about 19x 2026 consensus and 17.6x 2027 for a 3.29% yield that has grown for 22 years.

Bear Case

The growth is real, but the per-share arithmetic is harder than the headline. Free cash flow is −$1.28B, debt is $12.12B at 1.61x equity, and 12% rate-base growth only becomes 7% EPS growth after dilution and regulatory lag. The first half showed the gap: Iowa EPS down 24.7%, Q2 below consensus, GAAP TTM EPS down 2.5%. The rate freeze means Iowa has promised customers something the data centres must deliver on schedule, and the Iowa commission has just cut gas rates and opened an efficiency investigation. At 20.6x trailing GAAP earnings with 9.59% of the float sold short, the stock is priced for execution, not for slippage.

Top 3 Risks

01
Financing and dilution. A $13.4B plan on −$1.28B of free cash flow needs continuous access to debt and equity markets. The remaining ~$500M of equity and any increase in the plan at the November 5 update are set at prices the market chooses.
02
Data-centre timing. Earnings in Iowa depend on large loads ramping on schedule under a 0% rate commitment. Management named supply chains, transmission upgrades and local political opposition as risks; any slip moves cost ahead of revenue.
03
Regulatory relationship. The August 2026 IUC order and investigation, and upcoming large-load tariff and individual-rate filings in both states, decide how much of the growth shareholders keep. A 9.8% allowed ROE in Wisconsin is only worth what the next order says.
Industry Commentary
Data centres turn a slow utility into a growth utility
EMERGING TAILWIND

Alliant's projected demand growth moved from ~50% to 60% by 2031 within a quarter — ~50% in the Q4 2025 slides (February), 60% by the Q1 2026 update (May), reiterated at Q2 — on five executed agreements with Google, QTS and Meta sites plus a ~370 MW Iowa contract, and a further 2–4 GW in negotiation. For a regulated utility, load growth of that size is the rarest thing: a reason to build that also spreads fixed costs over more kilowatt-hours. Management's framing is that the growth itself pays for Iowa's rate freeze. The capital plan follows the load — $4.1B of gas generation, $5.0B of storage and renewables, $4.3B of distribution and technology across 2026–2029.

Affordability and the regulator
ONGOING HEADWIND

Wisconsin's settled rates rise 5.4% and 5.0% for electricity in 2026 and 2027, and include a pilot bill credit of $26 a month for eligible low- and moderate-income customers. Iowa's commission, reviewing the first future-test-year rate case to reach a follow-up hearing, cut IPL's residential gas rates and opened an investigation into management efficiency. Neither is large in dollars. Both say regulators are reading the bill as closely as the balance sheet — and a utility's earned return is ultimately what its commissions allow it to keep.

Funding the build
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Free cash flow has been negative every year from FY2021 (−$587M) to FY2025 (−$1.31B), and the capex plan peaks at $3.8B in 2028. The company has pre-arranged most of its $2.4B equity need through forward sales, which takes price risk off the table for that tranche but not for anything added at the November update. The bull framing: at ~12% rate-base growth the dilution is a small tax on a big increase. The bear framing: utilities are priced on per-share growth and the per-share line has been flat to down on a GAAP basis over the last year.
