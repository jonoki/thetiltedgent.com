01 Company Overview

L'Oréal describes itself as the world's leading beauty company, and its shelf is long: L'Oréal Paris, Garnier, Maybelline, Lancôme, Yves Saint Laurent Beauté, Kérastase, La Roche-Posay and CeraVe are some of its 40 global brands, sold in more than 150 countries. FY2025 sales were €44.05B, up 4.0% like-for-like and 1.3% reported, with a record 20.2% operating margin and EPS excluding non-recurring items of €12.71 (+0.4%). The first half of 2026 was faster: €23.78B of sales, +6.8% like-for-like (+6.5% after adjusting for the timing of an IT changeover), an operating margin of 21.3% and EPS excluding non-recurring items of €7.40 (+4.8%). The third quarter was faster again: nine-month sales reached €35,337.6M, +6.9% like-for-like and +7.7% reported, with Q3 alone up 7.7% on the adjusted measure.

The third quarter beat forecasts by a wide margin. Q3 sales were €11.56B, up 7.7% like-for-like on the adjusted measure (7.2% before the IT adjustment), against the 5.9% analysts expected in a Visible Alpha consensus cited by Reuters; Reuters reported that growth beat estimates in every region except Latin America. Every Division grew, led by Professional Products and Dermatological Beauty (both +13.9% adjusted), and by region SAPMENA-SSA (+19.5%), Europe (+8.9%) and North Asia (+7.8%) did best. "L'Oréal delivered an exceptional third quarter as adjusted like-for-like growth accelerated to +7.7%," CEO Nicolas Hieronimus said in the release. L'Oréal does not give numeric guidance. On the call he said the beauty market grew around 5% in the quarter and that for the rest of the year "we expect the beauty market to grow around +5%, and we will continue to outperform, albeit at a slightly more moderate rate." He added: "We will deliver on a full-year basis the promised acceleration of our top-line growth versus last year, significantly." On margins, CFO Christophe Babule said in July the company "could expect on a yearly basis and based on the past experience, around 20 to 30 basis points improvement if we keep with the same rhythm of growth." (A basis point is a hundredth of a percentage point, so that is 0.2 to 0.3 points of margin a year.) Asked in October by Morgan Stanley's Sarah Simon about how much of faster growth would reach the margin, Hieronimus answered "No, it's not a good assumption," because the priority "has always been, and always will be to fuel our growth" (Investing.com transcript). At September 30 exchange rates, L'Oréal estimates currency will take about 0.3% off 2026 sales.

The share price has started to catch up. The October 30, 2026 close of €436.95 is 10.6% above the October 2021 month-end close of €394.95, while France's CAC 40 index rose 16.7% over the same five years. It is the highest close since June 2024 and sits 3.4% below the May 2024 month-end high of €452.10. The valuation is a premium one: 37.1 times trailing earnings on IFRS figures, 33.5 times on the company's own headline EPS. The table has two very big stacks. The Bettencourt Meyers family owns 34.79% and Nestlé 20.16%, so about 45% of the shares trade freely. Since 2025 L'Oréal has also been spending heavily: Kering Beauté (Creed, plus the Bottega Veneta and Balenciaga licences, with Gucci from July 2027), a second 10% of Galderma, and Medik8 and Color Wow, paid for partly with new bonds.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

40 global brands, 4 channels A&P spend: 32.6% of sales 4,000 researchers · 725 patents (2025) €1B+ a year on IT & tech 37 plants · 160 fulfilment centres AA (S&P) / Aa1 (Moody's) credit Patient anchor shareholders

The moat is scale in advertising plus breadth of channel. In H1 2026 L'Oréal spent €7.74B, or 32.6% of sales, on advertising and promotion. Because the four Divisions cover the supermarket, the department store, the pharmacy and the salon, a shift in where people buy moves sales between L'Oréal's own pockets more often than it moves them to a competitor. The gross margin of 74.8% pays for that spending and still leaves a 21.3% operating margin. The risk is that this is a premium hand, and the price already assumes it keeps winning.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The deal spree has added leverage. Kering Beauté (acquisition price €4,246.5M, closed March 31, 2026), the second 10% of Galderma (€4.2B, closed February 10, 2026) and smaller deals took net debt to €12.66B at June 30, 2026, including €2.38B of lease liabilities. Bonds followed: €3B in November 2025, €1.75B in January 2026, CHF 500M in June 2026 and €2B priced on September 23, 2026. The ratings are still AA/Aa1, but the €4.0B of goodwill and intangible assets booked on Kering Beauté alone has to earn its keep.
02
China and travel retail. North Asia is 21.9% of nine-month sales and grew 5.5% like-for-like, below the group's 6.9%. Q3 was better (+7.8%), and Goldman Sachs noted that part of North Asia's return to high-single-digit growth came from the timing of shipments (Investing.com). On the call Hieronimus said: "In China, even though the market slowed down a tiny bit in Q3, it remains dynamic at 9 months at somewhere between 2 and 3." The Luxe Division, a third of sales, depends most on the Chinese customer; in Q2 its 4.7% growth lagged forecasts (Reuters), in Q3 it grew 7.0% adjusted.
03
The slowdown case has lost a round, not the argument. Deutsche Bank cut the stock to Sell from Hold on June 23, 2026, with a €340 target (from €360), arguing the strong first half borrowed from the second: retailer stock built up around the IT switch-over, weaker Chinese credit data, competition from fast-growing Chinese beauty exports to the US, and currency hedges rolling off (Investing.com's report). Q3 went the other way, but the bank kept its Sell rating, raising the target to €360 on October 26 (dpa-AFX). The IT timing is still in the numbers: in North America Q3 sales grew 2.2% like-for-like before the adjustment and 5.6% after it. And management itself expects fourth-quarter outperformance "at a slightly more moderate rate".
04
The price already assumes the winning streak continues. At 37.1 times IFRS trailing earnings and a 1.65% dividend yield, the shares price in years of above-market growth. A high valuation can shrink fast: the stock fell 27.3% from the May 2024 month-end high of €452.10 to the November 2024 month-end of €328.65. After October's 15.2% jump, the close is above MarketScreener's average analyst target of €433.00.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items separate the IFRS figures from the company's headline numbers. First, non-recurring items of €412.5M net of tax in H1 2026 (other income and expenses of −€300.5M before tax), which is why IFRS EPS (€6.63) trails headline EPS (€7.40). Second, France's exceptional corporate-tax contribution on large companies, €252.7M booked at December 31, 2025 and renewed for 2026. L'Oréal adjusts it out of net profit excluding non-recurring items. Separately, the Gucci licence changeover: Gucci's beauty licence with Coty ended early on July 7, 2026, at a cost to L'Oréal of about $360 million, of which $188 million was paid in July 2026. L'Oréal also holds two large listed stakes off the operating line: 7.30% of Sanofi (€6.66B at June 30, 2026) and 20% of Galderma (valued at €9.49B at the June 30 share price). Together they equal about 7% of L'Oréal's market value at the October 30 close (our calculation). Dividends received added €365.8M to H1 2026 pre-tax profit.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of €394.95 to the October 30, 2026 close of €436.95, L'Oréal gained 10.6% on price. The CAC 40 gained 16.7% over the same window (6,830.34 → 7,969.42). The path was a range, not a trend. Every month-end close sat between €318.10 (October 2022) and €452.10 (May 2024), and the stock has crossed the €370–€400 band many times.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Growth has accelerated through 2025 and into 2026 (+3.0% like-for-like in H1 2025, +4.2% in Q3, +6.0% in Q4, +6.8% in H1 2026, +7.7% adjusted in Q3 2026) and is broad-based: every Division and every region but Latin America grew in Q3, after a first half with a gross margin up to 74.8% and a record operating margin of 21.3% even while advertising rose 0.7 percentage points of sales. The fastest-growing Divisions, Dermatological Beauty (+11.6% like-for-like over nine months) and Professional Products (+12.4%), are also the most profitable, so mix keeps lifting margins. The acquisitions add growth that hasn't shown up in the numbers yet: Creed and the Kering licences from April 2026, Gucci from July 2027, and a 20% Galderma stake worth €9.49B at June 30. Free cash flow of €7.54B a year covers the dividend (about €3.8B at €7.20 a share, our calculation) roughly twice. With two long-term anchor shareholders, the company can be managed for the next decade rather than the next quarter. The second half did not slow: Q3 growth of 7.7% adjusted beat the 5.9% consensus, China and travel retail grew, and Hieronimus promised a full-year acceleration "significantly" ahead of last year. At least seven brokers raised their targets after the release, and Erste Group upgraded to Buy; the highest target on MarketScreener's panel is €500, 14.4% above the close.

Bear Case

About 37 times trailing IFRS earnings, after a 17.2% rise in four weeks, leaves little room for error: the close is already above the €433.00 average analyst target, and five years of a mostly flat share price show how hard it has been to earn a return from that premium. Profits are reported only twice a year, so the multiple rose on sales news alone; the February 2027 results have to show the margin keeping up, and Hieronimus said in October that the priority is to fund growth rather than to lift the margin faster. Deutsche Bank still rates the shares Sell (€360) on its argument that retailers stocked up around the IT switch-over and that Chinese demand is weaker than imports suggest; in North America Q3 growth was 2.2% before the IT adjustment, and management expects outperformance to moderate in Q4. Mass-market Consumer Products, the largest Division, grew only 3.8% adjusted in Q3. Net debt has risen to €12.66B, and four bond issues in eleven months helped pay for €8B+ of deals, which now have to deliver. North Asia, at over a fifth of sales, is still growing more slowly than the group over nine months (+5.5% against +6.9%). And 20.16% of the company sits with a shareholder whose own chairman now sits on L'Oréal's board; Nestlé sold 4% back to L'Oréal at €400 a share in 2021, and any further sale would put a large block of stock on the market.

Top 3 Risks

01
Expectations have caught up. Q3 answered the slowdown worry, but it also raised the bar: at 37.1 times trailing earnings and above the average analyst target, the price now assumes growth stays well ahead of a beauty market the company puts at about 5%. Management expects outperformance to moderate in Q4, and the IT timing still moves quarterly figures (North America: +2.2% like-for-like in Q3, +5.6% adjusted). The October 2025 release, followed by a 6.70% one-day fall, shows how the stock reacts to a soft quarter.
02
Integration and leverage. Kering Beauté cost €4,246.5M, of which €2,840.9M is goodwill; Galderma's second 10% cost €4.2B; the Gucci switch-over costs about $360 million. Net debt is €12.66B. The AA/Aa1 ratings leave room, but a stumble in Creed or the Gucci launch would hit returns on a much larger capital base.
03
China, travel retail and currency. North Asia is 21.9% of nine-month sales; travel retail only returned to growth in Q3, and Hieronimus put the Chinese market's growth at somewhere between 2 and 3 percent over nine months. Currency took 1.2 points off nine-month reported growth (2.8 points in H1). About a quarter of sales comes from North America, so a weaker dollar shrinks reported euro growth, as it did in H1 (+8.6% like-for-like, +4.2% reported).
Industry Commentary
Buying the next decade of prestige beauty
STRATEGIC SHIFT

In about twelve months L'Oréal agreed or closed a run of deals: Medik8 (77.4%, closed September 10, 2025), Color Wow (agreed June 2025), the Kering Beauté alliance (agreed October 19, 2025, closed March 31, 2026, €4,246.5M), a second 10% of Galderma (€4.2B, February 10, 2026) and a majority of India's Innovist (completed by the October 22, 2026 sales release). In the 2025 results release CEO Nicolas Hieronimus called it the group's "most strategic and transformational M&A offensive to date". The logic is portfolio breadth: luxury fragrance (Creed, Gucci from 2027), medical aesthetics (Galderma), science-led skincare (Medik8) and premium haircare (Color Wow). The test is whether these brands grow faster than the group once inside it. The first signs are positive: the H1 2026 release said Creed was off to a good start, and the Q3 release says it performed strongly in Luxe and in North America.

China, travel retail and the shape of demand
ONGOING HEADWIND

China is the region L'Oréal watches most closely, both at home and through duty-free sales to Chinese travellers. Its North Asia sales grew 4.6% like-for-like in H1 2026, and the company describes the Chinese beauty market as gradually improving, driven by a recovery in selective (prestige) beauty, with travel retail still under pressure. On the July 30 results call Hieronimus put a number on it: "In China, we have seen indeed the market stabilizing at a growth of around 2% over the last quarters." Deutsche Bank is more sceptical: in September it read China's rising cosmetics imports as almost entirely a Hainan duty-free effect rather than a consumer recovery. Q3 came in stronger: North Asia grew 7.8%, the company says China grew almost three times as fast as its market and travel retail returned to growth, and on the October 22 call Hieronimus put the Chinese market at "somewhere between 2 and 3" percent growth over nine months, a little slower in Q3. Goldman Sachs noted that part of the region's Q3 growth came from the timing of shipments. Elsewhere the mix is shifting toward online (over 50% of North Asia sales, more than 30% for the group in 2025) and toward the emerging regions, where SAPMENA-SSA grew 15.7% like-for-like over the first nine months of 2026. The simple version: no region is more than about 35% of L'Oréal's sales, so one weak market slows the group rather than stalling it.

The Nestlé question
OWNERSHIP

In December 2021 Nestlé sold 22.26 million shares back to L'Oréal at €400 each (€8.9B), saying it would keep 20.1% and "remains fully supportive of the company's value creation strategy". At December 31, 2025 the stake was 20.16%. At the €436.95 close that is a holding worth roughly €47B (our calculation: 20.16% of the €232.74B market value). In 2026 Nestlé's representation on L'Oréal's board changed: Paul Bulcke stepped down at the April 24 AGM and Nestlé's new chairman, Pablo Isla, joined as a director and Vice-Chairman, alongside Nestlé executive Anna Lenz. The bull framing: a patient shareholder that reinforces stability. The bear framing: a 20% block that, if Nestlé ever chose to sell, would be a very large supply of stock.
