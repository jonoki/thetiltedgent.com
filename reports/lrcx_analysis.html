01 Company Overview

Lam Research designs, builds and services the wafer fabrication equipment (WFE — the machines that pattern and build transistors on silicon wafers) used to etch, deposit and clean the microscopic three-dimensional structures inside every advanced logic and memory chip. It is the world's dominant supplier of plasma etch tools and a top-two supplier of thin-film deposition, with an especially concentrated franchise in memory — where 3D NAND layer stacking and DRAM scaling are effectively etch- and deposition-limited problems.

Fiscal 2026 (ended June 28, 2026) was a record: revenue of $23.23 billion, up 26.0% from $18.44 billion in FY2025, with GAAP diluted EPS of $5.76 and non-GAAP EPS of $5.82, up 41%. The September 2026 quarter, the first of fiscal 2027, set new records: $8.22 billion of revenue (+54% YoY), a 52.4% non-GAAP gross margin and a 40.2% non-GAAP operating margin. At roughly $287 billion of market capitalization (1.249 billion shares at the October 23 close) Lam ranks #38 in the S&P 500 and #20 in the Nasdaq-100. The stock is up 56% over the past year (price return, October 23, 2025 to October 23, 2026) but 47% below its June 2026 high, after an 11.8% fall the day after the September-quarter report.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
3D NAND Etch Monopoly ~100K Installed Chambers Process Recipe Lock-In Qualification Switching Cost $2B+ Annual R&D Memory Customer Depth Molybdenum Metallization Lead

Lam's moat is best understood as process ownership, not product ownership. A leading-edge memory or logic device is defined by a recipe — gas chemistries, pressures, temperatures, plasma conditions — co-developed with the customer over 18 to 36 months and then qualified for high-volume production. Once a step is qualified on a Lam chamber, moving it to a competitor's chamber means re-qualifying the device, re-running yield learning, and accepting months of production risk on a node where each week of delay costs a memory maker hundreds of millions of dollars. That is why WFE share shifts glacially: Lam has held roughly 45%+ of etch for over a decade despite well-capitalized attackers in Tokyo Electron and Hitachi High-Tech. The moat is deepest exactly where the physics is hardest — high-aspect-ratio cryogenic etch through 300+ layers of alternating film in 3D NAND, where Lam's served market per wafer roughly doubles from 128 layers to 500+ layers, and where no credible second source exists at volume. Layered on top is the installed base: ~100,000 chambers generating spares, service and upgrade revenue that grew about 40% year-over-year in the September quarter and represented about 36% of FY2026 revenue at above-corporate margins, converting a violently cyclical capital-equipment business into something with a meaningful annuity floor. R&D at over $2 billion annually — more than a quarter of the workforce is in R&D — sustains the cadence, and the five-year IBM collaboration announced in March 2026 on High-NA EUV-era and sub-1nm process integration keeps Lam inside the roadmap conversations that determine tool specifications three nodes out.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. China concentration and export-control whiplash. China was 34% of March-quarter revenue, 26% in June and 35% in September, and the CFO expects a significantly lower share in December, driven by domestic Chinese customers. In April 2026 the US Commerce Department told Lam and other toolmakers to stop certain shipments to two plants of Hua Hong, China's second-largest foundry, Reuters reported (via TrendForce). The company's FY2026 10-K says its sales to China "have been impacted, and are likely to be materially and adversely affected by export license requirements and other regulatory changes …" Every new rule hits a revenue base that Lam does not control and cannot forecast.

2. Chinese domestic substitution. A July 27, 2026 news report said a Shanghai state-backed firm had begun mass-producing domestic immersion DUV lithography scanners; the stock fell 4.5% that day and 7.5% the next (closing prices). Lithography is not Lam's business, but the read-through is that China's toolchain gaps are closing faster than assumed — and etch, where AMEC and Naura already compete at trailing nodes, is the segment most exposed. Localization directly attacks the trailing-edge and Reliant revenue that has been a stabilizer through prior downturns.

3. Memory cyclicality and customer concentration. Micron, Samsung, SK hynix and TSMC are named principal customers; memory alone was 46% of September-quarter systems revenue. Memory capital spending has historically swung 40–60% peak to trough. The FY2026 result was produced by a genuine capex boom — conventional DRAM contract prices rose about 93–98% quarter over quarter in Q1 2026 (TrendForce) — and equipment orders are the derivative of that. If memory pricing normalizes, order cuts arrive with a two-to-three quarter lag and hit Lam harder than it hits diversified peers like Applied Materials.

4. Valuation risk. At 34.3× trailing and 24.3× forward earnings, Lam is priced for the FY2027 consensus of $34.9 billion revenue and $9.48 EPS (stockanalysis, read October 24, 2026) to be delivered, not attempted. Twice in four months the price has fallen hard on news that cut no near-term number: about 42% from the $438.50 intraday high of June 30 to the $252.35 close on July 29, and 11.8% on October 22 after a quarter that beat and a guide that rose.

5. Supply constraints. Lam is now limited by what it can build and where its customers can put tools. The CFO said the December revenue guide would have been higher without near-term supply chain constraints, and that shipments slipping out of December move into the March quarter; Tim Archer said clean-room space and supply of certain components will keep limiting how fast chipmakers and toolmakers can meet demand into 2027. Revenue Lam cannot ship is revenue a competitor may win, or that arrives after the cycle has turned.

Notable One-Off Items

No large discrete charge distorts recent results. In the September 2026 quarter GAAP EPS was $2.19 and non-GAAP $2.20. For FY2026 the gap between GAAP EPS of $5.76 and non-GAAP EPS of $5.82 is only six cents — roughly a 1% reconciliation, consisting mainly of amortization of acquired intangibles and restructuring-related items. That is unusually clean for a company of this size and stands in contrast to FY2023–FY2024, when export-control-driven inventory and reserve charges materially depressed reported gross margin.

Three items still deserve flagging. First, export-control effects are embedded in the revenue line rather than shown as a charge — the halt on certain Hua Hong shipments reduces revenue and reserve inventory without a separately captioned expense, so the true "underlying" China run-rate is not disclosed. Second, buybacks swing with the price. Lam's cash flow statements show $3.85 billion of share repurchases in FY2026, of which only $247 million came in the June quarter as the stock peaked, then $1.02 billion in the September quarter after it fell. $3.03 billion remained under the $10 billion authorization approved in May 2024 at September 27 (10-Q). Third, the 10-for-1 stock split effective October 2024 means all pre-October-2024 per-share figures in this analysis have been restated on a split-adjusted basis; historical quotes from unadjusted sources will be ten times higher.

Five-year trajectory. From an October 2021 monthly close of $56.36 to $230.10 on October 23, 2026, LRCX returned about +308% in price terms on a split-adjusted basis — a compound annual rate near 33%, before dividends. That headline number conceals four distinct regimes. The first, October 2021 through October 2022, was a brutal de-rating: the memory glut, post-COVID demand normalization and the October 2022 China export controls drove the stock from $71.92 at the end of 2021 to a $36.60 monthly close in September 2022, a 49% drawdown. The second, late 2022 through mid-2025, was a slow grind in which the AI logic buildout lifted WFE but memory capex stayed disciplined; the stock roughly doubled off the low but spent all of 2024 and the first half of 2025 range-bound between roughly $72 and $106, going nowhere for eighteen months. The third regime began in September 2025, when DRAM contract pricing inflected and investors raised their view of how much memory makers would spend on equipment: from a $100.15 close in August 2025 the stock rose to $433.33 by June 2026, a 333% advance in ten months. The fourth is the shakeout since: down 32% in July, back to $328.51 at the end of September, then down 30% to $230.10 by October 23 after the September-quarter report. The stock had gained 78% in the four years to August 2025 and has gained 130% since, so most of the five-year return still rests on the memory cycle of the last fourteen months.

The July 2026 unwind deserves its own accounting. From the $438.50 intraday high on June 30 the stock fell to a $252.35 close on July 29 — roughly −42% in a month, with closing losses of 9.7%, 10.2%, 0.3% and 6.9% in the first four sessions of July alone. Lam reported no change in its business; the slide came with the stock at a high multiple and deepened after the July 27 report of Chinese domestic DUV lithography production. The Q4 print that evening lifted the stock 18.0% the next day. October repeated the lesson in the other direction: a quarter that beat and a guide that rose still cost the stock 11.8% in a day, because the guide was capped by supply rather than demand. A stock that moves this much on unchanged or rising guidance is moving on what investors had already priced in, not on its earnings.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Lam owns the two process steps — high-aspect-ratio etch and metal deposition — that gate 3D NAND layer scaling and DRAM node transitions, and its served market per NAND wafer roughly doubles from 128 layers to 500-plus layers. With NAND makers still in the middle of 256-layer-plus conversions, DRAM's share of Lam's systems revenue slipping to 20% while the DRAM build-out lies ahead, WFE seen in the mid-$150B range for 2026 and above $200B in 2027 by management, the revenue runway is visible rather than hoped for. September-quarter revenue rose 54% from a year earlier, December is guided higher again at $8.60B, gross margin hit a record 52.4% with a mid-50s target, and the installed-base business brings in nearly $2.5B a quarter, about 40% more than a year ago. After a 29% fall since August, the stock trades at 24× forward earnings and a PEG below 1.

Bear Case

The stock fell 11.8% on a quarter that beat and a guide that rose: the market had priced in more than a record. At 34.3× trailing earnings, it is still priced for a business whose end market has historically swung 40–60% peak to trough. The FY2027 consensus of $34.9B revenue and $9.48 EPS assumes the memory spending boom compounds; if DRAM and NAND prices settle, orders can be cut faster than backlog reveals. Lam cannot currently ship all it could sell, China's share jumped from 26% to 35% of revenue in one quarter and is guided significantly lower in December, Hua Hong shipments are restricted, and domestic Chinese etch suppliers are attacking the trailing-edge base. The July −42% fall and the October −30% slide show how quickly the multiple can vanish.

Top 3 Risks
01
Memory capex cycle reversal. Memory was 46% of September-quarter systems revenue and the FY2026 result was driven by a sharp DRAM/NAND price spike. Equipment orders follow the change in memory pricing: if contract prices flatten, capex plans are cut within one to two quarters and Lam's revenue can fall 30% or more from peak. Even at 24× forward earnings the multiple offers little cushion for that scenario.
02
China — both export controls and substitution. A quarter to more than a third of revenue (26% in June, 35% in September) is at the mercy of US licensing decisions Lam does not control, and the Hua Hong halt shows the direction of travel. Simultaneously, evidence that China can produce domestic DUV lithography implies faster domestic etch and deposition qualification, structurally shrinking the trailing-edge and Reliant revenue that historically cushioned downturns.
03
Multiple compression on a high-beta name. With a beta of 1.85, a 34.3× trailing P/E and about 88% of its shares held by institutions, LRCX tends to swing harder than the market whenever the mood about AI spending shifts. A broad de-rating of AI infrastructure — or simply a cautious guide from Lam or a peer — can remove 20–40% of the price without any cut to Lam's own earnings, as in July 2026 and again from the end of September to October 23 (−30%).
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
The 2026 Memory Supercycle
EMERGING TAILWIND
This is not a normal memory upturn. Conventional DRAM contract prices rose about 93–98% quarter over quarter in Q1 2026, and TrendForce expected a further 58–63% in Q2 (June 1, 2026). For Lam this converts directly into equipment demand: management has raised its calendar 2026 WFE estimate in steps from the mid-$130s to the mid-$150 billion range, and on October 21 Tim Archer said: "Our preliminary 2027 WFE outlook is for more than $200 billion of spending." The asymmetry worth noting is that DRAM's share of Lam's systems revenue fell to 20% in the September quarter from 23% in June, while NAND rose to 26% — so the DRAM leg of this cycle has largely not yet been billed.
The NAND Upgrade Cycle — Lam's Highest-Leverage Exposure
EMERGING TAILWIND
NAND capex was effectively frozen from 2022 through 2025 while the industry absorbed oversupply. That freeze is now reversing for a specifically AI-driven reason: expanding context windows, persistent memory and KV-cache offloading push AI inference workloads onto enterprise SSDs, forcing conversions to 256-layer-plus and eventually 500-plus-layer devices. NAND's dollar revenue more than doubled from March to June, and its share of Lam's systems revenue rose again to 26% in the September quarter from 23%. Lam is more levered to this than any peer: vertical layer stacking is fundamentally an etch-and-deposition problem, lithography is largely irrelevant to it, and Lam has stated its served market per NAND wafer roughly doubles from the 128-layer node to 500-plus layers. Critically, much of the near-term spend flows as upgrades to existing chambers through CSBG — record upgrade revenue drove CSBG to $2.47B in June and nearly $2.5B in September, about 40% above a year earlier — which is faster to recognize and higher margin than greenfield systems. Advanced packaging, which the CFO still expects to grow 70% in calendar 2026 on HBM copper plating, sits alongside as a second AI-native line, with a first 310mm × 310mm panel-level tool due to ship this year.
Export Controls & Chinese Substitution — The Constraint That Sets the Multiple
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every valuation argument on Lam eventually reduces to one question: how much of Lam's revenue will China be in the long run? The record of the last three quarters is a swing from 34% of revenue in the March quarter to 26% in June and back to 35% in September, with the CFO expecting a significantly lower share in December. Add the April restrictions on shipments to Hua Hong and the company's own 10-K, which says China sales "are likely to be materially and adversely affected." The July 27 report of Shanghai state-backed mass production of domestic immersion DUV scanners came with falls of 4.5% that day and 7.5% the next, not because lithography is Lam's market but because it changed investors' view of how fast the entire Chinese toolchain is closing. Etch is the segment where domestic players (AMEC, Naura) are furthest advanced.

Bull framing: China's share already fell to 26% in June without denting growth, so the derating is largely done — Korea, Taiwan and the rest have absorbed the mix, AI-driven demand outside China is more than replacing it, and any thaw in US–China chip negotiations would be a bonus on top.

Bear framing: the substitution is permanent and compounding. Chinese trailing-edge tool purchases were a profit pool that cushioned prior downturns; if that base is structurally lost, Lam's next cyclical trough is deeper than any in its history, and a 34.3× trailing multiple is being applied to peak-cycle earnings from a shrinking addressable footprint. The 72% spread between the $290 low and $500 high analyst targets is precisely this disagreement, priced.
