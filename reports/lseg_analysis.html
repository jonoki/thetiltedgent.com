01 Company Overview

London Stock Exchange Group is no longer mainly a stock exchange. It is three businesses under one roof: a financial-data company (the former Refinitiv, bought in an all-share deal completed in January 2021, which sells the Workspace terminal, real-time price feeds and analytics), an index company (FTSE Russell, whose benchmarks sit under $2.19 trillion of exchange-traded funds), and market plumbing — the London Stock Exchange itself, the LCH clearing houses and a controlling stake in the bond-trading platform Tradeweb. In the first half of 2026 it booked total income (excluding pass-through recoveries) of £4,799M, up 6.9% reported and 8.4% on an organic, constant-currency basis, an adjusted EBITDA margin of 52.7%, and adjusted earnings per share of 244.9p, up 17.2%. At the October 2 close the company was worth £39.03B ($51.52B).

The puzzle is that the reported numbers keep improving while the share price keeps falling. The stock's best month-end in the five-year window was 12,035p in January 2025; at 8,090p it is 32.8% below that. The fall came in lumps: −7.9% on the day of the H1 2025 results (July 31, 2025), −12.8% on February 3, 2026, and −6.4% on July 30, 2026 — the day LSEG reported what it called a "record first half performance" and raised guidance. The thread running through it is the question of what artificial intelligence does to a company that sells financial data, which CEO David Schwimmer described on the July 30 call as the market "having what we could call the terminal value debate." Management's answer has been to buy shares: £2.1B in the first half alone, at an average of £88.39, which the interim report says reflected "the Board's view on the dislocation between the intrinsic value of the business and the prevailing share price." The investor's question is whether LSEG's data is the raw material AI needs (management's view) or whether the human-facing terminal business, Workflows, growing just 2.7% in Q2 2026, is the part AI routes around.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Benchmark indices (FTSE, Russell) LCH clearing network Proprietary real-time data Tradeweb liquidity World-Check compliance standard Regulated status & trust Microsoft partnership

LSEG's strongest assets are network businesses: a clearing house is valuable because everyone else clears there, an index because everyone else is measured against it, a trading venue because the liquidity is already there. None of those is easily copied by a chatbot. Management's argument for the data business is that AI raises the value of trusted, licensed data rather than replacing it — Schwimmer told analysts that "90% of our data revenues come from real-time or data that is proprietary," and that demand for financial data has decoupled from headcount, with industry spend more than doubling over 17 years while the number of people in the industry fell by a quarter. The Microsoft partnership (ten years from December 2022) is the distribution half of that bet: Workspace already runs inside Teams and is coming to Copilot, which Schwimmer said has 1.5 million users at LSEG's top 50 customers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The terminal is the soft spot. Workflows — the human-facing Workspace desktop — grew 2.7% in Q2 2026 and 2.8% in the half, the slowest line in the group and the one AI assistants most directly compete with. LSEG's own pricing plan folds AI Search into Workspace's annual price review, so its value has to show up as price increases customers accept.
02
The margin story has a one-off inside it. Of the 320 basis points of H1 adjusted EBITDA margin expansion, 140 came from the new SwapClear revenue-share agreement and 60 from currency; the company says the SwapClear uplift will largely reverse in Q4 2026. The full-year guide is "around 100 bps" of constant-currency improvement.
03
A dollar business reporting in pounds. 58% of H1 income was in US dollars. LSEG's own sensitivity: a 10-cent fall in the dollar against sterling cuts total income by about 4.0% and EBITDA by about 4.5%.
04
Fixed content and cloud costs. The 30-year Reuters news agreement (from 2018) carries a minimum, inflation-linked payment — US$398M for 2025 — and the Microsoft deal a minimum cloud spend of US$2.8B over its term, all of it still outstanding at June 30, 2026. Token and cloud costs also rise as AI usage grows; management says these are "fully factored into our midterm margin guidance."

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the comparisons. First, the SwapClear revenue-share change: struck in October 2025, it cut what LSEG pays the swap market's founding banks and lowered Markets' cost of sales 38% in H1; the 2025 benefit was all booked in H2 2025, so the year-on-year margin gain unwinds in Q4 2026. It also added about £80M to H1 cash flow. Second, the buyback: LSEG bought 23.4 million shares for £2.1B in H1 and the share count excluding treasury fell from 510 million at December 2025 to 482.46 million at September 30, 2026, so per-share growth runs well ahead of profit growth (equity free cash flow per share +37.0% against equity free cash flow +28.9%). Third, finance costs: adjusted net finance expense more than doubled to £149M (from £66M) on refinanced debt, the higher borrowing that funds the buyback, and £35M of one-off gains in H1 2025.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of 7,100p to the October 2, 2026 close of 8,090p, LSEG gained 13.9% in price. The FTSE 100 rose 44.5% over the same window (7,237.6 → 10,462.0, Yahoo Finance ^FTSE), so LSEG lagged its home index by about 30 points. The path was two trips: a steady climb from the November 2021 low of 6,502p to a peak of 12,035p in January 2025 (+85%), then a slide that has left the stock just 13.9% above where the window began. Over the last twelve months the stock is down 6.0% (8,610p on October 2, 2025) and in 2026 to date it is down 9.6% from 8,952p, while the FTSE 100 is up 5.3% year to date.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows the two halves of the story clearly. It reached 90.6 in January 2025, the most overbought reading in the series, then fell to 18.8 in September 2025, the most oversold. It has not reached 60 since, and stands at 37.7 — weak but not oversold. The 10-month moving average is about 8,623p, above the current price: the downtrend that began in early 2025 has not been broken, even after a record half-year.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 17.7x trailing adjusted earnings and 16.8x this year's consensus for a business that has just raised its revenue and margin guidance, grows subscriptions at 6.3% with retention improving to 92.8%, converts profit into cash (£2.7B of equity free cash flow, a 7.0% yield on the market cap) and is retiring shares fast — 510 million to 482 million in nine months. Its clearing, index and trading-venue businesses are network businesses that AI does not obviously touch, and together with FTSE Russell they are about half of income. If the AI fear turns out to be about the terminal alone, the market is pricing a whole-company problem for a problem in one line of one division, and the buyback is buying the gap at around 8,240–8,330p (daily averages in the week to September 25).

Bear Case

The bear case does not dispute the half-year; it disputes the terminal value. Workflows, the desktop that justified the Refinitiv price, grew 2.7% in Q2, and its future depends on customers paying for AI features through price increases. The H1 margin leap was flattered by a SwapClear agreement that reverses in Q4 and by volatility-driven trading that management already says moderated in Q2. Leverage rose to 2.1x to fund buybacks, statutory ROE is 7.6% on a balance sheet carrying £31B of intangibles, and a Hold and a Sell appeared between the May and August editions of the company's own consensus. A stock that falls 6.4% on a record result and raised guidance is telling you the market wants evidence the next leg of growth is not just volatility.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI pressure on the Workspace business. Workflows is £980M a half and growing under 3%. If AI assistants reduce the number of people who need a terminal, or customers refuse to pay more for AI Search through the annual price review, the largest line in the largest division stalls — the outcome the bear case rests on.
02
Markets normalising. Markets grew 11.9% on volatile trading conditions management called exceptional, and its margin includes a SwapClear benefit that largely reverses in Q4 2026. Calmer markets would slow the 40% of income that has been doing the most to lift the group's growth rate.
03
Sterling strength and leverage. 58% of income is in dollars, and a 10-cent fall in the dollar against the pound cuts income by about 4% and EBITDA by about 4.5%. At 2.1x leverage after £2.1B of buybacks in six months, the room to keep buying back stock while absorbing a currency or volume shock is narrower than it was at 1.8x.
Industry Commentary
AI and the "terminal value debate" for data vendors
CRITICAL QUESTION

Schwimmer framed it himself on July 30: the market "has been debating these topics in great detail for the last 12 months." The bear argument is that AI models will let customers get what they used to pay a terminal for more cheaply. LSEG's counter has three parts: its data is real-time or proprietary and AI models need licensed, auditable inputs; it is selling that data through every AI platform rather than defending one screen; and it has a price list for AI use — a recurring licence to use data sets with AI models, a recurring MCP access charge with usage bands to come, and fair-use limits on Workspace AI with charges above them. The evidence so far supports the data side (Data & Feeds accelerating to 7.7%, API usage of Tick History up 48%) and leaves the terminal side open (Workflows 2.7%). MCP revenue, by the CFO's own account, "won't move the needle in 2026."

A volatility dividend for exchanges and clearers
CYCLICAL TAILWIND

Volatile markets in 2026 lifted every venue LSEG runs: Tradeweb volume up 24.8%, UK equities value traded up 33.5%, SwapClear client trades up 23.8%, ForexClear notional up 45.2%, and higher cash collateral balances pushed net treasury income up 12.6%. It is also the part of LSEG's growth least under management's control, and the half-year report already describes Q2 volumes as moderating from an exceptional Q1. Structural projects — LSE 24, the Private Securities Market (first transactions in H1), digital settlement through LSEG DiSH and the DIGIT gilt pilot — are LSEG's attempt to add volume that does not depend on volatility.

Buybacks at a discount — the bull and bear framing
CAPITAL RETURN

LSEG's buybacks have stepped up: £2.1B of buybacks in 2025, £2.1B more in H1 2026 at an average £88.39, and up to £1.35B to come by February 2027, funded partly by taking leverage from 1.8x to 2.1x. The bull framing: management knows the order book, retention and cash flow better than anyone, called the share price a "dislocation," and is now buying at about 17.7x trailing adjusted earnings — 5.4% of the share count gone since end-2025 (510M → 482.46M), with each pound of future cash flow spread across fewer shares. The bear framing: the H1 buyback averaged £88.39 and the shares now trade at £80.90, so the first £2.1B was bought about 9% above today's price; a company confident about AI could also have spent that money on proving it. The deciding evidence will not be the buyback but the next four quarters of Workflows growth and gross sales.
