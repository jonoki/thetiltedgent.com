01 Company Overview

For fifty years Southwest sold one idea: no assigned seats, two bags fly free, and one kind of airplane. Founded in 1967 by Herb Kelleher and Rollin King and flying since June 1971 out of Dallas Love Field, it grew into an all-Boeing 737 airline of 803 aircraft serving more than 100 destinations. The ticker says the rest: LUV. Then in June 2024 an activist, Elliott Investment Management, disclosed a roughly $1.9 billion stake and argued that the charm had stopped paying. Within two years the bags stopped flying free (from May 28, 2025), open seating ended (assigned seating began January 27, 2026), the company made its first-ever mass layoff (about 1,750 corporate roles, ~15% of corporate staff, in 2025), and it added premium seats, extra legroom, basic economy fares and red-eye flights.

The reinvention is showing up in revenue. In Q3 2026 operating revenue was a third-quarter record $8.42B, up 21.2%, and RASM rose 21.6% to an all-time company record of 18.55¢ while capacity shrank 0.4%. TTM revenue is $31.55B, up 14.5% on the prior twelve months, and TTM GAAP EPS is $2.08, against $0.79 for FY2025. The trouble is fuel. Southwest ended its fuel-hedging program in early 2025, and in March 2026 WTI crude hit $104.69 after, as 24/7 Wall St. reported, the Strait of Hormuz closed during the U.S.–Iran conflict. Q3 fuel cost $3.92 a gallon versus $2.40 a year earlier, $821M of extra expense in one quarter, and the company assumes about $4.55 for Q4 on the October 15 forward curve. The full-year adjusted EPS guide has come down twice: from "at least $4.00" to $3.25–$4.25 in July, and to $2.60–$2.85 on October 21. So the question for this stock is still whether the new revenue engine can outrun an unhedged fuel bill.

The airline arithmetic: RASM vs. CASM

An airline is a spread business. It earns revenue per seat-mile (RASM) and pays cost per seat-mile (CASM). CASM-X removes fuel, so it shows the part of cost that management actually controls. In Q3 2026 the spread was still thin: 18.55¢ earned vs 17.72¢ spent per seat-mile. The 0.83¢ margin × 45.4B seat-miles ≈ $377M, which ties to reported operating income of $379M.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Single-type 737 fleet Dallas Love Field home base Rapid Rewards loyalty program Chase co-brand economics #1 J.D. Power economy satisfaction (5 yrs) Investment-grade balance sheet

The old moat was simplicity. With one aircraft type, pilots, mechanics and spare parts are interchangeable, and a point-to-point network with fast turns kept costs below the hub carriers'. The new strategy deliberately gives up part of that simplicity to earn more per seat: premium rows, fewer seats on the 737-700s, lounges, a premium card. The customer franchise has so far survived the changes. Southwest ranked #1 in J.D. Power customer satisfaction among economy passengers for a fifth straight year, and its CFO says it was first in the Wall Street Journal's 2025 operational rankings. What it gave up is the "different from everyone else" story that made price-sensitive leisure travellers loyal without a loyalty program.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
No fuel hedge. Southwest ended its hedging program in early 2025, so every move in jet fuel goes straight to earnings. Higher fuel cost $1.08 of adjusted EPS in Q3 alone, and the Q4 guide assumes about $4.55 a gallon, up from $3.92 in Q3. In the August 2026 sell-off Southwest fell 14% while Delta, American and United lost 11–12%; 24/7 Wall St. put the gap down to Southwest's lack of a fuel hedge.
02
Thin unit margin. The 0.83¢ RASM-over-CASM spread in Q3 is only about 4.5% of revenue. Fuel cost 4.74¢ per seat-mile at $3.92 a gallon; at the $4.55 assumed for Q4, the same burn would cost about 0.76¢ more per seat-mile (our arithmetic: 4.74¢ × $4.55 ÷ $3.92 − 4.74¢), almost the whole Q3 spread. The initiatives have widened the revenue side, but not by enough to make the business fuel-proof.
03
The brand trade-off is not yet tested through a downturn. The new fees and seats were introduced into strong demand. How loyal travellers stay once free bags and open seating are gone will only become clear when demand softens. Q3 revenue passengers were down 3.2% year over year, even though yield more than made up for it.
04
Boeing dependency. An all-737 fleet means Boeing's delivery schedule is Southwest's growth schedule. The company expects 67 737-8 and 737-7 deliveries in 2026 while retiring about 60 aircraft, and the CFO said 2027 deliveries should be higher than 2026's. The FAA certified the 737-7 on August 3, 2026, and on the call the CFO said 27 already-built 737-7s start arriving at the end of 2026 and enter service in 2027. Any delivery delay keeps the older 737-700s flying longer.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the $285M breakage reversal in Q2 2026. That is revenue booked in 2022–2025 for flight credits Southwest assumed would expire unused, taken back because customers are redeeming more of them. It is why Q2 GAAP EPS was $0.47 while adjusted EPS was $0.94, and it is still inside the trailing figures. Second, Q3's special items: $30M of asset impairments, $30M of severance and $13M of litigation accruals, which help separate Q3 GAAP EPS of $0.55 from adjusted EPS of $0.66. Third, buybacks: $1.25B repurchased in the first nine months of 2026, none of it in Q3, with $450M left on a $2.0B authorization. The share count fell 9.8% year over year (StockAnalysis), so per-share growth runs ahead of profit growth.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The reinvention is working. RASM rose 21.6% in Q3 on slightly lower capacity, in the first full quarter to lap last year's bag fees, while CASM-X grew 3.8%. The CFO says the seating products are on track to go from $1 billion of EBIT in 2026 to about $1.5 billion in 2027, "if not ahead." Consensus EPS is $2.82 for FY2026 and $4.33 for FY2027 (StockAnalysis). At $41.75 the stock trades at 9.6x FY2027 consensus, the share count fell 9.8% in a year, and management will set out multi-year targets at a Capital Markets Day on March 2, 2027. If oil simply stops rising, the revenue gains should flow through to earnings.

Bear Case

Southwest has swapped a cost advantage for a pricing strategy and removed its fuel hedge at the same moment. Q3 unit margin was 0.83¢ per seat-mile, and the Q4 guide assumes fuel about 16% dearer than in Q3. The FY2026 guide has been cut twice, from "at least $4.00" to $2.60–$2.85. Free cash flow is negative on a trailing basis (−$450M per StockAnalysis), $2.15B of debt falls due within a year, and passenger counts are falling. Three analysts rate the stock a sell, and targets run as low as $34. At 20x trailing GAAP earnings, the stock is priced on a forecast that depends on the oil price.

Industry Commentary
Fuel shock without a hedge
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Per 24/7 Wall St., the closure of the Strait of Hormuz during the U.S.–Iran conflict pushed WTI up 56.3% in a month to $104.69 by March 30, 2026. Southwest had terminated its hedging program in early 2025, so it absorbed the shock at spot prices. Management has cut 2026 capacity growth to about 1.2% and cut the EPS range twice. Q3 fuel came in at $3.92 a gallon, above the $3.70–$3.75 it had assumed, and Q4 assumes about $4.55 while capacity grows 3–4% year over year, a bet that the revenue initiatives can cover fuel.

Activism as a business plan
EMERGING TAILWIND

Most activist campaigns end in a board reshuffle. Elliott's changed Southwest's product: bags, seats, fare classes, lounges, cards, and a first-ever layoff. By April 2026 the activist had cut its holding to 4.9% of the shares, for "portfolio management purposes" in its SEC filing's words, and two of its directors had left the board. The bull framing: the airline now charges for extras the way its rivals do while keeping a low-cost structure, a combination that did not exist before. The bear framing: the easy wins (turning free things into paid ones) are one-offs that are now being lapped. Q3 was the first full quarter to lap the bag fees and RASM still rose 21.6%; the CEO now calls the transformation complete. From here growth has to come from harder work.

In September the CFO said that, adjusted for inflation since 2019, unit revenue still lags most other products even after this year's increases. Q3's 18.55¢ is an all-time company record in plain dollars, and Q4 is guided up another 19–21%. Read one way, pricing is still catching up with the cost base and has room to run. Read the other way, the 2025–2026 surge has mostly recovered lost ground. The Capital Markets Day on March 2, 2027 is where management says it will lay out what comes next.
