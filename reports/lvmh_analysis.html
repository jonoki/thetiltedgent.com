01 Company Overview

LVMH is the house of houses: Louis Vuitton, Dior, Tiffany & Co., Bvlgari, Loro Piana, Celine, Fendi, Moët & Chandon, Dom Pérignon, Hennessy and Sephora all sit under one roof in Paris. FY2025 revenue was €80.81B, down 5% reported and down 1% organic, with profit from recurring operations of €17.76B (−9%) and diluted EPS of €21.85. The first half of 2026 brought €38.64B of revenue (−3% reported, +2% organic) and net profit of €5.70B, flat on the year; the first nine months brought €57.15B (−2% reported, +2% organic). The stock tells a harsher story than the accounts: the October 16, 2026 close of €397.00 is 38.4% below the December 2025 close of €645.00, and the September 2026 month-end close of €390.00 is the lowest point on this report's five-year chart.

The latest print (Q3 2026 revenue, October 12). Revenue was €18.50B, up 1% organically, against +1.3% expected in a Visible Alpha consensus cited by Reuters. The miss sat in one place: Fashion & Leather Goods fell 5% organically against an expected 1.8% decline, while every other business group grew. The release says business in China was less buoyant, while the United States, Europe and Japan saw good growth; Reuters reported Asia down 5% and the US up 5%. Reuters quoted finance chief Cécile Cabanis: "In the third quarter specifically, Asia was penalised by a sharp macro-led deterioration in Chinese consumer sentiment, with a tangible impact on discretionary expenses." Reuters also reported her saying that LVMH does not believe in drastic measures such as price cuts. On the analyst call she said Louis Vuitton "was also impacted by a one-off logistic and transportation disruption from one of our freight forwarder, which costed a bit more than a point of growth for the quarter," and that currencies would again cut the operating margin by about 70 basis points (0.7 percentage points) in the second half, similar to the first half. The outlook wording was repeated: the group "remains confident". Over nine months revenue was €57.15B (+2% organic, −2% reported). LVMH publishes only revenue for the third quarter, so the profit figures on this page are still the half-year's.

The half-year (H1 2026, July 27). The headline numbers beat; the division that matters most did not. AFP reported that net profit (€5.7B) and first-half sales (€38.6B) both beat the FactSet consensus. Reuters, citing Visible Alpha, said second-quarter sales of €19.5B (+3% organic) were broadly in line, while Fashion & Leather Goods' +1% fell short of the 1.7% analysts expected. On the call, finance chief Cécile Cabanis said "Vuitton and Dior are both in positive territories in Q2. Vuitton is consistent with the average. Dior is a bit above," and put the division's lower profit down to exchange rates: "At constant currencies, EBIT margin improved modestly, and operating income was flat." LVMH gives no numeric guidance. Its outlook reads: "Despite a geopolitical and economic environment that remains uncertain, the Group remains confident and will maintain a strategy focused on continuously enhancing the desirability of its brands …" Cabanis told analysts she expects currencies to take roughly the same bite out of margins in the second half as in the first.

Think of LVMH as a portfolio with one dominant hand. Fashion & Leather Goods (mostly Louis Vuitton and Dior) brought in 47% of revenue but 74% of recurring operating profit in FY2025, at a 35% margin. Everything else (champagne and cognac, perfume and make-up, watches and jewellery, Sephora) is a strong business in its own right, but none of it moves the group the way leather goods do. That is why the market reads LVMH through a single question: is the slowdown in handbags a pause or a new normal? Over the first nine months of 2026, Fashion & Leather Goods was −2% organic (+1% in Q2, then −5% in Q3), while watches and jewellery grew 8% and Selective Retailing (Sephora, DFS) 6%. The rest of the portfolio is growing; the hand that matters most is shrinking again.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Century-old brand equity Louis Vuitton scale Owned store network (6,283) Pricing power Portfolio across five categories Controlling family, long horizon Cash generation (€11.3B FCF)

The moat is desirability that cannot be manufactured quickly. Nobody can launch a rival to Louis Vuitton or Dom Pérignon in five years at any price, and LVMH sells through 6,283 stores of its own, which lets it set prices and keep the full margin. That is why Fashion & Leather Goods runs at a 34–35% operating margin. The portfolio also spreads risk: in 2025, when leather-goods profit fell 13%, Selective Retailing (Sephora, DFS) profit rose 28%. The weakness is that a moat protects the price, not the volume. When aspirational buyers in China, Korea or the US pull back, a luxury house can hold prices but cannot make people buy, and a high fixed cost base (stores, staff, marketing) means profit falls faster than sales.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One division earns most of the profit. Fashion & Leather Goods earned 74% of FY2025 recurring profit and 71% in H1 2026, and its H1 profit fell 7%. Strong jewellery, Sephora and cognac numbers cannot offset a soft leather-goods year, because together they are smaller than Louis Vuitton and Dior.
02
Operating leverage works both ways. FY2025 revenue fell 5% and recurring profit fell 9%. RBC's September 22, 2026 downgrade made the same point: it argued that LVMH's high fixed costs make its earnings especially sensitive to slower sales.
03
Currency. Only 21% of revenue is invoiced in euros. Currency cut 5 points from reported revenue growth in H1 2026, and the company attributes €1,065M of the 2025 profit decline to exchange rates. A strong euro shrinks the reported numbers even when demand is fine.
04
China and the Middle East. Asia excluding Japan was 31% of revenue in 2023 and 26% in 2025. HSBC's September 9 downgrade cited softer mainland China sentiment and a tougher basis of comparison, the company named the Middle East conflict as a drag on Q2 2026, and in Q3 Reuters reported Asia down 5% as Chinese consumer sentiment deteriorated.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the headline figures. First, currency: in H1 2026 exchange rates took 5 points off reported revenue growth, and in FY2025 they cut recurring profit by €1,065M of a €1,816M decline (company bridge). Second, disposals: DFS's Greater China business, two US airport concessions, DFS Okinawa and Marc Jacobs are sold or being sold, which trims revenue (−1 point in H1 2026) without much effect on profit. Third, buybacks: net purchases of its own shares (purchases less disposals) reduced equity by €1,659M in H1 2026, and LVMH cancelled 1,875,860 shares; the share count fell from 497,686,940 at December 31, 2025 to 494,606,080 at July 31, August 31 and September 30, 2026, which is why TTM EPS (€21.94) edged up while TTM net profit was flat (€10,877M). Other operating items were a small €23M gain in H1 2026 against a €656M charge in FY2025.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of €620.10 to the October 16, 2026 close of €397.00, LVMH lost 36.0% of its price. Adding back the €61.00 of dividends paid over the period (not reinvested) makes it about −26.1%. Over the same window the CAC 40 rose 22.0% on price. Relative performance

Against its home index LVMH has lagged badly: −36.0% over five years against +22.0% for the CAC 40, and −34.1% over one year against −2.8% (price only, Yahoo Finance closes, our calculation). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 18.1x trailing earnings for what LVMH calls the world's leading high-quality products group, at a 22.5% operating margin, with €11.4B of free cash flow (a 5.8% yield), a 3.27% dividend and gearing of just 11.8%. The rest of the hand is improving: group organic growth went from −4% in Q2 2025 to +3% in Q2 2026 and +1% in Q3, and in Q3 Selective Retailing grew 8% while Wines & Spirits and Watches & Jewelry each grew 6%. Bloomberg, citing Paris market filings, reported family purchases of about 757,000 shares in late January and early February 2026, and on September 23 the family group announced a plan to bring substantially all of its 50.33% stake together in a single listed company, which UBS read as a strong signal of confidence. Grupo Santander upgraded to Outperform in September, Goldman Sachs started at Buy in October, and DZ Bank and Deutsche Bank kept Buy ratings after the Q3 miss. In September the monthly RSI fell to 22.4, close to its June 2025 low of 20.6, which preceded a 45% rise in six months, and the consensus target of €502.95 sits 27% above the price.

Bear Case

The engine is stalled. Fashion & Leather Goods earns over 70% of profit, was −2% organic over the first nine months of 2026 and −5% in Q3 (worse than the −1.8% analysts expected), and its H1 profit fell 7%. Operating margin has fallen from 26.5% (2023) to about 22%, and with a high fixed cost base it can fall further if sales soften. HSBC and RBC downgraded in September; after the print JPMorgan, UBS, Jefferies and DZ Bank cut their targets, and Deutsche Bank expects consensus forecasts for 2027 to come down. LVMH blamed a sharp deterioration in Chinese consumer sentiment for the Q3 fall in Asia. Only 21% of revenue is in euros, so a strong euro keeps eating reported growth. An 18x multiple was cheap when the business grew double digits; for a group whose EPS fell from €30.34 (2023, basic) to €21.86 (2025, basic), it may not be.

Top 3 Risks

01
Leather goods do not recover. Louis Vuitton and Dior drive most of the profit. Q2's +1% organic growth turned into −5% in Q3. If that persists, the 34% division margin and the group's earnings both come under pressure; RBC models only 1% division growth for FY2027.
02
Macro and geopolitics hit the luxury buyer. The Middle East conflict dragged on Q2 2026, and in Q3 LVMH blamed a sharp deterioration in Chinese consumer sentiment for the fall in Asia. Luxury demand from aspirational buyers is the most sensitive to falling markets and confidence.
03
Currency translation. Euro strength cut H1 2026 reported revenue growth by 5 points and FY2025 profit by over €1B. It can keep reported numbers falling even in a demand recovery.
Industry Commentary
The leather-goods cycle has turned against the whole sector
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Over the year to October 16, 2026, Hermès fell 38.1%, LVMH 34.1% and Kering 29.7%, all far behind a CAC 40 that was down 2.8%. All three are built around fashion and leather goods, and LVMH's own numbers show the strain: Fashion & Leather Goods organic growth was negative in every quarter of 2025 and −2% in Q1 2026, then +1% in Q2 and −5% in Q3. The bull argument is that this is a pause after an unusual boom; the bear argument is that a higher price level has priced out the aspirational buyer for good. Q3 put the division back below zero; the FY2026 results, estimated for late January 2027, are the next data point.

Hard luxury is holding up
DIVERGENCE

Jewellery is the exception. Richemont rose 9.3% over the same year, and LVMH's Watches & Jewelry division grew 8% organic over the first nine months of 2026 (11% in Q2, 6% in Q3). For LVMH this is a partial hedge: Tiffany and Bvlgari are growing, but the division is about a seventh of revenue and under a tenth of profit.

Valuation after the reset
EMERGING TAILWIND

At €397.00 LVMH trades at 18.1x trailing and 18.1x forward earnings, with a 3.27% dividend yield and a 5.8% free-cash-flow yield. The dividend has been held at €13.00 for three years through falling profit. The bull framing: a cyclical low in demand meets a balance sheet with gearing of 11.8%, family share purchases reported in early 2026, and analysts whose lowest target is 5% above the price. The bear framing: the targets lag, the margin is still drifting down, and a stock whose earnings fell for two straight years does not deserve a premium multiple until they stop falling. Q3 sided with the bears on leather goods; the FY2026 margin, due with the annual results, is the next test.
