01 Company Overview

LVMH is the house of houses: Louis Vuitton, Dior, Tiffany & Co., Bvlgari, Loro Piana, Celine, Fendi, Moët & Chandon, Dom Pérignon, Hennessy and Sephora all sit under one roof in Paris. FY2025 revenue was €80.81B, down 5% reported and down 1% organic, with profit from recurring operations of €17.76B (−9%) and diluted EPS of €21.85. The first half of 2026 brought €38.64B of revenue (−3% reported, +2% organic) and net profit of €5.70B, flat on the year. The stock tells a harsher story than the accounts: the October 2, 2026 close of €378.55 is 41.3% below the December 2025 close of €645.00 and the lowest point on this report's five-year chart.

The latest print (H1 2026, July 27). The headline numbers beat; the division that matters most did not. AFP reported that net profit (€5.7B) and first-half sales (€38.6B) both beat the FactSet consensus. Reuters, citing Visible Alpha, said second-quarter sales of €19.5B (+3% organic) were broadly in line, while Fashion & Leather Goods' +1% fell short of the 1.7% analysts expected. On the call, finance chief Cécile Cabanis said "Vuitton and Dior are both in positive territories in Q2. Vuitton is consistent with the average. Dior is a bit above," and put the division's lower profit down to exchange rates: "At constant currencies, EBIT margin improved modestly, and operating income was flat." LVMH gives no numeric guidance. Its outlook reads: "Despite a geopolitical and economic environment that remains uncertain, the Group remains confident and will maintain a strategy focused on continuously enhancing the desirability of its brands …" Cabanis told analysts she expects currencies to take roughly the same bite out of margins in the second half as in the first.

Think of LVMH as a portfolio with one dominant hand. Fashion & Leather Goods (mostly Louis Vuitton and Dior) brought in 47% of revenue but 74% of recurring operating profit in FY2025, at a 35% margin. Everything else (champagne and cognac, perfume and make-up, watches and jewellery, Sephora) is a strong business in its own right, but none of it moves the group the way leather goods do. That is why the market reads LVMH through a single question: is the slowdown in handbags a pause or a new normal? In H1 2026, Fashion & Leather Goods was −1% organic for the half and +1% in Q2, while watches and jewellery grew 9% and Selective Retailing (Sephora, DFS) 5%. The rest of the portfolio is growing; the hand that matters most is flat.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Century-old brand equity Louis Vuitton scale Owned store network (6,283) Pricing power Portfolio across five categories Controlling family, long horizon Cash generation (€11.3B FCF)

The moat is desirability that cannot be manufactured quickly. Nobody can launch a rival to Louis Vuitton or Dom Pérignon in five years at any price, and LVMH sells through 6,283 stores of its own, which lets it set prices and keep the full margin. That is why Fashion & Leather Goods runs at a 34–35% operating margin. The portfolio also spreads risk: in 2025, when leather-goods profit fell 13%, Selective Retailing (Sephora, DFS) profit rose 28%. The weakness is that a moat protects the price, not the volume. When aspirational buyers in China, Korea or the US pull back, a luxury house can hold prices but cannot make people buy, and a high fixed cost base (stores, staff, marketing) means profit falls faster than sales.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One division earns most of the profit. Fashion & Leather Goods earned 74% of FY2025 recurring profit and 71% in H1 2026, and its H1 profit fell 7%. Strong jewellery, Sephora and cognac numbers cannot offset a soft leather-goods year, because together they are smaller than Louis Vuitton and Dior.
02
Operating leverage works both ways. FY2025 revenue fell 5% and recurring profit fell 9%. RBC's September 22, 2026 downgrade made the same point: it argued that LVMH's high fixed costs make its earnings especially sensitive to slower sales.
03
Currency. Only 21% of revenue is invoiced in euros. Currency cut 5 points from reported revenue growth in H1 2026, and the company attributes €1,065M of the 2025 profit decline to exchange rates. A strong euro shrinks the reported numbers even when demand is fine.
04
China and the Middle East. Asia excluding Japan was 31% of revenue in 2023 and 26% in 2025. HSBC's September 9 downgrade cited softer mainland China sentiment and a tougher basis of comparison, and the company names the Middle East conflict as a drag on Q2 2026.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the headline figures. First, currency: in H1 2026 exchange rates took 5 points off reported revenue growth, and in FY2025 they cut recurring profit by €1,065M of a €1,816M decline (company bridge). Second, disposals: DFS's Greater China business, two US airport concessions, DFS Okinawa and Marc Jacobs are sold or being sold, which trims revenue (−1 point in H1 2026) without much effect on profit. Third, buybacks: net purchases of its own shares (purchases less disposals) reduced equity by €1,659M in H1 2026, and LVMH cancelled 1,875,860 shares; the share count fell from 497,686,940 at December 31, 2025 to 494,606,080 at July 31 and August 31, 2026, which is why TTM EPS (€21.94) edged up while TTM net profit was flat (€10,877M). Other operating items were a small €23M gain in H1 2026 against a €656M charge in FY2025.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of €620.10 to the October 2, 2026 close of €378.55, LVMH lost 39.0% of its price. Adding back the €61.00 of dividends paid over the period (not reinvested) makes it about −29.1%. Over the same window the CAC 40 rose 21.1% on price. The shape is a round trip and then a slide: a run to an €871.00 peak in April 2023 (+40% from the start), a two-year decline to €444.60 in June 2025, a sharp rebound to €645.00 by December 2025 (+45%), and then a 2026 that has taken the stock 41.3% lower, with three months of roughly −15% each (January, March, September).

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 21.0 at the October 2 point, after 22.4 at the end of September. That is deep in oversold territory and close to the June 2025 reading of 20.6, the lowest in the series, which preceded a 45% six-month rebound. The 10-month moving average is about €466, roughly 23% above the price, so the long-term trend is still pointing down. Oversold says the selling has been one-sided; it does not say it is finished.

Against its home index LVMH has lagged badly: −39.0% over five years against +21.1% for the CAC 40, and −30.3% over one year against −2.0% (price only, Yahoo Finance closes, our calculation). Against luxury peers the one-year picture is mixed rather than uniquely bad: Hermès fell further (−39.4%) and Kering fell less (−26.7%), while Richemont, the jewellery-heavy group, rose 12.5%. That pattern matches LVMH's own segment numbers, where jewellery is the strongest division and fashion the weakest. The sell-off is a verdict on the leather-goods cycle across the sector, not on LVMH alone.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 17.3x trailing earnings for what LVMH calls the world's leading high-quality products group, at a 22.5% operating margin, with €11.4B of free cash flow (a 6.1% yield), a 3.43% dividend and gearing of just 11.8%. The rest of the hand is already improving: group organic growth went from −4% in Q2 2025 to +3% in Q2 2026, watches and jewellery accelerated to +11%, Selective Retailing grew 5%, and Wines & Spirits turned positive. Leather goods returned to organic growth in Q2 2026 for the first time since Q2 2024. Bloomberg, citing Paris market filings, reported family purchases of about 757,000 shares in late January and early February 2026, and on September 23 the family group announced a plan to bring substantially all of its 50.33% stake together in a single listed company, which UBS read as a strong signal of confidence. Grupo Santander upgraded to Outperform in September. The last time RSI was this low (June 2025), the stock rose 45% in six months, and the consensus target of €533.89 sits 41% above the price.

Bear Case

The engine is stalled. Fashion & Leather Goods earns over 70% of profit and was −1% organic in H1 2026, with its profit down 7%. Operating margin has fallen from 26.5% (2023) to about 22%, and with a high fixed cost base it can fall further if sales soften. HSBC and RBC downgraded in September, RBC sees FY2027 EPS 9% below consensus, Bernstein expects Fashion & Leather Goods to shrink 4% organically in Q3 against a +1.2% consensus, and analyst targets have been falling more slowly than the price. Only 21% of revenue is in euros, so a strong euro keeps eating reported growth. A 17x multiple was cheap when the business grew double digits; for a group whose EPS fell from €30.34 (2023, basic) to €21.86 (2025, basic), it may not be.

Top 3 Risks

01
Leather goods do not recover. Louis Vuitton and Dior drive most of the profit. If Q2's +1% organic growth fades, the 34% division margin and the group's earnings both come under pressure; RBC models only 1% division growth for FY2027, and Bernstein (−4% in Q3) and Barclays (−2%) now forecast a decline.
02
Macro and geopolitics hit the luxury buyer. The Middle East conflict already dragged on Q2 2026, and HSBC flags softer sentiment in mainland China. Luxury demand from aspirational buyers is the most sensitive to falling markets and confidence.
03
Currency translation. Euro strength cut H1 2026 reported revenue growth by 5 points and FY2025 profit by over €1B. It can keep reported numbers falling even in a demand recovery.
Industry Commentary
The leather-goods cycle has turned against the whole sector
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Over the year to October 2, 2026, Hermès fell 39.4%, LVMH 30.3% and Kering 26.7%, all far behind a CAC 40 that was down 2.0%. All three are built around fashion and leather goods, and LVMH's own numbers show the strain: Fashion & Leather Goods organic growth was negative in every quarter of 2025 and −2% in Q1 2026 before a +1% Q2. The bull argument is that this is a pause after an unusual boom; the bear argument is that a higher price level has priced out the aspirational buyer for good. LVMH's Q3 revenue on October 12 is the next data point.

Hard luxury is holding up
DIVERGENCE

Jewellery is the exception. Richemont rose 12.5% over the same year, and LVMH's Watches & Jewelry division grew 9% organic in H1 2026, accelerating to 11% in Q2. For LVMH this is a partial hedge: Tiffany and Bvlgari are growing, but the division is about a seventh of revenue and under a tenth of profit.

Valuation after the reset
EMERGING TAILWIND

At €378.55 LVMH trades at 17.3x trailing and 17.1x forward earnings, with a 3.43% dividend yield and a 6.1% free-cash-flow yield. The dividend has been held at €13.00 for three years through falling profit. The bull framing: a cyclical low in demand meets a balance sheet with gearing of 11.8%, family share purchases reported in early 2026, and analysts whose lowest target is 11% above the price. The bear framing: the targets lag, the margin is still drifting down, and a stock whose earnings fell for two straight years does not deserve a premium multiple until they stop falling. The October 12 print and the FY2026 margin will decide which is right.
