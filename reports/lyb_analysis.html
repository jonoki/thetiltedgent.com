01 Company Overview

LyondellBasell makes the plastic that most of the world's packaging, pipes and car parts start as. It cracks ethane and other feedstocks into olefins, turns those into polyethylene and polypropylene, and sells intermediates, compounded polymers and — as the largest licensor of polyethylene and polypropylene technologies — the process know-how itself. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $31.19B, down 1.75%, and trailing GAAP EPS is −$1.12. At $60.24 the equity is worth $19.46B, against $14.33B of debt.

This is a commodity business, and the last eighteen months are what a commodity cycle looks like from the inside. FY2025 revenue fell 9.71% to $30.15B, operating margin shrank to 3.12%, and Q3 2025 brought $1.202B of non-cash write-downs, mostly goodwill in the European polyolefins and Advanced Polymer Solutions segments. The stock closed December 2025 at $43.30, its lowest month-end in five years. On February 20, 2026 the board cut the quarterly dividend from $1.37 to $0.69; the company called it a "recalibration". On May 1, 2026 it closed the sale of four European olefins and polyolefins sites to AEQUITA and booked a $734M pre-tax loss. Then the cycle turned the other way: global supply disruption, which the company ties to the conflict in the Middle East, drove what it calls the largest polyethylene price increase on record, and Q2 2026 EBITDA excluding identified items reached $2.1B, more than three times Q1's $615M. The stock is up 39% from the December low and 28% below its 52-week high. The question is whether a smaller, cheaper-to-run company can earn its new dividend once the shortage ends.

The dividend math: why the payout was halved

A dividend is safe when free cash flow pays for it through the cycle, not in the best quarter. Here is the arithmetic, using the current 323.04M shares for every year (an assumption, because the share count moved slightly).

Old dividend: annual cost
$1.77B
$1.37 × 4 × 323.04M. Q3 2025 alone paid $443M in dividends. FY2025 free cash flow was $384M, which covered about a fifth of that bill. The gap was funded from the balance sheet.
New dividend: annual cost
$0.89B
$0.69 × 4 × 323.04M = $891.6M. Q2 2026 dividends paid were $224M, consistent with that run-rate.
Coverage on TTM FCF
1.77x
TTM free cash flow of $1,578M ÷ $891.6M. The old dividend would have been covered 0.89x by the same cash flow. StockAnalysis puts the trailing FCF payout (dividends actually paid) at 70.42%.
The catch
One quarter
TTM FCF includes Q4 2025 (+$1,057M) and Q2 2026 (+$482M), and Q1 2026 was −$538M. The company's stated policy is to return 70% of free cash flow to shareholders "through the cycle".
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

US ethane feedstock advantage Polyolefin process licensing World-scale integrated sites PO/TBA co-production chain Investment-grade balance sheet $7.09B liquidity

Commodity chemicals have no brand moat. What they have is a cost-curve position: every tonne of polyethylene sells at roughly the same world price, and the price is set by the marginal, highest-cost producer. A cracker running on cheap US ethane sits near the bottom of that curve. It earns thin margins in a glut, and fat margins whenever supply elsewhere is lost, as Q2 2026 showed. The European sale is an admission of the other side of that rule: the Berre, Münchsmünster, Carrington and Tarragona assets sat higher on the cost curve, and the company paid $310M in cash and took a $734M loss to leave. The licensing arm is the only part with a moat in the ordinary sense.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The upturn comes from a supply shock, not from demand. According to the Q2 call summary, about 6 million tonnes of polyethylene capacity was damaged, 20–25% of Middle East supply, and is unlikely to restart before 2027. Margins that come from someone else's outage go away when that capacity returns.
02
Leverage is high for a cyclical business. Total debt is $14.33B against $2.65B of cash, so net debt is $11.68B. Debt/EBITDA is 3.56x on a TTM EBITDA of $3.57B, and that EBITDA already includes the exceptional Q2 2026. The company names its investment-grade rating as a priority; cutting the dividend was partly how it protected that rating.
03
Europe is smaller, but not gone. Wesseling, Botlek, Fos, Brindisi (closing by end-2026) and APS in Tarragona remain. The Q3 2025 write-downs were caused by "a prolonged downturn in, and outlook for, the European petrochemical and global automotive industries", and European operating rates are guided at about 70% for Q3 2026.
04
The 12-year growth record is broken. StockAnalysis still counts 12 years of dividend growth, but the dividend fell 36.53% year on year. Income investors who owned the stock for the growth streak no longer have that reason to hold it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The cost base has been reset permanently while the price is set by the cycle. The company sold its highest-cost European crackers, cut 3,400 jobs, reduced capex to about $1.2B a year and halved the dividend to a level that TTM free cash flow covers 1.77x. It did all of this before the upturn. With about 6 million tonnes of Middle East polyethylene capacity described as offline until at least 2027, the low-cost US crackers can earn well above mid-cycle margins for several more quarters. At 6.5x FY2026 consensus EPS of $9.24, even a partial hold of Q2's $2.1B quarterly EBITDA would bring leverage down quickly, and a smaller company with a 4.58% covered yield would look far better in the next trough than the old one did in the last.

Bear Case

Take away one quarter of shortage pricing and little recovery is left. TTM EPS is still −$1.12. Net debt is $11.68B at 3.56x TTM EBITDA that already contains Q2 2026. FY2025 free cash flow of $384M could not have paid even the new $892M dividend. The damaged Middle East capacity will come back, and each tonne restored pushes polyethylene back down the cost curve. Goldman and Bank of America both kept Sell ratings after a 29% EPS beat. The stock has already given back a quarter of its March level. The "through the cycle" dividend policy has only been tested in one direction so far, and in that test the payout was cut.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margin normalisation when disrupted supply returns. Q2 2026 gross margin was 22.55%, against 4.85% two quarters earlier. The upturn depends on capacity outside the company's control staying offline, which the Q2 call summary describes as unlikely to change before 2027.
02
Dividend coverage in a normal year. At $0.69 a quarter the dividend costs about $892M a year. That is covered 1.77x by TTM free cash flow, but TTM includes a −$538M quarter and FY2025 produced only $384M. A second cut is not the base case, but it is possible if Q3 and Q4 cash flow drop back toward 2025 levels.
03
Remaining European exposure and leverage. Wesseling, Botlek, Fos and Tarragona APS remain in a region where the company impaired goodwill in 2025 and guides about 70% operating rates. Debt/equity is 1.33 and Altman Z is 2.04, so there is little room for another write-down cycle.
Industry Commentary
A supply shock rescued a long downturn
CURRENT TAILWIND

In January 2026 the signs of recovery were small. Per tikr.com, US polyethylene inventory fell from 45 days on hand in August 2025 to 40 days in November, industry operating rates had dropped to 83% from the mid-90s, and a single broker lifting its downside catalyst watch was enough to make LYB the best S&P 500 performer that day (+6%). By April the company was reporting the largest polyethylene price increase on record, $0.30/lb, which it attributes to global supply disruption from the conflict in the Middle East. Q2 2026 EBITDA excluding items more than tripled from the prior quarter to $2.1B. The industry did not fix its own oversupply. Someone else's capacity went offline.

Europe: paying to exit the high-cost end of the curve
STRUCTURAL HEADWIND

In June 2025, LyondellBasell agreed to sell four European O&P sites to AEQUITA, and the deal closed on May 1, 2026. It cost a $734M accounting loss and a $310M cash contribution. Between those dates the company wrote down $400M of European O&P goodwill. The message is that European commodity cracking had negative value to LyondellBasell: the company paid a buyer to take it. The buyer, now running the sites as Velogy, is betting it can operate them better as a "scaled and competitive European polymers platform". LyondellBasell keeps a smaller European position focused, in its CEO's words, on "circular and renewable solutions", including the MoReTec plant due in 2027.

The dividend reset: honest arithmetic, harder bull case
RE-RATING TEST

For years LYB was owned as a high-yield dividend grower. That story ended on February 20, 2026, when a $1.77B annual payout met FY2025 free cash flow of $384M. The bull framing: the cut came at the bottom, and the stock rose 17% that month. The new $0.89B bill is covered 1.77x by trailing cash flow, and any extra cash in the upturn goes to the balance sheet and, under the 70%-of-FCF policy, back to shareholders. The bear framing: a policy of returning 70% of free cash flow is variable by design, so investors should expect the payout to fall again in a bad year. At 4.58% the yield is priced as if the dividend might be cut again. Evidence to watch: whether Q3 and Q4 2026 free cash flow covers $223M a quarter while Middle East capacity is still offline. If it cannot do so now, it will not in a normal market.
