01 Company Overview

Live Nation Entertainment is the world's largest live-entertainment company: it promotes concerts, owns and operates venues and festivals, sells the tickets through Ticketmaster, and monetises the resulting audience through sponsorship and advertising. FY2025 revenue was $25.20B, up 8.8% from $23.16B, with 159 million fans across roughly 55,000 shows and 346 million fee-bearing tickets sold. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 2026 is $26.27B, up 10.8%. At $39.6B of market value it ranks roughly #651 globally by market capitalisation.

The central debate on this page is not whether the live business is growing — it plainly is, at record scale. It is where the profit actually sits, and whether the courts are about to take it apart. Concerts produced 82.6% of FY2025 revenue but only ~26% of segment adjusted operating income; Ticketing produced 12.3% of revenue and ~42% of segment AOI. That asymmetry is the business model — and it is also, almost verbatim, the government's theory of harm. In March 2026 the U.S. Department of Justice settled its portion of the antitrust case; in April 2026 a jury found for the plaintiff states on every claim. Section 03 and the Industry Commentary work through what is and is not settled.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
VERTICAL INTEGRATION VENUE OWNERSHIP ARTIST RELATIONSHIPS TICKETMASTER SCALE GLOBAL ROUTING FIRST-PARTY FAN DATA FESTIVAL PORTFOLIO

The core advantage is that Live Nation is the only participant that touches every link in the chain at global scale: it books the tour, owns or controls many of the rooms it plays, sells the ticket, collects the fan's email address, and sells a sponsorship against the whole thing. An artist routing a world tour can get venues, promotion, ticketing and marketing from one counterparty in dozens of countries. That is a real operating advantage — and it is legally the most exposed thing the company owns, because the government's case was built on exactly the same integration. Note also what the moat does not do: it does not protect Concerts margin. At 3.3% AOI margin, the promotion business has essentially no pricing power against talent. The economic moat lives in Ticketing and Sponsorship, and only Ticketing is under direct legal attack.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

01
An adverse jury verdict already on the books. On April 15, 2026, after a five-to-six-week trial and four days of deliberation, a jury found for the plaintiff states on all claims — unlawful monopolisation of primary ticketing, unlawful monopolisation of large amphitheatre markets, illegal tying of amphitheatres to promotion services, and the corresponding state-law claims. Live Nation has filed a renewed motion for judgment as a matter of law and has indicated it will appeal; the states continue to seek divestiture of Ticketmaster in the remedy phase. Stated as reported on the Paul, Weiss client memo fetched this session; no outcome is predicted here.
02
Concerts margin is going backwards while attendance grows. Q2 2026: fans +10%, Concerts revenue +8%, Concerts AOI −14%. Six months: AOI −14%. Talent costs and stadium mix are compressing the spread at the precise moment volume is at a record. If H2 does not deliver the promised margin expansion, the "record attendance" headline stops supporting the multiple.
03
Leverage and a thin balance sheet. Debt-to-equity of 6.20, total debt of $11.28B, a current ratio of 0.85 and an Altman Z-Score of 1.68. Much of the cash on the balance sheet is fan money held against future events, not free capital. A legal remedy requiring cash payment or divestiture lands on a structure with little slack.
04
Consumer discretionary exposure at record prices. The entire thesis rests on fans continuing to pay record prices for live events. Ticket spend is one of the most deferrable line items in a household budget, and the company has already pulled the pricing lever hard — Q2 2026 Ticketing revenue grew 15% on 8% unit growth.

RSI measures how much of recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 suggests the stock has risen quickly and may be stretched; below 30 suggests the opposite. It describes momentum, not value — a stock can stay stretched for a long time.

From the October 2021 close of $101.15 to $170.08 on September 10, 2026, LYV returned +68.1% over five years — a compound annual rate of roughly 11%, with no dividend. That headline conceals two completely different regimes. The first eighteen months were a rate-shock drawdown: from the December 2021 close of $119.69 to the April 2023 close of $67.78, the stock lost 43.4% as duration-sensitive, leverage-heavy consumer names were repriced. Everything since has been a single long recovery, interrupted twice.

The more useful observation is what the chart did not do. Between the November 2025 close of $131.45 and the June 2026 high close of $183.11 — a span that contains the DOJ settlement and an adverse jury verdict on every claim — the stock gained 39%. The market treated the March 2026 settlement as the removal of the tail risk (no forced federal divestiture) and treated the April verdict as largely priced, helped by the company's own framing of single damages at under $150M against a $39.6B market capitalisation. Whether that is correct depends entirely on the remedy phase, which is not resolved. The 15.6% short float says a meaningful cohort disagrees.

Momentum: the 6-period monthly RSI reads 55.8 at the September close, down from 74.4 in June — a cooling from stretched conditions rather than a breakdown. Price sits above the 10-month moving average of $163.62 but below the 3-month at $174.70, the classic shape of a pullback inside an intact uptrend. Relative performance: the 52-week change is +0.24% — effectively a flat year, and a full round-trip through the $189.25 high and the $125.34 low. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Demand is verifiably at a record and pre-sold: 49 million fans in Q2 (+10%), 143 million tickets sold through mid-July (14 million ahead of last year's pace), and event-related deferred revenue of $6.4B, up 25% — the single best forward indicator this company publishes, because it is cash already collected against shows not yet played. The profit engines are compounding faster than the revenue engine: Ticketing AOI +14% and Sponsorship AOI +16% in H1, at ~37% and ~65% margins. Management guides to double-digit full-year AOI growth. With the DOJ settled without divestiture and $1.38B of free cash flow, a 3.5% FCF yield on a business with 25% deferred-revenue growth is not an expensive asset.

Bear Case

A jury has already found the company an illegal monopolist on every claim, the states are still seeking divestiture of Ticketmaster in the remedy phase, and Ticketmaster is where roughly 42% of segment AOI lives. Meanwhile the operating business shows the wrong divergence: fans +10% but Concerts AOI −14%, meaning record volume is arriving at a worse spread. GAAP earnings are negative, debt-to-equity is 6.20, the current ratio is 0.85, and consensus 2026 adjusted EPS is still below zero. The stock trades within 10% of its all-time month-end high with a 15.6% short float — the market is pricing a benign remedy it has not yet seen.

01
Remedy-phase outcome. The states continue to seek divestiture of Ticketmaster following the April 15, 2026 verdict; Live Nation's renewed JMOL motion and planned appeal are pending. A structural remedy would separate the segment that produces roughly 42% of segment AOI from the segment that produces 83% of revenue. No outcome is predicted here — only the exposure is stated.
02
Concerts margin reversal. Six-month Concerts AOI of $312.4M is 14% below the prior year on 9% more revenue. Management's full-year guidance requires this to reverse in H2. If it does not, the double-digit AOI guide fails and the "record fans" narrative stops being a profit story.
03
Consumer discretionary and leverage together. $11.28B of debt, a 0.85 current ratio and an Altman Z-Score of 1.68 leave little room if ticket demand normalises. The $6.4B deferred balance is a forward indicator on the way up and an obligation on the way down — refunds are a cash liability, not just an accounting reversal.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The verdict is in; the remedy is not
Two separate things happened in 2026 and they are routinely conflated. The DOJ settled on March 9, 2026 — Notice of Settlement on the Antitrust Division docket, Proposed Final Judgment and Competitive Impact Statement filed in June, no Ticketmaster divestiture, but a $280M fund, a four-year cap on exclusive ticketing deals, 50% distribution rights for rival promoters, a 15% service-fee cap and divestiture of 13 venues. Separately, the plaintiff states went to verdict and won on April 15, 2026 on all claims, with a $1.72-per-ticket overcharge finding. Live Nation has a renewed JMOL motion pending and has said it will appeal; the states still seek divestiture. As of the pages fetched on September 11, 2026, that is where it stands. Anyone telling you how it ends is guessing.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Artists are winning the split
A 3.3% Concerts AOI margin on $20.9B of FY2025 revenue is what it looks like when the supplier has the pricing power. Q2 2026 sharpened it: fans +10%, Concerts revenue +8%, Concerts AOI −14%. Bigger tours in bigger buildings mean larger guarantees and a thinner promoter spread, and the stadium-heavy content cycle that drives the attendance record is precisely what compresses the margin. The structural response is to push fans toward on-site ancillary spend and toward the two high-margin segments — which is exactly what the H1 numbers show working, and exactly what the litigation constrains.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Deferred revenue is the tell
Event-related deferred revenue of $6.4B, up 25%, and Ticketmaster deferred gross transaction value of $5.2B, up 16%, both outrun reported revenue growth of ~9–11%. Year-end FY2025 deferred was $4B, up 21%. This is cash in hand for shows not yet played — the closest thing this business has to a backlog, and it is accelerating. Bull framing: the forward book is growing roughly 2.5x faster than reported revenue, so FY2027 volume is substantially de-risked and the flat 12-month share performance is a litigation discount, not a demand signal. Bear framing: a growing deferred balance on a 0.85 current ratio is also a growing obligation, it says nothing about the margin at which those shows convert — and Concerts AOI is already telling you that answer is getting worse.
