01 Company Overview

Walk into the tap-and-paint aisles of a big-box home store and you are standing in Masco's shop window. The company makes Delta and hansgrohe faucets, bath and shower fixtures, Behr paint, Liberty decorative and functional hardware and HotSpring spas — sold mainly into repair-and-remodel demand rather than new-home construction. FY2025 revenue was $7.56B, down 3.4% (down 2% in local currency excluding divestitures), with GAAP EPS of $3.86 and adjusted EPS of $3.96. At the October 29, 2026 close of $80.18 the equity is worth about $15.46B (our calculation: 192.77M shares on the cover of the Q3 2026 10-Q × $80.18).

The story of the last five years is a company that has stopped growing its top line and learned to grow everything else. Revenue peaked at $8.68B in FY2022 and fell each year to $7.56B in FY2025 (trailing twelve months: $7.67B) — yet operating margin rose from 12.4% in FY2021 to 17.9% trailing, and diluted EPS went from $1.62 to $4.61. The engine is pricing, cost and a shrinking share count (down 4.95% year on year). 2026 added a strange twist: in Q2 Masco recognised a net benefit of about $95 million from refunds of IEEPA tariffs, which pushed adjusted operating margin to 24.2% and lifted full-year guidance. Q3 was the first look underneath, and it held up: sales rose 3%, adjusted operating margin rose 1.6 points to 17.9%, North American plumbing sales grew 8% in local currency, and management raised adjusted EPS guidance again, to $4.65–$4.75, while keeping its refund estimate at about $85 million for the year. The open questions are whether those margins hold in 2027 with no refund to help, and whether a company with 38% of its sales going through one retailer can grow volume while DIY paint keeps shrinking.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Consumer brands (Delta, Behr, hansgrohe) Big-box shelf position Repair & remodel exposure Pricing power (ROIC 26.7%) Cash generation (FCF $1.04B TTM)

The moat is brand plus shelf. Nobody comparison-shops a faucet cartridge for long; they pick a name they recognise from the one aisle they are standing in. That lets Masco push price through — trailing gross margin is 37.6% against 31.3% in FY2022, and price alone added 5 points to Q3 2026 plumbing sales — and earn a 26.7% return on invested capital with very little capital. The flip side is that the shelf belongs to someone else. With 38% of sales going through The Home Depot, Masco's brands are strong with homeowners but the negotiating power sits with the retailer.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customer concentration. The Home Depot was 38% of FY2025 net sales. A shelf reset, a private-label push or a change in promotional terms at one account hits more than a third of revenue at once.
02
Volumes are shrinking. Revenue has fallen every year since FY2022 ($8.68B to $7.56B in FY2025), partly from divestitures (Kichler Lighting was sold to an affiliate of Kingswood Capital Management in September 2024; Bristan in the third quarter of 2026) and partly from weaker demand. Q3 2026 brought 8% growth in North American plumbing, but against an easy comparison, while international plumbing and DIY paint kept shrinking. Margin expansion on a flat base has a ceiling.
03
2026 earnings are flattered by a one-off. The ~$90M of net tariff refunds booked in the first nine months of 2026 is money back on costs already paid, not a new source of profit. It sits inside trailing EPS of $4.61 and will not recur in 2027.
04
Negative equity and borrowed buybacks. Total equity was −$193M at September 30, 2026: decades of repurchases have pushed shareholders' equity below zero. Debt is $3.25B against $610M cash, about 2.1 times trailing EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation and amortisation). That is manageable while cash flow holds, but it leaves less room in a housing downturn.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the figures. First, the IEEPA tariff refund: a net benefit of about $95M in Q2 2026, which management said helped drive that quarter's adjusted operating profit growth of 17% and adjusted EPS growth of 26%, and which accounted for July's guidance raise from $4.10–$4.30 to $4.40–$4.60. The nine-month net benefit is about $90M (Q3 10-Q) and the full-year estimate is unchanged at about $85M. October's second raise, to $4.65–$4.75, was put down to the business instead; in CFO Rick Westenberg's words on the Q3 call, "Reflecting the stronger operating performance, we are updating our full year guidance as follows." Second, buybacks: about $861M spent on roughly 12.3M shares in the first nine months of 2026 and a share count down 4.95% in a year, which lifts per-share figures relative to total profit. Third, smaller items in Q3: a preliminary $13M gain on the Bristan sale and $13M of restructuring ("rationalization") charges, both excluded from adjusted results. Reported 2026 EPS guidance is $4.51–$4.61, below the adjusted range.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 17.4x trailing and 17.0x forward (FY2026 consensus) earnings for a business with a 26.7% return on invested capital, a ~6.7% free-cash-flow yield and a share count down 4.95% in the last year. Q3 suggested the margin is not only the refund: adjusted operating margin rose 1.6 points, North American plumbing grew 8%, and guidance went up on operating performance with the refund estimate unchanged. Even FY2025, with no refund, delivered a 16.5% operating margin against FY2021's 12.4%. Any recovery in housing turnover or DIY spending lands on a cost base that has already been cut, and UBS's $98 target, the highest on the board, is 22% above the price.

Bear Case

After a 6.7% two-day jump the average analyst target of $82.42 is only 2.8% above the price, and 15 of 22 analysts rate the stock Hold. Consensus sees FY2027 EPS of $4.69, slightly below FY2026's $4.72: the Street is modelling no growth once the refund passes. Revenue is only 0.9% above a year ago, DIY paint and international plumbing are still shrinking, and Q3's North American plumbing gain came against an easy comparison, by the CFO's own account. 38% of sales go through one retailer, equity is negative, and much of the EPS growth comes from a shrinking share count rather than profit growth.

Top 3 Risks

01
Earnings quality after the refund. 2026 earnings include about $85M of full-year IEEPA refund benefit that will not come back in 2027. If margins revert toward FY2025's 16.8% adjusted level while commodity and employee costs rise, the trailing P/E is flattering the stock.
02
Housing and DIY demand. DIY paint is expected to fall mid-single digits in 2026 and tracks existing-home turnover, which management describes as at trough levels; it expects economic and geopolitical uncertainty to last through 2026 and likely into 2027. A weaker housing market hits a business with no volume tailwind to lean on.

Customer concentration and leverage. 38% of sales through The Home Depot, $3.25B of debt and negative book equity. A change in terms at the one big account, combined with a downturn, would squeeze both profit and balance-sheet flexibility at once.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs in, tariffs back out
ONE-OFF TAILWIND

Masco paid tariffs imposed under IEEPA, and is now getting some of that money back. It recognised a net benefit of about $95 million from IEEPA tariff refunds in Q2 2026 and about $90 million for the first nine months (Q3 10-Q, mostly in Plumbing), and it still expects about $85 million for the full year; management plans to put part of it back into growth investments. The lesson for reading 2026 numbers: Q2's 26% adjusted EPS growth said more about customs accounting than about demand, while Q3's 13% rise in adjusted operating profit came, per the CFO, from pricing, cost savings and lower year-over-year tariff costs, partly offset by higher commodity and employee costs.

Pro vs DIY — two different housing markets
MIXED DEMAND

Behr's split — pro paint up mid-single digits again in Q3 2026, DIY down low single digits — is the cleanest read on the housing consumer in this report. Contractors are still working; homeowners doing it themselves are pulling back, and management expects pro up and DIY down mid-single digits for the full year. It ties DIY paint to existing-home turnover, still at trough levels, and expects Masco's total 2026 sales to rise low single digits. That is a business priced for resilience, not recovery.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
OPEN DEBATE

At about 11.9x EV/EBITDA and 17.4x trailing earnings, Masco is not priced as a distressed business or as a growth business. The bull framing: a brand portfolio earning a 26.7% return on invested capital, which turns about $1B of free cash flow a year ($1.04B over the last twelve months) into a shrinking share count, will keep growing per-share value even with flat sales, and any housing upturn is free upside. The bear framing: flat sales, a one-off refund and negative equity leave little margin for error, and one customer controls the shelf. Q3 gave the bulls a point: adjusted margin rose 1.6 points, and guidance went up on operating performance with the refund estimate unchanged. The way to settle it is 2027 — the first full year in which the refund does none of the work.
