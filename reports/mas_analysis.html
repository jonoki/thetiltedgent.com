01 Company Overview

Walk into the tap-and-paint aisles of a big-box home store and you are standing in Masco's shop window. The company makes Delta and hansgrohe faucets, bath and shower fixtures, Behr paint, Liberty decorative and functional hardware and HotSpring spas — sold mainly into repair-and-remodel demand rather than new-home construction. FY2025 revenue was $7.56B, down 3.4% (down 2% in local currency excluding divestitures), with GAAP EPS of $3.86 and adjusted EPS of $3.96. At the September 21, 2026 close of $67.74 the equity is worth about $13.3B (our calculation: 196.39M shares × $67.74).

The story of the last five years is a company that has stopped growing its top line and learned to grow everything else. Revenue peaked at $8.68B in FY2022 and has drifted down every year since, to $7.62B on a trailing basis — yet operating margin rose from 12.4% in FY2021 to 17.8% trailing, and diluted EPS went from $1.62 to $4.33. The engine is pricing, cost and a shrinking share count (down 4.66% year on year). 2026 added a strange twist: in Q2 Masco recognised a net benefit of about $95 million from refunds of IEEPA tariffs, which pushed adjusted operating margin to 24.2% and let management lift full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $4.40–$4.60. The central question for this stock is how much of that margin is structural and how much was a one-off cheque from the customs office — and whether a company with 38% of its sales going through one retailer can ever earn a growth multiple while volumes fall.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Consumer brands (Delta, Behr, hansgrohe) Big-box shelf position Repair & remodel exposure Pricing power (ROIC 30.8%) Cash generation (FCF $1.13B TTM)

The moat is brand plus shelf. Nobody comparison-shops a faucet cartridge for long; they pick a name they recognise from the one aisle they are standing in. That lets Masco push price through — trailing gross margin is 37.2% against 31.3% in FY2022 — and earn a 30.76% return on invested capital with very little capital. The flip side is that the shelf belongs to someone else. With 38% of sales going through The Home Depot, Masco's brands are strong with homeowners but the negotiating power sits with the retailer.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customer concentration. The Home Depot was 38% of FY2025 net sales. A shelf reset, a private-label push or a change in promotional terms at one account hits more than a third of revenue at once.
02
Volumes are shrinking. Revenue has fallen every year since FY2022 ($8.68B to $7.56B in FY2025), partly from divestitures (Kichler Lighting was sold to an affiliate of Kingswood Capital Management in September 2024) and partly from weaker demand. Q2 2026 North American plumbing fell 6% and DIY paint fell high single digits. Margin expansion on a shrinking base has a ceiling.
03
2026 earnings are flattered by a one-off. The ~$95M Q2 tariff refund is a refund of costs already paid, not a new source of profit. It raised trailing EPS; the underlying run-rate is lower than the headline TTM $4.33 suggests.
04
Negative equity and borrowed buybacks. Book value is −$118M: decades of repurchases have pushed shareholders' equity below zero. Total debt is $3.49B against $548M cash (debt/EBITDA 2.20x). That is manageable while cash flow holds, but it leaves less room in a housing downturn.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 15.6x trailing and 15.0x forward earnings for a business with a 30.8% return on invested capital, an ~8.5% free-cash-flow yield and a share count down 4.66% in the last year. Its brands sit in the repair-and-remodel market, which management expects to be roughly flat in 2026 — a starting point, not a peak. Even FY2025, with no refund, delivered a 16.7% operating margin against FY2021's 12.4%. Any recovery in housing turnover or DIY spending lands on a cost base that has already been cut, and the consensus target of $80.33 is 18.6% above the price.

Bear Case

Revenue has shrunk three years running and the 2026 earnings beat was a customs refund. Strip the ~$95M out and Q2 is a quarter of falling North American plumbing and DIY paint volumes. 38% of sales go through one retailer, equity is negative, and much of the EPS growth comes from a shrinking share count rather than profit growth. Consensus sees FY2027 EPS of $4.66, barely above FY2026's $4.53: the Street itself is modelling almost no growth once the refund passes, and 13 of 21 analysts sit on Hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Earnings quality after the refund. The 2026 guidance raise rests on ~$85M of full-year IEEPA refund benefit. If second-half margins revert toward FY2025's 16.8% adjusted level while commodity costs rise, the trailing P/E is flattering the stock.
02
Housing and DIY demand. Q2 2026 North American plumbing −6%, DIY paint down high single digits. Management expects roughly flat global repair-and-remodel markets in 2026; a weaker outcome hits a business with no volume tailwind to lean on.
03
Customer concentration and leverage. 38% of sales through The Home Depot, $3.49B of debt and negative book equity. A change in terms at the one big account, combined with a downturn, would squeeze both profit and balance-sheet flexibility at once.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs in, tariffs back out
ONE-OFF TAILWIND

Masco paid tariffs imposed under IEEPA, and is now getting some of that money back. In Q2 2026 it recognised a net benefit of about $95 million from IEEPA tariff refunds, and it expects a full-year net benefit of about $85 million. Management is explicit that underlying performance "remains largely in line with our prior outlook" — the guidance raise is the refund. The lesson for reading 2026 numbers: headline EPS growth of 26% in Q2 says more about customs accounting than about demand.

Pro vs DIY — two different housing markets
MIXED DEMAND

Behr's Q2 2026 split — pro paint up mid-single digits, DIY down high single digits — is the cleanest read on the housing consumer in this report. Contractors are still working; homeowners doing it themselves are pulling back. For 2026 management said it expects global repair-and-remodel markets to be roughly flat and Masco's own sales roughly flat to up low single digits in local currency. That is a business priced for resilience, not recovery.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
OPEN DEBATE

At about 10.8x EV/EBITDA and 15.6x trailing earnings, Masco is not priced as a distressed business or as a growth business. The bull framing: a 30%-ROIC brand portfolio that turns $1B+ of free cash flow a year into a shrinking share count will compound per-share value even with flat sales, and any housing upturn is free upside. The bear framing: flat sales, a one-off refund and negative equity leave little margin for error, and one customer controls the shelf. The way to settle it is the Q3 and Q4 2026 underlying margin — the first quarters where the refund is no longer doing the work.
