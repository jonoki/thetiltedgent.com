01 Company Overview

McDonald's Corporation operates the world's largest restaurant system by systemwide sales — over 46,000 restaurants in over 100 countries, of which approximately 95% are owned and operated by independent franchisees. That structure is the single most important fact about the company: McDonald's corporate revenue is overwhelmingly rent and royalty income collected off a $139 billion global systemwide sales base, not the sale of hamburgers. Systemwide sales — the total spent by customers across all McDonald's restaurants, franchised and company-operated — exceeded $139 billion in fiscal 2025, up 7% (5% in constant currencies), while the corporation's own reported revenue was $26.89 billion, up 3.7% YoY (up 4% as reported by the company, 2% in constant currencies).

On TTM figures through September 2026, revenue is $27.78 billion (+5.8%) with net income of $9.00 billion — a 32.4% net profit margin and a 46.1% operating margin that would be unthinkable for an operator of restaurants and is entirely explained by the franchise model. McDonald's market value is $195.16 billion, and with its September raise it became a Dividend King, the company's term for the fewer than 60 U.S. public companies that have raised their dividend for at least 50 consecutive years. The stock has still had a poor year: down 7.34% over the past twelve months while the S&P 500 tracker SPY rose 9.69%. It closed September at $230.94, then recovered to $276.52 after the third-quarter report, with U.S. guest counts still negative.

This is the key to the whole company. Because roughly 95% of the 46,000+ restaurants are franchisee-owned, McDonald's does not book the food sales, does not pay the crew, and does not carry the food and paper cost for the vast majority of its system. It collects a rent on the real estate it owns or master-leases and a royalty on the franchisee's sales. Franchised revenues of $16.5 billion in FY2025 (up from $15.7B in FY2024) carry margins in the region of 80%+, while company-operated sales of $9.7 billion (down from $9.8B as refranchising continues) carry restaurant-level margins in the mid-teens. Blend them and you get a 57.6% gross margin, a 46.1% operating margin, and a 32.4% net margin on trailing figures — numbers that look like a software company's, on a business that sells cheeseburgers. In Q3 2026 franchised revenue was $4,487M (+3%), company-operated sales $2,480M (−3%) and other revenue $189M (+26%). The corollary is that systemwide sales, not corporate revenue, is the number that matters for judging brand health: because rent and royalties carry little extra cost, each extra point of comps adds disproportionately to McDonald's operating income.
Real economics: $139B systemwide sales → $26.9B corporate revenue → $12.4B operating income (FY2025)
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Owned Real Estate ~95% Franchised $139B Systemwide Scale ~220M Loyalty Members Supply-Chain Cost Advantage Global Brand Recognition 50-Yr Dividend Growth Value-Price Leadership

McDonald's moat is unusual because it rests on two independent foundations. The first is real estate: McDonald's owns or master-leases the land and buildings under a large share of its restaurants and sublets them to franchisees, which converts brand strength into contractual, inflation-linked rent that is far more durable than restaurant profit. The second is absolute scale — a $139 billion systemwide sales base spread over 46,000+ restaurants in 100+ countries buys purchasing power in beef, potatoes, chicken and packaging, and spreads advertising and technology spend over a sales base an order of magnitude larger than most rivals. Layer on the loyalty program — nearly 220 million 90-day active users across 70 markets, with $41 billion of trailing-twelve-month sales to loyalty members — and McDonald's now has first-party data that lets it target offers rather than discount across the board. The result is a business that can afford to lead on price, because the discount is borne largely at the franchisee's restaurant while McDonald's collects a royalty on the extra visits. That is a weapon Wendy's — with $2.20 billion of revenue, U.S. same-restaurant sales down 5.7% in Q3 2026, its 2026 outlook withdrawn and its dividend cut — does not have.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Traffic, not the size of each order, is the true health metric — and traffic is negative. Q3 2026 U.S. comps of −0.2% came with negative guest counts "largely offset by positive check growth," per the 10-Q. Comps fell from +6.8% in Q4 2025 to +0.8% in Q2 2026 and below zero in Q3. The head of the U.S. business changed in August, and cheaper entry-level menus went into testing in October. A value-led chain that grows only by charging each customer more is borrowing from future visits.

2. Low-income consumer dependence. McDonald's draws heavily on lower-income households, the group most exposed to squeezed real wages and higher shelter costs. Management won that group back in late 2025 with Extra Value Meals, McValue and $5/$8 nationally priced bundles, and Kempczinski now says entry-level value is still not where he wants it. Any further move to grocery or convenience-store food is a direct traffic loss, and any move to raise prices is a direct traffic risk.

3. Competition on both flanks. Restaurant Brands International, the owner of Burger King, grew trailing revenue 5.5%, and McDonald's September Investor Day set a target of gaining 1.5 percentage points of share in chicken and in beverages by 2030 — an acknowledgement of where it needs to win customers. Low-price rivals press on value while chains like Chick-fil-A press on chicken.

4. Little balance-sheet slack. $54.5 billion of total debt including leases against $1.19 billion of cash and negative stockholders' equity of −$343 million means the company funds its dividend and buyback from operating cash flow, not from a war chest. Total debt/EBITDA of 3.25x and a current ratio of 1.23 are manageable for a business with a 27% FCF margin and an Altman Z-Score of 4.91 (a bankruptcy-risk score where above 3 is considered safe), and the Investor Day added $8.5 billion of franchisee support through 2036 on top. That leaves little room for a large acquisition or a long sales slump without pressuring the 50-year dividend streak.

Notable One-Off Items

Restructuring — "Accelerating the Organization." This is the only material recurring distortion in the reported numbers. McDonald's booked net pre-tax charges of $56 million ($0.06 per share) in Q3 2026 and $155 million ($0.17 per share) in the first nine months of 2026, following $229 million ($0.25 per share) for full-year 2025; the 10-Q expects about $225 million of restructuring charges for all of 2026. Adjusting for these, Q3 2026 diluted EPS was $3.57 versus $3.51 GAAP, and FY2025 adjusted EPS was $12.20 versus $11.95 GAAP.

Other operating income. This line was $139 million of income in Q3 2026, versus $37 million a year earlier (10-Q), and the release names it first among the drivers of the quarter. The $102 million increase is worth about $0.14 per share before tax on 708 million diluted shares, against a $0.21 beat of the Nasdaq consensus.

Currency translation. A material swing in the growth figures. Q3 2026 revenue grew 1% both as reported and in constant currencies, but the first nine months grew 5% as reported and only 2% in constant currencies, and FY2025 systemwide sales grew 7% reported versus 5% constant currency. The boost from a weaker dollar that flattered the first half has gone.

Refranchising. Company-operated sales declined from $9.8B (FY2024) to $9.7B (FY2025) while franchised revenues rose from $15.7B to $16.5B — the continuing shift of restaurants from corporate to franchisee ownership. This raises the consolidated margin and lowers consolidated revenue growth, and can generate one-time gains on sale. Investors should not read a rising operating margin as pure operating improvement.

Other adjustments. Restructuring is the only net charge the Q3 2026 release adjusts for.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a 46.1% operating margin, 17.9% ROIC business with 50 straight years of dividend raises, at 21.4x FY2026 consensus earnings and a 2.69% yield (2.79% at the new dividend rate), about 19% below its February 2026 peak close. With ~95% of 46,000+ restaurants franchised, McDonald's collects rent and royalties on a $139 billion systemwide sales base and does not bear the food, labour or discount cost of the value war. Loyalty sales reached $41 billion over twelve months, giving the company a way to aim offers at individuals instead of cutting menu prices for everyone. U.S. comps turned positive in September, the new menu test could reach the whole country in early 2027, and management is targeting a low-to-mid 50% operating margin by 2030. With $7.49 billion of free cash flow, about 8% consensus upside and a 0.43 beta (over five years the stock has moved less than half as much as the market), this is a defensive holding with a real plan.

Bear Case

The core U.S. business is shrinking in the only unit that matters. Q3 2026 comps of −0.2% came with negative guest counts, and revenue rose just 1%, short of estimates; the release names higher other operating income first among the reasons profit rose. France and China are still negative. The Investor Day committed $8.5 billion to franchisees through 2036, with a payback of five to six years for McDonald's, and the price fix for U.S. traffic is still only a test. A 3.33 PEG says you are paying a premium multiple for mid-single-digit growth, and negative shareholders' equity of −$343 million against $54.5 billion of debt including leases leaves little cushion if sales go negative. The stock has also jumped about 20% from its October 5 low, with the 14-day RSI at 73.

Top 3 Risks
01
Sustained negative U.S. guest counts. The single most important number in the company. Q3 2026 was at least the second straight quarter of negative traffic, with comps held near zero only by higher spending per order; if the early-2027 menu does not turn traffic, the company may have to invest further in price, which squeezes franchisee restaurant margins and slows new openings.
02
Low-income consumer trade-down. McDonald's draws heavily on the group most exposed to real-wage pressure. The company won that group back in late 2025 only by conceding price through Extra Value Meals, McValue and $5/$8 national bundles. If the squeeze intensifies, the choice is between losing traffic and cutting price again, and neither is good for the 46.1% operating margin.
03
International slowdown removing the offset. International comps carried the system through 2025 (IOM +3.2%, IDL +4.6%). IOM slowed to +1.4% in Q3 2026, and France and China stayed negative. If those two drags persist while the U.S. is flat, consolidated comps drift toward zero, and a stronger dollar would take further points off reported revenue growth.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
The Low-Income Consumer and the Trade-Down Spiral
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the live issue and it is not abstract. McDonald's built the 2025 recovery on winning back price-sensitive households: McValue launched in January 2025, Extra Value Meals returned in September 2025, and $5 and $8 nationally priced meals came with them. It worked — U.S. comps hit +6.8% in Q4 2025, and management reported gaining share with low-income consumers that December. Nine months later, Q3 2026 U.S. comps were −0.2% with negative guest counts. On the Q3 call Kempczinski conceded the gap: "particularly for that low-income consumer, they're looking for great entry-level value, and we have not had that in place to the level that we are satisfied with." The eight-week test in about 1,900 restaurants is the response. Because McDonald's collects a royalty on franchisee sales rather than a margin on food, the corporation absorbs less of that pain than its franchisees do — but franchisee restaurant-level economics ultimately determine whether the system keeps building units. Watch the guest-count line, not the comp line.
GLP-1 Weight-Loss Drugs and the Shape of Food Demand
ONGOING HEADWIND
GLP-1 receptor agonists (the drug class behind semaglutide and tirzepatide) reduce appetite, and their adoption is a long-term question for anyone selling food. The survey evidence is more nuanced than the headlines. In an Acosta Group study, 34% of GLP-1 users reported dining out less, while 48% of Gen Z and Millennials said they are dining out more, though being more mindful of what they order. In a William Blair survey of 300 GLP-1 users, about 70% eat smaller portions more often, about half said smaller-portion options would get them to dine out more often, and restaurant spending fell 4% on average. William Blair's April 2026 report concludes that even a doubling of GLP-1 adoption over five years would have only a modest effect on restaurant sales growth. For McDonald's the exposure runs the wrong way: its sales depend on the number of orders and on customers adding fries and a drink. The offset is that a smaller-portion, higher-protein menu is a product problem McDonald's has the research budget and nearly 220 million loyalty users to work on. Treat this as a slow drag on what each customer spends and on which meals they buy, not as a cliff.
Franchise-Model Resilience — Bull vs Bear Framing
EMERGING TAILWIND
Everything above lands differently because of how McDonald's is structured. The bull framing: with ~95% of the system franchised, McDonald's revenue is contractual rent and royalty income off $139 billion of systemwide sales, not the residual profit on a hamburger. That is why the operating margin is 46.08%, ROIC is 17.88%, FCF margin is 27.0%, and the dividend has risen for 50 consecutive years. A traffic recession hits franchisee income first and McDonald's royalty second, a shock absorber no fully-owned operator enjoys. At 21.4x FY2026 consensus earnings with a 2.69% yield and a 0.43 beta, the market is charging a modest premium for that resilience. The bear framing: the same structure means McDonald's cannot fix a traffic problem by simply cutting its own prices — it must persuade thousands of independent owners to accept thinner restaurant margins, and the Investor Day showed it will take $8.5 billion of support to bring them along. And the cash has already been handed out: negative stockholders' equity of −$343 million and $54.5 billion of debt including leases show there is not much left to borrow against. Resilience is not the same as growth, and at a 3.33 PEG the price still assumes more growth than the last two quarters have delivered.
