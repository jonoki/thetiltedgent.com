01 Company Overview

Moody's Corporation is one half of a genuine duopoly. Through Moody's Investors Service (MIS, branded "Moody's Ratings") it assigns credit ratings to corporate, sovereign, structured and infrastructure debt; through Moody's Analytics (MA) it sells research, data, models and risk software on subscription. FY2025 revenue was $7,718M, up 8.9% from $7,088M, split $4,119M MIS / $3,599M MA, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $8.16B, up 11.7%. At an $85.47B market capitalisation the company ranks #290 globally. It is the smaller of the two big raters: S&P Global carries a $130.74B market cap on $16.12B of TTM revenue, but SPGI's ratings arm is only one of four segments.

The central point of this report is that "Moody's" is two businesses that have almost nothing in common except a brand and a customer list, and any analysis that blends them produces a number that describes neither. MIS is a toll booth on debt issuance — transactional revenue grew 34% in Q2 2026 because rated issuance volumes grew 33%, and it would fall just as hard in a frozen market. MA is a subscription business with ARR of $3,661M growing 9% and a 95% retention rate, whose reported revenue grew 4% in the same quarter. One segment carries a 68.3% adjusted operating margin; the other carries 33.6%. The two live debates — whether generative AI eats the Analytics research franchise or extends it, and whether the migration of borrowing into private credit shrinks the MIS toll booth — hit one segment each. Sections 02 and 03 take them separately.

FY25 Revenue Mix

The transactional / recurring split is the whole business model, so lead with it. In FY2025, MIS revenue of $4,119M was $2,741M transactional (67%) and $1,378M recurring (33%). Transactional revenue is billed when a specific bond, loan or securitisation is rated at issue; recurring revenue is annual monitoring and relationship-pricing fees that arrive whether or not the issuer comes to market. Two-thirds of the segment is therefore a direct, near-mechanical function of how much debt got issued in the quarter — which is why the segment's results look nothing like a subscription business.

Q2 2026 is the clean illustration. Total rated issuance volume rose 33%; MIS transactional revenue rose 34% to $891M — essentially one-for-one — while recurring revenue rose only 6% to $369M. Segment revenue was $1,260M, up 25%, and because incremental issuance revenue arrives with almost no incremental cost, the adjusted operating margin expanded 410 basis points to 68.3% — one of the highest segment margins in the entire S&P 500. That leverage runs both ways: FY2022, when issuance froze, saw total company revenue fall 12.1% and operating margin drop from 45.6% to 36.7%. Within Q2 2026, Corporate Finance was $651M (+27%), Public/Project & Infrastructure Finance $224M (+38%), Financial Institutions $222M (+16%) and Structured Finance $151M (+12%).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
REGULATORY DUOPOLY MANDATED IN INVESTMENT GUIDELINES TWO-RATING CONVENTION 120-YEAR RATING ARCHIVE 68.3% MIS SEGMENT MARGIN 95% MA RETENTION 76.9% ROE

The moat is a convention, not a patent. A large share of institutional debt buyers operate under investment mandates and regulatory capital rules written in terms of ratings, and market practice for most public benchmark issues is to carry two ratings. When an issuer needs two and there are effectively two incumbents of scale, the pricing conversation is short. That is why MIS earns a 68.3% adjusted operating margin and why the corporation as a whole earns a ROE of 76.9% and a ROIC of 32.0% on a business that requires almost no physical capital. Note the ROE/ROIC gap: the 76.9% figure is flattered by an equity base shrunk by years of buybacks (price/book is 28.3×); the 32.0% ROIC is the more honest measure of the franchise, and it is still exceptional.

The second layer is data gravity on the Analytics side. Ratings, credit research, entity data and default history feed the models that feed the software that banks and insurers embed in their own approval workflows. Once a risk platform is wired into a lender's origination process, switching is a project, not a purchase — which is what a 95% retention rate describes.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Two-thirds of the ratings business is a bet on the calendar. MIS transactional revenue of $2,741M in FY2025 exists only if issuers issue. FY2022 is the live-fire test: revenue fell 12.1% and operating margin fell nearly nine points in a single year because the primary market shut. Nothing about the moat prevents that — the duopoly protects price, not volume.
02
Private credit routes debt around the toll booth. Loans arranged directly between borrowers and private funds do not need a public rating to clear. Moody's is playing this well so far — more than 40% growth in private credit-related transactions and more than 110 new first-time mandates in Q2 2026 — but the structural question is whether the share of total borrowing that requires a rating drifts down over a decade. Every dollar that migrates from a rated public bond to an unrated private loan is a dollar the toll booth never sees.
03
Generative AI aimed at the Research & Insights line. Research & Insights was $995M in FY2025 and grew just 3% in Q2 2026 — the slowest of the three MA sub-lines. If a language model can synthesise credit commentary from public filings, the willingness to pay for written research compresses. The counter-argument is that Moody's owns proprietary, non-public data and the ratings themselves, which no model can reproduce, and that the company is charging for AI rather than absorbing it. That debate is unresolved and the sub-line's growth rate is where it will show up first.
04
Leverage and a thin balance sheet. Debt-to-equity is 2.41 against $7.62B of total debt and $1.50B of cash, and the current ratio is 1.19. This is comfortable for a business generating $2.97B of free cash flow, but it is a choice — capital has gone to buybacks rather than to a fortress balance sheet, and it removes the cushion that made 2022 survivable rather than painful.

Regulatory and litigation note: no specific regulatory action or litigation matter appeared on any page fetched for this report. Rating agencies are regulated (in the U.S. as Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organizations under SEC oversight, and in the EU by ESMA), but no current proceeding is asserted here because none was verified.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the split, because everything follows from it. FY2025: MIS $4,119M (53.4%) and MA $3,599M (46.6%). Inside MIS, $2,741M transactional (67%) and $1,378M recurring (33%). That single line is the most important disclosure Moody's makes. Roughly 35% of total company revenue — the transactional two-thirds of the ratings segment — is a direct function of how much debt the world issued that quarter. Nothing about the duopoly protects that number. The duopoly protects the price per deal; it does nothing about the number of deals.

Q2 2026 shows the mechanism running at full throttle. Rated issuance volume +33% → MIS transactional revenue +34% → MIS revenue +25% → MIS adjusted operating margin 68.3%, up 410 basis points. That chain is almost arithmetically tight, and it is why the segment's margin expands in booms: the analyst base is fixed, the deals are incremental, and the marginal deal drops through at close to 100%. Meanwhile MIS recurring revenue grew just 6% — the stable base underneath is growing at roughly the rate of the outstanding stock of debt, not the rate of new issue. Management has already told you the throttle is coming off: Q3 guided to low double-digit growth, Q4 to roughly flat.

Moody's Analytics is the counterweight, and its own numbers disagree with each other. Reported Q2 revenue grew 4%. Organic constant-currency growth was 8%. ARR grew 9% to $3,661M, with trailing-twelve-month retention at 95%. The right way to read this is that ARR and retention describe the business while the reported line describes the reporting; the four-point wedge between reported and organic constant-currency is disclosed but its composition was not itemised on the pages fetched, so it is not decomposed here. Segment adjusted operating margin of 33.6% is less than half of MIS's — and that gap is permanent, not a maturity issue. Software and data delivery cost real money; ratings do not. Anyone hoping MA growth will eventually make Moody's a non-cyclical company should notice that MA growing at 8–9% while MIS swings ±25% does not smooth the company much.

The AI question, on evidence rather than narrative. The threat is specific: Research & Insights, $995M in FY2025, is written credit research and screening tools — the most substitutable thing Moody's sells. It grew 7% in FY2025 and just 3% in Q2 2026, the slowest of the three MA sub-lines. That is the datapoint the bears should be pointing at. Against it, here is what the company has actually shipped and priced, from the Q2 disclosure and call: Research Assistant deployed as an agentic contextual chat on every company page; agentic AI capabilities added to the cloud Intelligent Risk Platform; the company's first AI skill on Microsoft 365 Copilot, with more than 20 engagements globally; Moody's Connected Intelligence delivered into Amazon QuickSight; and the Intelligent Risk Platform listed on the AWS Marketplace. Management's framing on model costs is that falling token prices would "expand usage more than compress price" — i.e. they expect volume, not deflation. Note what is not disclosed: no AI-attributed revenue, no AI ARR, no pricing uplift figure. Twenty engagements is a pilot, not a business line. The defensible conclusion is that Moody's is shipping real product into the workflow layer where its proprietary data is the moat, and that Research & Insights growth is the number that will tell you whether it is working — currently 3%.

The private credit question is the structural one. A loan arranged privately between a borrower and a direct-lending fund does not require a public rating to clear. If the share of corporate borrowing done that way rises over a decade, the addressable pool of rateable transactions shrinks — and that is a permanent problem, not a cyclical one. Moody's answer so far is to sell into the migration: Q2 2026 delivered more than 40% growth in private credit-related transactions and more than 110 new first-time mandates, and the Financial Institutions line (+16% in Q2) was explicitly attributed in part to fund finance and private credit. That is genuine evidence, and it is the right response. It is also early: 110 mandates in a quarter is a small absolute number against a $4.1B ratings business, and private-credit ratings are typically sought for a reason — insurance-capital treatment, fund-level financing — rather than because the market demands them the way it demands a public bond rating. Track the mandate count and the private-credit transaction growth every quarter; they are the only quantified handle on this that the company gives you.

Valuation. At $493.55 the stock trades at 31.35× trailing GAAP earnings of $15.74, 27.76× forward earnings, and about 28.8× trailing free cash flow ($85.47B ÷ $2.97B; EV/FCF ≈ 30.8× on the $91.60B enterprise value). Enterprise value to EBITDA is 22.6×. On adjusted EPS guidance of $16.50–$17.00 the multiple is 29.0–29.9× — barely below the trailing GAAP multiple, which is unusual and worth pausing on: it means the adjusted measure is not doing much flattering at the EPS line this year, even though the eight-point gap between guided GAAP (44–45%) and adjusted (52–53%) operating margin says it is doing plenty at the margin line. The PEG of 2.20 is the honest summary: you are paying a franchise price for a business whose growth rate depends on a cycle that management has told you is about to slow. The premium to S&P Global (27.0×) is the specific thing to justify — MCO is the purer ratings play, which cuts both ways.

Bull Case

Half of a two-firm oligopoly protected by investment mandates and the two-rating convention, earning a 68.3% segment operating margin and a 32.0% ROIC on almost no capital. Q2 2026 showed the operating leverage intact: 33% issuance growth converted into 34% transactional revenue growth and 410 basis points of margin. Underneath the cycle, MA compounds at 8–9% organic with $3,661M of ARR and 95% retention. Free cash flow of $2.97B exceeds accounting earnings and funds a buyback lifted to $3.0B — about 3.5% of the market cap annually. Private credit is being monetised (+40% transactions, 110+ new mandates), and AI is shipping as product into workflows the company already owns.

Bear Case

Roughly 35% of total revenue is transactional ratings fees that vanish when the primary market closes — FY2022 proved it, with revenue −12.1% and operating margin down nearly nine points in one year. Management has guided Q4 2026 revenue to roughly flat, so the deceleration is already scheduled. The subscription half grows at 8–9% and earns half the margin, so it cannot offset the swing. Research & Insights, the AI-exposed line, grew just 3% last quarter and no AI revenue is disclosed. Private credit structurally shrinks the pool of debt that must be rated. And you pay 31.4× trailing earnings with a 2.20 PEG and a 1.33 beta — a 16% premium to S&P Global — for the cyclical half of the duopoly, having earned −0.58% over the past year.

Top 3 Risks

01
An issuance freeze. The single largest risk and the one with a precedent five years old. A rate shock, a credit event or a risk-off market shuts the primary calendar, and $2.7B of annual transactional revenue with near-100% incremental margin reverses at near-100% incremental margin. The 2022 template: revenue −12.1%, EPS −36.8%, the stock −39.8% peak to trough. The duopoly does not help — both firms suffer together.
02
Structural disintermediation via private credit. Unlike the cycle, this does not come back. If a growing share of corporate borrowing is arranged bilaterally with funds that do not require a public rating, the rateable universe shrinks permanently. Moody's is currently winning share of the private-credit ratings that do get sought (+40% transactions, 110+ first-time mandates in Q2), but that is a smaller, differently-priced pool than the public bond market it partly replaces.
03
Multiple compression on a normalising cycle. The stock has returned −0.58% over twelve months while trailing EPS rose 15%, which is the market already de-rating this. At 31.4× trailing and a 2.20 PEG, with Q4 guided flat and every published analyst target sitting above the current price, the asymmetry in sentiment is one-sided. No operating metric needs to miss for the multiple to keep compressing — the guided slowdown arriving on schedule may be enough.
  Industry Commentary
Ratings Revenue Is the Issuance Calendar
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The whole rating industry's revenue line is a derivative of primary-market volume, and 2026 has been a boom: Moody's rated issuance volume rose 33% in Q2 alone, on top of more than $6.6 trillion of global rated volume in FY2025. That is the good half of the cycle. The bad half was 2022, when total company revenue fell 12.1% and operating margin fell from 45.6% to 36.7%. Management has already guided Q3 to low double-digit growth and Q4 to roughly flat. The constraint is not competitive — S&P Global's ratings arm faces exactly the same calendar — it is that neither firm controls the variable that sets two-thirds of its ratings revenue.
Private Credit Is Re-Plumbing Corporate Borrowing
ONGOING HEADWIND
The migration of leveraged lending from syndicated public markets into direct-lending funds changes who needs a rating and why. A public bond needs two ratings because the buyer base demands them; a bilateral private loan may need none, or may need one for a narrow purpose such as insurance capital treatment or fund-level financing. Moody's response has been to chase the new pool rather than defend the old one — >40% growth in private credit-related transactions and >110 new first-time mandates in Q2 2026, with fund finance and private credit named as drivers of the +16% Financial Institutions line. Whether that fully replaces the volume it displaces is the open question of the next decade, and it will be answered slowly, in mandate counts, not in a single quarter.
Generative AI: Threat to Research, Upsell to Workflow
EMERGING TAILWIND
Shipped and in market, not speculation: Research Assistant as an agentic contextual chat on every company page; agentic AI in the cloud Intelligent Risk Platform; the first Moody's AI skill on Microsoft 365 Copilot with 20+ global engagements; Connected Intelligence in Amazon QuickSight; IRP on the AWS Marketplace. Management's stated view on falling model costs is that they would "expand usage more than compress price." Bull framing: AI is a distribution and pricing layer on proprietary data — ratings history, entity data, default archives — that no external model can reproduce, and embedding it in Copilot and QuickSight puts Moody's inside workflows it did not previously reach. Bear framing: no AI revenue, AI ARR or pricing uplift is disclosed, "20 engagements" is a pilot, and the sub-line most exposed to substitution — Research & Insights, $995M — grew just 3% in Q2 2026, the slowest of the three. Until R&I growth re-accelerates, the AI story is a product roadmap, not a financial result.
