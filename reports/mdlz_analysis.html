01 Company Overview

Mondelez International is the world's largest biscuit and baked-snacks company and the No. 2 confectionery company globally, built around a portfolio of billion-dollar brands — Oreo, belVita, Chips Ahoy!, Ritz, Cadbury Dairy Milk, Milka, Toblerone, Trident and Halls — sold in roughly 150 countries. Fiscal 2025 net revenue was $38.54B, up 5.8% reported and +4.3% organic, but the composition of that growth is the whole story: pricing contributed +8.0 percentage points while volume/mix subtracted 3.7 points as the company priced through a historic cocoa cost shock. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 stands at $39.90B (+6.0%).

Roughly 40% of revenue comes from emerging markets, an unusually high share for a US-listed packaged-food name. The 2025 profit compression was severe — GAAP gross margin fell 10.7 points to 28.4% and GAAP EPS dropped 44.7% to $1.89 — but a meaningful part of that swing is non-cash mark-to-market movement on cocoa derivatives rather than operating deterioration. That distortion has reversed in 2026: Q3 2026 GAAP EPS rose 78.9% to $1.02, which the company puts down mainly to a favourable year-over-year change in mark-to-market on commodity and currency derivatives, while adjusted EPS rose 5.5% in constant currency to $0.78.

The latest quarter (Q3 2026, reported October 29). Net revenue rose 2.3% to $9.97B and organic revenue (which strips out currency, acquisitions and divestitures) rose 2.1% — and this time volume/mix did most of the work (+1.8 points) while pricing added only +0.3. Cheaper raw materials lifted adjusted gross margin 3.8 points to 34.2%, and the company reaffirmed its 2026 outlook: organic growth of at least 2%, adjusted EPS growth of flat to +5% in constant currency and about $3B of free cash flow. CEO Dirk Van de Put said: "We delivered strong third-quarter results, driven by broad based volume growth and robust share performance." Alongside the results it announced an agreement to acquire Undercover Snacks, a US maker of chocolate-covered quinoa crisps; terms were not disclosed, and Van de Put said on the call that the deal should close by early 2027.

Category Split — The View That Actually Explains The P&L

Geography obscures the real driver. On a category basis, Biscuits & Baked Snacks is ~48% of revenue (Oreo, belVita, Chips Ahoy!, Ritz, TUC, LU) and grew +2.5% organic in Q2 2026 with +1.3pp of volume/mix — healthy, cocoa-light, and the ballast of the business. Chocolate is ~33% of revenue (Cadbury, Milka, Toblerone, Lacta, Côte d'Or) and was essentially flat at −0.1% organic in Q2 2026, with volume/mix −1.6pp; year-to-date chocolate growth of +2.9% was entirely price-driven. In FY2025 the divergence was extreme: chocolate organic revenue rose +11.4% on pricing while volume/mix fell 7.5 points, and biscuits managed only +0.8%. Gum & Candy grew +2.8% in FY2025 and accelerated to +7.6% in Q2 2026 with +4.3pp volume/mix. Beverages & Cheese/Grocery is now a small residual after the JDE Peet's and cheese divestitures. The read-through at mid-year was simple: two-thirds of the portfolio was growing on units, while the chocolate third was buying revenue with price and paying for it in volume. Q3's group-wide volume/mix of +1.8 points, with Europe positive, suggests the chocolate side has started to turn.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
GLOBAL BRAND EQUITY OREO — #1 COOKIE WORLDWIDE DSD & RETAIL SHELF ACCESS 150-COUNTRY DISTRIBUTION 40% EMERGING-MARKET MIX SCALE PROCUREMENT & HEDGING LOCAL HERITAGE BRANDS

Mondelez's moat is brand-plus-distribution, and it is genuinely wide but not deep enough to be price-inelastic. Oreo, Cadbury, Milka and Ritz are each category-defining assets with a century or more of heritage in their home markets, which buys shelf space, retailer negotiating leverage and the ability to raise price ahead of the category. That capability was tested to destruction in 2024–2025 and mostly held: the company pushed through +8.0 points of pricing in FY2025 and still grew organic revenue +4.3%. Scale also matters upstream — Mondelez is one of the largest single buyers of cocoa in the world and runs a sophisticated multi-year hedging book, which smoothed (but also delayed) the cost shock. The emerging-market footprint is the most durable structural advantage; it is far harder to replicate than a brand, and it is where volume growth is still positive. What the moat does not do is protect gross margin against a 3–4x move in the primary raw material, and it does not stop a shopper from buying a store-brand cream cookie at a 40% discount.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Single-input commodity concentration. Cocoa is the dominant variable cost in a third of the portfolio and there is no substitute. GAAP gross margin fell from 39.1% (FY2024) to 28.4% (FY2025) — a 10.7-point collapse. Even adjusted gross margin fell 5.8 points to 32.0%. Hedging shifts the timing of the hit, it does not remove it: hedges locked in at elevated prices weighed on gross profit into mid-2026, and only in Q3 2026 did lower raw-material costs lift adjusted gross margin, by 3.8 points to 34.2%. The same lag works against the company if cocoa climbs again.

2. Demonstrated volume elasticity. The 2025 experiment produced a clear answer: chocolate volume/mix fell −7.5 points against +11.4% pricing. Management itself attributed roughly 3+ points of chocolate volume decline to revenue growth management (its own pricing, promotion and pack-size decisions) and product downsizing. Consumers noticed the smaller bars. Shelf prices have not come back down even as futures have — US chocolate prices were still up ~14% in early 2026 versus 2025 — which leaves the category exposed if the consumer simply stops trading up.

3. Private label and channel shift. North America organic revenue was negative for all of FY2025 (−1.9%), with US biscuits the weak point. Store brands have improved materially in cookies and crackers, hard-discount and club channels keep gaining share, and the price gap Mondelez opened with three years of pricing is exactly the gap private label needs.

4. Earnings quality and currency. Reported results are genuinely hard to read. GAAP operating income fell 44.1% in FY2025, rose 66.0% in Q2 2026 and rose 136.3% in Q3 2026 — none of those numbers describes the business (Q3 adjusted operating income rose 17.0% in constant currency). A large majority of both swings is unrealized mark-to-market on commodity derivatives. Layer on ~60% international revenue, high-inflation Latin American currencies, and a multi-year ERP program running through 2028, and the gap between "reported" and "underlying" stays wide.

Notable One-Off Items

⚠ Read GAAP results with extreme caution. Mondelez runs one of the largest commodity hedging books in packaged food, and unrealized mark-to-market gains and losses on those cocoa and other commodity derivatives flow straight through reported cost of goods sold. The magnitude dwarfs ordinary operating variance:

Commodity derivative mark-to-market. In Q3 2026 reported operating income rose 136.3% while adjusted operating income rose 17.0% in constant currency; the release puts the gap down mainly to a favourable year-over-year change in mark-to-market on commodity and currency derivatives. In Q2 2026 the company recorded an $827M unrealized gain on hedging activities versus a $93M loss in Q2 2025 — a ~$920M year-over-year swing. That single line is responsible for most of the reported +66.0% jump in operating income and the +144.9% jump in GAAP EPS, while adjusted operating income actually fell 4.8% (6.1% in constant currency) and adjusted EPS fell 2.7% in constant currency. The mirror image occurred in FY2025: reported gross profit fell $3.32B while adjusted gross profit fell $1.57B in constant currency, implying well over $1.7B of derivative-driven distortion. Use adjusted figures for any operating comparison.

JDE Peet's / Keurig Dr Pepper. Mondelez sold its remaining 85.9M JDE Peet's shares to JAB Holding in Q4 2024, exiting a long-standing coffee equity stake. Following JAB's agreement in August 2025 to sell JDE Peet's to Keurig Dr Pepper, Mondelez received an additional €145M (~$169M) cash payment from JAB in Q3 2025, recorded as an equity-method investment gain. This is non-recurring and should be stripped from FY2025 comparisons.

Restructuring and transformation. The long-running "Simplify to Grow" program completed in Q4 2024; a new restructuring program was initiated in Q4 2025 to reduce cost structure and streamline operations ($9M of charges in Q2 2026). Separately, a multi-year ERP implementation (a new company-wide accounting and operations system) is running through end-2028 and carried $59M of incremental IT cost in Q2 2026 alone — recurring for now, but finite. The Q3 release lists higher restructuring, intangible-asset impairment and ERP costs than a year earlier among the items weighing on GAAP results.

Impairments and geopolitical costs. Asset impairment and exit costs were modest at $13M in Q2 2026, and FY2025 impairment charges were lower year over year with no brand-specific write-down itemized in the headline release. Incremental costs from the Middle East and Ukraine conflicts (transportation surcharges, evacuation, committed employee compensation) were $11M in Q2 2026.

Currency. After years of translation headwind, currency has flipped to a tailwind: at October 22 exchange rates management now expects it to add about 1.5% to FY2026 net revenue and $0.043 to adjusted EPS (Q3 release; down from about 2.0% and $0.053 at mid-year). That flatters the reported growth rate — organic growth of at least 2% is the number that matters.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The cocoa shock is passing and Mondelez kept its brands and its shelf space through it. Cocoa futures near $5,600/tonne in late October are roughly 57% below the December 2024 record, and because the company hedges forward, the relief is only starting to reach the P&L: Q3 2026 adjusted gross margin rose 3.8 points to 34.2%, and the CFO expects strong EPS growth in 2027. Meanwhile volume has turned: Q3 volume/mix was +1.8 points group-wide, +5.5 in AMEA and +1.6 in Europe. Add a 3.13% dividend with 12 consecutive years of growth, about $3B of expected free cash flow this year, $2.8B returned to shareholders through Q3, and a beta of 0.39, and you have a defensive business whose earnings are climbing out of a trough.

Bear Case

Most of the easy money has been made — the stock is up 23% year to date, sits 0.4% below its 52-week high, and the consensus target implies only 4.4% upside. What remains is the hard part: Europe, 39% of revenue, still saw organic revenue fall 2.1% in Q3 2026, and the volume it won back came with 3.7 points of price cuts — giving price or grammage back eats the cocoa relief before it reaches margin. Structural demand is the deeper worry: chocolate volume/mix fell 7.5 points in FY2025, global cocoa grindings (the tonnes of beans processed, a gauge of demand) fell sharply in 2025, and GLP-1 adoption is a slow-burn headwind to discretionary snacking. At a 3.40 PEG on low-single-digit organic growth and a 9.90% ROIC near its cost of capital, there is little valuation cushion.

Top 3 Risks
01
Cocoa reverses higher. Cocoa has been violently volatile — Barchart puts the 2026 range of ICE futures at $2,846 (March 2) to $6,821 (August 31), with prices near $5,600 on October 23. On the Q3 call the CFO said a surplus this season is projected to swing to a deficit of around 190,000 tons next season. West African weather, disease and political risk in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana can re-tighten supply within a single season. A renewed spike would arrive before the hedge book can adjust and would undo the 2027 margin recovery the share price now assumes.
02
European volume does not recover. Europe is the largest segment and the one where pricing was most aggressive. Q2 2026 organic revenue fell 3.5% with volume/mix −2.1pp and adjusted operating income −22.9% in constant currency; in Q3 volume turned positive (+1.6 points) only alongside 3.7 points of price cuts. If elasticity proves structural rather than cyclical — consumers permanently trading into private-label and discounter chocolate — then the segment's earnings base resets lower regardless of input costs.
03
Earnings quality obscures deterioration. With ~$920M of year-over-year mark-to-market swing in Q2 2026 alone, GAAP results are close to uninformative, and the gap between adjusted and GAAP EPS ($2.92 vs $1.89 in FY2025) is unusually wide. A current ratio of 0.62, $21.74B of borrowings, a dividend equal to 65% of GAAP EPS and a 9.90% ROIC leave less room for error than the defensive label suggests.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
Cocoa & Commodity Cost Inflation
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the single variable that has explained Mondelez's share price for three years. Cocoa futures peaked above $12,000 per tonne in December 2024 — roughly a four-fold move driven by West African crop failure, black pod and swollen shoot disease, aging trees, and structural underinvestment in Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana. The unwind has been equally violent: Barchart's October 23, 2026 review of ICE futures puts the 2026 low at $2,846/tonne on March 2, 78% below the record, followed by a rebound to $6,821 on August 31 and a level near $5,600 on October 23, still roughly 57% below the peak. On the Q3 call CFO Amit Banati said he expects the market to end this year with a surplus of almost half a million tons, with a deficit of around 190,000 tons projected for next season. The demand side has been the mechanism: global grindings fell sharply — Europe −7.2%, Asia −16%, North America −2.8% year over year in Q2 2025 — and volumes at Barry Callebaut's cocoa division dropped 22% in the three months to November 2025.

For Mondelez the accounting lags the market. Management described FY2025 cocoa costs as unprecedented and at record highs, which produced a 10.7-point GAAP and 5.8-point adjusted gross margin decline and a 13.9% fall in adjusted operating income. In Q3 2026 the turn finally reached the P&L: adjusted gross margin rose 3.8 points to 34.2%, driven primarily by lower raw-material costs according to the release, and adjusted gross profit for the first nine months is up 3.7% in constant currency. The relief has started, and management expects much more of it in 2027.
Consumer Trade-Down & Private Label in Snacking
ONGOING HEADWIND
Three years of cumulative pricing opened the widest branded-to-private-label price gap in a generation. US chocolate shelf prices were still up ~14% in early 2026 versus 2025 even as futures collapsed, because manufacturers embedded reformulations, more inclusions and smaller pack sizes that have not reversed. Mondelez's own numbers reflect the consequence: North America organic revenue was −1.9% for FY2025, and group chocolate volume/mix fell 7.5 points against +11.4% pricing. Management concedes that roughly 3+ points of chocolate volume decline came from its own revenue growth management and downsizing decisions. The countermeasure — price-pack architecture and formats like the US "fresh stacks" hitting critical price points — is the right one, but it is a margin trade, not a free win. Hard discounters and improved store brands in cookies and crackers are the structural beneficiaries of any hesitation.
GLP-1 Drugs and the Long-Run Snacking Question — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND (FOR THE BEARS)
GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide and tirzepatide-class weight-loss and diabetes drugs) suppress appetite and, in published consumption studies, disproportionately reduce intake of sweet and high-calorie discretionary snacks — precisely Mondelez's categories. Adoption continues to broaden as supply constraints ease, oral formulations reach market, and reimbursement widens. This is a slow-burn, multi-year variable rather than a quarterly one, and it is not yet separable in Mondelez's reported volumes from cocoa-driven elasticity — but it raises the long-run growth question for chocolate and biscuits in developed markets specifically.

Bull framing: the effect is concentrated in developed markets, which are only ~60% of Mondelez's revenue and shrinking as a share; emerging markets — 40% of revenue, +4.1% organic and +2.2 points of volume/mix in Q3 2026 — have negligible GLP-1 penetration and decades of per-capita consumption runway. Biscuits and gum & candy, ~two-thirds of the portfolio, were growing on volume at mid-year, and group volume turned positive in Q3. Cocoa relief plus recovering units plus a 3.13% growing dividend at 21.8× FY2026 consensus earnings is a reasonable defensive setup.

Bear framing: if GLP-1s compound with private-label trade-down and permanently elevated shelf prices, developed-market chocolate volumes never fully recover, and the cocoa relief simply gets spent defending share. In that world FY2027 margin recovery is smaller than consensus expects, and a 3.40 PEG with 9.90% ROIC on a stock 0.4% off its 52-week high offers no protection.
