01 Company Overview

MediaTek is a Taiwanese chip designer and, by market value, the second-largest company on the Taiwan Stock Exchange after TSMC. It is fabless: it designs the chips and pays foundries, chiefly TSMC, to make them. Its chips go into more than 2 billion devices a year — smartphones, smart-home devices, AI PCs, cars and, from late 2026, AI data centers — and it reports three revenue groups: Smart Edge Platforms (53% of 2Q26 revenue), Mobile Phone (41%) and Power IC (6%). FY2025 revenue was NT$596.0B, up 12.3%, with EPS of NT$66.16, slightly below 2024's NT$66.92. At the October 2 close the company was worth NT$7.94 trillion, about US$249.6B.

The 2026 share price tells a different story from the income statement. The stock closed 2025 at NT$1,430 and stood at NT$4,950 on October 2, 2026 — up 246% in nine months — while trailing EPS fell from NT$66.16 to NT$60.68. At the year-end price the stock traded at about 21.6x FY2025 earnings; it now trades at 81.6x. The market is not paying for the phone-chip business it has. It is paying for a data-center business that has barely started: MediaTek's first custom AI accelerator chip, built for a major US cloud provider it does not name, enters production in the fourth quarter of 2026. Management expects data-center revenue above US$2B in 2026, puts the 2027 market it can address at US$80B, and targets a 15–20% share of it — US$12–16B, against trailing revenue of about US$18.6B for the whole company at the October 2 exchange rate. At the end of August, NVIDIA agreed to buy US$3.5B of a US$3.9B MediaTek convertible bond. Meanwhile the phone business is shrinking: Mobile Phone revenue fell 20% year over year in 2Q26, and management expects global smartphone units to drop about 15% this year. The question at NT$4,950: can the accelerator ramp grow fast enough to earn an 82x multiple before the phone business shrinks further?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Leading-edge design at 2nm with TSMC SerDes & die-to-die interconnect IP Scale: 2B+ devices a year Breadth: phone, PC, TV, Wi-Fi, auto, cloud NVIDIA partnership & capital NT$198B cash & current financial assets

MediaTek's advantage is reusable engineering. The SerDes link that moves data off a TV chip, the modem in a phone processor and the die-to-die interconnect in a cloud accelerator all come from the same R&D budget — NT$39.4B in 2Q26 alone, 25.9% of revenue. Spreading that cost over more than two billion devices a year pays for the high-speed I/O and 2nm design work a cloud customer needs to build its own AI chip. When the CEO explains the data-center wins, he points to the same things: "industry-leading IPs, deep ecosystem partnerships, and proven execution capabilities". The weakness is that the moat protects the capability, not the customer: cloud ASIC programmes are won and lost generation by generation, and MediaTek's data-center story currently rests on one customer.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One customer behind the whole re-rating. The first ASIC is for one unnamed US cloud provider, and management describes the second as designed "in close collaboration with our customer"; that second chip has already moved from an end-2027 target to early 2028. When JPMorgan's Gokul Hariharan asked on July 31 whether MediaTek could become that customer's majority supplier, the CEO declined to go into it, saying only that the company hopes for "better-than-fair share". The CFO said he could give no detail on the other engagements "right now".
02
The phone business is going backwards. Mobile Phone revenue fell 20% year over year in 2Q26, and management expects the smartphone market to shrink about 15% in units this year as component costs rise. The 2nm flagship launch helps the top of the market; it does little for the entry-level volume that the rising costs are hurting.
03
Margins are drifting down. Gross margin was 46.2% in 2Q26 against 49.1% a year earlier (the company attributes the year-over-year gap mainly to a one-time benefit in 2Q25) and 47.5% for FY2025. Operating margin fell to 15.0% from 19.5%. ASIC gross margins will be "slightly diluted" relative to the corporate average, per the CFO, so the mix shift that drives growth also pulls the margin down.
04
The stock moves faster than the business. In 2026 alone the monthly closes show −23.4% in March, +75.2% in April, +65.1% in May and −16.3% in July. The TWSE placed MediaTek under trading restrictions from May 7 to May 20, 2026 after the run-up (TWSE disposition notice of May 6). At 81.6x trailing earnings, small changes to the 2027 ASIC estimate move the price a lot.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the year-ago gross margin: the company attributes most of the 2.9-point year-over-year drop in 2Q26 gross margin to a one-time benefit in 2Q25, so the underlying decline is smaller than the headline. Second, first-half cash flow: operating cash flow fell to NT$6.6B in 1H26 from NT$59.1B, mainly because inventories absorbed NT$31.3B and income tax paid rose to NT$10.4B from NT$4.2B. 1Q26 alone showed an operating outflow of NT$17.7B. Third, the capital raise: on July 31 the board approved a discretionary US$5B financing budget, and at the end of August MediaTek sold a US$3.9B convertible bond — the largest overseas convertible offering by a Taiwan company, per Sullivan & Cromwell, US counsel to the initial purchaser Morgan Stanley — with NVIDIA taking US$3.5B. The bonds pay no interest, run five years from the scheduled September 8 issue date and convert into new shares at NT$4,513.75, 15% above the August 31 close, from three months after issue. MediaTek's filing of August 31 puts the maximum dilution from full conversion at about 1.67% and says the money will fund purchases paid in foreign currency. At the October 2 close of NT$4,950 the share price was already above the conversion price. Non-TIFRS adjustments (share-based pay, acquisition amortisation) are small: 2Q26 non-TIFRS EPS was NT$15.71 against NT$15.28 reported.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of NT$913 to the October 2, 2026 close of NT$4,950, MediaTek's price rose 442.2%. The TAIEX rose 185.4% over the same window, a strong five years for Taiwan's market as a whole. MediaTek also pays out most of its earnings: on Yahoo's dividend-adjusted series the five-year total return is about +639%. Most of the gain came late. At the end of 2025 the stock was only 57% above its October 2021 level; the remaining gain came in 2026, when it rose 246.2% against 67.4% for the TAIEX.

The 6-period monthly RSI stands at 80.1, deep in overbought territory, and peaked at 89.1 in May 2026, the highest reading in the five-year series. The low was 29.0 in September 2022, the lowest month-end close of the window. The 10-month moving average is about NT$3,371; the price is 47% above it, and even the 3-month average of about NT$4,598 sits below today's close. The trend is still up, but the stock is a long way above its averages and remains volatile.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

MediaTek has won a place in the fastest-growing part of the chip industry. The first AI accelerator ASIC ships in the fourth quarter, management says it is comfortable with supply for this year's and next year's revenue targets, and a 15–20% share of a US$80B 2027 market would add US$12–16B of revenue to a company with about US$18.6B of trailing sales. The CFO says that business is accretive to operating margin at scale, so earnings could grow faster than revenue as opex falls "substantially" as a share of sales. A second, larger ASIC is on track for 2028, other customers are in talks, and NVIDIA has put US$3.5B into the company. Meanwhile the edge business is gaining share, August revenue rose 44% year over year, the balance sheet holds NT$198B of cash and current financial assets against NT$27B of debt, and the company still pays out about 80% of its earnings. On this view, 81.6x trailing earnings is the price of a business whose earnings are about to grow sharply.

Bear Case

The price assumes the data-center plan works, while the reported numbers are flat to down: TTM EPS of NT$60.68 is below FY2024's NT$66.92, gross margin has slipped from 49.1% to 46.2%, the phone business is down 20% in a smartphone market expected to shrink 15%, and inventory is up 77% year over year, at 102 days of cost of sales. The whole re-rating rests on one unnamed customer and two chips, the second of which depends on a packaging technology still being proven. ASIC margins are dilutive at the gross line. The convertible bond can add up to about 1.67% more shares, per the company's filing. At 2.7x the median Taiwan chip stock's multiple and 3x the market's, a delayed ramp or a lost generation would hurt the price badly — and the stock has already fallen 23% and 16% in single months this year.

ASIC execution and customer concentration. The US$2B-plus 2026 data-center figure and the 2027 share target both rest on one US cloud customer and a production start in the fourth quarter. Industry Commentary
Cloud companies are designing their own AI chips
EMERGING TAILWIND

Alongside the GPUs they buy, cloud providers design custom accelerators. MediaTek's CEO said demand for "customized solutions" to optimise performance per watt and per dollar of total cost of ownership "remains strong". Those chips need a design partner with high-speed I/O, advanced packaging and leading-edge process experience. Competitors for this work include Broadcom, Marvell and Taiwan's Alchip and GUC. Management's US$80B 2027 SAM covers accelerators alone, and the CEO expects the 2028 figure to be "quite a bit higher". NVIDIA's US$3.5B investment and the NVLink Fusion tie-up link MediaTek's custom chips to NVIDIA's own data-center connectivity.

The AI build-out is squeezing consumer electronics
ONGOING HEADWIND

On the 1Q26 call MediaTek said the concentration of industry resources on data centers has raised costs for smartphones. Phone makers have raised prices and shifted to higher-end models, which the company says will lower demand. MediaTek expects 2026 smartphone units to fall about 15%. The same pressure lifted Smart Edge revenue: MediaTek charges more for some TV chips "to reflect the increasing DRAM costs". MediaTek is raising prices "to ensure these increases are appropriately reflected", and the CFO frames this as cost-sharing rather than margin expansion. The CEO argues that phone replacement demand will recover "when the supply conditions normalize"; nothing in the guidance dates that recovery.

Taiwan's market re-rated around AI — and MediaTek most of all
VALUATION CONTEXT

The TAIEX ended 2025 at 28,963.60 and stood at 48,475.74 on October 2, 2026, up 67.4%. On our calculation from exchange data, TSMC (NT$64.8T), MediaTek (NT$7.94T) and Delta Electronics (NT$4.90T) are the three largest TWSE-listed companies. The bull reading: Taiwan's chip designers are being repriced as AI infrastructure companies rather than consumer-electronics suppliers, and MediaTek's 81.6x reflects earnings that will arrive in 2027. The bear reading: the market has paid in advance for growth that has not yet appeared in a single reported quarter — 2Q26 revenue grew 1.2% — and a stock whose price has more than tripled in nine months can lose a lot quickly if the ASIC ramp slips. The September monthly sales figure and the 3Q26 results are the next tests.
