01 Company Overview

MediaTek is a Taiwanese chip designer and, by market value, the second-largest company on the Taiwan Stock Exchange after TSMC. It is fabless: it designs the chips and pays foundries, chiefly TSMC, to make them. Its chips go into more than 2 billion devices a year — smartphones, smart-home devices, AI PCs, cars and, from late 2026, AI data centers — and it reports three revenue groups: Smart Edge Platforms (58% of 3Q26 revenue), Mobile Phone (37%) and Power IC (5%). FY2025 revenue was NT$596.0B, up 12.3%, with EPS of NT$66.16, slightly below 2024's NT$66.92. At the November 6 close the company was worth NT$5.44 trillion, about US$166.0B.

The share price still tells a different story from the income statement. The stock closed 2025 at NT$1,430 and stood at NT$3,390 on November 6, 2026 — up 137.1% for the year, but 31.5% below the NT$4,950 of the previous edition (October 2) and 38.1% below the NT$5,480 intraday high of September 22. Trailing EPS has risen to NT$62.34 from NT$60.68 as 3Q26's NT$17.50 replaced 3Q25's NT$15.84. At the year-end price the stock traded at about 21.6x FY2025 earnings; it now trades at 54.4x. The market is paying for a data-center business that has only just started shipping: MediaTek's first custom AI accelerator chip, built for a major US cloud provider it does not name, began initial shipments in September. On October 30 the CEO said the company is confident of US$2.5B of revenue from it in the fourth quarter (in July the target for all of 2026 was above US$2B), expects the program to reach the high end of a roughly US$16B revenue target for 2027, about 20% of the AI accelerator chip market, and guided fourth-quarter revenue up 25% to 32% from the third. For scale, trailing revenue is about US$18.9B (NT$619.6B at the November 6 exchange rate, our arithmetic). The quarter beat the top of the company's revenue guidance by 5.2%, and the stock still fell 13.4% from the October 30 close to November 6, against 12.2% for the TAIEX index, as Taiwan's large electronics stocks sold off together. Meanwhile the phone business is shrinking: Mobile Phone revenue fell 17% year over year in 3Q26. The question at NT$3,390: will the accelerator ramp keep arriving on schedule, in a market that is marking down AI-linked stocks across the board?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Leading-edge design at 2nm with TSMC SerDes & die-to-die interconnect IP Scale: 2B+ devices a year Breadth: phone, PC, TV, Wi-Fi, auto, cloud NVIDIA partnership & capital NT$278B cash & current financial assets

MediaTek's advantage is reusable engineering. The SerDes link that moves data off a TV chip, the modem in a phone processor and the die-to-die interconnect in a cloud accelerator all come from the same R&D budget — NT$42.2B in 3Q26 alone, 25.1% of revenue. Spreading that cost over more than two billion devices a year pays for the high-speed I/O and 2nm design work a cloud customer needs to build its own AI chip. On October 30 the CEO described the first accelerator as showing the company's ability to "integrate industry-leading interconnect IPs, advanced process and advanced packaging technologies" with its supply-chain skills. The weakness is that the moat protects the capability, not the customer: cloud ASIC programmes are won and lost generation by generation, and MediaTek's data-center revenue so far comes from one customer; the second customer it has confirmed is not expected to bring revenue until 2028.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One customer behind the whole price surge. The first ASIC is for one unnamed US cloud provider. On October 30 the CFO said there is a second customer and that it "is not coming from China", with no further detail. In April, mass production of the second chip was "targeted to start by the end of 2027"; on October 30 the CEO gave high-volume production in the first quarter of 2028. Asked by JPMorgan about market talk of design and tape-out delays, the CEO acknowledged "some noise about the tape-out schedule" and said the project is on schedule. Packaging is a second dependency: when Deutsche Bank asked about reliance on Intel's EMIB-T packaging, the CEO answered that MediaTek is not dependent on one supplier and that "TSMC is our most important partner, period."
02
The phone business is going backwards. Mobile Phone revenue fell 17% year over year in 3Q26 and is 37% of revenue; management expected the smartphone market to shrink about 15% in units this year as component costs rise. The group's revenue fell 1% from 2Q26 as flagship phones outperformed with the 2nm launch, and the CEO cited seasonal softness in consumer electronics for the fourth quarter.
03
Margins are guided lower and cash is tied up. Gross margin was 46.3% in 3Q26 (46.5% a year earlier) and is guided to 45% ±1.5 points in 4Q26; the CFO said the data-center business is "currently still below the corporate average" and that this is why the guidance steps down one percentage point. For the nine months, operating margin was 15.5% against 18.3% a year earlier, operating cash flow was NT$2.3B against NT$98.3B, and inventory has grown 169.5% year over year to NT$153.5B.
04
The stock moves faster than the business. In 2026 the monthly closes show +23.1% in January, −23.4% in March, +75.2% in April, +65.1% in May, −16.3% in July, +25.4% in September and −20.4% in October, and the stock fell 31.5% between October 2 and November 6. The TWSE placed MediaTek under trading restrictions from May 7 to May 20, 2026 after the run-up (TWSE disposition notice of May 6). At 54.4x trailing earnings, small changes to the 2027 ASIC estimate move the price a lot.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, cash flow and inventory: operating cash flow was an outflow of NT$4.3B in 3Q26 and only NT$2.3B for the nine months, against NT$98.3B a year earlier, because changes in operating assets and liabilities absorbed NT$90.6B and income tax paid rose. Inventory stood at NT$153.5B at September 30, up 169.5% in a year. Second, the capital raise: on July 31 the board approved a discretionary US$5B financing budget, and MediaTek sold a US$3.9B convertible bond — the largest such offering ever by a Taiwanese company, MediaTek said (Focus Taiwan) — with NVIDIA taking US$3.5B and, per the CEO on October 30, Alphabet also taking part. The 3Q26 cash-flow statement shows NT$122.8B of bond proceeds, which lifted cash and current financial assets to NT$278.3B (NT$198.4B at June 30) and total liabilities to NT$492.3B. The bonds pay no interest, run five years from the scheduled September 8 issue date and convert into new shares at NT$4,513.75 (15% above the August 31 close), from three months after issue. MediaTek's filing of August 31 puts the maximum dilution (the shrinkage in existing holders' stake) from full conversion at about 1.67% and says the money will fund purchases paid in foreign currency. At the November 6 close of NT$3,390 the share price was 24.9% below the conversion price (our arithmetic), so converting into shares would not pay at that price. Third, non-TIFRS adjustments (share-based pay, acquisition amortisation) are small: 3Q26 non-TIFRS EPS was NT$17.92 against NT$17.50 reported.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

MediaTek has won a place in the fastest-growing part of the chip industry. The first AI accelerator ASIC began shipping in September, the CEO is confident of US$2.5B of revenue from it in the fourth quarter, and the company is aiming for the high end of a roughly US$16B target in 2027, against about US$18.9B of trailing sales. Fourth-quarter revenue is guided up 25% to 32% from the third, and the CFO says next year's operating margin should be above 20%, against 16.1% in 3Q26, so earnings could grow faster than revenue as operating costs fall as a share of sales (guided at 26% for 4Q26, against 30.2% in 3Q26 by our arithmetic). A second ASIC is on track for high-volume production in early 2028, the CFO confirmed a second customer with first revenue expected in 2028, and NVIDIA and Alphabet took part in the company's bond. Meanwhile the edge business is growing fast (Smart Edge revenue +63% year over year), the balance sheet holds NT$278B of cash and current financial assets, and the company still pays out about 80% of its earnings. On this view, 54.4x trailing earnings (21.9x consensus next-year EPS) is the price of a business whose earnings are about to grow sharply.

Bear Case

The price assumes the data-center plan works, while reported cash generation has turned: nine-month operating cash flow was NT$2.3B against NT$98.3B a year earlier, inventory is up 169.5% year over year at 127 days of turnover, and 3Q26 EPS grew 10.5% on revenue growth of 18.3%. Gross margin is guided down to 45% because the data-center business earns a lower gross margin than the rest. The whole price gain rests on one current customer and two chips, the second of which depends on packaging technology still being proven and was the subject of delay talk that the CEO called "noise". The convertible bond can add up to about 1.67% more shares, per the company's filing. At 2.2x the median Taiwan chip stock's valuation, a delayed ramp or a lost generation would hurt the price badly — and the stock has just fallen 31.5% in five weeks, with seven monthly moves of more than 15% this year.

Top 3 Risks

01
ASIC execution and customer concentration. The US$2.5B fourth-quarter figure and the 2027 target of about US$16B both rest on one US cloud customer and a production ramp that began in September, and the second ASIC depends on advanced packaging yields for first-quarter 2028 production.
02
A shrinking phone market. Mobile Phone is still 37% of revenue and fell 17% year over year in 3Q26; management expected a 15% drop in global smartphone units in 2026 as memory and other component costs rise. Passing those costs on keeps the margin steady but can cost volume.
03
Valuation, cash flow and volatility. At 54.4x trailing and 46.8x consensus 2026 earnings, the price depends on 2027 forecasts, while nine-month operating cash flow was NT$2.3B. The stock has moved more than 15% in seven of the ten months of 2026, fell 31.5% between October 2 and November 6, and spent two weeks under exchange trading restrictions in May. Its five-year monthly beta against the TAIEX is 2.17 (our calculation).
Industry Commentary
Cloud companies are designing their own AI chips
EMERGING TAILWIND

Alongside the GPUs they buy, cloud providers design custom accelerators. MediaTek's CEO said demand for "customized solutions" to optimise performance per watt and per dollar of total cost of ownership "remains strong". Those chips need a design partner with high-speed I/O, advanced packaging and leading-edge process experience. Competitors for this work include Broadcom, Marvell and Taiwan's Alchip and GUC. Management's US$80B 2027 SAM covers accelerators alone, and the CEO expects the 2028 figure to be "quite a bit higher". NVIDIA's US$3.5B investment and the NVLink Fusion tie-up link MediaTek's custom chips to NVIDIA's own data-center connectivity.

The AI build-out is squeezing consumer electronics
ONGOING HEADWIND

On the 1Q26 call MediaTek said the concentration of industry resources on data centers has raised costs for smartphones. Phone makers have raised prices and shifted to higher-end models, which the company says will lower demand. MediaTek expects 2026 smartphone units to fall about 15%. The same pressure lifted Smart Edge revenue: MediaTek charges more for some TV chips "to reflect the increasing DRAM costs". MediaTek is raising prices "to ensure these increases are appropriately reflected", and the CFO frames this as cost-sharing rather than margin expansion. The CEO argues that phone replacement demand will recover "when the supply conditions normalize"; nothing in the guidance dates that recovery.

Taiwan's market rose on AI — and then fell back
VALUATION CONTEXT

The TAIEX ended 2025 at 28,963.60 and stood at 39,701.64 on November 6, 2026, up 37.1% for the year but down 18.1% from 48,475.74 on October 2. On our calculation from exchange data (closing price × issued shares), TSMC (NT$55.37T), MediaTek (NT$5.44T) and Hon Hai (NT$3.16T) are the three largest TWSE-listed companies. The bull reading: Taiwan's chip designers are being priced as AI infrastructure companies rather than consumer-electronics suppliers, and MediaTek's 54.4x reflects earnings that will arrive in 2027. The bear reading: the market paid in advance for growth that had not yet appeared in reported earnings — 3Q26 EPS grew 10.5% year over year — and a stock whose price rose 137% in 2026 can lose a lot quickly when the whole market turns, as it did in the first week of November. October sales and the fourth-quarter results are the next tests.
