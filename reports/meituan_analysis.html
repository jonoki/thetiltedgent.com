01 Company Overview

Meituan is China's largest app for local services: you order dinner, groceries or medicine for delivery within the hour, buy discounted vouchers for restaurants, spas and cinemas, read and write reviews on Dianping, and book hotels and trips — all in one place. It reports two segments: Core Local Commerce (food delivery, the Meituan Instashopping quick-commerce service, and the in-store, hotel and travel business) and New Initiatives (grocery retail such as Xiaoxiang Supermarket, and Keeta, its food-delivery app outside mainland China, now running in Hong Kong, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE and Brazil). FY2025 revenue was RMB364.85B, up 8.1%, but the year ended in a loss of RMB23.35B against a profit of RMB35.81B in FY2024. At HK$70.20 the company is worth US$55.25B.

The loss is the whole story of the last year. In 2025 JD.com and Alibaba poured subsidies into instant delivery to take share, and Meituan answered in kind. Its own February 2026 profit warning described "unprecedentedly intense industry competition", and the Core Local Commerce segment swung from an RMB52.4B operating profit in 2024 to an RMB6.9B loss in 2025. The quarters tell it plainly: a net loss of RMB18.6B in Q3 2025, RMB15.1B in Q4, RMB6.8B in Q1 2026 — then a RMB2.2B profit in Q2 2026, as the price war cooled and Meituan cut user incentives. The Q2 numbers beat expectations by a wide margin, yet the stock is down 33.6% over the year and 9.4% since that print. The market's question is not whether the bleeding has stopped; it is whether the old profit level comes back while Douyin pushes into the in-store business, Keeta keeps spending abroad, and Beijing's regulators keep the platform in view.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Courier network density 1.5B user reviews (Dianping) Merchant base & ad tools One app, many services RMB168B cash & treasury Founder control Keeta launch playbook

Meituan's advantage is density. The more orders in a neighbourhood, the more deliveries each courier can chain together and the cheaper each one gets. That pulls in more merchants, then more users, then more orders. Add the review base — the Q2 2026 Must-Eat List drew on 1.5 billion user reviews — and you get a platform that pays a rival to undercut it for a while but is hard to replace. 2025 showed the limit of that argument. Density did not stop JD.com and Alibaba from buying share with subsidies; it only meant Meituan could afford to match them. Its moat protects its market position, not its margins, and in 2025 the margin was what gave.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rivals can restart the subsidy war. The 2025 fight erased a RMB52.4B segment profit in one year. Wang Xing told analysts on the August 28 call, "We don't think irrational competition is sustainable" — a view, not a guarantee. JD.com and Alibaba both have the balance sheets to restart it.
02
Douyin in the in-store business. Selling restaurant and leisure vouchers is the in-store half of Core Local Commerce. Li Chengdong, founder of the consultancy Dolphin, told the South China Morning Post that Douyin posed a "structural, long-term threat" there, using short video and live streams to send viewers to restaurants. On the call management said operating margin was likely to fall from Q2 as Meituan steps up in-store investment in Q3 and Q4.
03
Regulators keep finding things. October 2021: RMB3.442B anti-monopoly fine for "pick-one-from-two" exclusivity. Q1 2026: an RMB745.7M fine from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) over merchant-verification failures under the Food Safety Law. September 19, 2026: state broadcaster CCTV reported that SAMR's Beijing branch is investigating Meituan unit Beijing Sankuai for suspected unfair competition, one of four online hotel and travel booking platforms under review (Reuters). Courier welfare is a further cost: occupational injury insurance now covers every order.
04
Overseas spending. The company attributes the New Initiatives loss widening to RMB10.1B in FY2025 to increased investment in overseas businesses; Keeta entered four new countries in the second half of 2025. Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia are profitable; Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE and Brazil are still early.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort 2026's reported figures. First, investment gains flatter Q2: "other gains, net" were RMB3.49B in the quarter, mainly gains on certain investments. That is more than the RMB2.69B group operating profit, so the cleaner read is the RMB3.93B of segment operating profit, which excludes unallocated items. Second, a RMB22.2B fair-value gain on stakes held at fair value through other comprehensive income, including Z.AI, went straight to equity in Q2, never touching profit; it is why book value rose in a loss-making half. Third, Q1 2026 carried the RMB745.7M SAMR food-safety fine. On the financing side, holders of US$1.48B of the zero-coupon convertible bonds due 2028 put them back to the company on April 27, 2026 at 101.28% of principal; US$18.6M remain.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of HK$269.60 to the October 2, 2026 close of HK$70.20, Meituan lost 74.0% of its price. The Hang Seng Index fell 5.5% over the same window, so Meituan trailed its home market by 68.4 percentage points. Neither figure includes dividends, and Meituan pays none. Most of the window's damage was done before the price war: the stock had already fallen to HK$62.55 by January 2024. It then nearly tripled to HK$182.50 by October 2024, and has lost 61.5% from there.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how long the selling ran. It printed below 22 for five months in a row, February through June 2026, bottoming at 14.2 in June — the lowest reading since April 2022 (12.6), the series' first value. July's 35.0% rebound took it to 51.1, and it has drifted back to 33.5. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The price war is ending and the books show it: Core Local Commerce went from a RMB2.0B loss in Q1 2026 to a RMB5.7B profit in Q2, delivery revenue is growing again as subsidies come out, and adjusted profit came in at roughly seven times the Bloomberg-compiled forecast. Before the war the same segment earned RMB52.4B a year. Even a partial return of that, on a business worth US$55.25B with RMB79.6B of net cash and 0.76× EV/sales, leaves room for the 57.6% upside the average analyst target implies. Keeta has turned profitable in Hong Kong and Saudi Arabia and is repeating the playbook elsewhere; stakes in Li Auto, Z.AI and Unitree are extra value; and 31 of 37 analysts rate it a buy.

Bear Case

The war ended because everyone ran low on patience, not because anyone lost the ability to fight. JD.com and Alibaba can restart it, the CFO says subsidies will take a few quarters to normalize, and management already expects Q3 margins to fall as it spends more on the in-store business, where Douyin is pushing in. Trailing free cash flow is −RMB40.3B, ROE −24%, and Q2's group operating profit was smaller than its investment gains. Regulators fined the company RMB3.442B in 2021 and RMB745.7M in Q1 2026, and opened a new probe in September. A stock down 74% in five years with the trend still pointing down is not cheap merely because it fell.

Top 3 Risks

01
A renewed subsidy war. Core Local Commerce margin went from 20.9% (2024) to −2.6% (2025) in one round. The industry's subsidy level is still above 2024's, per the CFO, and the two challengers have the balance sheets to go again.
02
In-store margin erosion from Douyin. Management has said Q3 and Q4 will carry more in-store investment and a lower operating margin than Q2. If the voucher business needs subsidies like delivery did, the recovery stalls in the segment that used to fund it.
03
Regulation and deal risk. The open SAMR unfair-competition probe, one of four into hotel and travel booking platforms, the pending anti-monopoly review of the Dingdong deal, and rising courier-welfare obligations all sit with the same regulators that fined the company RMB3.442B in 2021 and RMB745.7M in 2026.
Industry Commentary
China's instant-delivery subsidy war
COOLING, NOT OVER

In 2025 JD.com and Alibaba used aggressive subsidies to win share in on-demand delivery (South China Morning Post), and Meituan answered by raising incentives for users, couriers and merchants — the three fronts its February 2026 profit warning lists. The cost shows in Meituan's own numbers: Core Local Commerce lost RMB14.1B in Q3 2025 at a −21.0% margin. By Q2 2026 the industry had "gradually shifted back to operational efficiency", in Meituan's words. Wang Xing's position on the call, "We don't think irrational competition is sustainable", is the bull case in one sentence. His CFO's caveat that normalisation will take a few more quarters is the bear case.

Douyin and the in-store business
ONGOING HEADWIND

The in-store business sells vouchers — paying in advance on the app for a meal, a massage or a theme-park ticket — to people already searching Dianping for where to go. Douyin turns entertainment into that intent, courting restaurant chains with discounted meals sold through short videos and live streams (Dolphin's Li Chengdong, via the South China Morning Post). Meituan's answer is reviews and AI: its "Must-Eat List" built on 1.5 billion user reviews, the Xiaotuan assistant, and AI agents for merchants. Per the SCMP, Wang told analysts, "We aim to become a digital co-pilot for merchants on our platform." On AI more broadly he said on the call, "We are not going to compete to be a token factory."

Keeta — exporting the delivery model
EMERGING TAILWIND

Keeta takes Meituan's dense, algorithm-dispatched delivery model abroad. Hong Kong (launched May 2023) reached positive unit economics in Q4 2025 and, per the Q2 2026 release, "stable profitability"; Saudi Arabia turned profitable in July 2026, per management on the call. In the second half of 2025 Meituan added Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE and Brazil, where it is concentrating on São Paulo before going wider. The pattern so far — heavy launch offers (free delivery and discounts at the September 2024 Riyadh launch, per Wikipedia), then efficiency gains as order density builds — costs money up front: it is the main reason New Initiatives lost RMB10.1B in FY2025. Each market that turns profitable shows the playbook works; each new launch restarts the spending.
