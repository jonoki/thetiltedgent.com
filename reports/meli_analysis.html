01 Company Overview

MercadoLibre operates the largest online commerce and payments ecosystem in Latin America, spanning marketplace retail (Mercado Libre), a logistics network (Mercado Envíos), a payments and financial services arm (Mercado Pago), a lending book (Mercado Crédito), an asset-management product, and a fast-growing advertising business. It is present across roughly 18 countries, with Brazil, Mexico and Argentina supplying the overwhelming majority of revenue.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $28.89B, up 39.1% year over year, and the trailing-twelve-month figure has since reached $38.52B, up 47.1% on the four quarters before it. Q3 2026 revenue was $10.75B, up 45% in dollars and 41% with currency moves stripped out; at Q2 the company counted 30 straight quarters of growth above 30%, and Q3 extends that run. It ranks #61 in the Nasdaq-100 by market capitalisation.

The catch is that growth is no longer free. Q3 2026 income from operations fell 6.5% year over year to $676M, and the operating margin was 6.3%, against 9.8% a year earlier and 6.7% in Q2. Net income still rose 6.4% to $448M, because the tax bill fell to $153M from $215M. "Two factors drove this: higher net shipping costs and higher sales and marketing spending as we continue to develop our affiliate program," CFO Martín de los Santos said on the earnings call. CEO Ariel Szarfsztejn described the trade directly: "We have accepted a lower margin to do that, and I think we've been open on that regard." Whether that is investment or erosion is the central question in the stock.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
Owned Logistics Network Two-Sided Marketplace Liquidity Proprietary Underwriting Data Payments + Commerce Flywheel Savings Product (AUM +67%) Local Regulatory Fluency 94M Active Buyers

The moat is not the website. It is the combination of a physical network that took a decade and a great deal of capital to build, and a data asset that a new entrant cannot replicate at any price. Mercado Envíos gives MercadoLibre delivery economics and delivery speed that no cross-border competitor can match in Brazil, Mexico or Argentina without making the same investment. Mercado Pago then sits on top of that flow and observes the transaction history of 94 million buyers and the sales history of the merchants selling to them. In markets where formal credit-bureau coverage is thin, that observation is the underwriting model. It lets MercadoLibre extend credit to borrowers a traditional bank cannot price, at interest rates high enough to absorb double-digit loss rates and still produce a 20.1% net interest margin after losses. On the Q3 call the CEO said: "we are gaining market share in every major market in both commerce and fintech."

The cycle reinforces itself: credit funds purchases, purchases generate transaction data, data improves lending decisions, better lending funds more purchases. It is also the source of the fragility, because each round puts more balance-sheet risk on a business the market values as a retailer. Total debt stands at $16.15B against $7.38B of cash and short-term investments. The company's own net debt figure is $8.03B, which counts only unrestricted cash and also subtracts long-term investments. The debt-to-equity ratio is 1.97, with a current ratio of 1.17 and a quick ratio of 0.43: ratios that look ordinary for a bank and tight for a retailer.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

Credit risk carried on balance sheet. The loan book grew 62% year over year to almost $18 billion while 15–90 day non-performing loans sat at 7.0% of the portfolio and quarterly provisions ran at $1,342M. A book growing that fast flatters its own delinquency ratio, because young loans have not had time to go bad. Growth has slowed for two quarters running, from 87% at Q1 to 75% and then 62%, while the early-delinquency rate went from 8.0% to 7.0% and then held. Management said it has been more cautious with the riskiest borrowers in Brazil. A sharper slowdown would be the harder test, since the reported ratio can rise even if lending standards are unchanged.

Deliberate margin compression with an uncertain payback. Operating margin fell 3.5 percentage points year over year to 6.3% in Q3 2026, gross margin has fallen from 50.24% (FY2023) to 42.02% over the last four quarters, and the card book's margin after losses was negative 2.5% at Q2. Management frames this as building habits: the Q3 letter says it is "confident in our long-term margin trajectory" and that "progress will not be linear", and it gives no margin targets. If card customers do not mature into profitable balances, the company will have spent several points of margin on volume that never earns its cost of capital.

Macro and currency translation. Revenue is earned in Brazilian reais, Argentine pesos and Mexican pesos, and reported in US dollars. Q3 2026 revenue grew 45% in dollars but 41% FX-neutral, so currency helped again. It has frequently done the opposite. Argentina in particular has run extreme inflation and repeated devaluation, which means reported Argentine dollar growth and real Argentine local-currency growth can point in opposite directions in the same quarter.

Country concentration and new competition. Brazil ($6.00B), Mexico ($2.43B) and Argentina ($1.82B) supplied about 95% of Q3 2026 revenue. Mexico's FX-neutral GMV growth slowed to 21%. The Q3 letter calls Brazil's market "intense but stable". Amazon continues to invest in Brazil and Mexico, and Asian low-price entrants, Shein and Temu, have targeted the low-ticket, high-frequency categories where MercadoLibre's shipping subsidies are most expensive to defend.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

The Q3 2026 decline in operating income (down 6.5% on 45% revenue growth) is a run-rate cost of the strategy rather than a one-off: it reflects shipping, fulfilment capacity and marketing that management says it chose to spend. The CFO said part of the shipping cost should reverse in Q4 and that some of the pressure from higher fuel and energy costs should remain. Net income rose 6.4% only because tax fell: income tax was $153M on $601M of pre-tax income (25.5%), against $215M on $636M (33.8%) a year earlier.

Two accounting features deserve flagging. First, the $1,342M quarterly provision for doubtful accounts is a genuine operating expense of the lending business and it scales with the loan book. Second, reported free cash flow of $12.25B is not comparable to a conventional retailer's, because it absorbs growth in customer deposits and payment float; the company's own adjusted free cash flow was $285M in Q3 and $443M for the first nine months of 2026. Term funding continued: on September 14, 2026 the company closed an offering of $1,000M of 10-year senior unsecured notes at 5.850%, announced on September 9, for general corporate purposes.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying two businesses for the price of one badly-labelled business. Commerce is growing GMV 33% FX-neutral with 94 million buyers and a logistics network no competitor can replicate quickly. Fintech adds $108.8B of TPV up 53%, 93M monthly active users and savings balances up 67%, and the credit book, near $18B at a 20.1% NIMAL, is built on transaction data no bank in the region has. The 15–90 day delinquency rate held at 7.0% as loan growth slowed. Advertising, up 58%, is the margin repair kit. Consensus expects EPS to rebound 45.7% to $54.44 in 2027; against that number the $1,510.00 close is 27.7x earnings for a business still growing revenue at 45%.

Bear Case

The stock has returned about 5.0% a year since November 2021 while revenue more than quintupled, and it still trades at 40.50x trailing earnings. Operating margin fell 3.5 points year over year to 6.3%, gross margin has dropped more than eight points since FY2023, and trailing EPS of $37.28 is below FY2025's. EPS missed Nasdaq's consensus in three of the last four quarters. Meanwhile a credit book growing 62% a year carries a 7.0% early-delinquency rate and $1,342M of quarterly provisions, and the card book was earning a negative 2.5% margin after losses at Q2. Fast loan growth holds the reported NPL ratio down; when growth slows, the ratio can rise. A 40x multiple on a business with bank-like balance-sheet risk, Argentine peso exposure, and Amazon, Shein and Temu attacking the same categories does not price the downside.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Credit deterioration in a young loan book. The portfolio grew 62% to almost $18 billion with 15–90 day NPLs at 7.0% and quarterly provisions of $1,342M. Rapid growth flatters delinquency ratios because new loans have not aged. A further slowdown in lending, voluntary or forced, would raise the reported NPL rate even with unchanged standards, and the market values this company as a retailer, not as a lender with a rising loss curve.
02
Margin investment that does not pay back. Operating margin of 6.3% is 3.5 points below the prior year, and management declined to forecast its path. Consensus assumes EPS recovers 45.7% in 2027. If card customers and advertising do not deliver that recovery, both the earnings base and the multiple applied to it are at risk at the same time.
03
Currency and country concentration. Brazil, Mexico and Argentina supply about 95% of revenue, earned in local currency and reported in dollars. Q3 2026 saw dollar growth (45%) exceed FX-neutral growth (41%), a tailwind that can reverse. Argentine devaluation and inflation can make reported dollar results and real local performance diverge sharply, and the loan book is denominated in the same volatile currencies.
  Industry Commentary
The Credit Book Is the Story Nobody Underwrites
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
If you read only one section of this report, read this one. MercadoLibre is running a consumer and merchant lending operation of almost $18 billion inside a company the market prices on e-commerce multiples. The book grew 62% year over year in dollars, down from 75% at Q2. Credit cards are 47% of total credit, up from 44% a year earlier, so the fastest-growing and youngest part of the book is also becoming the largest. The Q3 letter calls the card "our largest investment in Fintech", at almost $450M over the past 12 months, and says Brazil card issuance reached a record.

The credit metrics: 15–90 day non-performing loans (balances where the borrower has missed payments but is not yet 90 days late, the early-warning bucket) stood at 7.0%, which the Q3 letter calls broadly stable against Q2 and a year earlier. Provisions for doubtful accounts were $1,342 million in the quarter, roughly 12.5% of revenue and nearly twice operating income of $676M. NIMAL, net interest margin after losses, the yield on the book after funding cost and credit losses, was 20.1%, down 1 percentage point year over year. A margin that high after losses tells you the interest rates charged are very high, which is what lending profitably into markets with thin credit-bureau coverage and high local inflation requires.

The part that should make a careful reader uncomfortable is the credit card NIMAL, which was negative 2.5% at Q2; the Q3 letter says it improved on the quarter and gives no figure. Cards are being issued at a loss after credit costs, on the thesis that cardholders become valuable long-term customers. That may prove right. But it is a customer-acquisition subsidy funded by the balance sheet, in the fastest-growing part of the portfolio. Two cautions apply. First, a book growing 62% a year is young: the denominator of every delinquency ratio is swollen with loans too new to have defaulted, so the 7.0% reading probably understates where the book settles over a full credit cycle. Second, MercadoLibre keeps much of this risk itself, and every dollar of credit loss it keeps lands on the income statement of a company trading at 40.50x trailing earnings.
FX Translation: When 45% Growth and 41% Growth Are the Same Quarter
CUTS BOTH WAYS
MercadoLibre earns Brazilian reais, Argentine pesos and Mexican pesos and reports US dollars. The gap between those two facts is often the difference between a beat and a miss. In Q3 2026, total revenue grew 45% in US dollars but 41% FX-neutral (local-currency growth with exchange-rate movement stripped out). Currency added four points that quarter, after seven in Q2. It has subtracted more than that in others, and investors who anchor on headline dollar growth without checking the FX-neutral line will misread the trend.

The same gap shows up in GMV: $22.8 billion, +38% in dollars but +33% FX-neutral. By country, FX-neutral GMV growth was Argentina +40%, Brazil +36%, Mexico +21%; FX-neutral revenue growth was Argentina +42%, Brazil +41%, Mexico +36%. Note what that Argentine number does and does not mean. Argentina has run extreme inflation and repeated currency devaluation, so local-currency growth of 42% can be a real contraction if local prices rose faster. In dollars, Argentine revenue grew only 26%. The same underlying transactions can look like a boom in pesos and something much weaker in dollars.

Country concentration makes this unavoidable. Q3 2026 revenue by geography: Brazil $6.00B, Mexico $2.43B, Argentina $1.82B, about 95% of the $10.75B total from three countries. There is no diversification to smooth it. Any valuation work on MELI should be done on FX-neutral local-currency trends, with the dollar translation treated as a separate and separately volatile input.
Competition and What the Multiple Actually Implies
BULL VS BEAR — THE DECIDING FRAME
Amazon continues to invest in Brazil and Mexico, and the Asian low-price entrants, Shein and Temu, have gone directly at low-ticket, high-frequency categories. Those are the orders where MercadoLibre's shipping economics are worst, which is why the defence has been free-shipping thresholds and price investment, and why gross margin has fallen from 50.24% in FY2023 to 42.02% over the last four quarters. Mercado Envíos is a real barrier, but it must be continuously funded: in Q3 alone the company added almost 20% more fulfilment space. On Argentina, the CEO told the Q3 call that competition from Asian entrants had settled down after they gained share late last year and early this year.

What the multiple implies, numerically. At $1,510.00 the stock trades at 40.50x trailing EPS of $37.28, 27.74x consensus 2027 EPS, a PEG of 1.16, 1.99x sales, 9.34x book and 21.66x EV/EBITDA (the value of the whole company including debt, against operating earnings before depreciation). Consensus has 2026 revenue at $41.67B (+44.2%) with EPS of $37.37 (−5.2%), then 2027 revenue of $53.95B (+29.5%) with EPS of $54.44 (+45.7%). The whole valuation rests on one assumption: that the current margin compression is a deliberate, temporary investment that reverses in 2027. The bull case is that advertising and maturing card customers deliver exactly that. The bear case is that the lost margin is competitive erosion wearing an investment costume, and that a young $18B loan book with a 7.0% early-delinquency rate is a permanent new risk profile. The market's own answer is visible in the target range: $1,750 to $2,800, with even the lowest target 15.9% above the November 6 close, on a stock that has just lost 22% in ten weeks.
