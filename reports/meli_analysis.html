THE FRAME
Calling MercadoLibre "the Amazon of Latin America" tells you which half of the company to look at and then points you at the wrong one. In Q2 2026, commerce produced $5.8B of revenue and fintech produced $4.4B — but fintech is where a $16B+ credit book sits on the balance sheet, growing 75% year over year, with a 7.0% 15–90 day non-performing rate and $1,276M of provisions booked in a single quarter. That is a bank inside a retailer. A pure e-commerce multiple does not price it.
01 Company Overview

MercadoLibre operates the largest online commerce and payments ecosystem in Latin America, spanning marketplace retail (Mercado Libre), a logistics network (Mercado Envíos), a payments and financial services arm (Mercado Pago), a lending book (Mercado Crédito), an asset-management product, and a fast-growing advertising business. It is present across roughly 18 countries, with Brazil, Mexico and Argentina supplying the overwhelming majority of revenue.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $28.89B, up 39.1% year over year, and the trailing-twelve-month figure has since reached $35.18B, up 46.0%. Q2 2026 was the company's 30th consecutive quarter of revenue growth above 30% and its fastest growth in four years. It ranks as roughly the 246th most valuable listed company in the world by market capitalisation.

The catch is that growth is no longer free. Q2 2026 income from operations fell 17% year over year to $683M and the operating margin compressed roughly 550 basis points to 6.7%, while net income fell 11% to $466M. Management is deliberately spending margin to buy credit-card issuance, free shipping thresholds and advertising share. Whether that is investment or erosion is the central question in the stock.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
Owned Logistics Network Two-Sided Marketplace Liquidity Proprietary Underwriting Data Payments + Commerce Flywheel $23B AUM Funding Base Local Regulatory Fluency 89M Active Buyers

The moat is not the website. It is the combination of a physical network that took a decade and a great deal of capital to build, and a data asset that cannot be replicated by a new entrant at any price. Mercado Envíos gives MercadoLibre delivery economics and delivery speed that no cross-border competitor can match in Brazil, Mexico or Argentina without making the same investment. Mercado Pago then sits on top of that flow and observes something no local credit bureau has: the complete transaction history of 89 million buyers and the sales history of the merchants selling to them. In markets where formal credit-bureau coverage is thin and much of the population is thin-file or unbanked, that observation is the underwriting model. It lets MercadoLibre extend credit to borrowers a traditional bank cannot price, at nominal interest rates high enough to absorb double-digit loss rates and still produce a 20.7% net interest margin after losses.

The flywheel is genuinely self-reinforcing — credit funds purchases, purchases generate transaction data, data improves underwriting, better underwriting funds more purchases — but it is also the source of the fragility. Every turn of the wheel puts more balance-sheet risk on a business the market values as a retailer. Total debt stands at $13.21B against $5.75B of cash and a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.69, with a current ratio of just 1.12 and a quick ratio of 0.40 — balance-sheet ratios that look ordinary for a bank and tight for a retailer.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

Credit risk carried on balance sheet. The loan book grew 75% year over year to over $16 billion while 15–90 day non-performing loans sat at 7.0% of the portfolio and quarterly provisions ran at $1,276M. A book growing that fast mechanically flatters its own delinquency ratio, because young loans have not had time to go bad — the denominator is inflated by vintages that have not seasoned. If growth slows, the reported NPL rate will rise even if underwriting quality is unchanged. This is the classic dynamic that turns a fast-growing lender's credit metrics ugly at exactly the moment growth decelerates.

Deliberate margin compression with an uncertain payback. Operating margin fell roughly 550 basis points year over year to 6.7% in Q2 2026, gross margin has fallen from 57.95% (FY2023) to 47.75% trailing, and the credit card book is being written at a negative 2.5% NIMAL. Management frames this as customer acquisition. If card cohorts do not mature into profitable balances, the company will have spent several hundred basis points of margin to buy volume that never earns its cost of capital.

Macro and currency translation. Revenue is earned in Brazilian reais, Argentine pesos and Mexican pesos, and reported in US dollars. Q2 2026 revenue grew 50% in dollars but 43% FX-neutral — for once currency helped. It has frequently done the opposite. Argentina in particular has run extreme inflation and repeated devaluation, which means reported Argentine dollar growth and real Argentine local-currency growth can point in opposite directions in the same quarter.

Country concentration and new competition. Brazil ($5.53B), Mexico ($2.34B) and Argentina ($1.84B) supplied roughly 95% of Q2 2026 revenue. Amazon continues to invest in Brazil and Mexico, and Asian low-price entrants — Shein and Temu — have targeted exactly the low-ticket, high-frequency categories where MercadoLibre's shipping subsidies are most expensive to defend.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

No discrete write-down, litigation settlement, acquisition charge or one-time tax item was identified in the sources reviewed for this report. The earnings decline in Q2 2026 — operating income down 17% and net income down 11% on 50% revenue growth — appears to be structural and deliberate rather than exceptional: it reflects ongoing spending on credit card issuance, shipping and pricing. Investors should treat it as a run-rate margin reset to be re-underwritten, not as a non-recurring charge to be added back.

Two accounting features deserve flagging. First, the $1,276M quarterly provision for doubtful accounts is a genuine operating expense of the lending business, not a below-the-line item, and it scales with the loan book. Second, reported free cash flow of $12.41B is not comparable to a conventional retailer's FCF, because it absorbs growth in customer deposits and payment float; the company's own H1 2026 adjusted free cash flow figure was $158M. Reconciling those two numbers is essential before using any FCF-based valuation on this name. Financing activity has been active — the company issued $750 million of senior unsecured notes due 2033.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying two businesses for the price of one badly-labelled business. Commerce compounds at 36% FX-neutral GMV growth with 89 million buyers and a logistics moat no competitor can replicate quickly. Fintech is growing faster still — $101B TPV up 56%, 88M monthly active users, $23B of AUM up 68% — and the credit book, at $16B and 20.7% NIMAL, is a lending franchise built on underwriting data no bank in the region possesses. Advertising, at +73% growth and >10% regional share, is the margin repair kit. Consensus expects EPS to rebound 45% to $56.83 in 2027; against that number the current $1,936.20 is 34x forward-forward earnings for a business still growing revenue at 46%.

Bear Case

The stock has returned about 2.9% a year for five years while revenue quintupled, because the multiple was too high and is still 52.69x trailing earnings on falling earnings. Operating margin fell 550bp year over year; gross margin has dropped ten points since FY2023; EPS declined for the first time since 2021. Meanwhile a credit book growing 75% a year carries a 7.0% early-delinquency rate, $1,276M of quarterly provisions, and a card sub-book earning a negative 2.5% margin after losses. Fast loan growth mechanically suppresses the reported NPL ratio; when growth slows, the ratio rises. A 46-52x multiple on a business with bank-like balance-sheet risk, Argentine peso exposure, and Amazon, Shein and Temu all attacking the same categories does not price the downside.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Credit deterioration in an unseasoned loan book. The portfolio grew 75% to over $16 billion with 15–90 day NPLs at 7.0% and quarterly provisions of $1,276M. Rapid growth flatters delinquency ratios because new loans have not aged. A slowdown in origination — voluntary or forced — would raise the reported NPL rate even with unchanged underwriting, and the market values this company as a retailer, not as a lender with a rising loss curve.
02
Margin investment that does not pay back. Operating margin of 6.7% is roughly 550bp below the prior year and the credit card book is running at a negative 2.5% NIMAL by design. Consensus assumes EPS recovers 45% in 2027. If card cohorts and advertising do not deliver that recovery, both the earnings base and the multiple applied to it are at risk simultaneously.
03
Currency and country concentration. Brazil, Mexico and Argentina supply roughly 95% of revenue, earned in local currency and reported in dollars. Q2 2026 saw dollar growth (50%) exceed FX-neutral growth (43%) — a tailwind that reverses. Argentine devaluation and inflation can make reported dollar results and real local performance diverge sharply, and the loan book is denominated in the same volatile currencies as the collateral behind it.
  Industry Commentary
The Credit Book Is the Story Nobody Underwrites
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
If you read only one section of this report, read this one. MercadoLibre is running a $16+ billion consumer and merchant lending operation inside a company the market prices on e-commerce multiples. The book grew 75% year over year in dollars. Within it, the credit card portfolio reached $7.7 billion, up 91%, and now represents roughly 47% of total credit, up from 43% a year earlier — the fastest-growing and least-seasoned component is also becoming the largest. 2.6 million cards were issued in Q2 2026 alone.

The credit metrics: 15–90 day non-performing loans (balances where the borrower has missed payments but is not yet 90 days late — the early-warning bucket) stood at 7.0% for the total portfolio and 4.6% for credit cards. Provisions for doubtful accounts were $1,276 million in the quarter — for scale, that is roughly 12.5% of total quarterly revenue and nearly twice quarterly operating income of $683M. NIMAL — net interest margin after losses, the yield on the book after funding cost and credit losses — was 20.7%, down about 2 percentage points year over year. A 20.7% net margin after losses tells you the nominal interest rates charged are very high, which is what lending profitably into markets with thin credit-bureau coverage and high local inflation requires.

The part that should make a careful reader uncomfortable is the credit card NIMAL of negative 2.5%. The company is currently issuing cards at a loss after credit costs, on the thesis that cardholders become high-lifetime-value ecosystem customers. That may prove right. But it is an acquisition subsidy funded by the balance sheet, in the fastest-growing and most rapidly-expanding share of the portfolio, and it is the direct cause of the 550 basis points of operating margin the company gave up this quarter. Two structural cautions apply. First, a book compounding at 75% is definitionally unseasoned: the denominator of every delinquency ratio is swollen with loans too young to have defaulted, so the true through-cycle loss rate is higher than 7.0%. Second, MercadoLibre carries this risk directly — it is not originate-to-distribute. Every dollar of credit loss lands on the income statement of a company trading at 52.69x trailing earnings. A bank with these growth rates and these provisions would trade at a fraction of that multiple, and would be required to hold regulatory capital against it.
FX Translation: When 50% Growth and 43% Growth Are the Same Quarter
ONGOING HEADWIND
MercadoLibre earns Brazilian reais, Argentine pesos and Mexican pesos and reports US dollars. The gap between those two facts is not a rounding error — it is often the difference between a beat and a miss. In Q2 2026, total revenue grew 50% in US dollars but 43% FX-neutral (local-currency growth with exchange-rate movement stripped out). Currency added seven points that quarter. It has subtracted more than that in others, and investors who anchor on headline dollar growth without checking the FX-neutral line will systematically misread the trend.

The divergence is starkest in GMV: ~$22 billion, +44% in dollars but +36% FX-neutral — an eight-point gap on the single most important commerce metric. By country, FX-neutral GMV growth was Brazil +39%, Argentina +38%, Mexico +26%; FX-neutral revenue growth was Argentina +48%, Brazil +42%, Mexico +38%. Note what that Argentine number does and does not mean. Argentina has run extreme inflation and repeated currency devaluation. In a hyperinflationary economy, local-currency revenue growth of 48% may represent a real contraction if local prices rose faster, while simultaneously translating into a smaller dollar figure if the peso devalued more than prices rose. The same set of underlying transactions can be reported as a boom in pesos and a decline in dollars, or the reverse, depending purely on the timing of a devaluation relative to the quarter end.

Country concentration makes this unavoidable rather than incidental. Q2 2026 revenue by geography: Brazil $5.53B, Mexico $2.34B, Argentina $1.84B — roughly 95% of the $10.17B total from three countries. There is no diversification to smooth it. Any valuation work on MELI should be done on FX-neutral local-currency trends, with the dollar translation treated as a separate, and separately volatile, input.
Competition and What the Multiple Actually Implies
BULL VS BEAR — THE DECIDING FRAME
Amazon continues to invest in Brazil and Mexico, and the Asian low-price entrants — Shein and Temu — have gone directly at low-ticket, high-frequency categories. Those are precisely the orders where MercadoLibre's shipping economics are worst, which is why the defence has been free-shipping thresholds and price investment, and why gross margin has fallen from 57.95% in FY2023 to 47.75% trailing. Mercado Envíos is a real barrier — a competitor cannot replicate the fulfilment and last-mile footprint on a short timeline — but it is a barrier that must be continuously funded, not one that was built once and now defends itself for free.

What the multiple implies, numerically. At $1,936.20 the stock trades at 52.69x trailing EPS of $36.75, 45.97x forward earnings, a PEG of 1.63, 2.79x sales, 12.53x book and 27.14x EV/EBITDA. Consensus has 2026 revenue at $41.69B (+44.3%) with EPS roughly flat at $39.07, then 2027 revenue of $53.33B (+27.9%) with EPS of $56.83 (+45.5%). Take those numbers at face value and the stock is on 34.1x 2027 earnings. That is not obviously expensive for 28% revenue growth — provided the 45% EPS rebound arrives. The entire multiple therefore rests on a single assumption: that the current margin compression is a deliberate, temporary investment that reverses in 2027. The bull case is that advertising (+73%) and maturing card cohorts deliver exactly that. The bear case is that the 550bp of margin is competitive erosion wearing an investment costume, and that a $16B unseasoned loan book with a 7.0% early-delinquency rate and a negative-margin card business is not a temporary investment at all but a permanent new risk profile. The market's own answer is visible in the target range: $1,750 to $2,800, a 60% spread, with one house below the current price and another 45% above it.
