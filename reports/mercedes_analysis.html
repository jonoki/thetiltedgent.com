01 Company Overview

Mercedes-Benz Group is the company behind the three-pointed star: luxury and premium cars (Mercedes-Benz, Mercedes-AMG, Mercedes-Maybach, the G-Class), premium vans (Sprinter, Vito, the new VLE) and a captive lender, Mercedes-Benz Financial Services, that finances and leases those vehicles. It was called Daimler AG until February 1, 2022; two months earlier, on December 10, 2021, its truck and bus business started trading separately as Daimler Truck. In 2025 it sold 1,801,291 cars and 359,136 vans and reported revenue of €132.2B, down 9.2%, with EBIT of €5.8B (down 57%) and EPS of €5.34, against €10.19 in 2024.

The share price tells a harsher story than the income statement. At the October 2, 2026 close of €39.725, the stock was down 33.9% in 2026 from its €60.07 year-end close, sat at its 52-week low and at the lowest point of the five-year chart below. On 962.9 million issued shares that is a market value of €38.25B (our calculation) — while the industrial business alone held net liquidity of €30.4B at June 30, 2026. Put plainly, the market is paying about €7.8B for everything beyond that cash pile: the car and van franchises, a lender with €131.6B of contracts, and stakes in Daimler Truck and Beijing Benz.

The latest print, Q2 2026 on July 28, beat the Street: adjusted EBIT of €2.30B against a consensus near €1.65B (Investing.com, July 28), with the car division's adjusted return on sales at 4.0%, inside its 3–5% guide. The stock rose 2.9% that day and then slid into the autumn as China kept shrinking — Mercedes car sales there fell 30% in Q2 — and as German peers cut their own forecasts. The debate: is this a luxury brand in a cyclical trough, priced at 7.5x trailing earnings with an 8.8% trailing dividend yield, or a company whose most profitable market is being taken by Chinese brands for good?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

140-year luxury brand Top-End pricing power (S-Class, Maybach, G, AMG) Captive finance arm (€131.6B book) €30.4B industrial net cash Double-digit van margins Global plant network

The moat is the badge and what it lets Mercedes charge at the top of the range. The company steers to a 14–15% share of Top-End models (S-Class, Maybach, G-Class, AMG) in its sales mix — 14.3% in H1 2026 — and in Q2 the new S-Class started in Europe while new V8 AMG models and the Maybach GLS debuted. The finance arm deepens it: a lease ends with the customer back at a Mercedes dealer, the natural moment to sell the next car. The balance sheet is the other wall — €30.4B of industrial net cash lets the company fund the biggest launch programme in its history and still pay out. What the moat does not protect against is a market where the badge stops commanding a premium. That is what is happening in China.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China is shrinking, and maybe for good. Q2 car sales there fell 30%, and the group took €752M of impairments on Chinese equity-method investments in Q2, €704M of them in Cars (H1 2026 report). Its H1 outlook expects the Chinese market to be significantly below last year and says intense price competition, especially from local manufacturers, should weigh on unit sales for many foreign brands. Deutsche Bank's Tim Rokossa wrote on September 29 that China remains the biggest challenge, and a structural rather than merely cyclical one (dpa-AFX).
02
The German cost base. In June 2026 the board launched a "productivity offensive" for Germany: faster processes, leaner structures, more on-site presence and more working hours without extra pay, with a transformation payment due in July 2026 pushed to 2027. Further effects depend on talks with employee representatives — a negotiation that has to happen while the product launch programme is running at full tilt.
03
The car margin is thin for a luxury maker. Cars' 4.0% adjusted return on sales in Q2 leaves little room. Market pressure in China, a weaker mix and launch costs pushed it down from 5.1% a year earlier, and the full-year guide of 3–5% leaves room to slip further. UBS's Patrick Hummel wrote on September 21 that Mercedes' annual targets hinge heavily on a good fourth quarter (dpa-AFX).
04
Two Chinese strategic holders. BAIC and Li Shufu together hold close to 20% of the shares. Both have agreed to stay below 10% each, but a company whose largest car market is under pressure from Chinese competitors also counts Chinese automotive investors as its two largest holders in the 2025 shareholder table.

Notable One-Off Items

Q2 2026 reported and adjusted numbers diverge sharply. Cars' reported EBIT of €49M includes €704M of impairments on Chinese equity-method investments (€752M group-wide), with no matching cash outflow in the quarter. Group EBIT includes a €131M gain linked to the planned sale of Athlon and a €95M gain from revaluing buyback liabilities. On cash, H1 free cash flow carries about €1.1B of severance payments and €417M of proceeds from selling part of the Daimler Truck stake. Mercedes says M&A transactions added about €0.4B to H1 EBIT and €0.5B to industrial free cash flow, and its 2026 EBIT and cash-flow guidance now include M&A effects (originally they excluded them). On that basis the guidance stays unchanged: EBIT significantly above 2025's €5.8B, and free cash flow slightly below 2025's €5.4B.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end of €71.31 (spin-adjusted) to the October 2, 2026 close of €39.725, the share price fell 44.3%. Dividends change the picture a lot: Mercedes paid €5.00, €5.20, €5.30, €4.30 and €3.50 a share from 2022 to 2026 — €23.30 in all, 58.7% of today's price — and with them reinvested the five-year total return is −18.5%. Over the same window the DAX, which counts dividends, returned +60.8%. Mercedes has been a cash machine with a shrinking share price.

The shape has three acts. 2022 to early 2024 was a range between the low €50s and the low €70s, peaking at €73.81 in March 2024. From there the price stepped down as China weakened and margins fell, with a brief rally to €60.07 at the end of 2025. 2026 took it to new lows: three of the five worst months in the series are in 2026 (March −11.2%, June −15.8%, September −14.0%). The 6-period monthly RSI is 24.8, in oversold territory, and the 10-month moving average of about €48.91 is far above the price. The trend is still down; the RSI only says it has been unusually one-sided.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At €39.725 you pay 7.5x trailing earnings and 0.42x book for a company with €30.4B of industrial net cash, a 10.9% free-cash-flow yield and a €1B buyback running. The car business is at a trough margin of 4.0% while the biggest model launch in its history is still ramping: BEV sales are up 51%, European BEV orders more than doubled in Q2, and the electric GLC and C-Class only reach customers in the second half. Vans earns 10% margins and Financial Services just raised its return guide. Q2 showed the cost work is real: Q2 general administrative expenses fell 14% and R&D 12%, and Q2 adjusted EBIT beat consensus by roughly €650M. If China simply stops shrinking, earnings have room to recover from today's depressed base, and the average target of €55.92 sits 41% above the price.

Bear Case

The cheap multiple prices earnings that are still falling: EPS dropped from €10.19 in 2024 to €5.34 in 2025, and Cars' margin is 4.0% against 6.2% a year ago. China, 30.6% of 2025 car units, fell another 30% in Q2, and Deutsche Bank calls the problem structural. Mercedes' own outlook expects local brands' price war to keep hurting foreign makers. The 8.8% yield rests on a FY2025 dividend that took 66% of earnings, above the 40% policy; on 2026 profit, a smaller payout is possible. The German productivity offensive still has to be negotiated with labour, and both VW and BMW have cut forecasts this year. A stock at its five-year low with targets 41% above it can also mean the targets are late, not that the price is wrong.

China volume and pricing. A 30% Q2 sales fall, €752M of Q2 impairments on Chinese equity investments and a company outlook calling for significantly weaker unit sales for many foreign makers. Industry Commentary
China's luxury car market has turned against foreign brands
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For a decade China was the profit engine of the German luxury makers. Mercedes' H1 2026 outlook now expects the Chinese car market to be significantly below last year, and the Chinese premium and luxury segment to stay in intense price competition led by local manufacturers. Mercedes' Q2 sales there fell 30%, and it wrote down Chinese equity investments by €752M in the quarter. Bernstein's Stephen Reitman sees premium brands like Mercedes as better protected than mass-market brands as Chinese makers move into Europe, but protection in Europe does not replace lost profit in China.

A sector-wide profit reset in Germany
ONGOING HEADWIND

In 2026 the German car industry cut forecasts in turn. Jefferies tied Mercedes' June slide to BMW's profit warning; on September 18 Volkswagen cut its 2026 operating margin outlook to at most 1% from 4–5.5%, and Mercedes fell 4.8% that day. Mercedes' own fixed costs are already about 25% below 2019, and it is now asking German staff for more hours without extra pay. The common thread is high fixed costs meeting lower volumes in China and a slow, expensive shift to electric cars.

Electric demand is finally showing up in Europe
EMERGING TAILWIND

Mercedes' European BEV sales rose 87% in Q2 and European BEV order intake more than doubled. The new CLA and GLB are already contributing, and the electric GLC and C-Class come next. The electrified share guide was raised to 23–25% for 2026. If the new electric models sell at margins close to the combustion cars they replace, the launch programme ends the period when electric cars dragged down group profit. If they need discounts to move, it extends it.
