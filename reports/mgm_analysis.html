01 Company Overview

On June 1, 2026 a shareholder that already owned about a quarter of MGM Resorts put a number on it: $48.30 a share, in cash. People Inc. — Barry Diller's company, formerly IAC — proposed to buy the rest. On September 23 it walked away. Diller said in People's release, as quoted by CNBC, "We didn't feel the mix was coming together in the way we had hoped", and that People remains "open to and interested in the possibility of a strategic transaction with MGM Resorts". The next day the stock fell 11.0%. On October 30 it closed at $29.96: 38.0% below the withdrawn offer and 31.4% below the $43.67 it closed at on May 29, the last trading day before the proposal. Without a buyer, the market is pricing the business alone, and the business had a weak quarter.

The business itself is a hotel-and-casino operator that has sold much of the real estate it runs. MGM runs four reporting segments — Las Vegas Strip Resorts, Regional Operations, MGM China and MGM Digital — plus a 50% stake in the BetMGM joint venture. The company began as Grand Name Co. in 1986, became MGM Grand, Inc. in 1987, MGM Mirage after buying Mirage Resorts in 2000, and MGM Resorts International in 2010. Between 2020 and 2022 it sold much of its real estate to landlords including VICI Properties and Blackstone (per Wikipedia) in sale-leasebacks. That is why the balance sheet carries $23.7B of long-term operating lease liabilities against only $5.67B of long-term debt (September 30, 2026, Q3 10-Q), and why the cash raised has gone largely into buybacks: $9.4B of repurchases over FY2021–FY2025 (our sum of the annual figures in MGM's 10-Ks), more than the company's current market value of $7.54B. In the Q3 release the CFO put it as "the repurchase of nearly half of our outstanding share count over the past five years". FY2025 revenue was $17.54B (+1.72%), GAAP EPS $0.76.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Licensed, scarce gaming real estate Strip room & convention scale Macau concession via MGM China Loyalty data across properties Brand portfolio (Bellagio, Aria, MGM Grand) Osaka project (opening targeted 2030)

The moat is regulatory and physical: gaming licences are scarce, and a Strip resort cannot be copied by software — the point Diller made himself. But MGM separated the moat from the ownership. Much of the real estate now belongs to landlords, and the operator keeps the licences, the brands and the customer relationships while paying fixed rent. A resort business already has high fixed costs, so profit swings more than revenue; fixed rent makes the swing bigger. In a good year the rent is small next to property profit; in a soft year it is not. Q3 2026 showed it: revenue fell 0.9%, Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA fell 20%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Fixed rent against variable demand. Q3 2026 segment EBITDAR was roughly $1.06B; Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA was $403M, down 20% YoY, after $549M of rent. With Strip RevPAR down 8%, a 10% fall in total segment EBITDAR (our sum of the release's segment figures) became a 20% fall below the rent line. StockAnalysis puts interest coverage at 2.51x and debt (including leases) at 6.83 times EBITDA.
02
A large holder that has already walked once. People Inc. owns about 26.5%, its chairman sits on MGM's board, and it says it remains open to a deal. The amended voting agreement caps its buying at 29.9%, but MGM's own buybacks can lift its share above that, and on any change of control it votes with the board's recommendation, subject to conditions. A holder that knows the business well can wait; other shareholders have no way to make it bid again.
03
Macau profit is falling while revenue sets records. Revenue rose 5% and market share hit a record 17.1% in Q3, yet segment EBITDAR fell 7% (after a 15% fall in Q2). About 44% of that segment's economics belongs to MGM China's other shareholders.
04
Operational and cyber risk. The September 2023 cyberattack, attributed to the Scattered Spider group, cost about $100M (per Wikipedia); the stock's month-end close fell 16.4% that month. An integrated resort runs its hotels, payments and gaming floors on the same systems.

Read the TTM P/E with that in mind.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Without a bid, the case rests on the business and the buyback. On our calculation MGM trades at an 18.6% trailing free-cash-flow yield after rent, 5.4 times free cash flow. The board now has the amended voting agreement its chairman and CFO say clears the way for share repurchases, with $1.4B still authorised; at $29.96 a share, every dollar spent retires more stock than it would have at the $48.30 offer. MGM China set records for revenue and market share, MGM Digital is close to break-even, and Hornbuckle said October rebounded after the weak summer room rates. The average analyst target is 30% above the price, Osaka adds a long-dated growth leg, and the best-informed holder in the company says it remains open to a deal.

Bear Case

The shareholder that knows MGM best studied it for nearly four months and walked away; CNBC reported the debt a deal would have created was part of the reason. Property profit fell in every segment in Q3 — Strip EBITDAR −12% on 8% lower room rates, Regional same-store −7%, MGM China −7% despite record revenue — and after $549M of rent, Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA fell 20% on a 1% revenue dip, with adjusted EPS a loss. $23.7B of lease liabilities fix the rent bill whatever the visitor count. Short interest rose 20% to 22.9M shares by the October 15 settlement, every listed post-print target change with a prior figure was a cut, and Goldman Sachs' $26 Sell target sits below the price. The trailing P/E of 11.5 leans on a one-off Q2 property gain.

Top 3 Risks

01
Las Vegas room rates keep falling. Strip occupancy held at 89% in Q3, but the average daily rate fell 8% to $218, after a 4% RevPAR fall in Q2. Management says October rebounded; one month is not a trend, and the Q4 release will show whether it held.
02
Fixed rent turns small revenue drops into large profit drops. Lease liabilities of $23.7B and lease-inclusive debt of 6.83 times EBITDA (StockAnalysis) mean a small drop in Las Vegas or Macau demand falls straight through to earnings and free cash flow: Q3 revenue fell 0.9% and Consolidated Adjusted EBITDA 20%.
03
Macau margin erosion. MGM China grew revenue 5% while segment EBITDAR fell 7% in Q3 2026, after a 15% fall in Q2. If lower profit is the price of record market share, it is a recurring cost, not a one-off.
Industry Commentary
The asset-light casino operator
STRUCTURAL

MGM's sale-leasebacks converted owned real estate into cash and a long-term rent obligation. The equity became smaller and more indebted: shareholders' equity is $3.37B including $0.86B of minority interest — $9.95 per share of MGM's own equity — against $29.73B of lease-inclusive debt (10-Q and StockAnalysis, September 30, 2026). The cash went mostly to buybacks — $9.4B over five fiscal years. Whether that trade was good for shareholders depends on the price paid for the shares, and the five-year chart gives an unflattering answer: the repurchases were made while month-end closes ranged between $28.95 and $50.77, and the stock now sits near the bottom of that band.

Why the bid came and went
DEAL DYNAMICS

People Inc.'s June letter framed the offer against 30- and 90-day average prices that had spent most of 2025 and early 2026 in the $30s. A large holder with a board seat bids when it thinks the market price is below the company's value; it walks when the numbers stop working for it. On August 3, 2026 Yakira Capital Management, a People Inc. shareholder, published a letter urging People's board to "withdraw the proposal" and buy back its own shares instead (Yakira release via PR Newswire); on September 23 People did withdraw. What is left is the amended voting agreement: People cannot buy above 29.9%, but when MGM buys back and cancels shares, every remaining holder — People included — owns a larger slice without paying anything. Buybacks at today's price raise People's stake at no cost to People.
