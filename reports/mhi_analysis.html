01 Company Overview

Mitsubishi Heavy Industries builds the machines that sit underneath a country: power-station gas turbines, nuclear plant equipment, warships, missiles, defense aircraft, the H3 rocket, metals machinery, ships, industrial engines and air-conditioners. It dates its founding to July 7, 1884. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (MHI calls it FY2025) it booked ¥7.65 trillion of new orders, up 19.5%, recorded ¥4.97 trillion of revenue, up 14.1%, and earned business profit of ¥432.2 billion and net income of ¥332.1 billion (¥98.86 a share). MHI says orders, business profit, net income and free cash flow were all records.

The order book is the whole story. At June 30, 2026 MHI was sitting on ¥14.10 trillion of signed work — about 2.7 years of revenue — after a first quarter in which orders rose 25.7% to ¥2.02 trillion and outran revenue 1.7 to 1. Two engines drive it. The first is GTCC — large gas-turbine power plants, wanted by US utilities and, in MHI's own words, driven by "high electricity demand - especially from data centers". The second is Japan's defense build-up, which has given MHI roughly ¥1.5 trillion of defense orders a year for three years. The stock rose thirteen-fold on the back of both, from a split-adjusted ¥290.9 at the October 2021 month-end to ¥3,813 — then stalled. It closed October 2, 2026 24% below its February 2026 month-end peak of ¥5,014 and only 3.6% higher than a year earlier, while the Nikkei 225 rose 52.0% over the same twelve months. In the quarter MHI reported on August 4, business profit rose 65% and net income nearly doubled; the multiple is what came down. The question is whether a 32x trailing P/E on a long-cycle machinery maker is still a fair price for a cycle that management says is not softening.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

¥14.1T signed backlog Large-frame gas turbine franchise Long-term service agreements Japan's defense industrial base H3 launch vehicle Nuclear equipment know-how Net cash ¥1.16T

MHI's moat is capacity and know-how that take years to build. In large gas turbines, management says demand is "broadly in line with, or slightly above" the strong level it planned for, and it is adding production capacity rather than booking more orders than it can deliver. The American turbine orders come with long-term service agreements, which tie MHI to each plant for years of maintenance after delivery. Defense works the same way with one main customer: MHI is the biggest by market value of the three heavy-industry companies the Japanese financial press groups together as the core defense names (with Kawasaki Heavy and IHI), and its Aircraft, Defense & Space backlog stood at ¥3.9 trillion in June. The weakness is the other side of the same coin: long contracts carry long execution risk, and FY2025 already carried losses on some thermal power projects.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Project execution. Fixed-price, multi-year plants can go wrong years after they are signed. MHI's own summary of FY2025 says business profit grew in spite of losses on some thermal power projects and a goodwill impairment in Industrial Power Solutions. The Q1 FY2026 one-off gain worked the other way — ¥15B from finally agreeing a contract price — and the CFO warned that gains of that size will not recur often.
02
One product cycle carries the group. Energy Systems made 61% of segment profit in Q1 FY2026, and GTCC is most of that. Analysts on the August call were already asking about softening demand for small and mid-sized turbines. The CFO's answer was that the large-frame market MHI focuses on, with core US utility customers, has seen "no change" in supply and demand or in improving order margins.
03
The yen flatters the numbers. FY2026 guidance assumes ¥150 to the dollar; the yen closed October 2 at 157.93. Currency added ¥9.0B to Q1 business profit, and MHI credits foreign-exchange gains from the weaker yen for part of the jump in Q1 net income. A stronger yen would take both back.
04
Guidance leaves out the Middle East. MHI's FY2026 forecast "excludes impact from the situation in the Middle East"; the company says the current effect is limited and it is monitoring. Steam-power orders were strong in the Middle East in Q1, and Saudi boiler contracts are part of the book.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the latest figures. First, Q1 FY2026 one-offs of about ¥25 billion, almost all in Energy Systems: roughly ¥15B of profit recognised when the price of a large, long-term construction contract was finalised, and about ¥10B of gains from selling stakes in power-generation projects, including a South American plant MHI had invested in to win the turbine order. Second, currency: finance income rose to ¥20.5B from ¥3.8B, and MHI credits foreign-exchange gains from the continued weakening of the yen for part of the rise in net income. Third, the Logisnext exit, completed May 1, 2026: discontinued operations contributed ¥6.6B of Q1 profit after a ¥32.9B loss on transferring the shares, a ¥24.5B gain from realising currency translation differences and an ¥11.9B tax benefit. MHI's FY2026 guidance is for ¥380B of net income — only ¥245.3B over the remaining nine months, against ¥263.9B in the same nine months of FY2025 — which is why the CFO said there "may be some upside" to it.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of ¥290.9 (¥2,909 as traded, before the 10-for-1 split that took effect April 1, 2024) to the October 2, 2026 close of ¥3,813, MHI's price rose 1,210.8% — thirteen times the money, before dividends. The Nikkei 225 rose 136.4% over the same window. Most of the climb came between mid-2023 and February 2026, the years in which annual orders rose to ¥6.41 trillion in FY2024 and ¥7.65 trillion in FY2025.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how extreme that run was. It sat above 90 for most of 2024 and peaked at 96.3 in January 2025, and in the whole five-year window it never fell into oversold territory — its lowest reading was 43.5 in June 2026. It now reads 46.9, neutral. The price is 7.3% below its 10-month moving average of about ¥4,115, which has rolled over: after three years of trend, the stock has spent eight months going sideways to down.

Relative performance

Against its home benchmark MHI has been two different stocks. Over five years it beat the Nikkei 225 by a mile: +1,210.8% against +136.4% (October 2021 month-end to October 2, 2026, both price only). Over the last year it has lagged badly: +3.6% against +52.0%, and in calendar 2026 −0.7% against +35.7% for the index (December 2025 close 50,339.48). The business did not slow in that time — in Q1 FY2026 orders rose 25.7%, business profit 65% and net income 97%. What changed is what investors would pay: a stock already at a large premium to the index stood still while its earnings grew. Among its peers MHI's ¥12.81T market value is about 4.5 times IHI's and 6 times Kawasaki Heavy's, and roughly a third of GE Vernova's in dollars.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

MHI has ¥14.1 trillion of work signed, 80 large gas turbines under contract and a customer base — US utilities building for data-center load, and a Japanese government that has been ordering about ¥1.5 trillion of defense equipment a year — that is still asking for more. The CFO says turbine pricing "has not declined" and new orders keep getting more profitable, so margins should keep rising as those orders reach revenue. Q1 delivered 35% of the full-year profit plan; guidance looks conservative, and the Street already sits 16% above it. Customers are paying up front, leaving ¥1.16 trillion of net cash, and the next medium-term plan is where management has promised to say what it will do with it. Eight months of sideways trading have taken the trailing P/E down while profit grew; if the cycle runs to 2030 as the order book implies, the stock is growing into its multiple.

Bear Case

At 32x trailing earnings and four times book, MHI is priced as a growth company, but it is a long-cycle machinery maker whose profits come in waves. The turbine boom is the whole earnings story — Energy Systems made 61% of segment profit last quarter — and capital-goods cycles end. Q1 was flattered by ¥25 billion of one-offs and a weak yen that guidance itself does not assume. Management's own guide calls for orders to fall 8.5% this year. Much of the cash pile is customers' money, owed back as work, and the history of fixed-price project losses has not gone away. A stock that has fallen 24% from its February peak while the Nikkei is up 36% this year is telling you the buyers have moved on; a cheaper multiple can get cheaper.

Top 3 Risks

01
The gas-turbine cycle turns. GTCC is the main source of orders, margin growth and advance payments. If US power demand or utility capital plans cool, orders, cash flow and the expected margin expansion all weaken together — and the stock trades at a multiple that assumes they won't.
02
Execution on a record backlog. MHI is doubling turbine capacity and adding defense capacity while delivering a ¥14.1 trillion book. Late deliveries or cost overruns on fixed-price contracts have hurt it before; FY2025 already carried losses on some thermal projects.
03
Valuation and currency. A 32x P/E against the Nikkei's 17x leaves little room for a miss, and the yen at ¥157.9 to the dollar is flattering results against a ¥150 plan. A stronger yen and a lower multiple at the same time is the bad combination.
Industry Commentary
The gas-turbine super-cycle
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

MHI's FY2025 results deck puts the demand down to three things: the "transition away from coal- and oil-fired thermal power, need for peaking power to offset renewable energy intermittence, and high electricity demand - especially from data centers". The result is a seller's market for large gas turbines. MHI booked 35 large-frame units in FY2025 and ten more in Q1 FY2026; turbines ordered now are generally scheduled for 2028–2030, and projects still under discussion for around 2030–2031. The CFO's answers, in MHI's published summary of the Q1 briefing (dated August 6), are the bull case in management's own words: "Order pricing has not declined, and the profitability of newly booked projects continues to improve gradually," and for core US utility customers there has been no change in the strong supply-demand environment. MHI is not chasing every order — it says it is selective and is building the book in step with a capacity expansion it calls on track. The risk is the one every capital-goods cycle carries: utilities order in waves, and the waves end.

Japan's defense build-up
POLICY-DRIVEN

MHI's defense orders have run at about ¥1.5 trillion a year for three years, according to the CFO, and it now forecasts ¥1.5 trillion again for FY2026 after raising the guide by ¥100 billion — with no single new contract behind the raise. Revenue lags: Defense & Space revenue was ¥1,144.5B in FY2025 and is guided to ¥1,250B, so the backlog keeps building. Exports have opened up too, with Australia's three-frigate order. Policy is the swing factor. In mid-September Japanese press reports, relayed by Kabutan, said the government was considering a defense-spending target of 3.5% of GDP and that MUFG Bank would set lending criteria for defense firms; defense stocks rallied on both. As Minkabu Press put it, a bigger budget helps most the companies with the largest sales to the defense ministry — which is why MHI, Kawasaki Heavy and IHI tend to move together on these headlines.

The re-rating, both ways
VALUATION DEBATE

MHI went from a sleepy conglomerate to a thirteen-bagger because the market started valuing it on its order book rather than its history. That premium is now being tested. The bull framing: the business is unchanged and the multiple re-rated — profit grew, the share price did not; the February 2026 month-end price of ¥5,014 was 50.7 times what FY2025 went on to earn (¥98.86 a share), and today's ¥3,813 is 32.1 times trailing earnings, with consensus estimates above the company's guide and every dated analyst action since August holding or raising targets. The bear framing: a 32x multiple on cyclical heavy industry is still nearly double the Nikkei's 17x, the company's own guide shows orders falling this year, and the stock has lagged its index by almost 50 points over twelve months. The adjudicator is the November 6 print and, after that, the 2027 plan: guidance raised, a credible capacity path and a clear use for the cash would argue the premium is earned; a flat guide would argue it is not.
