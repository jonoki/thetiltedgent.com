01 Company Overview

Mitsubishi Corporation is the largest of Japan's five big general trading houses (sogo shosha) by market value: ¥18.16T ($115.3B) at the November 6, 2026 close, ahead of ITOCHU (¥15.41T), Mitsui & Co. (¥13.06T), Marubeni (¥7.66T) and Sumitomo Corporation (¥7.54T). It owns pieces of Australian steelmaking-coal mines, copper mines in Chile and Peru, LNG Canada, and, since July 14, 2026, a US shale-gas producer bought for $5.2B; alongside them sit salmon farming, food wholesaling, car distribution and financing, and a 50% stake in the Lawson convenience-store chain. In the year to March 2026 (Mitsubishi's FY2025) it earned ¥800.5B of net income attributable to shareholders, down 15.8%, but 14.4% above the ¥700B it had guided to. On November 4 it raised its FY2026 guide to ¥1.2T from ¥1.1T, which it expects to be a record and which would be 49.9% above FY2025, and announced a new buyback of up to ¥1 trillion. The dividend forecast stays at ¥125, up from ¥110.

The half to September 30, 2026 (reported November 4) came in at ¥619.7B, up 74% from ¥355.8B, on higher steelmaking-coal and copper prices, more LNG trading, higher dividend income and gains on asset sales. That is 52% of the new full-year guide in six months, which leaves ¥580.3B for the second half, 6.4% less than the first. Underlying operating cash flow was ¥728.1B (+63%), and its full-year guide rose to ¥1.38T from ¥1.25T. The stock has done more than the earnings: from a September 2021 close of ¥1,178.67 (split-adjusted) to ¥4,958 it is up 321% in five years, against 95% for the Nikkei 225. Two things ran alongside the rise: Berkshire Hathaway, which crossed 10% of the voting rights on August 28, 2025 and held 10.8% at the end of 2025, and a ¥1 trillion buyback that retired 318.4M shares, 7.9% of the company, in a single year; a second ¥1 trillion programme now runs to July 31, 2027. The price now asks 17.3x trailing earnings, against 13.6x for the other four trading houses. The question this report keeps returning to: is ¥1.2T the new floor, built on shale gas, LNG and a 12% return-on-equity target, or a peak flattered by coal and copper prices and one-off gains that management itself has said are in the number?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Stakes in long-life mines & LNG Seven groups, one balance sheet Berkshire as 10%+ anchor holder Decades-long partner relationships Selling weak businesses to fund new ones Net debt/equity cap of 0.6x Progressive dividend policy

The moat is access. A trading house sits in deals that a pure miner, gas producer or food company cannot easily assemble: a minority stake in a mine, the contract to buy its output, the shipping, the customer in Japan and the financing, all through one counterparty that has been around since the 1950s. LNG Canada and the Aethon purchase are the current proof: Mitsubishi can buy US gas at the wellhead and think, in its own words from the January notice, about building a value chain that runs into its US power, data-center and chemicals businesses. The second layer is capital allocation that the market now trusts: a ¥1T buyback executed in full, a dividend that has risen every year from FY2021's ¥50 to FY2025's ¥110 (¥60, ¥70 and ¥100 in between, split-adjusted), with ¥125 planned, and Berkshire Hathaway holding more than a tenth of the shares. The weakness is that none of this changes what coal and copper sell for.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Commodity prices drive the swing. The energy group made 31.6% of group profit in the half to September and Mineral Resources another 22.6%. The CFO named coal and copper prices as the main reason Q1 rose. FY2025's 15.8% fall shows the other source of swing: the filing attributes it mostly to the absence of the previous year's one-off gains (a remeasurement gain when Lawson became an equity-method affiliate, and an Australian coal asset sale). Earnings that swing on both prices and asset sales make a high price-to-earnings ratio hard to justify.
02
The FY2026 guide leans on one-off gains. Nakanishi said on May 1 that the ¥1.1T forecast includes "sizable asset and business recycling-related gains", and Shimazu that it includes "gains from large-scale sales and evaluations across several projects". Q1 already booked several: the Chiyoda remeasurement, a coal-business sale gain, food-ingredient divestment gains and the Mitsubishi Shokuhin settlement. The recurring base under ¥1.2T is lower than the headline. In the half to September, gains on securities rose to ¥104.9B from ¥39.8B, a line the interim filing links to the sale of MC Healthcare Holdings and the Chiyoda remeasurement.
03
A $5.2B bet on US gas. Aethon cost $5.2B for all of its interests and brings an operated shale business onto the balance sheet. Its profit depends on US natural-gas prices. The ¥60B profit increase management expects assumes the sale of 25% to the founding family goes through.
04
The yen cuts both ways. Mitsubishi reports in yen and earns much of its profit in dollars and Australian dollars. The weak yen (¥157.50 per dollar on November 6, against the ¥145 the company assumed for FY2025) helped Q1 and swelled equity through translation. A sharp yen recovery would reverse both.

Notable One-Off Items

The half-year net income of ¥619.7B includes gains on securities of ¥104.9B, up ¥65.1B from a year earlier, a rise the interim filing links to the sale of MC Healthcare Holdings and the Chiyoda remeasurement. Q1's ¥95.4B rise in net income was not all recurring. The filing's own list of drivers: higher market prices (gross profit +¥128.7B); a remeasurement gain when Chiyoda Corporation became an equity-method affiliate, which more than offset last year's gain on selling TH Foods; sale-related gains in the Australian steelmaking-coal business (+¥9.5B in disposal gains); a litigation settlement received at Mitsubishi Shokuhin (other income +¥10.5B); higher dividends from resource investees (finance income +¥34.8B); and gains on selling businesses in the overseas food-ingredients unit inside equity-method income. Against that, interest costs rose ¥10.8B on more borrowing, and selling and administrative costs rose ¥56.4B with the weaker yen and higher bad-debt provisions. The FY2026 guide itself, per management, assumes "sizable asset and business recycling-related gains". The ¥1T buyback also flatters per-share figures: average shares fell from 3.94B in Q1 FY2025 to 3.66B in Q1 FY2026.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 6, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of ¥1,178.67 (split-adjusted) to the November 6, 2026 close of ¥4,958, Mitsubishi rose 320.6% on price. Over the same window the Nikkei 225 rose 95.0% (29,452.66 to 57,434.77, Yahoo). Mitsubishi also paid about ¥428 per current share in dividends over the period, which lifts the simple total return, before reinvestment, to roughly 357%. It was not a straight line: two long rallies (mid-2023 to April 2024, and August 2025 to March 2026) were each followed by a sharp give-back, and every month-end since the March 2026 high of ¥5,317 has been lower.

The 6-period monthly RSI spent much of 2023, early 2024 and the 2025–26 rally above 70, peaking at 93.7 in April 2024 and 93.0 in March 2026. Its low was 31.0 in November 2024, just above oversold. It now reads 65.7, below the 70 line that marks overbought. The November 6 close sits above the 10-month moving average of about ¥4,912; the October month-end (¥4,805) was only the second month-end below it since May 2025 (June 2026 was the other), so the uptrend stalled but did not break.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At 15.0x its raised FY2026 guidance, Mitsubishi is guiding to a 50% profit increase, and the first half already delivered 52% of the ¥1.2T, so the second half needs ¥580.3B, 6.4% less than the first. Management raised the guide in November, as it had said it would consider. The business mix is moving toward things that last longer than a coal-price cycle: a full year of LNG Canada, 2.1 billion cubic feet a day of US gas, and a stated plan to exit about 30 weak businesses. Net debt is about 0.45x equity, under the company's 0.6x ceiling, the dividend is progressive and rising to ¥125, a second ¥1 trillion buyback is under way toward a 12% ROE, and the largest shareholder said in September it plans to stay for decades.

Bear Case

The stock has more than quadrupled in five years and now trades at 17.3x trailing earnings, about a quarter above its four peers, on profits that swing with coal, copper, oil, the yen and disposal gains. FY2025 profit fell 15.8% as the previous year's one-off gains dropped out; the half to September rose 74% on higher coal and copper prices and ¥104.9B of gains on securities, up ¥65.1B (the MC Healthcare sale and the Chiyoda revaluation). The May guide included "sizable" one-off gains, and the new ¥1.2T guide is built on the same year. A 6.0% one-day fall on November 6, the day the Nikkei 225 also fell 6.0%, shows how much of the price follows the market. Aethon is a $5.2B entry into operated US shale gas. Much of the rise ran alongside Berkshire's buying and the first ¥1T buyback; that one is finished, and the new one buys at 1.85x book value, after a −14.2% June.

Top 3 Risks

01
Commodity reversal. More than half of group profit in the half came from the minerals and energy groups. A fall in steelmaking-coal or copper prices goes straight to the line that drove that rise, and the new ¥1.2T guide would be at risk.
02
Quality of the FY2026 number. The guide was raised in November; if the extra ¥100B is made of disposal gains rather than recurring profit, the forward multiple is lower only on paper. Gains on securities in the half were already ¥104.9B, up ¥65.1B. The test is the recurring base once divestment gains are stripped out.
03
Yen strength. At ¥157.50 per dollar the currency is a tailwind to earnings and to book value. A sharp yen rally would cut translated profits, shrink equity and remove one of the reasons the company gave for a possible upgrade.
Industry Commentary
The Berkshire effect on the trading houses
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Berkshire's 2025 letter calls its Japanese holdings "comparable to our major U.S. holdings in importance and long-term value creation opportunity". At December 31, 2025 it owned 10.8% of Mitsubishi, 10.1% of ITOCHU, 10.4% of Mitsui, 9.8% of Marubeni and 9.7% of Sumitomo: $15.4B of cost worth $35.4B, matched by yen borrowing of roughly the same amount at an average cost of 1.2%. Mitsubishi was the largest position and earned Berkshire $273M in dividends in 2025. For the sector, a long-term holder of 10% that is not selling means fewer shares are available to trade. Mitsubishi cancelled 7.9% of its shares in one go, and has just started a second ¥1 trillion buyback.

Commodities, the Middle East and the yen
SWING FACTOR

Trading-house profits rise and fall with resource prices more than their diversified look suggests. Mitsubishi's FY2025 fell 15.8%, mostly as the previous year's one-off gains dropped out; its Q1 FY2026 rose 47% on higher coal and copper prices, with the yen weaker than the company had assumed. Management's August reading was that the Middle East disruption would last longer than assumed, that its direct Q1 impact was limited, and that higher oil prices could add upside not yet in the forecast. That combination (higher energy prices, a soft yen, firm metals) is close to the best case for the sector, which is part of why the guide was raised in November.

From buybacks back to deals
STRATEGY SHIFT

FY2025 at Mitsubishi was about returning cash: ¥1T of buybacks and a rising dividend. FY2026 does both: a second ¥1T buyback, and deployment: the $5.2B Aethon deal, a US data-center business, European energy, and ¥3T or more of growth investment planned through FY2027, funded partly by exits from about 30 businesses and by sales such as the 49.9% LNG Canada holding-company interest. The strategy now has to prove that deals made at today's prices earn more than the 12% ROE target, while every yen spent on buybacks now buys shares at 1.85x book value.
