01 Company Overview

McCormick sells the flavour, not the meal: herbs, spices, seasoning mixes, condiments and hot sauces under brands including McCormick, French's and Frank's RedHot, plus a second business that supplies flavours and seasonings to food manufacturers and foodservice operators. FY2025 (year to November 30, 2025) revenue was $6.84B, up 1.73%, with diluted EPS of $2.93 and a 15.7% operating margin (16.0% adjusted, before special charges). It is a slow, steady compounder of a business — revenue growth below 5% in four of the last five fiscal years — that has spent 2026 making the least slow, least steady decision in its history.

On March 31, 2026 McCormick agreed to combine with Unilever's Foods business — Hellmann's, Knorr and most of the rest of the portfolio — for $15.7B in cash plus shares worth $29.1B, an enterprise value of about $44.8B, or 13.8x Unilever Foods' FY2025 EBITDA, per McCormick's announcement. The minnow swallows the whale: after closing, today's McCormick holders will own 35.0% of the combined company, Unilever's shareholders 55.1% and Unilever itself 9.9%. The market voted before the shareholders could. MKC closed February at $71.04 and March at $50.44 — a 29.0% fall in one month, the worst month in the five-year chart below — and, per FoodNavigator, touched a seven-year low of $48.38 on April 1. Six months later the stock is below that level: it closed September at $46.40 and October 2 at $44.67, two sessions after a third-quarter report that beat on adjusted earnings and kept the year's guidance. The question for anyone holding MKC today is no longer "is this a good spice company?" It is "what is 35% of a $20B-revenue, 4x-levered food group worth, and will it be allowed to exist?"

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Category-defining spice brand Shelf space & retailer relationships Global sourcing of raw spices Embedded B2B flavour recipes Hot-sauce & condiment brands Dividend-paying defensive demand

The moat is small-ticket habit. A jar of spice is a trivial line on a grocery bill, bought by name, and a customer who switches to a cheaper brand saves pennies while risking the recipe. On the Flavor Solutions side the moat is different but just as sticky: once a seasoning is formulated into a customer's snack or sauce, changing supplier means reformulating and re-testing a product line. Neither moat is threatened by the Unilever deal. What changes is how much of the combined company those moats cover — Hellmann's and Knorr are strong brands, but condiments and bouillon compete in larger, more private-label-exposed aisles than the spice rack.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Dilution by design. McCormick shareholders go from owning all of McCormick to 35.0% of the combined company. The share consideration was set using McCormick's one-month VWAP of $57.84, per the announcement; the stock closed October 2 at $44.67, 22.8% below that. Whatever the strategic merit, holders are exchanging a pure-play spice business for a minority stake in a far larger, slower-growing food portfolio.
02
Leverage. The $15.7B cash leg is backed by a 364-day bridge loan facility of up to $15.7B (per the Q3 10-Q) and is to be funded from balance-sheet cash and new debt; the announcement expects net leverage of 4.0x or less at close, falling to 3.0x within two years. Today's balance sheet already carries $5.02B of debt at August 31, 2026 ($1.34B of short-term borrowings, $0.77B of long-term debt due within a year and $2.91B of long-term debt, per the 10-Q) and a current ratio of 0.66. Q3 interest expense rose to $68.4M from $50.2M. A food group at 4x leverage has less room for a commodity shock or a failed synergy plan.
03
Regulators get a say. The UK Competition and Markets Authority opened a Phase 1 review on September 17, 2026 with a decision deadline of November 11, 2026 (per FoodBev). On the Q3 call Foley said regulatory filings had been submitted on schedule (per the call transcript). The overlap is visible: McCormick owns French's mustard, Unilever owns Colman's. FoodNavigator reports Unilever has put Colman's up for sale, with Rothschild appointed, to address competition concerns.
04
Organic growth is thin. Q3 organic growth was 1.9% (Consumer 1.1%, with the Americas consumer business down 0.3%), after 1.7% in Q2. FY2025 reported growth was 1.73%, FY2024 0.92%. Costs are moving the other way: on the Q3 call management raised its cost-inflation expectation to 6–7% from mid-single digits (per the call transcript). The core business is not growing fast enough on its own to make the multiple argument without the deal.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort FY2026 reported figures. First, the $866.8M gain on remeasurement of the prior 50% equity stake in McCormick de Mexico — non-cash, non-recurring, and the reason trailing P/E, ROE and net margin all look better than the business. Second, special charges of $141.5M in Q3 and $226.6M for the nine months (vs $16.7M a year earlier): Q3's include $95.3M of transaction and integration expenses and a $43.1M non-cash impairment from shutting a development-stage pepper-sourcing project in Malaysia (per the 10-Q); they cut Q3 diluted EPS by $0.50. Third, a $28M IEEPA tariff refund credited to cost of goods sold in Q2, worth about 140bps of that quarter's gross margin. The tax rate also jumped, to 32.3% in Q3 from 15.9% (adjusted 22.6% from 16.1%). Management's adjusted EPS guide of $3.05–$3.13 strips out the first two.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 14.5x the FY2026 consensus EPS of $3.09 for a company whose shares were used as deal currency at $57.84. You collect a 4.30% dividend, and the deal commits to a payout consistent with its roughly 60% history. If the combination closes as planned, management guides to mid-to-high-single-digit EPS accretion in year one and mid-to-high-teens accretion by Year 3, with $600M of run-rate cost synergies. Even after the post-print cuts, every analyst target on the StockAnalysis panel — the lowest is $47 — sits above $44.67. And the core franchise is intact: Q3 adjusted EPS beat the Nasdaq consensus by 14.7%, adjusted operating margin widened to 17.7%, guidance held, nine-month operating cash flow rose to $598.8M from $420.2M, and Flavor Solutions grew organically at 3.0%.

Bear Case

You are buying 35% of a company that does not yet exist. It will carry about 4x net leverage at close and must digest a carve-out nearly twice McCormick's size (roughly $13B of the ~$20B combined 2025 revenue, our arithmetic). A UK regulator is reviewing it, and the obvious remedy, selling Colman's, is already on the table. The standalone business grows organically at about 2%, driven by price, and management now expects 6–7% cost inflation. The trailing P/E of 8.08 is an accounting mirage created by an $866.8M one-off gain; GAAP Q3 EPS fell to $0.36 under $141.5M of special charges. The market's verdict since March has been consistent: down 29% in the deal month, down 14% in the month the regulator arrived, and down again on a quarter that beat. Six target cuts in the two sessions after the print, per the StockAnalysis ratings table, suggest the sell side is starting to agree. Until closing, there is little to rerate the stock except the risk of the deal breaking.

Scale as the answer to slow growth
STRATEGIC BET

The deal is McCormick's answer to a packaged-food problem: volumes are flat, cost inflation keeps arriving — the Q2 release cited higher commodity costs and costs related to the Middle East conflict, and the Q3 release higher commodity and freight costs — and growth has to be bought. Combining creates a ~$20B-revenue flavour group with global reach in condiments and bouillon that McCormick could never build organically. Management's case is that flavour is a better category than food in general: sauces, seasonings and condiments rather than whole meals. The market's early answer, per FoodNavigator, was concern over "valuation, strategic fit and execution risk, particularly given the scale and complexity of combining two global food groups with overlapping categories and differing margin profiles."

What would change the market's mind
WATCH LIST

A clean Phase 1 outcome from the CMA on or before November 11; a shareholder vote that passes; evidence at the Q4 print that organic growth can move to the upper half of 1–3% (Q3 was 1.9%) while deal costs stay contained; and, after closing, the first year of synergy delivery against the $600M target. MKC holders are being asked to wait roughly nine months for closing and about three years for the synergy case to prove out. At 14.5x forward earnings and a 4.3% yield, they are being paid something to wait, but not much.
