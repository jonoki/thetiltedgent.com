01 Company Overview

Martin Marietta is a natural-resource-based building materials company: it owns quarries, digs up crushed stone, sand and gravel, and sells the tons into road, non-residential and residential construction, alongside downstream cement, ready mixed concrete and asphalt operations and a specialty magnesia and lime business. FY2025 revenue was $6,150M, up 8.6% on FY2024's $5,662M; the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $6,688M, up 14.8%. At roughly $35.7B of market value the company ranks about #704 globally by market capitalisation.

The central debate on this page is not whether aggregates is a good business — it demonstrably is — but whether the last eighteen months of portfolio surgery has bought growth or bought dilution. In the space of seven months Martin Marietta swapped its Midlothian cement plant and Texas ready mix operations away to QUIKRETE, bolted on New Frontier Materials, and then closed a ~$13.5B combination with Lhoist North America on August 24, 2026, funded in part by $5.5B of new senior notes and a $1.5B term loan. Reported aggregates ASP fell 2% in Q2 2026 and gross profit per ton fell 17%, even as shipments rose 17%. Both of those declines are, on the company's own numbers, artefacts of the acquisitions rather than of the underlying pricing machine. The stock has voted with its feet: down 17.3% over 52 weeks and sitting within eleven dollars of a fresh 52-week low. Sections 02 and 04 separate the volume story from the price story, because they are not the same story.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
RESERVE IRREPLACEABILITY FREIGHT-RADIUS MONOPOLY ZONING & PERMITTING BARRIER 2BN+ TONS LIMESTONE COASTAL DISTRIBUTION NETWORK PRICE-OVER-VOLUME DISCIPLINE PUBLIC-SPEND DEMAND ANCHOR

The moat here is geology plus freight arithmetic, and it is one of the most durable structures in industrial America. Crushed stone is a low-value, high-weight commodity: trucking it any meaningful distance costs more than the stone itself, so a quarry effectively holds a local franchise within roughly a 30–50 mile radius. You cannot compete by building a better quarry, because you cannot get a new one permitted — zoning, environmental review and community opposition make greenfield sites in growth markets close to unobtainable. The result is a business where the incumbent sets price and the customer pays it, which is exactly what the organic data shows: organic mix-adjusted ASP up 3.7% in a quarter when reported volumes were flat organically and the stock was making new lows. The Lhoist combination extends that logic vertically — the same limestone deposit that yields construction aggregates yields high-calcium lime, so 2 billion-plus tons of reserves now serve two demand curves (construction and industrial/steel/environmental) that do not cycle together. That diversification of the end market on top of a fixed reserve base is the single most interesting structural change at this company in a decade.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Volume cyclicality is not hedged by pricing power. Pricing is durable; tonnage is not. Organic shipments were the weak half of the Q2 2026 beat — the 17% headline growth came from acquisitions, and management flagged that organic momentum was concentrated in Central and West divisions where prices sit below company average. A residential or non-residential downturn shows up in tons immediately and in price only with a long lag, which means earnings fall before the moat does.
▸
Balance sheet risk from the Lhoist financing. The company priced $5.5B of senior notes across five tranches maturing 2029–2056 on August 12, 2026, supported by a $1.5B term loan, to fund the cash portion of a ~$13.5B transaction. Total debt already stood at $6.35B against $112M of cash and an EV/EBITDA of 19.96 before that debt landed on the reported balance sheet. Any integration stumble now has a leverage multiplier attached.
▸
Acquisition accounting is obscuring the operating trend. A $52M non-cash inventory step-up knocked roughly $0.85 off gross profit per ton in a single quarter, and a ~$1,977M gain on divestitures in the first half of 2026 inflated trailing net income to $2,457M against $1,439M of operating income. TTM EPS of $40.67 is therefore not an earnings number anyone should capitalise — see the one-off discussion in Section 04. When the reported numbers stop describing the business, the market's willingness to pay for the business tends to fall.
▸
Public-infrastructure demand concentration. A large share of aggregates tonnage flows to highway and public infrastructure work, which makes the demand curve a function of federal and state appropriations rather than of private capital cycles. That is a stabiliser in a recession and a cliff at reauthorisation. The specific timing and size of the current federal surface-transportation authorisation was not verified from the sources fetched and is therefore not stated here — but it is the single exogenous variable most worth tracking on this name.

Notable One-Off Items. This is the most distorted set of trailing financials the company has produced in years, and three items account for essentially all of it. In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 monthly close of $392.84 to the September 10, 2026 close of $502.47, MLM returned +27.9% in price over five years — a compound annual rate of about 5.0%, plus a dividend yielding well under 1% a year. That is a thin result for a business whose operating economics improved materially over the same window: gross margin went from 24.91% in FY2021 to 30.80% in FY2025, and revenue from $5,414M to $6,150M. The path was anything but linear. The stock bottomed at a monthly close of $299.24 in June 2022 as rate fears crushed construction-linked cyclicals, then ran +126% over the following twenty months to $613.94 in March 2024, chopped sideways through 2024–2025, made a fresh peak of $676.57 in February 2026, and has since given back 25.7% into today. Almost the entire round trip from February to September 2026 maps onto the Lhoist announcement and financing.

Relative performance. The stock is down 17.29% over the trailing twelve months against a beta of 1.10 to the S&P 500. A precise total-return comparison against the S&P 500 and against the NASDAQ-100 over the identical five-year window could not be verified from the sources fetched and is therefore not stated as a number. What can be said from the data on this page: the underperformance is company-specific rather than sector-wide in character — it is concentrated in the seven months since the Lhoist announcement, it coincided with a twelve-month period in which revenue grew 14.8% and adjusted EBITDA grew 13%, and the two most recent analyst actions on record are both upgrades arriving into the low. The market is repricing a balance sheet and an integration, not a demand collapse.

Momentum read. The price sits below its 1-month, 3-month and 10-month monthly moving averages, with the 10-month line having rolled over during the summer — a textbook downtrend on the monthly frame. Monthly 6-period RSI has been grinding lower since the February 2026 peak and is in the lower third of its range. Note what RSI does not tell you here: it cannot see that trailing EPS contains a $1,977M gain, or that gross profit per ton was depressed by a non-cash inventory charge. Momentum and fundamentals are pointing in opposite directions on this name right now, which is usually where the interesting disagreements live.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The pricing machine is intact and the market is reading the wrong line: organic mix-adjusted aggregates ASP grew 3.7% in Q2 2026 while the reported figure showed −2%, and gross profit per ton excluding a $52M non-cash inventory charge was roughly $7.63, not $6.78. Shipments grew 17% to 61.6 million tons, adjusted EBITDA grew 13% to $638M, and the company raised revenue guidance to $7.2–7.4B without any Lhoist contribution. On top of that sits a genuinely strategic combination — 2 billion-plus tons of limestone reserves serving both construction and industrial/steel/environmental demand curves — plus a $350M annualised cash-flow improvement programme against $810M of trailing free cash flow. You are buying an irreplaceable reserve base 29% below its high, with two major upgrades landing at the low and 31.7% of consensus upside.

Bear Case

Strip out the noise and you are paying 27.3× forward earnings for a company earning 6.66% on invested capital and 8.89% on equity — returns below most estimates of cost of capital, about to be diluted further by $13.5B of newly deployed Lhoist capital that carries $5.5B of fresh debt and a $1.5B term loan and contributes no synergy disclosure yet. Organic volume growth is roughly nil; the 17% shipment increase was bought. Reported gross margin fell from 30.80% to 28.23%, FY2026 consensus EPS is declining 1.9%, and free cash flow fell from $978M to $810M. Forty-two percent of the analyst panel is already at Hold or worse, and the stock has made a new 52-week low despite all of the above being known.

Top 3 Risks

01
Lhoist integration and leverage. A $13.5B transaction funded with $5.5B of new notes and a $1.5B term loan, closed August 24, 2026, with no pro-forma guidance published until Q3 results. If synergies disappoint or the lime end markets (steel especially) soften, the debt does not care.
02
Organic volume stagnation. Every ton of the 17% Q2 shipment growth was acquired. If construction starts roll over — residential, non-residential or a pause in public appropriations — the acquisitions will have bought volume into a falling market at peak multiples, and the price book cannot offset a tonnage decline in the same year.
03
Returns below cost of capital. ROIC of 6.66% and ROE of 8.89% on a business trading at 27.3× forward earnings and 19.96× EV/EBITDA is a mismatch that only resolves one of two ways: returns rise, or the multiple falls. The Lhoist capital deployment makes the denominator worse before the numerator improves.
Industry Commentary
Infrastructure spending is doing the volume work
EMERGING TAILWIND

Martin Marietta raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $7.2–7.4B on the back of shipment strength attributed to infrastructure demand, and posted a record $1,533M aggregates revenue quarter. Public infrastructure work is the most price-inelastic demand this company sees: highway contracts are awarded on specification and proximity, and the aggregate is a small fraction of total project cost, so the customer does not shop on stone price. That is the demand backdrop currently absorbing the acquired tonnage. The specific federal programme mechanics — the size and reauthorisation timing of the current surface-transportation authorisation — were not verified from the sources fetched and are not asserted here. What is verifiable is that management attributed the guidance raise to shipment growth, not to pricing, and that reported pricing went the other way.

Consolidation is now the sector's main event
ONGOING HEADWIND

In seven months Martin Marietta executed an asset exchange with QUIKRETE, a bolt-on of New Frontier Materials (8M+ tons annually), and a ~$13.5B combination with Lhoist North America. That is not opportunism; it is the aggregates industry's structural answer to the fact that you cannot permit new quarries. The consequence for investors is that reported financials become progressively harder to read — a $1,977M divestiture gain, a $52M inventory step-up, a reported-versus-organic ASP gap of nearly six percentage points, and the disappearance of separate cement, ready mixed and asphalt product-line disclosure all landed in the same two quarters. When the disclosure gets noisier at the same time as the leverage gets higher, multiples compress whether or not the business deteriorates. The stock's 25.7% drawdown since February is consistent with that mechanism.

Bull vs bear, framed on one number
THE DECIDING VARIABLE

Everything on this page reduces to which ASP series you believe describes the future. The bear takes the reported line: −2% pricing, $6.78 of gross profit per ton, 28.23% gross margin, 6.66% ROIC, $810M of free cash flow, FY2026 EPS declining — a capital-intensive cyclical paying too much for growth at the wrong point in the cycle, at 27.3× forward. The bull takes the organic line: +3.7% mix-adjusted pricing, ~$7.63 of gross profit per ton ex-charge, 13% adjusted EBITDA growth, a raised revenue guide, and 2 billion tons of reserves that will still be there in 2050 — a pricing franchise temporarily obscured by purchase accounting. Both readings are honestly derived from the same quarter. The Q3 2026 report, which carries the first Lhoist-inclusive guidance, is where the argument gets settled with data rather than interpretation. Until then, the 42% of the analyst panel sitting at Hold or worse is the most rational position on the tape.
