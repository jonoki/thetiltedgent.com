01 Company Overview

Mosaic digs two things out of the ground that no farm can do without: potash from Saskatchewan and phosphate from Florida's Bone Valley — and it sells both, plus imported product, through a large distribution business in Brazil. The company was formed in October 2004 by merging IMC Global (founded 1909) with Cargill's crop nutrition division; Cargill distributed its stake to its own shareholders in May 2011, and Mosaic joined the S&P 500 on September 26, 2011. FY2025 revenue was $12.05B, up 8.4%, with diluted EPS of $1.70. On a trailing-twelve-month basis to June 2026, revenue is $12.25B but the company is loss-making: −$638.5M net, −$2.01 per share.

Here is the hand Mosaic is playing. A phosphate producer is a spread business: it buys sulfur and ammonia, reacts them with rock, and sells the result. In Q2 2026 the selling price was good — DAP realised $773/tonne against $668 a year earlier — and the Phosphates segment still lost $104M at the operating line, because the inputs ran faster than the output. Sulfur averaged $522 per long ton in the quarter and the Q3 contract settled at $705. Mosaic's answer has been to stop playing marginal hands: its Louisiana phosphate operations were fully offline, Faustina idled and Bartow at 40% of its targeted rate, per the Q2 call. Meanwhile potash — steady volumes, ~$275/tonne, $278M of adjusted EBITDA — paid for everything. Then, on the banner date itself, the potash leg took a hit of its own: the US President said Washington was negotiating a "massive" deal to buy Belarusian potash for "substantially less than we are currently paying to Canada." Mosaic's potash is Canadian. The stock closed the day down 1.67% at $24.07, 30.6% below its September 2025 month-end.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Long-life Saskatchewan potash reserves Integrated Florida phosphate rock Brazil distribution footprint Mine permits & capital intensity Low potash cash cost ($84/t) Non-discretionary end demand

The moat is geology and permits. Nobody builds a new potash mine or phosphate complex quickly, and farmers cannot skip potassium and phosphorus indefinitely. That protects Mosaic's existence. It does not protect its margin: fertiliser is a commodity, the price is set globally, and the producer takes it. The Q2 2026 numbers show the distinction plainly — owning the rock did not stop Phosphates losing money when sulfur spiked, because a phosphate plant's rock is not its only input.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Sulfur is the swing input. Sulfur averaged $522 per long ton in Q2 and the Q3 contract settled at $705. On the Q2 call Bodine attributed the tight sulfur market to the ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz and a blockade affecting Kazakhstan (as reported in the Motley Fool transcript). Mosaic cannot hedge its way out of a physical shortage; its lever is to curtail production, which is what it has done — and curtailment raises unit fixed costs.
02
Canadian potash is now a political asset. On September 21, 2026 the US President posted that Washington was negotiating a potash deal with Belarus priced "substantially less than we are currently paying to Canada" (quoted by The Northern Miner). Belarusian producers were removed from US Treasury sanctions in March 2026, per the same report. Belarus's own president responded the same day that its output is already fully contracted (Capital Press, Sep 21, 2026). The deal may never happen; the risk premium arrived the same day.
03
The balance sheet is doing more work. Total debt rose from $5.05B at December 31, 2025 to $5.86B at June 30, 2026, against $294M of cash. First-half free cash flow was −$405.5M, and trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is −$947M. Mosaic still pays $0.22 a quarter. Management expects $300–500M of working-capital release this year, mostly in Q4, and put a $1B term loan in place to refinance commercial paper.
04
Operating reliability. The October 2025 phosphate production miss (Riverview, Bartow) and the 2026 curtailments mean Mosaic's phosphate plants have spent a long stretch running below capacity. Restarting idled assets costs money, and the market has already seen restart promises slip once.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a potash franchise that earned $278M of adjusted EBITDA in a single quarter at $84/t cash cost, plus a phosphate business priced for permanent impairment, at 0.67x book with a 3.66% dividend. The phosphate problem is an input-cost problem, not a demand problem: DAP realised $773/t and is guided to $820–840/t in Q3. If sulfur normalises, the spread reopens on idled capacity Mosaic has explicitly kept ready to restart. Add $300–500M of working-capital release in Q4, a lower $1.2B capex guide, a 20% cut in SG&A and the sale of non-core Brazilian assets, and the cash-flow line can turn without any help from the cycle. The Belarus headline may prove to be just that — Belarus itself said its output is fully contracted.

Bear Case

A commodity producer with negative trailing EPS (−$2.01), negative free cash flow (−$947M TTM), debt up $0.8B in six months and a dividend funded by the balance sheet is not cheap at 0.67x book — it is showing you what the book is worth. Phosphates lost money in a quarter when the product price rose 16%; Fertilizantes swung to a loss on volumes down a third. The one healthy segment, potash, is now exposed to a US government actively seeking cheaper non-Canadian supply. Forward earnings are thin — consensus FY2026 EPS is $0.39 — and the analyst range runs down to $17.

01
Sulfur and ammonia stay expensive. The Q3 sulfur contract at $705 per long ton is 35% above the Q2 average of $522. If input costs keep outrunning DAP, the phosphate curtailments stay in place and their fixed costs keep landing in the income statement.
02

Industry Commentary
The sulfur squeeze on phosphate producers
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Phosphate fertiliser needs sulfuric acid, and sulfuric acid needs sulfur — mostly a by-product of oil and gas refining, so its supply follows energy flows rather than farm demand. Mosaic's Q2 2026 call put the quarter's sulfur cost at $522 per long ton, the Q3 settlement at $705, and — per the Motley Fool transcript — attributed the tightness to the ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz and a Kazakhstan blockade. Mosaic's response across 2026 has been to curtail: Louisiana offline, Faustina idle, Bartow at 40%, New Wales and Riverview in the low-to-mid 70s percent, and Brazilian SSP curtailed since December 2025. That preserves cash per tonne but shrinks volume and raises unit fixed costs — the $60M of idle and turnaround costs in Q2 is the bill.

Potash: balanced market, political supply
ONGOING HEADWIND

Potash is a concentrated industry. The Northern Miner (September 21, 2026) put Canada's share of US potash imports at 79% over 2021–2024, with Russia at 12%; the US imported 0.5–1Mt a year from Belarus before 2021 sanctions, and the US Treasury removed Belaruskali and Belarusian Potash Company from sanctions in March 2026. Belarus produced 7.1Mt in 2024. Mosaic's MOP price was $275/t in Q2, up from $261, and management called the market balanced. A US-Belarus deal would not change world supply much — Belarus says its output is already contracted — but it could redirect flows into Mosaic's home market. RBC's Andrew Wong, in a note quoted by the same outlet: "Unless Belarusian potash were to displace Canadian potash in significant quantities, we think any mix-shift impact to realized prices would be moderate."

Self-help: a smaller, cheaper Mosaic
EMERGING TAILWIND

What Mosaic controls, it is cutting: Carlsbad sold, Araxá and Patrocínio under strategic review, capex trimmed to $1.2B, SG&A down about 20% year over year to $132M in Q2 and guided at $510–530M for the year, and a Biosciences business (Q2 net sales $25M) that management wants to reallocate capital toward. The bull framing: the company is sitting out the bad hands and keeping its chips for when sulfur normalises, with idled capacity ready to restart. The bear framing: selling assets and idling plants at the bottom of a cycle locks in a smaller business just before the recovery. The deciding data points are reported every quarter: phosphate operating result, sulfur cost per long ton, and free cash flow after the dividend.
