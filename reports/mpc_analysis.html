01 Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum is one of the largest refiners in the United States, running 13 refineries with 2.99 million barrels per day of crude capacity across the Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent and West Coast, alongside a branded fuel network and a controlling interest in MPLX LP, one of the largest midstream partnerships in North America (midstream means the pipes, plants and terminals that move and process oil and gas). On Nov 3 it agreed to sell two of those refineries, St. Paul Park in Minnesota and Mandan in North Dakota, which would leave 11 refineries and about 2.8 million barrels per day (2.986 million less the 177,000 the buyer is acquiring) once the deal closes, expected by the end of 2026. Revenue and other income for the nine months to Sept 30, 2026 was $143.39B, up 40.9% from $101.80B a year earlier, and trailing-twelve-month (TTM) net income is about $14.6B against $4.04B for all of FY2025. The company ranks #112 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation at $101.49B, ahead of Valero ($96.62B) and Phillips 66 ($95.85B).

Here is the thing most people get wrong about a refiner, and it is the organising idea of this page: MPC is not an oil-price business, it is a spread business. It buys crude and sells gasoline, diesel and jet fuel; what it earns is the gap between the two — the crack spread — and that gap can widen while crude falls or narrow while crude rallies. In Q3 2026 MPC's system-wide refining margin was $45.70 per barrel versus $17.60 a year earlier. That single line, applied across roughly 3.2 million barrels a day of throughput, is why quarterly net income went from $1.4B to $7.4B. The second half of the story is MPLX, which pays MPC a fee-based cash distribution every quarter regardless of where cracks sit — MPC now expects about $3.1B a year from it, more than twice MPC's whole dividend. The debate this page works through: whether that midstream floor, a share count that has fallen by more than half and now a sale of two refineries near the top of the cycle have changed the risk profile of a cyclical business, or whether investors are simply paying a peak-cycle price for peak-cycle earnings. The market's answer so far: the shares fell 12.8% from the Nov 3 close to Nov 6 even as analysts raised targets.

The spread, region by region. A crack spread is simply the difference between what a refiner pays for a barrel of crude and what it can sell the gasoline, diesel and jet fuel refined from that barrel for — the refiner earns the gap, not the oil price. In Q3 2026 MPC's refining margin per barrel was $43.46 Gulf Coast, $47.33 Mid-Continent and $47.46 West Coast, for a system total of $45.70 against $17.60 in Q3 2025 — Gulf Coast $14.77, Mid-Continent $19.88, West Coast $19.17 a year ago. Crude units ran at 100% of capacity (95% a year earlier), refining 2,973 thousand barrels a day of crude and 3,150 thousand in total throughput (3,005 a year earlier), with refining operating costs of $5.60 per barrel (vs $5.59). The company's own sensitivity disclosure is the number to memorise: a $1.00 per barrel move in the blended crack spread changes annual R&M segment EBITDA by about $1,125 million (from MPC's 2026 Investor Update, before the sale). On that rule the $28.10 widening in the margin since last year is worth roughly $31.6 billion of annualised segment EBITDA. Q4 2026 guidance: total throughput 3,080 thousand barrels a day (2,830 crude + 250 other), operating cost $5.60 per barrel, turnaround expense (planned maintenance shutdowns) $230M, distribution costs $1,725M.

The sale. On Nov 3 MPC agreed to sell the St. Paul Park and Mandan refineries, with their related marketing and midstream assets, to CVR Energy for about $4.2 billion: MPC receives $3.45 billion, which it intends to reinvest mainly on the Gulf Coast, and MPLX receives $750 million for the associated logistics assets (a multiple of 9 times forecast 2027 adjusted EBITDA of those assets), to redeploy in the Permian and Marcellus gas regions. The two refineries total 177,000 barrels a day (St. Paul Park 105,000, Mandan 72,000), and the marketing business supplies up to 590 branded locations in the Upper Midwest and Northern Great Plains (hydrocarbonprocessing.com, OPIS). The sale is expected to close at the end of 2026, subject to regulatory approvals. CEO Maryann Mannen said on the Q3 call: "We acted on an opportunity to align our portfolio with our strategy at valuations that I think we deem to be very attractive." CVR Energy CEO Dane Neumann said the two refineries have generated more than $1.1 billion of EBITDA in the first nine months of 2026 (Oil & Gas Journal): $4.2B is about 3.8 times those nine months of EBITDA, or under 3 times if annualised.

This is the part that makes MPC structurally different from a pure refiner. MPLX is a publicly traded master limited partnership (an MLP — a pass-through entity that pays out most of its cash flow as unit distributions) that gathers, processes and transports natural gas, natural gas liquids and crude. MPC owns approximately 64% of its common units plus the general partner interest. MPLX raised its quarterly distribution 12.5% to $1.2111 per unit ($4.84 a year), the third year running of a 12.5% increase, payable Nov 13 to holders of record Nov 6 (MPLX Q3 release). MPC now expects about $3.1 billion a year in distributions from MPLX. Set that against MPC's own obligations: the new quarterly dividend of $1.10 per share on about 272 million shares is roughly $300 million a quarter, about $1.2 billion a year, and MPC's standalone 2026 capital budget (excluding MPLX) is $1.5 billion. The MPLX distribution therefore covers the entire dividend about 2.6 times, and covers the dividend plus the whole standalone capital programme with about $0.4 billion left over. MPLX Q3 standalone: adjusted EBITDA attributable to MPLX $1,859M (up 5.3% from $1,766M), distributable cash flow $1,537M, distribution coverage 1.3× (cash available per dollar paid out), leverage 3.8× (debt to adjusted EBITDA). MPLX is keeping its 2026 organic growth capital at $2.9 billion, over 90% of it for natural gas and NGL (natural gas liquids) infrastructure, with expected mid-teens returns. It also sells the logistics assets tied to the two refineries for $750 million.

Why it matters for the cycle: in Q3 2025, Midstream was half of the three-segment adjusted EBITDA; in Q3 2026 it was 15.6%. Fee-based midstream cash does not care what the crack spread does, and the dividend has not depended on refining.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SCALE — 2.99M BARRELS A DAY, ~2.8M AFTER SALE MPLX CASH FLOOR — ~$3.1B A YEAR GEOGRAPHIC DIVERSITY — 3 REGIONS 100% CRUDE UTILISATION CRUDE FLEXIBILITY REPLACEMENT COST BARRIER 52% SHARE COUNT REDUCTION 2021–25

The moat in refining is not a brand and not technology — it is that new US refineries are almost never built: the first in nearly half a century, planned for the Port of Brownsville in Texas, was only announced in March 2026 (Hydrocarbon Processing). Permitting, capital cost and the local politics of siting a brand-new refinery in the United States have all but stopped new plants, which turns every existing refinery into a scarce asset; the Nov 3 deal is itself a sign of it, as a buyer is paying $4.2B for two existing plants and their marketing network rather than build. On top of the scarcity, MPC has two edges that are its own doing. The first is running the plants hard: 13 refineries across three separate regional markets, each able to change which crudes it runs and how much gasoline, diesel or jet fuel it makes, ran at 100% of crude capacity in Q3 2026 against 95% a year earlier, at an operating cost of $5.60 a barrel, flat on the year. The second is the MPLX structure. A pure refiner's dividend is hostage to the crack spread; MPC's is funded by a fee-based midstream distribution stream of about $3.1B a year that covers the dividend about 2.6× and the dividend-plus-standalone-capex with a little room to spare. That makes its payouts to shareholders less dependent on refining profit than at Valero or PBF, and it is the single most defensible reason to pay a premium for MPC within the group. The sale changes the mix, not the model: it trades two Upper Midwest refineries for $3.45B to be reinvested mainly on the Gulf Coast, where management says it wants to be.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The spread is the earnings, and the spread is not controlled by MPC. The company's own sensitivity says a $1.00/bbl move in the blended crack changes annual R&M EBITDA by about $1,125M. The Q3 2026 refining margin of $45.70/bbl was $28.10 above the year-ago $17.60. Reverse that and roughly $31 billion of annualised segment EBITDA reverses with it. No amount of good plant management offsets a crack spread that falls back toward normal; net income was $14.5B in FY2022, $3.4B in FY2024, $4.0B in FY2025, and is running at about $14.6B TTM.
▸
The current margin is disruption-driven, not structural. CEO Maryann Mannen said on the Q3 call that 10 million barrels a day of global refining capacity was offline, planned and unplanned, about 3 million above its historical norm, "reflecting ongoing Persian Gulf disruptions and Ukrainian attacks on Russian infrastructure", and that the Iran conflict has been "really elevating margins". Supply interruptions are, by construction, hard to forecast and can end quickly. Management's view that the market stays in an "enhanced mid cycle environment through the end of the decade" is a forecast about other people's outages and decisions.
▸
The balance sheet is large, though much of it belongs to MPLX. Debt-to-equity is 1.15 on $34.45B of total debt against $13.61B of cash and short-term investments (stockanalysis.com). The 10-Q puts consolidated debt at $33.0B: $6.4B at MPC and $26.6B at MPLX, serviced by fee-based cash. The $5B revolving credit facility was undrawn at quarter-end. Leverage has fallen from September (debt-to-equity 1.33) as profit built up equity.
▸
Execution and policy risk. The CVR sale needs regulatory approval and is expected to close at the end of 2026. West Coast refining (a high-margin region) and the renewable-diesel segment, which earned $254M in the quarter, both depend on state and federal policy that can move against the company as easily as for it.

Why the trailing P/E is a trap here — read this before using any multiple on this page

A refiner's earnings swing violently with the cycle, so trailing P/E is a poor guide and often an inverted one. Look at MPC's own record: net income of $14.51B (FY2022) → $9.67B (FY2023) → $3.44B (FY2024) → $4.04B (FY2025) → about $14.6B TTM. The lowest P/E in that series occurred when earnings were highest and the cycle was about to roll; the highest P/E occurred at the trough, when the stock was cheapest on any normalised basis. Buying refiners on a low trailing P/E is the classic way to buy the top. Today MPC trades at 7.4× trailing and 5.5× on 2026 consensus — both look very cheap, and both are computed on a quarter in which the refining margin was two and a half times the year-ago level. The honest framing is not "7.4× is cheap"; it is "what does this system earn at a mid-cycle crack, and what am I paying for that?" With a $1,125M-per-dollar-of-crack sensitivity, that question is answerable, and it is the only one worth answering. The market did the same arithmetic: in September the stock traded at 13.5× TTM earnings, and with profit up and the price slightly down the multiple is now barely more than half that.

Notable One-Off Items

The margin environment itself is the one-off. Q3 2026 net income attributable to MPC was $7.4 billion ($26.59 per diluted share) against $1.4 billion ($4.51) in Q3 2025; adjusted net income was $7.5 billion ($26.93) against $915 million ($3.01). This is not an accounting distortion; it is a genuine cash quarter — operating cash flow for nine months was $22.2 billion. But it is a quarter produced by wide crack spreads that management ties to events outside its control, and it is annualised into the TTM figures throughout this table.

The buyback is the second distortion, and it is enormous. Diluted weighted-average shares went from 638 million in 2021 to 306 million in 2025 — a 52% reduction — on roughly $41 billion of repurchases over five years ($4.7B in 2021, $11.9B in 2022, $11.6B in 2023, $9.2B in 2024, $3.5B in 2025, per MPC's 2026 Investor Update). In Q3 2026 MPC bought back 8 million shares for $3.0 billion at an average of $357.68 (including excise tax) and returned $3.3 billion in total. At Sept 30 it had $3.1 billion of authorisation left, and the board added $5 billion, bringing the total to $8.1 billion. Shares outstanding fell to 275 million at Sept 30 (283 million at June 30) and 272.28 million at Oct 29. Understand what this does to the per-share numbers: at 638 million shares the same Q3 net income would have printed roughly $11.67 per share instead of $26.59. A large part of the earnings-per-share story of the last five years is arithmetic, not operations — which is not a criticism, it is simply what you are buying.

The refinery sale. The $4.2 billion sale of St. Paul Park and Mandan to CVR Energy ($3.45B to MPC, $750M to MPLX) is not in the Q3 numbers: the 10-Q lists it as a subsequent event. It removes 177,000 barrels a day of capacity and brings in cash MPC says it will reinvest mainly on the Gulf Coast.

Renewable diesel stayed positive. The segment earned $254M of adjusted EBITDA in Q3 2026 against a $56M loss in Q3 2025, and $550M over nine months against a $117M loss. The policy credits behind it are a regulatory variable, and therefore a recurring item only for as long as the policy holds.

MPLX raised its payout again. The 12.5% distribution increase to $1.2111 per unit is the third in a row, and lifts MPC's expected annual receipts from MPLX to about $3.1 billion.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and on a cyclical like this one, a stretched RSI at a cycle peak has historically been a better warning than any multiple.

Five-year trajectory. From the December 2021 close of $63.99 to the November 6, 2026 close of $372.74, MPC returned +482.5% on price alone, excluding dividends — a compound annual rate of about 44%. That is an extraordinary five years for what is nominally a mature commodity processor, and it happened in three distinct acts. Act one, Dec 2021 to Mar 2024: the post-COVID refining supercycle took the stock from $63.99 to $201.50, a 215% move, as global refining capacity that shut during the pandemic failed to come back and cracks blew out. Act two, Mar 2024 to Apr 2025: a −31.8% fall between month-end closes to $137.41 as crack spreads narrowed and FY2024 net income fell to $3.44B from $9.67B. Act three, Apr 2025 to today: +171%, of which the most violent part is the summer: +74% from the June 2026 close of $255.67 to the October month-end close of $444.73, with a daily closing high of $484.92 on Oct 14 as the crack spread blew wide open. Since that peak the stock has fallen 23.1% to $372.74, including a 16.2% fall from the Oct 30 close in the five sessions that followed. The stock now sits 24.5% below its 52-week intraday high of $493.98 and 64% of the way up its 52-week range.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

MPC is one of the largest US refiners (2.99 million barrels a day across 13 plants) in an industry where new plants are almost never built, ran its crude units at 100% in Q3, and generated $20.84B of trailing free cash flow. Its capital return is structurally protected: about $3.1B a year of fee-based MPLX distributions covers the $1.2B dividend about 2.6× and the dividend plus the entire $1.5B standalone capital budget with a little to spare, and MPLX has raised its payout 12.5% three years running. The buyback has retired 52% of the share count between 2021 and 2025 and continues, with $8.1B of authorisation after the $5B addition. At 5.5× 2026 consensus earnings with an 8.7% payout ratio and 31% ROIC, an investor is not paying a premium for a business that has proven it can pay shareholders through the trough, and the sale of two refineries for $4.2B puts a hard price on what a buyer will pay for these assets.

Bear Case

Every dollar of the 2026 story is a crack spread that MPC does not control. The refining margin of $45.70/bbl is $28.10 above last year's $17.60, and at $1,125M of annual R&M EBITDA per dollar of blended crack, a return to last year's level erases roughly $31B of annualised segment EBITDA. Management ties the margin to Persian Gulf and Russian supply disruptions, which can end. Analysts' own numbers fall from $67.60 of EPS in 2026 to $62.14 in 2027. The stock has already dropped 23% from its Oct 14 close and 12.8% in the three sessions after the print, so the market was not reassured by a quarter five times as profitable as last year's, a dividend increase and a sale. Debt-to-equity is 1.15 with $34.45B of debt. The trailing P/E of 7.4× looks cheap precisely because earnings are at a cycle peak — which is historically when refiners look cheapest and are most dangerous.

Top 3 Risks

01
Crack spread mean reversion. The single dominant variable. A return to the Q3 2025 refining margin of $17.60/bbl would, on the company's own $1,125M-per-dollar sensitivity, remove roughly $31 billion of annualised R&M EBITDA. Nothing in operations, capital allocation or the MPLX structure offsets that; it only cushions it.
02
Paying a cyclical peak multiple on peak earnings. At $372.74 the stock is 24.5% below its 52-week high but still up 93% in a year, and consensus EPS falls from $67.60 (2026) to $62.14 (2027). The forward P/E of 5.5× is computed on 2026 EPS, a figure that assumes the disruption-driven margin largely persists through Q4. If it does not, earnings and the price investors will pay for those earnings can fall together.
03
Leverage, policy exposure and deal execution. Consolidated debt-to-equity of 1.15 and $34.45B of total debt against $13.61B of cash means the balance sheet is a buffer, not a shock absorber. The CVR sale needs regulatory approval, and a meaningful slice of the earnings base (West Coast refining, renewable diesel at $254M a quarter) is set by policymakers rather than by markets.
Industry Commentary
Geopolitical Supply Disruption
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2026 refining boom has a named cause. On the Nov 3 call CEO Maryann Mannen said: "Obviously, Iran conflict has had significant impact, really elevating margins as you see," and said global refining outages about 3 million barrels a day above normal were "reflecting ongoing Persian Gulf disruptions and Ukrainian attacks on Russian infrastructure." The mechanical result: system refining margin of $45.70/bbl versus $17.60 a year earlier, with all three regions at $43–47. This is the biggest swing factor in the report and it comes from outside the company: no decision at MPC's Findlay headquarters moves it in either direction.
Structural Capacity Scarcity
EMERGING TAILWIND
The durable half of the refining thesis is that the US capacity base is not growing: in the United States the direction has been closures, not new builds, and the Nov 3 deal shows a buyer paying $4.2B for two existing refineries and their fuel network. Scarce assets run harder, and MPC ran its crude units at 100% in Q3 2026, up from 95% a year earlier. Management's own view is that the market will stay in an "enhanced mid cycle environment through the end of the decade" (Mannen, Q3 call).
Midstream vs Refining — Bull/Bear Frame
ONGOING DEBATE
The whole argument reduces to one comparison. In Q3 2025, Midstream was $1,709M of $3,415M across the three segments — 50% of the company. In Q3 2026, it was $1,882M of $12,031M — 15.6%. The midstream business grew 10%; refining grew five and a half times. The bull reads that as a floor: MPLX's roughly $3.1B annual distribution to MPC covers the dividend about 2.6× and grows regardless of cracks, so the downside is limited and the refining earnings are a bonus on top. The bear reads the same numbers as a warning: 82% of today's segment EBITDA comes from a spread that was less than half as wide twelve months ago. Both readings are correct about the arithmetic; they differ only on how long the disruption lasts.
