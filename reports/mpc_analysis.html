01 Company Overview

Marathon Petroleum is the largest refiner in the United States, running 12 refineries with 2.87 million barrels per day of crude capacity across the Gulf Coast, Mid-Continent and West Coast, alongside a branded fuel network and a controlling interest in MPLX LP, one of the largest midstream partnerships in North America. FY2025 revenue was $133.17B, down 4.4% from $139.34B in FY2024 — and then the cycle turned: trailing-twelve-month revenue through Q2 2026 is $154.15B, up 15.2%, with TTM net income of $8.55B against $4.04B for all of FY2025. The company ranks #213 globally by market capitalisation at $109.21B, just ahead of Valero ($106.74B) and Phillips 66 ($101.79B).

Here is the thing most people get wrong about a refiner, and it is the organising idea of this page: MPC is not an oil-price business, it is a spread business. It buys crude and sells gasoline, diesel and jet fuel; what it earns is the gap between the two — the crack spread — and that gap can widen while crude falls or narrow while crude rallies. In Q2 2026 MPC's system-wide refining margin was $36.33 per barrel versus $17.58 a year earlier. That single line, applied across roughly 2.9 million barrels a day, is why quarterly net income went from $1.2B to $5.1B. The second half of the story is MPLX, which throws off a fee-based cash distribution to MPC every quarter regardless of where cracks sit — and which now more than covers MPC's entire dividend and its standalone capital budget. The debate this page works through: whether that midstream floor plus a fifty-two-percent share-count reduction has genuinely changed the risk profile of a cyclical, or whether investors are simply paying a peak-cycle price for peak-cycle earnings.

The spread, region by region. A crack spread is simply the difference between what a refiner pays for a barrel of crude and what it can sell the gasoline, diesel and jet fuel refined from that barrel for — the refiner earns the gap, not the oil price. In Q2 2026 MPC's realised refining margin per barrel was $36.52 Gulf Coast (2 refineries, 1,248 mbpd, 42% of capacity), $33.68 Mid-Continent (6 refineries, 1,199 mbpd, 40%) and $41.28 West Coast (4 refineries, 540 mbpd, 18%), for a system total of $36.33 against $17.58 in Q2 2025 — Gulf Coast $15.17, Mid-Continent $17.86, West Coast $23.18 a year ago. Throughput ran 2.9 million barrels per day at 94% crude capacity utilisation, with refining operating costs of $5.72 per barrel (vs $5.34). Management reported margin capture of 112% in the quarter and 108% year to date — capture is the share of the theoretical market crack the refinery system actually converts into realised margin, and above 100% means the commercial and optimisation layer added value beyond the benchmark. The company's own sensitivity disclosure is the number to memorise: a $1.00 per barrel move in the blended crack spread changes annual R&M segment EBITDA by $1,125 million — roughly $4 per share pre-tax. Q3 2026 guidance: total throughput 3,005 mbpd (2,820 crude + 185 other), operating cost $5.60/bbl, turnaround expense $290M.

This is the part that makes MPC structurally different from a pure refiner. MPLX is a publicly traded master limited partnership (an MLP — a pass-through entity that pays out most of its cash flow as unit distributions) that gathers, processes and transports natural gas, natural gas liquids and crude. MPC owns 647 million of ~1,015 million MPLX common units — about 64% — plus the general partner interest. MPLX declared a Q2 2026 distribution of $1.0765 per unit, which on MPC's 647M units is roughly $697 million of cash to MPC in the quarter, about $2.79 billion annualised. Set that against MPC's own obligations: the quarterly dividend of $1.00 per share on ~283 million shares is about $283 million a quarter, ~$1.13 billion a year, and MPC's standalone 2026 capital budget (excluding MPLX) is $1.5 billion. The MPLX distribution therefore covers the entire MPC dividend ~2.5 times over, and covers the dividend plus the whole standalone capital programme with cash left over — which is precisely what the company claims when it says the "growing annualized distribution from MPLX is expected to more than fund MPC's standalone capital and dividend." MPLX has guided to 12.5% annual distribution growth in both 2026 and 2027 on mid-single-digit EBITDA growth, and raised its 2026 growth capital outlook by $500M to $2.9 billion, over 90% of it directed at natural gas and NGL infrastructure. Q2 2026 MPLX standalone: adjusted EBITDA $1.775B, distributable cash flow $1.450B, coverage 1.3×.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
LARGEST US CAPACITY — 2.87 MBPD MPLX CASH FLOOR GEOGRAPHIC DIVERSITY — 3 REGIONS 112% MARGIN CAPTURE CRUDE FLEXIBILITY REPLACEMENT COST BARRIER 52% SHARE COUNT REDUCTION

The moat in refining is not a brand and not technology — it is that nobody is building a new US refinery. Permitting, capital cost and the political economy of siting a greenfield refinery in the United States have effectively frozen the domestic capacity base, which turns every existing complex into a scarce asset. MPC's own planning materials frame it as anticipated global closures nearly offsetting announced capacity additions through 2030, with refined product demand growth expected to exceed net capacity additions over 2026–2030. On top of the scarcity, MPC has two edges that are its own doing. The first is optimisation: 12 refineries across three disconnected regional markets, each able to shift crude slate and product yield, is why margin capture came in at 112% of the benchmark crack in Q2 2026 and 108% year to date — capture above 100% is the difference between owning refineries and running them well. The second is the MPLX structure. A pure refiner's dividend is hostage to the crack spread; MPC's is funded by a fee-based midstream distribution stream of roughly $2.79B a year that covers the dividend 2.5× and the dividend-plus-standalone-capex with room to spare. That is a genuinely different capital-return risk profile from Valero or PBF, and it is the single most defensible reason to pay a premium for MPC within the group.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The spread is the earnings, and the spread is not controlled by MPC. The company's own sensitivity says a $1.00/bbl move in the blended crack changes annual R&M EBITDA by $1,125M. The Q2 2026 realised margin of $36.33/bbl was $18.75 above the year-ago $17.58. Reverse that and roughly $21 billion of annualised segment EBITDA reverses with it. No amount of operational excellence offsets a mean-reverting crack; the last five years of this company's own income statement prove it — net income was $14.5B in FY2022, $3.4B in FY2024, $4.0B in FY2025, and is running at $8.5B TTM.
▸
The current margin is disruption-driven, not structural. Contemporaneous coverage of the Q2 print attributed the surge to "higher crack spreads driven by Strait of Hormuz supply disruptions," and management cited tight global inventories plus refining outages in the Middle East and Russia. Geopolitical supply interruptions are, by construction, temporary. Management's view that the environment stays constructive "well into 2027" is a forecast about other people's outages.
▸
Leverage sits above the peer norm. Debt-to-equity is 1.33 with $34.29B of total debt against $7.77B of cash and an enterprise value of $135.74B. A large share of that debt is at MPLX and serviced by fee-based cash, which is a real mitigant — but the consolidated balance sheet still shows a levered company entering a cycle downturn, and the buyback has been funded partly by not deleveraging.
▸
West Coast and renewable-diesel policy risk. The West Coast system (540 mbpd, 18% of capacity) earned the highest margin in the quarter at $41.28/bbl, partly because California is a structurally short, high-regulation market — the same regulatory regime that has driven competitors to shut capacity there. Renewable diesel profitability, freshly positive at $258M in the quarter, was explicitly credited in part to "improved regulatory credit values." Both lines depend on state and federal policy that can move against the company as easily as for it.

Why the trailing P/E is a trap here — read this before using any multiple on this page

A refiner's earnings swing violently with the cycle, so trailing P/E is a poor guide and often an inverted one. Look at MPC's own record: net income of $14.51B (FY2022) → $9.67B (FY2023) → $3.44B (FY2024) → $4.04B (FY2025) → $8.55B TTM. The lowest P/E in that series occurred when earnings were highest and the cycle was about to roll; the highest P/E occurred at the trough, when the stock was cheapest on any normalised basis. Buying refiners on a low trailing P/E is the classic way to buy the top. Today MPC trades at 13.5× trailing and 8.1× forward — both look cheap, and both are computed on a quarter in which the realised margin was more than double the year-ago level. The honest framing is not "13.5× is cheap"; it is "what does this system earn at a mid-cycle crack, and what am I paying for that?" With a $1,125M-per-dollar-of-crack sensitivity, that question is answerable, and it is the only one worth answering.

Notable One-Off Items

The margin environment itself is the one-off. Q2 2026 net income attributable to MPC was $5.1 billion ($17.73 per diluted share) against $1.2 billion ($3.96) in Q2 2025 — described in contemporaneous coverage as a level not reached in four years, and driven by crack spreads that widened on Strait of Hormuz supply disruption plus refining outages in the Middle East and Russia. This is not an accounting distortion; it is a genuine cash quarter. But it is a quarter produced by other people's supply problems, and it is annualised into the TTM figures throughout this table.

The buyback is the second distortion, and it is enormous. Diluted weighted-average shares have gone from 638 million in 2021 to 306 million in 2025 — a 52% reduction — on roughly $41 billion of repurchases over five years ($4.7B in 2021, $11.9B in 2022, $11.6B in 2023, $9.2B in 2024, $3.5B in 2025). Capital returned as a share of average market capitalisation ran 17% / 27% / 23% / 17% / 9% across those five years. Q2 2026 alone: $2.8 billion of total capital returned including $2.5 billion of buybacks, with $6.1 billion of authorisation remaining and 283 million shares outstanding at June 30. Understand what this does to the per-share numbers: if the share count had stayed at 638 million, the same Q2 2026 net income would have printed roughly $8 per share instead of $17.73. Half of the earnings-per-share story of the last five years is arithmetic, not operations — which is not a criticism, it is simply what you are buying.

Renewable diesel flipped sign. The segment swung from a $19M loss in Q2 2025 to $258M of adjusted EBITDA in Q2 2026, and from a $61M H1 loss to $296M. Management credits margins, throughput and regulatory credit values — the last of which is a policy variable, and therefore a recurring item only for as long as the policy holds.

MPLX growth capital stepped up. MPLX raised its 2026 growth capital outlook by $500 million to $2.9 billion, over 90% aimed at natural gas and NGL infrastructure, reflecting accelerated Gulf Coast fractionation work. That is a deliberate reinvestment decision that reduces near-term distributable cash in exchange for the 12.5% distribution growth guided through 2027.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and on a cyclical like this one, a stretched RSI at a cycle peak has historically been a better warning than any multiple.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $65.93 to the September 4, 2026 close of $388.90, MPC returned +489.9% on price alone — a compound annual rate of about 42.6%, before roughly $17 per share of dividends paid over the period. That is an extraordinary five years for what is nominally a mature commodity processor, and it happened in three distinct acts. Act one, Oct 2021 to Mar 2024: the post-COVID refining supercycle took the stock from $65.93 to $201.50, a 206% move, as global refining capacity that shut during the pandemic failed to come back and cracks blew out. Act two, Mar 2024 to Apr 2025: a −31.8% drawdown to $137.41 as cracks normalised and FY2024 net income fell to $3.44B from $9.67B. Act three, Apr 2025 to today: +183%, of which the most violent part is the last three months — +52% from the June 2026 close of $255.67 as Strait of Hormuz supply disruption and Middle East and Russian refining outages blew the crack spread wide open again. The stock now sits within 2.4% of its 52-week high of $398.53 and 96% of the way up its 52-week range.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

MPC is the largest US refiner (2.87 mbpd across 12 plants) in an industry where nobody builds new capacity, running at 94–95% utilisation with 112% margin capture, and it is generating $12.90B of trailing free cash flow. Its capital return is structurally protected: ~$2.79B a year of fee-based MPLX distributions covers the $1.13B dividend 2.5× and the dividend plus the entire $1.5B standalone capital budget with cash to spare, growing at a guided 12.5% a year through 2027. Meanwhile the buyback has retired 52% of the share count in five years on ~$41B of repurchases, with $6.1B of authorisation left. At 8.1× forward earnings with a 13.9% payout ratio and 18.1% ROIC, an investor is not paying a premium for a business that has already proven it can pay shareholders through the trough.

Bear Case

Every dollar of the 2026 story is a crack spread that MPC does not control. The realised margin of $36.33/bbl is $18.75 above last year's $17.58, and at $1,125M of annual R&M EBITDA per dollar of blended crack, mean reversion to last year's level erases roughly $21B of annualised segment EBITDA. The proximate cause is Strait of Hormuz supply disruption plus Middle East and Russian refinery outages — temporary events by construction. The stock is at 96% of its 52-week range after +117% in twelve months, the consensus price target is 16% below the market, and even the panel's highest target is only 6% above. Debt-to-equity is 1.33 with $34.29B of debt. The trailing P/E of 13.5× looks cheap precisely because earnings are at a cycle peak — which is historically when refiners look cheapest and are most dangerous.

Top 3 Risks

01
Crack spread mean reversion. The single dominant variable. A return to the Q2 2025 realised margin of $17.58/bbl would, on the company's own $1,125M-per-dollar sensitivity, remove roughly $21 billion of annualised R&M EBITDA. Nothing in operations, capital allocation or the MPLX structure offsets that; it only cushions it.
02
Paying a cyclical peak multiple on peak earnings. At $388.90 the stock sits within 2.4% of its 52-week high having doubled in a year, while the sell-side consensus target sits 16% lower. The forward P/E of 8.1× is computed on consensus 2026 EPS of $50.95 — a figure that assumes the disruption-driven margin largely persists. If it does not, both the E and the multiple compress at the same time.
03
Leverage and policy exposure into a downturn. Consolidated debt-to-equity of 1.33 and $34.29B of total debt against $7.77B of cash means the balance sheet is not a shock absorber. Layer on West Coast regulatory exposure (540 mbpd, highest realised margin at $41.28/bbl) and a renewable diesel segment whose freshly positive $258M quarter was credited in part to regulatory credit values, and a meaningful slice of the earnings base is set by policymakers rather than by markets.
Industry Commentary
Geopolitical Supply Disruption
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2026 refining boom has a named cause. Contemporaneous coverage of the August 4 print attributed MPC's $5.1B quarter to "higher crack spreads driven by Strait of Hormuz supply disruptions," and management on the earnings call cited tight global inventories and refining outages in the Middle East and Russia as the reason it expects the environment to stay constructive "well into 2027." The mechanical result: system realised margin of $36.33/bbl versus $17.58 a year earlier, West Coast at $41.28. This is the highest-beta variable in the entire report and it is exogenous — no operational decision at Findlay moves it in either direction.
Structural Capacity Scarcity
EMERGING TAILWIND
The durable half of the refining thesis is that the capacity base is not growing. MPC's own planning materials project that anticipated closures nearly offset announced capacity additions globally through 2030, and that refined product demand growth exceeds net capacity additions over 2026–2030. In the US specifically the direction has been closures, not builds — the widely cited recent examples being LyondellBasell's Houston refinery and Phillips 66's Los Angeles refinery, neither of which could be verified from the sources fetched for this report and which are therefore flagged as unverified context rather than asserted. What is verified is MPC's utilisation advantage: 95% in 2025 against a 92% industry average. Scarce assets run harder, and MPC runs its harder than most.
Midstream vs Refining — Bull/Bear Frame
ONGOING DEBATE
The whole argument reduces to one comparison. In the trough quarter of Q2 2025, Midstream was $1,641M of $3,286M total adjusted EBITDA — 50% of the company. In the peak quarter of Q2 2026, it was $1,778M of $8,460M — 21%. The midstream business barely changed; refining quadrupled. The bull reads that as a floor: MPLX's ~$2.79B annualised distribution to MPC covers the dividend 2.5× and grows 12.5% a year regardless of cracks, so the downside is bounded and the refining earnings are a free option. The bear reads the same numbers as a warning: 79% of today's EBITDA comes from a spread that was less than half as wide twelve months ago, and buying at 96% of the 52-week range means paying for the option at its most expensive. Both readings are correct about the arithmetic; they differ only on how long the Strait of Hormuz stays disrupted.
