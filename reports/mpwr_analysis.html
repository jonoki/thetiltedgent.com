01 Company Overview

Monolithic Power Systems (MPS) designs power-management semiconductors — the DC-to-DC converters, voltage regulators and, increasingly, complete power modules that take the raw electricity coming into a server rack, a car or a phone and deliver it, cleanly and at exactly the right voltage, to the chip that needs it. It is fabless: MPS owns its proprietary process technology but rents manufacturing capacity from third-party foundries. FY2025 revenue was $2.79B, up 26.4% from $2.21B, and the trailing twelve months through September 2026 reached $3.74B, up 40.5%. At a $64.78B market value (48.77M shares on the Oct 27, 2026 10-Q cover × the Nov 3, 2026 close) it is mid-sized by semiconductor standards, but investors pay about 73 times its last twelve months of profit for it.

The debate on this page is specific. In late 2024 MPS was widely reported to have lost share on the voltage regulators for Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs, and the stock fell roughly 39% from its September 2024 monthly close to its November 2024 close. The company's Enterprise Data revenue then declined in FY2025 — $701.8M versus $716.3M — while every other end market grew 26–46%. The question now is whether the Q3 2026 print — the first quarter above $1 billion of revenue, with Enterprise Data up 165% year over year to $506.7M and management lifting its Enterprise Data growth floor from 130% to 150% — marks a lasting recovery built on vertical power modules and a broader customer base, or another single-platform surge that a customer can design out again. The stock closed November 3 22.5% below its 52-week intraday high of $1,714.09 (May 26, 2026) even though revenue growth sped up in each quarter since, so the market is not yet convinced.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
PROPRIETARY BCD PROCESS ON RENTED FABS MULTI-FOUNDRY, MULTI-GEOGRAPHY SUPPLY POWER-DENSITY LEADERSHIP VERTICAL POWER MODULE KNOW-HOW 55% GROSS MARGIN THROUGH THE CYCLE NET CASH $1.1B, NO DEBT 1,500+ NEW AUTO SOCKETS IN 1H 2026

The moat is an unusual combination: MPS behaves like an integrated device manufacturer on process technology and like a fabless chip designer on capital. Its BCD process (Bipolar-CMOS-DMOS — a way of building precision analog, digital logic and high-voltage power transistors on the same die) is proprietary and runs at third-party foundries, which means MPS gets differentiated silicon without owning a fab, and can qualify additional foundries and assembly partners in different countries when a customer asks. On Sep 9, 2026 GlobalFoundries and MPS announced a long-term agreement to run MPS's process at GF's 300mm fab in Singapore, with volume production in 2027 (GF release), and on the Q3 call management said capacity had passed $6 billion of annual revenue support, with $8 billion targeted by the second half of 2027. The commercial expression of that is power density: more amps per square millimetre lets the same board deliver more current to a hotter chip, which is the whole problem in AI servers. The move from selling chips to selling assembled 3D modules — which the CEO said is "more complicated than to assemble a phone, even" (Q2 call) — raises the know-how barrier and the content per socket at the same time. The financial fingerprint is a gross margin that has sat between 55.2% and 58.4% every year from FY2021 through the TTM, through a 2023 growth stall, a 2025 share loss and a 2026 boom. Stable gross margin during a mix whiplash like that is what pricing power looks like in a semiconductor company.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Platform concentration — the Blackwell lesson. The moat did not stop MPS losing share on a single Nvidia platform in 2024; Enterprise Data revenue fell 2% in FY2025 as a direct result and the stock fell 39% in two months. The CEO said on the Q2 call, "We don't have any concentrated customers." That is a claim about the customer list, not about the platform list — if a large fraction of the 164% growth rides on one or two accelerator generations, the exposure is the same as before, just less visible. The 10-Q names only the distributors that each take more than 10% of sales (the largest, 32% in Q3 2026), not the end customers or platforms behind them, so the market cannot check.
▸
Competitive intensity from larger, vertically integrated rivals. Infineon, Renesas, Analog Devices and Texas Instruments all sell into the same sockets with far larger balance sheets and, in Infineon's and TI's case, their own fabs; Vicor competes directly in high-density factorised power for AI. ADI's trailing revenue is up 33.6% and TI's 20.3% — demand is lifting everyone, so MPS's edge has to be re-won every generation.
▸
Technology transitions MPS did not lead. On the Q2 call the CEO said of GaN (gallium nitride — a material that switches faster and more efficiently than silicon in power stages): "it's up to a point where we can't ignore GaN." On the Q3 call MPS said it had begun sampling GaN-based solutions for 800-volt data-center designs, with 800-volt revenue expected around late 2027 and 2028. An 800V rack architecture shift is exactly the kind of discontinuity that reshuffles power suppliers.
▸
Execution on a self-built module supply chain, with a new CFO. Moving from chips to 3D modules means qualifying new back-end assembly vendors and buying equipment; inventory rose to $764.9M at Sep 30, 2026 (about 131 days of cost of sales), and free cash flow for the twelve months to September was $609.4M against $666.2M in FY2025 as capital spending rose to $263.6M from $172.0M. Tony Balow became CFO on Oct 27, 2026 after eight months with an interim in the seat — a company more than doubling a business line in a year now has a CFO in his first weeks.

Notable One-Off Items

The FY2024 net income of $1,592M is not comparable to anything. Q4 2024 GAAP net income was $1,254.7M on $163.3M of operating income, lifted by a deferred tax benefit of roughly $1.1B, net, tied to a foreign subsidiary's ten-year tax incentive (FY2025 10-K; the restatement below cut it by $194.6M). It pushed FY2024 diluted EPS to $32.60 and a 72% net margin. Any "earnings down" headline for FY2025 (GAAP EPS −61%, $32.60 to $12.86) or the TTM is an artefact of that base; without the benefit, FY2024 net income would have been about $0.5B, roughly $10 a share ($1,592.1M − ~$1.1B over about 48.8M diluted shares), making FY2025's $12.86 GAAP EPS growth, not decline. Use Q3 2026's +47% GAAP EPS growth (+73% non-GAAP) and the FY2025 non-GAAP EPS of $17.77 as the clean trend lines.

GAAP vs non-GAAP gap. Q3 2026 GAAP EPS $5.51 versus non-GAAP $8.19; GAAP operating income $357.7M versus non-GAAP $464.5M. Of the ~$107M difference, $106.3M is stock-based compensation and related expenses (paying staff in shares). That is about 8.8% of revenue — real dilution: diluted shares were 49.18M in Q3 against 48.04M a year earlier (+2.4%).

Buybacks stepped up. The authorisation was doubled to $1B on Jul 30, 2026; MPS then bought back $453.4M of stock in Q3 (against $4.0M in Q2), and in October the board added another $500M, taking the remaining authorisation back to $1B. Over the past year repurchases have still not offset share issuance (stockanalysis.com buyback yield −2.23%). Dividend raised 28% to $2.00/quarter in February 2026. Restatement: on Feb 27, 2026 MPS said (8-K, Item 4.02) that its FY2024 statements and 2025 quarterly statements could not be relied on because of an error in deferred taxes on a foreign tax incentive; the FY2025 10-K restated FY2024 diluted EPS from $36.59 to $32.60 and put FY2025 at $12.86, not the $12.75 in the Feb 5 release. Revenue and non-GAAP figures were unaffected. This page uses the restated figures.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 month-end close of $553.46 to the November 3, 2026 close of $1,328.30, MPWR returned +140.0% in price terms (closing prices, dividends excluded) over roughly 4.9 years — a compound annual rate of about 19.5% before a dividend that has run at well under 1%. The path was anything but smooth. The stock lost 38.7% from its November 2021 close ($553.46) to the October 2022 low ($339.45), nearly tripled to $934.68 by August 2024 as the first AI-server wave lifted power suppliers, then gave up 38.6% in two months (Sep → Nov 2024) on the Blackwell share-loss reports. From that April close it is down 17.7% to the Nov 3 close, after an 11% August drop following a quarter in which revenue grew 48%, a September rebound to $1,347.22 and a 10.1% October fall to $1,211.63 (it was down 11.0% at the Oct 29 close, before the Q3 release). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the premise and check it against the numbers. The standing story says MPS lost Blackwell voltage-regulator share in late 2024 and the stock fell ~40%. The data supports that: the Sep → Nov 2024 monthly drawdown was 38.6%, and FY2025 Enterprise Data revenue fell 2.0% to $701.8M while the rest of the company grew 26–46%. The story then asks whether Enterprise Data has re-accelerated. It has, unambiguously: $144.0M (Q2'25) → $233.5M (Q4'25) → $380.6M (Q2'26) → $506.7M (Q3'26), and a full-year floor of +150% means at least ~$1.75B in FY2026 ($701.8M × 2.5), two and a half times the year before. The company did not attribute it to "Rubin-era wins": it named no platform and no customer; it talked about module content per platform, CPU refreshes, hyperscaler accelerators and a broad customer list. Treat the Rubin framing as market inference, not company disclosure.

Thread 1 — the diversification actually happened. This is the part of the thesis that is finished rather than promised. In FY2025, the year the AI line shrank, Storage & Computing grew 46%, Automotive 43%, Communications 37%, Industrial 35% and Consumer 26%. Total revenue grew 26%. A power-semiconductor company that can grow revenue by a quarter with its marquee segment going backwards has a real second engine. The irony is that by Q3 2026 the mix has swung back: Enterprise Data plus Communications — both AI-driven — are now 58% of revenue, up from 36% for FY2025, and supplied about 91% of the year-over-year revenue increase in Q3. The company is more AI-concentrated today than it was when the market punished it for AI concentration.

Thread 2 — modules change the economics and the risk. Selling an assembled vertical power module instead of a regulator chip multiplies content per socket and raises the switching cost for the customer — you cannot swap a module supplier the way you can second-source a chip. That is the bull argument for why this recovery is stickier than the Hopper-era position was. It also means MPS is now running a manufacturing-adjacent business: new back-end assembly partners, new equipment, a new foundry in Singapore, capacity above $6B of annual revenue support heading for $8B, inventory at about 131 days, and free cash flow that fell to $609.4M in the twelve months to September while net income rose. Gross margin has held at about 55%, so the module transition has not lowered margins so far — that is the number to watch.

Thread 3 — valuation, and which earnings base. On trailing GAAP EPS of $18.15 the stock is 73.2×. On a derived trailing non-GAAP EPS of ~$24.58 it is ~54×. On FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $29.24 it is 45.4×, and on FY2027's $46.34, 28.7×. Free cash flow is the harsh one: ~106× price to free cash flow, because capex is elevated. Peers trade at 40.5× (TXN) and 49.9× (ADI) trailing earnings while growing 20% and 34% respectively; MPS grew 63% in Q3 and its Q4 guide implies about 80%. Whether ~29× next year's consensus earnings is expensive depends on whether the 51% revenue growth analysts expect for 2027 arrives — and management declined to give a 2027 figure on the Q3 call.

Bull Case

Enterprise Data has re-accelerated on a structurally better product — modules, not chips — with the CEO on record that MPS has no concentrated customers. Communications is a second AI market growing 135%. Automotive has 1,500+ new sockets shipped this year with 5–7 year lives and first 48-volt orders. Gross margin has held at 55% through a share loss and a boom, ROIC is 33%, there is $1.09B of cash and no debt even after $453M of buybacks in one quarter, and a fabless model with a new GlobalFoundries site lets capacity scale without owning a fab. At about 29× 2027 consensus EPS with revenue guided up about 80% next quarter, a price 22.5% below the May high is an entry, not a warning, and every brokerage in the ratings tables rates it Buy or better, with a $1,821 average target.

Bear Case

The stock fell 39% the last time a single AI platform went the other way, and today the company is more AI-dependent (58% of Q3 revenue) than it was then. Having no concentrated customers is not the same as having no concentrated platforms, and the company does not disclose the difference. Infineon, TI, ADI and Vicor are all chasing the same sockets; the CEO said in July that MPS "can't ignore GaN." Storage & Computing is flat and Consumer is down 11.6%, free cash flow fell as capex rose, inventory is about 131 days of cost of sales, and the CFO is new in the seat. At 73× GAAP earnings and ~106× free cash flow, a stock that dropped 11% after a 48%-growth quarter and 10% in the month it reported a 63%-growth one has no room for a disappointment.

Top 3 Risks

01
Platform-cycle concentration in Enterprise Data. A 150%-plus growth year built on one or two accelerator generations creates a 2027 comparison problem and a design-out risk simultaneously. FY2025 showed the downside case is real (−2% in a year the market grew), and the company gives investors no customer- or platform-level disclosure to size it.
02
A falling price without a miss. The stock is 22.5% below its high while revenue and earnings kept rising. At 73× trailing GAAP and 29× FY2027 consensus earnings, the price already assumes a lot of 2027 growth; if growth slows below the 51% analysts expect, investors can pay less for each dollar of profit even as profit still rises.
03
Execution on the module supply chain and technology transitions. Building 3D module assembly capacity, qualifying GaN and silicon-carbide devices for 800V architectures, and ramping DDR5 controllers are all new competencies being added at once, with $764.9M of inventory on the balance sheet and a CFO appointed on Oct 27, 2026. Any of them slipping would show up first in gross margin and inventory days.
  Industry Commentary
AI Power Delivery Is a Winner-Take-Most Socket Fight
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every AI accelerator generation re-opens the power-stage socket, and the decision is made by a handful of chip designers and hyperscalers. MPS's own history is the case study: Enterprise Data revenue $716.3M (FY2024) → $701.8M (FY2025) → a run-rate above $2.0B (Q3 2026 × 4) — nearly three times FY2025 within nine months. Infineon, Renesas, ADI, TI and Vicor are all bidding for the same sockets, and the shift to 800V rack architectures and vertical power modules will re-run the contest again. For the whole sub-sector, this makes reported growth rates far less predictive than they look.
The Non-AI Analog Cycle Is Still Uneven
ONGOING HEADWIND
Strip out AI and the analog/power market is recovering slowly. MPS's Consumer revenue fell 11.6% in Q3 2026 and Storage & Computing grew 3.0%, though Automotive picked up to 19.2% and Industrial to 29.0%. Texas Instruments — the broadest company in the sector — grew 20.3% over its last twelve months, respectable but a fraction of the AI-exposed lines. For MPS this matters because the diversified base that carried FY2025 is now the part growing slowest: about 91% of Q3's year-over-year revenue increase came from Enterprise Data and Communications.
Power Density Is Becoming the Binding Constraint in AI Infrastructure
EMERGING TAILWIND
As accelerator power draw climbs, the limiting factor shifts from the chip to how much current can be delivered into it within the board area available — which is why vertical power modules, optical-module power and 800V distribution are all growing faster than the underlying server count. ADI (+33.6% trailing revenue) and Vicor (+41.4% trailing revenue; shares up 97% over 52 weeks, stockanalysis.com) are seeing the same pull. Bull framing: MPS's whole identity is power density, its module content per platform is rising, and every step up in accelerator wattage expands its dollar content. Bear framing: the same tailwind is funding every competitor's R&D, the CEO said in July that MPS can no longer ignore GaN, and a power-density leader that lost a socket in 2024 can lose one in 2027 — at a much higher multiple.
