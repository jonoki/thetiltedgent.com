01 Company Overview

Monolithic Power Systems (MPS) designs power-management semiconductors — the DC-to-DC converters, voltage regulators and, increasingly, complete power modules that take the raw electricity coming into a server rack, a car or a phone and deliver it, cleanly and at exactly the right voltage, to the chip that needs it. It is fabless: MPS owns its proprietary process technology but rents manufacturing capacity from third-party foundries. FY2025 revenue was $2.79B, up 26.4% from $2.21B, and the trailing twelve months through June 2026 reached $3.27B, up 28.7%. At a $60.14B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #426 globally — a mid-cap by semiconductor standards, but one that trades on a large-cap multiple.

The debate on this page is specific. In late 2024 MPS was widely reported to have lost share on the voltage regulators for Nvidia's Blackwell GPUs, and the stock fell roughly 39% from its September 2024 monthly close to its November 2024 close. The company's Enterprise Data revenue then declined in FY2025 — $701.8M versus $716.3M — while every other end market grew 26–46%. The question now is whether the Q2 2026 print, in which Enterprise Data revenue rose 164% year over year to $380.6M and management lifted its full-year Enterprise Data growth floor to 130%, marks a durable re-acceleration built on vertical power modules and a broader customer base — or another single-platform surge that can be designed out again. The stock has already fallen 28.6% from its April 2026 intraday high of $1,714.09 while the fundamentals kept improving, so the market is not yet convinced.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
PROPRIETARY BCD PROCESS ON RENTED FABS MULTI-FOUNDRY, MULTI-GEOGRAPHY SUPPLY POWER-DENSITY LEADERSHIP VERTICAL POWER MODULE KNOW-HOW 55% GROSS MARGIN THROUGH THE CYCLE NET CASH $1.4B, ZERO DEBT 1,500+ AUTO SOCKETS / YEAR

The moat is an unusual combination: MPS behaves like an integrated device manufacturer on process technology and like a fabless chip designer on capital. Its BCD process (Bipolar-CMOS-DMOS — a way of building precision analog, digital logic and high-voltage power transistors on the same die) is proprietary and runs on mature 65-nanometer nodes at third-party foundries, which means MPS gets differentiated silicon without owning a fab, and can qualify additional foundries and assembly partners in different countries when a customer asks — a point management stressed on the Q2 call as it builds capacity "beyond $6 billion." The commercial expression of that is power density: more amps per square millimetre lets the same board deliver more current to a hotter chip, which is the whole problem in AI servers. The move from selling chips to selling assembled 3D modules — which the CEO compared to phone assembly, "but more challenging" — raises the know-how barrier and the content per socket at the same time. The financial fingerprint is a gross margin that has sat between 55.1% and 58.4% every year from FY2021 through the TTM, through a 2023 growth stall, a 2025 share loss and a 2026 boom. Stable gross margin during a mix whiplash like that is what pricing power looks like in a semiconductor company.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Platform concentration — the Blackwell lesson. The moat did not stop MPS losing share on a single Nvidia platform in 2024; Enterprise Data revenue fell 2% in FY2025 as a direct result and the stock fell 39% in two months. Management now says there are "no concentrated customers" in Enterprise Data. That is a claim about the customer list, not about the platform list — if a large fraction of the 164% growth rides on one or two accelerator generations, the exposure is the same as before, just less visible. The company does not disclose a 10%-customer breakdown quarterly, so the market cannot check.
▸
Competitive intensity from larger, vertically integrated rivals. Infineon, Renesas, Analog Devices and Texas Instruments all sell into the same sockets with far larger balance sheets and, in Infineon's and TI's case, their own fabs; Vicor competes directly in high-density factorised power for AI. ADI's revenue is growing 33.6% and TI's 16.7% on AI data-center demand — the same tailwind is lifting everyone, so MPS's edge has to be re-won every generation.
▸
Technology transitions MPS did not lead. On the Q2 call the CEO conceded on GaN (gallium nitride — a wide-bandgap material that switches faster and more efficiently than silicon in power stages): "I think I was proven wrong. But it is up to a point where we cannot ignore GaN." MPS now has working proprietary GaN devices and relies on silicon carbide for 800-volt data-center distribution, but both are catch-up positions against Infineon and others who invested years earlier. An 800V rack architecture shift is exactly the kind of discontinuity that reshuffles power suppliers.
▸
Execution on a self-built module supply chain, mid-transition in the CFO chair. Moving from chips to 3D modules means qualifying new back-end assembly vendors and buying equipment; inventory already sits at $675.8M (140 days) and FCF fell to $585.5M TTM from $666.2M in FY2025 as capex rose. This is happening with an interim CFO after the retirement of a ten-year incumbent — competent hands, but a thinner bench for a company tripling a business line in a year.

Notable One-Off Items

The FY2024 net income of $1,592M is not comparable to anything. Q4 2024 GAAP net income was $1,255.0M on $163.3M of operating income — a roughly $1.1B non-operating, one-time item that stockanalysis.com classifies as a one-time gain (the underlying nature — a non-cash tax-related benefit — was not re-verified from a primary filing here). It pushed FY2024 diluted EPS to $32.60 and a 72% net margin. Every "earnings down 52–61%" headline for FY2025 and the TTM is an artefact of that base; normalised FY2024 EPS from the other three quarters plus a typical Q4 would have been roughly $10–11, making FY2025's $12.75 GAAP EPS growth, not decline. Use Q2 2026's +86% GAAP EPS growth and the FY2025 non-GAAP EPS of $17.77 as the clean trend lines.

GAAP vs non-GAAP gap. Q2 2026 GAAP EPS $5.22 versus non-GAAP $6.50; GAAP operating income $303.9M versus non-GAAP $367.7M. The ~$64M quarterly difference is overwhelmingly stock-based compensation, plus amortisation. That is about 6.5% of revenue — real dilution, and the reason the share count is rising (+1.15% YoY) despite buybacks.

Buyback authorisation doubled to $1B on Jul 30, 2026 (an additional $500M). Buyback yield over the past year was −1.15% — i.e., repurchases have so far not offset issuance. Dividend raised 28% to $2.00/quarter in February 2026. Small EPS discrepancies: the company's release shows Q4 2025 GAAP EPS of $3.46 and FY2025 of $12.75; stockanalysis.com shows $3.57 and $12.86 — the company's figures are used in prose here.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $525.46 to the September 4, 2026 close of $1,223.86, MPWR returned +132.9% in price terms over roughly 4.9 years — a compound annual rate of about 18.7% before a dividend that has run at well under 1%. The path was anything but smooth. The stock lost 38.7% from its November 2021 close ($553.46) to the October 2022 low ($339.45), nearly tripled to $934.68 by August 2024 as the first AI-server wave lifted power suppliers, then gave up 38.6% in two months (Sep → Nov 2024) on the Blackwell share-loss reports. It spent the next year rebuilding, and in April 2026 alone rose 47.7% — from $1,093.35 to $1,614.41 — when Q1 results and an 85% Enterprise Data growth floor demolished the "designed out" narrative. From there it has fallen 24.2% to today, including an 11% August drop after a quarter in which revenue grew 48% and guidance rose again. A NASDAQ-100 or S&P 500 total-return comparison over the identical window was not verified from the sources fetched and is deliberately not stated; what can be said is that the 52-week gain of +48.0% is well above what either index would typically deliver in a year, and that the stock's 1.66 beta and this chart agree with each other.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the premise and check it against the numbers. The standing story says MPS lost Blackwell voltage-regulator share in late 2024 and the stock fell ~40%. The data supports that: the Sep → Nov 2024 monthly drawdown was 38.6%, and FY2025 Enterprise Data revenue fell 2.0% to $701.8M while the rest of the company grew 26–46%. The story then asks whether Enterprise Data has re-accelerated. It has, unambiguously: $144.0M (Q2'25) → $233.5M (Q4'25) → $380.6M (Q2'26), and a full-year floor of +130% implies at least ~$1.61B in FY2026 (derived), more than double the year before. What the sources fetched do not support is the specific attribution to "Rubin-era wins" — the company named no platform and no customer; it talked about module content per platform, CPU refreshes, hyperscaler accelerators and a broad customer list. Treat the Rubin framing as market inference, not company disclosure.

Thread 1 — the diversification actually happened. This is the part of the thesis that is finished rather than promised. In FY2025, the year the AI line shrank, Storage & Computing grew 46%, Automotive 43%, Communications 37%, Industrial 35% and Consumer 26%. Total revenue grew 26%. A power-semiconductor company that can grow a quarter with its marquee segment going backwards has a real second engine. The irony is that by Q2 2026 the mix has swung back: Enterprise Data plus Communications — both AI-driven — are now 52% of revenue, up from 36% for FY2025. The company is more AI-concentrated today than it was when the market punished it for AI concentration.

Thread 2 — modules change the economics and the risk. Selling an assembled vertical power module instead of a regulator chip multiplies content per socket and raises the switching cost for the customer — you cannot swap a module supplier the way you can second-source a chip. That is the bull argument for why this recovery is stickier than the Hopper-era position was. It also means MPS is now running a manufacturing-adjacent business: new back-end assembly partners, new equipment, capacity being built "beyond $6B," inventory at 140 days, and free cash flow that fell to $585.5M TTM while net income rose. Gross margin has held at 55.2%, so the module transition is not (yet) dilutive — that is the number to watch.

Thread 3 — valuation, and which earnings base. On trailing GAAP EPS of $16.39 the stock is 74.7×. On a derived trailing non-GAAP EPS of ~$21.12 it is ~58×. On FY2026 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $27.43 it is 44.6×, and on FY2027's $34.80, 35.2×. Free cash flow is the harsh one: 102.6× P/FCF, because capex is elevated. Verified peers trade at 38.6× (TXN) and 42.3× (ADI) trailing earnings while growing 17% and 34% respectively; MPS is growing 48% and guiding to 56%. Whether ~45× next year's earnings is expensive depends entirely on whether 2027 growth of 28% is a floor or a ceiling — and management has explicitly declined to say.

Bull Case

Enterprise Data has re-accelerated on a structurally better product — modules, not chips — with management on record that there is no concentrated customer and CPU server share above 30%. Communications is a second AI vector growing 78%. Automotive has 1,500+ new sockets shipped this year with 5–7 year lives. Gross margin has held at 55% through a share loss and a boom, ROIC is 32%, there is $1.41B of cash and no debt, and a fabless multi-foundry model lets capacity scale without a fab. At 35× 2027 EPS with revenue guided +56% next quarter, the 29% drawdown from April is an entry, not a warning, and the sell side's $1,840 average target says so unanimously.

Bear Case

The stock fell 39% the last time a single AI platform went the other way, and today the company is more AI-dependent (52% of revenue) than it was then. "No concentrated customers" is not the same as "no concentrated platforms," and the company does not disclose the difference. Infineon, TI, ADI and Vicor are all chasing the same sockets; the CEO admitted being "proven wrong" on GaN. Storage, Consumer and notebooks are flat to down, FCF is falling as capex rises, inventory is at 140 days, and the CFO seat is interim. At 74× GAAP earnings and 103× FCF, a stock that dropped 11% on a 48%-growth quarter has already told you the multiple has no room for a second disappointment.

Top 3 Risks

01
Platform-cycle concentration in Enterprise Data. A 130% growth year built on one or two accelerator generations creates a 2027 comparison problem and a design-out risk simultaneously. FY2025 showed the downside case is real (−2% in a year the market grew), and the company gives investors no customer- or platform-level disclosure to size it.
02
Multiple compression without a fundamental miss. The stock is down 28.6% from its high while every reported number improved. At 74× trailing GAAP and 45× FY2026 consensus, the market is already discounting a lot of 2027; if growth "only" decelerates to the 28% consensus, the multiple can fall further with earnings still rising.
03
Execution on the module supply chain and technology transitions. Building 3D module assembly capacity, qualifying GaN and silicon-carbide devices for 800V architectures, and ramping DDR5 controllers are all new competencies being added at once, with $675.8M of inventory on the balance sheet and an interim CFO. Any of them slipping would show up first in gross margin and inventory days.
  Industry Commentary
AI Power Delivery Is a Winner-Take-Most Socket Fight
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every AI accelerator generation re-opens the power-stage socket, and the decision is made by a handful of chip designers and hyperscalers. MPS's own history is the case study: Enterprise Data revenue $716.3M (FY2024) → $701.8M (FY2025) → a run-rate above $1.5B (Q2 2026 × 4) — a swing of more than 2× in six quarters driven by platform positioning rather than end demand. Infineon, Renesas, ADI, TI and Vicor are all bidding for the same sockets, and the shift to 800V rack architectures and vertical power modules will re-run the contest again. For the whole sub-sector, this makes reported growth rates far less predictive than they look.
The Non-AI Analog Cycle Is Still Uneven
ONGOING HEADWIND
Strip out AI and the analog/power market is recovering slowly. MPS's Consumer revenue fell 4.8% in Q2 2026, Storage & Computing grew 2.3%, Automotive slowed to 8.2%, and management called notebooks, storage and consumer "cautious" for the second half. Texas Instruments — the sector's broadest bellwether — is growing 16.7% TTM, respectable but a fraction of the AI-exposed lines. For MPS this matters because the diversified base that carried FY2025 is now the part growing slowest; the company's 48% growth is almost entirely two end markets.
Power Density Is Becoming the Binding Constraint in AI Infrastructure
EMERGING TAILWIND
As accelerator power draw climbs, the limiting factor shifts from the chip to how much current can be delivered into it within the board area available — which is why vertical power modules, optical-module power and 800V distribution are all growing faster than the underlying server count. ADI (+33.6% revenue) and Vicor (+25%, market cap up 279% in a year) are seeing the same pull. Bull framing: MPS's whole identity is power density, its module content per platform is rising, and every step up in accelerator wattage expands its dollar content. Bear framing: the same tailwind is funding every competitor's R&D, the company itself concedes it was late on GaN, and a power-density leader that lost a socket in 2024 can lose one in 2027 — at a much higher multiple.
