01 Company Overview

Merck & Co. (known as MSD outside the U.S. and Canada) is a research-intensive pharmaceutical company built on three pillars: oncology, human vaccines and animal health. Full-year 2025 revenue was $65.01B, up +1.3% YoY — a deliberately unflattering headline, because a ~$3B collapse in Gardasil (driven by China) masked Keytruda growing to roughly $31.9B and a step-change in newer launches. Q2 2026 revenue of $16.6B (+5%) and raised full-year guidance of $66.3–67.3B confirm the base business is re-accelerating.

One number defines this equity: Keytruda is roughly 49% of company revenue and loses U.S. exclusivity in 2028. Everything management has done since 2024 — the subcutaneous Keytruda Qlex conversion, Winrevair, the $10B Verona acquisition, the ~$9.2B Cidara deal, Terns, and the oral PCSK9 Lipfendra approval — is an attempt to build a post-2028 revenue base before the biosimilars arrive. The market has re-rated that effort violently: MRK bottomed near $77.58 in late summer 2025 and has since gained +67.6% over 52 weeks, closing at $132.92 on August 12, 2026. It is roughly the 48th-largest public company globally by market capitalization and the third-largest U.S.-listed pharmaceutical company behind Eli Lilly (~$1.08T) and Johnson & Johnson (~$630B).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Keytruda label breadth (~40 indications) Qlex subQ IP → 2040+ ~$18B annual R&D Global vaccine manufacturing scale Oncology trial infrastructure Animal Health + Allflex data platform 15 consecutive dividend increases

Merck's moat is unusually concentrated and unusually time-stamped. The strongest layer is the Keytruda clinical evidence base: roughly 40 approved indications supported by hundreds of trials, embedded in NCCN guidelines as standard of care across most solid tumours. Biosimilar molecules can copy the antibody; they cannot instantly copy 15 years of indication-by-indication evidence, oncologist familiarity or the combination data that make Keytruda the backbone partner for everyone else's new agent. The second layer is formulation and delivery — Keytruda Qlex converts an infusion-chair product into a two-minute injection with its own patent estate running past 2040, which is simultaneously a patient-convenience play, an infusion-capacity play for oncology practices, and a legal moat that biosimilar IV pembrolizumab cannot cross. The third is manufacturing and regulatory scale in vaccines, where cold-chain, multi-decade WHO prequalification and tender relationships create barriers that have nothing to do with patents. The fourth, and the one investors systematically ignore, is Animal Health: a ~$6.4B business with its own distribution, no U.S. pricing-policy exposure and, via Allflex, a livestock-monitoring installed base. What Merck conspicuously does not have is diversification — no immunology franchise of scale, no obesity asset, and a research organisation that despite ~$18B of annual R&D has been buying rather than originating its post-cliff pipeline: Acceleron ($11.5B), Prometheus ($10.8B), Verona ($10B), Cidara (~$9.2B), Terns.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The 2028 Keytruda cliff is the whole equity. At roughly $32B, Keytruda is ~49% of revenue and a far higher share of profit, and key U.S. composition-of-matter protection lapses in 2028 with Europe following around 2030. Amgen, Sandoz, Samsung Bioepis, Celltrion and others have biosimilars in late-stage development. Historic precedent for large-molecule LOE is a 50–80% revenue erosion within three years. Every element of the bull case — subQ conversion, Winrevair, Lipfendra, Cidara's CD388, tulisokibart, sac-TMT — is ultimately a claim about how much of a ~$15–20B hole can be pre-filled by 2030.

2. Serial acquisition has replaced internal discovery, at rising cost. Merck has taken enormous acquired IPR&D charges: $9.0B for Cidara and $5.7B for Terns in 2026 alone, which is why full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance collapsed from $5.04–5.16 to $2.66–2.76. These are real cash outflows for pre-revenue science. The strategy may work, but it converts a research company into a capital-allocation company, and each deal is underwritten at auction prices set by competitors facing their own cliffs.

3. Gardasil demonstrated how fast a franchise can break. An $8.58B product fell ~36% in a single year because one country's distributor inventory and consumer demand reversed. China exposure has not been rebuilt, domestic Chinese HPV vaccines are gaining share on price, and U.S. vaccine policy — ACIP composition, schedule changes, public messaging — is now a live variable rather than background noise. Vaccines are ~16% of revenue and were the direct cause of the 2024–25 de-rating.

4. Policy is a permanent operating parameter. Januvia's Medicare-negotiated price is already visible in a −31% quarter; Keytruda is expected in a subsequent IRA negotiation cycle, and the most-favored-nation pricing framework plus Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs make U.S. price and supply-chain economics subject to negotiation with the federal government. Merck's >$20B U.S. manufacturing commitment is partly a hedge against exactly this.

Notable One-Off Items

This issuer's GAAP financials are currently dominated by non-recurring items, and any ratio built on trailing net income is unusable without adjustment:

• Cidara Therapeutics acquisition — ~$9.0B acquired IPR&D charge (2026), for CD388, a long-acting influenza prophylactic. Booked as an expense because the transaction is treated as an asset acquisition; a $3.62/share hit to full-year EPS guidance.

• Terns Pharmaceuticals acquisition — $5.7B charge ($2.31/share), recognised in Q2 2026, for MK-4208 in chronic myeloid leukemia. This alone turned a profitable quarter into a reported GAAP loss of $(0.54)/share and a non-GAAP loss of $(0.13).

• Verona Pharma ($10B, closed late 2025) — purchase accounting, intangible amortisation on Ohtuvayre, plus a flagged H2 2026 channel-inventory adjustment that management has already warned will depress second-half revenue optics.

• Gardasil China inventory and demand reset (2025) — the shipment pause and distributor de-stocking produced a step-down of roughly $3B in a single year. The 2026 comparisons are therefore flattered by an easy base, not by underlying recovery in China.

• Restructuring — the multi-year cost programme announced in 2025 targets ~$3B of annual savings by 2027 and carries associated severance and site-exit charges through the period.

• Historical context: FY2023 GAAP EPS of $0.14 was similarly destroyed by the ~$10.2B Prometheus IPR&D charge. This is a recurring pattern of non-recurring items — which is itself a finding.

Five-Year Trajectory

MRK closed September 2021 at $75.14 and August 12, 2026 at $132.92 — a price gain of +76.9%, or roughly +100% on a total-return basis once ~$15.40/share of dividends are reinvested. Over the same window the S&P 500 rose from ~4,308 to roughly 7,830, about +82% on price and ~+93% total return. On a five-year view MRK therefore roughly matched the index — which conceals the fact that as recently as twelve months ago it was down 5% over four years while the index had nearly doubled.

The shape is what matters. Three regimes: a 2022–2023 defensive bid (from ~$76 to ~$111 as the market rotated into low-beta pharma), a brutal 21-month de-rating from the March 2024 high near $132 to the mid-2025 trough near $78 — a ~41% drawdown driven by the Gardasil China collapse and the market pricing the 2028 Keytruda cliff — and then a violent 2026 re-rating, with the stock up from $99.45 at the end of 2024 and $103.80 at the end of 2025 to $132.92, a +28% year-to-date move that has taken it back to the March 2024 all-time-high area. Note what did not drive it: FY2025 revenue grew just 1.3% and GAAP EPS is currently $1.27. This is almost entirely multiple expansion on a changed view of 2028 — the mirror image of the 2024–25 de-rating.

The 6-period monthly RSI now reads ~91 — one of the most overbought readings in the five-year series — after bottoming near 10 in mid-2025, an equally extreme oversold print. The price sits ~17% above its 10-month (≈200-day) moving average of ~$114 and ~2% above its 3-month average. Relative Performance

Against the S&P 500, MRK's five-year price gain of +76.9% is broadly in line with the index's ~+82%, but the correlation is close to non-existent — the 0.21 beta is real, and the two largest MRK moves of the period (the 2024–25 drawdown and the 2026 recovery) both occurred against a rising market. Against the pharmaceutical sector, MRK was a bottom-quartile performer from March 2024 through mid-2025 and has been a top-decile performer over the last twelve months, outrunning the sector's roughly +20% by a wide margin. The comparison that frames the current setup most usefully is AbbVie: both faced an existential single-product LOE, and AbbVie's stock re-rated before its earnings inflection arrived. MRK is attempting the same sequence, but with the actual LOE still two years out and unproven — which is why the low target in the analyst range sits 21% below the current price.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The 2028 cliff is being pre-filled in real time. Keytruda Qlex is already 5.5% of the franchise and accelerating on a permanent J-code, with IP past 2040; Winrevair is at a ~$2.4B run-rate growing 75%; Welireg +67% and Capvaxive +42%; Lipfendra brings the first oral PCSK9 into a primary-care market of enormous scale; and ~$25B of business development since 2024 has added CD388, MK-4208 and Ohtuvayre. At ~15.4× underlying earnings — a ~32% discount to the S&P 500 — with 73% gross margins, $16.1B of free cash flow, a 2.56% yield raised for 15 straight years and only 1.18% short interest, an investor is paying a mid-teens multiple for a company whose revenue guidance was just raised. Rob Davis's "more of a hill than a cliff" framing is now supported by data rather than assertion.

Bear Case

The stock has already re-rated 68% in twelve months on essentially no earnings improvement — FY2025 revenue grew 1.3%, TTM GAAP EPS is $1.27, and the average analyst target sits just 3% above the price with monthly RSI near 91. The core problem is unchanged: roughly $32B — 49% of revenue and more of profit — loses U.S. exclusivity in 2028, and every historic large-molecule LOE has produced 50–80% erosion within three years. Subcutaneous conversion at 30–40% still leaves a $15B+ hole; Gardasil has not recovered in China; Januvia is −31%; and the pipeline is being bought at auction with multi-billion IPR&D charges that have made GAAP earnings unusable for three of the last four years. The window for the market to re-price 2028 pessimistically has not closed — it has only been deferred.

  Industry Commentary
The three macro dynamics that matter most for Merck specifically, right now.
The 2028 Keytruda Cliff Is the Entire Investment Question
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
There is no way to own MRK without taking a view on this. Keytruda generated roughly $31.9B in 2025 and $8.4B in Q2 2026 — about 49% of company revenue and a materially higher share of operating profit — and key U.S. patent protection expires in 2028, with Europe following around 2030. The industry's own history is the base rate: Humira fell from $21.2B (2022) to $4.54B (2025), a −79% collapse; Remicade, Rituxan, Herceptin and Avastin all lost the majority of their revenue within three years of biosimilar entry. Merck's mitigation stack is more sophisticated than most: Keytruda Qlex, the subcutaneous version approved in September 2025, carries its own IP beyond 2040 and did $463M in Q2 2026 with a stated conversion goal of 30–40% of U.S. volume before biosimilars arrive; growth has shifted toward earlier-stage curative-intent settings where switching is stickier; and roughly $25B of business development since 2024 has bought CD388 (Cidara), MK-4208 (Terns) and Ohtuvayre (Verona). The arithmetic still binds. Even 40% conversion plus every launch hitting consensus peak sales leaves a gap in the low-teens of billions to fill by 2030 — which is why the shares trade at ~15.4× forward earnings while the index trades at ~22.5×. That gap is not an inefficiency; it is the price of the uncertainty.
U.S. Drug Pricing: MFN and IRA Are Now Fixed Costs of Doing Business
ONGOING HEADWIND
Two regimes now operate simultaneously and neither is going away. The Inflation Reduction Act's Medicare negotiation process delivered its first maximum fair prices effective January 1, 2026; Januvia/Janumet was a first-cycle selection and fell −31% to $258M in Q2 2026 as a direct result. Keytruda is widely expected in a subsequent negotiation cycle — which, uncomfortably, would bite at almost exactly the moment biosimilars arrive. Separately, the administration's most-favored-nation framework seeks to benchmark U.S. prices to the lowest developed-market price, and Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs threaten import economics for any company manufacturing offshore. Merck's response has been to commit more than $20B to U.S. manufacturing — including new sites in Virginia and North Carolina — trading capital intensity for policy insulation. The structural implication for the sector is that terminal margin assumptions have to come down: U.S. pricing is now negotiated with the federal government rather than with payers alone, and that is the principal reason large-cap pharma multiples sit in the mid-teens rather than the low-twenties.
The Industry-Wide LOE Wave and the M&A Auction — Bull vs. Bear Framing
EMERGING TAILWIND
Merck is not alone at this cliff. Bristol Myers (Opdivo, Eliquis), Pfizer (Eliquis, Ibrance), Johnson & Johnson (Stelara, already gone) and Amgen all face major exclusivity losses in the 2026–2030 window, which has produced the most aggressive licensing and acquisition environment since 2019 — and, crucially, a wave of Chinese-originated assets being in-licensed at a fraction of Western prices.

The bull framing: Merck enters this auction with ~$20B of annual operating cash flow, investment-grade capacity and a demonstrated willingness to move fast — Verona, Cidara and Terns inside eighteen months. It has already secured a first-in-class oral PCSK9, a first-in-class anti-TL1A (tulisokibart) with positive Phase 3 data in ulcerative colitis, an ADC platform via Daiichi Sankyo, and a once-weekly oral HIV regimen with Gilead. Each closes part of the 2028 gap, and the market has begun to underwrite that — which is what the 68% twelve-month move represents.

The bear framing: everyone is bidding for the same assets at the same time, which is how you get a $9.0B charge for a pre-revenue influenza prophylactic and a $5.7B charge for a CML candidate. Merck's GAAP earnings have now been rendered meaningless in three of the last four years by IPR&D write-offs (Prometheus $10.2B in 2023; Cidara and Terns in 2026), and the FY2026 non-GAAP EPS guide of $2.66–2.76 versus an ex-charge $8.6 is the clearest possible illustration. Buying the pipeline works only if the science does; if CD388 or MK-4208 fail, the cash is simply gone, and the 2028 bridge is shorter than it looked.
