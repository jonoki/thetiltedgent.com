01 Company Overview

Moderna develops and manufactures messenger-RNA (mRNA — a molecule that instructs a patient's own cells to make a target protein, so the drug is an information payload rather than a manufactured protein) medicines: respiratory vaccines for COVID-19, RSV and influenza; latent-virus vaccines for cytomegalovirus, Epstein-Barr and HIV; oncology therapeutics partnered with Merck; and rare-disease programmes. FY2025 revenue was $1.944B, down 39.9% from $3.236B in FY2024 — and down 89.9% from the FY2022 peak of $19.263B. The company is ranked roughly #440 globally by market capitalisation at $58.11B, against a 2021 market-cap peak of about $103B.

This is not an earnings story and it cannot be read as one. There is no trailing P/E to compute, because trailing EPS is −$8.01; there is no forward P/E either, because consensus 2026 EPS is −$8.43. TTM gross profit is negative $1.471B on $2.228B of revenue: Moderna currently loses money before it pays a single research or sales dollar. The only three questions that matter are (i) how much cash it has, (ii) how fast that cash leaves, and (iii) what the pipeline is worth. On August 19, 2026 the answer to (iii) changed violently — a Phase 3 readout in melanoma added roughly $45B of market value in one session and re-rated the stock +176.97% in a day. Everything on this page is built around that discontinuity.

Influenza is the newer and more consequential leg. mRNA-1010, branded mFLUSIVA, received a unanimous positive recommendation from the FDA's Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee on June 18, 2026 and full FDA approval on August 5, 2026 for adults aged 50 and over — Moderna's fifth approved product. Individualised neoantigen therapy (INT — a therapy manufactured one patient at a time: the patient's tumour is sequenced, the mutations unique to that tumour are identified, and a bespoke mRNA is made to teach the immune system to recognise them) is what the market is now paying for. On August 19, 2026 Merck and Moderna announced that the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial — 1,137 patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV melanoma, randomised 2:1 to intismeran autogene plus KEYTRUDA versus KEYTRUDA alone — met its primary endpoint of recurrence-free survival and its key secondary endpoint of distant metastasis-free survival at a pre-specified interim analysis, with no new safety signals. The companies withheld the actual hazard ratios for a medical meeting; the only quantified efficacy in the release was the earlier Phase 2b KEYNOTE-942 data (HR 0.51 for recurrence or death, HR 0.411 for distant metastasis or death). The trial continues for overall survival, and the partners said they will engage regulators on filings.

Read the disclosure precisely. A statistically significant interim result with the effect size withheld is a binary that has resolved in the right direction — and nothing more. The market added ~$45B of value to a number nobody has seen. Evercore ISI's Cory Kasimov made exactly this point, warning the new valuation "already prices in substantially more conviction than the data" disclosed. William Blair's Myles Minter, upgrading, modelled $5.4B of peak annual melanoma sales on the 50-50 revenue split with Merck. Both of those are on the same fetched page; they are not reconcilable, and the gap between them is the position.

Also disclosed Q2 2026: multiple additional Phase 2 and Phase 3 intismeran studies are ongoing across other tumour types with Merck. Their individual readout dates were not verified from sources fetched and are not listed.

The real asset is a manufacturing and regulatory capability, not a molecule. Anyone can design an mRNA sequence on a laptop. Very few organisations can make a different mRNA for every individual patient, at clinical-grade quality, on a turnaround short enough to matter after tumour resection, and then take that through a 1,137-patient randomised Phase 3 with a partner like Merck. That industrialised personalisation is what August 19 validated, and it is not something a competitor buys quickly. The corollary is that the moat is only worth something if the pipeline converts: the platform has no standalone earnings power, and the commercial base beneath it — $2.23B of TTM revenue at a negative 66% gross margin — is currently a cost, not a cushion.

Key Vulnerabilities

◆
Structural cash burn against a shrinking base. FY2026 guidance implies roughly $5.6B of cost of sales, R&D and SG&A ($1.7B + $2.9B + $1.0B) against revenue guided to no more than about 10% growth on FY2025's $1.944B — call it ~$2.1B. That is a designed gap of roughly $3.5B. Cutting costs 10% a year does not close a 2.7× spending-to-revenue ratio on any near horizon.
◆
Single-asset concentration in the valuation. Roughly $45B of the current $58.11B market capitalisation was created in one session by one interim analysis in one indication. If the hazard ratio presented at the medical meeting disappoints relative to the KEYNOTE-942 precedent, there is no earnings floor beneath the stock to catch it — only cash.
◆
Policy and reimbursement risk on the commercial base. Moderna's remaining revenue is almost entirely government- and payer-mediated vaccine demand. Recommendation frameworks, purchasing contracts and label scope are set by bodies outside the company's control, and changes to them move revenue directly rather than gradually. See Section 06 for what could and could not be verified here.
◆
Dilution as the funding mechanism. The August 28, 2026 upsized $2.6B convertible senior notes due 2032 convert at roughly $210.58 per share (4.7487 shares per $1,000), implying about 12.35M potential new shares — 3.1% of the 399.24M outstanding. Issuing converts a week after a 177% spike is disciplined treasury management; it is also an admission that the operating business does not fund itself.

06 Upcoming Catalysts & News

A necessary note on the U.S. vaccine policy environment. Policy risk — advisory-committee recommendation changes, federal contract decisions and label scope — is widely cited as a primary driver of this stock, and this page was commissioned with specific claims about ACIP and CDC recommendation changes, a BARDA contract cancellation and FDA label restrictions. None of those specific items could be verified on any page fetched for this report, and this page will not assert them. What was verified, from Moderna's own Q2 2026 disclosure and its press-release tape, is the opposite-signed evidence: the FDA's advisory committee voted unanimously in favour of mRNA-1010 on June 18, 2026; the FDA granted it full approval on August 5, 2026 for adults 50+; and the FDA approved Moderna's updated 2026–2027 COVID-19 formulations on August 27, 2026. Three favourable U.S. regulatory outcomes in ten weeks is a fact about the fetched record. It does not disprove the existence of policy headwinds — it simply means this page reports what it saw, and no more. Readers should treat U.S. vaccine policy as an unquantified risk on this page, not a quantified one.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and after a single-session 177% move it is close to useless as a timing tool.

Five-year trajectory — and the round trip that is the point of this page. From the September 2021 monthly close of $384.86 to the September 4, 2026 close of $145.55, MRNA is down 62.2% over five years — a compound annual return of about −17.7%, with no dividend. That single number hides one of the most violent price paths in the S&P 500. The stock fell from $384.86 to a monthly close of $24.09 in August 2025 — a decline of 93.7%, sustained over nearly four years, wiping out roughly $79B of market capitalisation against a 2021 peak of about $103B. It then rose 504% from that low to today. An investor who bought the top and held has lost nearly two-thirds of their money; an investor who bought in August 2025 has six-folded it. Same company, same twelve months apart.

Relative performance. A precise total-return comparison against the S&P 500 over the identical five-year window could not be verified from the sources fetched and is therefore not stated as a number. What can be stated from verified data: MRNA's 52-week price change of +505.20% is a multiple of any plausible index return over that span, and its five-year change of −62.2% is far below any plausible index return over that span. The S&P 500 is the benchmark used throughout this report; Moderna is listed on NASDAQ, but NASDAQ-100 membership could not be verified and the brief states the company was removed, so no NASDAQ-100 comparison is offered. The practical conclusion is unchanged either way: this stock has not tracked an index in either direction for five years, and the trailing beta of 1.11 is an artefact of averaging a long grinding decline against two explosive months.

Where it sits now. $145.55 is 17.6% below the 52-week high of $176.66 set around the August 19 spike, 553% above the 52-week low of $22.28, and 79.8% of the way up the 52-week range. It closed at $174.38 on August 19 and has given back roughly 16.5% of that in the sixteen sessions since, on no new clinical information — which is what price discovery looks like when a stock reprices on a press release rather than a data set.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the cash, because there is no earnings line to start with. At June 30, 2026 Moderna held $6.9B in cash, cash equivalents and investments, down from $7.5B at March 31 — a $600M draw in a single quarter. Net cash used in operating activities was $526M in Q2 2026 and $1,156M across the first half, an annualised operating burn of roughly $2.3B. Management guides year-end 2026 cash and investments to $4.7–5.2B, which implies a second-half draw of $1.7–2.2B — but that guidance was issued on July 31 and includes the $950M litigation settlement paid in July, so the underlying operating burn in the second half is closer to $0.8–1.3B, helped by the seasonal revenue that lands in Q3 and Q4.

Then add what happened after that guidance was set. On August 28, 2026 Moderna priced an upsized $2.6B convertible senior note offering due 2032. Adding gross proceeds to the guided year-end range gives a pro-forma year-end 2026 liquidity position of roughly $7.3–7.8B (derived arithmetic — not a company-stated figure, and before issuance costs and any use of proceeds). Against the ~$2.3B annualised operating burn, that is roughly three to three and a half years of runway — again, derived here, not guided. This is the single most important calculation on the page, and it is why the August 19 spike was commercially rational quite apart from the science: a company that raises $2.6B at a $210 conversion price nine days after its stock triples has converted a clinical event into years of independence.

What management is doing about the burn. Cash costs fell 10% year over year in Q2 2026. The component detail: cost of sales down 22% to $93M, R&D down 7% to $651M, SG&A down 6% to $216M, total operating expenses down 8% to $960M. Full-year 2026 guidance was improved by roughly $0.2B during the quarter — cost of sales cut from ~$1.8B to ~$1.7B, R&D from ~$3.0B to ~$2.9B, SG&A held at ~$1.0B. That is real discipline. It is also a $5.6B cost base against guided revenue of roughly $2.1B. No stated year for cash-cost break-even was disclosed on any page fetched for this report, and none is asserted here — a reader given a break-even year for Moderna should ask which document it came from.

The revenue reality, quantified. COVID vaccine sales have not softened; they have collapsed and then turned seasonal. Total revenue: $18.471B (2021) → $19.263B (2022) → $6.848B (2023) → $3.236B (2024) → $1.944B (2025), with TTM at $2.228B, down 27.6%. That is −89.9% from the 2022 peak. Within the remainder, the seasonal amplitude is extreme: Q3 2025 revenue of $1,016M against Q2 2026 revenue of $145M — a 7× swing between quarters of the same business. Moderna has held meaningful U.S. retail COVID share (24% overall, 34% in adults 65+) and now has a fifth approved product in mFLUSIVA, but the −66.02% gross margin says plainly that the current volume does not cover the manufacturing base. On RSV: mRESVIA revenue was not disclosed in any fetched source and is not estimated here; the constraint is that total Q2 2026 product sales across every product were $94M, which bounds it tightly.

So the pipeline is the equity, and one asset now dominates it. Strip $6.9B of cash from a $58.11B market capitalisation and roughly $52B of enterprise value is a claim on programmes, of which intismeran autogene is the only one with a positive Phase 3 in hand. The verified facts are narrow and should be held tightly: both endpoints met at interim in 1,137 resected stage IIB–IV melanoma patients, no new safety signals, effect sizes withheld, regulatory discussions to follow, overall survival still maturing, and a 50-50 economic split with Merck. William Blair models $5.4B of peak melanoma sales to Moderna on that split; Evercore ISI and Leerink both say the price already assumes more than the disclosure supports. Elsewhere the picture is mixed rather than uniformly improving: flu approved, COVID formulations approved, propionic acidemia registrational data due in 2026 — against a failed norovirus interim and a deferred methylmalonic acidemia pivotal. The flu/COVID combination and CMV programmes named in this report's brief could not be dated from verified sources and are flagged as such rather than filled in.

Bull Case

The platform has now produced a positive Phase 3 in a hard oncology indication with a partner that owns the standard of care, validating individualised neoantigen therapy as a modality rather than a hypothesis — and intismeran is in multiple further Phase 2 and 3 studies across other tumours. Management converted that moment into $2.6B of convertible financing at a 45% premium, pushing derived runway to roughly three years and removing the going-concern discount that held the stock at $24 a year ago. Meanwhile the commercial base is quietly rebuilding: a fifth approved product in mFLUSIVA, updated COVID formulations cleared for the 2026–27 season, 24% U.S. retail COVID share, cash costs down 10% year over year, and the first guided revenue growth since 2022.

Bear Case

Roughly $45B of the $58.11B market capitalisation was created in one session on a press release that withheld the effect size, and the sell-side response was to raise targets while refusing to raise ratings — the consensus target sits 23% below spot on stockanalysis.com and 47% below on MarketBeat, with a fresh downgrade to Sell on September 3. Beneath the option value the business loses $1.41 per dollar of revenue, carries a −66% gross margin, burns roughly $2.3B a year, and guides a $5.6B cost base against ~$2.1B of revenue. There is no P/E, no PEG, no free cash flow and no dividend to anchor a valuation — only 26× falling sales, 13.23% short interest, and a hazard ratio nobody has seen.

Top 3 Risks

01
The melanoma effect size disappoints on presentation. Statistical significance at an interim analysis says the direction is right; it says nothing about magnitude. If the hazard ratio presented at the medical meeting is materially weaker than the Phase 2b KEYNOTE-942 precedent (HR 0.51 RFS), the commercial model behind a $5.4B peak-sales estimate shrinks, and there is no earnings floor beneath the stock — the pre-readout price was $62.98.
02
The burn outlasts the pipeline. Derived runway of roughly three years assumes the $2.3B annualised operating burn does not rise. Launching mFLUSIVA, running multiple Phase 3 oncology trials and building individualised manufacturing all push the other way. If break-even requires the melanoma programme to be approved and launched — a multi-year sequence that has not yet begun — this company will raise capital again, and the next raise may not follow a 177% day.
03
Policy and reimbursement risk on the residual commercial base — unquantified here. Moderna's remaining revenue is government- and payer-mediated. Advisory-committee recommendations, federal purchasing decisions and label scope determine how much of it exists, and they change faster than the business can adapt. This report could not verify the specific ACIP, CDC, BARDA or FDA-label developments it was asked to cover, so it does not price them — but a reader should treat that as a gap in this page's evidence, not as evidence of absence.
  Industry Commentary
The Post-Pandemic Vaccine Cliff Is a Sector Condition, Not a Company Problem
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Moderna's revenue fell from $19.263B in FY2022 to $1.944B in FY2025 — an 89.9% decline in three years — as emergency procurement gave way to seasonal retail demand. The financial signature of that transition is the −66.02% gross margin: a manufacturing footprint built for billions of doses is being carried against a business that sold $94M of product in a quarter. Every mRNA-era vaccine developer faces some version of this, and the industry-wide answer has been the same one Moderna is executing — cut cash costs (down 10% year over year), narrow the pipeline, and hope a non-respiratory asset arrives before the cash does not.
Policy Is the Sector's Undisclosed Variable
ONGOING HEADWIND
Vaccine revenue is uniquely dependent on decisions made outside the company: which populations a product is recommended for, whether a government purchases it, and how a label is scoped. Those decisions move revenue in steps rather than in curves, which is why vaccine equities carry a policy discount that pure-therapeutic biotech does not. This page reports only what it verified on the record: a unanimous FDA advisory-committee vote for mRNA-1010 on June 18, 2026, full approval on August 5, and approval of updated COVID formulations on August 27. Claims about ACIP or CDC recommendation changes, a BARDA contract cancellation, or FDA label restrictions were not verifiable from the sources fetched and are therefore neither asserted nor priced here. Investors should source those independently before weighting them.
mRNA Oncology Just Cleared Its First Phase 3 Bar
EMERGING TAILWIND
INTerpath-001 is the readout the entire modality has been waiting for: a randomised Phase 3 in 1,137 patients where a bespoke, patient-specific mRNA added measurable benefit on top of a KEYTRUDA backbone, with no new safety signals. That result belongs to the field, not just to Moderna — it de-risks individualised neoantigen therapy for every developer working on it. Bull framing: if the modality is real, a $52B enterprise value for the platform that proved it first, with Merck sharing economics 50-50 and multiple further trials running, is early rather than expensive. Bear framing: the effect size is unpublished, overall survival is immature, the sell-side raised targets without raising ratings, and the same platform's norovirus Phase 3 interim failed in the same year. Both framings rest on the same disclosure — which is exactly why the target range spans $40 to $170.
