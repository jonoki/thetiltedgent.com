01 Company Overview

Moderna develops and manufactures messenger-RNA (mRNA — a molecule that instructs a patient's own cells to make a target protein, so the drug is an information payload rather than a manufactured protein) medicines: respiratory vaccines for COVID-19, RSV and influenza; latent-virus vaccines for cytomegalovirus, Epstein-Barr and HIV; oncology therapeutics partnered with Merck; and rare-disease programmes. FY2025 revenue was $1.944B, down 39.9% from $3.236B in FY2024 — and down 89.9% from the FY2022 peak of $19.263B. Revenue over the last four reported quarters is $2.525B, up 13.1%, and Moderna now guides to 15%–25% growth for 2026, which on FY2025's base means roughly $2.24–2.43B (our arithmetic). At the November 9, 2026 close the company is worth $108.84B (404.62M shares outstanding at October 30 × $269.00). It rejoined the Nasdaq-100 on October 9, 2026, replacing Warner Bros. Discovery (Nasdaq announcement, October 1, 2026); it first joined that index on July 20, 2020 (Nasdaq, July 13, 2020).

One profitable quarter does not make this an earnings story. Q3 2026 produced net income of $162M and diluted EPS of $0.38, because autumn is when respiratory vaccines sell. Over the trailing twelve months (TTM) EPS is still −$7.04, so there is no trailing P/E, and analysts expect a 2026 loss of −$7.58 a share on an adjusted basis (stockanalysis.com consensus, read November 9, 2026). The questions that matter are still (i) how much cash it has, (ii) how fast that cash leaves, and (iii) what the pipeline is worth. The answer to (i) improved: $8.0B of cash and investments at September 30, after a $3.0B convertible bond sale. The answer to (iii) moved twice. On August 19 an interim Phase 3 readout in melanoma lifted the stock +176.97% in a day; on October 24 the full numbers showed a 42% cut in the risk of recurrence or death (hazard ratio 0.58 — a hazard ratio compares the rate of bad outcomes between two groups, so 0.58 means 42% fewer), smaller than the earlier Phase 2b study's 0.51. The stock fell 17.6% when the data leaked on October 22, then climbed to a new 52-week closing high after the Q3 print.

This is the second respiratory leg, and it has not yet visibly moved the top line. mRESVIA (RSV — respiratory syncytial virus, a seasonal respiratory infection that is dangerous in older adults) is approved and selling; Moderna does not break out its sales.

Influenza is the newer and more consequential leg. mRNA-1010, branded mFLUSIVA, received a unanimous positive recommendation from the FDA's Vaccines and Related Biological Products Advisory Committee on June 18, 2026, and in August the FDA approved it for adults aged 50–64, with accelerated approval for adults 65 and over (accelerated approval is granted on early evidence and can require a confirmatory study) — Moderna's fifth approved product. Because the approval came after the annual buying cycle for flu shots, Moderna is running only a limited U.S. launch for the 2026–2027 season; mFLUSIVA is also approved in Canada. A standalone flu vaccine matters because it is a far larger annual market than RSV and the building block of a flu/COVID combination shot.

Flu/COVID combination (mRNA-1083): approved in the European Union as mCOMBRIAX and in Canada as mCOFLU; under review in Japan, the U.K. and Australia; Moderna is waiting for FDA guidance on refiling in the U.S. (Q3 2026 release).

Individualised neoantigen therapy (INT — a therapy manufactured one patient at a time: the patient's tumour is sequenced, the mutations unique to that tumour are identified, and a bespoke mRNA is made to teach the immune system to recognise them) is what the market is now paying for. On August 19, 2026 Merck and Moderna announced that the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial — 1,137 patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV melanoma, randomised 2:1 to intismeran autogene plus KEYTRUDA versus KEYTRUDA alone — met its primary endpoint of recurrence-free survival and its key secondary endpoint of distant metastasis-free survival at a pre-specified interim analysis, with no new safety signals. The August release held back the effect size for a medical meeting.

The numbers arrived in October. At the ESMO cancer congress on October 24, 2026, Merck and Moderna reported that intismeran plus KEYTRUDA reduced the risk of recurrence or death by 42% (hazard ratio 0.58) and the risk of distant metastasis or death by 38% (hazard ratio 0.62) compared with KEYTRUDA alone, across all randomised patients. After the six-month immunisation course the gap was wider: a 58% reduction (hazard ratio 0.42) in a pre-specified sensitivity analysis (Q3 2026 release). Those effects are smaller than in the earlier Phase 2b KEYNOTE-942 study (hazard ratio 0.51 for recurrence or death; a 59% cut in distant metastasis risk). Some of the data were published by an oncology news site on October 22, breaking the congress's embargo; ESMO then released the abstract, and Moderna closed down 17.6% at $202.63 that day (STAT, republished by The Boston Globe). William Blair's Myles Minter wrote that "There appears to be a sell-the-news reaction playing out here" (same article). Overall survival is not yet mature, and the partners are working toward regulatory submissions and preparing for a possible launch if approved.

What the market is paying for. The stock closed at $62.96 the day before the August readout and at $269.00 on November 9. Moderna and Merck split the programme's economics 50-50; on August 19 William Blair's Minter, upgrading the stock to Outperform, extended his melanoma model to 2040 with intismeran sales to Moderna above $5.4B on that split (Benzinga, August 20, 2026); his firm cut its rating back to Market Perform on October 27.

Also in the oncology pipeline (Q3 2026 release): mRNA-4359 in Phase 2 in melanoma and lung cancer; mRNA-2808 has an oral presentation at the American Society of Hematology meeting on December 12, 2026; mRNA-4203 in Phase 1 with Immatics.

The real asset is a manufacturing and regulatory capability, not a molecule. Anyone can design an mRNA sequence on a laptop. Very few organisations can make a different mRNA for every individual patient, at clinical-grade quality, on a turnaround short enough to matter after tumour surgery, and then take that through a 1,137-patient randomised Phase 3 with a partner like Merck. That industrialised personalisation is what INTerpath-001 validated, and it is not something a competitor buys quickly. The corollary is that the moat is only worth something if the pipeline converts: the commercial base beneath it — $2.53B of TTM revenue, a TTM net loss of $2.79B — still costs more than it brings in, even after a profitable autumn quarter.

Key Vulnerabilities

◆
Costs still far above revenue. FY2026 guidance implies roughly $5.4B of cost of sales, R&D and SG&A ($1.7B + $2.7B + $1.0B), of which about $0.9B is a one-off litigation charge, against revenue guided to grow 15%–25% on FY2025's $1.944B — about $2.24–2.43B (our arithmetic). Even without the one-off charge, spending runs near twice revenue.
◆
Single-asset concentration in the valuation. The stock closed at $62.96 the day before the August 19 readout and at $269.00 on November 9, a market value of $108.84B. Most of that gain rests on one programme in one indication. The effect size is now public (hazard ratio 0.58, weaker than Phase 2b), so the remaining tests are regulators, overall survival, the launch and the other tumour types — and there is no earnings floor beneath the stock if any of them disappoints.
◆
Policy and reimbursement risk on the commercial base. Moderna's remaining revenue is almost entirely vaccine demand that depends on governments and insurers. Recommendation frameworks, purchasing contracts and label scope are set by bodies outside the company's control, and changes to them move revenue directly rather than gradually.
◆
Dilution as the funding mechanism. The $3.0B of 0% convertible senior notes due March 1, 2032 (priced August 27, closed September 1, 2026) convert at roughly $210.58 per share (4.7487 shares per $1,000), or about 14.25M potential new shares — 3.5% of the 404.62M outstanding. At $269.00 the stock is 27.7% above that conversion price. Moderna also bought capped calls (option contracts that offset the dilution up to a share price of about $392.62) for $329M (Moderna 8-K, September 1, 2026; Q3 cash flow statement).

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — and after a single-session 177% move it is close to useless as a timing tool.

Five-year trajectory — and the round trip that is the point of this page. From the November 2021 month-end close of $352.43 to the November 9, 2026 close of $269.00, MRNA is down 23.7% on the chart — a compound annual return of about −5.3%, with no dividend. That single number hides an extraordinarily violent price path. The stock fell from $352.43 to a month-end close of $24.09 in August 2025 — a decline of 93.2% over nearly four years. It then rose 1,017% from that low to the November 9 close, more than an elevenfold gain. An investor who bought at the start of the chart has lost about a quarter of the money; one who bought at the August 2025 month-end close has multiplied it eleven times. Same company, fifteen months apart.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the cash, because the earnings line is still negative. At September 30, 2026 Moderna held $8.0B in cash, cash equivalents and investments, up from $6.9B at June 30. The rise came from about $2.6B of net proceeds from the convertible notes, partly offset by the $950M litigation settlement payment and by cash used in operations, including autumn sales not yet collected. Net cash used in operating activities was $2.715B in the first nine months (against $2.803B a year earlier); over the trailing twelve months it was $1.785B, because the fourth quarter brings in the cash from autumn sales. Management now guides year-end 2026 cash and investments to $8.1–8.3B, up from $4.7–5.2B, and has about $0.9B more available under its credit facility (Q3 2026 release).

What that buys in time. Divide the guided year-end cash of $8.1–8.3B by the TTM operating cash use of $1.785B and you get about 4.5 years before capital spending (our arithmetic, not a company figure); the TTM figure includes the one-off $950M settlement, so the underlying use is lower. Against that, $3.0B of convertible notes fall due in March 2032 unless they convert into shares. This is the most important calculation on the page, and it is why the August financing mattered as much as the science: a company that raised $3.0B at 0% interest eight days after its stock nearly tripled in a session turned a clinical result into years of independence.

What management is doing about the burn. In Q3 2026 R&D fell 27% to $583M as late-stage infectious disease trials wound down, SG&A rose 5% to $282M and cost of sales rose 54% to $318M on higher volume. The full-year R&D outlook was cut again, to about $2.7B from about $2.9B; cost of sales stays at about $1.7B (including the roughly $0.9B settlement charge) and SG&A at about $1.0B. That is real discipline. It is also a $5.4B cost base against guided revenue of roughly $2.24–2.43B.

The revenue reality, quantified. COVID vaccine sales collapsed and then turned seasonal. Total revenue: $18.471B (2021) → $19.263B (2022) → $6.848B (2023) → $3.236B (2024) → $1.944B (2025) — −89.9% from the 2022 peak. 2026 is guided to be the first annual rise since 2022: TTM revenue is $2.525B, up 13.1%, and Q3 2026 revenue of $1,313M was up 29%, mostly from international COVID sales under government partnerships. The seasonal swing is extreme: Q2 2026 revenue was $145M, Q3 2026 nine times that. mFLUSIVA is only on a limited U.S. launch this season, so the flu vaccine's first full selling season is still ahead.

So the pipeline is the equity, and one asset dominates it. Strip $8.0B of cash and investments from a $108.84B market value and add back the debt, and roughly $104B of enterprise value is a claim on programmes, of which intismeran autogene is the only one with a positive Phase 3 in hand. The disclosed facts: both endpoints met in 1,137 resected stage IIB–IV melanoma patients, a 42% cut in the risk of recurrence or death (hazard ratio 0.58), a 38% cut in distant metastasis or death, no new safety signals, overall survival not yet mature, filings being prepared, and a 50-50 economic split with Merck. The effect is smaller than the Phase 2b study suggested, and the stock fell 17.6% when it leaked — then went on to new highs. Elsewhere the picture is mixed: flu approved (on a narrower label for over-65s), a flu/COVID combination approved in the EU and Canada, propionic acidemia pivotal data due in 2026 — against a paused norovirus programme and a deferred methylmalonic acidemia pivotal study.

Bull Case

The platform has produced a positive Phase 3 in a hard cancer indication with the partner that owns the standard of care, and the full data held up: a 42% cut in the risk of recurrence or death, rising to 58% after the immunisation course. Intismeran is in further studies across other tumours, and more readouts are coming. Management turned the August moment into $3.0B of 0% convertible debt, lifting cash to $8.0B and the year-end guide to $8.1–8.3B, which removes the going-concern fear that held the stock at $24 a year ago. Meanwhile the commercial base is growing again: Q3 revenue +29%, a profit in the seasonal quarter, 2026 growth guided to 15%–25%, R&D cut again, a fifth approved product in mFLUSIVA and a combination shot approved in Europe and Canada.

Bear Case

At $108.84B the stock prices a launch that has not been filed for, on an effect size that came in below the Phase 2b study, with overall survival still immature. The sell side keeps raising targets without raising ratings: the consensus target sits 43% below the price, only one of eight post-print actions is a buy, and Citi and William Blair both turned more negative since September. Beneath the hopes priced in for the pipeline, the business lost $1.10 per dollar of revenue over the last year, used $1.79B of operating cash, and guides a $5.4B cost base against roughly $2.3B of revenue. There is no P/E, no PEG, no positive free cash flow and no dividend to anchor a valuation — only 43× sales, a profit that came from one seasonal quarter, and $3.0B of notes that could become 14.25M new shares, with the stock now above their $210.58 conversion price.

Top 3 Risks

01
The melanoma programme stumbles between data and sales. The effect size is now public — hazard ratio 0.58, weaker than the Phase 2b KEYNOTE-942 precedent (0.51) — so the next tests are regulators, overall survival, manufacturing one-patient-at-a-time at launch scale, and pricing. A setback at any step meets no earnings floor beneath the stock: the close before the August readout was $62.96, and the leak alone cost 17.6% in a day.
02
The burn outlasts the pipeline. The roughly 4.5 years of derived cash cover assumes operating cash use does not rise. Launching intismeran, running further Phase 3 oncology trials and building individualised manufacturing all push the other way. If break-even needs the melanoma programme approved and launched — a multi-year sequence that has not yet begun — the company may raise capital again.
03
Policy and reimbursement risk on the remaining commercial base. Moderna's remaining revenue is government- and payer-mediated. Advisory-committee recommendations, federal purchasing decisions and label scope determine how much of it exists, and they change faster than the business can adapt.
  Industry Commentary
The Post-Pandemic Vaccine Cliff Is a Sector Condition, Not a Company Problem
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Moderna's revenue fell from $19.263B in FY2022 to $1.944B in FY2025 — an 89.9% decline in three years — as emergency procurement gave way to seasonal retail demand. The financial signature of that transition is a business that swings from $94M of product sales in a spring quarter (Q2 2026) to $1,273M in an autumn one (Q3 2026), carrying a manufacturing footprint built for pandemic volumes all year. Every mRNA-era vaccine developer faces some version of this, and the usual answer is the one Moderna is executing — cut costs (2026 R&D now guided to about $2.7B, from $3.1B in 2025), narrow the pipeline, sign multi-year government supply deals, and try to bring a non-respiratory product to market while the cash lasts.
Policy Is the Sector's Undisclosed Variable
ONGOING HEADWIND
Vaccine revenue is uniquely dependent on decisions made outside the company: which populations a product is recommended for, whether a government purchases it, and how a label is scoped. Those decisions move revenue in steps rather than in curves, which is why vaccine equities carry a policy discount that pure-therapeutic biotech does not. On the record: a unanimous FDA advisory-committee vote for mRNA-1010 on June 18, 2026, approval in August (full for ages 50–64, accelerated for 65 and over), approval of updated COVID formulations on August 27, and a flu/COVID combination approved in the EU and Canada but still awaiting FDA guidance on refiling in the U.S.
mRNA Oncology Cleared Its First Phase 3 Bar
EMERGING TAILWIND
INTerpath-001 is the readout the whole approach had been waiting for: a randomised Phase 3 in 1,137 patients where a bespoke, patient-specific mRNA cut the risk of recurrence or death by 42% on top of KEYTRUDA, with no new safety signals, presented at ESMO's Presidential Symposium on October 24, 2026. That result belongs to the field, not just to Moderna — it lowers the risk for every developer of individualised neoantigen therapy. Bull framing: if the approach works across tumours, a $104B enterprise value for the platform that proved it first, with Merck sharing economics 50-50 and further trials running, is early rather than expensive. Bear framing: the effect is smaller than Phase 2b, overall survival is immature, nothing is filed yet, the sell side raised targets without raising ratings, and the same platform's norovirus Phase 3 interim missed in the same year. Both framings rest on the same data — which is why the consensus panel's targets span $45 to $270.
