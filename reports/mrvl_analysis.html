01 Company Overview
Marvell Technology designs data-infrastructure semiconductors — custom AI accelerators (XPUs) and attached chips built for individual hyperscalers, optical DSPs and electro-optics that move data between AI racks, Ethernet switching silicon, and storage and networking controllers. Fiscal 2026 (ended January 31, 2026) revenue was $8.195B, up 42.1% YoY, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through the quarter ended August 1, 2026 is $9.45B, up 30.6%. Alongside Broadcom, Marvell is one of only two firms that meaningfully enable hyperscaler custom AI silicon — and since August 19, 2026 it has a signed, expanded agreement with Google, the hyperscaler most associated with Broadcom.
The business has been reshaped three times in eighteen months: the $2.5B all-cash sale of the automotive Ethernet unit to Infineon (announced April 7, 2025; closed August 14, 2025); a reporting reorganisation into two segments — Data Center and Communications and Other, with Data Center now 79% of quarterly revenue; and the Google agreement, which covers custom programs attached to the TPU ecosystem (inference accelerators, storage, network and memory-interface controllers, near-memory compute) and hands Google a warrant for up to 58.97M Marvell shares at $206.58 that vests only as Google buys — one $500M revenue bucket at a time. After the Q2 FY2027 print management guided FY2027 revenue to approximately $12B (~+45%) and FY2028 to approximately $18B (~+50%), up from $11.5B and $16.5B a quarter earlier. The next set piece is an Investor Day on October 6, 2026, where management has said it will lay out its long-term strategy.

Two distinct engines sit inside one reported line. The first is custom AI silicon — full-custom XPUs (accelerators) and attached devices co-designed with individual hyperscalers on TSMC 3nm/2nm. Two flagship programs were already publicly identified — Amazon's Trainium family and Microsoft's Maia 200 — and on August 19, 2026 Marvell added a third: an expanded agreement with Google covering custom programs that attach to the TPU ecosystem (AI inference accelerators, storage controllers, network interface controllers, memory interface controllers and near-memory compute). Google's warrant for up to 58.97M shares at $206.58 vests 1.36M shares over the first four quarters and the rest in 240 buckets, each tied to $500M of Marvell revenue from Google, so the headline "$120B" is a ceiling through 2033, not a backlog. On the Q2 call management pointed to "a significant acceleration in our Custom business beginning in the second half of fiscal 2027" and kept custom silicon on track to more than double in FY2028; at Q1 it had guided custom above 20% growth in FY2027. The second engine is interconnect: PAM4 optical DSPs (the Ara-class 1.6T parts are in volume production), coherent/ZR DSPs for data-centre-interconnect, and Teralynx Ethernet switching. At Q1, interconnect was guided to grow above 70% YoY in FY2027 and switching revenue above $600M in FY2027, roughly double FY2026 (sub-guides not restated in the Q2 materials reviewed for this edition). Q2 FY2027 Data Center revenue was $2.171B, +46% YoY and +18% sequentially — an acceleration from +27% in Q1 — and management now expects Data Center to grow about 60% in FY2027 and more than 60% in FY2028, per earnings-call coverage. Non-GAAP gross margin held at 58.9% in Q2, but the Q3 guide of 57.5–58.5% is the first visible cost of the mix shift: custom XPU work carries structurally lower margin than merchant optical silicon, so margin compresses even as revenue accelerates.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
OPTICAL DSP LEADERSHIP ~60–65% HYPERSCALER CO-DESIGN SEATS INPHI ELECTRO-OPTICS IP 3nm / 2nm TSMC ACCESS SerDes IP LIBRARY 1.6T FIRST-MOVER MULTI-YEAR SOCKET LOCK-IN GOOGLE TPU-ATTACHED PROGRAMS
Marvell's moat is narrower than Broadcom's but genuinely hard to replicate. It rests on three assets. First, SerDes and electro-optics IP — the analog circuitry that pushes 200 gigabits per second down a lane of copper or fibre is among the hardest engineering in the industry, and the Inphi acquisition (2021, ~$10B) bought Marvell the franchise that made it the reference vendor for PAM4 DSPs. It holds an estimated 60–65% of the optical DSP market and is already in volume production at 1.6T while Credo's Cardinal and MaxLinear's Rushmore are still ramping. Second, a seat at the hyperscaler design table: only Broadcom (~70% share) and Marvell (~20–25%) do full-custom AI accelerators at scale, and hyperscalers deliberately dual-source, which structurally protects the number two — the August 2026 Google agreement is that dual-sourcing logic in writing. Third, switching costs measured in years — an XPU program takes 18–30 months from architecture to silicon, and once a hyperscaler's software stack is tuned to a specific accelerator, moving is expensive. The vulnerability is that these are program-level moats, not platform-level ones: each moat has to be re-won at each node transition.
Customer and program concentration. Until August 2026, two named hyperscaler programs — AWS Trainium and Microsoft Maia 200 — carried the custom-silicon story. The Google agreement adds a third name and weakens the sharpest version of the concentration bear case, but it does not dilute concentration in the near term: the vesting schedule ties Google's rewards to revenue that has yet to ship, and the new programs ramp over years. Marvell has not disclosed a precise concentration figure in the materials reviewed here; with Data Center at 75.9% of trailing revenue, the top customer plausibly represents well north of 20% of total revenue. (Exact 10-K customer-concentration percentages were not verified for this snapshot — check the FY2026 10-K before relying on it.) March 2025 demonstrated the mechanic: the stock fell 33% in a single month on fears that AWS would shift Trainium volume elsewhere.
Broadcom's structural dominance. Broadcom holds roughly 70% of custom AI accelerator design share against Marvell's 20–25%, serves more programs (Google TPU, Meta, OpenAI among them), and has disclosed AI semiconductor revenue of $8.4B in a single quarter growing above 100% YoY with a multi-billion backlog. Google's late-2025/2026 extension of its Broadcom TPU relationship reportedly runs through 2031. Marvell competes for the remaining sockets from a position of far less scale, and Broadcom can price aggressively on attached content.
The price of the Google seat. The Google warrant is paid for in equity. If fully vested and exercised, 58.97M new shares would equal about 6.7% of the 876.93M shares outstanding (our calculation), issued at $206.58 — below the September 21 close of $257.38. Economically it works like a volume rebate: the more Google buys, the more of Marvell it can own at a fixed price. It also lets Google walk away — it has no obligation to exercise, and The Next Platform's reading of the deal documents is that Google appears to want a part it links to the codename "Kestrel" qualified by November 10, 2027 — its inference, not a term confirmed in Marvell's own disclosure. The warrant aligns a customer with the stock; it is not a purchase commitment.
Technology substitution at the interconnect layer. Marvell's DSP franchise depends on pluggable optics remaining the dominant way to connect racks. Co-packaged optics, linear-drive pluggable optics (LPO) that remove the DSP entirely, and NVIDIA's proprietary NVLink/Spectrum-X scale-up fabrics all attack that assumption from different angles. Credo already owns ~88% of the active-electrical-cable market that substitutes for short-reach optics inside the rack; Astera Labs owns over half of PCIe retiming. Each speed transition — 800G to 1.6T to 3.2T — is a re-qualification war Marvell must win again.
Earnings quality and valuation compression risk. Reported GAAP profitability is heavily influenced by acquisition accounting from Cavium and Inphi and by the one-time gain on the Infineon divestiture (see Section 04). Investors are effectively underwriting non-GAAP earnings at a very high multiple against a business whose revenue guidance now implies roughly 45% and 50% growth in consecutive years. In a stock with a beta of 2.25 that fell 50% from its June high into late July 2026, and then dropped 10.3% on a quarter that beat and raised, the mismatch between multiple and execution risk is the single biggest drawdown driver.

Notable One-Off Items Marvell's GAAP income statement is materially distorted, and reading the headline P/E without adjusting for that is the single most common analytical error on this name.
1. Gain on the Infineon divestiture (FY2026). Marvell sold its automotive Ethernet business to Infineon for $2.5B in cash, closing August 14, 2025. FY2026 operating income was $1,339M but GAAP net income was $2,670M — a ~$1.3B positive gap that cannot be explained by interest income alone. The overwhelming likelihood is a large book gain on sale plus associated tax effects (a valuation-allowance release is plausible given years of prior losses). The exact gain line item was not verified against the FY2026 10-K for this snapshot — treat the attribution as inferred, not confirmed. The practical consequence: trailing EPS of $2.98 and the 27.93% net margin overstate underlying earnings power, and the 86.4x trailing P/E is therefore understating the true multiple on core profit.
2. Acquisition intangible amortisation (ongoing, very large). The Cavium (2018, ~$6B) and Inphi (2021, ~$10B) deals left Marvell carrying an unusually heavy intangible amortisation load. It is the reason trailing GAAP gross margin is 52.2% while non-GAAP gross margin was 58.9% in Q2 FY2027, and the reason Marvell posted GAAP net losses in FY2022, FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 while generating positive non-GAAP EPS every one of those years. This is a recurring non-cash charge that will decline over time, but it is not "one-off" in the current window — lead with non-GAAP for operating comparisons and with cash flow for valuation.
3. Restructuring and portfolio actions. The FY2026 reorganisation into two reportable segments, the exit of the automotive business, and associated restructuring charges make year-over-year segment comparisons unreliable before FY2026. Historical enterprise networking, carrier infrastructure, consumer and automotive/industrial lines are no longer separately disclosed.
4. Revenue lost to the divestiture. Roughly $225–250M of annualised revenue left with the automotive Ethernet unit. Reported growth rates from Q3 FY2026 onward are therefore understated relative to like-for-like organic growth — a rare case where a distortion works in the company's favour analytically.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Marvell is one of two companies on earth that can put a hyperscaler's custom AI accelerator into volume production, it owns an estimated 60–65% of the optical DSP market that connects those accelerators together, and it now has a signed third hyperscaler — Google — whose warrant only vests as it buys, up to ~$120B of revenue through 2033. Management has raised guidance again, to ~$12B for FY2027 and ~$18B for FY2028 (from $8.2B in FY2026), with Data Center accelerating to +46% in Q2 and custom silicon guided to more than double in FY2028. At a ~1.0 PEG, the multiple is defensible if those numbers land — and the market sold the beat-and-raise, so the bar for the October 6 Investor Day is lower than the guidance.

Bear Case

Three customers carry the custom story, one dominant competitor re-competes every socket, and a 6.94% ROIC says Marvell has not yet earned its cost of capital on $16B of acquisitions. The Google seat was bought partly with equity — up to 6.7% dilution at $206.58 — and none of its revenue is committed. Custom-silicon mix is already pulling the gross-margin guide down, Communications & Other slowed to +10%, GAAP earnings still lean on a one-time divestiture gain, and the stock trades at ~47x forward earnings and ~24x sales. It fell 10% on a beat-and-raise: with a 2.25 beta and 4.35% short interest, any deceleration in hyperscaler capex gets amplified, not absorbed.

01
Customer / program concentration. Data Center is 75.9% of trailing revenue and rests on three publicly identified hyperscaler relationships (AWS Trainium, Microsoft Maia 200 and, since August 2026, Google's TPU-attached programs, which are still ramping). Custom silicon sockets are re-competed at every process node, and March 2025 already showed that a rumour about one customer's sourcing plans can cut the stock 33% in a month.
02
Valuation, dilution and earnings quality. 47.2x forward earnings and 23.9x trailing sales (our calculations at the Sep 21 close) price in near-flawless execution of a ~45%-then-~50% two-year growth plan. Trailing GAAP earnings include the Infineon gain, so the real multiple on core profit is higher than the headline 86.4x suggests; the ~6-point gap between GAAP and non-GAAP gross margin is permanent-feeling acquisition amortisation; and the Google warrant could add up to 58.97M shares if it vests in full.
03
Interconnect technology substitution. The DSP franchise assumes pluggable optics stay dominant. Co-packaged optics, linear-drive pluggables that eliminate the DSP, Credo's 88%-share active electrical cables inside the rack, and NVIDIA's proprietary scale-up fabrics each chip away at that assumption — and every speed transition from 800G to 1.6T to 3.2T is a fresh re-qualification Marvell has to win.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
Custom AI Silicon vs. Merchant GPUs
EMERGING TAILWIND
Hyperscalers are systematically building alternatives to buying merchant GPUs. The custom AI ASIC market is estimated to grow roughly 45% in 2026 and to reach approximately $118B by 2033, and the design work sits with almost nobody: Broadcom at roughly 70% share and Marvell at 20–25% together enable more than 80% of hyperscaler custom AI silicon. Broadcom serves Google TPU, Meta and OpenAI programs among others and guided a single quarter of AI semiconductor revenue to $10.7B, up ~140% YoY; Marvell serves AWS Trainium and Microsoft Maia 200, has now added Google, and guides custom silicon to more than double in FY2028. The structural point for Marvell is that hyperscalers deliberately dual-source design partners to avoid Broadcom dependency — that behaviour is what preserves a viable number-two seat, and it is why the August 2026 Google agreement — custom programs attached to Google's TPU ecosystem, from inference accelerators to memory-interface and near-memory compute — matters more than its near-term revenue. Morningstar's William Kerwin read it as "a growing pie at Google for new sources, rather than a competitive displacement of Broadcom"; Broadcom's shares still fell more than 5% on the day.
Optical Interconnect Scaling — 800G to 1.6T and "Scale-Across"
EMERGING TAILWIND
As AI clusters outgrow single buildings, the bottleneck moves from compute to the links between compute. At Q1 Marvell guided interconnect revenue above 70% YoY growth in FY2027 — materially faster than the 20%+ then guided for custom silicon — and management has called interconnect "the standout" of the portfolio. In September 2026 it expanded a multi-year silicon-germanium capacity deal with GlobalFoundries for pluggable, near-packaged and co-packaged optics — capacity bought ahead of the optical ramp. Optical DSP alone is a $3–5B market in 2026 where Marvell holds an estimated 60–65% share and is shipping 1.6T parts in volume ahead of Credo's Cardinal and MaxLinear's Rushmore. Switching silicon is guided above $600M in FY2027, roughly double FY2026, with a path beyond $1B annualised in FY2028. Marvell's own framing puts the total AI-cluster connectivity TAM near $100B by 2028. The competitive caveat matters: Credo holds ~88% of active electrical cables inside the rack and Astera Labs over 50% of PCIe retiming, so Marvell owns the between-rack layer, not the whole fabric.
Customer Concentration Risk — the Bull/Bear Fault Line
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Everything above resolves into one question, and the market has repriced it violently in both directions within eighteen months. August 2026 moved the goalposts: Google signed on as a third custom partner, and the question shifted from "will there be a third hyperscaler?" to "how fast does it pay, and at what margin and dilution?" The bear framing: 75.9% of revenue is Data Center, resting on a few named programs, in a market where the leader holds 70% share, reportedly has Google's TPU itself locked through 2031, and can bundle attached content Marvell cannot; the Google deal is option-like for Google, not a commitment to Marvell. Custom sockets are not annuities — they are re-bid every node. March 2025 (-32.9% in a month) and July 2026 (-37.0% in a month) are both, at root, the market marking down the durability of those two programs. The bull framing: concentration cuts both ways. Three programs at a 45%+ company growth rate is enough, hyperscalers structurally need a second design partner, the interconnect franchise is a genuinely diversified 60–65%-share business that grows regardless of who wins the XPU sockets, and the FY2028 guide — now ~$18B — already assumes new programs in production. The August 27 print raised the numbers and the stock still fell 10%; the October 6 Investor Day is where management has to show the Google revenue path. If it does, the concentration discount compresses; if a program slips instead, the 2.25 beta does the rest.
