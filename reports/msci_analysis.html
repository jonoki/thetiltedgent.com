01 Company Overview

MSCI Inc. licenses equity indexes, risk and portfolio analytics, sustainability and climate data, and private-asset data to asset managers, asset owners, banks and hedge funds. Roughly $2.82 trillion of ETF assets were benchmarked to MSCI equity indexes at June 30, 2026 — the single most important number in the business, because it is the base on which MSCI's fastest-growing revenue line is charged. FY2025 revenue was $3,134.5M, up 9.7% from $2,856M, and the TTM figure through June 2026 is $3.33B, up 11.6%. At a $39.81B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #644 globally.

Judging MSCI on revenue misses the machine. Management runs the company on three operating metrics, and so should anyone valuing it: Run Rate (the annualised value of every recurring contract in force — $3,479.7M at June 30, 2026, up 12.0%), organic recurring subscription Run Rate growth (8.1% in Q2 2026, up from 7.7% at year-end 2025), and the Retention Rate (95.3% in Q2 2026 versus 94.4% a year earlier). The central debate on this page: run-rate growth is accelerating and retention is improving, yet the stock has fallen roughly 18% over five years while the S&P 500 rose about two thirds. The gap between those two facts is entirely a multiple story, and the question is whether the multiple compressed because the market re-rated quality down, or because it re-rated the source of growth — an increasingly beta-linked asset-based fee stream — as lower quality than the subscription base it is replacing at the margin.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
BENCHMARK STANDARD-SETTING SWITCHING COST = FUND RENAMING 97.5% INDEX RETENTION EMBEDDED RISK MODELS (BARRA) DATA HISTORY DEPTH AUM-LINKED ROYALTY 83% GROSS MARGIN

The moat is not the data — it is the coordination. An index only has value because everyone agrees to use the same one. Once a $50B ETF is named "MSCI Emerging Markets," switching the underlying index means a prospectus change, a shareholder communication, a tracking-error event, a rebalance cost borne by the fund's investors, and a period during which the fund's performance is not comparable to its own history. The ETF sponsor bears all of that cost to save a few basis points of licence fee. That asymmetry is why Index retention was 97.5% in Q2 2026 and why MSCI can charge a percentage of AUM rather than a flat fee for a dataset that costs essentially nothing to deliver a second time — gross margin of 82.98% and operating margin of 55.67% are the financial signature of a licence, not a service.

Key Vulnerabilities

A
Asset-based fees are a beta bet wearing a subscription costume. Asset-based fees are now $948.2M of the $2,027.0M Index Run Rate and 27% of the $3,479.7M total. ETF AUM linked to MSCI indexes rose from $2,025B to $2,818B year-on-year on just $39B of quarterly inflows — the rest was market appreciation. A 25% equity drawdown would take roughly a quarter off that revenue line with no client loss whatsoever, and would do so at exactly the moment clients are also cutting discretionary subscription budgets. The two revenue streams are positively correlated in a downturn, not offsetting.
B
Fee compression from the client's own price war. MSCI's largest customers are ETF sponsors running a decade-long race to zero on expense ratios. Every basis point they cut from an investor-facing fee has to come from somewhere, and the index licence is one of the few variable costs in a passive fund. The largest sponsors have already demonstrated they will self-index to escape the fee — BlackRock, Vanguard and others operate proprietary or alternative benchmarks on selected products. MSCI's blended realised rate of roughly 3.4bp (derived) leaves visible room to be negotiated down.
C
The ESG franchise is rolling over. Sustainability & Climate grew 3.4% in Q2 2026 against 8.4% for FY2025, with retention at 92.8%. This was sold to investors as a structural growth engine; it is currently the slowest-growing and second-least-retained segment. Politicised demand means the revenue is exposed to a variable no management team can forecast.
D
Financial leverage with no equity cushion. Total debt of $6.54B against negative shareholders' equity of −$2,690M and $352.7M of cash. Leverage was 3.3× adjusted EBITDA at FY2025, inside the company's own 3.0×–3.5× target. The business generates enough cash to carry it comfortably in normal conditions — but there is no book equity to absorb an impairment, and the current ratio of 0.89 means the company runs on negative working capital by design.

Five years of going nowhere. Here is the part that makes this stock analytically interesting rather than simply disappointing. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The operating engine is accelerating while the stock de-rates. Run Rate is $3,479.7M (+12.0%), organic subscription Run Rate growth improved to 8.1% from 7.7%, and retention rose to 95.3% from 94.4% — all three of the metrics management is judged on moved the right way in Q2 2026. ROIC of 39.93% on an 83% gross margin, free cash flow guided to $1,485M–$1,545M, and a share count shrinking 4.85% a year mean the per-share compounding continues even with a flat multiple. At 29.99× trailing — well below the multiple this franchise carried in 2021 — buying an index monopoly with 97.5% segment retention is a reasonable place to be wrong.

Bear Case

The growth is increasingly borrowed from the market. Asset-based fees are 27% of Run Rate and grew 26.6% in Q2 — but linked ETF AUM rose from $2,025B to $2,818B on only $39B of inflows, meaning market appreciation, not distribution wins, drove the beat. Strip that out and organic subscription growth is 8.1% on a 1.95 PEG. Sustainability & Climate has stalled at 3.4% growth with 92.8% retention, Analytics compounds at 6.6%, and the balance sheet carries $6.54B of debt against negative equity. A market drawdown hits asset-based fees and subscription budgets simultaneously.

01
Equity-market beta embedded in "recurring" revenue. $948.2M of the $2,027.0M Index Run Rate is asset-based. A sustained 20–25% global equity drawdown would remove roughly $200M+ of annualised revenue at ~100% incremental margin, with no client having churned. Investors buying MSCI as a defensive subscription business are underwriting more market exposure than the label implies.
02
Multiple risk on a 1.95 PEG. At 29.99× trailing and 25.86× forward against high-single-digit organic growth, a substantial portion of the valuation is durability, not growth. The five-year record shows exactly what happens when the market revises its durability assumption: the business grew 65% and the stock fell 18%. Nothing prevents a further de-rating toward the peer average of ~24.7×.
03
Two of four segments are growing below 5%. Sustainability & Climate (+3.4%, 92.8% retention) and Private Assets (+4.9%, 91.3% retention) together are 19.2% of revenue and are the two weakest-retained franchises. If Index carries the company alone, MSCI is a more concentrated — and more fee-compression-exposed — business than its four-segment presentation suggests.
Industry Commentary
Critical Constraint
Passive fee compression reaching the index licence
MSCI's customers are engaged in a decade-long race to zero on ETF expense ratios, and the index licence is one of the few genuinely variable costs in a passive fund. MSCI's derived blended realised rate is roughly 3.4 basis points on $2,706B of average linked ETF AUM. When a sponsor cuts an investor-facing fee from 9bp to 5bp, a 3.4bp licence is no longer a rounding error — it is most of the remaining cost base. The largest sponsors have the scale to self-index and have demonstrated willingness to do so on selected products. This is why Index revenue can grow 17.5% in a rising market and still not support a 2021 multiple: the market is pricing the rate, not just the assets.
Ongoing Headwind
The ESG demand reversal
Sustainability & Climate grew 8.4% in FY2025 and just 3.4% in Q2 2026, with retention slipping from 93.2% to 92.8%. US institutional appetite for discretionary ESG product has cooled under political and legal pressure on ESG mandates, while European demand has shifted from product development to regulatory compliance — stickier revenue, but price-capped, because compliance buyers purchase the minimum dataset the rule requires. At 3.4% growth against 92.8% retention, gross new sales are barely outrunning churn. This was pitched to investors as a structural growth engine and is currently the slowest-growing segment MSCI owns.
Emerging Tailwind — and How It Cuts Both Ways
Private markets need a benchmark standard
Private capital is the one large asset class without an agreed benchmark language, which is precisely the vacuum MSCI's index franchise was built to fill in public equities. Management flagged accelerating Run Rate growth in Private Capital Solutions in Q2 2026 even as reported segment revenue slowed to 4.9% — the two are reconcilable if new bookings are running ahead of recognised revenue.

Bull reading: MSCI is early in replicating the index playbook in a category where it can define the standard, and Run Rate is the leading indicator saying so — with reported revenue to follow within two or three quarters. Bear reading: MSCI is a challenger here, not an incumbent, against Preqin/BlackRock, PitchBook/Morningstar and StepStone; the 91.3% retention rate is the weakest in the company, the client base is not yet embedded, and Run Rate commentary that does not convert into reported revenue by Q1 2027 will read as management guiding on the most flattering available metric.
