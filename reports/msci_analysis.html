01 Company Overview

MSCI Inc. licenses equity indexes, risk and portfolio analytics, sustainability and climate data, and private-asset data to asset managers, asset owners, banks and hedge funds. Roughly $2.91 trillion of ETF assets were benchmarked to MSCI equity indexes at September 30, 2026 — the single most important number in the business, because it is the base on which MSCI's fastest-growing revenue line is charged. FY2025 revenue was $3,134.5M, up 9.7% from $2,856M, and the TTM figure through September 2026 is $3.43B, up 12.3%. At a $43.22B market capitalisation (71.5M shares × the $604.46 close, our calculation) the company ranks #260 in the S&P 500.

Judging MSCI on revenue misses the machine. Management runs the company on three operating metrics, and so should anyone valuing it: Run Rate (the annualised value of every recurring contract in force — $3,571.9M at September 30, 2026, up 12.1%), organic recurring subscription Run Rate growth (8.7% in Q3 2026, up from 8.1% in Q2 and 7.7% at year-end 2025), and the Retention Rate (95.0% in Q3 2026 versus 94.7% a year earlier). The central debate on this page: run-rate growth is accelerating and retention is holding up, yet the stock is still about 9% below its October 2021 month-end close five years on, while the S&P 500 rose about two thirds. The gap between those two facts is entirely a multiple story, and the question is whether the multiple compressed because the market re-rated quality down, or because it re-rated the source of growth — fees charged as a share of fund assets, which rise and fall with the stock market — as lower quality than the subscription revenue they are gradually overtaking.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
BENCHMARK STANDARD-SETTING SWITCHING COST = FUND RENAMING 97.1% INDEX RETENTION EMBEDDED RISK MODELS (BARRA) DATA HISTORY DEPTH AUM-LINKED ROYALTY 83% GROSS MARGIN

The moat is not the data — it is the coordination. An index only has value because everyone agrees to use the same one. Once a $50B ETF is named "MSCI Emerging Markets," switching the underlying index means a prospectus change, a shareholder communication, a tracking-error event, a rebalance cost borne by the fund's investors, and a period during which the fund's performance is not comparable to its own history. The ETF sponsor bears all of that cost to save a few basis points of licence fee. That asymmetry is why Index retention was 97.1% in Q3 2026 and why MSCI can charge a percentage of AUM rather than a flat fee for a dataset that costs essentially nothing to deliver a second time — gross margin of 82.89% and operating margin of 55.55% (trailing twelve months, stockanalysis.com) are the financial signature of a licence, not a service.

Key Vulnerabilities

A
Asset-based fees are a bet on the market dressed as a subscription. Asset-based fees are now $975.6M of the $2,082.7M Index Run Rate and 27% of the $3,571.9M total. ETF AUM linked to MSCI indexes rose from $2,211B to $2,905B year-on-year. Q3 itself was mostly new money ($76B of inflows against $11B of market gains), but a fee charged on asset value falls with the market whatever the flows. A 25% equity drawdown would take roughly a quarter off that revenue line with no client loss whatsoever, and would do so at exactly the moment clients are also cutting discretionary subscription budgets. The two revenue streams are positively correlated in a downturn, not offsetting.
B
Fee compression from the client's own price war. MSCI's largest customers are ETF sponsors running a decade-long race to zero on expense ratios. Every basis point they cut from an investor-facing fee has to come from somewhere, and the index licence is one of the few variable costs in a passive fund. The largest sponsors have already demonstrated they will self-index to escape the fee — BlackRock, Vanguard and others operate proprietary or alternative benchmarks on selected products. MSCI's blended realised rate of roughly 3.4bp (derived) leaves visible room to be negotiated down.
C
The ESG franchise is rolling over. Sustainability & Climate grew 2.2% organically in Q3 2026 (4.0% reported) against 8.4% for FY2025, with retention down to 91.0%. This was sold to investors as a structural growth engine; it is currently the slowest-growing and least-retained segment. Politicised demand means the revenue is exposed to a variable no management team can forecast.
D
Financial leverage with no equity cushion. Debt of $6.8B in principal ($6,716.9M on the balance sheet) against a shareholders' deficit of −$3,016.4M and $261.0M of cash at September 30, 2026. Total debt was 3.2× trailing adjusted EBITDA, inside the company's own 3.0×–3.5× target. The business generates enough cash to carry it comfortably in normal conditions — but there is no book equity to absorb an impairment, and the current ratio of 0.82 means the company runs on negative working capital by design.

RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 suggest the stock has risen a long way in a short time relative to its own history ("overbought"); below 30 suggests the opposite ("oversold"). It describes momentum, not value — a stock can stay overbought for months in a strong trend. This panel is calculated on monthly closes, so each point covers a month rather than a day.

Five years of going nowhere. MSCI closed October 2021 at $664.88 and October 21, 2026 at $604.46 — a price return of −9.1% over five years, before a dividend that has averaged well under 2%. Over the same window the S&P 500 (SPY as a stand-in, plain closes without dividends) went from $459.25 at the end of October 2021 to $773.09 on October 21, 2026, +68.3% — an underperformance of roughly 77 percentage points. Over the last year MSCI rose 11.9% against SPY's 15.2% (October 21, 2025 to October 21, 2026 closes).

Here is the part that makes this stock analytically interesting rather than simply disappointing. Over those five years revenue grew from $2,044M in FY2021 to $3,432M over the last twelve months, Run Rate reached $3,571.9M and ROIC is about 40%. The business grew about 68% and the stock fell 9%. That is a pure multiple de-rating — the market paid a far higher price for each dollar of MSCI's earnings in 2021 than it does now. Two readings are available. The charitable one: 2021 was a zero-rate bubble in every long-duration compounder and MSCI is simply completing a normalisation that is now largely done at ~32× trailing. The uncharitable one: the market has decided that a business where 27% of Run Rate is fees that rise and fall with stock-market levels, and whose second-largest growth story (ESG) has stalled, deserves a lower multiple permanently. Section 04's ROIC line supports the first reading; Section 02's asset-based-fee arithmetic supports the second.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The operating engine is accelerating while the multiple sits far below 2021's. Run Rate is $3,571.9M (+12.1%), organic subscription Run Rate growth improved again to 8.7% from 8.1% in Q2 and 7.7% at year-end 2025, retention was 95.0% against 94.7% a year earlier, and Q3 brought best-ever recurring net-new sales in Analytics and Private Capital Solutions (CEO, Q3 release). ROIC of 40.55% on an 83% gross margin, free cash flow guided to $1,485M–$1,545M, and $627.2M of buybacks in Q3 and early October mean the per-share compounding continues even with a flat multiple. At 31.98× trailing — well below the multiple this franchise carried in 2021 — buying an index monopoly with 97.1% segment retention is a reasonable place to be wrong.

Bear Case

The growth still leans on the market. Asset-based fees are 27% of Run Rate and grew 24.9% in Q3; record inflows helped this quarter, but a fee charged on asset value falls with the market whatever the flows. Strip it out and organic subscription Run Rate growth is 8.7% on a 1.95 PEG. Sustainability & Climate has stalled at 2.2% organic growth with 91.0% retention, Analytics revenue slowed to 4.8% with its segment margin down to 44.9% from 49.4%, and the balance sheet carries $6.8B of debt against a $3.0B shareholders' deficit. A market drawdown hits asset-based fees and subscription budgets simultaneously.

01
Equity-market beta embedded in "recurring" revenue. $975.6M of the $2,082.7M Index Run Rate is asset-based. A sustained 20–25% global equity drawdown would remove roughly $200M+ of annualised revenue, almost all of it profit because MSCI's costs would not fall with it, with no client having left. Investors buying MSCI as a defensive subscription business are underwriting more market exposure than the label implies.
02
Multiple risk on a 1.95 PEG. At 31.98× trailing and 27.00× 2027 consensus EPS against high-single-digit organic growth, a substantial portion of the valuation is durability, not growth. The five-year record shows exactly what happens when the market revises its durability assumption: the business grew about 68% and the stock fell 9%. Nothing prevents a further de-rating toward the peer average of ~26.6×.
03
Two of four segments grew below 5% in Q3. Analytics (+4.8%, 94.3% retention) and Sustainability & Climate (+4.0% reported, 2.2% organic, 91.0% retention) together are 31.9% of Q3 revenue. If Index carries the company alone, MSCI is a more concentrated — and more fee-compression-exposed — business than its four-segment presentation suggests.
Industry Commentary
Critical Constraint
Passive fee compression reaching the index licence
MSCI's customers are engaged in a decade-long race to zero on ETF expense ratios, and the index licence is one of the few genuinely variable costs in a passive fund. MSCI's derived blended realised rate is roughly 3.4 basis points on $2,878B of average linked ETF AUM in Q3 2026. When a sponsor cuts an investor-facing fee from 9bp to 5bp, a 3.4bp licence is no longer a rounding error — it is most of the remaining cost base. The largest sponsors have the scale to self-index and have demonstrated willingness to do so on selected products. This is why Index revenue can grow 18.1% in a rising market and still not support a 2021 multiple: the market is pricing the rate, not just the assets.
Ongoing Headwind
The ESG demand reversal
Sustainability & Climate grew 8.4% in FY2025, 3.4% in Q2 2026 and 2.2% organically in Q3 2026 (4.0% reported), with Q3 retention at 91.0% against 93.6% a year earlier. US institutional appetite for discretionary ESG product has cooled under political and legal pressure on ESG mandates, while European demand has shifted from product development to regulatory compliance — stickier revenue, but price-capped, because compliance buyers purchase the minimum dataset the rule requires. At 2.2% organic growth against 91.0% retention, gross new sales are barely outrunning churn. This was pitched to investors as a structural growth engine and is currently the slowest-growing segment MSCI owns.
Emerging Tailwind — and How It Cuts Both Ways
Private markets need a benchmark standard
Private capital is the one large asset class without an agreed benchmark language, which is precisely the vacuum MSCI's index franchise was built to fill in public equities. In Q3 2026 the segment's reported revenue growth picked up to 6.7% from 4.9% in Q2, Run Rate grew 8.9%, and the CEO cited best-ever recurring net-new sales in Private Capital Solutions — bookings running ahead of recognised revenue, with revenue starting to follow.

Bull reading: MSCI is early in replicating the index playbook in a category where it can define the standard, and Run Rate is the leading indicator saying so — Q3's revenue pick-up is the first sign of it. Bear reading: MSCI is a challenger here, not an incumbent, against Preqin/BlackRock, PitchBook/Morningstar and StepStone; segment retention has improved to 94.2% but the segment's adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 24.8% from 27.6%, and Run Rate commentary that does not keep converting into reported revenue by Q1 2027 will read as management guiding on the most flattering available metric.
