01 Company Overview

Strategy is the world's largest corporate holder of bitcoin — 845,050 BTC as of August 30, 2026, bought for an aggregate $63.73B (average cost $75,412 per coin) — wrapped around a small enterprise-analytics software business that produced $477.2M of FY2025 revenue, up 3.0%. The software is what the stock exchange's sector tags describe; the bitcoin is what the $54.87B market capitalisation is pricing. The company ranks roughly #451 globally by market cap, having lost 56.8% of its value over the past twelve months.

The debate this page is built around is a single ratio. At a bitcoin price of roughly $79,300, Strategy's coins are worth about $67.0B — more than the entire equity market cap. On the basic mNAV (market cap ÷ market value of bitcoin held) the stock trades at ~0.82×, a discount to its own bitcoin, after spending most of 2024–2025 at premiums of 1.5×–3×. That inversion breaks the machine the company was built on: issuing stock above NAV to buy bitcoin below it. Strategy spent June–August 2026 selling bitcoin (about 6,900 coins at ~$62,000) to fund preferred dividends and debt reduction, then in late August sold $602.8M of common stock at a discount to NAV to buy 4,603 coins back at $80,318. Whether the discount is a gift or a warning is the whole question.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Scale: 845k BTC, ~19× the #2 holder Capital-markets access ($44B+ ATM capacity) Preferred-stock franchise (STRC $10B) Index / options liquidity (Nasdaq-100) Saylor brand & retail following $6.7B USD reserve = dividend runway

The honest description of Strategy's advantage is that it is a capital-markets moat, not an operating one. Bitcoin is a commodity anyone can buy; what Strategy has that a spot ETF does not is the ability to issue securities other than shares — convertible notes at near-zero coupons in 2021–2024, and since 2025 a family of perpetual preferreds — and to use the proceeds to buy more bitcoin per share than the shares it issued represented. That works only while the common trades at a premium to NAV. The company's own metric, BTC Yield (the percentage change in bitcoin held per assumed diluted share), was 9.4% year-to-date as of May 3, 2026, against 26.0% YTD at the same point in 2025. It is worth being precise about what that number is: it is dilution accounting. It measures how much faster the coin pile grew than the share count. It is not a return on bitcoin, not a cash yield, and it can be positive while the stock falls 57% — which is exactly what happened over the past year, with shares outstanding up 24.6% YoY.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The premium is gone, so the flywheel runs backwards. With basic mNAV at ~0.82×, every dollar of common stock sold to buy bitcoin buys less bitcoin per share than existing holders already own. In the week of Aug 24–30 the company sold 4.53M shares for $602.8M (~$133/share, or ~0.00168 BTC per share at $79,300) while existing shares each represent 0.00220 BTC. That is BTC-per-share dilutive on the arithmetic shown here, even if some proceeds went to preferred buybacks and cash. The ATM machine only manufactures BTC Yield when mNAV is above 1.
▸
Fixed-cost preferred dividends on a zero-cash-flow asset. ~$1.4–1.5B a year (est.) of dividends must be paid from the USD reserve, new issuance, or bitcoin sales. Two of the three are pro-cyclical: preferred demand and bitcoin prices fall together. The summer 2026 sales — ~6,900 BTC near $62,000 — were the first time the "never sell" company sold, and it bought back at $80,318 ten weeks later.
▸
Cheaper substitutes now exist. Spot bitcoin ETFs deliver 1.00× NAV exposure for ~0.25%; a leveraged bitcoin ETF delivers the beta. The 2020–2024 case for paying a premium (no ETF, no easy institutional wrapper) has weakened structurally, which is one reason the premium collapsed.
▸
Accounting volatility as a governance cost. Under fair-value accounting (ASU 2023-08, adopted Jan 1, 2025) every quarter's bitcoin move hits net income. Q1 2026 alone booked a $14.46B unrealised loss; TTM net loss is $31.37B. The company itself has flagged the possibility of corporate alternative-minimum-tax exposure on unrealised gains in prior filings — not re-verified here, but a live consideration whenever bitcoin rallies.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What you actually own. One share of MSTR at $142.80 is a claim on 0.00220 BTC (~$174 at $79,300), minus a pro-rata share of roughly $8B of convertible debt and ~$13.5B of preferred stock that rank ahead of you, plus a pro-rata share of $6.7B of cash reserves and a flat, cash-neutral software business. The market is discounting that package at ~0.82× the bitcoin alone. Two things explain the discount: the senior claims (on this page's arithmetic, enterprise value ÷ bitcoin ≈ 1.04×, so the whole structure is not cheap — only the common is), and the loss of the mechanism that justified a premium. When the stock traded at 2× NAV, each $100 of new shares bought $100 of bitcoin for holders who had paid $200 for the same exposure; BTC per share rose every time the ATM ran. At 0.82×, the arithmetic reverses, and the company's late-August common issuance was — on the numbers shown in Section 03 — dilutive to bitcoin per share.

BTC Yield, translated. The company's headline KPI was 9.4% YTD at May 3, 2026. It is the percentage growth in bitcoin per assumed-diluted share. It is a useful number for exactly one thing — measuring whether the financing machine is accretive — and a misleading one for everything else. It is not income; there is no cash. It goes up when preferred stock or convertibles fund purchases (no new common shares) regardless of what bitcoin does, and it can be strongly positive in a year when the stock loses half its value, as it just was. Treat it as the company's dilution report card, and note that with the common below NAV and preferred issuance stalled (zero sold in the Aug 17–23 week), the report card is likely to weaken through the second half unless the premium returns.

The preferred stack is the real story of 2026. $13.5B of perpetual preferreds paying 8%–12% (~$1.4–1.5B a year, est.) is a fixed obligation against an asset that yields nothing. Management's answer is the $5.10B USD Reserve plus $1.61B USD Cash — about four years of dividends — and the argument that bitcoin's expected appreciation exceeds a 10–12% cost of capital. The summer sales showed what happens when both bitcoin and the preferreds fall at once: the company sold coins at a realised loss and bought them back 29% higher. The preferreds trading below par (STRK $74, STRD $72 on a $100 preference) says the market currently prices that risk at 11%–15%.

Bull Case

You are buying 845,050 bitcoin at an 18% discount through the common, with a $6.7B cash buffer covering four years of preferred dividends and no meaningful debt maturity until 2027–28. If bitcoin recovers toward its $126,000 high, the fair-value mark alone is a ~$39B reported gain, the premium historically re-expands in bull phases, and the preferred franchise reopens at lower yields — a three-way lever on the way up. Sixteen analysts see $224.60 on average; beta of 3.6 works in both directions.

Bear Case

The discount is rational: enterprise value already exceeds the bitcoin (~1.04×), the flywheel is dilutive below 1× mNAV, and ~$1.5B a year of preferred dividends must be paid from reserves, new issuance or coin sales — the company has now done all three. Spot ETFs offer the same exposure at 0.25% with no senior claims. A bitcoin round-trip to $60,000 puts the coins $13B under water on cost, shrinks the reserve's runway, and forces the choice between selling bitcoin or issuing common at an even deeper discount. Average cost $75,412 vs spot $79,300 leaves a 5% cushion.

Bitcoin price. Everything else is derivative. At 845,050 coins, each $1,000 move is $845M of equity value and reported income; a return to the June 2026 lows (~$60,000) would be a ~$16B fair-value loss and would push the position ~$13B below cost.

Funding the preferred dividends without a premium.   Industry Commentary
The Treasury-Company Premium Has Collapsed Sector-Wide
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2024–2025 boom in "bitcoin treasury companies" rested on one trade: sell stock at a premium to NAV, buy bitcoin, report BTC Yield, repeat. Strategy is the template and the largest by a factor of ~19 over the next holder (Twenty One Capital, 43,514 BTC per bitcointreasuries.net). With the leader itself at ~0.82× basic mNAV and having sold coins to pay dividends, the sector's business model is in question. For MSTR specifically, the constraint is that the largest and most liquid version of the trade is now a discount vehicle — and discount vehicles historically close the gap through buybacks or liquidation, both of which mean selling bitcoin, not buying it.
Fair-Value Accounting Makes the Income Statement a Price Chart
ONGOING HEADWIND
Since ASU 2023-08 took effect in 2025, Strategy has reported quarterly net income of +$10.0B, +$2.6B, −$12.6B, −$12.5B (restated on the release as −$12.77B attributable to common) and −$8.6B in five consecutive quarters on $111M–$129M of revenue. Screens, index methodologies and quantitative funds ingest those numbers as "earnings," which drives forced flows in both directions and inflates realised volatility (beta 3.60). It also creates a recurring headline problem: a "record loss" quarter when bitcoin falls, a "record profit" when it rises, neither of which changes the coin count.
Bitcoin Back Above $80,000 — With a Buyer of Last Resort Returning
EMERGING TAILWIND
Bitcoin's recovery from roughly $60,000 in June to above $80,000 on Sep 3 has restored a ~$3B unrealised gain on Strategy's book and let management restart purchases. Bull framing: the discount to NAV is the best entry the stock has offered since 2023; if bitcoin holds and preferred demand returns, the flywheel resumes and the premium historically re-expands quickly. Bear framing: the company just demonstrated that it will sell coins into weakness and buy them back into strength — the opposite of what a "permanent capital" holder is supposed to do — and the next leg of preferred dividends ($350M+ per quarter, est.) is due regardless of where bitcoin trades. The cushion between average cost ($75,412) and spot ($79,300) is about 5%.
