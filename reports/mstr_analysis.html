01 Company Overview

Strategy is the largest institutional holder of bitcoin — 849,327 BTC as of November 1, 2026, bought for an aggregate $64.08B (average cost $75,451 per coin) — wrapped around a small enterprise-analytics software business with $495.1M of revenue over the last twelve months. The software is what the stock exchange's sector tags describe; the bitcoin is what the $55.38B market capitalisation is pricing. The stock has lost 51.5% since the October 31, 2025 close, while the S&P 500 (SPY) gained 10.3%.

The debate this page is built around is a single ratio. At bitcoin's November 2 price of about $76,700, Strategy's coins are worth about $65.1B — more than the entire equity market cap. On the basic mNAV (market cap ÷ market value of bitcoin held) the stock trades at ~0.85×, a discount to its own bitcoin, after spending most of 2024–2025 at premiums of 1.5×–3×. That inversion breaks the machine the company was built on: selling new shares for more than the bitcoin behind each one, and using the money to buy more coins per share. The third quarter showed both faces of it. Bitcoin rose about 43% and the accounting turned that into a record $19.12B of net income to common, $44.75 a diluted share. Yet the company sold $5.41B of new common stock and 5,552 bitcoin (to help pay preferred dividends and buy back its STRC preferred), and bitcoin per share is now 3.7% lower than at the start of the year on the company's own count. Whether the discount is a gift or a warning is the whole question.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Scale: 849k BTC, ~4% of all bitcoin Capital-markets access ($43.9B ATM capacity) Preferred-stock franchise (STRC near par) Index / options liquidity (Nasdaq-100) Saylor brand & retail following $4.9B USD reserve = dividend runway

The honest description of Strategy's advantage is that it is a capital-markets moat, not an operating one. Bitcoin is a commodity anyone can buy; what Strategy has that a spot ETF does not is the ability to issue securities other than shares — convertible notes at near-zero coupons in 2021–2024, and since 2025 a family of perpetual preferreds — and to use the proceeds to buy more bitcoin per share than the shares it issued represented. That works only while the common trades above the value of the bitcoin behind it. The company's own metric, BTC Yield (the percentage change in bitcoin held per assumed diluted share), is −3.7% for 2026 to date in the Q3 release; on the call CFO Andrew Kang said: "Year to date, Bitcoin per share is 3.7% lower …" It is worth being precise about what that number is: it is dilution accounting. It measures how much faster the coin pile grew than the share count. It is not a return on bitcoin and not a cash yield. This year it turned negative because the share count (up 21.9% in a year, StockAnalysis) grew faster than the coin pile, partly because the company sold coins.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The premium is gone, so the flywheel runs backwards. With basic mNAV at ~0.85×, every dollar of common stock sold to buy bitcoin buys less bitcoin per share than existing holders already own. In the week of Oct 26–Nov 1 the company sold 1.18M shares for $164.0M (about $139 a share). At the $80,846 it paid per coin that week, $139 buys about 0.00172 BTC, while each existing share represents 0.00201 BTC. That is dilutive to bitcoin per share on this arithmetic, and only $65.5M of the proceeds bought bitcoin; $98.5M went to cash (8-K, Nov 2). Management's stated aim is the reverse: on the Q3 call CEO Phong Le described issuing equity "above 1x mNAV" to pay the preferred dividends.
▸
Fixed-cost preferred dividends on an asset with no cash flow. Q3's $419.6M of preferred dividends (about $1.7B a year at that pace, est.) must be paid from the USD reserve, new issuance, or bitcoin sales. Two of the three get harder at the same moment: demand for the preferreds and the bitcoin price tend to fall together. In Q3 the company sold about 5,552 BTC for $348.6M; the release says the proceeds funded $239.9M of preferred dividends and $108.6M of STRC buybacks. Year to date it has sold about $431.8M of bitcoin, while buying $14.33B.
▸
Cheaper substitutes now exist. Spot bitcoin ETFs deliver 1.00× NAV exposure for about 0.25% a year (iShares Bitcoin Trust); a leveraged bitcoin ETF delivers the extra swing. The 2020–2024 case for paying a premium (no ETF, no easy way for institutions to own bitcoin) has weakened, which is one reason the premium collapsed.
▸
Index rules aimed at companies that mainly hold assets. On Oct 15, 2026 MSCI finalised eligibility rules for "non-operating" companies, tested on operating assets and five financial ratios, starting with its November index review; The Block reported that Strategy is at risk of exclusion and that MSCI will publish its list of affected companies on Nov 11. A removal would mean selling by funds that track MSCI indexes.
▸
Accounting volatility as a governance cost. Under fair-value accounting (ASU 2023-08, adopted Jan 1, 2025) every quarter's bitcoin move hits net income. Q3 2026 booked a $20.91B gain on digital assets; over the last twelve months the net loss to common is still $14.89B. The company had expected to owe corporate alternative minimum tax on unrealised gains; after Treasury and IRS interim guidance of Sep 30, 2025 let companies disregard them, it said in an Oct 1, 2025 8-K that it no longer expects to. It still booked a $1.41B income-tax provision in Q3 2026.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. For MSTR it is really a momentum gauge on bitcoin with the volume turned up — it is not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 month-end close of $72.14 (split-adjusted) to the November 2, 2026 close of $130.76, MSTR rose +81.3%, about 13% a year compounded, with no dividend. Almost nobody experienced it that way. The stock fell 80% from that November 2021 close to $14.16 in December 2022 as bitcoin broke below $17,000 and the company wrote down its coins under the old accounting rules, which only allowed write-downs. It then rose 28-fold — from $14.16 to a monthly-close peak of $404.23 in June 2025 — as bitcoin ETFs launched, bitcoin went from about $16,500 to over $100,000, and the premium over the value of the coins widened to 2–3× at the November 2024 peak. Since June 2025 it has fallen 68%, with the June 2026 close of $86.93 marking the low. Q3 2026 brought a 76% rebound as bitcoin rose 43%; October gave back 14.7% ($153.09 → $130.51). The one-year change of −51.5% compares with bitcoin's 39% fall from its October 2025 intraday high: the stock has fallen harder than its coins, the extra decline coming from the premium turning into a discount.

Relative performance. Recovery without a premium
08 Summary & Key Risks

What you actually own. One share of MSTR at $130.76 is a claim on 0.00201 BTC (~$154 at $76,677), minus a pro-rata share of $6.7B of convertible debt and $13.1B of preferred stock that rank ahead of you, plus a pro-rata share of $4.9B of cash reserves and a flat software business that roughly breaks even on cash. The market is pricing that package at ~0.85× the bitcoin alone. Two things explain the discount: the senior claims (on this page's arithmetic, enterprise value ÷ bitcoin ≈ 1.08×, so the whole structure is not cheap — only the common is), and the loss of the mechanism that justified a premium. When the stock traded at 2× NAV, each $100 of new shares bought $100 of bitcoin for holders who had paid $200 for the same exposure; bitcoin per share rose every time the ATM ran. At 0.85× the arithmetic reverses, and the common the company sold in the week to Nov 1 was — on the numbers shown in Section 03 — dilutive to bitcoin per share.

BTC Yield, translated. The company's headline KPI is −3.7% for 2026 to date (Q3 release). It is the percentage growth in bitcoin per assumed-diluted share. It is a useful number for exactly one thing — measuring whether the financing machine adds bitcoin per share — and a misleading one for everything else. It is not income; there is no cash. It goes up when preferred stock or convertibles fund purchases (no new common shares) regardless of what bitcoin does, and goes down when common is sold below NAV or coins are sold. This year both happened: no preferred stock was sold through the ATM programs in Q3, $5.41B of common was, and the company sold 5,552 coins. Treat it as the company's dilution report card. It turned negative this year and will stay under pressure unless the premium returns or preferred issuance restarts.

The preferred stack is the real story of 2026. $13.1B of perpetual preferreds paying 8%–12% ($419.6M of dividends in Q3, about $1.7B a year at that pace, est.) is a fixed obligation against an asset that yields nothing. Management's answer is the $4.79B USD Reserve plus $132.7M of USD Cash — about 3.4 years of dividends and interest on the company's own count — and the argument that bitcoin's expected appreciation exceeds a 10–12% cost of capital. This year showed what happens when bitcoin and the preferreds fall at once: the company sold coins below its average cost to pay dividends and buy back STRC. It has since spent $2.04B pulling STRC back toward par ($99.28 on Nov 2) and moved it to daily dividends, but STRK ($67.12) and STRD ($69.65) on a $100 preference say the market still prices that risk at 12%–14%.

Bull Case

You are buying 849,327 bitcoin at a 15% discount through the common, with a $4.9B cash buffer covering about 3.4 years of preferred dividends and interest, and $1.05B of debt due within a year. If bitcoin recovers to its $126,198 high, the fair-value mark alone is a ~$42B reported gain, the premium historically re-expands in bull phases, and the preferred franchise reopens at lower yields — a three-way lever on the way up. Saylor says "We're at the beginning of the next bitcoin bull market." Sixteen analysts see $234.27 on average; a beta of 3.6 works in both directions.

Bear Case

The discount is rational: enterprise value already exceeds the bitcoin (~1.08×), selling common below 1× mNAV cuts bitcoin per share (BTC Yield −3.7% this year), and preferred dividends running at about $1.7B a year must be paid from reserves, new issuance or coin sales — the company has done all three in 2026. Spot ETFs offer the same exposure at 0.25% with no senior claims. MSCI may drop the stock from its indexes. A bitcoin round-trip to $60,000 puts the coins $13B under water on cost, shrinks the reserve's runway, and forces the choice between selling bitcoin or issuing common at an even deeper discount. Average cost $75,451 vs spot $76,677 leaves a 1.6% cushion.

  Industry Commentary
The Treasury-Company Premium Has Collapsed Sector-Wide
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2024–2025 boom in "bitcoin treasury companies" rested on one trade: sell stock at a premium to NAV, buy bitcoin, report BTC Yield, repeat. Strategy is the template and by far the largest. With the leader itself at ~0.85× basic mNAV, BTC Yield negative for the year and coins sold to pay dividends, the sector's business model is in question, and index providers are now writing rules for it: MSCI's October 2026 screen for companies that mainly hold assets could push Strategy out of its indexes. For MSTR specifically, the constraint is that the largest and most liquid version of the trade is now a discount vehicle — and discount vehicles historically close the gap through buybacks or liquidation, both of which mean selling bitcoin, not buying it.
Fair-Value Accounting Makes the Income Statement a Price Chart
ONGOING HEADWIND
Since ASU 2023-08 took effect in 2025, Strategy has reported net income to common of +$2.65B (Q3 2025), −$12.62B, −$12.77B, −$8.62B and +$19.12B (Q3 2026) in five consecutive quarters, on $122M–$129M of quarterly revenue. Stock screens, index rules and computer-driven funds take those numbers in as "earnings," which can force buying and selling in both directions and adds to the stock's swings (beta 3.57). It also creates a recurring headline problem: a "record loss" quarter when bitcoin falls, a "record profit" when it rises — Q3 2026's operating income was, in CFO Andrew Kang's words, "the largest ever in Strategy's history" — and none of them changes the coin count.
Bitcoin's Rebound Stalled Just Above Strategy's Cost
MIXED
Bitcoin rose from $58,559 at the end of June to $83,554 at the end of September (Yahoo Finance month-end closes), which produced the record Q3 profit, then slipped to $76,677 by Nov 2 — leaving Strategy about $1.0B (1.6%) above its $64.08B cost. Bull framing: the discount to NAV is the best entry the stock has offered since 2023; if bitcoin holds and preferred demand returns, the flywheel resumes and the premium historically re-expands quickly. Bear framing: the company has shown that it will sell coins into weakness and buy into strength — the opposite of what a "permanent capital" holder is supposed to do — and the next quarter of preferred dividends (Q3: $419.6M) is due regardless of where bitcoin trades. The cushion between average cost ($75,451) and spot ($76,677) is about 1.6%.
