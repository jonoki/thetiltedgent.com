01 Company Overview

Micron Technology is the only U.S.-based manufacturer of memory semiconductors, producing DRAM (dynamic random-access memory — the working memory in servers, PCs, and phones), NAND flash (persistent storage), and HBM (high-bandwidth memory — stacked DRAM bolted directly onto AI accelerators). It ranks third in global DRAM by revenue (~25% share, closing fast on SK Hynix's ~26%) and second in HBM, and has become one of the primary beneficiaries of the AI memory supercycle. Fiscal 2025 (ended August 28, 2025) revenue was $37.38B, up 49% YoY — and the trailing twelve months through May 2026 have exploded to $90.27B (+167%) as DRAM pricing repriced dramatically higher, briefly carrying Micron's market cap above $1 trillion in June 2026.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Only U.S. Memory Maker 1-gamma DRAM Node Lead HBM4 Ramp 2x Faster $100B Contracted Minimums CHIPS Act ($6.4B Grants) DRAM + NAND Full Stack Auto Qualification Lock-in

Memory is a brutal capital-intensity oligopoly — three players control ~90% of DRAM — and the moat is scale plus process technology plus, uniquely for Micron, geography. Its 1-gamma DRAM node (using EUV — extreme ultraviolet lithography) is competitive with or ahead of Samsung and SK Hynix, its HBM4 is ramping roughly twice as fast as HBM3E did with 11 Gbps parts sampling, and it is the only memory supplier headquartered and expanding fabs in the United States — worth real money in an era of export controls, tariffs, and sovereign-AI procurement. The newest layer of moat is contractual: 16 Strategic Customer Agreements signed in FY26 cover ~20% of DRAM and ~one-third of NAND volume through calendar 2030, with ~$100B of cumulative minimum revenue and $22B of customer cash deposits — converting the most cyclical business in semiconductors into something partially resembling a take-or-pay utility. That said, memory moats are historically leaky: pricing power evaporates whenever the three oligopolists (plus now China's CXMT) all add capacity at once.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸Extreme cyclicality. Micron lost $5.8B in FY2023; it earned more than that in a single month of Q3 FY26. Both are the same company. Current 85% gross margins are a pricing phenomenon, not a structural one — every prior memory peak (2018, 2021) was followed by ASP collapses of 50%+ within 18 months.
▸Chinese competition (CXMT). CXMT grew DRAM revenue ~716% YoY and is undercutting on price in commodity segments. It cannot yet touch HBM, but every commodity bit it ships pressures the conventional DRAM pricing that currently drives Micron's 87%-margin CDBU/MCBU units.
▸Customer concentration. Over 50% of FY25 revenue came from the top ten customers, and roughly half from data center. A capex pause by two or three hyperscalers — or an AI infrastructure digestion phase — would hit the highest-margin revenue first.
▸Rival capacity response. Samsung (targeting 30%+ HBM share by 2027) and SK Hynix are both scaling capacity aggressively into 2027, and HBM3E pricing has already softened YoY. Q2 FY26-era shortage pricing invites exactly the supply response that ends supercycles.

Most importantly — not technically one-off but functionally so — current 85%+ gross margins are the product of a once-in-decades pricing dislocation; mid-cycle memory gross margins have historically averaged 30–40%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

The AI memory shortage is structural, not cyclical: capacity is sold out through 2026, HBM4 is ramping twice as fast as its predecessor, and $100B of contracted minimums with $22B of cash deposits put a floor under 2027–2030 economics that no prior memory cycle ever had. At a 6.1x forward P/E with $26B of TTM FCF, net cash, and Q4 guidance of $50B revenue at 86% gross margin, even a partial normalization leaves the stock cheap — and the July correction already took out 31% of the froth.

▼ Bear Case

Every memory supercycle in history ended the same way: windfall margins triggered a synchronized capacity response, and 85% gross margins became 30% within two years. Samsung is gunning for 30%+ HBM share in 2027, CXMT grew 716% YoY in commodity DRAM, HBM3E pricing is already softening, and Micron itself is adding fabs in Idaho, Taiwan, and Singapore by mid-2027. Trailing P/E of ~20 on peak earnings could easily be 40x+ on mid-cycle earnings — the "cheap" multiple is the trap.

01Cycle reversal / ASP collapse. Micron's earnings are a leveraged bet on memory pricing. If DRAM contract prices mean-revert even halfway from the current dislocation, consensus FY27–28 estimates (and the $1,502 average target built on them) break.
02AI capex concentration. Over half of revenue traces to data center and the top ten customers exceed 50% of sales. A hyperscaler digestion phase — even a two-quarter pause — hits the 83–87% margin business units first and hardest.
03Competitive/geopolitical supply shocks. Samsung's HBM4 qualification, SK Hynix's capacity adds, CXMT's subsidized expansion, and any China retaliation (the 2023 CAC ban precedent) can each independently compress pricing or access to ~a quarter of end demand. Beta of 2.21 means the stock will overshoot any of these in both directions.
  Industry Commentary — The Memory Supercycle
The AI Memory Supply Crisis CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

AI data-center buildouts consumed memory supply faster than any demand shock in industry history. Conventional DRAM ASPs rose in the low-60s percent range and NAND in the mid-80s percent range in a single quarter (Micron Q3 FY26, QoQ); total DRAM industry revenue is annualizing above $400B versus ~$100B in 2024. All three major producers are effectively sold out into 2027, and PC/phone OEMs are now bidding against hyperscalers for wafers. This is the entire earnings story — Micron's bit shipments grew only modestly; price did almost all the work.

China's Commodity DRAM Push (CXMT) ONGOING HEADWIND

CXMT grew DRAM revenue ~716% YoY (Nanya +690%) by underpricing the oligopoly in DDR4/LPDDR4-class commodity parts, and Chinese self-sufficiency policy guarantees continued subsidized expansion. It cannot produce HBM at scale and remains years behind on advanced nodes, but its bits cap the upside of exactly the conventional DRAM pricing that currently generates Micron's fattest margins. The July 2026 correction across memory stocks was, in large part, the market pricing in this ceiling.

Contracts vs. the Cycle — What Breaks First? EMERGING TAILWIND

The genuinely new variable this cycle is contract structure: Micron's 16 Strategic Customer Agreements (~$100B minimums, $22B deposits, through 2030) and similar deals across the industry attempt to convert spot-price chaos into utility-like visibility. Bull implication: if the contracts hold, 2027–2028 earnings troughs are far shallower than any prior cycle, and today's 6x forward P/E is simply wrong. Bear implication: take-or-pay memory contracts have been renegotiated or walked in every prior glut — deposits soften the blow but don't repeal the cycle. Which of these is true is, in effect, the entire debate embedded in the $361–$2,200 analyst target range.
