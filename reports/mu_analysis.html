01 Company Overview

Micron Technology is the only U.S.-based manufacturer of memory semiconductors, producing DRAM (dynamic random-access memory — the working memory in servers, PCs, and phones), NAND flash (persistent storage), and HBM (high-bandwidth memory — stacked DRAM bolted directly onto AI accelerators). It ranks third in global DRAM by revenue (~25% share, closing fast on SK Hynix's ~26%) and third in HBM (18% in Q2 2026, behind SK Hynix and Samsung, per Counterpoint), and has become one of the primary beneficiaries of the AI memory supercycle. Fiscal 2026 — a 53-week year that ended September 3, 2026 — brought record revenue of $133.19B, up 256% YoY from $37.38B, and GAAP net income of $84.97B. The fourth quarter alone ($54.23B) was larger than all of fiscal 2025 combined, because memory prices kept climbing: management put the quarter's DRAM price increase in the high teens and NAND's at about 30%. At the October 2, 2026 close Micron was worth about $1.21 trillion (1.13B shares at the 10-Q's June 17 count × $1,074.89, our calculation).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Only U.S. Memory Maker 1-gamma DRAM Node Lead HBM4 Ramp 2x Faster 26 Strategic Customer Agreements CHIPS Act ($6.4B Grants) DRAM + NAND Full Stack Auto Qualification Lock-in

Memory is a brutal capital-intensity oligopoly — three players control ~90% of DRAM — and the moat is scale plus process technology plus, uniquely for Micron, geography. Its 1-gamma DRAM node (using EUV — extreme ultraviolet lithography) is competitive with or ahead of Samsung and SK Hynix, its HBM4 12-high was ramping twice as fast as HBM3E 12-high, management said in June, and it is the only memory supplier headquartered and expanding fabs in the United States — worth real money in an era of export controls, tariffs, and sovereign-AI procurement. The newest layer of moat is contractual, and it grew this quarter: Micron has signed 26 Strategic Customer Agreements (up from 16 at the June print), which management estimates at over 35% of its revenue through 2030, backed by $32B of customer financial commitments, the vast majority in cash deposits. On the balance sheet, customer contract liabilities, mostly these deposits, were $0.42B at May 28 (10-Q); the noncurrent portion alone was $12.9B at September 3. The aim is to turn the most cyclical business in semiconductors into something partly resembling a take-or-pay utility. That said, memory moats are historically leaky: pricing power evaporates whenever the three oligopolists (plus now China's CXMT) all add capacity at once.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸Extreme cyclicality. Micron lost $5.8B in FY2023; it earned $37.7B in the single quarter to September 2026. Both are the same company. Current 87% gross margins are a pricing phenomenon, not a structural one — the last peak, in fiscal 2022, was followed by that FY2023 loss.
▸Chinese competition (CXMT). CXMT grew DRAM revenue ~716% YoY and is undercutting on price in commodity segments. It cannot yet touch HBM, but every commodity bit it ships pressures the conventional DRAM pricing that currently drives Micron's 90%-gross-margin CDBU/MCBU units.
▸Customer concentration. About half of revenue in each of the last three fiscal years came from the top ten customers (FY25 10-K), and Cloud Memory plus Core Data Center were 63% of Q4 FY26 revenue. A capex pause by two or three hyperscalers — or an AI infrastructure digestion phase — would hit the highest-margin revenue first.
▸Rival capacity response. Samsung (which lifted its HBM share from 21% to 33% between Q1 and Q2 2026, passing Micron, per Counterpoint) and SK Hynix are both scaling capacity aggressively, and Micron itself is adding fabs from 2027. Management expects supply to be even tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in 2026; Goldman Sachs, holding a Neutral rating, worries instead that extra industry supply could pressure HBM pricing in 2028. Shortage pricing invites exactly the supply response that ends supercycles.

08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

The AI memory shortage is structural, not cyclical: management expects supply to be tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in 2026, the vast majority of 2027 HBM supply is already contracted at higher prices, and 26 Strategic Customer Agreements — over 35% of revenue through 2030 by management's estimate, backed by $32B of commitments — put a floor under the coming years that no prior memory cycle had. At 14.5x trailing and 6.7x forward earnings, with $62.3B of FY26 free cash flow, $68B of net cash and a guide to $61.5B of quarterly revenue at about 86% gross margin, even a partial normalization leaves the stock cheap — and capital returns are about to rise.

▼ Bear Case

Every memory supercycle has ended the same way: windfall margins trigger a synchronized capacity response, and peak margins collapse. Samsung took its HBM share from 21% to 33% in a single quarter, CXMT grew 716% YoY in commodity DRAM, Goldman worries about HBM supply in 2028, and Micron itself brings Singapore, Idaho and Taiwan capacity online in 2027. A 14.5x trailing P/E on 87%-gross-margin earnings could be several times that on mid-cycle earnings — the "cheap" multiple is the trap, and part of today's cash flow is customers' prepayments.

01Cycle reversal / ASP collapse. Micron's earnings are a leveraged bet on memory pricing. If DRAM contract prices mean-revert even halfway from the current dislocation, consensus FY27–28 estimates (and the $1,520 average target built on them) break.
02AI capex concentration. Cloud Memory and Core Data Center were 63% of Q4 FY26 revenue, and the top ten customers have supplied about half of revenue in each of the last three fiscal years. A hyperscaler digestion phase — even a two-quarter pause — hits the 83–90% gross-margin business units first and hardest.
03Competitive/geopolitical supply shocks. Samsung's HBM4 qualification, SK Hynix's capacity adds, CXMT's subsidized expansion, and any China retaliation (the 2023 CAC ban precedent) can each independently compress pricing or access to Chinese customers. Beta of 2.22 means the stock will overshoot any of these in both directions.
  Industry Commentary — The Memory Supercycle
The AI Memory Supply Crisis CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

AI data-center buildouts consumed memory supply faster than any demand shock in industry history. After conventional DRAM prices rose in the low-60s percent range in Micron's Q3 FY26, they rose again in Q4 — by a high-teens percentage for DRAM and about 30% for NAND, on mid-single-digit DRAM and about 10% NAND bit growth, per CFO Mark Murphy on the call. CEO Sanjay Mehrotra said Micron expects memory and storage supply-demand conditions to be much tighter in fiscal 2027 and 2028 than in 2026, and PC/phone makers are bidding against hyperscalers for wafers. This is the entire earnings story — Micron's bit shipments grew only modestly; price did almost all the work.

China's Commodity DRAM Push (CXMT) ONGOING HEADWIND

CXMT grew DRAM revenue ~716% YoY (Nanya +690%) by underpricing the oligopoly in DDR4/LPDDR4-class commodity parts, and Chinese self-sufficiency policy guarantees continued subsidized expansion. It cannot produce HBM at scale and remains years behind on advanced nodes, but its bits cap the upside of exactly the conventional DRAM pricing that currently generates Micron's fattest margins. CXMT's Shanghai listing in late July 2026 renewed exactly that worry, and Yahoo Finance named it among the causes of that month's sell-off in memory stocks.

Contracts vs. the Cycle — What Breaks First? EMERGING TAILWIND

The genuinely new variable this cycle is contract structure: Micron's 26 Strategic Customer Agreements (over 35% of revenue through 2030 by management's estimate, $32B of commitments, mostly cash deposits) and similar deals across the industry attempt to convert spot-price chaos into utility-like visibility. Bull implication: if the contracts hold, 2027–2028 earnings troughs are far shallower than any prior cycle, and today's 6.7x forward P/E is simply wrong. Bear implication: take-or-pay memory contracts have been renegotiated or walked in every prior glut — deposits soften the blow but don't repeal the cycle. Which of these is true is, in effect, the entire debate embedded in the $361–$2,200 analyst target range.
