01 Company Overview

Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group is Japan's largest bank holding company and, at a $259.6B market value, the #1 company in Japan by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap country ranking — ahead of SoftBank Group and Toyota) and #61 globally. Its home listing is on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, where it is a Nikkei 225 constituent; the NYSE line is an ADR at one ADR per ordinary share. For the fiscal year ended March 2026 (MUFG calls it "FY2025") it reported J-GAAP net profits of ¥2,427.2B, up from ¥1,862.9B (+30.3%), and an ROE of 11.3% — its best since formation. Profit is earned roughly 44% in Japan, 35% in the Americas and 16% in Asia (MUFG FY2025 IR presentation).

The premise that brought us here is that Japan's exit from negative rates is reflating the bank's interest margin. That premise is only partly right, and the numbers say so. The Bank of Japan ended negative rates in March 2024 and MUFG's FY2026 plan assumes a policy rate of about 1%. Net interest income did grow — to ¥3,006.2B, +¥129.6B (+4.5%) in FY2025 — but that was the smallest of the profit drivers. Gross profits rose ¥1,125.1B. The bigger contributors were Global Markets, which swung from a ¥649.4B operating loss to a ¥57.7B profit as bond-portfolio losses rolled off; fees and commissions, +¥299.7B; income from equity-method investees (Morgan Stanley is the principal one; its exact share was not verified), ¥845.5B vs ¥596.9B; and gains from selling off cross-shareholdings. Domestic corporate loan spreads actually narrowed (0.69% → 0.67%). So the rate story is real, but it lives in deposit repricing rather than lending spreads, and so far it is a supporting actor. The central question for the stock at 1.74x book: how much of an 11–12% ROE is recurring banking, and how much is a one-time harvest of gains on equity stakes?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Japan's largest deposit franchise Low loan-to-deposit funding 24% of Morgan Stanley Project finance leadership Asian bank platform (Krungsri, Danamon) G-SIB scale (¥433.9T assets)

The moat is cheap funding that has finally started to pay. For a decade under zero and negative rates, a huge yen deposit base earned almost nothing, because there was nowhere positive to put it. Group total assets were ¥433.9T at June 30, 2026, and the loan-to-deposit ratio is low (about 53%, per IR Tracker). With rates above zero, that same deposit base is the earnings lever. Deposit-rich CWM is where it shows up most. The Morgan Stanley stake gives MUFG a US investment-banking and wealth franchise it could not have built itself. MUFG also cites 16 straight years as No.1 in project finance (IR presentation). The weakness is that most of these advantages depend on conditions MUFG does not control: the BOJ's path, the yen, and Morgan Stanley's earnings.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
ROE partly depends on selling equity stakes. MUFG's FY2026 target is ROE of about 12%. Its own plan puts ROE excluding gains on equity sales at about 10.4%. The gap of about 1.6 points comes from selling cross-shareholdings, and that pool is finite: acquisition cost is down to ¥0.92T. As the pool shrinks, recurring earnings have to replace the gains.
02
The capital buffer is thin on the measure management uses. Regulatory CET1 of 12.95% looks comfortable. The management-basis 9.2% at March 2026 (down from 10.8%) is below the target range, after the Shriram Finance deal and RWA growth. The CFO's "returning to the target range quickly should come first" explains why buybacks are capped at ¥100B for H1 FY2026.
03
The rate story assumes BOJ follow-through. The plan assumes a policy rate of about 1%. If the BOJ pauses, or reverses in a downturn, the ~¥180B-per-25bp sensitivity works in reverse on the increments not yet banked. Domestic loan spreads are not widening (JCIB 0.69% → 0.67%), so the benefit rests on deposit pricing staying sticky.
04
Morgan Stanley is a large single-name exposure. A 24.12% stake in a capital-markets business means a US investment-banking downturn reaches MUFG's income statement through equity-method earnings. Resuming sales into Morgan Stanley's buybacks trims the stake at the margin; it does not remove the exposure.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the FY2025 comparison. First, the Global Markets swing: the FY2024 base included a ¥649.4B operating loss from repositioning the bond portfolio, so part of FY2025's growth is simply that loss not recurring. Second, gains on cross-shareholding sales are recurring for now but finite; management's own ex-gains ROE target is ~10.4% against the ~12% headline. Third, FX added roughly ¥200B to gross profits and ¥100B to expenses as the yen weakened (USD/JPY 149.52 at end-FY2024 to 159.88 in March 2026, per Fintech Observer). Fourth, accounting basis: J-GAAP profits (¥2,427.2B) and the US GAAP figures StockAnalysis uses for annual periods (¥1,729.4B) differ by about ¥700B for the same year. Any trailing multiple on this stock depends on which basis you pick.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value, and a stock can stay overbought for a long time.

Momentum is stretched. The 6-period monthly RSI is 87.5, and it has been above 70 for most of the past three years. The only real breaks were the sell-offs of August 2024 and March 2026. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Japan now has positive rates, and MUFG has the country's largest deposit base to earn on. It discloses ~¥180B of extra net interest income per 25bp and books ~¥140B from hikes already made. Q1 FY2026 profit rose 48% to ¥809.4B, 30% of a ¥2.7T full-year target in one quarter. ROE reached 11.3% with a ~12% target; fees grew more than three times as fast as interest income (+14.3% vs +4.5%); regulatory CET1 rose to 12.95%. The 24% Morgan Stanley stake gives MUFG a US investment-bank and wealth earnings stream. Cross-shareholdings are already below 20% of net assets. If rates keep rising, recurring bank earnings replace the gains from equity sales and a ~15x J-GAAP forward multiple is not demanding.

Bear Case

The rate reflation is visible in NII (+4.5%), but it is not what drove FY2025 profit. The drivers were a ¥707B swing in Global Markets from a one-off loss base, higher equity-method income, FX, and gains from selling equity stakes. Management's own ex-gains ROE target is ~10.4%, not 12%. Domestic loan spreads are narrowing. Management-basis CET1 fell to 9.2%, below target, which caps buybacks at ¥100B. The CFO calls the ¥2.7T target a "moonshot" subject to revision. The ADR is on an 87.5 monthly RSI, 20% above its 10-month average and above both US-line analysts' average target. A stock priced for everything going right is exposed to a BOJ pause, a stronger yen or a Morgan Stanley earnings miss.

Top 3 Risks

01
Quality of earnings. About 1.6 points of the ~12% ROE target come from selling cross-shareholdings, and FY2025 growth was flattered by the non-repeat of a ¥649.4B Global Markets loss. As those tailwinds fade, profit growth has to come from interest income and fees alone.
02
BOJ and yen. The plan assumes a policy rate of ~1% and USD/JPY in the low 150s. A pause cuts off the sensitivity upside. A sharp yen rally would cut yen-reported overseas profit (over half the total) while lifting the dollar value of the ADR, so the two effects partly offset, but the operating hit is real.
03
Capital and Morgan Stanley concentration. Management-basis CET1 at 9.2% leaves little room for larger buybacks or another Shriram-sized deal. A 24.12% stake in one US capital-markets firm brings that firm's earnings cycle straight onto MUFG's income statement.
Industry Commentary
Japan's "world with positive interest rates"
EMERGING TAILWIND

CEO Hanzawa's phrase describes the change in Japanese banking. After the March 2024 exit from negative rates, the megabanks' deposit bases, idle for a decade, began to earn. MUFG quantifies it: ~¥180B of NII per 25bp and ~¥140B already in the FY2026 plan. The honest caveat is where the money comes from. The benefit is flowing through deposits placed at positive rates, not wider loan margins; JCIB's domestic loan spread fell from 0.69% to 0.67%. That makes the tailwind real but mechanical, and it ends if the BOJ stops raising rates.

Cross-shareholding unwinds
FINITE TAILWIND

Japanese banks have long held equity in their corporate clients as a relationship anchor. Governance pressure is dismantling the practice. MUFG cut domestic listed equity at acquisition cost from ¥1.86T (Mar 2021) to ¥0.92T (Mar 2026), and cut equity holdings from 32.9% of net assets to 18.0%, already below its "less than 20%" target. Each sale books a gain, often large because the stakes were bought decades ago, and frees capital. About ¥3.7T of market value remains (20-F). Management separates this in its own numbers: ~12% ROE with the gains, ~10.4% without. Investors should value the bank on the second number.

STRATEGIC ANCHOR

The stake is not ~20%: it is 24.12% (380.5M shares, 13D/A as of July 7, 2026), up from 23.95% at March 31. It rises because Morgan Stanley buys back its own stock and MUFG's share count stays fixed. MUFG's response is to resume the 2018 sales plan from July 30, 2026, selling into Morgan Stanley's buybacks to hold the percentage roughly steady rather than to exit. An "Alliance 2.0" (IR presentation) adds joint work in global investment banking and asset management. The standstill has been extended to October 13, 2028. For an MUFG shareholder, this is effectively a leveraged position in US capital markets, carried at equity-method value and producing a large and growing share of group profit.
