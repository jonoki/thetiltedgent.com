01 Company Overview

Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group is Japan's largest bank holding company and, at a $210.3B market value, the #2 company in Japan by market capitalisation, behind Toyota (companiesmarketcap country ranking, read November 23, 2026). Its home listing is on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, where it is a Nikkei 225 constituent; the NYSE line is an ADR at one ADR per ordinary share. For the fiscal year ended March 2026 (MUFG calls it "FY2025") it reported J-GAAP net profits of ¥2,427.2B, up from ¥1,862.9B (+30.3%), and an ROE of 11.3%. Profit is earned roughly 44% in Japan, 35% in the Americas and 16% in Asia (MUFG FY2025 IR presentation). In the first half of FY2026 (April–September 2026) net profits rose 26.1% to ¥1,630.9B, and on November 13, 2026 management raised its full-year target to ¥2.8T from ¥2.7T.

Is Japan's exit from negative rates what is driving the bank's profits? Only partly, and the numbers say so. The Bank of Japan ended negative rates in March 2024 and MUFG's FY2026 plan assumes a policy rate of about 1%. Net interest income did grow — to ¥3,006.2B, +¥129.6B (+4.5%) in FY2025 — but that was the smallest of the profit drivers. Gross profits rose ¥1,125.1B. The bigger contributors were Global Markets, which swung from a ¥649.4B operating loss to a ¥57.7B profit as bond-portfolio losses rolled off; fees and commissions, +¥299.7B; income from equity-method investees (Morgan Stanley is the principal one), ¥845.5B vs ¥596.9B; and gains from selling off cross-shareholdings. Domestic corporate loan spreads actually narrowed (0.69% → 0.67%). So the rate story is real, but it lives in deposit repricing rather than lending spreads, and so far it is a supporting actor. The first half of FY2026 fits the same pattern: net interest income rose 29% to ¥1,851.2B, but net losses on debt securities were ¥196B, mostly from rebalancing the bond portfolio, and net gains on equity securities rose to ¥210.4B from ¥130.2B (MUFG 6-K, November 13, 2026; the reason for the bond losses per Investing.com). The central question for the stock at 1.43x book: how much of an 11–12% ROE is recurring banking, and how much is a one-time harvest of gains on equity stakes?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Japan's largest deposit franchise Low loan-to-deposit funding 24% of Morgan Stanley Project finance leadership Asian bank platform (Krungsri, Danamon) G-SIB scale (¥425.8T assets)

The moat is cheap funding that has finally started to pay. For a decade under zero and negative rates, a huge yen deposit base earned almost nothing, because there was nowhere positive to put it. Group total assets were ¥425.8T at September 30, 2026, and loans were only 57% of deposits (¥134.0T against ¥233.4T, our calculation from the 6-K). With rates above zero, that same deposit base is the earnings lever. Deposit-rich CWM is where it shows up most. The Morgan Stanley stake gives MUFG a US investment-banking and wealth franchise it could not have built itself. MUFG also cites 16 straight years as No.1 in project finance (IR presentation). The weakness is that most of these advantages depend on conditions MUFG does not control: the BOJ's path, the yen, and Morgan Stanley's earnings.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
ROE partly depends on selling equity stakes. MUFG's FY2026 target is ROE of about 12%. Its own plan puts ROE excluding gains on equity sales at about 10.4%. The gap of about 1.6 points comes from selling cross-shareholdings, and that pool is finite: acquisition cost is down to ¥0.92T. As the pool shrinks, recurring earnings have to replace the gains.
02
The capital buffer is thin on the measure management uses. Regulatory CET1 of 12.65% (September 30, 2026, preliminary) looks comfortable. The management-basis ratio fell to 9.2% at March 2026 (from 10.8%), below the 9.5–10.5% target range, after the Shriram Finance deal and RWA growth; that is why the first-half buyback was capped at ¥100B. At 9.6% on September 30 it is back inside the range, but only just, even as the second-half authorisation, announced November 13, doubles to ¥200B.
03
The rate story assumes BOJ follow-through. The plan assumes a policy rate of about 1%; the BOJ held its rate at 1.25% on October 30, 2026 in an 8–1 vote (Newsquawk). If the BOJ pauses, or reverses in a downturn, the ~¥180B-per-25bp sensitivity works in reverse on the increments not yet banked. Domestic loan spreads are not widening (JCIB 0.69% → 0.67%), so the benefit rests on deposit pricing staying sticky.
04
Morgan Stanley is a large single-name exposure. A 24.12% stake in a capital-markets business means a US investment-banking downturn reaches MUFG's income statement through equity-method earnings. Resuming sales into Morgan Stanley's buybacks trims the stake at the margin; it does not remove the exposure.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the FY2025 comparison. First, the Global Markets swing: the FY2024 base included a ¥649.4B operating loss from repositioning the bond portfolio, so part of FY2025's growth is simply that loss not recurring. Second, gains on cross-shareholding sales are recurring for now but finite; management's own ex-gains ROE target is ~10.4% against the ~12% headline. Third, FX added roughly ¥200B to gross profits and ¥100B to expenses as the yen weakened (USD/JPY 149.52 at end-FY2024 to 159.88 in March 2026, per Fintech Observer). Fourth, accounting basis: J-GAAP profits (¥2,427.2B) and the US GAAP figures StockAnalysis uses for annual periods (¥1,729.4B) differ by about ¥700B for the same year. Any trailing multiple on this stock depends on which basis you pick. The first half of FY2026 adds a fifth: net losses on debt securities of ¥196B, mostly from portfolio rebalancing including sales of U.S. mortgage-backed securities, set against equity-sale gains of ¥210.3B (Investing.com, November 13, 2026).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value, and a stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $5.28 to the November 20, 2026 settled close of $18.68, the ADR is up +254% on price (3.54x), before dividends. From the September 2022 low of $4.50 it is up 4.2x. These are unadjusted closes. Part of the move is earnings growth, and part is investors paying more for each dollar of the bank's book value (shareholders' equity): 1.43x book now, 1.74x at the September 21 edition. The yen weakened over the same period, which works against a dollar holder.

Momentum has broken. The 6-period monthly RSI is 42.6, after readings of 86.8 at the end of September and 66.0 at the end of October. It was above 70 in 26 of the 36 month-end readings through September 2026. The ADR fell 6.5% in October ($23.07 to $21.56) and is down 13.4% so far in November (to $18.68, a partial month). It now sits about 6.9% below its 10-month moving average (~$20.07) and 23.0% below its 52-week high of $24.26 (Sep 3, 2026). RSI measures how one-sided recent moves have been; it says the trend has turned down, not how far it will go.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Japan now has positive rates, and MUFG has the country's largest deposit base to earn on. It discloses ~¥180B of extra net interest income per 25bp and books ~¥140B from hikes already made. H1 FY2026 profit rose 26.1% to ¥1,630.9B, 58% of the raised ¥2.8T full-year target after six months. ROE reached 11.3% with a ~12% target; fees grew more than three times as fast as interest income (+14.3% vs +4.5%); management-basis CET1 was back inside its 9.5–10.5% target range at 9.6% (September 30, 2026). The 24% Morgan Stanley stake gives MUFG a US investment-bank and wealth earnings stream. Cross-shareholdings are already below 20% of net assets. If rates keep rising, recurring bank earnings replace the gains from equity sales and a ~12x J-GAAP forward multiple is not demanding.

Bear Case

The benefit of higher rates is visible in NII (+4.5%), but it is not what drove FY2025 profit. The drivers were a ¥707B swing in Global Markets from a one-off loss base, higher equity-method income, FX, and gains from selling equity stakes. Management's own ex-gains ROE target is ~10.4%, not 12%. Domestic loan spreads are narrowing. Management-basis CET1 fell to 9.2% in March, below target, which held the first-half buyback to ¥100B; at 9.6% in September it sits just inside the 9.5–10.5% range. In May the CFO described the ¥2.7T target as ambitious and open to a downward revision (as summarised by BigGo Finance); management has since raised it to ¥2.8T. First-half net losses on debt securities were ¥196B. The ADR has already fallen 20.2% since the September 21 edition, and a BOJ pause, a stronger yen or a Morgan Stanley earnings miss would add to the pressure.

Top 3 Risks

01
Quality of earnings. About 1.6 points of the ~12% ROE target come from selling cross-shareholdings, and FY2025 growth was flattered by the non-repeat of a ¥649.4B Global Markets loss. As those tailwinds fade, profit growth has to come from interest income and fees alone.
02
BOJ and yen. The plan assumes a policy rate of ~1% and USD/JPY in the low 150s. A pause cuts off the sensitivity upside. A sharp yen rally would cut yen-reported overseas profit (over half the total) while lifting the dollar value of the ADR, so the two effects partly offset, but the operating hit is real.
03
Capital and Morgan Stanley concentration. Management-basis CET1 of 9.6% (September 30, 2026), just inside the 9.5–10.5% target range, leaves little room for larger buybacks or another Shriram-sized deal. A 24.12% stake in one US capital-markets firm brings that firm's earnings cycle straight onto MUFG's income statement.
Industry Commentary
Japan's "world with interest rates"
EMERGING TAILWIND

Hanzawa's phrase, from his April 2025 message as chair of the Japanese Bankers Association, describes the change in Japanese banking. After the March 2024 exit from negative rates, the megabanks' deposit bases, idle for a decade, began to earn. MUFG quantifies it: ~¥180B of NII per 25bp and ~¥140B already in the FY2026 plan; in the first half of FY2026 net interest income rose 29%, and higher yen rates added about ¥140B (Investing.com). The catch is where the money comes from. The benefit is flowing through deposits placed at positive rates, not wider loan margins; JCIB's domestic loan spread fell from 0.69% to 0.67%. That makes the tailwind real but mechanical, and it ends if the BOJ stops raising rates.

Cross-shareholding unwinds
FINITE TAILWIND

Japanese banks have long held equity in their corporate clients as a relationship anchor. Governance pressure is dismantling the practice. MUFG cut domestic listed equity at acquisition cost from ¥1.86T (Mar 2021) to ¥0.92T (Mar 2026), and cut equity holdings from 32.9% of net assets to 18.0%, already below its "less than 20%" target. Each sale books a gain, often large because the stakes were bought decades ago, and frees capital. About ¥3.7T of market value remains (20-F). Management separates this in its own numbers: ~12% ROE with the gains, ~10.4% without. Investors should value the bank on the second number.

The Morgan Stanley stake, 18 years on
STRATEGIC ANCHOR

The stake is 24.12% (380.5M shares, Schedule 13D/A filed July 15, 2026), up from 23.95% in the April 13, 2026 filing. It rises because Morgan Stanley buys back its own stock and MUFG's share count stays fixed. MUFG's response is to resume the 2018 sales plan from July 30, 2026, selling into Morgan Stanley's buybacks to hold the percentage roughly steady rather than to exit. An "Alliance 2.0" (IR presentation) adds joint work in global investment banking and asset management. The standstill has been extended to October 13, 2028. For an MUFG shareholder, this ties a large and growing share of group profit to US capital markets: MUFG books its share of Morgan Stanley's earnings each period (equity-method accounting).
