01 Company Overview

Nebius is what the market now calls a "neocloud": it buys Nvidia GPUs by the tens of thousands, houses them in data centers it builds or leases, and rents the compute to AI labs, software companies and hyperscalers under multi-year contracts. It is the rump of Yandex — the Russian search company sold its Russian businesses in 2024, kept the Dutch parent, a Finnish data center and a few venture bets, and relisted on Nasdaq as Nebius on October 21, 2024. FY2025 revenue was $529.8M; Q3 2026 revenue alone was $980.8M, up 571% year on year, trailing-twelve-month revenue is $2.19B, and annualized run-rate revenue (ARR, the company's measure: a single month's revenue times twelve) was $6.4B at the end of September. Management's first look at 2027 is $12–13B of revenue. Its market value is about $33.9B (292.9M shares at the November 13 close), down from $62.05B at the last edition.

The stock does not trade on earnings — it has none that matter — it trades on contracted capacity, how fast it can be switched on, and the cost of funding it. Microsoft signed for $17.4B (up to $19.4B with options) in September 2025; Meta added $3B in November 2025 and then $12B firm plus up to $15B more in March 2026; Nvidia agreed to invest $2B for an 8.3% stake the same month. Remaining performance obligations (contracted revenue not yet recognised) were $38.7B at the end of September. Q3 showed both halves of the story: revenue and margins were the best yet, but colocation sites (data centers run by other operators), New Jersey among them, slipped into the first half of 2027, so 2026 revenue now lands at the low end of guidance while capex rises to about $27B. The debate this page works through is the neocloud debate itself: whether renting depreciating GPUs financed with $14.2B of debt, $9.4B of customer prepayments and repeated share and convertible-note sales ever returns more than its cost of capital, or whether Nebius is simply a debt-funded middleman renting out Nvidia's chips to a handful of customers. The clue that it is not settled: ROIC (operating profit after tax as a share of the capital invested) is −3.0%, and 22.3% of the freely traded shares were sold short at the October 30 settlement.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

$38.7B contracted backlog (RPO) Nvidia allocation + 8.3% Nvidia stake In-house server & rack design 4 GW+ contracted power (5 GW target) Q3 prepayments covered 100%+ of capex Full software stack (cloud, inference, MLOps) European data-sovereignty positioning

The honest description of a neocloud moat is that it is time and access, not technology. Nebius's advantage is that it has power, land, Nvidia allocation and anchor contracts today, while demand for Blackwell-class capacity exceeds supply — annual contract value per megawatt rising from more than $20M on Q2 deals to more than $25M on Q3 deals is the evidence. Its genuine differentiators versus the other neoclouds are vertical integration (it designs its own hardware) and the prepayment structure: management says prepayments on Q3 agreements covered more than 100% of the associated capex, and it expects $10B of committed customer prepayments in 2026, which pushes financing risk onto investment-grade customers. That is a better balance sheet model than CoreWeave's ($66.2B of debt on $9.9B of trailing revenue), and it is why Nebius's debt-to-equity is 1.30 against CoreWeave's 16.45 (both on stockanalysis.com's basis, which counts leases as debt). Q3 also showed the limit of the moat: where Nebius relies on other operators' data centers, it does not control the date the power comes on.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Customer concentration. Microsoft (up to $19.4B) and Meta ($15B firm plus up to $15B conditional) are the two anchors behind most of the $38.7B of remaining performance obligations, and the five-year agreements signed in Q3 were all expansions by existing customers. Databricks, Snowflake and Datadog are new but smaller. Both anchors are among the largest builders of their own AI data centers; both rent from neoclouds to bridge timing gaps. When the bridge is no longer needed, renewals reprice — and Microsoft's contract runs only five years.
▸
GPU depreciation life. D&A was 36% of Q3 revenue. If Vera Rubin obsoletes Blackwell faster than the 5–6 year lives the industry assumes, every neocloud's EBITDA margin is overstated and the residual value assumption behind asset-backed lending weakens. Contracts of 1–5 years against assets depreciated over 5–6 years means a contract can end before its GPUs are paid off; investors currently overlook that.
▸
Funding dependence. Capex is now about $27B for 2026 and $40–45B for 2027, against 2026 revenue at the low end of $3.0–3.4B. That gap is filled by prepayments ($9.4B of deferred revenue on the balance sheet at September 30), $14.2B of debt (interest expense was $112.5M in Q3 alone) and equity: weighted-average shares in Q3 were 24% higher than a year earlier, and at-the-market sales raised about $1.0B more in Q3. If the share price falls — it has roughly halved since early September — the cheapest funding source gets expensive precisely when it is needed.
▸
Hyperscaler pricing power. Oracle, AWS, Azure and Google can bundle GPU capacity with software and credits, and can accept lower returns on compute as a customer-acquisition cost. Today's neocloud pricing of more than $25M of annual contract value per megawatt reflects scarcity, not a durable price. When supply catches up — and Nvidia is investing to make sure it does — today's rates are the ceiling, not the floor.

October took away 34.7%, and after the Q3 print (delayed capacity, higher capex) the November 13 close was $115.88, 51% below the September close. The monthly RSI has fallen from 92 in June to 39.6.
Funding and timing back in focus
08 Summary & Key Risks

The neocloud arithmetic, using management's own numbers. Start with the unit: one megawatt of contracted capacity now brings more than $25M of annual contract value (Q3 deals). Management guides a group adjusted EBITDA margin of about 40% for 2026 (Q3 was 47.5% with the non-recurring revenue in it). At the company level the 2026 plan is about $27B of capex for ARR of more than $7B by February, about $3.9 of capex per dollar of run-rate revenue, and 2027 adds $40–45B of capex for $12–13B of revenue: capacity is being built ahead of the contracts that will fill it. Over the two years, $67–72B of capex stands behind a $12–13B revenue year, about $5.5 of capital per $1 of revenue (our arithmetic; part of the 2027 spend is for 2028 revenue). At a 40% EBITDA margin that is an EBITDA yield on invested capital of roughly 7% in 2027, and it improves only as the new capacity fills.

Now subtract what EBITDA ignores. If a GPU cluster is depreciated over six years, depreciation alone is ~17% of the capex per year; over four years it is 25%. That is why D&A was 36% of Q3 revenue even with capacity still filling. Against a 7% EBITDA yield, that depreciation leaves operating profit negative on the 2027 capital base until the capacity built in 2026–27 is earning — before interest expense of $112.5M a quarter on $14.2B of debt, before stock compensation, and before tax. Management's own marker is a pre-tax breakeven in Q4 2027. Today's reported ROIC is −3.0%. At about 2.8× 2027 consensus revenue the stock is pricing that path being delivered; another round of slipped capacity would move the breakeven, not just a quarter.

What makes Nebius different from CoreWeave — and what does not. The prepayment model is real: $9.4B of deferred revenue at September 30, $10B of committed prepayments expected in 2026, and Q3 prepayments covering more than 100% of the capex behind those contracts. That is why Nebius carries $16.0B of debt against CoreWeave's $66.2B, and a debt-to-equity of 1.30 against 16.45 (both counting leases, stockanalysis.com). It is also why Nebius's trailing operating cash flow (+$5.07B) looks healthy while free cash flow is −$12.42B: the prepayments arrive as cash before the GPUs are delivered. Q3 operating cash flow alone was −$273M. What is not different is the exposure. Both companies rent one supplier's product to a handful of customers who are also building their own supply, both depend on capital markets staying open — Nebius issued $5.75B of convertible notes and sold about $1.0B of shares in Q3 alone, and its weighted-average share count is 24% higher than a year earlier — and both are valued on backlog that turns into revenue only as fast as power is connected. Q3 showed that last point: the delays were at sites other operators were building.

Concentration, stated plainly. Against $38.7B of remaining performance obligations, Microsoft's contract is worth up to $19.4B and Meta's commitments up to $30B ($3B, then $12B firm plus up to $15B conditional), part of each already delivered or not yet counted. Those two names are most of the disclosed backlog. The five-year agreements signed in Q3 were all expansions by existing customers; the new names (Databricks, Snowflake, Datadog, and Palantir as a sales partner) are the diversification story, and it is early. The five-year Microsoft contract signed in September 2025 runs through 2031 (Form 6-K, Sep 8, 2025); by then Rubin-successor GPUs will be on the market and more of Microsoft's own data centers will be built. The price at which that renews is unknowable, and it is the single most important number for what the business is worth in the long run.

Hidden assets, and what they are worth. The ClickHouse stake (an undisclosed share of a company valued at about $15B in January 2026), Toloka and Avride are worth something the market cap may not reflect. The company does not disclose the stake sizes; at a $15B ClickHouse valuation, every 10 percentage points of ownership is worth $1.5B, about 4% of Nebius's $33.9B market cap (our arithmetic). Enough to matter in a drawdown, not enough to underwrite the valuation. They also account for every dollar of positive GAAP net income over the last twelve months.

Bull Case

Nebius is the best-financed neocloud: $38.7B of contracted backlog, prepayments that covered more than 100% of the capex behind Q3 contracts, a debt-to-equity of 1.30 against CoreWeave's 16.45 (counting leases), and Nvidia as an 8.3% shareholder. Revenue rose 571% in Q3, contract value per megawatt rose above $25M, and new customers (Databricks, Snowflake, Datadog) broaden the book. Management's first 2027 outlook is $12–13B of revenue with a pre-tax breakeven by Q4 2027. At about 2.8× 2027 consensus revenue and 58% below its June close, the stock prices in a lot of failure for a company whose delays were at other operators' sites and whose CEO says none of the delayed capacity has been cancelled.

Bear Case

ROIC is −3.0%, free cash flow is −$12.4B, D&A consumes 36% of revenue, and the only GAAP profit in the last year came from marking up a database company. Capex of about $27B this year and $40–45B next, against 2026 revenue at the low end of $3.0–3.4B, is funded by convertible notes, a share count up 24% in a year, $14.2B of debt and prepayments from customers who are building their own supply. The first missed timetable came from data centers Nebius does not control. With 22% of the float short, a $315 spread in analyst targets and a 32-month gap in its own price history, this is a leveraged bet on Nvidia's product cycle at about 19× enterprise value (market cap plus debt minus cash) to trailing sales.

Top 3 Risks

01
Funding window closes. The model needs about $27B this year and $40–45B next of customer and outside capital. A 30% drawdown in the stock (three have happened in 25 months, the latest −58%) raises the cost of share sales and convertible funding exactly when capex commitments are fixed. The Altman Z-score (a formula for bankruptcy risk) of 1.38 (stockanalysis.com) is in the distress zone on a mechanical reading — misleading for a prepaid business, but a reminder that the balance sheet is a bet on continuity.
02
GPU useful life proves shorter than modelled. Rubin's arrival in 2027 will set the market price of Blackwell capacity. If contract values fall from more than $25M per megawatt toward the low teens as supply catches up, six-year depreciation schedules turn into write-downs and the EBITDA margin becomes an accounting artefact.
03
Anchor renewal risk and concentration. Microsoft and Meta are most of the disclosed backlog; both are among the largest builders of their own AI data centers. Hyperscalers rent neocloud capacity to bridge timing gaps, and bridges get dismantled. A non-renewal or repricing when the five-year contracts end (Microsoft's runs through 2031) would reset the long-run value the current price assumes.
  Industry Commentary
Power, Not GPUs, Is the Binding Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every neocloud can buy Nvidia chips if it pays; none can conjure a grid connection. Q3 proved it: delivery dates at several colocation sites, including the remaining New Jersey tranches, slipped into the first half of 2027, which is why 2026 revenue now lands at the low end of guidance. Nebius says its own sites are on time or ahead of schedule, and Bloom fuel-cell generation is online in New Jersey (Q3 letter). Contracted power (more than 4 GW now, 5 GW targeted by year-end) is the right metric to watch because it is the one the company cannot buy its way to — and Q3 added a second: how much of it sits on sites Nebius controls.
Hyperscalers Are Customers Today and Competitors by Design
ONGOING HEADWIND
Microsoft and Meta rent from Nebius, CoreWeave and Oracle because their own build-outs cannot keep pace with model demand. That is a bet on timing, and it is why neocloud contracts run one to five years rather than fifteen. Oracle's OCI has shown the hyperscaler version of the same trade — accept thin compute margins to win the workload. The sector's revenue is therefore real but its duration is short: the buyers will set renewal terms as the contracts end, from the late 2020s, in a market with far more supply. Nebius's prepayment structure improves the funding of that bet; it does not change its nature.
Inference Is Where Neoclouds Become Software
EMERGING TAILWIND
Training clusters are rented by the megawatt; inference is sold by the token, to many more customers, on stickier integrations. Nebius's "Token Factory" is approaching a multi-hundred-million-dollar run rate, with contract value up 17× quarter on quarter in Q3 (shareholder letter), and in October it bought Inferize, whose technology shortens the time needed to launch and scale large AI models (Form 6-K). Bull framing: if inference demand spreads the customer base beyond two hyperscalers and lifts utilisation on ageing GPUs, the depreciation problem shrinks and investors start to value the company more like software. Bear framing: inference is exactly where AWS, Azure and Google can bundle and subsidise, and where the valuations of AI labs — Nebius's other customers — are most exposed if the model-economics boom cools. The −3.0% ROIC is the number that has to move before either framing is proven.
