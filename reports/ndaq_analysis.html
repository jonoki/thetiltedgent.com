01 Company Overview

Nasdaq, Inc. is no longer primarily a stock exchange. It presents itself as a technology company serving capital markets and other industries, and the reported numbers back that up: in the third quarter of 2026 the trading business — Market Services — produced $309M of the company's $1,481M in net revenue, about 21%. The other 79% came from index licensing, listings, data, anti-financial-crime software, regulatory reporting technology and capital-markets infrastructure software, which Nasdaq groups as Solutions ($1,172M, up 17%). Full-year 2025 net revenue was $5,249M, up 12.9% from $4,649M in 2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure through September 30, 2026 is $5,780M (FY2025 plus the first nine months of 2026, less the first nine months of 2025). At a $56.92B market capitalisation the company ranks #210 in the S&P 500 by market value.

A point of order, because the ticker invites confusion: NDAQ is listed on the Nasdaq Stock Market but is not a member of the Nasdaq-100 index, which excludes financial companies by construction — so the company that owns and licenses the index is not in it. The benchmark used throughout this page is the S&P 500, with the Nasdaq-100 referenced only as a business driver. The central debate is this: Nasdaq has spent the last several years converting a cyclical, volume-dependent exchange into a recurring-revenue software and index business, and the market has awarded it a P/E of 28.0× against a peer median near 25.8×. The third quarter sharpened the argument: Solutions grew 17% while trading grew 2%, and the shares rose 7.47% on results day. The question is whether the recurring mix justifies the premium, or whether the $8.74B of debt and the gap between 57% non-GAAP and 49% GAAP operating margins mean investors are paying a software multiple for an acquisition-assembled one.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
NASDAQ-100 BRAND $1.16T INDEX ETP AUM $3.37B ARR BASE REGULATORY LICENCE LISTINGS NETWORK EFFECT PROPRIETARY MARKET DATA BANK SWITCHING COSTS

The core advantage is not the trading venue — order flow is fickle and venues compete on fractions of a cent. It is that Nasdaq owns a brand that funds flow into. The Nasdaq-100 is one of a small handful of index brands worldwide that retail and institutional money will buy by name, and the fee on it is a basis-point charge against $1.16 trillion of tracking assets that cost little more to service as they grow. That is why Index revenue grew 39% while Data & Listing Services grew 10% and Workflow & Insights grew 6% in the same quarter. Layered underneath is a second, less glamorous moat: $3.37B of ARR in compliance-critical software sitting inside banks' regulatory and trade-processing stacks — systems that are audited, examined and painful to rip out. Neither moat depends on winning a price war for order flow.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Index revenue is a leveraged bet on equity market levels. A basis-point fee on $1.16T of ETP assets falls when those assets fall, and it falls without any operational failure by the company. The line that grew 39% and drove most of the segment's outperformance is the same line that would contract fastest in a drawdown — and because it costs little extra to serve, the operating-profit impact is amplified in both directions.
02
Leverage, with buybacks ahead of debt paydown. Total debt was $8.74B against $365M of cash at September 30, 2026, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.77 (stockanalysis.com, on its wider $9.30B debt figure) and a current ratio of 1.00. In the first nine months of 2026 the company repaid $431M of debt while spending $1,226M on buybacks and $500M on dividends (10-Q cash flow statement) — deleveraging is real but deliberate rather than urgent. ROIC of 10.87% against ROE of 17.33% is the visible signature of that borrowed capital.
03
An 8-point GAAP-to-adjusted margin gap. Q3 2026 GAAP operating margin was 49%; non-GAAP was 57%. GAAP diluted EPS was $0.96 (+31%) while non-GAAP EPS was $1.04 (+18%); in Q2 it was the other way round (+14% GAAP, +25% non-GAAP), so the gap narrowed this quarter. Almost every valuation you will see quoted on this name still uses the larger number. The forward P/E of 22.45× versus the trailing 28.02× is partly that same gap expressed as a multiple.
04
Regulatory dependence cuts both ways. Exchange status is a licence granted by the SEC, and market-data pricing, access fees and the consolidated-tape economics are all subject to regulatory review. The same rulemaking that creates demand for Regulatory Technology can compress the economics of Market Services and proprietary data. Nasdaq is unusually exposed to the direction of US market-structure policy in both directions at once.

Notable one-off items. Three things distort the reported figures and are worth naming. First, FY2024 GAAP EPS of $1.93 was an outlier low — net income of $1,117M on $4,649M of revenue, a 24% margin, against $1,788M on $5,249M (34%) in FY2025. The 60% FY2025 EPS jump is partly a recovery from a depressed base, not pure operating improvement. Second, the GAAP-to-non-GAAP bridge is large and recurring: 49% versus 57% operating margin, $0.96 versus $1.04 of quarterly EPS. Amortisation of acquired intangibles is a permanent feature of this company's income statement, not a one-time event, and excluding it every quarter for years is a choice, not a correction. Third, Index revenue growth of 39% in Q3 2026 is not a run-rate — it reflects ETP assets rising to $1.16T on $141B of twelve-month inflows and $190B of market gains, and the comparison base resets each year.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 split-adjusted month-end close of $69.96 to the October 23, 2026 close of $102.56, NDAQ rose +46.6% in price — roughly 8.0% a year — plus a dividend currently yielding 1.21%. Over the same dates SPY rose 65.5% and QQQ 82.4% (price only), so the stock lagged both broad benchmarks. That is a respectable but unspectacular outcome, and it conceals a genuine two-act structure: a 30.6% drawdown from the December 2021 month-end close of $70.00 to the September 2023 trough of $48.59, followed by a +111% advance from that trough to the October 23, 2026 close, the highest point on the chart. An investor who bought at the December 2021 month-end waited until August 2024 ($72.08) for a higher month-end close, two years and eight months; one who bought the 2023 low more than doubled.

Relative performance. Over the past year (October 23, 2025 close to October 23, 2026 close, price only) NDAQ returned +15.5% against SPY +13.1% and QQQ +15.4% — level with the Nasdaq-100 fund and slightly ahead of the S&P 500 fund. Over five calendar years (October 22, 2021 to October 23, 2026) it returned +52.5% against SPY +67.7% and QQQ +88.3%. On the Nasdaq-100: the index is a revenue driver for this company, not a benchmark for its stock — NDAQ is not a constituent, since the Nasdaq-100 excludes financial companies. The relationship runs the other way, which is precisely the asymmetry worth understanding: Nasdaq collects fees on the index's assets whether or not its own shares participate in the index's returns.

Earnings-driven re-rating
08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the mix, because that is the whole argument. Of $1,481M of Q3 2026 net revenue, $309M — 20.9% — came from trading. The rest came from index licensing, listings, data, and software sold to banks. Total ARR is $3,365M, split $1,954M in Financial Technology and $1,411M in Capital Access Platforms, growing 12% reported and 14% organic, with annualised SaaS revenue at 38% of ARR growing 16% organically. Against TTM net revenue of $5,780M, that ARR base covers roughly 58% of the company's revenue on a contracted, recurring footing. That is the number that separates Nasdaq from a pure exchange and it is the number that justifies a 28.0× trailing P/E against a 25.8× peer median.

Now the part that complicates it. The fastest-growing revenue line in the company is Index, at +39%, and Index is not really recurring in the software sense — it is a basis-point fee on $1.16 trillion of exchange-traded product assets. It recurs as long as the assets remain and the market does not fall. Strip Index out and Capital Access Platforms grew at Data & Listing's 10% and Workflow & Insights' 6%. So the segment carrying the "recurring, high-quality revenue" banner is disproportionately driven by the one line that moves most with stock-market levels. The third quarter showed the other side too: trading, the visibly cyclical part, grew only 2%, and the company still grew net revenue 13% because Solutions grew 17%. The bull case and the cyclicality live in the same place.

The valuation, on three bases. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $3.66, NDAQ trades at 28.0× — the fully-loaded number, charging the company for the amortisation of what it paid to build the Financial Technology segment. (ii) On 2026 consensus EPS of $4.14 (adjusted basis), it trades at 24.8×; the published forward P/E, on the next twelve months' forecasts, is 22.45×. (iii) On trailing free cash flow of $2.26B, it trades at about 25× equity value, or roughly 29× on the $65.70B enterprise value — the EV multiple is the honest one, because it charges for the $8.74B of debt that the equity multiple ignores. Against a peer set trading at 23.0–26.5× (ICE, CME, Cboe) with MSCI at 32.6×, Nasdaq's premium is real but modest, and the direction of travel — toward MSCI's index-and-data profile and away from ICE's exchange profile — is the implicit bet.

What the balance sheet is doing. $8.74B of debt against $365M of cash and $27.38B of total assets, a 0.77 debt-to-equity ratio (stockanalysis.com), ROE of 17.33% against ROIC of 10.87%. In the first nine months of 2026, management sent $1,726M to shareholders and $431M to lenders. That is a deliberate statement: the leverage is regarded as comfortable rather than pressing. Note too that the forward multiple looks cheaper than the trailing one partly because the forecasts are on the adjusted basis, which leaves out the amortisation charged against GAAP earnings.

Bull Case

The mix shift is done and it worked: 79% of Q3 2026 revenue came from outside trading, $3.37B of ARR covers roughly 58% of revenue on a contracted basis, and Solutions grew 17% with Financial Technology ARR up 18%. Index ETP assets reached $1.16 trillion on a record $141B of twelve-month inflows, SpaceX chose Nasdaq for what the company calls the largest IPO in exchange history, and non-GAAP operating margin rose to 57% from 56% a year earlier. Fourteen of eighteen analysts rate it Buy or Strong Buy with a $114.25 average target, 11.4% above the current price, and short interest of 1.54% of float says almost nobody is betting against it.

Bear Case

You are paying a ~9% P/E premium to exchange and index peers for a recurring-revenue story whose fastest-growing component — Index at +39% — is a basis-point fee on $1.16T of assets that will shrink with the market, not a subscription. Ex-Index, the rest of Capital Access Platforms grew 6–10%, and Market Services grew 2%. GAAP operating margin is 49% against the 57% everyone quotes; the difference is mostly the amortisation of acquired intangible assets ($121M in Q3), and the company carries $8.74B of debt, a 1.00 current ratio and ROIC six points below ROE. The company raised its 2026 expense range again in October, and over the chart's five years the stock rose 46.6% against SPY's 65.5%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Index AUM is the single point of leverage. A basis-point fee on $1.16 trillion costs little extra to serve, so most of each extra dollar is profit on the way up and most of each lost dollar is lost profit on the way down. A 20% equity drawdown would cut the fastest-growing, highest-margin revenue line by roughly the same proportion, with no offsetting cost relief — and would do so in exactly the quarter that listings and index flows also dry up. The three exposures are correlated, not diversifying.
02
The multiple depends on the non-GAAP bridge holding. At 28.0× trailing GAAP earnings versus a 25.8× peer median, Nasdaq is priced as a software-and-data company. If ARR growth decelerates from the current 12–14% toward the 6–10% growth rates in Data & Listing Services and Workflow & Insights, the premium has no support. No earnings miss is required for that de-rating — a single soft ARR print would do it.
03
Regulatory and market-structure risk on both sides of the business. Exchange status, market-data pricing and access-fee economics are all subject to SEC rulemaking, and the same policy environment that creates demand for Regulatory Technology can compress the margins in Market Services and proprietary data. With a 1.00 current ratio and $8.74B of debt, the company has less balance-sheet cushion to absorb an adverse ruling than its market-like 1.00 beta implies.
  Industry Commentary
Passive Flows Are the Exchange Industry's Real Growth Engine
EMERGING TAILWIND
The structural shift from active management to index products has turned index ownership into the most valuable franchise in capital-markets infrastructure — better than trading, better than data. Nasdaq's index ETP assets reached $1.16 trillion with a record $141 billion of net inflows in twelve months, and Index revenue of $286M grew 39% in Q3 2026 while the rest of its segment grew 6–10%. The economics are extraordinary: a basis-point fee that requires no additional headcount as assets grow. This is the same dynamic that supports MSCI's 32.6× P/E, the highest in the peer group. The catch is that it is a fee on a stock of assets, so it inherits the market's cycle wholesale.
Bigger IPOs, Fewer of Them
ONGOING HEADWIND
In Q3 2026 Nasdaq listed 15 operating-company IPOs raising $32 billion, including 8 of the quarter's 10 largest, against 46 operating-company IPOs a year earlier; it also listed 31 special-purpose acquisition companies (listed shells that raise cash to buy a private business later), against 30. The money is concentrated in fewer, larger deals: year to date, new operating-company listings raised $144 billion. And listings monetise thinly in any case: Data & Listing Services revenue grew just 10% in the quarter of SpaceX's $86 billion raise, which Nasdaq calls the largest IPO in exchange history, and 10% again in the quarter after. A marquee listing is worth more as a franchise signal — index inclusion candidacy, data volume, credibility with the next issuer — than as a revenue line. Exchanges compete hard for listings they barely profit from directly.
Everyone Is Buying the Same Software Business
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Exchange operators have spent the past several years converting trading franchises into financial-software companies, and they have done it with debt. Nasdaq carries $8.74B of borrowings against $365M of cash, an enterprise value $8.78B above its market cap, and an 8-point gap between 49% GAAP and 57% non-GAAP operating margin that is mostly acquisition amortisation. Bull framing: the strategy worked — 79% of revenue now sits outside trading, ARR of $3.37B covers ~58% of revenue, and the company can deleverage out of $2.26B of annual free cash flow while still returning about $500M a quarter to shareholders ($496M in Q3). Bear framing: every peer bought the same kind of asset, which means the differentiation investors are paying a ~9% P/E premium for is not obviously proprietary — and the only fully unique asset in the portfolio, the Nasdaq-100 brand, is levered to the very market cycle the software was supposed to diversify away from.
