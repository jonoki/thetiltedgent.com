01 Company Overview

Nordson is an Ohio company that sells the ability to put exactly the right amount of a fluid in exactly the right place. In its own description, it "engineers, manufactures, and markets products and systems to dispense, apply, and control adhesives, coatings, polymers, sealants, biomaterials, medical components, and other fluids." That covers the adhesive on a package, the coating on a circuit board and components inside medical devices. FY2025 (year ended October 31, 2025) revenue was $2.79B, up 3.78%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $8.51. Over the twelve months to July 31, 2026 revenue reached $2.98B and GAAP EPS $9.89, up 24.4%.

The hook is the margin. A company that makes dispensing equipment earns a 55.4% gross margin and a 27.2% operating margin on a trailing basis, which is closer to a technology company than a machine shop. That is what the market pays 31.8x trailing earnings for. The story of the last twelve months is that growth came back. FY2025 was a low-growth year, and the initial FY2026 guide in December 2025 was sales of $2,830–$2,950M and adjusted EPS of $10.80–$11.50. By the Q3 print on August 19, 2026 that had become $3,035–$3,075M and $11.80–$12.00, with backlog up 35% year over year and organic growth of about 12% in the quarter. Most of the lift came from the electronics-facing segment. The stock rose 8.00% the next day. The question for a buyer at $314.35 is whether that growth holds as a new level or whether an electronics up-cycle is being priced as permanent.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Precision dispensing know-how 55% gross margin Proprietary medical components Diversified end markets 63 straight dividend increases 32% EBITDA margin

Nordson's moat is a small-share-of-cost, large-share-of-risk position. Its equipment puts down adhesive, coating or fluid in amounts where an error means a scrapped circuit board, a leaking package or a failed medical device. For the customer the dispenser is a small line in the budget, and the cost of a bad dispense is far larger. That gap is why a hardware company holds a 55% gross margin through a full cycle: 56.05% in FY2021, 54.56% at the FY2023 low and 55.39% trailing. The dividend record says the same thing another way. The August 27, 2026 increase was the 63rd consecutive annual increase, and it ran through the 2022 rate shock and the FY2023–FY2024 years when EPS fell. The vulnerability is that the margin protects price, not volume. When electronics or industrial customers stop building lines, Nordson's order book stops with them, as FY2023–FY2024 revenue growth of 1.5% and 2.3% showed.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth is concentrated in the most cyclical segment. ATS grew 31% organically in Q3 FY2026 and IPS grew 3%. In our view, electronics equipment demand tends to move in sharp up-cycles followed by digestion years. If ATS reverts to flat, group organic growth falls back toward the low single digits of FY2023–FY2025, while the stock is priced near its five-year high.
02
Valuation leaves little room for error. At 31.8x trailing and 26.3x FY2026 consensus EPS, the stock trades at a premium multiple for a business whose five-year revenue growth ran from $2.36B (FY2021) to $2.98B (TTM), about 26% in total. The multiple assumes the 2026 acceleration continues.
03
Leverage after the Atrion deal. Total debt is $1.82B against $113.9M of cash (1.85x debt/EBITDA), following the ~$800M Atrion acquisition in 2024. That is manageable and falling, since $258M was repaid year-to-date. It does mean that the buyback and any further M&A compete with deleveraging.
04
Medical is still being reshaped. The contract-manufacturing exit reduces reported MFS sales, and the segment's growth now depends on proprietary components such as the Atrion lines. The 11% organic growth excluding the divestiture is strong. It is also one quarter of evidence for a business mix that is still new.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Nordson has held a 55% gross margin and a 32% EBITDA margin through a flat patch, and growth has now come back: organic sales up about 12% in Q3 FY2026, record sales in every segment, and backlog up 35%. Management raised the FY2026 guide from $2,830–$2,950M to $3,035–$3,075M in sales and from $10.80–$11.50 to $11.80–$12.00 in adjusted EPS. Free cash flow of $724M TTM is paying down the Atrion debt and funding buybacks and a 63-year dividend streak, with the latest increase at 15%. If electronics demand is the start of a multi-year build-out rather than a one-year spike, 26x forward earnings for a business of this quality is reasonable, and consensus still has EPS growing 8.7% in FY2027 (StockAnalysis).

Bear Case

The bear case accepts the Q3 numbers and questions how long they last. One segment, ATS, the lowest-margin of the three and the one most tied to electronics demand, supplied about two-thirds of the quarter's dollar growth, and consensus already has revenue growth slowing to 5.2% in FY2027. The stock trades at 31.8x trailing GAAP earnings, near its five-year high, with monthly RSI above 70. The price is up 30.7% since the December 2025 month-end in under nine months, while TTM GAAP EPS grew 24.4% over a full year. One analyst rates it Sell and the low target is $282. An electronics digestion year would hit both the growth rate and the multiple.

Top 3 Risks

01
Electronics cycle reversal. ATS grew 31% organically in Q3 FY2026. In our view, electronics equipment demand tends to move in up-cycles followed by digestion years. A reversal would take away the growth engine while the valuation assumes it keeps running.
02
Multiple compression. At 31.8x trailing and 26.3x forward, a return to low-single-digit growth like FY2023–FY2025 would likely mean a lower multiple on top of slower earnings. The stock already fell 19.8% in one month (December 2024) in this window.
03
Guidance and FY2027 set-up. The Q4 FY2026 release will also set FY2027 expectations. The raised FY2026 range and a 35% higher backlog set a high bar. The initial FY2026 guide in December 2025 ($2,830–$2,950M) sat well below where the year is now tracking. A similarly cautious FY2027 opening guide could reset the price quickly.
Industry Commentary
Electronics demand is driving the up-cycle
CYCLICAL TAILWIND

Nordson's 2026 story is mainly an electronics story. ATS sales rose from $171.3M to $220M year over year in Q3, and the CEO pointed to "accelerating demand in key end markets" and "above-market organic growth." The product cadence continued with the ASYMTEK Vantage XL, announced on August 25, 2026 for panel-level packaging. The specific end markets behind the ATS surge were not broken out in the sources fetched, so this report does not attribute it to any single theme. What can be said is that the segment is the smallest and most volatile of the three, and it is now carrying the growth rate.

Medical: from contract work to proprietary components
MIX IMPROVEMENT

In two moves, Nordson bought Atrion (~$800M, completed August 21, 2024) and sold its medical contract-manufacturing product lines (September 2025). Together they swap lower-value build-to-print work for owned products in infusion and cardiovascular devices. The result shows in the margin: MFS earned a record 38% EBITDA margin in Q3 FY2026, the highest of the three segments. In our view medical demand is typically less cyclical than electronics or industrial capex, so this segment is the natural offset if ATS slows.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
KEY DEBATE

At $314.35 NDSN trades at 31.8x trailing GAAP EPS, about 26x the adjusted FY2026 guide, a 4.1% FCF yield and a 1.20% dividend yield. The bull framing: this is a compounder whose growth has come back. Margins held through the flat years, backlog is up 35%, and the balance sheet is deleveraging while it pays a rising dividend, so a premium multiple is deserved. The bear framing: almost all of the stock's five-year gain came in the 2026 rally (the December 2025 month-end of $240.43 was barely above the September 2021 start of $238.15), and the growth behind that rally sits in the most cyclical segment. Paying a peak multiple on earnings that may themselves be cyclically high is the classic way to overpay for quality. The FY2027 guide in December is where the two framings will be tested against each other.
