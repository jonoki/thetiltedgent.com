01 Company Overview

NextEra Energy is a two-engine electric power company: Florida Power & Light (FPL), a vertically integrated regulated utility providing electricity to approximately 12 million people across Florida (Q3 2026 release), and NextEra Energy Resources (NEER), the largest competitive developer, owner and operator of wind, solar and battery storage assets in North America. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $27.41B, up 10.74% year over year. Nine-month 2026 revenue was $22.325B (+6.8% from $20.912B), and TTM revenue through September 2026 is $28.83B.

At a $172.07B market capitalization (our calculation at the October 26 close) NextEra is the largest US utility by market value. The company has raised its dividend for more than 30 consecutive years and currently yields 3.04%. In May 2026 NextEra announced a proposed combination with Dominion Energy. Shareholders of both companies approved it on Sept. 3, the companies expect final Virginia and South Carolina orders in January 2027, and they expect it to close in the second half of 2027. Management says it would lift the pro-forma adjusted EPS growth target to 9%+ annually through 2032 (Q3 2026 release, sec.gov).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
REGULATED MONOPOLY (FL) 4-YR RATE DEAL → 2029 LOWEST-COST OPERATOR 36.7 GW BACKLOG SUPPLY-CHAIN SCALE INTERCONNECTION QUEUE POSITION 30+ YR DIVIDEND STREAK

NextEra's moat is genuinely two moats bolted together, and they reinforce each other. FPL is a legal monopoly — no competitor can serve its territory — operating in one of the fastest-growing states in the country. Because FPL's typical residential bill sits about 37% below the national average (Q3 2026 release), it enjoys unusual regulatory goodwill; that goodwill is what produced a negotiated, coalition-backed four-year rate settlement in November 2025 rather than a contested rate case. Regulatory predictability through 2029 is worth real multiple points in a sector where adverse commission rulings are the main idiosyncratic risk.

NEER's moat is scale in a business where scale is the whole game. Being the largest buyer of turbines, panels and batteries in North America produces procurement pricing peers cannot match; two decades of development produces land positions, interconnection queue slots and permitting relationships that cannot be bought; and a 36.7 GW signed backlog means the growth is contracted, not hoped-for. Critically, the two halves share a balance sheet: FPL's regulated, rating-agency-friendly cash flows support the group's cost of capital, and cheap capital is the binding constraint on renewables development. The dividend growth record of more than 30 years is both a signal of that stability and a source of a sticky, yield-oriented shareholder base.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Federal clean-energy tax policy has already turned against the core product. Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, enacted July 4, 2025), wind and solar projects that begin construction more than twelve months after enactment — i.e. after July 4, 2026 — must be placed in service by December 31, 2027 to claim §45Y production credits or §48E investment credits. That is a brutally short window for utility-scale development. A July 7, 2025 Executive Order directed Treasury to tighten the "beginning of construction" rules, and Treasury’s Notice 2025-42 (August 15, 2025) did so: for wind projects, and solar projects above 1.5 MW, starting on or after September 2, 2025, spending 5% of a project’s cost no longer counts as starting construction (the old "5% safe harbor"); significant physical work on the project still does. Layered on top, Foreign Entity of Concern (FEOC) rules deny credits to projects with disqualifying Chinese, Russian, Iranian or North Korean ownership, debt or "material assistance" content — material-assistance rules bite for projects starting construction after December 31, 2025, and the supply chain for solar modules and batteries is heavily China-linked. The economics of post-2027 wind and solar development are structurally worse than they were in 2024.

2. Balance sheet leverage and rate sensitivity. Total debt is $113.9B against $5.58B of cash (StockAnalysis; the release shows $97.1B of long-term debt plus an $8.8B current portion); debt-to-equity is 1.53, current ratio is 0.53, and trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is negative $18.01B on $14.82B of operating cash flow, because the company is spending far more than it earns to build rate base and backlog. That model works beautifully when capital is cheap and works badly when it is not — the 2023 de-rating was precisely this lesson. On the Q3 call CFO Mike Dunne put the total equity need at roughly $12B–$17B through 2032, so shareholders fund part of the build. Enterprise value ($280.4B, our calculation from StockAnalysis at the October 26 close) is 63% larger than market cap; debtholders own a large share of the asset base.

3. Concentration in a single, physically exposed jurisdiction. Two-thirds of revenue comes from one state. Florida carries hurricane risk (storm restoration costs recovered through regulatory mechanisms, but with timing and political friction), a single utility commission whose composition can change, and a customer-growth story that depends on continued in-migration. A hostile PSC after 2029, or a severe storm season colliding with an unpopular bill increase, is the concentrated downside. The risk is current: Hurricane Isaias crossed Northwest Florida in early October 2026, and the Q3 release says FPL had restored power to more than 97% of affected customers by the Tuesday after the storm left its service area on October 10.

4. Execution and integration risk on the Dominion combination. The proposed Dominion Energy transaction — approved by both companies' shareholders on Sept. 3, with Virginia and South Carolina hearings in November and December, final orders expected in January 2027, North Carolina expert-witness hearings starting in February 2027 and closing targeted for the second half of 2027 (Q3 2026 release) — is the largest strategic bet in the company's history. The companies have sweetened the Virginia package: residential bill credits doubled from 2 to 4 years, $100 million of shareholder-funded customer bill assistance through 2038 and up to a $5 billion Virginia supplier program. It brings multi-jurisdiction regulatory risk and a long window of deal uncertainty. The stock fell 11.1% in May 2026, the month the deal was announced (month-end closes of $97.88 and $87.01).

Notable One-Off Items

Hedging mark-to-market is the dominant distortion, and in Q3 it is very large. In Q3 2026 NextEra reported GAAP net income of $4.775B ($2.28/share) against adjusted earnings of $2.592B ($1.24/share) — a $2.183B favorable gap, or 46% of reported GAAP profit. NextEra excludes the mark-to-market on non-qualifying hedges (interest rate and commodity derivatives that do not receive hedge accounting) precisely because these swing violently with rates and power prices and reverse over time. By segment (release): Corporate & Other earned $1.664B GAAP against −$275M adjusted, a $1.939B gap; NEER $1.491B GAAP against $1.247B adjusted, a $244M gap; FPL showed no adjustment ($1.62B both ways).

The distortion runs in both directions across periods, which is why single-quarter GAAP comparisons are close to meaningless here. In FY2025 the sign flipped: GAAP EPS was $3.30 while adjusted EPS was $3.71 — a $0.41/share unfavorable GAAP gap. Management's own 8%+ long-term growth target is measured “off the 2025 base of $3.71” per the release, i.e. off the adjusted number. Any valuation built on the TTM GAAP EPS of $5.55 (and therefore on the 14.78 trailing P/E) overstates the underlying earnings power. The forward P/E of 19.84 is closer to the market's view of underlying earnings.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten down and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. NEE closed October 2021 at $85.33 and October 26, 2026 at $82.04 — a −3.9% price return over five years (month-end close to banner-date close, dividends not included). Over the same window SPY, the S&P 500 fund, went from $459.25 to $765.37, a +66.7% price return on the same basis. That is a poor five years in absolute terms for a company whose earnings grew throughout, and a dramatically worse one relative to the index. The shape matters more than the endpoint: the stock made a cycle high of $93.36 in December 2021, spent two years de-rating to a low of $55.19 in February 2024 (−40.9% from the high), then rebuilt to a five-year high month-end close of $97.88 in April 2026 — a +77.4% recovery off the bottom — before giving back 11.1% in May on the Dominion announcement. The October 26 close is 16.2% below that April month-end.

The de-rating and the recovery were both about the same variable: the discount rate. NEE is, in market terms, a long-duration bond with an equity ticker. When the 10-year Treasury repriced upward through 2022–23, a company funding a negative-$18B free cash flow build program with debt and equity was hit twice — once on the multiple, once on the cost of the growth itself. The 2024–26 recovery ran the same mechanism in reverse, amplified by the AI-power narrative. 08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

NextEra owns both halves of the electricity trade at a moment when electricity demand is inflecting for the first time in two decades. FPL delivers ~9.2% regulated rate base growth under a four-year rate agreement locked through 2029, with 8 GW of large-load demand targeted by 2032. NEER carries a 36.7 GW contracted backlog, a restarting nuclear asset in Duane Arnold, and three federal hubs totalling about 16.5 GW that it builds and operates for fees without investing its own capital. Management targets the high end of 2026 adjusted EPS guidance ($3.92–$4.02) and commits to 8%+ adjusted EPS growth through 2035 off a $3.71 2025 base — 9%+ if Dominion closes — plus ~10% dividend growth through 2026 and ~6%/yr thereafter, on a dividend growth streak of more than 30 years. On 2026 adjusted guidance the stock trades at 20.4–20.9x, in line with a 20.8x sector, with a $94.58 consensus target implying 15% upside.

▼ Bear Case

The earnings quality is worse than the headline: TTM GAAP EPS of $5.55 embeds $2.18B of favorable hedge mark-to-market in Q3 alone, and the 14.78 trailing P/E is far lower than the ~20x the market pays for adjusted earnings. Free cash flow is −$18.01B, debt is $113.9B against $5.58B cash, D/E is 1.53 and ROIC is 3.13% — a company that must continuously access capital markets to grow, with management putting its equity need at roughly $12B–$17B through 2032, in an era when it can no longer count on federal tax credits. Post-July-2026 construction starts must be in service by December 2027 to earn §45Y/§48E credits, and FEOC content rules threaten the supply chain regardless. Add Dominion regulatory risk through 2027, a $56 low target on the Street, and a stock that is 16.9% below its 52-week intraday high of $98.75 (May 1, 2026) and below its 200-day moving average.

Top 3 Risks
01
Clean-energy tax credit rollback. Wind and solar projects beginning construction after July 4, 2026 must be placed in service by December 31, 2027 to claim §45Y/§48E credits — a window too short for most utility-scale development — and FEOC material-assistance rules apply to projects starting after December 31, 2025. Treasury’s Notice 2025-42 ended the traditional 5%-of-cost safe harbor for wind and larger solar projects starting on or after September 2, 2025. The value of NEER's post-2027 pipeline depends heavily on how much capacity had started construction in time and how strictly the rules are read.
02
Interest rates and the funding model. With $113.9B of debt, negative $18.01B free cash flow and a 0.53 current ratio, NextEra is structurally dependent on cheap capital. The 2022–24 drawdown (−40.9% on month-end closes, December 2021 to February 2024, with no deterioration in operations) is the empirical proof of how this stock behaves when the long end backs up. A renewed rise in long yields hits the multiple and the project economics simultaneously.
03
Dominion merger execution. A close window through H2 2027, multi-state regulatory review (Virginia and South Carolina hearings in November and December, final orders expected January 2027, North Carolina hearings from February 2027), a sweetened Virginia benefits package, and integration risk on the largest transaction in company history. The 11.1% single-month drop in May 2026 shows the market's initial verdict; a blocked, delayed or repriced deal removes the 9%+ pro-forma growth math that the highest price targets on the Street depend on.
  Industry Commentary — Macro Themes That Matter Most Right Now
AI Data-Center Electricity Demand
EMERGING TAILWIND
US electricity demand is growing again after roughly two decades of flat load, and the marginal buyer is a hyperscaler with an urgent, price-insensitive need for firm power. The evidence in NextEra's own numbers is specific: an FPL large-load target raised from 6 GW to 8 GW by 2032, which converts demand directly into regulated rate base, the highest-quality earnings a utility can add; and at NEER three federal hubs totalling about 16.5 GW, with $5.7B of initial funding secured, plus hub discussions with AI and data-center customers expected to grow from 30 to 40 by year-end 2026 (Q3 2026 release). The scarce commodity is not panels; it is interconnection, land and speed — precisely NextEra's accumulated advantages. Watch for the first announced FPL large-load transaction, guided for year-end 2026, as the moment the narrative becomes a contract.
Clean-Energy Policy Reversal
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the largest quantifiable change to NextEra's operating environment in a decade, and it runs directly against the business that made the company. Under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (enacted July 4, 2025), wind and solar projects beginning construction after July 4, 2026 must be placed in service by December 31, 2027 to qualify for §45Y production or §48E investment tax credits — effectively an eighteen-month build clock on assets that routinely take longer. A July 7, 2025 Executive Order directed Treasury to enforce the termination of the credits strictly and to prevent circumvention of the "beginning of construction" rules, and Treasury’s Notice 2025-42 (August 15, 2025) then removed the long-standing 5%-of-cost safe harbor for wind and for solar above 1.5 MW, for projects starting on or after September 2, 2025. Separately, Foreign Entity of Concern rules deny credits where Chinese, Russian, Iranian or North Korean entities hold 25%+ equity, issued 15%+ of debt, appoint officers, or supply an impermissible share of "material assistance" — the last applying to projects starting construction after December 31, 2025, against a module and cell supply chain that is overwhelmingly China-linked. What survives comparatively intact: battery storage under §48E (unchanged eligibility timeline) and nuclear — which is precisely why 2.4 GW of the 3.7 GW added to backlog in Q3 2026 was battery storage, and why Duane Arnold matters more than its megawatts suggest. The strategic pivot is visible in the numbers; the question is whether it is fast enough.
Interest Rates & Utility Valuations — the Bull/Bear Fulcrum
ONGOING HEADWIND
Everything above resolves through the discount rate. With beta of 0.64, a 3.04% dividend yield and $113.9B of debt funding a negative-$18.01B free cash flow build program, NEE is a rate-duration instrument that happens to trade on an equity exchange. The bull framing: if long yields drift lower, the utility complex re-rates, NextEra's cost of capital falls exactly when its 36.7 GW backlog and 8 GW of large-load opportunity need funding, and an 8%+ adjusted EPS growth rate on a dividend streak of more than 30 years becomes scarce and valuable — the $94.58 average target and its $110 high follow naturally. The bear framing: if the long end stays high or rises, the same leverage that amplifies growth amplifies the damage, and 2022–24 is the template — a 40.9% drawdown on month-end closes with no operational deterioration whatsoever. Note the current setup: NEE trades at 14.8x trailing GAAP earnings versus a 20.8x utility sector (Oct 27, 2026), but about 20.4–20.9x 2026 adjusted guidance, so the apparent discount is mostly hedge gains in the earnings. Whether the stock is pricing merger risk or offers an entry point is the actual investment question — and it will be settled by the yield curve and by whether the first large-load contract lands before year-end.
