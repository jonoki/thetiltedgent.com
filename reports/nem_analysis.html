01 Company Overview

Newmont Corporation is the world's largest gold producer, mining roughly 5.89 million attributable ounces of gold in fiscal 2025 across a portfolio spanning North America, South America, Australia, Papua New Guinea and Africa. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $22.67 billion, up 21.3% YoY, and trailing-twelve-month revenue has since climbed to $26.61 billion (+23.7%) — an increase driven far more by the metal price than by volume, since Newmont has been shrinking its asset count on purpose. The company ranks #108 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization on October 26, 2026, and at $26.61B of TTM revenue it accounts for about 19% of the entire listed gold-mining industry's revenue (StockAnalysis industry aggregate: 55 companies, $142.64B revenue, $634.13B combined market value).

The critical thing to understand about Newmont as an equity is that it is a leveraged claim on the gold price, not a compounding operating business. When the realized gold price rose 45% in 2025 to $3,498/oz, GAAP net income went from $3.35B to $7.09B, and free cash flow hit a record $7.30B. That operating leverage runs in both directions, and it is the single most important variable in any NEM valuation.

Cost of Capital Advantage

Mining is a commodity business with no pricing power, so a gold miner's moat is not about the product — it is about orebody quality, mine life and cost of capital. Newmont's edge is that after the Newcrest acquisition it owns an unusual concentration of long-life, large-scale assets (Cadia, Lihir, Boddington, Tanami, Ahafo, Nevada Gold Mines) that can be sustained for decades rather than replaced every 8–10 years, which is the treadmill that destroys value at mid-tier producers. Scale also buys the cheapest capital in the sector: total debt of just $5.58B against $8.83B of cash at September 30, 2026 gives a net cash position ($3.2B per the Q3 release, before the $1.95B Nevada Gold Mines payment due in the fourth quarter) and a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.16, so Newmont can fund Cadia PC1-2, Red Chris Block Cave and Tanami Expansion 2 out of operating cash flow while simultaneously buying back stock. Finally, index membership matters more than investors admit: as the only gold miner in the S&P 500, Newmont is held by every fund that tracks the index — the largest holders are Vanguard, BlackRock and State Street — a source of steady buyers that peers listed only in Canada, Australia or Johannesburg do not get.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Total dependence on an exogenous price. Newmont controls its costs and its volumes; it controls nothing about revenue per ounce. Gold futures' 52-week range of $3,901.30 to $5,586.20 (COMEX front month, intraday, Yahoo Finance) — a 43% spread — swamps every operational lever management can pull, and the October 26 close of $4,038.40 sat below both the Q3 realized price of $4,317/oz and the $4,500/oz assumption behind 2026 guidance. A trailing P/E of 12.9x looks cheap only if gold stays near today's levels; if earnings halved on a lower metal price, the same share price would be about 26 times earnings.

2. Costs are still rising, if less than feared. In the Q3 release Newmont cut its 2026 by-product AISC guidance to $1,520/oz from the $1,680/oz it guided through the Q2 release, citing cost discipline and higher realized co-product prices; Q3 printed $1,454/oz and the nine months $1,365/oz. Even the lower guide is 12% above the $1,358/oz of 2025, and the release warns that unit costs rise in the fourth quarter (guided at $1,900/oz AISC) on heavier sustaining capital spending. Labour, contractor and equipment costs have reset higher, and declining grades at Cadia and Yanacocha mean more tonnes moved per ounce recovered. Margin expansion has been a gold-price story, not an efficiency story.

3. Reserve depletion and a shrinking asset base. Attributable production is guided down to ~5.26 Moz in 2026 from 5.89 Moz in 2025. Some of that is deliberate divestiture, but the pipeline replacing it (Tanami Expansion 2, now due in commercial production in H1 2027; Cadia PC1-2; Red Chris Block Cave, estimated at $4.2–$4.8B of development capital with a funding decision in early 2027 and commercial production targeted for 2033; Ahafo North) is multi-year and capital-intensive, with $1.95B of sustaining and $1.40B of development capital budgeted for 2026 alone, plus the $1.95B payment to Barrick for the expanded Nevada joint venture. There is no path to organic growth without either large capex or another acquisition — and Newmont's acquisition record includes the $779M Yanacocha Sulfides impairment.

4. Jurisdictional and social-licence concentration. Peru (Yanacocha), Argentina (Cerro Negro), Suriname (Merian), Ghana (Ahafo) and Papua New Guinea (Lihir) together account for a meaningful share of ounces, and each carries distinct expropriation, royalty-change, community-blockade or currency-control risk. Cadia's April 2026 seismic events — which took until mid-June to normalise, with Q3 output higher on the recovery — are a reminder that block-cave mining also carries irreducible geotechnical risk at the single asset that matters most for copper. At Lihir the company is watching El Niño water-supply effects, which it expects to intensify through year-end.

Notable One-Off Items. Newmont's recent income statements carry more non-recurring noise than a typical large-cap, and several items materially distort the reported multiples above:

Yanacocha Sulfides impairment — $779 million (FY2025). Newmont indefinitely deferred the Yanacocha Sulfides project and wrote down the carrying value. This is the largest single charge in the period and reduces FY2025 GAAP net income of $7.085B relative to adjusted net income of $7.634B ($6.89 adjusted EPS).

Reclamation liability revision — $137 million net benefit (FY2025). A reduction in estimated future mine-closure obligations flowed through as income. Reclamation re-estimates are non-cash, recur annually, and can go either way — Newmont has taken large charges from this line in prior years. Do not capitalise it.

Divestiture proceeds and gains — $4.5 billion after tax. The non-core disposal programme sold Telfer, Cripple Creek & Victor, Musselwhite, Éléonore, Porcupine and Akyem, and the residual equity stake in Greatland Resources was monetised for $134 million in January 2026. These proceeds funded buybacks and debt reduction but also mechanically reduce the 2026 ounce base (5.89 Moz → ~5.26 Moz guided). Gains on sale inflate reported earnings without being repeatable.

Newcrest purchase accounting (2023–2025). The Newcrest acquisition, completed November 2023, brought fair-value step-ups on acquired mineral properties that continue to run through depreciation, depletion and amortisation at elevated rates. This is why FY2024 revenue jumped 58.16% while net income recovered far more slowly, and it is why NEM's GAAP earnings have historically screened worse than its cash generation. Cash flow is the cleaner lens for this business.

On the dividend. The current declared dividend is a flat $0.26 per share per quarter under the enhanced capital allocation framework announced with FY2025 results, which prioritises sustaining capital, then a sustainable base dividend, then development projects, then balance-sheet optimisation, then buybacks for excess cash. Newmont historically operated a gold-price-linked variable dividend framework; the February 2026 framework instead sets a $1.1B-a-year dividend, divided by the share count each February, and sends excess cash to steady buybacks, which lower the share count and so lift the per-share dividend. Shareholder returns still rise with gold, but through buybacks rather than a variable dividend top-up.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen a lot very quickly and may be stretched; below 30 means it has fallen hard and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a stock can stay overbought for months in a strong trend.

Five-year trajectory. On closing prices (price return, dividends excluded), NEM went from $54.00 at the end of October 2021 to $110.39 on October 26, 2026 — a price return of +104%, or about 15.4% annualised. But that headline conceals two entirely different regimes. From October 2021 through February 2024 the stock fell 42% on month-end closes, bottoming at $31.25 as the Fed's hiking cycle drove real rates sharply positive and mining cost inflation compressed margins on a flat gold price. Essentially the entire five-year return was generated in the 32 months since: from the February 2024 month-end low to the October 26, 2026 close the stock is up 253%. This is the defining characteristic of miner equities — long stretches of value destruction punctuated by violent re-ratings when the metal moves.

The 2026 action deserves its own note. NEM's highest month-end close is still the $130.00 of February 2026, just after gold futures set their 52-week intraday high of $5,586.20 on January 29; the stock then gave back 28% into a June close of $93.40 as gold corrected, held there in July ($93.71), and August rebounded 34.5% to a $126.06 close, and the intraday 52-week high of $135.29 came on August 25. Since then gold has slipped — COMEX front-month futures fell 11.2% from $4,545.30 on August 19 to $4,038.40 on October 26 — and NEM fell 11.7% from the previous edition's $125.08 to $110.39 over the same dates, 12.4% below the August close. The share price tracked the metal almost one for one, even through a quarter that beat estimates and cut cost guidance. Realized volatility here is far above what the 0.53 five-year beta suggests, because the beta is measured against the S&P 500, not against the risk that actually drives the stock.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Newmont is the world's largest gold producer running a deliberately high-graded portfolio at a moment of extraordinary margin: a Q3 2026 realized price of $4,317/oz against by-product AISC of $1,454/oz is roughly $2,860 of margin per ounce, on 5.26 Moz of guided 2026 production — and management just cut full-year cost guidance rather than raising it. That has produced $10.07B of TTM free cash flow, a net cash balance sheet ($8.83B cash vs $5.58B debt), a doubled $6.0B buyback authorisation and 3.91% buyback yield. At 12.1x consensus 2026 EPS of $9.13 and 11.0x the $10.06 expected for 2027 (StockAnalysis), the stock is not pricing durable $4,000-plus gold — and demand from central banks (288.9 tonnes of net purchases in Q2 2026, a record for any second quarter, +62% YoY, with 89% of central banks in the World Gold Council's survey expecting global reserves to rise over the next year) suggests the buying is not a short-term trade.

Bear Case

Every favourable metric on this page is a function of a metal price that has swung 43% within its own 52-week range and already sits 28% below its $5,586 January high — gold futures closed October 26 at $4,038.40, below both the Q3 realized price and the $4,500 assumption behind guidance. Costs still rise — even the reduced 2026 AISC guide of $1,520/oz is 12% above 2025 actuals, and the fourth quarter is guided at $1,900/oz — while volumes shrink, from 5.89 Moz to a guided 5.26 Moz, and $1.95B of cash goes to Barrick in Q4. If a lower gold price halved earnings, EPS would fall far below the $8.59 TTM figure and today's 12.9x trailing multiple would become about 26x on trough earnings, with no growth to justify it. The stock fell 4.6% on a quarter that beat estimates, and the $70 low end of StockAnalysis's target range shows what a genuine gold mean reversion could do.

Top 3 Risks
01
Gold price reversion. Newmont's earnings are a levered derivative of an exogenous, non-yielding, sentiment-driven asset. Gold futures' 52-week range of $3,901.30–$5,586.20 shows how far the metal can move in a year. A 25% fall from the Q3 realized price of $4,317/oz would take gold to about $3,240, and with AISC at the $1,520/oz guide the margin per ounce would drop from about $2,800 to about $1,720 — roughly two-fifths gone while costs stay put. Capital allocation and execution. With the $4.5B divestiture programme complete, growth now requires capex or M&A — and Newmont's record includes the $779M Yanacocha Sulfides impairment following a large acquisition. Tanami Expansion 2 (commercial production now due H1 2027), Cadia PC1-2 and Red Chris Block Cave ($4.2–$4.8B estimated, funding decision early 2027) are multi-year, capital-hungry projects against $1.95B sustaining plus $1.40B development capital in 2026 alone, on top of the $1.95B Nevada Gold Mines payment. Cadia's April 2026 seismic events show how quickly block-cave execution risk can materialise at the asset that matters most.
  Industry Commentary
Gold as a Monetary Hedge: Central Banks Are the Marginal Buyer
EMERGING TAILWIND
The World Gold Council recorded 288.9 tonnes of net central-bank purchases in Q2 2026 — up 62% year over year and a record for any second quarter — and in its survey 89% of central banks expected global gold reserves to rise over the next year, with a record 45% planning to add to their own (Gold Demand Trends Q2 2026, July 30, 2026). Central banks buy for reserves rather than to trade the price, and they were not buying at this scale in earlier cycles. Layered on top is the rate channel: because bullion pays no yield, higher real Treasury yields raise the cost of holding it. That channel has worked against gold since August: COMEX front-month futures fell from $4,545.30 on August 19 to $4,038.40 on October 26, an 11.2% drop, and NEM fell 11.7% over the same dates. For Newmont, that is the entire revenue line moving on the macro backdrop.
Mining Cost Inflation Is Structural, Not Transitory
ONGOING HEADWIND
The industry's cost base has permanently reset. Newmont's by-product AISC goes from $1,358/oz in FY2025 to a guided $1,520/oz for 2026 — a 12% increase in a single year, even after the Q3 cut from $1,680/oz, which the company put down to cost discipline and higher co-product prices; Q3 2026 printed $1,454/oz and the fourth quarter is guided at $1,900/oz. The drivers are labour scarcity in Western Australia and Nevada, contractor and equipment rates, energy, and above all declining head grades: lower grades at Cadia take the 2026 copper guide down to 102 kt from 135 kt. Sustaining capital of $1.95B is itself a cost of standing still. The uncomfortable implication is that the sector's margin expansion since 2024 has come almost entirely from the numerator; if gold flatlines, unit costs keep climbing and margins compress without any change in the metal price.
Post-Newcrest High-Grading: Fewer, Bigger, Longer — Bull vs Bear
STRUCTURAL SHIFT
Since absorbing Newcrest in November 2023, Newmont has sold Telfer, Cripple Creek & Victor, Musselwhite, Éléonore, Porcupine and Akyem for $4.5 billion in after-tax proceeds, closing the programme with the $134M Greatland Resources stake sale in January 2026. The bull framing: what remains is a concentrated set of Tier 1, multi-decade orebodies — Cadia, Lihir, Boddington, Tanami, Ahafo, Nevada Gold Mines — with embedded copper optionality at Cadia PC1-2 (35–55 kt Cu/yr at its 2030–2040 peak) and the newly permitted Red Chris Block Cave; proceeds went straight into a $6.0B buyback rather than into another deal. The bear framing: the company has shrunk its ounce base by roughly 11% (5.89 Moz → 5.26 Moz guided), has no organic growth until Tanami Expansion 2 reaches commercial production in H1 2027, has exhausted its disposal proceeds, is paying $1.95B to fold Barrick's Fourmile into Nevada Gold Mines, and is now more concentrated in single large assets — meaning one Cadia-style seismic event moves the consolidated result. High-grading raises average asset quality and lowers diversification at the same time.
