01 Company Overview

Newmont Corporation is the world's largest gold producer, mining roughly 5.89 million attributable ounces of gold in fiscal 2025 across a portfolio spanning North America, South America, Australia, Papua New Guinea and Africa. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $22.67 billion, up 21.3% YoY, and trailing-twelve-month revenue has since climbed to $25.77 billion (+25.2%) — an increase driven far more by the metal price than by volume, since Newmont has been shrinking its asset count on purpose. The company ranks roughly #179 globally and #92 in the United States by market capitalization, and at $25.77B of TTM revenue it accounts for about 19% of the entire listed gold-mining industry's revenue (industry aggregate: 53 companies, $135.57B revenue, $698.5B combined market value).

The critical thing to understand about Newmont as an equity is that it is a leveraged claim on the gold price, not a compounding operating business. Roughly two-thirds of the all-in cost per ounce is fixed in the near term; when the realized gold price rose 45% in 2025 to $3,498/oz, GAAP net income went from $3.35B to $7.09B, and free cash flow hit a record $7.30B. That operating leverage runs in both directions, and it is the single most important variable in any NEM valuation.

Cost of Capital Advantage

Mining is a commodity business with no pricing power, so a gold miner's moat is not about the product — it is about orebody quality, mine life and cost of capital. Newmont's edge is that after the Newcrest acquisition it owns an unusual concentration of long-life, large-scale assets (Cadia, Lihir, Boddington, Tanami, Ahafo, Nevada Gold Mines) that can be sustained for decades rather than replaced every 8–10 years, which is the treadmill that destroys value at mid-tier producers. Scale also buys the cheapest capital in the sector: total debt of just $5.60B against $9.01B of cash gives a net cash position and a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.16, so Newmont can fund Cadia PC1-2, Red Chris Block Cave and Tanami Expansion 2 out of operating cash flow while simultaneously buying back stock. Finally, index membership matters more than investors admit: as the sole gold miner in the S&P 500, Newmont receives a persistent passive bid — 68.85% institutional ownership dominated by Vanguard, BlackRock and State Street — that peers listed only in Canada, Australia or Johannesburg do not get.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Total dependence on an exogenous price. Newmont controls its costs and its volumes; it controls nothing about revenue per ounce. Spot gold's 52-week range of $3,301.77 to $5,597.23 — a 70% spread — swamps every operational lever management can pull. Trailing P/E of 15.8x looks cheap only if $4,400 gold persists; on a mid-cycle $2,800–$3,200 assumption, earnings power roughly halves and the same share price is a 30x multiple.

2. Cost inflation is outrunning the ounce count. By-product AISC is guided to $1,680/oz in 2026 versus $1,358/oz actual in 2025 — a 24% increase — and Q2 2026 already printed $1,621/oz, up 18% year over year. Labour, diesel, grinding media, contractor rates and mining-equipment costs have all reset higher, and declining grades at Cadia and Yanacocha mean more tonnes moved per ounce recovered. Margin expansion has been a gold-price story, not an efficiency story.

3. Reserve depletion and a shrinking asset base. Attributable production is guided down to ~5.26 Moz in 2026 from 5.89 Moz in 2025. Some of that is deliberate divestiture, but the pipeline replacing it (Tanami Expansion 2 in H2 2027, Cadia PC1-2, Red Chris Block Cave, Ahafo North) is multi-year and capital-intensive, with $1.95B of sustaining and $1.40B of development capital budgeted for 2026 alone. There is no path to organic growth without either large capex or another acquisition — and Newmont's acquisition record includes the $779M Yanacocha Sulfides impairment.

4. Jurisdictional and social-licence concentration. Peru (Yanacocha), Argentina (Cerro Negro), Suriname (Merian), Ghana (Ahafo) and Papua New Guinea (Lihir) together account for a meaningful share of ounces, and each carries distinct expropriation, royalty-change, community-blockade or currency-control risk. Cadia's April 2026 seismic events — which took until mid-June to normalise — are a reminder that block-cave mining also carries irreducible geotechnical risk at the single asset that matters most for copper.

Notable One-Off Items. Newmont's recent income statements carry more non-recurring noise than a typical large-cap, and several items materially distort the reported multiples above:

Yanacocha Sulfides impairment — $779 million (FY2025). Newmont indefinitely deferred the Yanacocha Sulfides project and wrote down the carrying value. This is the largest single charge in the period and reduces FY2025 GAAP net income of $7.085B relative to adjusted net income of $7.634B ($6.89 adjusted EPS).

Reclamation liability revision — $137 million net benefit (FY2025). A reduction in estimated future mine-closure obligations flowed through as income. Reclamation re-estimates are non-cash, recur annually, and can go either way — Newmont has taken large charges from this line in prior years. Do not capitalise it.

Divestiture proceeds and gains — $4.5 billion after tax. The non-core disposal programme sold Cripple Creek & Victor, Musselwhite, Éléonore, Porcupine and Akyem during 2025, and the residual equity stake in Greatland Resources was monetised for $134 million in January 2026. These proceeds funded buybacks and debt reduction but also mechanically reduce the 2026 ounce base (5.89 Moz → ~5.26 Moz guided). Gains on sale inflate reported earnings without being repeatable.

Newcrest purchase accounting (2023–2025). The Newcrest acquisition, completed November 2023, brought fair-value step-ups on acquired mineral properties that continue to run through depreciation, depletion and amortisation at elevated rates. This is why FY2024 revenue jumped 58.16% while net income recovered far more slowly, and it is why NEM's GAAP earnings have historically screened worse than its cash generation. Cash flow is the cleaner lens for this business.

On the dividend. The current declared dividend is a flat $0.26 per share per quarter under the enhanced capital allocation framework announced with FY2025 results, which prioritises sustaining capital, then a sustainable base dividend, then development projects, then balance-sheet optimisation, then buybacks for excess cash. Verify before relying on this: Newmont historically operated a gold-price-linked variable dividend framework, and the current disclosures reviewed here describe a fixed base dividend with excess cash routed to repurchases rather than an explicit variable top-up. The economic effect is similar — shareholder returns rise with gold — but the mechanism appears to have shifted from dividend to buyback.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward versus downward. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen a lot very quickly and may be stretched; below 30 means it has fallen hard and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — a stock can stay overbought for months in a strong trend.

Five-year trajectory. On dividend-adjusted monthly closes, NEM went from $47.41 in September 2021 to $125.08 in August 2026 — a total return of roughly +164%, or about 21% annualised. But that headline conceals two entirely different regimes. From September 2021 through February 2024 the stock fell 37%, bottoming at $29.85 as the Fed's hiking cycle drove real rates sharply positive and mining cost inflation compressed margins on a flat gold price. Essentially the entire five-year return was generated in the 30 months since: from the February 2024 low to the August 2026 close the stock is up 319%. This is the defining characteristic of miner equities — long stretches of value destruction punctuated by violent re-ratings when the metal moves.

The 2026 action deserves its own note. NEM peaked at $129.44 on a monthly-close basis in February 2026 (intraday 52-week high $134.88) as spot gold reached its 52-week high of $5,597.23, then gave back roughly 28% into a July close of $93.71 as gold corrected. The August rebound to $125.08 — including a single-day +7.85% move on August 19 — retraced almost all of it in three weeks. Realized annualised volatility here is far above what the 0.50 five-year beta suggests, because the beta is measured against the S&P 500, not against the risk that actually drives the stock.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Newmont is the world's largest gold producer running a deliberately high-graded portfolio at a moment of extraordinary margin: a Q2 2026 realized price of $4,414/oz against by-product AISC of $1,621/oz is roughly $2,800 of cash margin per ounce, on 5.26 Moz of guided 2026 production. That has produced $9.73B of TTM free cash flow, a net cash balance sheet ($9.01B cash vs $5.60B debt), a doubled $6.0B buyback authorisation and 3.94% buyback yield. At 12.4x forward earnings with consensus 2026 EPS of $9.41, the stock is not pricing durable $4,000-plus gold — and structural demand from central banks (288.9 tonnes of net purchases in Q2 2026, a record for any second quarter, +62% YoY, with 89% of surveyed central banks expecting reserves to grow further) suggests the bid is not a trade.

Bear Case

Every favourable metric on this page is a function of a metal price that has already risen 70% within its own 52-week range and sits 22% below a $5,597 spike high. Costs are moving the wrong way — 2026 AISC guidance of $1,680/oz is 24% above 2025 actuals — and volumes are shrinking, from 5.89 Moz to a guided 5.26 Moz. On a mid-cycle $2,800–$3,200 gold assumption, cash margin per ounce roughly halves, EPS falls well below the $7.90 TTM figure, and today's 15.8x trailing multiple becomes a 30x-plus multiple on trough earnings with no growth to justify it. Three analysts — BNP Paribas Exane at $102, Argus at $110, Goldman at $111.40 — already carry targets below spot, and the $67 low end of the Street range shows what a genuine gold mean reversion would do.

Top 3 Risks
01
Gold price reversion. Newmont's earnings are a levered derivative of an exogenous, non-yielding, sentiment-driven asset. Spot gold's 52-week range of $3,301.77–$5,597.23 implies that a return to the low end would cut the realized price by roughly 25% while costs stay put — mathematically wiping out well over half of operating profit. Real rates and the Fed path, not mine plans, set the outcome.
02
Cost inflation outrunning grade. By-product AISC is guided 24% higher in 2026 ($1,680/oz vs $1,358/oz) and Q2 already ran up 18% YoY. Declining grades at Cadia (2026 copper guided down to 102 kt from 135 kt) and Yanacocha mean more tonnes for fewer ounces. At 5.26 Moz, each unplanned $100/oz of cost inflation removes about $525M of annual pre-tax cash flow.
03
Capital allocation and execution. With the $4.5B divestiture programme complete, growth now requires capex or M&A — and Newmont's record includes the $779M Yanacocha Sulfides impairment following a large acquisition. Tanami Expansion 2 (H2 2027), Cadia PC1-2 and Red Chris Block Cave are multi-year, capital-hungry projects against $1.95B sustaining plus $1.40B development capital in 2026 alone. Cadia's April 2026 seismic events show how quickly block-cave execution risk can materialise at the asset that matters most.
  Industry Commentary
Gold as a Monetary Hedge: Central Banks Are the Marginal Buyer
EMERGING TAILWIND
The World Gold Council recorded 288.9 tonnes of net central-bank purchases in Q2 2026 — up 62% year over year and a record for any second quarter on file — and 89% of 76 surveyed central banks said they expect global gold reserves to expand over the following twelve months. This is a price-insensitive, non-speculative bid that did not exist at this scale in prior cycles. Layered on top is the rate channel: because bullion pays no yield, higher real Treasury yields raise its opportunity cost. When softer August 2026 inflation data cut priced-in odds of a September Fed hike from ~55% to ~35%, gold rallied ~9% in two weeks, from about $4,050/oz on August 3 to $4,400–$4,420/oz by August 16, closing at $4,368/oz on August 19. For Newmont, that is the entire revenue line moving on a macro data print.
Mining Cost Inflation Is Structural, Not Transitory
ONGOING HEADWIND
The industry's cost base has permanently reset. Newmont's by-product AISC went from $1,358/oz in FY2025 to a guided $1,680/oz for 2026 — a 24% increase in a single year — with Q2 2026 printing $1,621/oz, up 18% YoY. The drivers are labour scarcity in Western Australia and Nevada, contractor and equipment rates, energy, and above all declining head grades: at Cadia the 2026 copper guide falls to 102 kt from 135 kt on lower grades alone. Sustaining capital of $1.95B is itself a cost of standing still. The uncomfortable implication is that the sector's margin expansion since 2024 has come almost entirely from the numerator; if gold flatlines, unit costs keep climbing and margins compress without any change in the metal price.
Post-Newcrest High-Grading: Fewer, Bigger, Longer — Bull vs Bear
STRUCTURAL SHIFT
Since absorbing Newcrest in November 2023, Newmont has sold Cripple Creek & Victor, Musselwhite, Éléonore, Porcupine and Akyem for $4.5 billion in after-tax proceeds, closing the programme with the $134M Greatland Resources stake sale in January 2026. The bull framing: what remains is a concentrated set of Tier 1, multi-decade orebodies — Cadia, Lihir, Boddington, Tanami, Ahafo, Nevada Gold Mines — with embedded copper optionality at Cadia PC1-2 (40–60 kt Cu/yr at peak) and the newly permitted Red Chris Block Cave; proceeds went straight into a $6.0B buyback rather than into another deal. The bear framing: the company has shrunk its ounce base by roughly 11% (5.89 Moz → 5.26 Moz guided), has no organic growth until Tanami Expansion 2 completes in H2 2027, has exhausted its disposal proceeds, and is now more concentrated in single large assets — meaning one Cadia-style seismic event moves the consolidated result. High-grading raises average asset quality and lowers diversification at the same time.
