01 Company Overview

Nestlé sells Nescafé, Nespresso, Purina, KitKat, Maggi and a long tail of nutrition brands around the world — CHF 89.5B of sales in 2025, down 2.0% in francs but up 3.5% organically. It is the third-largest holding in the Swiss Market Index, and at the October 27, 2026 close of CHF 83.25 the market valued it at about CHF 214.2B ($258.8B). Every price and per-share number on this page is in Swiss francs, the currency Nestlé reports in.

The story of the last five years is a slow leak. The stock closed October 2021 at CHF 120.82 and reached its highest month-end close, CHF 127.44, in December 2021; it now sits at CHF 83.25, 31.1% lower, while the SMI rose 20.6% over the same window. The pricing that carried sales through the inflation years came with weak volumes (RIG of 0.8% in both 2024 and 2025), a strong franc shaved 5.7% off 2025 sales in translation, and the board replaced its chief executive — it dismissed Laurent Freixe on September 1, 2025 and appointed Nespresso's head, Philipp Navratil. His plan is narrower and blunter: four core businesses (Coffee, Petcare, Nutrition, Food & Snacks), the waters business moved into a joint venture, a sale of mainstream vitamins agreed (closing expected in the first half of 2027) and ice cream up for sale, about 16,000 job cuts planned and CHF 3.0B of savings by the end of 2027. The scorecard he chose is RIG: selling more, not just charging more. First-half 2026 RIG was 1.5% and third-quarter RIG 2.8% (1.9% for the nine months), against a medium-term target of 2%+.

The latest print. Nestlé's third-quarter and nine-month sales on October 22, 2026 beat forecasts on volume. Organic growth was 4.0% in the quarter, with RIG of 2.8% and pricing of 1.1%, against the AWP consensus of 3.5% and 1.7% (ten analysts, published by cash.ch the day before); RIG was positive in every zone and in the globally managed businesses. Nine-month sales were CHF 65,865M, flat in francs (organic +3.7%, currency −3.7%), against CHF 65,622M expected. Petcare (+5.3%) and Food & Snacks (+5.0%) led; Coffee slowed to +3.4% against its strongest quarter of 2025. Guidance for 2026 was kept. The same release said Nestlé no longer consolidates its Russian business from the end of September 2026: it remains the legal owner but has no control of management. The stock rose 1.08% that day. Sales updates carry no profit figures, so the earnings, margin and balance-sheet numbers on this page are still those of the first half.

The last full results. The half-year results on July 23, 2026 matched or slightly beat the consensus Nestlé compiles from analysts' forecasts before each report (17 to 18 analysts per line): organic growth 3.6% vs 3.5% expected, RIG 1.5% vs 1.5%, UTOP margin 16.4% vs 16.2%, underlying EPS CHF 2.22 vs CHF 2.16. Basic EPS of CHF 1.35 fell well short of the CHF 1.97 expected, because of the CHF 1.3B write-down on businesses held for sale. The stock still fell 7.98% that day; the same release cut the second-half margin outlook to "broadly similar to the first half". Since then Nestlé has agreed to sell its mainstream vitamins business to Yellow Wood Partners for USD 1.0B (September 1), and a Russian presidential decree of September 17 placed Nestlé Russia under temporary external administration.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Billionaire brands (KitKat, Maggi, Milo) Global distribution, developed and emerging Procurement scale in coffee and cocoa Nespresso Vertuo system Science-based nutrition E-commerce at 21.6% of sales 66 years of steady or rising dividends

The moat is shelf presence at global scale. Nestlé sells in developed markets, where it grew 2.3% organically in the first half of 2026, and in emerging markets, where it grew 5.6% (7.1% excluding China). Nestlé says it holds market-leading positions in three truly global categories — Coffee, Petcare and Nutrition, about 70% of sales — which is why the strategy narrows the group to those three plus Food & Snacks. The weakness is that brands protect shelf space, not margin: when coffee and cocoa costs spiked, Nestlé raised prices and lost volume, and the gross margin still fell 110 bps in 2025.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The franc eats the reported numbers. Nestlé sells in 185 countries and reports in Swiss francs. Currency moves cut reported sales by 5.7% in 2025 and by 6.2% in the first half of 2026, so 3.6% organic growth became a 2.5% fall in reported sales. Management expects about −3% from currency for full-year 2026 at spot rates. Dividends and earnings per share are paid and reported in francs, so a strong franc caps both.
02
Commodity exposure in the two biggest categories. Coffee and Food & Snacks (confectionery) are 56% of first-half sales and both rely on commodities whose prices rose. Coffee's margin fell 100 bps to 19.0% in H1 2026, and tariffs added to the pressure. Lower coffee and cocoa costs are part of management's second-half margin outlook; if they stay high, that outlook weakens.
03
A leveraged balance sheet for a staples company. Net debt was CHF 56.3B on June 30, 2026, net debt to adjusted EBITDA was 2.85x at end-2025, and the CHF 8.0B dividend outflow in April pushed net debt up from CHF 51.4B in December. The 2025 dividend of CHF 3.10 was 88% of basic EPS. Asset sales (waters JV, ice cream, vitamins) are the main route to deleveraging; deconsolidating Russia adds about CHF 0.9B to year-end net debt, by Nestlé's own estimate.
04
Execution and leadership churn. A new CEO since September 2025, a new chair since October 2025, a new general counsel from January 2027 (Manuela Bernasconi, replacing the retiring Leanne Geale, announced September 24, 2026), a reorganised Nutrition business and a 16,000-job cost programme are all running at once. Restructuring and net other trading items doubled to CHF 0.8B in H1 2026, and net profit fell 31.4%, driven by those restructuring costs and a CHF 1.3B write-down on businesses held for sale.
05
Russia. Nestlé had kept offices and factories running in Russia (Reuters). A presidential decree published on September 17, 2026 placed Nestlé Russia under temporary external administration by L.E.V. Management, alongside the French retailer Auchan (Reuters). Nestlé said the next day that it was assessing the situation and its options and would take all necessary steps to protect its rights; its Russian unit said on September 21 that it was continuing to trade in full. With the nine-month sales on October 22, Nestlé said it no longer consolidates the business from the end of September 2026: it remains the legal owner but does not control management. Annualised, Russia was about 2% of group sales and 3–4% of UTOP and free cash flow; Nestlé recognised a non-cash charge of about CHF 1.4B for cumulative currency translation adjustments, which has no effect on total equity, and expects limited impact on 2026 underlying results. Russia has used the same mechanism since 2023 on other Western companies, including Danone and Carlsberg.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between basic and underlying earnings is large right now, and it is the reason the trailing P/E looks expensive. In H1 2026, basic EPS fell 31.4% to CHF 1.35 while underlying EPS fell only 2.4% to CHF 2.22. Three items explain it: a CHF 1.3B non-cash write-down as the businesses being sold were classified as held for sale; restructuring and net other trading items of CHF 0.8B versus CHF 0.4B a year earlier, mainly from the cost programme; and a reported tax rate of 28.7% (underlying 22.3%) because the write-down is largely not tax-deductible. Administration costs also include a 30 bps benefit from a pension scheme change. In FY2025, restructuring and net other trading items were CHF 1.7B, covering impairments, litigation and recall-related inventory write-offs, and net debt benefited from a CHF 2.0B extraordinary distribution from the Froneri ice cream joint venture. Coming in H2 2026: deconsolidating Russia derecognised about CHF 1.6B of net assets and brought a non-cash charge of about CHF 1.4B for cumulative currency translation adjustments, which Nestlé says has no effect on total equity (Q3 sales release).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of CHF 120.82 to the October 27, 2026 close of CHF 83.25, Nestlé lost 31.1% of its price. The SMI rose 20.6%. The decline was not a crash but a staircase: the stock spent 2022 and most of 2023 between about CHF 104 and 127 at month-ends, broke below CHF 100 in October 2023, and fell to a month-end low of CHF 71.10 in July 2025. Since then it has moved sideways between about CHF 73 and 84 at month-ends.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 18.4 times consensus 2026 earnings and getting a 3.72% dividend yield from a company that, by its own count, has maintained or raised its dividend in francs for 66 years. The volume engine is restarting: RIG went from 0.2% in H1 2025 to 1.5% in H1 2026, 1.8% in Q2 and 2.8% in Q3, positive in every zone, with emerging markets growing 6.8% in the third quarter. Coffee and cocoa costs, which squeezed margins, are now expected to ease in the second half. The portfolio is being cut to its best businesses, with about CHF 2.8B of cash from the waters venture and USD 1.0B from the vitamins sale to come, and CHF 3.0B of savings due by the end of 2027. Free cash flow of CHF 10.2B over the last twelve months is a 4.8% yield. J.P. Morgan moved to Overweight in October, and at the October 27 close the average analyst target implies 7% upside.

Bear Case

Five years of falling prices have a reason. Organic growth was carried by pricing for years, and one strong quarter of volume (Q3 RIG 2.8%) leaves nine-month RIG at 1.9%, still short of the 2%+ the company set for itself; Q3 was helped by earlier seasonal orders and favourable weather in Europe. The franc keeps turning organic growth into falling reported sales, and margin guidance was trimmed in July. Net debt of CHF 56.3B and a dividend that took 88% of 2025 basic earnings leave little room for error, while a new CEO, a new chair and a 16,000-job restructuring all run at once. Ten of 23 analysts rate it a Hold, and the lowest target, CHF 72, sits 13.5% below the price. Nestlé has lost control of its Russian business, about 3–4% of its operating profit and free cash flow. A stock that rose 4.2% in a year when its home index rose 16.5% has not yet shown that the turnaround is working.

Top 3 Risks

01
RIG stalls below 2%. The whole plan is built on volume replacing price. Q3 reached 2.8%, but nine-month RIG is 1.9%; if Q4 and 2027 fall back toward 2025's 0.8% as pricing fades, organic growth drops toward the bottom of the 3% to 4% range and the multiple has little support.
02
Margin and commodities. The second-half margin outlook relies on lower coffee and cocoa costs and further savings, offset by higher transport and energy costs that Nestlé links to the Middle East conflict. Any of these moving the wrong way hits a margin already guided down from "strengthening" to "broadly similar".
03
Balance sheet and the dividend. CHF 56.3B of net debt (plus about CHF 0.9B from deconsolidating Russia by year-end), net debt / adjusted EBITDA of 2.85x and a payout of 88% of basic EPS depend on the disposals closing and free cash flow staying above CHF 9B. A delayed waters deal or weak cash generation would put the 66-year dividend record and deleveraging in conflict.
Industry Commentary
Coffee and cocoa: the input-cost squeeze
EASING HEADWIND

In 2025 Nestlé raised prices to cover higher coffee and cocoa costs — pricing contributed 2.8% of organic growth — and still lost 110 bps of gross margin. Confectionery and coffee were the largest contributors to organic growth, both on high single-digit pricing; in confectionery, the company said shoppers cut back more when prices rose. In 2026, pricing is easing as Nestlé laps those increases, and the H1 release expects lower coffee and cocoa costs to help the second half. For every large food company with chocolate or coffee exposure, the question is the same: how much volume comes back as prices stop rising.

The strong franc
STRUCTURAL

Nestlé reports in Swiss francs, and the franc strengthened significantly in 2025, according to the company: currency took 5.7% off 2025 sales, 6.2% off H1 2026 sales, and is expected to take about 3% off full-year 2026 sales at spot rates. In US dollar terms, H1 2026 sales rose to $54.8B from $51.2B (Nestlé's illustrative translation) even as they fell in francs. A Swiss-based investor sees the franc number; a dollar-based investor sees the stock price converted at USD/CHF, which on October 27, 2026 was 0.8276 francs per dollar. The third quarter was a break: currency added 1.5% to reported sales, though over nine months it still took off 3.7%.

Big food is shrinking to grow
PORTFOLIO RESET

Nestlé's 2026 strategy is to do less: four businesses, growth platforms covering 30% of sales with CHF 0.6B of extra investment, waters into a joint venture, mainstream vitamins sale agreed (September 2026, closing expected in the first half of 2027), ice cream up for sale, Blue Bottle Coffee sold and the rest of yfood bought. The company expects its categories to grow 3% to 4% a year and aims to beat that with 4%+ organic growth. The bull reading: a narrower Nestlé with more marketing behind fewer brands can grow volume again. The bear reading: disposals shrink the earnings base, and restructuring charges keep reported profit below underlying for years. The CEO's own framing on the H1 call: "Our strategy is clear. Now it is all about consistent execution, quarter after quarter, half after half." The October 22 sales report was the first test: RIG of 2.8%, positive in every zone. The full-year results on February 18, 2027 are the next.
