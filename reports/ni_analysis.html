01 Company Overview

NiSource is a regulated gas and electric utility holding company: roughly 3.3 million natural-gas customers and 500,000 electric customers across six states, run through two reporting segments — Columbia Operations (gas distribution in Ohio, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Kentucky and Maryland) and NIPSCO Operations (gas and electric in northern Indiana). The company was known as NIPSCO Industries until April 1999. TTM revenue is $6.96B, up 9.96%, and FY2025 GAAP EPS was $1.95 (adjusted $1.90). A regulated utility does not sell a product so much as rent out a rate base — $21.3B at year-end 2024 — at a return a state commission sets.

That is why the story of 2026 is not in the income statement. The plan management sells is aggressive for a utility, and on October 28 it got bigger: a $40.0B capital programme for 2027–2033 ($32.0B base plan, $8.0B for data-center generation), 9–10% consolidated investment-base growth and a target of 10–11% adjusted EPS growth a year from 2027 to 2033, up from 9–10%, powered by special contracts with Amazon, Alphabet and, since October 16, Microsoft in Indiana. The stock priced the old plan in fully — a $48.28 month-end close in April 2026 — and then Indiana changed the odds. In August the governor fired a utility commissioner, the commission held technical conferences on the formal investigations it had opened in July into allowed ROE and cost-recovery trackers (charges that let a utility recover certain spending between rate cases), it had already rejected NIPSCO's full infrastructure tracker request, and two research houses cut their view of Indiana as a regulatory jurisdiction. The stock fell to $39.00 at the end of September, its lowest month-end close since January 2025, then rose 14.7% in October to close October 29 at $44.75, still 7.3% below the April close. On the Q3 call CEO Lloyd Yates said NiSource is engaged in the commission’s broader affordability review, “including proceedings on authorized returns and tracker mechanisms.” The central question: is the larger growth plan worth paying for while the regulator that sets the return on half the rate base is reworking its rules?

NIPSCO Generation ("GenCo") is a separate affiliate built so that, in Utility Dive's words, "NIPSCO's existing customers don't pay for infrastructure needed to serve hyperscalers" (the largest cloud and AI data-center operators). The first contract, approved by the IURC on June 17, 2026, supplies Amazon with up to 3 GW (2.4 GW by end-2032) over 15 years, backed by about $7B of investment: two 1.3-GW gas-fired plants and a 400-MW, four-hour battery. An Alphabet contract (~340 MW) was approved in July 2026; management said on the Q3 call that the transmission to serve it was finished ahead of schedule and billing began this summer. A 400-MW Amazon increase was filed July 17 and still awaits an IURC order. On October 16 NiSource signed a third hyperscaler: Microsoft takes service for its northwest Indiana campus under a 20-year term from 2028 and fully funds the generation and transmission built for it; GenCo's pool of resources grows by about 1,100 MW, made up of roughly 500 MW of combustion-turbine plants and 600 MW of battery storage under a long-term tolling contract (GenCo pays for the use of a battery that an AGP Group affiliate builds). NIPSCO expects to file the contract with the IURC by year-end and seeks an order in the first half of 2027. Management now puts savings for existing customers at ~$1.75B over the contract terms, up from ~$1.4B, and CEO Lloyd Yates said on the Q3 call that 2 GW more is in negotiation with another 2 GW behind it; CFO Shawn Anderson said that pipeline is not in guidance. This is the piece that lifts consolidated EPS growth from the 6–8% base plan to 10–11%.

Risk shape: contracted, but concentrated — three counterparties, one state, and construction risk on large gas plants.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Franchise monopoly service territories $21.3B regulated rate base ~85% capital recovery within 12 months Six-state regulatory diversification 15- and 20-year hyperscaler contracts Ring-fenced GenCo structure

The moat is legal, not competitive: nobody else is allowed to lay a gas main to a house in Columbus or a wire to a data center in northern Indiana. That makes demand safe and returns negotiated. The flip side is that the moat has exactly one gatekeeper per state, and the gatekeeper is political. NiSource cannot lose customers to a rival; it can lose return to a commission. 2026 is the year the market remembered that the second risk is the one that matters.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Indiana regulatory reset. On August 3, 2026 Governor Mike Braun fired Commissioner Andy Zay; the IURC opened investigations into ROE under multi-year rate plans (Cause 46428) and into the use of trackers (Cause 46429) in July, with technical conferences on August 7. On August 10 Wolfe Research cut its rating of Indiana's political independence to "below average," writing that "political intervention in the regulatory process and volatility in the regulatory environment has meaningfully increased the risk and ROEs needed for investing in Indiana utilities," and Jefferies said "investor confidence in Indiana has materially deteriorated." Half of NiSource's rate base sits there.
02
Tracker denial. The IURC rejected NIPSCO's full infrastructure tracker request (reported as a $741M plan). Management says the order "doesn't preclude us from seeking recovery ... in future regulatory proceedings." Trackers are what keep regulatory lag short; losing one means spending now and waiting for a rate case to earn on it.
03
Capex outruns cash. TTM free cash flow is −$1.94B, debt is 5.87x EBITDA (a year's operating profit before interest, tax and depreciation), operating profit covers interest 2.24x, and shares outstanding are up 1.84% in a year (StockAnalysis). The plan depends on selling $400–600M of new shares a year in the near term (CFO Shawn Anderson, Q3 call) and on holding FFO-to-debt (cash from operations as a share of debt) at 14–16% in every year of the plan.
04
Labour and weather shocks. The April 2026 lockout of ~1,600 USW-represented NIPSCO employees and record tornado activity turned Q2 adjusted EPS to $0.16 from $0.22; the lockout ended when the clerical unit ratified its contract on May 1, 2026 (CBS Chicago). The storms kept coming: an August 11 derecho crossed northern Indiana with winds up to 110 mph, and Q3 O&M (operating and maintenance cost) rose on storm response, though Q3 adjusted EPS still rose to $0.27 from $0.19.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 29, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $24.67 to the October 29, 2026 settled close of $44.75, NI gained 81.4% on price — before dividends, which rose from $0.895 a share in FY2021 to $1.20 a year now. For a regulated utility that is a strong half-decade, and almost all of it came in two bursts: the 2024 run-up after the Blackstone deal closed and the data-center story took hold, and the run from late 2025 to April 2026 while the Amazon contract awaited the regulator's approval (granted June 17, 2026). May to September 2026 handed back the second burst, to $39.00; October to the 29th added 14.7%, the largest monthly gain in the window, on the Microsoft contract and the extended plan.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how unusual 2024–2026 was for a utility: it sat above 70 for most of mid-2024 through 2025, peaking at 93.3 in November 2024. It bottomed at 29.8 in September 2026, a shade below the 29.9 at the September 2023 low and the most oversold reading in the window, and October's rebound has lifted it to 54.4, mid-range. The 10-month moving average is $44.94, 0.4% above the price: the long-term trend has levelled off rather than turned back up.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 19.9x 2027 consensus EPS ($2.25, StockAnalysis) for a utility guiding to 10–11% adjusted EPS growth a year from 2027 to 2033 and 9–10% investment-base growth, with a 2.68% yield whose dividend rose 7.1% in the past year. The data-center growth is contracted for 15 to 20 years with three hyperscalers — Amazon, Alphabet and Microsoft — built through a ring-fenced affiliate that management expects to save existing customers ~$1.75B, and 2 GW more is in negotiation outside guidance. Half the rate base is in five non-Indiana gas jurisdictions with ~85% of capital recovered within 12 months. Even after October's rebound the stock is 7.3% below its April month-end close, and the first print after the Indiana scare raised the growth target rather than cutting it. If Indiana's investigations land at a low-9% ROE rather than something worse, the damage is a fraction of a percentage point of return on part of the base.

Bear Case

The plan only works if regulators let NiSource earn on $40B of spending from 2027 to 2033 quickly, and the regulator for half of the rate base has just been reshaped for "affordability." The commission rejected NIPSCO's full tracker, is re-examining ROEs and trackers, and a commissioner was fired. Free cash flow is −$1.94B, debt is 5.87x EBITDA, interest cover 2.24x, and the plan calls for $400–600M of new shares a year, so any slippage in allowed return or recovery speed goes straight to per-share growth and to equity needs. A 23.0x trailing multiple is a growth-utility multiple, the stock now trades above the lowest analyst target, and the growth still depends on Indianapolis.

Top 3 Risks

Indiana allowed-return and recovery reset. Causes 46428 and 46429 could lower the 9.75% electric ROE and slow recovery on 50.6% of the rate base.

Financing a negative-FCF plan. $4.32B of TTM capex against $2.38B of operating cash flow, debt at 5.87x EBITDA and a 14–16% FFO/debt band to defend. A credit-rating downgrade or a higher cost of equity squeezes the arithmetic behind the 10–11% growth target.
03
GenCo execution and concentration. Two 1.3-GW gas plants and a 400-MW battery to build on time and on budget for Amazon, plus about 1,100 MW of new resources (600 MW of it battery storage) for Microsoft, for three counterparties; the 400-MW Amazon amendment still needs an IURC order, and the Microsoft contract has yet to be filed.
Industry Commentary
Affordability politics vs. utility capex
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Indiana is a clean case study of a wider tension: utilities want to spend heavily on grids, gas pipe and generation for new load, while governors and commissions face customer bills. In July 2026 the IURC opened investigations into allowed ROEs and trackers as the state moves to multi-year, performance-based rates, and in August the governor fired a commissioner, pressing his case for regulators who put affordability first (Indiana Capital Chronicle). Wolfe Research said political intervention "has meaningfully increased the risk and ROEs needed for investing in Indiana utilities." For NiSource the transmission from politics to earnings is direct: allowed ROE times equity ratio times rate base.

Operating shocks: storms and labour
ONGOING HEADWIND

Record tornado activity in 2026 drove outage response costs that were not adjusted out of Q2, the August 11 derecho added more in Q3, and the April NIPSCO lockout added $21.4M of cost that was adjusted out. Neither changes the rate base; both show how thin a quarter's margin is when the allowed return is fixed and the costs are not.

Data-center load as a utility growth engine
EMERGING TAILWIND

The GenCo structure is NiSource's answer to the affordability problem: hyperscalers pay for the plants built for them, and existing customers get ~$1.75B of savings over the contract terms, with $57.5M of bill credits proposed from data-center revenue. The bull framing: that design is exactly what an affordability-focused commission should want, so the Amazon amendments, the Microsoft contract and future deals (2 GW in negotiation and 2 GW more behind it, per CEO Lloyd Yates) keep moving. The bear framing: the same commission that approved GenCo has just shown it will reopen returns and trackers, and a 15-year contract is only as stable as the regulatory regime around it. The pending order on the Amazon amendment and the Microsoft filing are the next tests.
