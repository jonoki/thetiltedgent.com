01 Company Overview

Nintendo started in Kyoto in 1889 making hanafuda playing cards, and it still sells them. Today it is a video-game platform company: it designs the console, makes most of the best-selling games for it, and earns a growing slice from licensing Mario, Zelda and the rest to film studios, theme parks and phone games. It reports as a single business segment. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (Nintendo calls it FY26) it booked ¥2,313.1 billion of sales, up 98.6%, on the June 2025 launch of the Nintendo Switch 2, and operating profit of ¥360.1 billion, up 27.5%. Profit attributable to shareholders rose 52.1% to ¥424.1 billion. It sold 19.86 million Switch 2 consoles in that first year; by September 30, 2026 the life-to-date total was 26.35 million, against 157.20 million for the original Switch.

The stock tells a different story from the sales line. It closed June 2025, the month the Switch 2 launched, at a record month-end ¥13,880; by June 2026 it was ¥6,815, down 50.9%, and it ended the banner date at ¥8,606. The problem is the second year. Nintendo's own forecast for FY27 (April 2026 – March 2027), left unchanged on November 5, is sales down 11.4% to ¥2,050 billion, Switch 2 hardware down 16.9% to 16.5 million units, and profit attributable to shareholders down 26.9% to ¥310 billion, with about ¥100 billion of extra cost from memory-chip prices and US tariffs built into cost of sales. The first half (April–September 2026) ran ahead of that plan on profit: sales fell 12.1% to ¥966.0 billion, but operating profit rose 49.1% to ¥216.5 billion, 58.5% of the full-year target, helped by a one-off refund of US tariffs (about $300 million, booked in the first quarter) and a weaker yen, which added about ¥22.2 billion to operating profit (Nintendo). The second quarter on its own was weaker: operating profit fell 16.3% to ¥73.9 billion, below the ¥88.1 billion analysts expected (Wccftech), and Switch 2 shipments fell 41.2% to 2.67 million. To protect margins Nintendo raised console prices: the Switch 2 went from ¥49,980 to ¥59,980 in Japan on May 25, 2026, and from $449.99 to $499.99 in the US on September 1; after the results Furukawa said no further increase is being considered (Reuters, as reported by KitGuru). Currency matters too: 78.9% of first-half sales came from outside Japan (the Americas alone 43.4%), so a weaker yen inflates reported sales and profit. The FY27 forecast assumes ¥150 per dollar; the first-half average was ¥159.29 (Nintendo) and the ECB rate on November 9, 2026 was ¥157.95.

The cash pile

Nintendo's balance sheet is unusual for a company its size. At September 30, 2026 it held ¥1,553.9 billion of cash and deposits plus ¥374.6 billion of short-term securities — ¥1,928.5 billion, about 19% of its market value — and has no borrowings: its liabilities are trade payables, provisions and taxes. Equity was 75.8% of total assets. Inventories rose to ¥814.6 billion from ¥539.8 billion at the end of March. Cash and securities also earn money: interest income was ¥46.1 billion in FY26 and ¥26.7 billion in the first half of FY27. Exchange-rate swings on its dollar and euro deposits added ¥44.3 billion in FY26 but cost ¥7.3 billion in the first half, when the yen ended September (¥157.09 per dollar) a little stronger than it ended March (¥159.31). That is why ordinary profit (¥542.2 billion in FY26, ¥300.7 billion in the first half) runs well above operating profit.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Mario, Zelda, Donkey Kong IP Hardware + software designed together 100M+ annual playing users Switch 1 library plays on Switch 2 Evergreen first-party catalogue ¥1.93T cash & securities, no borrowings

Nintendo's moat is characters nobody else can use, sold on hardware nobody else makes. Because it controls both the console and the best-selling games on it, it can design a system around a game idea instead of chasing raw processing power, and first-party titles keep selling for years — Mario Kart 8 Deluxe and Nintendo Switch Sports were still steady sellers in FY26. Players' Nintendo Accounts carry over from Switch to Switch 2, which, in Nintendo's account, is what made the transition smooth. The weakness is the flip side of the same model: profits rise and fall with one console cycle and a handful of in-house hits.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Memory chips. The FY27 plan absorbs about ¥100 billion from higher component prices, "particularly for memory", and tariffs. That is 27% of the year's ¥370 billion operating-profit target. After the first-half results Furukawa said Nintendo can secure enough memory to hold current prices (Reuters, as reported by KitGuru); Nintendo has not said how much it has secured or for how long. Trade press tied the December 2025 slide in the shares to rising RAM costs for the Switch 2.
02
Price rises meet a second-year console. Switch 2 prices went up 20% in Japan (May 2026) and 11% in the US (September 2026) within 15 months of launch, while Nintendo itself forecasts fewer units. Second-quarter Switch 2 shipments, a quarter that included one month at the higher US and European prices, fell 41.2%. Furukawa told analysts on May 8 that the increase "raises the barrier for entry to a certain extent for people deciding whether to make a purchase". If demand proves price-sensitive, the hardware forecast is at risk.
03
Hit dependence. First-party titles were 76.6% of first-half software sales. Results ride on Nintendo's own release calendar: the holiday quarter's lead title is a remake (Ocarina of Time, November 5), followed by a Switch 2 edition of Pikmin 4 (November 12); Metroid Ravenous comes on January 28, and the next new mainline Pokémon games are 2027 releases.
04
Yen and tariffs. About 79% of first-half sales were outside Japan. The first half had a ¥63.1 billion currency tailwind on sales (about ¥22.2 billion on operating profit) and a $300 million one-off tariff refund; neither can be counted on to repeat. US tariff policy remains an input to the cost forecast. A proposed class action filed for two US buyers by the law firms Emery Reddy and Milberg in federal court in Seattle on April 21, 2026 alleges that Nintendo passed the tariffs on to US buyers and seeks to make it reimburse them; on July 20 Nintendo of America moved to dismiss, arguing the buyers have no legal entitlement to the refunds (Hoffert v. Nintendo of America, W.D. Wash. court filings; Game Developer).

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the trailing figures. First, the US tariff refund: about $300 million of IEEPA tariffs, previously expensed, was booked as a reduction in first-quarter FY27 cost of sales; Nintendo says the tariffs had mostly been absorbed rather than passed on in prices. Second, income booked below operating profit: the trailing twelve months include about ¥18.6 billion of net exchange-rate gains (¥44.3 billion in FY26, of which ¥18.5 billion came in its first half, less a ¥7.3 billion loss in the first half of FY27; TTG arithmetic) — driven by the yen, not by games. FY26's ¥32.7 billion gain on selling investment securities came almost entirely in its first quarter, so it is mostly outside the trailing window. Third, the launch-year base: the Switch 2 launched in June 2025, so year-on-year comparisons through 2026 are against unusually large hardware quarters.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 9, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of ¥5,010 (split-adjusted; ¥50,100 as actually traded) to the November 9, 2026 settled close of ¥8,606, Nintendo gained 71.8% on price over five years, before dividends. The path was two different stocks. For three and a half years it climbed, from that ¥5,010 low in November 2021 to a ¥13,880 high in June 2025, the month the Switch 2 launched — up 177.0%. Then it gave half of it back: down 50.9% to ¥6,815 in June 2026, with the biggest monthly fall a 20.2% drop in December 2025. July and August 2026 recovered 32.7%; September took back 12.2%, October added 4.3%, and the stock closed November 9 at ¥8,606, 3.8% above the October month-end.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The stock has lost more than a third of its value since mid-2025 while the business has grown. At ¥8,606 Nintendo trades on 22.34 times trailing earnings — a little above the Prime Market average — with ¥1.93 trillion of cash and securities, no debt, and a 60% payout policy. The Switch 2 had shipped 26.35 million units by September 30, 2026, the Switch 1 library plays on it, and software, where the margin is, is gaining share: first-half operating profit rose 49.1% and already covers 58.5% of the full-year target. The FY27 forecast assumes ¥150 per dollar against ¥157.95 on the banner date, the analyst consensus for revenue and EPS already sits above guidance, and Furukawa says Nintendo can secure enough memory to hold prices. Add a $1 billion Mario film, a Zelda film and new mainline Pokémon games in 2027, and the pipeline beyond the holiday quarter is full.

Bear Case

Nintendo itself is guiding to lower sales, fewer consoles and 26.9% less profit this year, which puts the forward P/E on guidance at 32.00. The second quarter showed the strain: operating profit fell 16.3%, below analysts' estimates, and Switch 2 shipments fell 41.2%, leaving 10.01 million units to ship in the second half to hit the target. Memory prices are an industry-wide cost Nintendo cannot design away, and it has already raised Switch 2 prices in Japan, the US, Canada and Europe. The strongest first-half numbers leaned on a one-off tariff refund and a weak yen, and the holiday slate is led by a remake. UBS has a Sell and a ¥5,500 target; the range of targets runs from ¥5,000 to ¥21,260.

Top 3 Risks

01
Component costs keep rising. The ¥100 billion memory-and-tariff cost in the FY27 plan was set in May and kept in November. If memory prices rise further, Nintendo can absorb it (margins fall) or raise prices again (units fall); for now Furukawa says no further price rise is being considered.
02
The second-year console slump deepens. Switch 2 shipments were down 37.4% in the first half and 41.2% in the second quarter. The full-year plan is down 16.9% and needs 10.01 million units from October to March, more than the 9.50 million shipped in those months a year earlier. A weak holiday quarter after September's price rises would put the 16.5 million forecast — and the software sales that ride on it — at risk.
03
A stronger yen. 78.9% of first-half sales were outside Japan, and Nintendo holds large dollar and euro deposits whose revaluation runs through ordinary profit; a slightly firmer yen at September 30 than at March 31 already meant a ¥7.3 billion exchange loss in the first half. A move from ¥157.95 back toward the ¥150 planning rate would remove the cushion in the forecast.
Industry Commentary
The memory squeeze on game consoles
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Nintendo's FY27 plan names "rising component prices, particularly for memory" as part of a roughly ¥100 billion cost hit. Trade press reports the same squeeze on PC, phone and laptop makers as memory capacity shifts to AI data centers (Outlook Respawn, December 2025, citing TrendForce). For a console maker the choice is between eating the cost, raising prices, or both; Nintendo has done both. The first full test of whether consumers accept a $499.99 Switch 2 is the holiday quarter; the July–September quarter, with one month at the new US and European prices, saw Switch 2 shipments fall 41.2%.

Year two of a console cycle
ONGOING SWING FACTOR

Nintendo describes Switch 2 sales as more concentrated in the launch year than its previous systems. The investment case rests on what happens next: the installed base turns into software sales, and software carries the margin. The first half of FY27 showed the mechanism working — hardware down to 54.5% of platform sales, gross margin up to 50.4% — but it needs enough new consoles sold to keep feeding it, and second-quarter shipments fell 41.2%. Management is not worried yet: on May 8 Furukawa called the pace of adoption "extremely fast even when compared to Nintendo Switch" and said "we do not see any particular concerns about its momentum at this time"; Nintendo's August 6 presentation notes say second-year sales "compare favorably to the adoption trajectory of Nintendo Switch".

Characters beyond the console
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull framing: two Mario films have each passed $1 billion at the box office, and a live-action Zelda film is dated for 2027 — Nintendo's characters now reach audiences who have never bought its hardware, and IP income rose 87.9% in the first half of FY27. The bear framing: at ¥73.5 billion in FY26, IP income was 3.2% of sales and fell 9.7% in a year without a film. It smooths the cycle; it does not replace it.
