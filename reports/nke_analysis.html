01 Company Overview

NIKE, Inc. designs, develops and markets athletic footwear, apparel, equipment and accessories worldwide under the NIKE, Jordan and Converse brands, selling through wholesale partners and its own NIKE Direct channel (owned stores plus Nike.com and the Nike app). It remains the largest athletic footwear company on earth — independent market research puts the global athletic footwear market at roughly $150–153 billion in 2026, of which the top five players (NIKE, adidas, PUMA, New Balance, Under Armour) hold approximately 43% combined, with NIKE the clear leader.

Fiscal 2026 (ended May 31, 2026) revenue was $46.40 billion, essentially flat as reported (+0.2%) and down 2% currency-neutral — a second straight year without growth after FY2025's −9.8% collapse. Net income was $3.11 billion and diluted EPS $2.10, both down about 3%. The first quarter of fiscal 2027 (to August 31, 2026, reported October 1) was weaker at the top line: revenue $11.21 billion, down 4% (−5% currency-neutral), Greater China down 26% currency-neutral, and a full-year outlook for a high-single-digit revenue decline. With it, NIKE announced Pace, an operating-model overhaul that regroups the business into three geographies and cuts roles. At $33.96 the stock sits roughly 80% below its November 2021 month-end peak of $169.24 and is down 52.8% over the past year, against a 15.8% rise for the S&P 500 (SPY, price return, Oct 3, 2025 to Oct 5, 2026). NIKE ranks #228 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Swoosh Brand Equity Jordan Franchise Athlete & League Endorsements Global Wholesale Network Sourcing Scale Air / Flyknit / Aero-FIT IP Membership Data

NIKE's moat has always been demand-side rather than cost-side: a brand that lets it charge premium prices for commodity-manufactured goods, reinforced by the deepest athlete and federation endorsement portfolio in sport and by the Jordan sub-brand — arguably the most valuable single franchise in consumer products. That moat is intact but visibly narrower than it was in 2021. The evidence is in the numbers: gross margin of 42.9% in FY2026 versus 46.0% in FY2022, a ~310 basis point erosion, and ROE of 21.6% (trailing twelve months) against peers at 24–43%. A brand with unimpaired pricing power does not need to liquidate inventory for two consecutive years. What remains genuinely defensible is scale in wholesale distribution — no challenger can service Foot Locker, Dick's, JD Sports and 40+ country distributor networks simultaneously — and an R&D budget that challengers with $4–5 billion of revenue cannot match. The bear counter is that in running, the category that now drives the market, product superiority no longer requires NIKE's scale.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Greater China structural share loss. China revenue fell 11% currency-neutral in FY2026, 17% in Q4 and 26% in Q1 FY2027 — three steps down, not a bottom — and management's FY2027 guidance assumes it gets worse. This is not purely macro: Anta gained five points of sportswear share (14%→19%) and Li-Ning three (6%→9%) between 2019 and 2023 while NIKE's footwear share drifted from 25% to 24% and adidas's halved. National-brand preference among younger Chinese consumers is a durable, non-cyclical headwind that no amount of Win Now execution in Beaverton addresses.

2. Challenger brands in performance running. On grew revenue 18.5% and quadrupled net income; HOKA-driven Deckers grew 7.9% with a 57.8% gross margin — 14 points above NIKE's. Both are winning in the highest-margin, highest-growth part of the market with narrow assortments and specialty-retail credibility. NIKE's running growth (20%+ in Q3 FY2026, double digits again in Q1 FY2027, with management claiming share gains) is off a depressed base and does not yet demonstrate a broad recapture; it demonstrates that the category was under-served internally for years.

3. Sourcing concentration and tariff whiplash. Roughly 50% of NIKE footwear is made in Vietnam and 27% in Indonesia — about 75% of unit volume in two countries. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court decision striking down the IEEPA tariffs (which had hit 46% on Vietnam and 32% on Indonesia) produced a $986 million recovery, but replacement Section 122 tariffs at 15% took effect four days later and Section 301/232 duties are untouched; the Yale Budget Lab pegs the residual effective US tariff increase at 6.7 percentage points versus pre-2025. Structural exposure is unchanged. Refunds also require individually filed customs claims and "will not come automatically."

4. Channel transition execution risk. Rebuilding wholesale means handing margin and control back to retailers NIKE spent five years disintermediating. Wholesale grew 6% in FY2026 but slipped 1% in Q1 FY2027, while NIKE Direct fell 8% and digital 13% in the quarter, so the company is trading high-margin owned revenue for lower-margin partner revenue and hoping volume recovery more than compensates. On the Q3 call management conceded sell-through trends "remain below targets" even as order books improved — order growth without sell-through is future inventory risk.

Notable One-Off Items

The FY2026 numbers are materially distorted and must be adjusted before use.

1. IEEPA tariff recovery — $986 million, Q4 FY2026. On February 20, 2026 the US Supreme Court ruled 6–3 in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump that the executive branch exceeded its authority under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, vacating tariffs that had reached 46% on Vietnamese and 32% on Indonesian goods. NIKE recognised a $986 million expected recovery in the fourth quarter, worth approximately 900 basis points of Q4 gross margin and $0.52 of the quarter's $0.72 diluted EPS. Reported Q4 gross margin of 49.2% (+890bps) therefore corresponds to an underlying rate near 40%, roughly in line with Q3 FY2026's 40.2%. Q4 net income "up 407%" is almost entirely this item. Strip it and FY2026 EPS is approximately $1.58. NIKE's FY2027 guidance of $1.15–$1.35 adjusted EPS sits 15–27% below that underlying base. Recovery timing is uncertain: refunds require individually filed customs claims and are not automatic.

2. Deliberate inventory liquidation. Management quantified the intentional removal of unhealthy classic-franchise inventory as roughly a five-point headwind to reported revenue in Q3 FY2026. This depresses revenue and gross margin but is a management choice, not demand destruction — the offsetting question is whether the cleaned marketplace generates full-price sell-through afterward.

3. Currency. FY2026 revenue was flat reported but −2% currency-neutral, i.e. FX added roughly 2 points. The distortion is largest in EMEA (+3% reported vs −3% currency-neutral, ~600bps) and Greater China (Q4 −12% reported vs −17% currency-neutral). Every reported growth figure in FY2026 flatters the underlying business.

4. Restructuring — FY2026 severance and the new Pace charges. A March 2026 workforce plan left $243 million of severance accrued at May 31, 2026, substantially paid in Q1 FY2027 (10-Q); CFO Dave Denton put FY2026 severance-related costs at about $300 million. Pace adds pre-tax charges of about $1.0 billion, mainly severance and other employee costs, about $0.3 billion of them in fiscal 2027; none were booked in Q1 because the program was announced in October. The FY2027 EPS guidance of $1.15–$1.35 is on an adjusted basis that excludes these charges, so reported (GAAP) FY2027 EPS will come in lower.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The brand is not broken — the distribution model was, and the repair shows where NIKE has put its money: the performance portfolio grew high single digits in Q1 FY2027, running grew double digits again, North America grew 2% and gross margin rose 60bps to 42.8%. The revenue NIKE is losing in Dunk, Jordan retro and Chinese distribution is partly being shed on purpose, and Pace targets about $2.5B of cumulative savings through fiscal 2031. At $33.96 with a 4.8% yield, a 24-year dividend-growth streak and $8.4B of cash and short-term investments, an investor is paid to wait — and the CFO says the dividend is supported "under all scenarios." Bernstein still rates the stock Outperform at $45, about 33% above spot, and at roughly 1.1x trailing sales ($50.4B market cap on $45.9B revenue) a company with 22% ROE is priced for permanent decline. The November 16–17 Investor Day is the natural re-rating event.

Bear Case

Two years into the turnaround the top line is still shrinking, and management has just said it will shrink faster: currency-neutral revenue fell 2% in FY2026, 4% in Q4 and 5% in Q1 FY2027, and FY2027 is guided down high single digits. Greater China went from −10% in Q3 FY2026 to −17% in Q4 to −26% in Q1, and the guidance assumes it gets worse. Adjusted FY2027 EPS of $1.15–$1.35 compares with an underlying ~$1.58 in FY2026 once the $0.52 tariff recovery is stripped out, before about $0.3B of Pace charges this year. Trailing free cash flow is $2.11B and FCF per share of $1.42 doesn't cover the $1.64 dividend; Q1 operating cash flow was $135M against $610M of dividends paid. At about 27x the guidance midpoint the stock is dearer on forward earnings than on trailing (16.2x) — paying up for shrinking earnings while On and Deckers take the profitable end of the market at 58–65% gross margins.

Top 3 Risks

01
Greater China does not stabilise. The Q1 FY2027 currency-neutral decline of 26% was worse than any quarter of FY2026, management's guidance assumes further deterioration this year, and Chinese consumers are structurally rotating to Anta and Li-Ning. If China revenue halves again from $5.8B, roughly $3B of NIKE's highest-margin revenue disappears — enough on its own to push consolidated gross margin below 42% and make the current dividend indefensible from cash flow.
02
Gross margin recovery is structurally capped, not cyclically delayed. Underlying Q4 FY2026 gross margin ex-tariff-recovery was near 40% and Q1 FY2027 came in at 42.8%, versus 44.7% in FY2024 and 46.0% in FY2022; on the call the CFO said the discounts NIKE is taking to reset the marketplace dampen gross margin over time. The channel mix is moving toward lower-margin wholesale by design, and residual tariffs still add roughly 6.7 points to the effective US import rate. If 43% is the new ceiling rather than a waypoint back to 46%, the earnings power the bull case assumes does not exist.
03
The dividend is not covered by free cash flow. FCF per share of $1.42 against a $1.64 annual dividend and a 78.5% payout ratio means the 24-year growth streak is being funded from the balance sheet, and Pace will cost about $1.0B in charges, mostly severance and other employee costs. NIKE has $8.4B of cash and short-term investments and management calls the dividend a significant priority, so it can sustain this for years, but a token increase — or worse, a cut — would remove the single largest reason non-believers still hold the stock, and would likely trigger forced selling from dividend-growth mandates.
  Industry Commentary
Chinese Consumer Demand and Local Brand Preference
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
China's sportswear market is roughly $61 billion (2023), about 15% of global demand, with athletic footwear alone at ~$15.2B in 2026E. The problem is not market size — it is who is winning it. Between 2019 and 2023, Anta's sportswear share rose from 14% to 19% and Li-Ning's from 6% to 9%, while adidas's footwear share collapsed from 19% to 10% and NIKE's slipped from 25% to 24%. NIKE's FY2026 China revenue of $5.8B fell 11% currency-neutral, with Q4 down 17% and Q1 FY2027 down 26% — an accelerating decline, not a bottoming one. Anta has also built a Western-brand portfolio (Fila China, Descente, Amer Sports) that competes with NIKE at the premium end using domestic distribution. Meanwhile Lululemon quadrupled its China share since 2019 and On holds under 1%, so the share NIKE is losing is going overwhelmingly to national champions. This is the one headwind on this page with no obvious management fix.
Tariffs on Asian Footwear Sourcing
ONGOING HEADWIND
NIKE sources roughly 50% of footwear from Vietnam and 27% from Indonesia — about 75% of volume in two countries. The February 20, 2026 Supreme Court ruling in Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump vacated IEEPA tariffs that had reached 46% on Vietnam, 49% on Cambodia and 32% on Indonesia, producing a $986M recovery for NIKE. But Section 122 replacement tariffs at 15% took effect four days later on February 24, and Section 232 and Section 301 China duties were untouched. The Yale Budget Lab estimates the effective US tariff rate still runs 6.7 percentage points above pre-2025. Refunds require individually filed customs claims — over 1,000 corporate entities have started the process, and the National Retail Federation has had to lobby for a workable refund mechanism. Practically: the accounting benefit is booked, the cash is uncertain, the structural cost is permanent, and the supply-chain diversification and price increases taken during the high-tariff period remain in place. Sportswear stocks surged then slumped on the ruling — the market has already worked this out.
The Athletic Footwear Landscape — Scale vs. Challengers
STRUCTURAL SHIFT — CUTS BOTH WAYS
The global athletic footwear market is $150–153B in 2026 growing 3.9–5.5% annually toward $212–235B by 2034/35, with running shoes alone over 37% of the market and the top five players holding ~43% combined. The decade's defining change is that scale stopped being a prerequisite for product superiority: On Holding built a $4.0B revenue business at a 64.8% gross margin with 18.5% growth, and HOKA carried Deckers to $5.5B at 57.8% — both roughly 15–22 points above NIKE's FY2026 42.9%. They win in specialty running, the exact category NIKE ceded while chasing lifestyle classics on Nike.com.

Bull framing: combined, On and Deckers are under $10B of revenue against NIKE's $46.4B; NIKE's Q3 running growth of 20%+ and sold-out Nike Mind launch show that when Beaverton actually points its R&D and endorsement machine at a category, it still moves the market — and no challenger can service global wholesale at NIKE's scale. Bear framing: those challengers are compounding at 8–19% with double NIKE's margin structure and roughly a quarter of its capital intensity (Deckers ROIC 90.6% vs NIKE 18.9%), and they are taking the premium consumer first. The market has already voted: NIKE trades at about 27x the midpoint of its own FY2027 adjusted EPS guidance — earnings the company expects to fall. Being the largest player in a growing market is worth very little if you are the only one not growing.
