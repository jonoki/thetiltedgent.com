01 Company Overview

NRG Energy is two businesses bolted together and sold as a hedge: a retail energy and home-services franchise selling electricity, natural gas and smart-home security to roughly eight million customers across North America (brands include Reliant, Direct Energy, Green Mountain Energy and Vivint), and a generation fleet of roughly 25 GW that supplies part of that load. It is classed as a utility, but it is not a regulated one: there is no rate base and no allowed ROE. NRG earns a spread between what it charges customers and what power costs it — to produce or to buy — and it keeps or loses that spread depending on weather, fuel and wholesale prices. FY2025 revenue was $30.71B, up 9.18%; trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 30, 2026 is $33.13B.

The story of the last eighteen months is a single enormous bet. In May 2025 NRG agreed to buy 13 GW of gas-fired plants and the CPower demand-response platform from LS Power for about $12B of enterprise value — $6.4B cash, $2.8B in stock (about 11% of NRG to LS Power), $3.2B of assumed net debt. The market loved it: the stock went from a $109.58 close in April 2025 to $155.90 in May, and peaked at a $178.96 month-end in February 2026, just after the deal closed on January 30, 2026 and doubled the fleet. Then the bill arrived. Cash fell from $4.71B to $0.16B, total debt rose from $16.63B to $23.47B, and the first two quarters as a bigger company both printed adjusted EPS of $1.49 — below consensus each time, as the interest and depreciation on the new plants landed before the earnings power did, and a mild Texas winter hurt the retail book. The stock closed the September 21, 2026 session at $103.28, 42.3% below the February month-end and 1.9% above its 52-week low. The central debate: is this a levered company that overpaid at the top of the power-scarcity trade, or a company whose guided $5.3–5.8B of 2026 adjusted EBITDA is now priced at roughly 8x?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

~8M retail customers ~25 GW owned generation Hard-to-replicate gas fleet CPower demand response Vivint recurring revenue Texas Energy Fund 3% financing Brand portfolio (Reliant et al.)

The moat is the match between customers and megawatts. A pure generator is exposed to wholesale price collapses; a pure retailer is exposed to price spikes. Owning both lets NRG net the two, and the LS Power deal pushed the balance toward owned supply — natural gas went from 48% to 76% of the fleet mix, per the Q2 slide summary. Existing gas plants in constrained grids are hard to replicate: new builds cost real money (NRG's own data-center plant is budgeted at about $2,700/kW) and take years. The weakness is that the moat protects volume, not margin. Weather, gas prices and wholesale curves still set the spread, and in 2026 they moved the wrong way in Texas.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage after the deal. Total debt is $23.47B against $4.86B of book equity (debt/equity 4.83), interest coverage is 2.03x, and the current ratio is 0.97. Management pushed its three-times net-debt-to-EBITDA target from 2028 to 2029. Leverage magnifies every other risk on this list.
02
Retail margins at the mercy of weather. A roughly 30% drop in Texas heating degree days was enough to take $214M out of first-half Texas EBITDA. Around-the-clock ERCOT power averaged about $33/MWh in Q2 against a $52/MWh 2026 planning assumption (Q2 slide summary) — a reminder that the Texas scarcity story does not show up every season.
03
Accretion that has not arrived yet. The LS Power deal was sold as highly accretive. Two quarters in, adjusted EPS is $2.98 for the half versus $4.42 a year earlier, with extra shares, interest and depreciation arriving before the full earnings benefit. Guidance of $7.90–9.90 implies about $4.92–6.92 in the second half.
04
GAAP cash flow versus the adjusted story. Trailing GAAP free cash flow is only $348M (operating cash flow $1.56B less $1.21B capex), versus a 2026 FCF-before-growth guide of $2.8–3.3B. The gap is definitional (growth capex, working capital, collateral), but it is large, and investors need to trust the company's own measure to believe the cheap-valuation case.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported numbers. First, derivative marks: NRG hedges its customer load with contracts that are marked to market each quarter while the customer contracts are not, so GAAP net income swings — Q2 2026 GAAP net income of $506M versus a $104M loss a year earlier, while adjusted net income actually fell to $315M from $339M. Second, acquisition timing: the LS Power plants were owned for five of the first six months of 2026, so 2026 is a partial year for the new earnings but a full year for much of the financing. Third, share count: buybacks cut shares 1.94% year on year, but the LS Power stock consideration raised the count 1.92% quarter on quarter — per-share comparisons across the January 30 close are not like-for-like.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 close of $40.83 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $103.28, NRG gained 153.0% on price, before dividends. That number hides two very different halves. From 2021 to late 2023 the stock went nowhere, bottoming at $31.82 in December 2022. Then, from early 2024, the market decided power was scarce — data centers, electrification, tight grids — and NRG went from $51.70 at the end of 2023 to a $178.96 month-end in February 2026, a 3.5x run. It has since given back 42.3%, and is down 35.1% since the end of 2025.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how quickly the mood flipped. It spent most of 2024 above 80 — peaking at 96.3 in May 2024 — and now reads 29.3, the first sub-30 reading in the five-year series and lower than the December 2022 trough of 34.7. The 10-month moving average is about $142.04, 37.5% above the price. By momentum, this is the most washed-out NRG has been in five years; whether that is a buying signal or just a description of a de-rating depends on the Q3 print.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $103.28 you pay about 11.7x 2026 consensus adjusted EPS and 9.2x 2027's $11.19 (calc), and roughly 8x the guided adjusted EBITDA midpoint, for a company that just doubled its generation fleet with modern gas plants that cannot be quickly replicated. The East segment's EBITDA has already gone from $99M to $469M a quarter. Vivint adds growing, weather-proof subscription cash. The data-center plant, if signed, adds ~$500M of largely capacity-backed EBITDA from late 2029. Guidance has been reaffirmed twice, and the 2026 FCF-before-growth guide of $2.8–3.3B is about 14% of the market cap. If the second half lands inside guidance, today's price looks like a weather problem being priced as a solvency problem.

Bear Case

NRG bought 13 GW near the top of the power-scarcity trade and paid with cash, stock and debt. Net debt has doubled to $23.3B, interest coverage is 2.03x, the current ratio is below 1, and the deleveraging target just slipped a year — while $1.4B a year still goes out in buybacks and dividends. Adjusted EPS has missed twice as interest and depreciation arrive ahead of the earnings. GAAP free cash flow is $348M against the company's multi-billion adjusted measure. Q2 ERCOT power averaged about $33/MWh against a $52 planning assumption. The data-center plant is a 2029 story. And the whole IPP group is de-rating, so there is no sector wind to help.

Top 3 Risks

01
Balance-sheet leverage. $23.47B of debt, 4.83 debt-to-equity, 2.03x interest coverage and a three-times target now dated 2029. A bad weather year, a gas-price spike against the retail book, or a credit-rating action would hit equity holders first.
02
Guidance credibility. Two quarters of $1.49 adjusted EPS leave about $4.92–6.92 needed in the second half to land in the $7.90–9.90 range. A cut at Q3 would reset both the earnings base and the multiple.
03
Execution on growth capital. The data-center plant is still in final documentation, and three Texas builds plus the LS Power integration are running at the same time. Late or over-budget delivery on any of them lands on a balance sheet with little room.
Industry Commentary
The power-scarcity trade, reversed
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

From 2024 into early 2026 the market paid up for any company owning dispatchable power in constrained U.S. grids, on the idea that data centers would absorb every spare megawatt. In 2026 that trade unwound across the group: StockAnalysis's year-on-year market-cap figures show Vistra −33.1%, Talen −22.9%, Constellation −7.5% and NRG −31.8%. For NRG the reversal hit just as it had doubled its exposure. Management's answer is structural rather than cyclical — CEO Gaudette on the Q2 call: "For the largest new loads, new demand should be matched with new generation, with the customer supporting the investment." Building new plants for signed customers is a lower-risk way to monetise demand than hoping existing plants reprice. It is also slower.

Texas: weather, prices and policy
ONGOING HEADWIND

ERCOT is an energy-only market with no capacity payments, so a generator-retailer earns its money in a few hot or cold weeks. In 2026 it got a mild winter (heating degree days down about 30%), higher supply costs and soft Q2 wholesale prices. The state is pushing new dispatchable supply through the Texas Energy Fund, which lends at 3% and pays completion bonuses — useful for NRG's 1.5 GW build programme, but it also adds supply to the market NRG sells into. Management says its policy thesis is intact: "The environment has changed. Our strategy has not."

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The same company trades at 26.7x trailing GAAP earnings and about 11.7x consensus adjusted earnings; at 13.8x reported EBITDA and about 8x guided adjusted EBITDA. The bull framing: GAAP is distorted by hedge marks and a part-year of acquired earnings, the adjusted numbers are the real ones, and the sell-side's $188.56 average target — with not one Sell among 16 analysts — says the price has overshot. The bear framing: "adjusted" is doing too much work when guidance has been missed twice, GAAP free cash flow is $348M, and the leverage target keeps moving. The way to settle it is not the multiple. It is Q3 adjusted EBITDA against the $5.3–5.8B range, and whether net debt starts falling.
