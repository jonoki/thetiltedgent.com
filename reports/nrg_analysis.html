01 Company Overview

NRG Energy is two businesses bolted together and sold as a hedge: a retail energy and home-services franchise selling electricity, natural gas and smart-home security to roughly eight million customers across North America (brands include Reliant, Direct Energy, Green Mountain Energy and Vivint), and a generation fleet of roughly 25 GW (about 20 GW once a 4.9 GW sale agreed in November closes) that supplies part of that load. It is classed as a utility, but it is not a regulated one: there is no rate base and no allowed ROE. NRG earns a spread between what it charges customers and what power costs it — to produce or to buy — and it keeps or loses that spread depending on weather, fuel and wholesale prices. FY2025 revenue was $30.71B, up 9.2%; trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 30, 2026 is $34.96B (calc from the FY2025 and Q3 2026 releases).

The story of the last eighteen months is a single enormous bet, and the first quarter in which it paid. In May 2025 NRG agreed to buy 13 GW of gas-fired plants and the CPower demand-response platform from LS Power for about $12B of enterprise value — $6.4B cash, $2.8B in stock (about 11% of NRG to LS Power), $3.2B of assumed debt. The market loved it: the stock went from a $109.58 close in April 2025 to $155.90 in May, and peaked at a $178.96 month-end in February 2026, just after the deal closed on January 30, 2026 and doubled the fleet. Then the bill arrived. Cash fell from $4.71B at the end of 2025 to $0.15B at September 30, 2026, long-term debt and finance leases rose from $16.44B to $22.92B, and the first two quarters as a bigger company both printed adjusted EPS of $1.49 — below consensus each time, as a mild Texas winter hurt the retail book and interest expense rose with the new debt. On November 4, the first session after Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor's race on a call to pause new data-center development, the stock fell 13.5%, and it touched a 52-week low of $79.65 the next day. Then the third quarter delivered: on November 9 NRG reported adjusted EPS of $4.68 against a $3.44 Nasdaq consensus, adjusted EBITDA up 67% to $2.01B, reaffirmed 2026 guidance, set 2027 guidance, and agreed to sell six gas plants (4.9 GW) to an LS Power affiliate for $4B in cash to fund more buybacks and debt reduction. The stock rose 10.3% that session and closed November 13 at $97.10, 45.7% below the February month-end. The central debate: is this an indebted company that overpaid at the top of the power-scarcity trade, or a company whose guided $5.3–5.8B of 2026 adjusted EBITDA is now priced at under 8 times?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

~8M retail customers ~25 GW owned generation Hard-to-replicate gas fleet CPower demand response Vivint recurring revenue Texas Energy Fund 3% financing Brand portfolio (Reliant et al.)

The moat is the match between customers and megawatts. A pure generator is exposed to wholesale price collapses; a pure retailer is exposed to price spikes. Owning both lets NRG net the two, and the LS Power deal pushed the balance toward owned supply, roughly doubling the fleet to about 25 GW. Existing gas plants in constrained grids are hard to replicate: new builds cost real money (NRG's own data-center plant is budgeted at $3.2B for 1.2 GW, about $2,700/kW) and take years. The weakness is that the moat protects volume, not margin. Weather, gas prices and wholesale prices still set the spread: in the first half of 2026 they moved the wrong way in Texas, and in the summer quarter they moved back.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Debt after the deal. Long-term debt and finance leases are $22.92B against $5.27B of book equity at September 30, 2026 (debt/equity 4.35); operating income covered interest 2.7 times in the first nine months, and the current ratio is 0.99. Management pushed its target of net debt at three times EBITDA from 2028 to 2029 on the Q2 call; the $2B of debt reduction tied to the asset sale is meant to keep leverage where it is while the rest of the plan funds buybacks. Debt magnifies every other risk on this list.
02
Retail margins at the mercy of weather. A roughly 30% drop in Texas heating degree days took $214M out of first-half Texas EBITDA, and a hot summer added $211M back in Q3. Around-the-clock power in ERCOT's Houston zone averaged $33/MWh in Q2 against a $52/MWh 2026 planning assumption (CFO Bruce Chung on the Q2 call) — a reminder that the Texas scarcity story does not show up every season.
03
Per-share gains that arrived late. After two misses, nine-month adjusted EPS is $7.68 versus $7.17 a year earlier (+7.1%), while adjusted EBITDA rose 33%: diluted shares rose to 210M from 195M (Q3) and nine-month interest expense to $897M from $498M. Guidance of $7.90–9.90 implies $0.22–2.22 for the fourth quarter, a wide range for the quarter that completes the deal's first year.
04
GAAP cash flow versus the adjusted story. Trailing GAAP free cash flow is $689M (calc: operating cash flow $2.17B less $1.48B capital spending over the twelve months to September 30, 2026), versus a 2026 FCF-before-growth guide of $2.8–3.3B. The gap is definitional (growth capex, working capital, collateral), but it is large, and investors need to trust the company's own measure to believe the cheap-valuation case.
05
Texas politics. Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who takes office as governor on January 19, has called for a moratorium on new data-center development until the legislature passes protections for household ratepayers (Heatmap, November 5, 2026); Governor Greg Abbott had already ordered a project-by-project review of new data-center grid connections in August (Utility Dive). NRG's Texas growth plans, including its 1.2 GW data-center plant, lean on that demand. The stock fell 13.5% in the first session after the vote.

Notable One-Off Items

Four things distort the reported numbers. First, derivative marks: NRG hedges its customer load with contracts that are marked to market each quarter while the customer contracts are not, so GAAP net income swings — Q3 2026 GAAP net income was $561M against adjusted net income of $983M. Second, acquisition timing: the LS Power plants were owned for eight of the first nine months of 2026, so 2026 is a partial year for the new earnings but a full year for much of the financing. Third, share count: the LS Power stock consideration lifted Q3 diluted shares to 210M from 195M a year earlier, while $977M of buybacks through October 31 pulled the other way — per-share comparisons across the January 30 close are not like-for-like. Fourth, the asset sale: 2027 guidance excludes the 4.9 GW sale, so it will change once the sale closes, expected in Q2 2027.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 13, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the November 2021 month-end close of $36.02 to the November 13, 2026 settled close of $97.10, NRG gained 169.6% on price, before dividends; over the same dates the SPY S&P 500 fund gained 65.6% and the Nasdaq-100's QQQ 72.6%. That number hides two very different halves. From 2021 to late 2023 the stock went nowhere, bottoming at $31.82 in December 2022. Then, from early 2024, the market decided power was scarce — data centers, electrification, tight grids — and NRG went from $51.70 at the end of 2023 to a $178.96 month-end in February 2026, a 3.5x run. It has since given back 45.7%, and is down 39.0% since the end of 2025.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how quickly the mood flipped. It was above 80 in ten of 2024's twelve months — peaking at 96.4 in May 2024 — and its last three readings are below 30: 27.0 at the September 2026 month-end, the lowest in the five-year series, 27.4 in October and 28.5 at the November 13 close, all below the December 2022 trough of 36.5. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At $97.10 you pay about 11.0 times 2026 consensus adjusted EPS and 9.7 times 2027's $10.05 (calc), and under 8 times the guided adjusted EBITDA midpoint, for a company that just doubled its generation fleet with modern gas plants that cannot be quickly replicated. The third quarter showed what the bigger fleet can earn: adjusted EBITDA up 67% and adjusted EPS of $4.68, 36% above the Nasdaq consensus. The $4B sale prices six plants at about 8 times NRG's estimate of their 2027 EBITDA — roughly what the market pays for the whole company — and supports a plan of $3B of buybacks across 2026–27 and $3B of debt reduction in 2027. Vivint adds growing, weather-proof subscription cash, and the data-center plant, if completed, adds ~$500M of largely capacity-backed EBITDA from late 2029 (CEO Gaudette on the Q2 call). The 2026 FCF-before-growth guide of $2.8–3.3B is about 15% of the market cap.

Bear Case

NRG bought 13 GW near the top of the power-scarcity trade, paid with cash, stock and debt, and is now selling six gas plants (4.9 GW) to an LS Power affiliate. Net debt is $22.8B, operating income covers interest 2.7 times, the current ratio is below 1, and the three-times leverage target has already slipped a year — while more money goes to buybacks. One strong summer quarter followed two misses, and nine-month adjusted EPS of $7.68 leaves a wide $0.22–2.22 range for Q4. GAAP free cash flow is $689M against the company's multi-billion adjusted measure. Texas, NRG's home market, just elected a governor who wants to pause new data-center development, and the data-center plant is a 2029 story. Vistra and Constellation are down 25% and 21% over the year, so there is no sector wind to help.

Balance-sheet debt. $22.92B of debt, 4.35 debt-to-equity, operating income 2.7 times interest in the first nine months, and a three-times target dated 2029. Industry Commentary
The power-scarcity trade, reversed
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

From 2024 into early 2026 the market paid up for any company owning dispatchable power (plants that can run whenever needed) in constrained U.S. grids, on the idea that data centers would absorb every spare megawatt. In 2026 that trade unwound across most of the group: StockAnalysis's 52-week price changes to November 13 show Vistra −25.4% and Constellation −20.9%, with Talen +2.3% the exception; NRG is −42.5% on the same measure. For NRG the reversal hit just as it had doubled its exposure. Management's answer, as CEO Gaudette described it on the Q2 call, is to build new plants for large new customers who help pay for them, rather than count on existing plants earning more. That is a lower-risk way to sell into the demand. It is also slower, and in Texas it now runs into the politics of who pays for data-center power.

Texas: weather, prices and policy
ONGOING HEADWIND

ERCOT is an energy-only market with no capacity payments, so a generator-retailer earns its money in a few hot or cold weeks. 2026 showed both sides: a mild winter (heating degree days down about 30%), higher supply costs and soft Q2 wholesale prices cut first-half Texas EBITDA by $214M, and a summer of higher retail margins and favourable weather added $211M in Q3. The state is pushing new dispatchable supply through the Texas Energy Fund, which lends at 3% and pays completion bonuses — useful for NRG's 1.5 GW build programme, but it also adds supply to the market NRG sells into. The November election added policy risk: the governor-elect has called for a moratorium on new data-center development until lawmakers pass ratepayer protections (Heatmap, November 5, 2026).

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The same company trades at 17.0 times trailing GAAP earnings and about 11.0 times 2026 consensus adjusted earnings; at 10.6 times trailing reported EBITDA (StockAnalysis) and about 7.7 times guided 2026 adjusted EBITDA. The bull framing: GAAP is distorted by hedge marks and a part-year of acquired earnings, Q3 showed the adjusted numbers arriving, and the analysts' $161 average target — with not one Sell among 16 — says the price has overshot. The bear framing: "adjusted" is doing a lot of work, GAAP free cash flow is $689M, and the leverage target has already moved once. The way to settle it is not the multiple. It is Q4 delivery against the 2026 range, the plant sale closing, and whether net debt starts falling in 2027.
