01 Company Overview

Nu — Nubank to its customers — calls itself the largest digital bank in Latin America: 142.6 million customers at September 30, 2026, of whom nearly 120 million are in Brazil, 16.6 million in Mexico and 6.1 million in Colombia. It has no branches. It makes money three ways: interest on credit cards and personal loans, a spread on deposits ("float"), and fees. In Q3 2026 (quarter ended September 30, 2026) it booked gross revenue of $6.26B, up 37% FX-neutral, and record net income of $1.09B, up 31% FX-neutral. ROE was 32%. At a $61.35B market value it ranks #3 among Brazil-based companies on companiesmarketcap's country ranking, behind Petrobras and Itaú Unibanco, and #408 globally. It is not in the S&P 500; the home-market index is the Ibovespa, but Nu's only listing is the NYSE.

Nu is a digital bank at emerging-market scale, and the numbers show why the model works. A traditional bank pays for branches and staff for every customer. Nu spends about $1.1 a month to serve an active customer and earns about $17.7 a month from one (ARPAC). That gap of roughly 16 to 1 is the business. The risk sits on the other side of the ledger. Most of the revenue is unsecured consumer credit to Brazil's mass market, and the 90+ day NPL ratio is 7.3%. That is the price of lending at a 22.5% NIM. The stock has fallen 33% from its 52-week high of $18.98, although earnings are growing at more than 30%. The market is asking whether those earnings survive a Brazilian credit cycle, and whether the Mexico and US build-outs add to returns or dilute them.

FX exposure: Nu reports in US dollars, but almost all of its revenue is earned in Brazilian reais, Mexican pesos and Colombian pesos. Brazil holds $38.6B of the $47.5B deposit base. A weaker real cuts dollar-reported results, and a stronger one flatters them. In Q3 2026, reported USD revenue grew 45.0% YoY against 37% FX-neutral. The gap is currency.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

~$1/month cost to serve 143M-customer scale Primary-bank relationships in Brazil Proprietary credit data (NuFormer) Deposits at 87% of interbank Bank licences: Mexico live, Brazil pending

The moat is cost plus data. With no branches, Nu can profitably serve customers an incumbent would lose money on, and each of those customers adds transaction history that improves underwriting for the next. In its Q2 2026 release the company said it leads Brazil in primary banking relationships, and that customers who bank primarily with Nu fall behind on payments at roughly half the portfolio average rate. Those two claims show how the moat reaches the credit line. AI is also cutting costs: in the same release Nu said AI agents handle more than 60% of customer-support conversations in Brazil. The weakness is that cheap distribution makes it cheap to lend, and lending to Brazil's mass market is a cyclical business however good the model is.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Unsecured consumer credit in one country. Cards and personal loans make up $38.2B of the $41.6B book, and Brazil holds about 84% of customers. The 90+ NPL of 7.3% is up 0.6 percentage points YoY, and management is deliberately moving into higher-risk segments. A rise in Brazilian unemployment would hit the largest revenue line and the cost of credit at the same time.
02
Rate and currency sensitivity. Float income depends on Brazil's high policy rate. Results are translated into USD from the real and the peso. In Q3 2026 reported revenue growth beat FX-neutral growth by about 8 points (+45.0% against +37%). That tailwind can reverse.
03
Founder control. Vélez holds 74.4% of the votes on a much smaller economic stake. Class A holders cannot change strategy, the board or capital allocation. Class B holders also have pre-emptive rights to keep their share of the company, about 21% of all shares (20-F counts at March 1, 2026), in any new issue.
04
Expansion spend. Mexico now runs as a full bank, Colombia is still scaling, Nu US launched on September 10, 2026 through a partner bank, Nu Global went live on October 15, and Brazil is adding a banking licence by acquisition. Each carries upfront cost. Nu says the US and Nu Global launches will add up to 1 percentage point to the efficiency ratio in each of 2026 and 2027.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the comparisons. First, one-offs. Brazil's central bank liquidated some payment institutions, and Nu took a one-time charge on receivables it had bought from them. On the call the CFO said the charge cut Q3 risk-adjusted NIM by 0.63 percentage points and had nothing to do with how customers' loans performed, and he put the quarter's one-off hit to net income at about $53M. Q2's figure had been lifted 0.55 points by a one-off in the other direction. Excluding these items, risk-adjusted NIM rose to 12.7% from 11.9%. Second, the accounting basis. IFRS income tax was $258.7M. The Managerial P&L shows EBT (earnings before tax) of $1,743.4M and tax of $651.9M, because the reconciliation moves $393.1M of "reclassifications & adjustments" from above the pre-tax line into the tax line. Net income is the same $1,091.6M under both. Revenue and margin comparisons depend on which view you use. Third, currency. Reported USD growth (+45.0% for Q3 revenue) overstates the underlying +37% FX-neutral growth.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value, and a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

NU has no full five-year record: its NYSE history starts with the December 9, 2021 IPO, and the chart shows all 59 month-ends that exist. From the December 2021 month-end close of $9.38 to the November 16, 2026 settled close of $12.68, the stock gained 35.2%; over the same dates SPY gained 55.8% and QQQ 66.3% (price only). NU pays no dividend, so its figure is also the total return. The path was brutal. The stock lost 60% in its first six months, to a $3.74 close in June 2022, then went up nearly 5x to a $17.75 month-end high in January 2026 as the company turned profitable (FY2023 net income $1.03B after a $365M FY2022 loss). It has since given back 28.6% of that high.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At 15.8x trailing and about 14.6x 2026 consensus earnings, you get a bank earning a 32% ROE that grew net income 31% FX-neutral in its latest quarter. ARPAC is $17.7 against a $1.1 cost to serve, and the gap is widening. The deposit base costs 87% of interbank rates, and the bank serves 143 million customers. Mexico is now a licensed bank, Colombia's customers grew 62% in a year, and management cut its cost guidance for 2026. Consensus implies a PEG of 0.43 and EPS growth of +37.6% then +24.9%. If 90+ NPLs stay near 7% while ARPAC compounds, the current price is about 11.6x 2027 consensus EPS for a bank earning a 32% ROE.

Bear Case

A P/B of about 4.3x prices in a ~30% ROE indefinitely, on a book that is 92% unsecured consumer credit in an emerging market. Both NPL buckets are higher YoY (90+ at 7.3% from 6.7%), and management is deliberately lending into riskier segments. Risk-adjusted NIM slipped to 12.1% in Q3. Float income depends on Brazil's high rates, and reported growth is inflated by currency. On top of that, the company is paying to enter the US and launch Nu Global, markets where it has no data advantage. The founder holds 74% of the votes, so Class A holders cannot stop any of it. The stock has fallen 29% from its January month-end high despite record earnings, which suggests many investors already hold this view.

Top 3 Risks

01
Brazilian consumer-credit cycle. The 90+ NPL is 7.3% and the 15–90 is 4.9%, both up YoY. Credit is 61% of revenue, and cost of credit was $1.79B in a single quarter. A macro shock in Brazil hits revenue, provisions and ROE together.
02
International expansion dilutes returns. The Mexico bank conversion, Colombia, the US partner-bank launch and Nu Global all add cost ahead of revenue. The efficiency ratio improved to 19.3% in Q3, but the CFO expects it to rise in Q4, and Nu says the US and Nu Global launches will add up to 1 percentage point to it in each of 2026 and 2027. The December 8 Investor Day should show whether management sets return hurdles for each market.
03
Rates, currency, politics and governance. Lower Brazilian rates compress float income. A weaker real cuts USD-reported results. Brazilian politics moves the stock too: on October 26, the Monday after Lula's runoff win, Nu fell 16.95% as Brazilian stocks plunged. The dual-class structure leaves outside shareholders no say in either response.
Industry Commentary
Unsecured credit growth vs. the delinquency curve
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Nu's Q2 2026 release described "intentional expansions into higher-risk, higher-return segments" alongside a wider NIM. That is a deliberate trade: more yield for more loss. On the numbers it is still working. Q3 risk-adjusted NIM was 12.1% (12.7% excluding one-offs), against 10.8% a year earlier, even though the 90+ NPL rose from 6.7% to 7.3%. On the Q3 call the CFO said the headline ratios rose mainly because interest-bearing balances grew to 61% of the portfolio, and that on those balances the early 15–90 bucket improved. The Q2 release also said 90+ delinquency had improved in every income band since July 2025 while peer banks' segments worsened. These statements fit together if the higher portfolio NPL comes from mix, meaning more lending to riskier bands and more interest-bearing balances, rather than from deterioration within any band. That can hold until the cycle turns. In a lending business, the loans written in the good years are the ones that show losses in the bad years.

From fintech to bank: licences in three countries
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

In 2026 Nu is turning itself into a regulated bank across its markets. In Mexico, the CNBV granted operating authorisation as a multiple bank (6-K, July 10, 2026), and Nu launched there in August; Q3 was its first quarter as a Mexican bank. In Brazil it agreed to buy Banco Porto Real to add a banking licence under Joint Resolution No. 17. In the US it has preliminary conditional approval for a national bank charter from the OCC, granted January 2026, and runs on a partner bank in the meantime. Bank licences mean cheaper deposits and a wider product range. They also mean more capital regulation and supervisory cost. Nu's cost advantage was built outside that regime and will now be tested inside it.

AI in underwriting and service — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

In its Q2 2026 release Nu said NuFormer, its foundation model for financial behaviour, underwrites cards in Brazil and Mexico and unsecured loans in Brazil, with SME and Colombian cards in testing, and that AI agents handle more than 60% of support conversations in Brazil at or above human parity. The bull framing: a decade of transaction data from more than 100 million customers is a proprietary training set that incumbents cannot easily copy, and it pushes down both cost to serve and credit losses. The bear framing: better models allowed the move into riskier segments, and model-driven credit has no track record through a full Brazilian recession at this scale. The test is the 90+ NPL of the loans written in 2026, measured in 2027, and it cannot be seen yet.
