01 Company Overview

Nucor is the largest steel producer in the United States and North America's largest recycler of any material. It runs electric arc furnace (EAF) minimills — furnaces that melt scrap steel with electricity rather than smelting iron ore with coke in a blast furnace — across sheet, plate, bar and structural products, plus a downstream fabrication business and an upstream raw-materials arm. FY2025 revenue was $32.49B, up 5.7% from $30.73B in FY2024; the trailing-twelve-month figure through July 2026 is $36.10B, up 17.2%. At a $59.23B market capitalisation it is roughly the #440th most valuable listed company in the world (estimated from a #451 rank recorded at a $56.46B market cap in August 2026).

Read one number and you have read most of this company: the metal spread — what a ton of steel sells for minus what the scrap to make it costs. In Q2 2026 Nucor's steel mills sold to outside customers at $1,145 per ton against an average scrap and scrap-substitute cost of $422 per gross ton, a spread of roughly $723. A year earlier the same two numbers were $1,041 and $403, a spread of $638. Shipments over the same year rose about 10%; the spread rose about 13%; steel mill pre-tax earnings rose 85%, from $843M to $1,556M. Volume is the smaller lever. Spread is the business.

The central debate of this page: how much of the 2026 spread is policy and how much is demand. Section 232 tariffs at a 50% headline rate have suppressed imports and lifted domestic prices; separately, data-centre, grid and reshoring construction has genuinely tightened order books. The stock has re-rated +79.8% over 52 weeks and now trades at 20.9x trailing earnings that are themselves recovering off a trough. If the spread is structural, the forward multiple of about 12x is cheap. If it is a tariff-widened peak, the trailing multiple is the wrong one to look at in either direction.

This is the melting operation and the reason the whole company works differently from an integrated mill. An EAF minimill melts scrap and direct-reduced iron with electricity. Capital cost per ton is far lower than a blast furnace; more importantly, the cost base is variable rather than fixed. A blast furnace cannot be economically banked and restarted, so an integrated producer facing weak demand keeps melting and eats the price. Nucor can idle a furnace for a shift and its largest single input cost — scrap — simply stops being purchased. That is the mechanical reason Nucor stays profitable through cycles that put integrated mills into losses: in FY2025, a trough year, Nucor still earned $1.74B net, while Cleveland-Cliffs, blast-furnace-weighted, lost $1.48B.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
EAF variable cost base Scrap flow ownership (DJJ) DRI + gas vertical hedge Downstream fabrication capture Non-union, profit-sharing pay model Balance sheet: D/E 0.31 Melted-and-poured domestic status

The core advantage is cost structure shape, not cost level. Nucor is not always the lowest-cost ton in the world — imported steel from subsidised capacity often is, which is why tariffs matter. What Nucor has is a cost curve that bends with volume. Roughly 70–80% of the cost of a ton is scrap and energy, both purchased per ton. Add a compensation model in which a large share of mill pay is production-and-profit bonus, and labour flexes with output too. The result: when steel prices fall, Nucor's costs fall alongside them and margin compresses rather than inverting. FY2025 is the proof — the trough year still produced $1.74B of net income and $7.52 of EPS, and the dividend was raised for a 53rd straight year. Secondary moats: owning the scrap supply chain, having DRI as a scrap substitute, capturing the downstream fabrication margin, and a conservative balance sheet that lets the company build capacity counter-cyclically while levered competitors cannot.

Key vulnerabilities

01
Tariff dependence is real and it is policy, not moat. The Section 232 regime at a 50% headline rate is the single largest reason domestic hot-rolled prices sit where they do. Tariff rates are set by proclamation and can be changed by proclamation — the structure was already rewritten on April 2, 2026, effective April 6, into a tiered system. A future administration, an adverse trade ruling, or negotiated country carve-outs could compress the spread without any change in Nucor's operations.
02
Scrap is a cost Nucor does not control. The input side of the spread is a globally traded commodity. Scrap has risen from $403 to $422 per gross ton year over year while prices rose faster — a favourable sequence. Reverse it and the leverage runs the other way with the same violence. Rising EAF capacity across the industry is itself a source of scrap demand, and prime scrap in particular is structurally tight.
03
The capital programme is large, front-loaded and unproven. FY2026 capex guidance is $2.5B with about 60% for growth. The West Virginia sheet mill began commissioning in July 2026, with commercial shipments expected in early 2027 and only ~50% utilisation targeted by end-2027, against roughly $120M per quarter of pre-operating and start-up costs running through 2027. That is a real earnings drag arriving regardless of where prices go, and it adds domestic sheet supply into a market whose tightness is partly policy-created.
04
Cyclicality makes valuation genuinely hard. There is no "normal" P/E for this business. FY2022 EPS was $28.79; FY2025 was $7.52 — a 3.8x swing in three years with no change in the assets. Any multiple quoted on a single year is a statement about where in the cycle you think that year sits, not about the price of the company.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $111.65 to $261.07 on September 4, 2026, NUE returned +133.8% on price alone — a compound annual rate of roughly 18.9% over the 4.9-year window, before a dividend that has been raised every year of it. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The metal spread has widened from ~$638 to ~$723 per ton year over year while utilisation went from 85% to 91%, and management has guided to further margin expansion in Q3 2026 — so the earnings recovery is not yet complete. Consensus calendar-2026 EPS of $18.73 puts the stock at ~13.9x on a business with 12.05% ROIC, 0.31 debt-to-equity, 53 straight years of dividend increases and an aggressive buyback. The West Virginia sheet mill and the coating, tower and micro-mill projects add earning assets into demand that is genuinely broad-based across energy, infrastructure, data centres and reshoring, with a sole-source border-wall stream running to 2028.

Bear Case

The spread is where it is largely because a 50% tariff wall removed the marginal import — a policy input, not an operating one, and one already rewritten once in 2026. Buy at 93% of the 52-week high, after a 79.8% run, against a consensus target implying 8.3% upside, and you are underwriting peak-ish spreads. Meanwhile the company is spending $2.5B this year, carrying ~$120M per quarter of start-up drag through 2027, and adding domestic sheet supply into the same tight market that created the spread. Steel Products earnings are already down year to date. A tariff softening, a scrap spike, or a construction slowdown compresses this at 1.88 beta.

Top 3 risks

01
Policy reversal on Section 232. The dominant swing factor in domestic steel pricing is set by executive proclamation and has already been restructured once in 2026. Carve-outs, bilateral deals or a rate reduction would compress the selling-price side of the spread with no offsetting change on the cost side.
02
Scrap cost inflation into new EAF supply. Nucor's input is a globally traded commodity it does not control, and the industry-wide shift to electric arc furnaces — including Nucor's own new capacity — increases competition for prime scrap. A scrap move that outruns pricing collapses the spread quickly; the $422/gross ton figure is the number to watch each quarter.
03
Execution and timing on the capital programme. The West Virginia mill ships commercially in early 2027 at ~50% utilisation by year-end 2027, with ~$120M quarterly start-up costs through 2027. If the cycle turns before the ramp completes, Nucor will be commissioning its largest-ever asset into a falling price — the classic way cyclicals destroy returns on otherwise sound projects.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Tariff policy is the price-setter
Section 232 went to a 50% global rate in June 2025, and on April 2, 2026 (effective April 6) was restructured into a tiered schedule: 50% on articles made entirely or almost entirely of steel, 25% on derivatives above 15% metal content, 15% transitional on metal-intensive industrial equipment and grid components through 2027, 10% on foreign-finished goods using ≥95% U.S.-origin melted-and-poured steel, and exempt below 15% metal content — with duty now assessed on full customs value rather than metal-content value. Canada and Mexico lost their USMCA exemptions in 2025. Nucor's monthly price chart turned within a month of the June 2025 rate rise and made its largest single monthly gain (+33.2%) in April 2026. That correlation is the honest framing of this stock: it is a leveraged position on continued protection as much as on industrial demand.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Data centres, grid and reshoring
Management describes demand as "broad-based across energy, infrastructure, data centers and reshoring," and Nucor has been building directly into it — utility transmission tower plants in Indiana (late 2026), Alabama and Utah (full production mid-2027). This matters because it is the one demand source that is not policy-dependent: a data centre needs structural steel, rebar, and grid steel regardless of tariff rates. Automotive shipments hit two-year highs in Q2 2026 as well. If the tariff regime softens, the question becomes whether this demand is deep enough to hold the spread on its own.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Consolidation and new domestic supply
Nippon Steel completed its acquisition of U.S. Steel on June 18, 2025 at $14.9B; the shares were delisted at a final $54.84 close, with the U.S. government holding a "golden share" over major decisions. A well-capitalised foreign parent now stands behind a large domestic integrated producer and has signalled investment in it. At the same time Nucor, Steel Dynamics and others are commissioning new domestic sheet capacity into a market whose tightness is partly tariff-created. Domestic supply is going up while the import wall is what holds the price up — a combination that historically ends spread cycles.
BULL VS BEAR — HOW TO FRAME IT
Is $723 the new floor or the old ceiling?
Everything reduces to one question. The bull says a ~$723 spread at 91% utilisation is the new mid-cycle, because the import wall is structural, the demand is grid- and data-centre-driven, and Nucor's EAF cost curve means it earns well even if the spread reverts toward $650. The bear says $723 is a policy-inflated peak, that FY2025's $7.52 EPS is closer to true mid-cycle than 2026's $18.73 run-rate, and that paying 20.9x trailing at a 52-week high for a 1.88-beta cyclical is the textbook cycle-top error. Note that both cases are consistent with the same verified facts. The discriminator is not in the financials — it is in the tariff schedule and the scrap price, and both are observable quarter by quarter.
