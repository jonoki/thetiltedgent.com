01 Company Overview

Novo Nordisk is the Danish company that turned GLP-1 drugs into the biggest commercial story in medicine. Ozempic and Wegovy — both the molecule semaglutide — made up DKK 206.2B of FY2025 sales of DKK 309.06B (+6.43%), and the company is still #1 by market cap in Denmark (country ranking via companiesmarketcap) at $176.4B, more than three times the size of #2 Maersk. That is where the good news stops. The ADR was a $142.74 month-end close in June 2024; on Monday, September 21, 2026 it settled at $39.80, down 7.96% on the day of its Capital Markets Day, on 48.4 million shares — about four times its 20-day average volume. The stock is now #119 in the world, while Eli Lilly, the rival that took its place, is worth $1.04T.

This report asks one question: is Novo cheap, or broken? On the numbers it looks cheap — 9.9x trailing earnings and about 10.3x our normalised earnings of DKK 25.2 ($3.87) per ADR (worked out in Section 04), for a business with a 78% adjusted gross margin. On the trend it looks broken. Novo's own data show that it still sells more volume but earns less on each dose. US Wegovy injectable sales fell 22% at CER in Q2 2026 "driven by lower realised prices, partly countered by increased volumes." US list prices for Wegovy and Ozempic fall to $675 a month on January 1, 2027 — about 50% and 35% cuts. The company guides 2026 adjusted sales at 0% to −6%. Lilly's trailing revenue is growing 49.6%. The premise of this report holds up: Novo was the GLP-1 leader and has lost the lead. The open question is whether the earnings base has stopped falling — and on Monday the market voted no.

Currency: Novo reports in Danish kroner (DKK, pegged to the euro); the NYSE ADR trades in dollars. US Operations produced 52% of Q2 2026 sales (DKK 40.9B of 78.5B). Novo says a 5% move in the dollar changes adjusted operating profit by about DKK 5.0B over 12 months, so a weaker dollar hurts both the reported numbers and the ADR. Home market: Nasdaq Copenhagen, where Novo is the largest listed company and a constituent of the OMX Copenhagen 25 (OMX C25) index.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Semaglutide scale & manufacturing Oral peptide delivery (Wegovy pill) 100-year diabetes franchise SELECT cardiovascular label Foundation control — no activist risk AA / Aa3 credit rating

The old moat was being first and alone: one molecule, two brands, supply-limited demand and US list prices that held. All three are gone. Lilly's tirzepatide is the stronger injectable. The semaglutide compound patent has expired in some international markets. The US price is being reset by policy. What is left is scale and delivery. Novo makes a GLP-1 peptide that works as a pill and plans ~10x its oral manufacturing capacity by 2030, the pill is the fastest GLP-1 volume launch in the US to date, and the SELECT heart-outcome data are on the label. That moat defends volume, not price.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Lost the efficacy race on the injectable. In REIMAGINE 4 (type 2 diabetes, 68 weeks) CagriSema 2.4/2.4 mg was non-inferior to tirzepatide 15 mg on weight but not on HbA1c: 15.2% vs 15.8% weight loss, −1.9 vs −2.2 points HbA1c. Monday's REIMAGINE 5 "win" (12.4% vs 9.1% at week 60, per Novo's Sep 21, 2026 release) compared CagriSema 1/1 mg with tirzepatide 5 mg — Lilly's lowest maintenance dose. A win at low doses is not a win at the top doses where the market competes.
02
Price is falling faster than volume is growing in the US. Novo expects US sales to fall in 2026. The January 2027 list-price cut, Medicaid coverage losses and the shift to self-pay ($149–299 a month for the pill) all lower revenue per patient. The Medicare "Bridge" pilot (from July 1, 2026) adds patients at government prices.
03
Pipeline write-downs are piling up. Q2 2026 carried DKK 6.3B of intangible impairments, including monlunabant (DKK 4.0B, terminated). The ziltivekimab ZEUS cardiovascular trial failed its primary endpoint in July, and Novo warns this "may result in a non-cash impairment charge in Q3 2026." The 2035 pipeline target of DKK 150B+ rests on assets that have not yet read out.
04
Semaglutide loses exclusivity. Novo says the compound patent has expired "in certain markets" in International Operations. Canada is the first visible case: lower prices, flat volume. Each further market follows the same pattern.

Normalised earnings — how we got DKK 25.16 ($3.87) per ADR

Step 1 — company-adjusted TTM EPS. Novo's adjusted diluted EPS (it excludes 340B reversals, restructuring, impairments and intangible amortisation): Q3 2025 6.56 + Q4 2025 6.44 + Q1 2026 6.63 (H1 12.81 − Q2 6.18) + Q2 2026 6.18 = DKK 25.81. Step 2 — put back a real cost. Amortisation of intangibles is a recurring expense of acquisitions: H1 2026 DKK 3,340M × 2 × (1 − 21.9% tax) ÷ 4,442M shares = −DKK 1.17. Step 3 — take out a one-off Novo left in. The Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin includes "around DKK 3 billion" of one-time costs for right-sizing manufacturing contracts: 3,000 × (1 − 21.9%) ÷ 4,436M = +DKK 0.53. Normalised EPS = 25.81 − 1.17 + 0.53 = DKK 25.16 per B share = $3.87 per ADR (÷ 6.5061), so P/E ≈ 10.3x. For comparison, reported TTM EPS is DKK 26.20: the DKK 26.8B 340B reversal (≈ DKK 4.7 after tax) adds more than the impairments and restructuring take away. This is an estimate: it treats the last four quarters' prices as the new baseline. The January 2027 US list-price cut is not in it, and consensus FY2026 EPS of DKK 21.58 says the baseline is still falling.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the trailing numbers. (1) 340B reversal: Novo fully reversed its USD 4.2B (DKK 26.8B) US 340B provision in Q1 2026. It was non-cash and went straight into sales, which is why Q1 2026 revenue was DKK 96,823M and operating profit DKK 59,618M, against DKK 78,488M and 33,389M (adjusted) in Q2. A smaller DKK 2.6B reversal sat in Q2 2025. (2) Impairments: DKK 6.3B in Q2 2026 (monlunabant DKK 4.0B), with a possible further charge in Q3 2026 for ziltivekimab. (3) Restructuring: the 2025 company-wide transformation cost DKK 8,014M (in adjusted-profit terms), mostly in Q3 2025, and is now producing savings — Q2 2026 sales and distribution costs fell 13% at CER. (4) Manufacturing right-sizing: ~DKK 3B of one-time costs sits inside the Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin. Buybacks are modest: DKK 5.9B in H1 2026 under a DKK 15B programme.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case — cheap

You pay about 10x normalised earnings of DKK 25.16 ($3.87) per ADR for a business with a 78% adjusted gross margin, a 42.5% adjusted operating margin, a 6.6% FCF yield, a 3.2% dividend and an AA balance sheet. Volume is not the problem: International obesity grew 37% in Q2, and the Wegovy pill reached ~285,000 US weekly scripts in eight months with 80% of new users new to GLP-1s. Capex is peaking (DKK 60B in 2025, ~55B in 2026, "expected to decline"), so free cash flow should rise even on flat profit. The 2025 restructuring is showing up in 13% lower selling costs. If GLP-1s become a mass-market self-pay product, the company with the scaled oral peptide and the manufacturing wins on volume. The consensus target of $46.80 already sits 18% above the price.

Bear Case — broken

"Normalised" assumes the base has stopped falling, and nothing sourced says it has. US Wegovy injectable is −22% on price, the US list price is cut by up to half in January 2027, semaglutide is losing exclusivity outside the US, diabetes value share fell 3.6 points in a year, and CVS opens its formulary to rivals on October 1. Consensus FY2026 EPS of DKK 21.58 is 14% below our normalised figure. The next-generation answer, CagriSema, is non-inferior to tirzepatide at top doses rather than better, and its headline win on Monday was against Lilly's lowest dose. Management's own 2030 plan is mid-single-digit growth — the profile of a mature pharma, which does not trade far above 10x once patents expire. Cheap on trailing earnings is what a business with falling prices looks like on the way down.

Verdict on the premise: both halves are true, in order. Novo is cheap on what it earns today (~10x normalised) and broken as the price-setter it used to be. The stock only works if volume growth outruns price erosion after January 2027. The Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 reports are the first where that can be measured. Until then, the price action says the market expects price to win.

01
US net price reset. The $675 list price from January 1, 2027, MFN pricing, Medicaid coverage losses and the self-pay shift all lower revenue per patient. Q2 2026 already showed US Wegovy injectable −22% at CER, mostly price.

Industry Commentary
GLP-1s are becoming a volume business
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For three years the GLP-1 market was supply-limited and priced like a specialty drug. Every data point in Novo's H1 2026 report says that era is over. Volume is growing fast (IQVIA branded obesity volume +87% in the US, moving annual total, May 2026), while realised price falls in every region Novo reports — US commercial rebates, MFN pricing, China list-price cuts in late Q4 2025, and semaglutide patent expiry in some markets. Self-pay is now the main US channel for the pill ($149–299 a month, 90% self-pay). Pharma investors know this shape from statins and insulin: the market grows, the price per unit falls, and the winner is whoever has the lowest cost at scale. Novo is building that position — 10x oral capacity by 2030 — but it caps what the franchise can earn.

Lilly vs Novo: from duopoly to leader and follower
ONGOING HEADWIND

On September 21, 2026 Lilly was worth $1.04T against Novo's $176B, and Lilly's TTM revenue ($79.67B, +49.6%) is well above Novo's ($50.34B reported, including the one-off 340B reversal). Novo's defence rests on the oral channel, where it launched first, and on CagriSema. The head-to-head data are mixed: REIMAGINE 4 was non-inferior on weight and missed on HbA1c at top doses, while REIMAGINE 5 won at low doses. Management's own reply on Monday was to widen the company into blood, endocrine, liver and cardiovascular disease instead of claiming GLP-1 leadership. On the day, that read as conceding the leadership.

The oral channel — Novo's one clear lead
EMERGING TAILWIND

The Wegovy pill has more than 5 million US prescriptions since its January 5, 2026 launch, 265,000+ weekly scripts by mid-July and ~285,000 by September. MarketBeat's Capital Markets Day summary says oral drugs are about a third of obesity prescriptions. It has launched in the UAE (June) and UK (July), with EU approval in July. The pill brings in patients new to GLP-1s (80% of new starts) at self-pay prices. That grows the market, but at lower revenue per patient than the injectable it partly replaces — Novo's own outlook lists "potential cannibalisation effects". Bull framing: cheaper to make at scale, first to market, and it reaches patients injectables never did. Bear framing: Novo is swapping full-list-price injectables for $149–299 pills, and calling it growth.
