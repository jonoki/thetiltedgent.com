01 Company Overview

Novo Nordisk is the Danish company that turned GLP-1 drugs into the biggest commercial story in medicine. Ozempic and Wegovy — both the molecule semaglutide — made up DKK 206.2B of FY2025 sales of DKK 309.06B (+6.43%), and the company is still #1 by market cap in Denmark (country ranking via companiesmarketcap) at about $180.3B (our calculation at the November 6 close), more than three times the size of the next Danish company. Then came a better quarter than the market feared. On November 4, 2026 Novo reported Q3 adjusted sales of DKK 79.2B (+4% at CER) and adjusted operating profit up 27% to DKK 31.7B, and raised its 2026 outlook to −2% to +2%. The ADR closed up 4.95% that day at $42.18, fell 4.46% the next, and ended the week at $40.31 on Friday, November 6. That is 1.3% above the September 21 Capital Markets Day close and still about 72% below the $142.74 month-end close of June 2024. Eli Lilly, the rival that took its place, is worth about $1.21T on our calculation.

This report asks one question: is Novo cheap, or broken? On the numbers it looks cheap — 10.6x trailing earnings and about 10.8x our normalised earnings of DKK 25.02 ($3.74) per ADR (worked out in Section 04), for a business with an 82% adjusted gross margin in Q3. The Q3 print gave the bulls a real data point: US sales rose 1% at CER, because the Wegovy pill (DKK 4.2B in the US) offset a 13% fall in US Wegovy injectable sales, and Novo still guides US sales down for the full year. On the trend it still looks like a company giving up price. Over nine months, US Wegovy injectable sales fell 15% at CER, "driven by lower realised prices, partly countered by increased volumes." On the Q3 call, Jamey Millar, Novo's EVP of US Operations, said Wegovy price erosion was "30%, 35% in the quarter." US list prices for Wegovy and Ozempic fall to $675 a month on January 1, 2027 — about 50% and 35% cuts. Lilly's trailing revenue is growing 45.5% (our calculation from its quarterly reports). The premise of this report holds up: Novo was the GLP-1 leader and has lost the lead. The open question is whether the earnings base has stopped falling. Q3 says it is falling more slowly; the market took the gain back within a day.

Currency: Novo reports in Danish kroner (DKK, pegged to the euro); the NYSE ADR trades in dollars. US Operations produced 53% of Q3 2026 sales (DKK 41.9B of 79.2B). In its H1 2026 report Novo said a 5% move in the dollar changes adjusted operating profit by about DKK 5.0B over 12 months, so a weaker dollar hurts both the reported numbers and the ADR. Home market: Nasdaq Copenhagen, where Novo is the largest listed company and a constituent of the OMX Copenhagen 25 (OMX C25) index.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Semaglutide scale & manufacturing Oral peptide delivery (Wegovy pill) 100-year diabetes franchise SELECT cardiovascular label Foundation control — no activist risk AA / Aa3 credit rating

The old moat was being first and alone: one molecule, two brands, supply-limited demand and US list prices that held. All three are gone. Lilly's tirzepatide is the stronger injectable. The semaglutide compound patent has expired in some international markets. The US price is being reset by policy. What is left is scale and delivery. Novo makes a GLP-1 peptide that works as a pill and plans ~10x its oral manufacturing capacity by 2030, Novo calls the pill the "strongest-ever GLP-1 volume launch in the US" (H1 report), and the SELECT heart-outcome data are on the label. That moat defends volume, not price.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Lost the efficacy race on the injectable. In REIMAGINE 4 (type 2 diabetes, 68 weeks) CagriSema 2.4/2.4 mg was non-inferior (statistically no worse) to tirzepatide 15 mg on weight but not on HbA1c, a measure of long-term blood sugar: 15.2% vs 15.8% weight loss, −1.9 vs −2.2 points HbA1c. The September 21 REIMAGINE 5 "win" (12.4% vs 9.1% at week 60, per Novo's Sep 21, 2026 release) compared CagriSema 1/1 mg with tirzepatide 5 mg — Lilly's lowest maintenance dose. A win at low doses is not a win at the top doses where the market competes.
02
Price is falling faster than volume is growing in the US. Novo expects US sales to fall in 2026 (Nov 4 outlook). Q3 US sales were +1% at CER only because the Wegovy pill (DKK 4.2B) offset a 13% fall in US Wegovy injectable sales. The January 2027 list-price cut, Medicaid coverage losses and the shift to self-pay ($149–299 a month for the pill) all lower revenue per patient. The Medicare "Bridge" pilot (from July 1, 2026) adds patients at government prices, at about 60,000 weekly prescriptions across injectable and oral products, per Millar.
03
Pipeline write-downs keep piling up. Q3 2026 carried DKK 8.7B of non-cash impairments — DKK 4.6B for ziltivekimab, whose ZEUS and HERMES Phase 3 trials did not meet their primary endpoints, and DKK 4.1B for right-sizing the manufacturing network — after DKK 6.3B in Q2 (including monlunabant, DKK 4.0B). Reported operating profit fell 9% at CER in Q3 while adjusted operating profit rose 27%. The 2035 pipeline target of DKK 150B+ rests on assets that have not yet read out.
04
Semaglutide loses exclusivity. Novo says the compound patent has expired "in certain markets" in International Operations. Canada is the first visible case: lower prices, flat volume. Each further market follows the same pattern.

Normalised earnings — how we got DKK 25.02 ($3.74) per ADR

Step 1 — company-adjusted TTM EPS. Novo's adjusted diluted EPS (it excludes 340B reversals, restructuring, impairments and intangible amortisation): Q4 2025 6.44 + Q1 2026 6.63 + Q2 2026 6.18 + Q3 2026 5.79 (Q3 announcement) = DKK 25.04. Step 2 — put back a real cost. Amortisation of intangibles is a recurring expense of acquisitions: H1 2026 DKK 3,340M × 2 × (1 − 21.9% tax) ÷ 4,442M shares = −DKK 1.17 (H1 2026 annualised). Step 3 — take out two one-offs Novo left in. The Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin includes "around DKK 3 billion" of one-time costs for right-sizing manufacturing contracts: 3,000 × (1 − 21.9%) ÷ 4,436M = +DKK 0.53. In Q3 the CFO cited "around DKK 3.5 billion of one-time costs" within other operating expenses and R&D: 3,500 × (1 − 21.9%) ÷ 4,436M = +DKK 0.62. Normalised EPS = 25.04 − 1.17 + 0.53 + 0.62 = DKK 25.02 per B share = $3.74 per ADR (÷ 6.6952), so P/E ≈ 10.8x. For comparison, reported TTM EPS is DKK 25.47: the DKK 26.8B 340B reversal (≈ DKK 4.7 after tax) adds more than the impairments take away. This is an estimate: it treats the last four quarters' prices as the new baseline. The January 2027 US list-price cut is not in it, and consensus FY2026 EPS of DKK 22.63 says the baseline is still falling.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the trailing numbers. (1) 340B reversal: Novo fully reversed its USD 4.2B (DKK 26.8B) US 340B provision in Q1 2026. It was non-cash and went straight into sales, which is why Q1 2026 revenue was DKK 96,823M and operating profit DKK 59,618M. A smaller DKK 2.6B reversal sat in Q2 2025. (2) Impairments: DKK 6.3B in Q2 2026 (monlunabant DKK 4.0B) and DKK 8.7B in Q3 2026 (ziltivekimab DKK 4.6B, manufacturing network DKK 4.1B). (3) Restructuring: the 2025 company-wide transformation carried "approximately DKK 9 billion" of costs in Q3 2025 (CFO, Q3 call), which flatters the Q3 2026 profit growth: adjusted operating profit +27% at CER, against a year-earlier base that included those costs. (4) Manufacturing right-sizing: ~DKK 3B of one-time costs sits inside the Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin and ~DKK 3.5B inside Q3 other operating expenses and R&D. Buybacks are modest: 41.7M B shares for DKK 11.56B bought by November 2 under the DKK 15B programme.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the split-adjusted November 2021 month-end close of $53.42 to the November 6, 2026 settled close of $40.31, the ADR lost 24.5% of its price over five years. The endpoints hide the whole story. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case — cheap

You pay about 10.8x normalised earnings of DKK 25.02 ($3.74) per ADR for a business with an 82% Q3 adjusted gross margin, a 40.1% adjusted operating margin, a 6.0% free-cash-flow yield, a 4.3% dividend yield and an AA credit rating. Volume is not the problem: the Wegovy pill sold DKK 5.4B in Q3 and took about 80% of US new-to-brand prescriptions among obesity pills, and International Operations grew 8% (EUCAN +18%). Capex is falling (DKK 60B in 2025, about DKK 50B guided for 2026) and free cash flow is now guided at DKK 60–70B. The 2025 restructuring is showing up in lower selling costs (Q2 2026 sales and distribution costs fell 13% at CER). If GLP-1s become a mass-market self-pay product, the company with the scaled oral peptide and the manufacturing wins on volume. The consensus target of $46.01 sits 14% above the price.

Bear Case — broken

"Normalised" assumes the base has stopped falling, and Q3 shows it falling more slowly, not stopping. US Wegovy injectable sales fell 13% at CER on price, Wegovy price erosion was "30%, 35%" in the quarter, the US list price is cut by up to half in January 2027, semaglutide is losing exclusivity outside the US, diabetes value share fell 3.6 points in a year, and CVS Caremark scheduled Zepbound's return to its commercial formularies for October 1. Q3 adjusted EPS fell 12% in DKK to DKK 5.79 even as adjusted operating profit rose 27% at CER on an easy comparison. Consensus FY2026 EPS of DKK 22.63 is 10% below our normalised figure. The CFO is leaving at year-end. The next-generation answer, CagriSema, is non-inferior to tirzepatide at top doses rather than better, and its September 21 headline win was against Lilly's lowest dose. Management's own 2030 plan is mid-single-digit growth — the profile of a mature pharma, which does not trade far above 10x once patents expire. Cheap on trailing earnings is what a business with falling prices looks like on the way down.

Verdict on the premise: both halves are true, in order. Novo is cheap on what it earns today (~10x normalised) and no longer able to set prices in its market the way it used to. The stock only works if volume growth outruns price erosion after January 2027. The Q4 2026 and Q1 2027 reports are the first where that can be measured. Q3 gave the first evidence on volume (guidance up, the pill ahead of expectations), but the ADR gave back nearly all of its post-print gain within a day, which is a reading that the market is still waiting for the January 2027 price cut to show in the numbers.

Industry Commentary
GLP-1s are becoming a volume business
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For three years the GLP-1 market was supply-limited and priced like a specialty drug. Every data point in Novo's H1 and Q3 2026 reports says that era is over. Volume is growing fast (IQVIA branded obesity volume +71% in the US, moving annual total, August 2026), while realised price falls in every region Novo reports — US commercial rebates, "Most Favoured Nations" pricing, China list-price cuts in late Q4 2025, and semaglutide patent expiry in some markets. The pill sells to self-pay patients at $149–299 a month by dose (H1 report). Pharma investors know this shape from statins and insulin: the market grows, the price per unit falls, and the winner is whoever has the lowest cost at scale. Novo is building that position — 10x oral capacity by 2030 — but it caps what the franchise can earn.

Lilly vs Novo: from duopoly to leader and follower
ONGOING HEADWIND

On November 6, 2026 Lilly was worth about $1.21T against Novo's $180B (our calculations), and Lilly's TTM revenue ($86.46B, +45.5%) is well above Novo's ($50.70B reported, including the one-off 340B reversal). Novo's defence rests on the oral channel, where it launched first, and on CagriSema. The head-to-head data are mixed: REIMAGINE 4 was non-inferior on weight and missed on HbA1c at top doses, while REIMAGINE 5 won at low doses. Management's own reply at the September 21 Capital Markets Day was to widen the company into blood, endocrine, liver and cardiovascular disease instead of claiming GLP-1 leadership. On the day, that read as conceding the leadership.

The oral channel — Novo's one clear lead
EMERGING TAILWIND

The Wegovy pill has had more than 8 million US prescriptions since its January 5, 2026 launch (Novo's US head, Q3 call) and took about 80% of new-to-brand prescriptions among obesity pills in Q3. Reuters reported on September 22, citing company presentations, that oral drugs are about a third of US obesity prescriptions. It has launched in the UAE (June) and UK (July), with EU approval in July; Novo's Q3 announcement counts eight markets. The pill brings in patients new to GLP-1s (the H1 report credits its growth to them) at self-pay prices. That grows the market, but at lower revenue per patient than the injectable it partly replaces — Novo's own outlook lists "potential cannibalisation effects". Bull framing: cheaper to make at scale, first to market, and it reaches patients injectables never did. Bear framing: Novo is swapping full-list-price injectables for $149–299 pills, and calling it growth.
