01 Company Overview

Novartis is a Basel-based prescription-drug company. It calls itself a "pure-play" innovative medicines company with four core therapeutic areas (cardiovascular-renal-metabolic, immunology, neuroscience, oncology). The premise holds: the generics arm Sandoz was spun off in October 2023, and the Alcon eye-care spin came before that, so what is left is branded, patent-protected drugs. FY2025 net sales were $54.53B, up 8% (+8% at cc), with a core operating margin of 40.1% and core EPS of $8.98 (IFRS EPS $7.21). At a $268B market value Novartis ranks #2 in Switzerland, behind Roche, and #59 in the world by companiesmarketcap's count. At home it is one of the heavyweights of the SMI, the Swiss blue-chip index.

This report turns on two numbers. The first is margin. Novartis reached a 40% core operating margin in 2025, a level most large pharma companies do not reach, and it bought that margin partly by shedding lower-margin businesses. The second is the patent cliff. Entresto, its heart-failure drug and its biggest seller at $7.75B in FY2025, got US generic competition in July 2025. In Q2 2026 Entresto sales fell 51% at cc to $1.18B. Management's 2026 guide is net sales up low single digits and core operating income down low single digits. The Q2 2026 core margin was 41.2%, down 70bps, which management attributed mainly to incremental cost from the Avidity acquisition. Filling the gap is the job of Kisqali, Kesimpta, Pluvicto, Scemblix and Leqvio, the brands management named on the Q2 call. Together with Cosentyx they are still growing at double-digit rates. September 2026 reopened the question in a different place. Three pipeline setbacks came in one week: pelacarsen (a cholesterol-type heart drug that lowers Lp(a)), del-desiran (the main asset from the ~$12B Avidity deal) and a safety pause on the cell therapy rap-cel. The US line fell 13.93% on September 8, and a major shareholder publicly called for board changes. The market is no longer asking whether the launches can replace Entresto. It is asking whether the next wave, which is the one that was bought through acquisitions, is worth what was paid for it.

For a pure-play drug company past a patent cliff, the two numbers to watch are core operating margin (how much of each sales dollar survives R&D and launch spending) and the gap between growth brands and generic erosion.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Patent-protected franchises Radioligand manufacturing (Pluvicto) Global commercial scale 40% core margin funds R&D $16.8B TTM free cash flow RNA / gene / cell platforms

A drug company's moat is temporary by construction. Each patent is a lease with an expiry date, and the durable asset is the ability to keep replacing expiring leases with new ones. Novartis has shown the commercial half of that clearly. Kisqali, Kesimpta and Pluvicto turned into multi-billion-dollar brands, and the company kept a ~40% core margin while Entresto went generic. Radioligand therapy also has a real manufacturing barrier: the radioactive payload decays within days, so supply depends on a network of specialised sites near patients. The weak half is what September 2026 exposed: the replacement pipeline itself, and in particular the part bought through acquisitions.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Acquired pipeline has failed its first big tests. Del-desiran was the main asset from the ~$12B Avidity deal, and it failed Phase 3. Pelacarsen failed its cardiovascular-outcomes trial. Rap-cel cell-therapy trials were paused after patient deaths. Reuters reported investors stepping up scrutiny of Novartis's dealmaking, and a major shareholder called for board changes. A company that buys its growth is only as good as its record at picking deals, and that record has just been marked down in public.
02
More erosion is already in the numbers. Besides Entresto (−51%), the older brands Promacta/Revolade (−65%) and Tasigna (−58%) fell sharply in Q2 2026. Generics took 14 points off Q2 sales growth. The 2026 guide already assumes core operating income falls.
03
Price pressure. Pricing took 3 points off Q2 2026 sales. US drug-pricing policy affects all large pharma, and the Q2 price drag shows it already reaching Novartis's reported numbers.
04
Balance-sheet capacity is thinner. Debt/equity is 1.17 and the current ratio 0.86 (StockAnalysis). Net debt is $39.4B, with $5.6B of the $10B buyback still to run. The balance sheet is not stretched, since TTM free cash flow is $16.8B. But the room for another large deal to fix the pipeline is smaller than it was in 2025, and shareholder patience for one is lower.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, core vs IFRS: Novartis guides and is valued on core numbers, which leave out amortisation of acquired intangibles and impairments. FY2025 core EPS was $8.98 vs IFRS $7.21, and Q2 2026 was $2.41 vs $1.71. The gap will widen as Avidity amortisation starts, and failed Avidity assets are a candidate for impairment charges in coming quarters. That last point is our inference, not something the company has disclosed. Second, portfolio reshaping, including the 2023 Sandoz spin-off, makes older IFRS EPS figures unusable for comparison. StockAnalysis shows diluted EPS of $10.63 in FY2021 and $7.10 in FY2023, against $3.17 in FY2022 (net income $24.0B in FY2021 vs $7.0B in FY2022), which points to large one-off gains in those years. Third, buybacks: 77.6M shares were repurchased for $8.9B in 2025 and 18.2M for $2.8B in H1 2026, and the share count is down 3.34% YoY. Per-share growth therefore runs ahead of absolute profit growth.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

How the series treats the Sandoz spin-off: the chart uses unadjusted closes. digrin's "Real price" column matches Yahoo's monthly closes to the cent and the company's own year-end ADR prices ($100.97 for 2023, $97.31 for 2024, $137.87 for 2025). The October 2023 spin-off is therefore not back-adjusted. When Sandoz shares were distributed, their value left the NVS price, and it shows as part of the −8.1% step from $101.86 (Sep 2023) to $93.58 (Oct 2023). That step is a distribution to holders, not a loss to them. The spin ratio and the value of the Sandoz shares received were not sourced this session, so the size of the adjustment is not given. The practical consequence: the chart understates the five-year return to a holder who kept their Sandoz shares.

On price alone, NVS went from $81.78 (September 2021 close) to $140.98 (September 21, 2026), +72.4%, before dividends and before the Sandoz distribution. The path is a slow 2021–2023 base below $105, a 2024–early-2026 re-rating as the pure-play story and the growth brands came through, a peak month-end close of $168.62 in February 2026 (6-month RSI 89.0, the most overbought reading in the window), and then a 16% slide to today, most of it on September 8. The 6-month RSI is now 43.2 and the 10-month moving average about $151.19. The price is below its long-term trend for the first time since 2024.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The Entresto cliff is being absorbed rather than feared. Entresto halved in Q2 2026, yet group sales still grew 1% at cc and the core margin held at 41.2%, because Kisqali (+43%), Kesimpta (+32%), Pluvicto (+43%), Scemblix (+89%) and Leqvio (+59%) are already bigger than the drug they replace. Management points to mid-single-digit growth in H2 2026 as the Entresto comparison annualises, and it keeps a 5–6% sales CAGR for 2025–2030. At ~16x forward core EPS, with $16.8B of free cash flow, a 2.2% dividend and $5.6B of buyback left, the September sell-off priced in two failed trials that Barclays had risk-weighted anyway. HSBC upgraded on exactly that logic. It is a cheap entry into a 40%-margin franchise.

Bear Case

The commercial engine is fine. The capital-allocation engine is what failed. Novartis added ~$17B of net debt in six months, mostly for Avidity, and the lead Avidity asset has failed Phase 3. The cholesterol-type heart programme meant to succeed Entresto has failed too, and a cell therapy is on safety hold. 2026 core operating income is guided down, and consensus core EPS falls from $8.98 to $8.83. IFRS earnings will carry Avidity amortisation and possibly impairments. When a major shareholder publicly asks for board changes, the market is being told the deal process is in question, and the 5–6% CAGR assumed the pipeline that has just shrunk. A stock can look cheap because its growth is being rebuilt, or because it is shrinking. Until del-zota and the growth brands carry 2027, the market cannot tell which one this is.

Pipeline and M&A execution. Del-desiran (HARBOR), pelacarsen (HORIZON), the rap-cel pause and VHB937 (ALS) all failed or stalled in about three weeks. Any Avidity impairment or further readout failure would add to that record.

Industry Commentary
Buying the pipeline — and paying for misses
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Large pharma companies facing patent cliffs buy late-stage assets to fill them, and they pay acquisition prices that assume a high chance of success. Novartis's September 2026 shows what happens when that probability does not come through. Reuters reported that investors would "step up scrutiny" of the company's dealmaking after setbacks to acquired drugs. The headline "$30 billion wipeout" refers to the market value lost on the del-desiran and pelacarsen days. The wider lesson is that the sector's main risk is no longer only patent expiries, which are known and dated. It is the replacement assets, which are uncertain and bought at full price.

Heart disease: from franchise to graveyard
ONGOING HEADWIND

A September 2026 WSJ piece carried the headline "Heart Disease, a Historic Strength for Big Pharma, Becomes a Weakness." Cardiovascular outcomes trials are huge, slow and expensive, and big drugs in this area go generic at deep discounts, as generic Entresto did at a reported ~90% off list. Pelacarsen's failure also resets the race to lower Lp(a), in which Eli Lilly is developing a competing therapy. For Novartis this area now rests on Leqvio ($480M in Q2, +59%).

New treatment platforms — radioligand, RNA, cell and gene
EMERGING TAILWIND

Novartis has bet on new ways of delivering drugs: radioligand therapy (Pluvicto, $651M in Q2 and +43%), gene therapy (Zolgensma, +20%), RNA drugs attached to antibodies (the Avidity platform) and cell therapy. The bull framing: Pluvicto shows a platform can become a multi-billion-dollar franchise with real manufacturing barriers, and one failed drug does not discredit a platform. The bear framing: the platforms that have paid off are the older ones, while the newest (Avidity RNA, rap-cel cell therapy) are the ones that just stumbled, and platforms are bought at a premium to their first product. How to judge it: del-zota's FDA path and Pluvicto's growth rate over the next four quarters.
