01 Company Overview

Most people hear "News Corp" and picture a newspaper. The company that trades under NWSA is closer to a holding company with four roughly equal legs: Dow Jones (The Wall Street Journal, Barron's, MarketWatch, plus risk-and-compliance and energy data sold to businesses), Digital Real Estate Services (a majority stake in Australia's REA Group and the US site Realtor.com), Book Publishing (HarperCollins), and News Media (The Times and The Sun in the UK, the New York Post, and the Australian newspapers). Fiscal 2026 — the year to June 30, 2026 — produced revenue of $9.03B, up 7%, and Total Segment EBITDA of $1.63B, up 15%. The first quarter of fiscal 2027, the three months to September 30, 2026, brought revenue of $2.28B, up 6%, and Total Segment EBITDA of $403M, up 19%. The company called it a record first quarter.

The company exists in its current form because of a split. On June 28, 2013 the original News Corporation renamed itself 21st Century Fox and spun off the publishing assets as the "new" News Corp, which took the old company's S&P 500 seat the same day. It lists two share classes on Nasdaq: Class A (NWSA), the larger line and the subject of this report, and Class B (NWS), the voting line through which the Murdoch family controls the company. Both are S&P 500 index lines — the second was added in September 2015 — and they share one set of accounts, so every business figure here applies to both. At the November 6 close the voting B shares cost 9.7% more than the A shares. That gap is the market's price for a vote.

The story the market keeps coming back to is a recombination. The Murdochs split News Corp from Fox, tried to put them back together in 2022, and dropped the idea in January 2023. Court documents from the family succession case, reported on August 27, 2026, show the family discussed it; Fox said afterwards that the references concerned a merger considered in 2022 and that there have been no merger discussions since. The family trust settlement of September 8, 2025 left LGC Holdco, a holding company owned equally by trusts for Lachlan, Grace and Chloe Murdoch, with 33% of News Corp's Class B shares. NWSA holders get the economics but, outside a few limited circumstances, no vote, so any deal will be decided by other people.

The November 4 results beat expectations and the stock fell anyway. Adjusted earnings per share were $0.28 against a Nasdaq consensus of $0.26. The shares closed at $30.56 before the release, $26.48 on November 5, a fall of 13.35%, and $26.39 on November 6. REA Group, the Australian property business News Corp controls, rose 11% in Sydney on November 5 after its own quarterly update. The soft spots in News Corp's numbers were free cash flow, which went from $4M a year earlier to negative $89M; News Media, where segment EBITDA fell 40%; and Realtor.com, where users fell 8% and leads 11%. On the call the CFO also said REA's October listings had dropped 13%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

WSJ / Barron's brands 6.9M paying news subscribers REA's Australian listings position HarperCollins backlist Proprietary archives for AI licensing Low net debt ($1.78B cash vs $2.01B borrowings)

The moat is content that people pay for and that nobody else owns. The WSJ subscriber base, the HarperCollins backlist and REA's listings network all took decades to build and none can be copied. The AI licensing strategy rests on the same asset: News Corp has signed partnerships with OpenAI and Meta and is suing Perplexity and Brave over alleged unauthorised use of its content, and HarperCollins stands to receive part of Anthropic's book-copyright settlement. Where the moat is weaker is the ad-funded tabloids in News Media, which have large audiences but thin margins, and Realtor.com, which is growing revenue while losing users.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
You own the economics, not the votes. NWSA holders do not control the company. LGC Holdco, the family holding company set up in the September 2025 settlement, held 33% of the Class B stock at that time, and court documents reported in August 2026 show the family has discussed recombining with Fox as recently as 2022. If that comes back, the terms will be set by people whose interests may not match yours.
02
The biggest profit engine depends on housing. Digital Real Estate made $741M of segment EBITDA, the largest of any segment. It depends on listing volumes in Australia and the US, both of which move with mortgage rates, and the CFO has already flagged that risk. REA's October listings were down 13%.
03
AI may buy the content or may copy it. Licensing deals are a new source of revenue, but the lawsuits against Perplexity and Brave show the other possibility: AI answers that use the reporting and pay nothing for it. If the AI companies win, News Media, with a 6% margin in FY2026 and 3% in the latest quarter, has little room to absorb lost traffic.
04
Reported earnings are hard to read. Minority interests in REA, discontinued Foxtel income in the prior year, and write-offs in publishing all distort the reported figures. For fiscal 2026, StockAnalysis shows EPS down 50% while the company reports continuing-operations EPS up 22.6%. Both are correct because they measure different things, and a reader who only sees the headline figure can easily misjudge the year.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, Foxtel: the Australian pay-TV business was classified as discontinued, and the prior-year Q4 included $690M of income from discontinued operations, per the results release. That explains why StockAnalysis shows FY2025 EPS of $2.07 and FY2026 EPS "down" 50%. FY2022 revenue of $10.39B is the figure reported in August 2022, which included $2,026M from the Foxtel segment (Subscription Video Services), so the −22.9% FY2023 revenue decline StockAnalysis shows is a change in presentation, not a collapse. Second, buybacks: News Corp repurchased $643M of stock in FY2026, versus $150M in FY2025. The CFO said this was helped by about $380M of Foxtel shareholder-loan repayments, so that pace may not be repeatable; in Q1 FY2027 it bought back another $166M (5.6M shares), against $92M a year earlier, with about $501M left on the program. Third, publishing write-offs: $16M of inventory and a $13M receivable, which explain HarperCollins' 3% full-year EBITDA decline. Reported Q1 FY2027 EPS of $0.29 also includes one-off gains and restructuring charges; the company's adjusted figure, which removes them, was $0.28. Still to come: HarperCollins's share of the Anthropic settlement, which the 10-Q says has not been recorded as a gain.

Over the same windows SPY gained 61.4% and 9.7%, and QQQ gained 66.6% and 7.3% (price returns, dividends excluded). The stock has lagged both benchmarks in both windows, even though Total Segment EBITDA rose 15% in fiscal 2026 and 19% in the latest quarter — the gap the bulls point to.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The market still prices this as a newspaper company. In the latest quarter Dow Jones grew EBITDA 11%, Risk & Compliance grew 11%, REA grew revenue 13% despite fewer listings, and Total Segment EBITDA rose 19% to $403M. Gross margin has risen four years running. Borrowings exceed cash by only about $235M, buybacks continued at $166M in the quarter with about $501M left on the program, and few investors are betting against the stock (short interest 3.08% of Class A at the Oct 15 settlement). AI licensing turns the archive into revenue, and HarperCollins stands to collect part of the Anthropic settlement. A Fox recombination could unlock value, and the Aug 27 report moved News Corp up, not down. At 19.5x forward earnings with consensus targets 38% higher, the valuation looks fair for a business whose profit mix keeps improving.

Bear Case

At 23.6x trailing EPS this is not cheap, and a large part of the profit belongs to REA's other shareholders. The strongest segment depends on housing: REA's October listings fell 13% (8% allowing for one fewer working day) and Realtor.com lost 8% of its users. Free cash flow was negative $89M in the first quarter and cash fell $318M to $1.78B. News Media's segment EBITDA fell 40% to $18M, and its audiences are shrinking: The Sun lost about a quarter of its monthly users in a year. The FY2026 buyback pace used about $380M of one-off Foxtel loan repayments, so it will not recur at that size. NWSA holders have no votes, so any recombination terms will be set by the family. The stock fell 13.35% the day after a quarter that beat on profit, so beating estimates was not enough.

Top 3 Risks

01
Governance and a recombination on the family's terms. The family holding company (33% of Class B) and the chairman decide. The 2022 plan to re-merge with Fox failed after investors balked (Reuters), and court records show the idea could come back.
02
Housing-cycle exposure in the largest profit segment. Digital Real Estate made $741M of EBITDA. A drop in Australian or US listings would hit the segment that has been driving group growth.
03
AI answers taking readers from news sites. Licensing deals and lawsuits are the defence. If AI summaries cut referral traffic faster than licensing revenue grows, News Media's thin margin (6% in FY2026, 3% in Q1 FY2027) has little room before it turns to a loss.
Industry Commentary
Publishers and AI: licence or lawsuit
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

News Corp is pursuing both options against AI companies at once. It licenses its content to some (OpenAI, Meta — of which Thomson said "These are not merely transactional arrangements. These are partnerships.") and sues others (Perplexity, Brave). The outcome will decide whether decades of journalism and a large book backlist become a licensing income stream or free training data. The company's public position, per its CEO, is that without professional content AI output is "a slimy sea of AI slop." On the November 4 call he said talks with AI platforms "are progressing well."

Two share classes, one family
STRUCTURAL

The S&P 500 holds both of News Corp's lines, NWSA and NWS, and splits the company's index weight between them. The Class B line closed at $28.96 against $26.39 for Class A on November 6, 2026, a 9.7% premium for the vote. The premium reflects the market's view on control events: a recombination, a buyout or another trust dispute would be decided by Class B holders.

Sum of the parts
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull argument is that Dow Jones' professional-data lines, a majority stake in a listed Australian property portal and a large publisher are each worth more to a specialist buyer than they are inside a conglomerate run by a family. The bear response is that the discount exists precisely because of that control structure, and it will stay unless the family chooses to break up or sell. That makes governance, not earnings, the factor most likely to move the stock.
