01 Company Overview

ONEOK gathers, processes, fractionates, transports and stores natural gas and NGLs (natural gas liquids — the ethane, propane, butane and natural gasoline that come out of the gas stream and get sold separately as petrochemical feedstock, heating fuel and blending components), and, since 2023, also ships refined products and crude oil. FY2025 revenue was $33.63B, up 55.0% from $21.70B in FY2024, and revenue over the twelve months to September 2026 was $43.19B, up 36.8%. Revenue here mostly tracks the price of the commodities ONEOK buys and resells, so the better gauge is adjusted EBITDA, which rose 6% in the third quarter of 2026. The company is worth $60.41B on the stock market (630.45M shares at $95.82).

The central debate is not whether the assets are good. It is whether the acquisitions pay off faster than the debt that bought them. Between 2023 and 2025 ONEOK bought Magellan Midstream, Medallion Midstream, and EnLink Midstream together with Global Infrastructure Partners' interests in it; on October 6, 2026 it closed a fifth deal, Brazos Midstream's Permian Midland Basin assets, for about $4.6 billion including closing adjustments. To pay for it without issuing shares, it sold Apollo funds a $9 billion stake in a new holding subsidiary and used the money to extinguish $5.46 billion of debt in September. At September 30 total debt was $28.52B against $186M of cash, about 3.3× the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance (before the Brazos payment, part of which was borrowed). On the third-quarter call the CFO set the target at about 3.25× or lower, with 3.5× now the upper end of the range. S&P Global Ratings and Fitch both upgraded ONEOK to BBB+.

Structure — This Matters at Tax Time
C-corp, not an MLP
ONEOK is a corporation, not a master limited partnership — so it issues a 1099, not a K-1. In plain terms: your dividends arrive on a form your tax software already understands, in January, with no partnership return, no state filings in every state a pipeline crosses, and no unrelated business taxable income problem inside an IRA. Since September 10, 2026 the listed company is a new holding company of the same name: each old share became one new share, the ticker stayed OKE, and the 8-K says the swap was meant to be tax-free. Apollo's $9 billion sits one level down, in nonvoting units of a subsidiary, not in OKE shares.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INTEGRATED WELLHEAD-TO-GULF NGL CHAIN FEE-BASED CONTRACTS IRREPLACEABLE RIGHTS-OF-WAY FOUR-SEGMENT DIVERSIFICATION LONG-DATED ACREAGE DEDICATIONS GULF COAST EXPORT ACCESS C-CORP INDEX ELIGIBILITY

The core advantage is vertical integration along a single molecule's journey. A barrel of raw NGL that ONEOK gathers in the Williston basin can be processed in an ONEOK plant, moved on an ONEOK pipeline, fractionated at an ONEOK Gulf Coast facility and exported off an ONEOK dock — and the company earns a fee at every one of those handoffs. Competitors who own one link have to negotiate with owners of the others; ONEOK negotiates with itself. That is why the company can profitably take third-party plant volumes onto its system when regional price gaps shift, as it did with up to 100,000 barrels per day out of the Permian in Q2 2026 (Q2 call). Layered on top is the plainest moat in infrastructure: a new interstate NGL pipeline across the American interior takes years to permit and build, so the existing pipe keeps its value. The Brazos assets, closed October 6, 2026, come with roughly 600,000 dedicated acres under fixed-fee contracts with more than 12 years left on average — moats in this business are written on paper as much as laid in steel.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Leverage is better, not finished. Total debt of $28.52B against $186M of cash at September 30 gives net debt of about $28.33B, roughly 3.3× the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance. That is before the October 6 Brazos payment, part of which was funded with short-term borrowings (10-Q), and it leaves out the $9.14B Apollo stake, which the balance sheet carries as a noncontrolling interest (the part of a subsidiary owned by outsiders), not as debt. The new target is about 3.25× or lower. The interest bill is a first claim on cash flow that ranks ahead of the dividend.
▸
Integration risk compounds rather than resets. Magellan, Medallion, EnLink/GIP and now Brazos are four separate integrations running concurrently across different asset classes — refined products, crude gathering, intrastate gas and Permian processing. ONEOK's 2026 guidance counts on about $150 million of new acquisition synergies on top of the $475 million it says it achieved through 2025 (February 2026 release), and the Brazos price assumes about $80 million more.
▸
Contract rollovers and fee resets. Management said in Q2 that Rocky Mountain volumes grew despite contract rollovers, and the nine-month 2026 NGL results show $76M lost to lower average fee rates and narrower price differentials in the Gulf Coast/Permian and Mid-Continent regions. Volume growth does not automatically become margin growth when the fee per barrel is being reset downward.
▸
The Apollo structure is debt wearing an equity costume. The $9 billion Apollo investment, closed September 10, 2026, is a nonvoting Class B interest with a capped return — 7.0% IRR (internal rate of return: the yearly return on the money invested) for nine years, stepping to 7.35% in year 10 and 7.85% in year 15 (August 30 announcement). A capped, contractually escalating return on a balance ONEOK can repurchase is economically closer to preferred stock than to common equity, even though it keeps common shares undiluted. In its first 20 days it was already allocated $33M of net income (Q3 release).

08 Summary & Key Risks

What three years of acquisitions actually bought. Start with the segment table, because it is the whole argument. In the first nine months of 2026, ONEOK's four segments earned $6,330M of adjusted EBITDA: $2,083M from Natural Gas Liquids (32.9%), $1,804M from Refined Products & Crude (28.5%), $1,576M from Natural Gas Gathering & Processing (24.9%) and $867M from Natural Gas Pipelines (13.7%). Magellan brought the refined-products pipelines and terminals; Medallion brought Permian crude gathering; EnLink brought intrastate gas. What was a wellhead-to-Gulf NGL specialist is now a four-legged midstream company, and the third quarter shows why that matters: NGLs fell 4% and gathering and processing was flat, yet refined products rose 18% and pipelines 16%, and the company's adjusted EBITDA still grew 6%. That absorption is the acquisitions' real product. The caution is what did the absorbing: much of the gain in both growing segments came from marketing and trading margins (blending, crude marketing, the Waha-to-Katy gas price gap) rather than from fees, and management said the Waha-to-Katy gap narrowed after the second quarter.

Synergies: what the company says. In February 2026 ONEOK said it had achieved $475 million of cumulative acquisition-related synergies through the end of 2025, and that its 2026 guidance included about $150 million more of commercial and cost synergies (Q4 2025 release). Brazos brings its own figure: about $80 million of synergies included in the 7.5× 2027 EBITDA multiple (August 30 announcement). For a company whose equity story is acquisition integration, those are the numbers to hold the consolidated adjusted EBITDA line against.

Against the leverage. This edition's biggest change. The Apollo money arrived on September 10, and ONEOK used it to extinguish $5.46 billion of debt principal that month. Total debt at September 30 was $28.52 billion against $186 million of cash — net debt of about $28.33 billion, or roughly 3.3× the $8.55B midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance, down from the 3.9× this page computed at the last edition. S&P Global Ratings and Fitch upgraded the company to BBB+, and on the Q3 call the CFO lowered the target to about 3.25× or lower, with 3.5× now the ceiling rather than the goal. Two things keep this from being a clean win. The September 30 figure comes before the October 6 Brazos payment, part of which was borrowed. And the ratio leaves out the $9.14B Apollo stake, though it is owed a capped return that steps up over time.

But read the Apollo terms before celebrating. A 7.0% IRR cap for nine years, stepping to 7.35% in year 10 and 7.85% in year 15, on a declining capital balance that ONEOK may repurchase from year 8 or once it falls to $200 million, is not common equity in any economic sense. It is a fixed-cost, escalating, repayable claim. It removes debt from the leverage ratio without removing the cash obligation: in its first 20 days, September 10 to 30, the stake was allocated $33 million of net income (Q3 release). The plain way to describe the transaction: ONEOK swapped about $5.5 billion of debt for a capped-return, preferred-like instrument, and paid for most of the $4.6 billion Brazos purchase with the rest. Whether that is deleveraging depends on where you sit in the capital structure — and the common shareholder sits at the bottom of it.

Volumes and the export escape valve. Underneath the financial engineering, the physical business is doing well and unevenly. NGL raw feed throughput rose 15% in Q1, 7% in Q2 and 6% in Q3 year over year, and the composition tells the story: the Gulf Coast/Permian region was up 31%, 15% and 16%, helped in Q2 by up to 100,000 barrels per day of third-party plant volumes pulled onto ONEOK's system by the Waha-to-Katy gas price gap. The Rocky Mountain region grew despite contract rollovers, management said in Q2, and the nine-month NGL results show $76M lost to lower average fee rates and narrower price differentials. Permian gas processed rose only 4% in Q3: ONEOK is moving more barrels than its own plants produce, which is good for asset utilisation and less good for the wellhead-connected margin. Brazos and the Bighorn plant are the answer on the processing side. The relief valve on the other end is export: the 200,000 bpd LPG export terminal under construction reached its 80% contracting target (Q2 call), and the CFO listed the dock among 2027 projects on the Q3 call. The dock is where the extra barrel finds a buyer, and ONEOK has contracted four-fifths of its new dock capacity before it opens.

Valuation, and the tax point that actually matters to a taxable investor. OKE trades at 14.88× trailing earnings (16.2× without the one-time tax benefit) and 15.73× 2027 consensus earnings, against peer medians of 29.83× and 25.77×, and at 11.52× EV/EBITDA against a peer median of 15.72×. On trailing and forward P/E and on EV/EBITDA it is the cheapest of the four large midstream names. The PEG of 2.99 against a peer median of 1.48 explains why: the market does not expect much growth, and consensus agrees — 2027 EPS ($6.09) is forecast slightly below 2026's ($6.16) and 2027 revenue to fall 4.7%. The dividend is $4.28, a 4.47% yield, covered 2.19× by operating cash flow but only 0.94× by free cash flow after a capex programme now running at about $3.5B a year, at a 66.5% payout of GAAP earnings (about 72% without the tax benefit) — covered by earnings, not by cash left after investment. And here is the part general readers most often miss: because ONEOK is a corporation rather than a master limited partnership, that dividend arrives on a Form 1099, not a Schedule K-1. You get an ordinary dividend, reportable like any other, in January. You do not get a partnership return, you do not get state filing obligations in every state a pipeline crosses, you do not get a return-of-capital basis-tracking exercise that runs for a decade, and you do not get the unrelated-business-taxable-income complication that makes MLPs awkward inside an IRA. Enterprise Products and Energy Transfer own comparable assets and issue K-1s. For most taxable investors that single administrative difference is worth more than a modest yield gap — and it is also why OKE is index-eligible and MLPs generally are not.

Bull Case

The cheapest large midstream name on earnings (14.88× vs 29.83× peers) and on EV/EBITDA (11.52× vs 15.72×), with the highest yield (4.47%) of the four. Four diversified segments absorb single-segment weakness — Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA grew 6% while NGLs fell 4%. Guidance has been raised three times this year, to an $8.55B midpoint. The Apollo money has landed: $5.46B of debt retired, leverage down to about 3.3× from 3.9×, two rating upgrades to BBB+, a new target of 3.25× or lower, and Brazos's premier Midland assets bought at ~7.5× 2027 EBITDA (company estimate) without issuing a share. The 200,000 bpd LPG export terminal is already 80% contracted. It pays you in dividends on a 1099 while you wait.

Bear Case

The leverage fix is a $9B capped-return instrument that is preferred stock in all but name — 7.0% IRR rising to 7.85%, repayable — and the 3.3× ratio leaves it out. Brazos was partly paid for with new short-term borrowing. Free cash flow ($2.53B) no longer covers the $2.70B dividend bill as capex rises to about $3.5B. The headline Q3 beat leaned on a $0.53 one-time tax gain, and the growing segments leaned on marketing margins and a Waha-to-Katy price gap that is already narrowing. Four concurrent integrations, with $150 million of new synergies counted in 2026 guidance and $80 million more priced into Brazos; consensus has 2027 EPS slightly below 2026 and revenue down 4.7%. Thirteen of twenty-two analysts are on Hold, short interest is the highest of the peer group at 3.75%, and the average target implies 6% upside.

The rest of the path from about 3.3× to 3.25× or lower runs through the borrowed part of the Brazos price and through 2027 EBITDA arriving as hoped, while capex rises to about $3.5B and free cash flow no longer covers the dividend. Acquisition fatigue and integration overlap. Four large deals since 2023 across four different asset classes, with about $150 million of new synergies built into 2026 guidance and about $80 million more priced into Brazos. If the Magellan and EnLink integrations are still consuming management attention as Brazos is folded in, the promised accretion arrives late — and the market has already shown, in May 2023, how it reacts to an ONEOK acquisition it does not like.
03
Volume growth that does not convert to margin. Permian gas processed grew 4% in Q3 while Gulf Coast/Permian NGL throughput grew 16%, much of the difference coming from third-party plants, and part of 2026's earnings growth came from a Waha-to-Katy price gap that management says is already narrowing as new pipelines open. Meanwhile average NGL fees are lower ($76M over nine months). A business moving more barrels at a declining fee per barrel can post rising volumes and flat earnings for years — and with free cash flow already below the dividend bill, there is not a lot of slack if it does.
  Industry Commentary
Midstream Has Run Out of Organic Growth and Is Buying It
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Consolidation is the sector's default growth strategy, and ONEOK has used it repeatedly: Brazos, announced August 30 and closed October 6, 2026, is its fourth large acquisition since 2023, after Magellan, Medallion and EnLink. The reason is structural — new interstate pipelines take years to permit and build, so the fastest way to add route miles is to buy someone who already has them. That makes existing rights-of-way more valuable, which is the moat. It also means large midstream balance sheets carry acquisition debt, and investors hear the same deleveraging story from several companies at once. ONEOK's version now has numbers behind it: about 3.3× at September 30 and a target of 3.25× or lower.
Private Capital Is Rewriting How Infrastructure Is Financed
ONGOING HEADWIND
The Apollo transaction is the story here, and it is bigger than ONEOK. A $9 billion nonvoting Class B interest with a 7.0% IRR cap let a public company raise a sum equal to about 15% of its market value without issuing a share or adding a dollar of rated debt — and both S&P Global Ratings and Fitch upgraded ONEOK to BBB+ afterwards. Apollo gets a steady capped return; ONEOK gets a leverage ratio inside its target range. Both parties are better off on their own metrics — but the instrument's economic substance is a capped, escalating, repayable claim, and the common shareholder is the residual behind it. Expect this structure to proliferate across capital-hungry infrastructure, and expect the sector's headline leverage ratios to become progressively less comparable as it does.
The Marginal NGL Barrel Now Clears at a Dock, Not a Cracker
EMERGING TAILWIND
US NGL production keeps rising as gas comes up alongside oil from Permian wells whether or not anyone wants the propane. The outlet is export, and Gulf Coast dock capacity is the bottleneck. ONEOK's 200,000 bpd LPG export terminal reached its 80% contracting target before entering service (Q2 call), and Seabrook crude loadings set a record in May 2026. Bull framing: contracted export capacity is fee income with international demand behind it, arriving as Gulf Coast/Permian NGL volumes grow 15–31% a quarter year over year — and it is already sold. Bear framing: export economics depend on international arbitrage spreads and on shipping capacity, both outside ONEOK's control, and the same logic has every US midstream operator building docks at once. Contracted capacity protects the first 80%; the incremental barrel still has to find a buyer.
