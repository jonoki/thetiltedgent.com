01 Company Overview

ONEOK gathers, processes, fractionates, transports and stores natural gas and NGLs (natural gas liquids — the ethane, propane, butane and natural gasoline that come out of the gas stream and get sold separately as petrochemical feedstock, heating fuel and blending components), and, since 2023, also ships refined products and crude oil. FY2025 revenue was $33.63B, up 55.0% from $21.70B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure is $39.37B, up 40.8%. Almost none of that growth is organic: it is the arithmetic of three years of acquisitions. The company ranks roughly #421 globally by market capitalisation at $60.16B.

The central debate is not whether the assets are good. It is synergy realisation versus leverage. Between 2023 and 2025 ONEOK bought Magellan Midstream, Medallion Midstream, and EnLink Midstream together with Global Infrastructure Partners' interests in it — and on August 30, 2026 it announced another $4.425 billion for Brazos Midstream's Permian Midland Basin assets. Total debt now stands at $33.02B against $161M of cash. Management's stated long-term target is 3.5× debt-to-EBITDA; the current run-rate is closer to 3.9×. Everything on this page turns on whether the acquired earnings arrive faster than the interest bill.

Structure — This Matters at Tax Time
C-corp, not an MLP
ONEOK is a corporation, not a master limited partnership — so it issues a 1099, not a K-1. In plain terms: your distributions arrive as ordinary qualified dividends on a form your tax software already understands, in January, with no partnership return, no state filings in every state a pipeline crosses, and no unrelated business taxable income problem inside an IRA. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INTEGRATED WELLHEAD-TO-GULF NGL CHAIN ~90% FEE-BASED (3RD-PARTY EST.) IRREPLACEABLE RIGHTS-OF-WAY FOUR-SEGMENT DIVERSIFICATION LONG-DATED ACREAGE DEDICATIONS GULF COAST EXPORT ACCESS C-CORP INDEX ELIGIBILITY

The core advantage is vertical integration along a single molecule's journey. A barrel of raw NGL that ONEOK gathers in the Williston basin can be processed in an ONEOK plant, moved on an ONEOK pipeline, fractionated at an ONEOK Gulf Coast facility and exported off an ONEOK dock — and the company earns a fee at every one of those handoffs. Competitors who own one link have to negotiate with owners of the others; ONEOK negotiates with itself. That is why a 7% increase in raw feed throughput translates into more than 7% of incremental margin, and why the company can profitably take third-party plant volumes onto its system when regional spreads shift, as it did with up to 100,000 barrels per day out of the Permian in Q2 2026. Layered on top is the plainest moat in infrastructure: nobody is getting a new interstate NGL pipeline permitted across the American interior on a reasonable timeline, so the existing pipe is worth more each year that fact stays true. The Brazos assets come with roughly 600,000 dedicated acres under contracts averaging 12+ years — moats in this business are written on paper as much as laid in steel.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Leverage is the constraint, and it is self-imposed. Total debt of $33.02B against $161M of cash gives net debt of about $32.86B — roughly 3.9× the midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance, against a stated target of 3.5×. Every acquisition that closes before the ratio comes down pushes the target further out, and the company has now announced four in three years. The interest bill on $33B is a first claim on cash flow that ranks ahead of the dividend.
▸
Integration risk compounds rather than resets. Magellan, Medallion, EnLink/GIP and now Brazos are four separate integrations running concurrently across different asset classes — refined products, crude gathering, intrastate gas and Permian processing. ONEOK's own Q1 and Q2 2026 releases fetched for this report did not restate synergy realisation figures at all, which is a disclosure gap on the single most important question about the strategy.
▸
Producer concentration and contract rollovers. The gathering and processing segment fell 4% year over year in Q1 2026, and management explicitly flagged that Rocky Mountain volumes grew "despite contract rollovers." Legacy Williston contracts signed in a tighter market are repricing as they renew. Volume growth does not automatically become margin growth when the fee per barrel is being reset downward.
▸
The Apollo structure is debt wearing an equity costume. The $9 billion Apollo investment is a nonvoting Class B interest with a capped return — 7.0% IRR for nine years, stepping to 7.35% in year 10 and 7.85% in year 15. A capped, contractually escalating return on a balance ONEOK can repurchase is economically closer to preferred debt than to common equity, and rating agencies and analysts will treat it accordingly even though it keeps common shares undiluted.

08 Summary & Key Risks

What three years of acquisitions actually bought. Start with the segment table, because it is the whole argument. In the first half of 2026, ONEOK earned $4,133M of adjusted EBITDA: $1,365M from Natural Gas Liquids (33.0%), $1,119M from Refined Products & Crude (27.1%), $1,013M from Natural Gas Gathering & Processing (24.5%) and $636M from Natural Gas Pipelines (15.4%). Three years ago the second-largest of those four did not exist. Magellan brought the refined-products pipelines and terminals; Medallion brought Permian crude gathering; EnLink brought intrastate gas. What was a wellhead-to-Gulf NGL specialist is now a four-legged midstream company where a bad quarter in gathering and processing — and Q1 2026 was a bad quarter, down 4% year over year — gets absorbed by a 60% jump in pipelines and a 4% gain in refined products. The company grew 13% that quarter anyway. That absorption is the acquisitions' real product, and it is worth more than any synergy number.

Synergy realisation: what is claimed, and what the company will actually say. This is where the analysis has to be careful. A third-party analysis of ONEOK cites $500 million of realised Magellan synergies with a further $150 million expected in 2026, and values the $5.9 billion EnLink and Medallion purchases at 6.3× 2025 estimated EBITDA. Those are plausible figures and they are consistent with the reported results — but ONEOK itself did not restate a single synergy figure in the Q1 2026 release, the Q2 2026 release, or the Q2 2026 earnings call transcript reviewed for this report. The only synergy number the company has put its own name to recently is the ~$80 million included in the Brazos 7.5× 2027 EBITDA multiple. For a company whose entire equity story is acquisition integration, that silence is itself information. It may mean the Magellan synergies are fully banked and no longer worth discussing; it may mean they are harder to isolate now that four asset bases are commingled. Either way, an investor is asked to infer synergy delivery from the consolidated EBITDA line rather than to verify it from a bridge. Treat the $500M/$150M figures as third-party estimates, not disclosure.

Against the leverage. The other side of the ledger is precise and company-sourced. Total debt is $33.02 billion against $161 million of cash — net debt of about $32.86 billion, or roughly 3.9× the $8.35B midpoint of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance. Management's stated long-term target, from the Q2 2026 call, is 3.5×. The gap between 3.9× and 3.5× is roughly $3.3 billion of debt, and until August 30 the only route to closing it was retained cash flow — which, after a $2.70B dividend and $2.7–3.2B of capex, is not much. Then came the Apollo structure: $9 billion of nonvoting Class B capital, $5 billion of it earmarked for debt repayment, with the first $2 billion tender offer launched the same day across 20 note series. Pro-forma 2027 leverage was guided to about 3.25× — through the target — with no common equity issued. If that lands as advertised, the balance-sheet objection that has capped this stock since November 2024 is resolved, and the twelve Hold ratings carrying $105–$106 targets become Buys.

But read the Apollo terms before celebrating. A 7.0% IRR cap for nine years, stepping to 7.35% in year 10 and 7.85% in year 15, on a declining capital balance that ONEOK may repurchase from year 8 or once it falls to $200 million, is not common equity in any economic sense. It is a fixed-cost, escalating, repayable claim that sits ahead of the common shareholder. It removes debt from the leverage ratio without removing the cash obligation from the cash flow statement. The honest way to describe the transaction is: ONEOK refinanced $5 billion of rated debt into a capped-return preferred-like instrument, and bought $4.425 billion of Permian assets with the rest. Whether that is deleveraging depends entirely on where you sit in the capital structure — and the common shareholder sits at the bottom of it.

Volumes and the export escape valve. Underneath the financial engineering, the physical business is doing well and unevenly. NGL raw feed throughput rose 15% in Q1 and 7% in Q2 year over year, but the composition tells the story: the Gulf Coast/Permian region was up 31% in Q1 and 15% in Q2, boosted by up to 100,000 barrels per day of third-party plant volumes pulled onto ONEOK's system by tighter Waha-to-Katy gas spreads. The Rocky Mountain region grew — but "despite contract rollovers," which is the company acknowledging that Williston fees are resetting lower as legacy contracts renew. The Mid-Continent participated in growth across all regions but was not separately quantified in the releases fetched. Meanwhile, gas processed rose only 5% and then 2%: ONEOK is moving more barrels than it is producing, which is good for asset utilisation and less good for the wellhead-connected margin. The relief valve is export. The 200,000 bpd LPG export terminal under construction has hit its 80% long-term contracting threshold, and Seabrook crude export throughput rose ~20% quarter over quarter with record May loadings. US NGL production has outgrown domestic petrochemical demand; the dock is where the marginal barrel clears, and ONEOK has now contracted four-fifths of its new dock capacity before it opens.

Valuation, and the tax point that actually matters to a taxable investor. OKE trades at 16.50× trailing and 15.46× forward earnings against a peer median of 27.72× and 25.67×, and at 12.13× EV/EBITDA against a peer median of 14.85×. On every conventional multiple it is the cheapest of the four large midstream names. The PEG of 2.80 against a peer median of 1.47 explains why: the market does not believe the growth is durable, and consensus agrees — 2027 revenue is forecast to fall 2.5% as the acquisitions annualise. The dividend is $4.28, a 4.49% yield, covered 1.08× by free cash flow and 2.28× by operating cash flow, at a 74% payout of GAAP earnings — comfortable, not luxurious, and dependent on the capex programme not overrunning. And here is the part general readers most often miss: because ONEOK is a corporation rather than a master limited partnership, that dividend arrives on a Form 1099, not a Schedule K-1. You get an ordinary dividend, reportable like any other, in January. You do not get a partnership return, you do not get state filing obligations in every state a pipeline crosses, you do not get a return-of-capital basis-tracking exercise that runs for a decade, and you do not get the unrelated-business-taxable-income complication that makes MLPs awkward inside an IRA. Enterprise Products and Energy Transfer own comparable assets and issue K-1s. For most taxable investors that single administrative difference is worth more than a modest yield gap — and it is also why OKE is index-eligible and MLPs generally are not.

Bull Case

The cheapest large midstream name on earnings (16.50× vs 27.72× peers) and on EV/EBITDA (12.13× vs 14.85×), with the highest yield (4.49%) and the best ROIC (8.41%) of the four. Four diversified segments now absorb single-segment weakness — Q1 2026 grew 13% company-wide despite gathering and processing falling 4%. Guidance has been raised twice this year to an $8.35B midpoint. The Apollo structure funds $4.425B of premier Permian assets at ~7.5× 2027 EBITDA falling to ~6.0× in 2028, repays $5B of debt, takes pro-forma 2027 leverage to ~3.25× — through the 3.5× target — and dilutes nobody. The 200,000 bpd LPG export terminal is already 80% contracted. It pays you in dividends on a 1099 while you wait.

Bear Case

$33.02B of debt at ~3.9× EBITDA against a 3.5× target, and the fix is a $9B capped-return instrument that is preferred debt in all but name — 7.0% IRR rising to 7.85%, repayable, senior to the common. Four concurrent integrations, and the company has stopped quantifying synergies entirely in its own releases. Growth is acquisition arithmetic: consensus has 2027 revenue falling 2.5%, and the PEG of 2.80 says the market has already worked this out. Gathering and processing went backwards in Q1 2026, Rocky Mountain fees are rolling over, and gas processed grew just 2% in Q2. Twelve of twenty-one analysts are on Hold, short interest is the highest of the peer group at 3.98%, and the average target implies 4% upside after a 42% run off the October 2025 low.

Top 3 Risks

01
Leverage does not come down as advertised. The entire deleveraging path from ~3.9× to ~3.25× runs through the Apollo transaction funding, the $5B debt repayment executing, and 2027 EBITDA arriving at guidance. Any of the three slipping leaves ONEOK carrying $33B+ of debt into a period when consensus expects revenue to decline. The Goldman Sachs $89 target — the only one below the current price — is that scenario priced.
02
Acquisition fatigue and integration overlap. Four deals in three years across four different asset classes, with no synergy realisation figures disclosed in either 2026 quarterly release. If the Magellan and EnLink integrations are still consuming management attention when Brazos closes in Q4 2026, the promised accretion arrives late — and the market has already shown, in May 2023, exactly how it reacts to an ONEOK acquisition it does not like.
03
Volume growth that does not convert to margin. Gas processed grew 2% in Q2 while NGL throughput grew 7%, much of the difference coming from third-party plants attracted by a temporary Waha-to-Katy spread. Spreads close. Meanwhile Rocky Mountain contracts are rolling over at lower fees. A business that is moving more barrels at a declining fee per barrel can post rising volumes and flat earnings for years — and at a 74% payout ratio there is not a lot of slack in the dividend if it does.
  Industry Commentary
Midstream Has Run Out of Organic Growth and Is Buying It
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Consolidation is now the sector's default growth strategy, and 2026 has been relentless: ONEOK's $4.425B Brazos deal announced August 30, and Williams closing its $5.5B Momentum Midstream acquisition. The reason is structural — you cannot permit a new interstate pipeline across the continental US on any commercially sensible timeline, so the only way to add route miles is to buy someone who already has them. That makes existing rights-of-way more valuable every year, which is the moat. It also means every large midstream balance sheet is carrying acquisition debt at the same time, and every one of them is telling investors the same deleveraging story. ONEOK's 3.9× is not an outlier in the sector; it is the sector.
Private Capital Is Rewriting How Infrastructure Is Financed
ONGOING HEADWIND
The Apollo transaction is the story here, and it is bigger than ONEOK. A $9 billion nonvoting Class B interest with a 7.0% IRR cap lets a public company raise a sum comparable to 15% of its market capitalisation without issuing a share or, on the face of it, adding a dollar of rated debt. Apollo gets an infrastructure-grade fixed return; ONEOK gets a leverage ratio that clears its target. Both parties are better off on their own metrics — but the instrument's economic substance is a capped, escalating, repayable senior claim, and the common shareholder is the residual behind it. Expect this structure to proliferate across capital-hungry infrastructure, and expect the sector's headline leverage ratios to become progressively less comparable as it does.
The Marginal NGL Barrel Now Clears at a Dock, Not a Cracker
EMERGING TAILWIND
US NGL production keeps rising as associated gas comes off Permian oil wells whether or not anyone wants the propane, and domestic petrochemical demand has not kept pace. The clearing mechanism is export, and Gulf Coast dock capacity is the binding constraint. ONEOK's 200,000 bpd LPG export terminal has already reached its 80% long-term contracting threshold before entering service, and Seabrook crude loadings set a record in May 2026. Bull framing: contracted export capacity is fee income with international demand behind it, arriving just as Permian volumes surge 15–31% — this is the highest-quality growth in the portfolio and it is already sold. Bear framing: export economics depend on international arbitrage spreads and on shipping capacity, both outside ONEOK's control, and the same logic has every US midstream operator building docks at once. Contracted capacity protects the first 80%; the incremental barrel still has to find a buyer.
