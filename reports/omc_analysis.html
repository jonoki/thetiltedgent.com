01 Company Overview

Omnicom is an advertising and marketing holding company: it owns the agencies that plan and buy media, make the ads, run the PR and stage the events for the world's biggest brands. On November 26, 2025 it closed its all-stock acquisition of The Interpublic Group (IPG) — 0.344 Omnicom shares for each IPG share — and became, by the trade press's count, the world's largest agency holding company, with pro forma combined revenue above $25 billion. Two days later IPG left the S&P 500 (replaced by Sandisk, effective before the open on November 28, 2025). Omnicom itself has been an S&P 500 constituent since December 31, 1997.

That merger rewrites every number in this report, so read them with the join in mind. Shares: legacy Omnicom holders came out owning about 60.6% of the combined company and IPG holders 39.4%; weighted diluted shares went from 194.9M in Q3 2025 to 275.4M in Q3 2026, and shares outstanding today are 272.85M after $3.2B of buybacks through September under the $5B programme. Revenue: the trailing-twelve-month figure is $24.76B (the last four quarters added together); Q3 2026 revenue of $6.42B was up 59.1% on the year, almost all of it acquired, in the third full quarter that includes IPG. The like-for-like number is 7.2% organic growth in core operations. Earnings: the deal came with $1.1B of repositioning charges, $186.7M of transaction costs and a $543.4M loss on planned disposals in Q4 2025, which is why GAAP trailing EPS (the last four quarters' diluted EPS added together) is just $1.78 and the trailing P/E looks like 45x while the Street's 2026 adjusted EPS estimate of $10.18 puts the forward multiple near 8x. The whole investment case sits in the gap between those two numbers.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Largest agency holding company Pooled media buying scale Acxiom identity data Omni data & tech platform Global network (7 regions) Embedded client relationships

The bet behind the IPG deal is that scale and data are now the moat. A holding company that buys more media than anyone else and can tie ads to identity data can promise clients measurable outcomes — the phrase Omnicom Media Group's CEO used on the Q2 call — which a stand-alone creative shop cannot. The EU cleared the deal unconditionally, citing competing networks including WPP, Dentsu, Publicis Groupe and Havas; that same list is the reminder that scale is not scarce in this industry. The vulnerability is that agency revenue is ultimately people selling time and judgement, and both clients and technology platforms have been squeezing what that is worth.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Integration is the product for now. Advertising revenue fell by a high-single-digit rate in Q2 (mid-single-digit in Q3) while brands were merged and eliminated, and the head of Omnicom Advertising announced his retirement on September 9. Account losses during an agency merger are the classic failure mode; the stock fell 3.5% that day.
02
The margin gain has to keep coming. Q3 adjusted EBITA margin on core operations was 18.2%, up 200 basis points on the year (call summary), after Q2 adjusted EBITDA missed consensus by 9.8% (StockStory). Management called the full-year outlook conservative and Q4 project spending hard to forecast (MarketBeat summary); the stock fell 3.3% between the October 22 close and the October 26 close. Synergies are a promise until they show up in margin.
03
Leverage and a thin GAAP cushion. Debt-to-equity is 1.07, the current ratio 0.91, and the Altman Z-score (a bankruptcy-risk score) 1.34. The $3.20 dividend is about 180% of trailing GAAP EPS — covered on adjusted earnings and free cash flow, not on reported profit.
04
A brutal pitch market. Management described competition for accounts as about as tough as it has ever been, and Middle East & Africa revenue declined by a double-digit rate in Q2 on regional conflict (a mid-single-digit decline in Q3). Scale helps win pitches; it also means more accounts to defend.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 8x the Street's 2026 adjusted EPS and 7.6x trailing free cash flow for the world's largest agency holding company, with a 4.01% dividend yield. Organic growth of 7.2% in Q3 is faster than the "agencies are dying" story allows, Integrated Media is growing over 10%, adjusted EBITA margin rose 200 basis points to 18.2%, $900M of cost synergies are on track for 2026, and $3.2B of the $5B buyback is done. Once the charges stop and the disposals finish, GAAP earnings converge on adjusted and the 45x trailing P/E stops scaring screens. The average analyst target of $101.17 is 27% above the price.

Bear Case

Adjusted EPS is management's number; GAAP EPS over the last four quarters is $1.78 and the dividend is about 180% of it. The company now carries $6.2B of net debt, a sub-1.0 current ratio and an Altman Z of 1.34. Advertising is still shrinking while brands are merged, the head of that division is retiring, the World Cup boost to Experiential ends in Q4, and consensus has 2027 revenue falling 3.2%. Almost 9% of the float was sold short at the Sep 30 settlement. The five-year chart says the market has doubted this model for a long time — and the IPG deal doubled the size of the bet rather than changing it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Integration and client retention. Merging two creative networks, eliminating brands and changing division leadership in the same year is when accounts walk. Advertising's mid-single-digit decline in Q3, better than the first half, is the early warning to watch.
02
Synergies that don't reach the margin. The $900M 2026 target has to show up as profit; Q3 adjusted EBITA margin of 18.2% is a good start, but management called Q4 project spending difficult to forecast (MarketBeat call summary). A miss in Q4 would mean the forward P/E is built on estimates that are too high.
03
Leverage in a cyclical business. Ad budgets are among the first things clients cut in a downturn. The combined company carries $6.22B of net debt after $3.2B of buybacks this year, with a dividend that GAAP earnings do not yet cover.
Industry Commentary
Consolidation as a survival strategy
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The trade press framed the IPG close as agencies that "chase scale to revive growth." Technology and AI have pressured traditional agency work, and the holding companies' answer has been to get bigger and own more data. Omnicom's version is Omni plus Acxiom plus the largest pooled media buy in the industry. The EU found no competition problem precisely because WPP, Dentsu, Publicis Groupe and Havas remain — the industry is concentrating, not cornering.

AI: cost lever or price deflator
ONGOING HEADWIND

Omnicom is selling "agentic marketing" — AI agents that plan and run cross-channel work on its data. Its CTO put the pitch as "agentic workflows drive both efficiency and effectiveness for clients." The same efficiency that fattens Omnicom's margin can shrink what clients are willing to pay per campaign. For now the evidence is mixed: media is growing double digits while creative advertising shrinks.

The valuation gap
EMERGING TAILWIND

The market prices OMC at about 8x forward adjusted earnings and 7.05x EV/EBITDA, with a 4.01% yield. The bull framing: this is merger-noise pricing — GAAP charges, disposals and a restructuring overhang that fade within a year, leaving a cash-generative leader bought back at single-digit multiples. The bear framing: the discount is the market pricing a structurally shrinking creative business that just got much bigger and more leveraged. The way to settle it is not the P/E; it is organic growth holding near 5% while synergies reach the margin, quarter after quarter.
