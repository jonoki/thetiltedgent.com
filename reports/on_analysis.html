01 Company Overview

onsemi — legally ON Semiconductor Corporation — is a Scottsdale, Arizona analog and power semiconductor manufacturer that spun out of Motorola in 1992 and now sells what it calls "intelligent sensing and power solutions" into automotive, industrial, AI data-centre, computing and robotics end markets. It reports in three segments: Power Solutions Group, Analog & Mixed-Signal Group and Intelligent Sensing Group — verified against the company profile and the Q2 2026 release, so the segment structure named in the brief for this report is correct. FY2025 revenue was $5.995B, down 15.35% from $7.082B in FY2024 — the second consecutive year of double-digit decline. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through the July 3, 2026 quarter is $6.198B, and market capitalisation of roughly $27.3B places onsemi around #870 globally (companiesmarketcap ranks it #842 at the higher Sep 11 close).

Nasdaq-100 membership: verified NO. The brief asked this to be checked. ON trades on Nasdaq but is not a constituent of the Nasdaq-100 — it does not appear in the 102-ticker constituent list on stockanalysis.com. It is an S&P 500 member, which is why the benchmark column and the relative-performance discussion on this page use the S&P 500 rather than the NDX.

The central debate is a cyclical one dressed up as a structural one. onsemi bet the company on silicon carbide (SiC — a wide-bandgap semiconductor that switches high voltages with lower losses than silicon, used in EV traction inverters, on-board chargers and now AI data-centre power) and on the automotive and industrial end markets that consume it. Both of those markets then went into a deep inventory-led downcycle: revenue fell from $8.33B in FY2022 to $6.00B in FY2025, and GAAP gross margin fell from 49.26% to 38.32%. The Q1 2026 quarter printed a GAAP net loss of $33.4M. Since then the numbers have turned up: revenue is growing year-over-year again, utilisation has moved from 77% to 83%, inventory days are falling, and management guides Q3 gross margin to 40–42%. This page does not take a view on whether the cycle has turned. It lays out the four numbers that will tell you — end-market mix, utilisation, the fab right-sizing programme, and inventory days — and states, for each, what the bull and the bear would each need to see next.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Internal SiC supply chain Automotive design-win lock-in Owned 200mm fab base Long-term supply agreements Qualified auto-grade catalogue Fab Right cost programme Power + sensing + compute bundle

The durable advantage is vertical integration in silicon carbide combined with automotive qualification. onsemi runs SiC from substrate through device and module internally, which is unusual — most power-semi competitors buy substrates from merchant suppliers. That matters in two directions: in a shortage it guarantees supply, and in a glut it means the company owns the fixed cost of the underutilised capacity. The second half of that sentence is exactly why gross margin fell from 49.26% in FY2022 to 38.32% in FY2025 and why the utilisation number below is the single most important operating metric on this page. The automotive side of the moat is more conventional and more durable: a traction-inverter SiC module designed into a vehicle platform stays there for the life of the platform, typically five to seven years, and requires re-qualification to displace. That is why PSG can grow 19% year-over-year while the catalogue analog business shrinks — design wins booked in 2022–2023 are ramping now, largely independent of current order patterns.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Operating leverage cuts both ways. A company that owns its fabs converts volume declines into margin declines at a brutal rate. Q1 2025 gross margin was 20.32% — a number that reflects underloaded fabs plus charges, not a change in pricing power. The same leverage is now working in reverse, which is why Q3 guidance calls for a "step-function" 40–42% gross margin on only a modest revenue increase. The vulnerability is that nothing about this is structural: a second demand air pocket would take the margin straight back down.
02
Automotive and Chinese EV concentration. Automotive is 48.7% of revenue, and the fastest-growing piece of the SiC story is explicitly China's EV market, where management guides to 60–70% year-over-year growth. That concentrates the recovery in the single end market most exposed to EV subsidy policy, tariffs, export controls and domestic Chinese competition from vertically integrated local power-semi suppliers.
03
Integration risk on the largest deal in company history. The $7B all-stock Synaptics acquisition, announced June 25, 2026, is not expected to close until mid-2027, with $200M of cost synergies targeted within 18 months of closing and accretion "within 18 months." That is a long window of execution risk, dilution of roughly 12% to existing shareholders, and a strategic pivot into embedded compute and connectivity — adjacent to, but not the same as, power and sensing. The market's initial reaction was to sell the stock sharply.
04
The inventory overhang is large in absolute terms. 192 days of inventory is a high number by any semiconductor standard, even allowing for the strategic bridge inventory onsemi built deliberately during the fab transitions. It fell 9 days sequentially, which is the right direction, but at that pace it is a multi-year normalisation — and every day of it is working capital that is not free cash flow.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Every operating metric the brief asked about is moving the right way at once: utilisation 77%→83%, inventory days −9 to 192, distributor weeks 10.8→10.1 with sell-through above sell-in, non-GAAP gross margin +80bp sequentially with a guided step to 40–42%, and free cash flow margin up from ~7% to 27% year-over-year. Revenue is growing again (+9.2% YoY) and the fastest-growing bucket — Other, at 24.9% of revenue and +34% sequentially — is AI data centre, a market that did not exist for onsemi two years ago. Three-quarters of the 200bp Fab Right margin benefit is still unbooked, and the forward P/E of 18.1 against a trailing 45.0 says the market has not yet paid for the recovery. Every one of 29 analyst targets sits above the price.

Bear Case

The recovery is narrow and the base is weak. PSG is doing all the growing; AMG, a third of revenue, is still shrinking year-over-year. Automotive — half the company — grew 4% sequentially-adjusted at best and actually fell 2% sequentially. 192 days of inventory is enormous; at −9 days a quarter it is years from normal, and the channel at 10.1 weeks is not clean. The margin story depends on utilisation that management itself guides only "flat to slightly up," meaning the guided 40–42% gross margin has to come from mix rather than volume. Meanwhile the company has committed to a $7B all-stock deal that dilutes holders ~12%, does not close until mid-2027, and pushes it outside its competence into embedded compute. 16 of 29 analysts rate it Hold, 7.49% of shares are short, and the stock has already given back 42% from its May high.

What each side would need to see next. For the bull: Q3 non-GAAP gross margin at or above 41% (the upper half of guidance) with utilisation confirmed at 83%+, inventory days below 185, distributor weeks below 10, and automotive revenue growing sequentially rather than falling. For the bear: gross margin landing at the 40% floor or below, utilisation flat, inventory days stalling in the high 180s, AMG still negative year-over-year in Q3, or any softening in the China EV SiC ramp that underwrites the 60–70% growth guide. Both sets of numbers are reported quarterly by the company. This page takes no view on which arrives.

Top 3 Risks

01
Utilisation reverses. The entire margin recovery is a volume-through-owned-fabs story. Q1 2025 showed what 20.32% gross margin looks like when the fabs are empty. A renewed automotive order cut would put the margin back there faster than the cost programme could offset it, and $35M of annualised Fab Right savings is immaterial against that swing.
02
The Synaptics deal breaks or disappoints. $7B all-stock, ~12% dilution, mid-2027 close, $200M of synergies promised 18 months after that. Regulatory delay, a financing-market shift, or integration slippage all land on a share count that has already been issued. The market's first reaction was a 21.6% monthly decline.
03
Valuation is carrying forward expectations, not current earnings. Trailing P/E of 45.0 on $1.56 of TTM EPS. Consensus needs EPS to reach $3.20 in FY2026 for the forward 18.1x to be real. Miss that and the stock is expensive on a 2.0 beta with 7.49% short interest and 16 of 29 analysts already at Hold — a combination that historically produces violent repricings in both directions, as April and June 2026 both demonstrated.
Industry Commentary
The automotive and industrial destocking cycle
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

This is the defining fact of onsemi's last three years. Revenue went $8.33B (FY2022) → $8.25B (FY2023) → $7.08B (FY2024) → $6.00B (FY2025), a 28% peak-to-trough decline, while gross margin went 49.26% → 47.06% → 45.41% → 38.32%. Quarterly year-over-year revenue growth bottomed at −22.4% in Q1 2025 and has since run −15.4%, −12.0%, −11.2%, +4.7%, +9.2%. The inflection is real and it is four quarters old at the bottom line, but the end markets themselves are still growing at low single digits: automotive +7% YoY and industrial +4% YoY in Q2 2026. Nothing in the disclosed data supports calling this a boom; it supports calling it the end of a bust. Distributor inventory at 10.1 weeks and company inventory at 192 days are the two numbers that say the channel has not yet finished clearing.

Silicon carbide finds a second end market in AI data centres
EMERGING TAILWIND

onsemi built SiC capacity for electric vehicles. The capacity arrived into an EV slowdown — and then a second buyer appeared. AI data centres are moving to higher-voltage DC power architectures to cut conversion losses at rack scale, and that creates demand for exactly the wide-bandgap switching devices onsemi already makes. Management guides AI data-centre SiC revenue up roughly 60% year-over-year in 2026 and total AI data-centre revenue to "more than double" this year, with the "Other" bucket that houses it up 34% sequentially to $400M and now 24.9% of company revenue. The strategic value is diversification: a second demand curve with a different cycle from automotive, absorbing the same fixed manufacturing base. The caveat is size — $400M a quarter against $781M of automotive, and against a fixed-cost base sized for the 2022 peak.

Fab Right: what the company can control while it waits
ONGOING HEADWIND

Fab Right is onsemi's programme to shrink and modernise its manufacturing footprint — in El-Khoury's words, "exiting subscale legacy operations to improve our cost structure." The concrete actions so far are the divestitures of the Mountain Top, Pennsylvania and Philippines facilities, worth roughly $35M in annualised savings starting in 2027 and fully realised in 2028. Management sizes that at about 50 of a planned 200 basis points of total gross-margin improvement from the programme. Read carefully, that is both the bull and the bear point in one sentence. Bull: three-quarters of the benefit is still to come and is largely within management's control regardless of demand. Bear: two years of announced restructuring has so far delivered a quarter of the target, none of it arriving before 2027, against a gross margin that fell more than 1,000 basis points from the FY2022 peak. Capex below 5% of revenue and Q3 capex of just $40–50M confirm the company is not building its way out — it is waiting for volume to fill what it already owns. Utilisation at 83%, up from 77%, is the only lever actually moving the margin today.
