01 Company Overview

onsemi — legally ON Semiconductor Corporation — is a Scottsdale, Arizona analog and power semiconductor manufacturer that spun out of Motorola in 1999 and now sells what it calls "intelligent sensing and power solutions" into automotive, industrial, AI data-centre, computing and robotics end markets. It reports in three segments: Power Solutions Group, Analog & Mixed-Signal Group and Intelligent Sensing Group — per the company profile and the Q3 2026 release. FY2025 revenue was $5.995B, down 15.35% from $7.082B in FY2024 — the second consecutive year of double-digit decline. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through the October 2, 2026 quarter is $6.37B, and market capitalisation of roughly $24.6B (389.28M shares × the $63.31 close) places onsemi #361 in the S&P 500.

Nasdaq-100 membership: no. ON trades on Nasdaq but is not in the Nasdaq-100, according to Nasdaq's own list of the index's companies. It is an S&P 500 member, which is why the benchmark column and the relative-performance discussion on this page use the S&P 500 rather than the NDX.

The central debate is a cyclical one dressed up as a structural one. onsemi bet the company on silicon carbide (SiC — a wide-bandgap semiconductor that switches high voltages with lower losses than silicon, used in EV traction inverters, on-board chargers and now AI data-centre power) and on the automotive and industrial end markets that consume it. Both of those markets then went into a deep inventory-led downcycle: revenue fell from $8.33B in FY2022 to $6.00B in FY2025, and GAAP gross margin fell from 49.26% to 38.32%. The Q1 2026 quarter printed a GAAP net loss of $33.4M. Since then the numbers have turned up: Q3 2026 revenue was $1,722.3M, up 11% on the year; non-GAAP gross margin reached 41.2%, inside the 40–42% the company had guided, and Q4 is guided to 41.5–43.5%. On the Q3 call, management put factory utilisation (the share of manufacturing capacity in use) at 83%, flat on the quarter, with inventory down six days to 186. The Synaptics acquisition has also changed shape: announced in June as a $7B all-stock deal, it was re-cut on October 1 to about $5.7B in cash. This page does not take a view on whether the cycle has turned. It lays out the four numbers that will tell you — end-market mix, utilisation, the fab right-sizing programme, and inventory days — and states, for each, what the bull and the bear would each need to see next.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Internal SiC supply chain Designed into car platforms Owned factory base Long-term supply agreements Qualified auto-grade catalogue Fab Right cost programme Power + sensing + compute bundle

The durable advantage is vertical integration in silicon carbide combined with automotive qualification. onsemi runs SiC from substrate through device and module internally. That matters in two directions: in a shortage it guarantees supply, and in a glut it means the company owns the fixed cost of the underutilised capacity. The second half of that sentence is exactly why gross margin fell from 49.26% in FY2022 to 38.32% in FY2025 and why the utilisation number below is the single most important operating metric on this page. The automotive side of the moat is more conventional and more durable: a traction-inverter SiC module designed into a vehicle platform stays there for the life of the platform and requires re-qualification to displace. PSG grew 21% year-over-year in Q3 while the catalogue analog business managed only +2%; on the Q3 call management pointed to power demand in AI data centres and industrial energy, while automotive revenue fell 1%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Fixed costs cut both ways. A company that owns its fabs sees every volume swing hit its margins hard, because the factories cost the same whether they are full or empty. Non-GAAP gross margin, which leaves out one-off charges, was 40.0% in Q1 2025, when GAAP gross margin was 20.32% because of those charges. The effect of fuller factories is now working in reverse: non-GAAP gross margin rose from 39.3% in Q2 to 41.2% in Q3, with utilisation flat at 83%, and Q4 is guided to 41.5–43.5%. The vulnerability is that nothing about this is permanent: a second sudden drop in demand would take the margin back down.
02
Automotive and Chinese EV concentration. Automotive was 45.1% of Q3 revenue, and on the Q3 call the CEO said China "remains a strong pull for silicon carbide". That ties part of the recovery to the end market most exposed to EV subsidy policy, tariffs, export controls and competition from Chinese power-chip makers.
03
Integration and financing risk on the largest deal in company history. The Synaptics acquisition was announced June 25, 2026 as a $7B all-stock deal and re-cut on October 1 to $123 a share in cash, about $5.7B. The amendment followed an unsolicited competing proposal from a third party. It removes the roughly 12% ownership Synaptics holders would have taken under the June terms, but onsemi held $4.37B of cash and short-term investments at October 2 (against about $4.46B of debt), so the purchase will use cash on hand plus fully committed debt financing from Morgan Stanley; the CFO said on the Q3 call that paying down debt will be the top priority after closing. Closing is expected by mid-2027; the US Federal Trade Commission has approved the deal and other regulators are still reviewing it. The company targets $200M of annual synergies and says the cash deal should add to non-GAAP earnings per share immediately after closing. That is a long stretch of execution risk and a move into embedded compute and connectivity — adjacent to, but not the same as, power and sensing. ON shares fell 23.7% on June 26, the first session after the original announcement.
04
The inventory overhang is large in absolute terms. At the end of Q3 onsemi held 186 days of inventory, down six days on the quarter. Strategic inventory, held deliberately, fell seven days; base inventory rose one day to support demand. That is the right direction, but at that pace getting to a normal level takes years — and every day of it is cash tied up in stock.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means buyers have dominated to an unusual degree ("overbought"); below 30 means sellers have ("oversold"). It describes momentum, not value — an overbought stock can keep rising and an oversold one can keep falling.

Over the window from the December 2021 close of $67.92 to the November 6, 2026 close of $63.31, ON returned −6.8% in price terms — roughly −1.4% a year, with no dividend. That number conceals almost everything interesting. The path ran to $107.75 in July 2023, fell 63% to $39.70 by April 2025, more than tripled to $120.62 in May 2026, then gave back 47% by November. A beta of 2.15 understates it.

The 10-month moving average (the average of the last ten month-end closes) tells the cleanest version of the story. Price closed below it in 19 of the 21 months from October 2023 to June 2025 — the automotive/industrial inventory-cut cycle — and above it every month from September 2025 to July 2026, and the average rose through the spring. In August 2026 price fell back below it and has stayed there. At the November 6 close, price at $63.31 sits below the 10-month average of $81.19 and below the 3-month average of $70.62: a stock that has fallen out of its uptrend. The 6-period monthly RSI reads 39.1 — leaning weak, having peaked at 90.0 in May 2026 (the most overbought monthly reading in the window) and bottomed at 16.2 in April 2025 (the most oversold).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The operating numbers keep moving the right way. Q3 non-GAAP gross margin was 41.2% (39.3% in Q2) against a Q4 guide of 41.5–43.5%; free cash flow was $488.6M, about 28% of revenue; revenue grew 11% and earnings per share more than 40%. All three segments grew year over year in Q3. AI data-centre revenue grew 175% on the year and the company raised its 2030 target for it to more than $3B. On the Q3 call: utilisation held at 83%, inventory days down 6 to 186, distributor weeks 10.1→9.9. Fab Right is due to add a small gross-margin benefit in 2027 and a larger one in 2028, and at 13.4 times FY2027 non-GAAP consensus the market has not yet paid for the recovery. The lowest published target ($75) sits above the Nov 6 close.

Bear Case

The recovery is narrower than the headline. PSG is growing 21%; AMG and ISG are growing 2% each. Automotive, 45% of Q3 revenue, fell 1% on the quarter and on the year, and management expects it to fall by a high-single-digit percentage in Q4. 186 days of inventory is enormous; at −6 days a quarter it is years from normal. Utilisation was flat at 83% in Q3 and the CFO expects similar levels in Q4, so the guided margin has to come from product mix rather than volume. The company has agreed to pay about $5.7B in cash for Synaptics — more than the $4.37B of cash and short-term investments it held on October 2 — for a push into embedded compute outside its power-and-sensing core, and the deal does not close until mid-2027. Buybacks fell to $19.9M in Q3 from $332M in Q2. On November 6 it dropped a planned Czech SiC expansion, citing the evolving global SiC market. 14 of 29 analysts rate it Hold, 7.8% of shares are sold short, and the stock sits 47.5% below its May close after an 11.55% one-day fall in the November 5 chip selloff.

What each side would need to see next. For the bull: Q4 non-GAAP gross margin at or above 42.5% (the midpoint of the 41.5–43.5% guide) on revenue at or above $1,765M (the midpoint of $1,715–1,815M), inventory days below 186, distributor weeks at or below 9.9, and automotive revenue falling less than the high-single-digit percentage management expects. For the bear: gross margin landing at the 41.5% floor or below, revenue under $1,715M, utilisation below 83%, inventory days stalling near 186, AMG slipping back to negative year-over-year, or any softening in silicon carbide demand from China. Both sets of numbers are reported quarterly by the company. This page takes no view on which arrives.

Top 3 Risks

01
Utilisation reverses. The margin recovery depends on fuller factories. A renewed automotive order cut would lower utilisation and the margin faster than the cost programme could offset it, and about $35M a year of Fab Right savings is small against that swing.
02
The Synaptics deal breaks or disappoints. About $5.7B in cash, a mid-2027 expected close, and $200M a year of synergies promised after that. Regulatory delay, a financing-market shift or integration slippage all land on a balance sheet that held $4.37B of cash and short-term investments against about $4.46B of debt on October 2. The first-session reaction to the June announcement was a 23.7% one-day fall.
03
Valuation is carrying forward expectations, not current earnings. Trailing P/E of 36.8 on $1.72 of TTM EPS. Consensus needs non-GAAP EPS to reach $3.29 in FY2026 for the forward 19.2x to be real. Miss that and the stock is expensive on a 2.15 beta with 7.8% short interest and 14 of 29 analysts already at Hold — a combination that produces violent repricings in both directions, as April, June and early November 2026 all showed.
Industry Commentary
The automotive and industrial destocking cycle
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

This is the defining fact of onsemi's last three years. Revenue went $8.33B (FY2022) → $8.25B (FY2023) → $7.08B (FY2024) → $6.00B (FY2025), a 28% peak-to-trough decline, while gross margin went 49.26% → 47.06% → 45.41% → 38.32%. Quarterly year-over-year revenue growth bottomed at −22.4% in Q1 2025 and has since run −15.4%, −12.0%, −11.2%, +4.7%, +9.2% and, in Q3 2026, +11%. The turn is real: three straight quarters of growth, though in Q3 automotive revenue fell 1% on the year while industrial grew 9%. Nothing in the disclosed data supports calling this a boom; it supports calling it the end of a bust. Company inventory at 186 days and distributor inventory at 9.9 weeks (both at the end of Q3) are the two numbers to watch as the clearing continues.

Silicon carbide finds a second end market in AI data centres
EMERGING TAILWIND

onsemi built SiC capacity for electric vehicles. The capacity arrived into an EV slowdown — and then a second buyer appeared. AI data centres are moving to higher-voltage DC power architectures to cut conversion losses at rack scale, and that creates demand for exactly the wide-bandgap switching devices onsemi already makes. In Q3 2026 AI data-centre revenue grew 39% on the quarter and 175% on the year to $180M, and the company raised its 2030 target for it by $500M to more than $3B; management expects about $620M from it in 2026. The strategic value is diversification: a second source of demand with a different cycle from automotive, using the same factories. The caveat is size: $180M in Q3 against $777M of automotive, and against a cost base sized for the 2022 peak.

Fab Right: what the company can control while it waits
ONGOING HEADWIND

Fab Right is onsemi's programme to shrink and modernise its manufacturing footprint, by exiting subscale legacy operations (as El-Khoury has put it). The concrete actions so far are the divestitures of the Mountain Top, Pennsylvania and Philippines facilities, worth roughly $35M a year in savings starting in 2027, as sized on the Q2 call. On the Q3 call the CFO counted 225 basis points from Fab Right on the path to the 53% long-term gross-margin target (a basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point), with a small benefit in 2027 and a larger one in 2028 as Mountain Top exits. Against that, GAAP gross margin fell more than 1,000 basis points from the FY2022 peak. On November 6 onsemi and the Czech government agreed to end a planned SiC expansion at Rožnov, and full-year capex is expected at about 2% of revenue (about 5% in 2027), so the company is not building its way out; it is waiting for volume to fill what it already owns. Utilisation, 83% in Q3, is the lever moving the margin.
