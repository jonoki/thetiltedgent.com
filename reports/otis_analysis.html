01 Company Overview

Otis makes, installs and — above all — services elevators, escalators and moving walkways in the United States, China and around the world. The company was founded in 1853 and spent decades inside United Technologies; on April 3, 2020 UTC spun off Otis and Carrier before merging with Raytheon, and Otis joined the S&P 500 the same morning, replacing Raytheon Co. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to June 30, 2026 was $14.91B, up 5.2%, with GAAP EPS of $3.89. At a $25.4B market value it ranks roughly #971 globally.

Here is the hand Otis is playing. Selling a new elevator is a thin, cyclical business: in Q2 2026 the New Equipment segment earned a 3.1% operating margin. Keeping that elevator running for the next thirty years is a very different business: Service earned 23.2%. Service is about two-thirds of revenue and roughly 94% of segment operating profit ($599M of $639M in Q2 2026). The market has long paid a premium multiple for that annuity. In 2026 it stopped paying: the stock closed February at $92.56 and settled on September 21, 2026 at $66.64 — down 28% in seven months and a hair above the 52-week low of $66.57 printed the same day. Twice this year management lowered its profit outlook while backlog grew. The question for the stock is whether the service annuity is being impaired (price pushback, churn, labour cost) or just temporarily taxed while China's new-build market shrinks and a modernization wave builds.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed-base service annuity Safety-code-mandated maintenance OEM parts & diagnostics Global field-technician network 173-year brand Modernization pull-through ROIC 65%

The moat is the installed base. An elevator gets sold once and serviced for decades, and the maker is best placed to keep that contract. That is why a company with 3% margins on new equipment earns a 65% return on invested capital overall (StockAnalysis ROIC 65.33%). Service needs little capital: TTM capex was $159M against $1.87B of operating cash flow. The weak spot showed up in 2026. The annuity protects the relationship, but not unlimited pricing power. When Otis pushed algorithm-driven price increases through maintenance contracts, retention outside China slipped and management backed off. It called the dip "a small number of basis points" tied to contract timing, per call coverage. Either way, the pricing lever has a limit.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Service margin is sliding while service revenue grows. Service organic sales rose 9% in Q2 2026, yet segment margin fell 170 bps to 23.2%. Adjusted operating profit for the company fell $25M even as sales rose 7%. If labour and material costs keep outrunning price, growth stops turning into profit, and that is the whole case for owning the stock.
02
China new-build is still shrinking. New Equipment sales in China fell more than 20% in Q1 2026 and by the high teens in Q2. China orders fell by the high teens in Q2. Fewer new units today means a smaller service portfolio later, so this drags on the annuity with a lag, not just on this year's New Equipment line.
03
Leverage and negative equity. Total debt is $8.85B against $813M of cash: net debt of $8.03B, 3.09x EBITDA. Book equity is −$5.45B, so return on equity and debt-to-equity are meaningless. Debt is manageable at 11.75x interest coverage. But the balance sheet leaves little room if the cash-flow guide keeps coming down while buybacks continue at about $800M in the first half of 2026 alone.
04
Guidance credibility. The FY2026 adjusted EPS outlook went from $4.20–$4.24 in April to $4.01–$4.05 in July. MarketBeat calls this the second cut of the year. A defensive compounder only earns its multiple if its guidance holds, and after two cuts the market is no longer taking it on faith.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $82.28 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $66.64, OTIS lost 19.0% of its price. Twenty quarterly dividends over the window add up to $7.12 per share. On digrin's dividend-adjusted series ($75.72 → $66.64) the total return is roughly −12%. For most of the period the stock did what a service compounder should: it rose from a $63.80 low in September 2022 to a $103.94 month-end high in September 2024, and held near $100 into March 2025. It has fallen in steps since. July 2025 lost 13.5%, when a China-driven cut to the sales outlook hit the shares. March 2026 lost 16.7%. And the post-Q2 2026 slide has taken it to within cents of a new 52-week low.

The 6-period monthly RSI now reads 21.8, the most oversold reading of the five-year series. That is below the September 2022 bear-market trough of 27.9 and below May 2026's 25.5. It has stayed under 30 since May 2026, five straight readings. The 10-month moving average, at about $77.25, sits 16% above the price. The long-term trend is down and the stock is trading below it. Deeply oversold readings like this can mark a floor, but the RSI says nothing about when; the October 28 print will matter more.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 17.1x trailing GAAP earnings and 16.5x this year's adjusted consensus, the lowest multiples in years, for a business where service is two-thirds of revenue and 94% of segment profit, returns 65% on invested capital, and yields 6.7% in free cash flow and 2.64% in dividends. Revenue is growing again (+7% in Q2, Service organic +9%), modernization backlog is up 26%, and consensus expects adjusted EPS to rise 12% in 2027 once the micro-pricing retreat and mechanic onboarding costs are behind it. Every analyst target in the S&P Global panel, including the $75 low, is above the price, and monthly RSI is the most oversold it has been in five years. If the service margin holds at Q3, 2026 will look like a one-year pause, not a broken franchise.

Bear Case

The bear case is not about sales. It is that the annuity has less pricing power than the multiple assumed. Otis pushed price through maintenance contracts, saw retention slip, and backed off, and the service margin fell 170 bps in a quarter when service revenue grew 9%. China, the largest new-build market, keeps shrinking, which slowly thins the service portfolio of the future. Guidance has been cut twice in one year, EPS will not grow in 2026, and the balance sheet carries $8.03B of net debt against negative book equity while buybacks continue. A 17x multiple is cheap for a compounder, but not obviously cheap for a flat-earnings industrial.

Top 3 Risks

01
Service margin erosion. 23.2% in Q2 2026, down 170 bps, after −160 bps in Q1. Service is 94% of segment operating profit, so every point of lost margin comes almost straight out of earnings.
02
China and the future service base. New Equipment sales in China fell more than 20% in Q1 2026 and by the high teens in Q2, with orders still falling. Units that are never installed never join the maintenance portfolio.
03
A third guidance cut. Two reductions in 2026 have already cost the stock its premium. Another at the October 28 print, against a balance sheet with 3.09x debt/EBITDA and negative equity, would leave little of the defensive-compounder case intact.
Industry Commentary
China's building slowdown and the new-equipment cycle
ONGOING HEADWIND

China has been a drag on Otis's New Equipment line for more than a year. Orders there fell more than 20% in Q2 2025, New Equipment sales fell more than 20% in Q1 2026, and both sales and orders fell by the high teens in Q2 2026. The effect on a service-led company comes with a delay. This year it shows up as lower New Equipment revenue and a 3% segment margin. Over the next decade it means fewer units entering the maintenance portfolio. One offset: per Q2 call coverage, China modernization orders more than doubled, partly on stimulus-funded projects. China's installed base is starting to need upgrades even while its new-build market shrinks.

Pricing the service contract — algorithm vs. retention
KEY DEBATE

Otis's 2026 lesson is about how far an installed-base monopoly can be pushed. AI-driven "micro-pricing" sets maintenance price increases account by account, and it works in repair, where each job is priced on its own. In multi-year maintenance contracts, retention outside China slipped, and management gave back about $20M of planned price to protect it. CEO Judy Marks called the retention dip "a small number of basis points," tied to contract timing rather than price or competition, per call coverage. Whether Q3 retention recovers will show which explanation is right.

The modernization wave — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

Modernization backlog up 26% at constant currency and organic modernization sales up 24% are the strongest numbers Otis reports. The bull framing: an ageing installed base is reaching the age when controllers and drives need replacing, and modernization revenue grew 33% from FY2021 to FY2025 while New Equipment shrank 22%. Otis trades at 12.7x EV/EBITDA while the most visible growth line is accelerating. The bear framing: modernization is still only 12.9% of revenue and is project work, with project margins and project cyclicality, not the 23% annuity margin. Swapping lost New Equipment volume for modernization volume does not fix a falling service margin. Both views agree on the next data point: service margin and retention on October 28.
