01 Company Overview

Otis makes, installs and — above all — services elevators, escalators and moving walkways in the United States, China and around the world. The company was founded in 1853 and spent decades inside United Technologies; on April 3, 2020 UTC spun off Otis and Carrier before merging with Raytheon, and Otis joined the S&P 500 the same morning, replacing Raytheon Co. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to September 30, 2026 was $15.14B, up 5.8%, with GAAP EPS of $4.02. At a $28.8B market value it ranks #329 in the S&P 500.

Here is the hand Otis is playing. Selling a new elevator is a thin, cyclical business: in Q3 2026 the New Equipment segment earned a 3.2% operating margin. Keeping that elevator running for the next thirty years is a very different business: Service earned 24.2%. Service is about two-thirds of revenue (67% of sales in the first nine months of 2026) and roughly 94% of segment operating profit ($633M of $675M in Q3 2026). Investors have long paid a premium multiple for that annuity, meaning a share price high relative to earnings. In 2026 they stopped paying it: the stock closed February at $92.56, fell to a 52-week intraday low of $63.58 on October 2, and settled on October 30, 2026 at $75.75 — down 18% from February and 19% above that low. Management lowered its profit outlook in July and held it at the October 28 print while backlog grew. The question for the stock is whether the service annuity is being impaired (price pushback, churn, labour cost) or just temporarily taxed while China's new-build market shrinks and a modernization wave builds.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed-base service annuity Safety-code-mandated maintenance Maker's own parts & diagnostics Global field-technician network 173-year brand Upgrades of its own installed base ROIC 58%

The moat is the installed base. An elevator gets sold once and serviced for decades, and the maker is best placed to keep that contract. That is why a company with 3% margins on new equipment earns a 58% return on invested capital overall (StockAnalysis ROIC 57.96%). Service needs little capital: TTM capex was $161M against $1.83B of operating cash flow. The weak spot showed up in 2026. The annuity protects the relationship, but not unlimited pricing power. When Otis pushed algorithm-driven price increases through maintenance contracts, retention outside China slipped and management backed off. On the Q3 call, per the gloom.sh transcript, CEO Judy Marks called the retention problem "predominantly a service quality issue" and said, "We don't tend to lose service contracts based on price." On the July call, per Otis's own transcript, she had called the dip "a small number of basis points" and tied it to customers on multi-year contracts choosing to switch, not to price. Either way, the pricing lever has a limit.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Service margin is still below last year while service revenue grows. Service organic sales rose 7% in Q3 2026, yet segment margin was 24.2%, down 130 bps from a year earlier (a 100 bps recovery from Q2). Adjusted operating profit for the company was $626M, down $6M, on sales up 6%. If labour and material costs keep outrunning price, growth stops turning into profit, and that is the whole case for owning the stock.
02
China new-build is still shrinking. New Equipment sales in China fell more than 20% in Q1 2026, by the high teens in Q2 and by more than 20% again in Q3. Fewer new units today means a smaller service portfolio later, so this drags on the annuity with a lag, not just on this year's New Equipment line.
03
Leverage and negative equity. StockAnalysis puts total debt at $8.69B against $866M of cash: net debt of $7.83B, and Debt/EBITDA of 3.16x. The release's balance sheet shows $8.28B of borrowings at September 30 ($1.22B short-term and current, $7.05B long-term). Book equity is −$5.43B, so return on equity and debt-to-equity are meaningless. Interest coverage of 10.79x is manageable. But the balance sheet leaves little room if cash flow keeps coming in below plan while buybacks continue: about $800M in the first nine months of 2026, with adjusted FCF guidance now about $1.5B.
04
Guidance credibility. The FY2026 outlook has moved down through the year. In January Otis guided adjusted EPS up mid to high single digits (on $4.05 for 2025) and adjusted free cash flow of $1.6–$1.7B; in April, $4.20–$4.24 and $1.60–$1.65B; in July, $4.01–$4.05 and $1.50–$1.55B. On October 28 it held the EPS range and put free cash flow at about $1.5B. A steady business earns its premium multiple only if its guidance holds, and after the July cut the market is no longer taking it on faith.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 18.8x trailing GAAP earnings and 18.6x this year's adjusted consensus, well below the 24.6x year-end FY2025 multiple, for a business where service is two-thirds of revenue and 94% of segment profit, returns 58% on invested capital, and yields 5.8% in free cash flow and 2.32% in dividends. Revenue is growing (+6% in Q3, Service organic +7%), modernization backlog is up 20%, Service margin recovered 100 bps in Q3, and consensus expects adjusted EPS to rise 11% in 2027 ($4.52 against $4.07). The average analyst target in the S&P Global panel is $86.46, 14% above the price. If the service margin holds at Q3's 24.2% or better in Q4, 2026 will look like a one-year pause, not a broken franchise.

Bear Case

The bear case is not about sales. It is that the annuity has less pricing power than the multiple assumed. Otis pushed price through maintenance contracts, saw retention slip, and backed off, and the service margin is still 130 bps below a year ago in a quarter when service revenue grew 7% organically. China, the largest new-build market, keeps shrinking, which slowly thins the service portfolio of the future. Guidance was cut in July and only held in October, with the cash-flow guide trimmed to about $1.5B; the outlook puts 2026 adjusted EPS at or below 2025's $4.05, and the balance sheet carries $7.83B of net debt against negative book equity after about $800M of buybacks in the first nine months. An 18.8x multiple is not obviously cheap for a flat-earnings industrial company.

Top 3 Risks

01
Service margin erosion. 24.2% in Q3 2026, down 130 bps year over year (Q2: 23.2%, −170 bps; Q1: 23.0%, −160 bps). Service is 94% of segment operating profit, so every point of lost margin comes almost straight out of earnings.
02
China and the future service base. New Equipment sales in China fell more than 20% in Q1 2026, by the high teens in Q2 and by more than 20% in Q3. Units that are never installed never join the maintenance portfolio.

Industry Commentary
China's building slowdown and the new-equipment cycle
ONGOING HEADWIND

China has been a drag on Otis's New Equipment line for more than a year. Orders there fell more than 20% in Q2 2025, New Equipment sales fell more than 20% in Q1 2026, both sales and orders fell by the high teens in Q2 2026, and sales fell by more than 20% again in Q3 2026. The effect on a service-led company comes with a delay. This year it shows up as lower New Equipment revenue and a 3% segment margin. Over the next decade it means fewer units entering the maintenance portfolio. One offset: on the Q2 call, Judy Marks said China modernization orders were up over 100%, partly because of government stimulus. China's installed base is starting to need upgrades even while its new-build market shrinks.

Pricing the service contract — algorithm vs. retention
KEY DEBATE

Otis's 2026 lesson is about how far an installed-base advantage can be pushed, when independent service firms also bid for the contracts. AI-driven "micro-pricing" sets maintenance price increases account by account, and it works in repair, where each job is priced on its own. In multi-year maintenance contracts, retention outside China slipped, and management gave back about $20M of planned price to protect it. On the Q3 call, per the gloom.sh transcript, CEO Judy Marks said, "We don't tend to lose service contracts based on price," and called retention "predominantly a service quality issue". Maintenance and repair organic growth was 4% in Q3, after 6% in Q2. Whether retention keeps improving will show which explanation is right.

The modernization wave — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

Modernization backlog up 20% and organic modernization sales up 20% in Q3 are the fastest growth rates in Otis's Q3 release. The bull framing: an ageing installed base is reaching the age when controllers and drives need replacing, and modernization revenue grew 33% from FY2021 to FY2025 while New Equipment shrank 22%. Otis trades at 14.6x EV/EBITDA while the most visible growth line is growing at 20%. The bear framing: modernization is still only 12.9% of revenue and is project work, with project margins and project cyclicality, not the 23–24% annuity margin. Swapping lost New Equipment volume for modernization volume does not fix a falling service margin. Both views agree on the next data points: service margin and retention at the Q4 print.
