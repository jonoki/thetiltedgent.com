01 Company Overview

Occidental Petroleum is a large-cap independent oil and gas producer built around a Permian Basin core, with additional US positions in the Rockies and the Gulf of America and international operations in Oman, the UAE and Algeria. Following the January 2026 divestiture of its chemicals arm, the company now reports through two operating segments — Oil and Gas and Midstream and Marketing — plus a development-stage Low Carbon Ventures effort centred on direct air capture. Continuing-operations revenue for fiscal 2025 was $21.59B (-1.9% YoY), restated to exclude the chemicals business; in the twelve months to September 2026 revenue reached $24.97B (+34.7% YoY) as the 2026 oil-price shock flowed through. At a $58.74B market capitalisation, Occidental ranks #209 in the S&P 500 by market value.

The equity story right now is not really a growth story — it is a balance-sheet repair story levered to crude. Principal debt has fallen from $20.8B at the end of Q3 2025 to $10.0B at the end of Q3 2026, the milestone management had set, funded by the OxyChem sale ($9.7B agreed price; $9.5B after adjustments, per the Q3 10-Q) and a windfall from war-driven oil prices. The next milestone is $6B. Sitting on top of the common stock is Berkshire Hathaway's ~$8.5B, 8% preferred position — roughly $700M a year of cash that leaves before common holders see anything — plus warrants on about 83.9M shares. How and when that preferred is retired is the single most important structural variable in the story.

THE BERKSHIRE STRUCTURE — WHY IT DOMINATES THE COMMON
Berkshire's August 2019 $10B preferred investment financed the Anadarko takeover. At September 30, 2026, 84,897 preferred shares remained (Q3 10-Q) — roughly $8.5B of face value at an 8% coupon — about $700M of annual cash dividends that rank ahead of the common. The terms require Occidental to redeem preferred dollar for dollar, at 110% of face value, for every dollar of common distributions (dividends or buybacks) above $4.00 per share over a trailing twelve months, which is precisely why buybacks and dividend growth have been kept deliberately modest. The preferred first becomes optionally redeemable in August 2029 at a 5% premium; on the Q3 call CFO Sunil Mathew said the company is preparing for that August 2029 redemption and, until then, "we'll just be opportunistic in terms of share repurchase." He set a new principal-debt milestone of $6B and said the company would "definitely get there and potentially a net cash position in 2027" (net cash: more cash than debt). Separately, warrants on about 83.9M shares struck at $59.59 (the strike in the Q3 10-Q; ~$5B of exercise value, expiring one year after the preferred is redeemed) sit just below the money at the $58.75 close — a dilution overhang of roughly 8% of the current share count whenever the stock trades above the strike.

Gathering, processing, transportation and marketing of oil, gas and NGLs, plus an equity interest in Western Midstream Partners (WES), which contributed $121M of equity investment income in Q3 2026. The segment is small on revenue but swings the quarter: pre-tax income fell to $55M in Q3 from a record $1.3B in Q2, as the Waha-to-Gulf-Coast natural gas price gap narrowed and crude marketing margins fell on the timing of sales, partly offset by higher sulfur volumes and prices at Al Hosn. Occidental's DAC (direct air capture) and carbon-sequestration business, developed through subsidiary 1PointFive. The flagship Stratos facility in West Texas is transitioning from construction to operations: on the Q3 call CEO Richard Jackson said: "We remain on track to begin full plant start-up around the end of the year as we transition to operations in 2027." He pointed to project EBITDA of $90M–$130M in 2028 (EBITDA: earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation, a rough measure of operating cash). The near-term financial consequence is favourable — approximately $400M of annual Low Carbon Ventures capital rolls off starting in 2027, which mechanically improves free cash flow regardless of whether DAC ever works commercially. The strategic logic is real but narrow: CO2 and power represent roughly 30% of the operating cost of an EOR barrel, so captured CO2 has a genuine internal use in Occidental's Permian enhanced-recovery business. Beyond that, economics depend on the 45Q tax credit regime and on voluntary carbon-removal offtake at prices no one has yet demonstrated at scale. Management intends to bring in partners. Treat this as option value, not as an earnings stream — capital-hungry, policy-dependent, and with no disclosed revenue.
Industry Size: The engineered carbon-removal market is pre-commercial, so it has no meaningful size yet. The relevant variable is the 45Q credit per tonne and the durability of voluntary CDR offtake contracts.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
PERMIAN CORE SCALE CO₂ EOR EXPERTISE LOW-COST OPS $8.54/BOE PERMIAN EGRESS CAPACITY OMAN & UAE CONCESSIONS BERKSHIRE ANCHOR DAC FIRST-MOVER

Occidental's moat is a cost-and-inventory moat rather than a pricing moat — no oil producer sets the price of its product. What differentiates OXY is the combination of a deep, contiguous Permian position (deepened by CrownRock in August 2024), domestic lease operating expense of $8.54/boe in Q3 2026, inside its own guidance, and forty years of accumulated CO2 enhanced-oil-recovery know-how that few peers can replicate. That EOR franchise matters twice over: it lowers the decline rate on legacy barrels, and it gives the Low Carbon Ventures business an internal customer for captured CO2 at a time when nobody else has one. Management's claim of a 45% recovery uplift in Permian CO2 pilots — which CEO Richard Jackson said on the Q2 call could lift recovery from about 10% of the oil in an unconventional well to 15%, and eventually 20% — is, if it scales, worth more than any carbon-credit narrative. Sustaining capital is guided to fall from $5.0–5.1B in 2027 to $4.5B by 2030 on lower decline rates and better well costs, and management has laid out a path to more than $4B of incremental annual sustainable cash flow by 2030, roughly 85% of it achievable at lower oil prices. The Midstream and Marketing arm adds a real if lumpy hedge: when Permian gas goes negative at the wellhead, the firm-transport position monetises the basis. Finally, Berkshire's 26.5% stake and clearance to go to 50% functions as a partial floor on the equity — though as discussed below, the same relationship is also the largest claim ahead of common holders.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Commodity price beta with a levered balance sheet. Oil and Gas pre-tax income went from $1.0B in Q1 2026 to $2.8B in Q2 on roughly a 38% jump in realised crude, then slipped to $2.6B in Q3 as realised crude fell 11%. That works spectacularly on the way up and is brutal on the way down. Even after cutting principal debt to $10.0B, Occidental still carries $11.83B of debt on the balance sheet against $43.80B of equity (Q3 10-Q), and the 2026 debt paydown leaned on war-inflated crude that swings hard both ways: Brent spot averaged $117.29 a barrel in April, $83.76 in July and $123.93 in October, then fell to about $92 by November 11 (EIA).

2. The preferred and warrant overhang. ~$8.5B of 8% preferred consumes about $700M of cash annually before common holders participate, the mandatory-redemption formula caps how fast common distributions can grow, and warrants on about 83.9M shares struck at $59.59 sit just above the $58.75 close — roughly 8% potential dilution if the stock rises. Management says buybacks stay opportunistic until the August 2029 preferred redemption. Any equity holder is, in effect, subordinated to Berkshire.

3. Loss of the OxyChem counter-cyclical buffer. Selling chemicals removed the one segment whose earnings did not move with WTI, while retaining its legacy environmental and tort liabilities. Reported EPS is now almost entirely a function of one input price, and the residual liability tail has no offsetting revenue.

4. Low Carbon Ventures is capital-hungry and unproven. Stratos has consumed roughly $400M a year of capital and will not begin operations until 2027. The economics depend on the 45Q credit regime — a policy variable, not a commercial one — and on voluntary carbon-removal offtake at prices no market has yet validated. Management is openly seeking partners, which is a reasonable de-risking move and also an admission that the capital intensity is uncomfortable on its own balance sheet.

5. Permian gas takeaway and basis risk. Domestic natural gas realised negative $1.48/Mcf in Q2 2026 before recovering to $1.61 in Q3. Occidental turned the Q2 gap into a midstream profit, but the underlying condition — associated gas with insufficient egress — is a structural drag on Permian returns and can force curtailments if pipelines go down.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS — READ BEFORE TRUSTING ANY TTM MULTIPLE

OxyChem disposal gain (Q1 2026). The $9.7B all-cash sale closed January 2, 2026 and is the dominant reason TTM EPS is $7.42 against FY2025 EPS of $1.61: $3.05 a share of the first nine months of 2026 came from discontinued operations (Q3 10-Q). Every trailing multiple on this page — P/E, net margin, ROE — is distorted by it. The forward P/E of 10.98 on 2027 consensus EPS of $5.35 is the more honest valuation anchor.

Discontinued-operations restatement. Chemicals has been reclassified out of continuing operations, restating FY2025 revenue to $21.59B and FY2024 to $22.02B. Any comparison to pre-2026 published figures for Occidental will not tie.

Reported versus adjusted Q3 2026 EPS. Reported $1.61 against adjusted $1.91 from continuing operations; the release attributes the gap mainly to derivative losses. Nasdaq's consensus was $1.43, so the beat holds on either basis. In Q2 the gap ran the other way (reported $2.75, adjusted $2.40).

Preferred redemption premia. Mandatory redemptions of the Berkshire preferred carry a 10% premium and the optional August 2029 redemption a 5% premium. These premia are charges against income available to common shareholders when they occur and will recur as the preferred is retired.

Interest expense step-down. At $11.8B of principal debt, management put the go-forward annual interest bill about $630M below 2025's interest payments (Q2 2026 call). This is a genuine, recurring improvement — the opposite of a one-off — and it flatters 2026 comparisons against 2025 in a way that will not repeat at the same magnitude.

Midstream windfall, reversed. Midstream pre-tax income was a record $1.3B in Q2 on an unusually wide Waha-to-Gulf-Coast gas price gap, then $55M in Q3 as the gap narrowed. Q2 still flatters every trailing figure on this page.

Five-year trajectory. From a November 2021 month-end close of $29.65 to $58.75 on November 13, 2026, OXY returned +98.2% in price terms — roughly 14.8% annualised, against +65.6% for SPY (an S&P 500 fund) and +72.6% for QQQ (a Nasdaq-100 fund) over the same dates — but that headline flatters a genuinely miserable middle. The stock more than doubled from late 2021 into the October 2022 high of $72.60 on the post-invasion energy shock and Berkshire's very public accumulation, then did nothing for two years, drifting sideways in the high-$50s to mid-$60s through 2023 before grinding lower all through 2024 as crude softened and the CrownRock debt landed. It bottomed at $39.41 in April 2025 — a 46% drawdown from the 2022 high — and spent the rest of 2025 in the $40s. The entire 2026 recovery is a war trade: the late-February Iran–Israel–US conflict lifted Brent spot from a $66.60 January average to $117.29 in April (EIA), and OXY from $45.39 to a March close of $65.00. As Brent eased the stock fell back to $48.57 by June, then reached $60.18 at the end of August after the Q2 beat. It closed September at $55.32 and October at $64.00, while Brent averaged $123.93 in October, and, with Brent back near $92 after the Q3 print, closed November 13 at $58.75. Anyone who bought at the October 2022 month-end close is still down 19% four years later. That is the honest characterisation of this stock: a leveraged bet on the oil price that tends to give its gains back, not a business that grows steadily year after year.

Relative performance. Over Vicki Hollub's tenure as CEO, OXY's share price fell 26% while ConocoPhillips rose 139% and Chevron 79% (our calculation from Yahoo closes, April 29, 2016 to May 29, 2026, price only) — a devastating comparison that explains both the CEO change and the current strategic urgency. On a one-year view the gap has narrowed: OXY is +39.8% from November 13, 2025 to November 13, 2026, against +59.1% for ConocoPhillips, +44.9% for EOG, +43.6% for ExxonMobil, +41.3% for Chevron, +32.9% for Devon, +28.1% for Diamondback and +12.3% for SPY (share-price change, our calculation from Yahoo closes). So OXY has been a middle-of-the-pack performer in a strong year for the sector, after a decade of severe underperformance.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The balance sheet is being repaired faster than the market priced. Principal debt has gone from $20.8B to $10.0B in four quarters, hitting management's milestone, with $3.9B of cash against it; the next milestone is $6B, and CFO Sunil Mathew says the company could reach it and potentially hold more cash than debt in 2027. That sets up the redemption of the Berkshire preferred, retiring ~$700M of annual preferred dividends and freeing cash for buybacks after August 2029. Underneath, the operating business is low-cost ($8.54/boe domestic LOE in Q3, inside guidance), production beat the guidance midpoint in Q3 despite a Gulf of America curtailment, sustaining capital is guided down to $4.5B by 2030, and management has laid out a path to more than $4B of additional annual sustainable cash flow by 2030. At 10.98x 2027 consensus earnings with a 9.49% FCF yield (StockAnalysis), you are paid to wait — and Berkshire, with 26.5% of the common and clearance to go to 50%, is a large and patient holder standing underneath the stock.

Bear Case

Strip out the war and much of the story goes with it. Brent spot averaged $66.60 in January, $117.29 in April and $123.93 in October, then fell to about $92 by November 11 (EIA); much of the 2026 debt paydown was funded by a war premium that can vanish as fast as it came, and Q3 showed earnings stepping down from Q2 (adjusted EPS $1.91 from $2.40) as realised crude fell 11% and the midstream windfall vanished. FY2025 — a normal price year — produced EPS of $1.61, and the current TTM EPS of $7.42 includes $3.05 a share of discontinued-operations income, mainly the OxyChem sale gain, that will never repeat. Consensus has 2027 EPS falling to $5.35. The company sold its only counter-cyclical earnings stream while keeping its environmental liabilities, still earns a 13.18% ROE and 10.38% ROIC in a windfall year, below the peer medians, and sits behind $8.5B of 8% preferred plus warrants on about 83.9M shares struck just above the share price (~8% dilution). Under the prior decade of management the stock fell 26% while ConocoPhillips rose 139%.

  Industry Commentary
Oil Prices, OPEC+ Policy and the Hormuz Premium
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every material number on this page traces back to one variable. Brent spot averaged $66.60 in January 2026; the Iran–Israel–US conflict that erupted in late February disrupted Middle East supply and Strait of Hormuz shipping, driving it to $117.29 in April. It eased to $83.76 in July, surged again to a $123.93 average in October and was back near $92 by November 11 (EIA), after President Trump said the war could end soon (CNBC, November 10). Occidental realised $85.91/bbl for crude in Q3, down from $96.78 in Q2, and Oil and Gas pre-tax income slipped to $2.6B from $2.8B. The company's own international assets in Oman and the UAE sit inside the disrupted region — Q2 international volumes were reduced by the conflict, so the geopolitics cut both ways. The asymmetry to watch: a ceasefire or an OPEC+ supply response would compress prices toward the pre-war $70 level and take the deleveraging story with it, while any further escalation around Hormuz would do the opposite. Separately, domestic natural gas realised negative $1.48/Mcf in Q2 before recovering to $1.61 in Q3; management expects new Permian pipeline capacity to cut its gas marketing income in Q4.
Balance-Sheet Repair Is the Equity Story
EMERGING TAILWIND
Occidental has been a deleveraging story since the $55B Anadarko acquisition in 2019 (a value that includes Anadarko's debt). Principal debt reached $10.0B at the end of Q3 2026, the milestone management had set, with $3.9B of cash against it. Balance-sheet debt fell from $22.40B at FY2025 year-end to $11.83B and equity rose from $36.60B to $43.80B (Q3 10-Q); debt-to-equity fell from 0.64 to 0.29 (StockAnalysis), and the current ratio moved from 0.94 to 1.38. Two things funded it: $9.7B of OxyChem proceeds and a war-inflated crude price. With the $10B milestone reached, the next is $6B, which the CFO says could come with a net cash position in 2027; after that come the preferred redemption in 2029 and then buybacks. This is a real, measurable improvement that management controls. Consolidation across the sector (Devon's all-stock merger with Coterra, completed in May 2026; Occidental's own CrownRock deal) continues to concentrate Permian inventory in fewer hands.
Carbon Capture Economics and 45Q — Bull vs Bear
ONGOING HEADWIND
Occidental is the most exposed US producer to engineered carbon removal, and the market has never been sure whether that is an asset or a distraction. The bull framing: CO2 and power are ~30% of the operating cost of an EOR barrel, so Occidental is the one DAC developer with a captive internal customer for the product; Stratos moves from construction to operations in 2027; roughly $400M of annual Low Carbon Ventures capital rolls off from next year, mechanically lifting free cash flow; and management is bringing in partners to share the burden. Layered on top, management says Permian CO2-EOR pilots have shown a 45% uplift, which Jackson said could take recovery from about 10% of an unconventional well's oil to 15%, and eventually 20% — a far larger prize than carbon credits. The bear framing: DAC has no demonstrated commercial market. Its returns depend on the 45Q tax credit, a policy variable subject to political reversal, and on voluntary carbon-removal offtake at prices that have never been validated at scale. The plant is not yet commissioned, a non-technology repair (structural supports for a calciner) was still under way at the Q3 call, and Occidental has spent hundreds of millions annually on it while carrying $8.5B of 8% preferred. The resolution: the capex roll-off is close to certain and near-term positive regardless of outcome; the commercial proof is a 2027–2030 question that should be valued as an option, not underwritten as an earnings stream.
