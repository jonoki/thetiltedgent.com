01 Company Overview

For fifteen years this company was Pure Storage: the upstart that bet, in 2009, that spinning hard disks were finished and that enterprise data belonged on flash. It sells all-flash storage systems (FlashArray, FlashBlade), the Purity software that runs them, and a subscription model called Evergreen that lets customers upgrade hardware without ripping it out. In February 2026 it renamed itself Everpure to signal a move from storage boxes to data management, and in April it traded the PSTG ticker for a single letter, P. On Monday September 21, 2026 it joined the S&P 500, promoted from the MidCap 400 in the quarterly rebalance.

The numbers explain the promotion. Revenue for the quarter ended August 2, 2026 was $1.186B, up 38% — management's eighth straight quarter of accelerating growth — with product revenue up 54%. Full-year FY27 guidance was raised from $4.41–4.51B to $5.03–5.07B. Two things are pushing at once. First, the hyperscalers: in late 2024 Pure won a design deal with a top-five hyperscaler (widely reported as Meta) to put its DirectFlash modules inside that company's own data centres, and on August 10, 2026 it announced a second top-five hyperscaler. Second, flash itself got expensive: the CFO described "historic industry price increases" in the first half of FY27, which lifts reported revenue on every box shipped. The stock has priced in the story — $104.14 against $0.73 of trailing GAAP earnings per share — which makes this a report about whether the growth is structural or partly a price-cycle echo.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

DirectFlash raw-NAND architecture Purity software across all systems Evergreen non-disruptive upgrades Two top-5 hyperscaler design wins $4.1B RPO backlog 15,000 customers Net cash ($782.6M)

The moat is an engineering decision made early: talk to the flash chips directly instead of through someone else's drive. That lets one software layer, Purity, manage wear, density and power across every system Everpure ships, and it is the reason two hyperscalers that design their own hardware chose to buy it. The subscription model deepens it — an Evergreen customer upgrades in place and has little reason to re-tender. Per the Q2 release, Gartner named it a Leader in the 2026 Magic Quadrant for Enterprise Storage Platforms, positioned highest in execution and furthest in vision for the second consecutive year. The vulnerability is that the thing underneath the moat, NAND flash, is a commodity Everpure does not make, and its price moves in violent cycles.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Flash pricing cuts both ways. Management described pricing "not seen in 10 years" and "unprecedented price increases." Higher component prices inflate reported product revenue, but they also compress product margin (66.2%, bottom of range) and consumed cash: Everpure prepaid for supply, turning Q2 free cash flow to −$238M. When the NAND cycle turns, the revenue tailwind turns with it.
02
Customer concentration is coming. The hyperscale thesis rests on two buyers, neither named by the company, placing very large, lumpy orders. That is a different business from selling to 15,000 enterprises: fewer customers, more negotiating leverage on their side, and revenue that can move by quarters, not weeks.
03
GAAP profit is thin; stock compensation is not. First-half FY27 stock-based compensation was $281.9M against GAAP operating income of $83.1M. The 19.4% non-GAAP operating margin excludes it. Share count keeps rising — diluted weighted shares were 345.6M in Q2 vs 337.7M a year earlier — and buybacks ($69M in Q2, $176M authorisation left) have not offset it.
04
Well-funded competitors. Dell, HPE and NetApp all sell enterprise flash storage, and Dell alone carries a market value above ten times Everpure's. Everpure's differentiation is architectural, but competitors can respond on price in any down-cycle.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — an expensive stock can stay overbought for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Everpure has eight straight quarters of accelerating growth, a 38% quarter, a guide raised by more than half a billion dollars, $4.1B of RPO growing 44%, and two of the world's five largest data-centre operators designing its flash into their own infrastructure — with management guiding the second one to a "meaningful ramp" in FY28 at 75–85% margins. The balance sheet has $782.6M of net cash. On $2.78 of consensus FY27 non-GAAP earnings the stock trades at 37.5x, and if the FY28 consensus of $3.55 holds, under 30x. Membership in the S&P 500 adds a permanent passive holder base. The architecture choice made in 2009 is being validated by the most demanding buyers in the market.

Bear Case

At 142.7x trailing GAAP earnings, the price assumes the hyperscale ramp arrives on time and at the margins promised. Part of today's growth is the flash price cycle — "unprecedented price increases," in management's words — which inflates revenue now and will deflate it later. Cash is going the wrong way: −$238M of free cash flow in Q2, a 0.37% trailing FCF yield, and stock compensation more than three times GAAP operating income in the first half. The index-inclusion buying is finished, the monthly RSI is 77.5, and the last time it was this high the stock lost 40% in five months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Hyperscale execution and concentration. The largest part of the forward story depends on two customers, one unnamed, whose revenue is "de minimis" this year. A slip in the FY28 ramp would hit the valuation directly, because that is where the analyst target range diverges.
02
The NAND cycle. Everpure buys flash it does not make. Rising prices have lifted revenue, squeezed product margin to the bottom of its range and tied up cash in prepayments. A reversal would cut reported revenue growth; a further spike would pressure margins and supply.
03
Valuation after an index event. The stock rose 12% in September into inclusion day. Index additions often mark a peak in forced buying; with a 142.7x trailing multiple and an overbought monthly RSI, the margin for a disappointing Financial Analyst Meeting or Q3 print is small.
Industry Commentary
A flash price cycle "not seen in 10 years"
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

On the Q2 FY27 call management described flash pricing at a level "not seen in 10 years" and said the "rapid increase in semiconductor demand and cost continues to affect our industry in multiple and complex ways." Everpure's response was to buy ahead — the $577M jump in prepaid and other assets — and to accept product margin at the bottom of its 65–70% range to keep taking share. It also stated that hyperscalers source their own NAND, so the hyperscale business is less exposed to component cost than the enterprise one. Back in December 2025, an analyst quoted by SiliconANGLE was already flagging NAND price increases of 60%+ in some cases as an industry-wide cost being passed to customers. The question for every storage vendor is the same: how much of 2026's revenue growth is units, and how much is price?

Hyperscalers move from disk to flash
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

The largest data-centre operators have historically stored their bulk data on hard disks, designed in-house around parts from the disk makers. Everpure's pitch is that AI-era power and rack-space constraints make dense flash cheaper to run even when it costs more to buy — the release describes the second win as reclaiming "vital power and rack space." Two top-five design wins are evidence that argument is landing. They are not yet evidence of the revenue size: management's commitments speak of "tens of exabytes" and orders into calendar 2028, but the disclosed revenue contribution this year is de minimis.

From storage vendor to data platform — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The rename is a strategy statement: Everpure wants to be paid for managing data (Data Intelligence from the 1touch deal, Data Stream pipelines, Portworx, a Databricks connector), not just for holding it. The bull framing: subscription ARR of $2.1B, Evergreen//One contract value up 121%, and software that spans on-premises, public cloud and hyperscale make this a platform with recurring economics, and the S&P 500 promotion confirms the company has become a large-cap growth name. The bear framing: subscription revenue is growing 20% while product grows 54% — the business is becoming more hardware-driven, not less, in the very year it rebranded away from storage. Data-management software is a crowded field, and the new products have only "first sales." The way to adjudicate is the mix: if ARR growth accelerates toward product growth over the next four quarters, as management expects from the RPO build, the platform story is real.
