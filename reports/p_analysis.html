01 Company Overview

For fifteen years this company was Pure Storage: the upstart that bet, in 2009, that spinning hard disks were finished and that enterprise data belonged on flash. It sells all-flash storage systems (FlashArray, FlashBlade), the Purity software that runs them, and a subscription model called Evergreen that lets customers upgrade hardware without ripping it out. In February 2026 it renamed itself Everpure to signal a move from storage boxes to data management, and in April it traded the PSTG ticker for a single letter, P. On Monday September 21, 2026 it joined the S&P 500, promoted from the MidCap 400 in the quarterly rebalance.

Growth is still speeding up. Revenue for the quarter ended November 1, 2026 was $1.460B, up 51%, after 38% the quarter before, with product revenue up 77%. Full-year FY27 revenue guidance has now been raised in three consecutive quarters, to $5.20–5.21B, and at its September 23 Financial Analyst Meeting the company put out a first look at FY28: $7.0–7.3B of revenue. Two things are pushing at once. First, the hyperscalers: in late 2024 Pure won a design deal with a top-five hyperscaler (widely reported as Meta) to put its DirectFlash modules inside that company's own data centres, and on August 10, 2026 it announced a second top-five hyperscaler, which the CEO expects to start shipping next year. Second, flash itself got expensive, which lifts reported revenue on every box shipped; at the start of Q4 Everpure began to cut its own product prices. The stock has swung harder than the business: from $104.14 at the last edition to a record $156.97 close on October 13 and back to $92.05 against $0.86 of trailing GAAP earnings per share. The question for this report is how much of the growth lasts once flash prices turn.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

DirectFlash raw-NAND architecture Purity software across all systems Evergreen non-disruptive upgrades Two top-5 hyperscaler design wins $4.2B RPO backlog Net cash ($882.4M)

The moat is an engineering decision made early: talk to the flash chips directly instead of through someone else's drive. That lets one software layer, Purity, manage wear, density and power across every system Everpure ships, and it is the reason two hyperscalers that design their own hardware chose to buy it. The subscription model deepens it — an Evergreen customer upgrades in place and has little reason to put the business out to bid again. Per the Q2 release, Gartner named it a Leader in the 2026 Magic Quadrant for Enterprise Storage Platforms, positioned highest in execution and furthest in vision for the second consecutive year. The vulnerability is that the thing underneath the moat, NAND flash, is a commodity Everpure does not make, and its price moves in violent cycles.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Flash pricing cuts both ways. In August management described pricing "not seen in 10 years" and "unprecedented price increases." Higher component prices inflate reported product revenue, but they also squeeze product margin (67.3% in Q3 against 72.9% a year earlier) and tied up cash: Everpure prepaid for supply, turning Q2 free cash flow to −$238M before Q3 recovered to +$265M. Everpure began cutting its own product prices at the start of Q4; when the NAND cycle turns, the revenue tailwind turns with it.
02
Customer concentration is coming. The hyperscale thesis rests on two buyers, neither named by the company, placing very large, lumpy orders. That is a different business from selling to thousands of enterprises: fewer customers, more negotiating leverage on their side, and revenue that can move by quarters, not weeks.
03
GAAP profit is thin; stock compensation is not. Stock-based compensation for the first nine months of FY27 was $470.4M against GAAP operating income of $233.2M. The 23.6% Q3 non-GAAP operating margin excludes it. Share count keeps rising — diluted weighted shares were 350.8M in Q3 vs 345.7M a year earlier — and buybacks ($67M in Q3, $220M over nine months) have not offset it.
04
Well-funded competitors. Dell, HPE and NetApp all sell enterprise flash storage, and Dell alone carries a market value nearly six times Everpure's at the November 20 close. Everpure's differentiation is architectural, but competitors can respond on price in any down-cycle.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — an expensive stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $30.97 to the November 20, 2026 settled close of $92.05, the stock rose 197% — just under 3x, with no dividends to add. The ticker change in April 2026 is cosmetic: it is one continuous share class, and no split occurred in the window. The path was anything but smooth. The stock went nowhere for two years, closing April 2023 at $22.83, the low of the window; it then climbed through 2024 as the flash-for-disk argument gained ground, doubled again from April to October 2025, and lost 40% between October 2025 and March 2026. The 2026 rally then carried it to a record $156.97 close on October 13, and it fell 41% from there by November 20.

The 6-period monthly RSI reached 85.2 at the end of September 2026 — overbought, and the highest month-end reading since the 86.0 of October 2025, which preceded that 40% fall. The pattern repeated: by November 20 the reading was back to 50.0, neither overbought nor oversold. The 10-month moving average sits at about $87.29, with the price 5.5% above it, and the 3-month average at about $116.60 is well above it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Growth sped up again, to 51% in Q3; full-year guidance has been raised in three consecutive quarters; RPO of $4.2B is growing 44%; and two of the world's five largest data-centre operators are designing its flash into their own infrastructure, with the second due to start shipping next year at margins management put at 75–85% in August. Free cash flow turned back to +$265M in Q3 and the balance sheet has $882.4M of net cash. After the fall from October's record, the stock trades at 31.1x consensus FY27 non-GAAP earnings of $2.96 and 21.5x the FY28 consensus of $4.29 — cheaper than at the last edition, with a preliminary FY28 outlook ($7.0–7.3B) the company reiterated in November. S&P 500 membership means index funds now hold it as long as it stays in the index. The architecture choice made in 2009 is being backed by the most demanding buyers in the market.

Bear Case

At 107.0x trailing GAAP earnings, the price still assumes the hyperscale ramp arrives on time and at the margins promised. Part of the growth is the flash price cycle — "unprecedented price increases," in management's words in August — and Everpure has already started cutting its own prices for Q4, while the CFO would not call product margin's low point. The FY28 outlook was reiterated, not raised, on a higher FY27 base, so the growth it implies for next year is now about 34–40% rather than the 39–45% first stated. The CEO said many customers have spent their budgets for the year, and stock compensation ($470.4M over nine months) is about twice GAAP operating income. The stock has fallen 41% from its October record in five weeks, in a market that sold off AI-infrastructure names, and it can move 10–16% in a day.

Top 3 Risks

01
Hyperscale execution and concentration. The largest part of the forward story depends on two customers, neither named by the company; the second's contribution is "de minimis" this year, with shipments expected to start early-to-mid next year. A slip in the FY28 ramp would hit the valuation directly, because that is where the analyst target range diverges.
02
The NAND cycle. Everpure buys flash it does not make. Rising prices have lifted revenue, squeezed product margin (67.3% in Q3 against 72.9% a year earlier) and tied up cash in prepayments and inventory. Everpure cut its product prices at the start of Q4; a sharp fall in flash prices would cut reported revenue growth, and a further spike would pressure margins and supply.
03
AI-spending sentiment. The stock has been moving with AI data-centre stocks: it fell 16.4% on November 5, the day the S&P 500 dropped 4.4% in an AI-led sell-off (AP), and 12.75% on November 20, two days after results that beat its own guidance and raised the full-year outlook. At 107.0x trailing GAAP earnings, a slowdown in data-centre building — which the CEO says he does not expect to hurt Everpure — would weigh on the multiple before it showed up in the numbers.
Industry Commentary
A flash price cycle "not seen in 10 years"
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

On the Q2 FY27 call management described flash pricing at a level "not seen in 10 years" and said the "rapid increase in semiconductor demand and cost continues to affect our industry in multiple and complex ways." Everpure's response was to buy ahead — the $577M jump in prepaid and other assets in Q2 — and to accept product margin at the bottom of its 65–70% range to keep taking share. It also stated that hyperscalers source their own NAND, so the hyperscale business is less exposed to component cost than the enterprise one. By the Q3 call in November the tone had shifted: product margin recovered to 67.3%, Everpure cut product prices on most models at the start of Q4, and the CEO said he expects component costs "to stabilize towards the end of the year and to remain high going into next year." Back in December 2025, an analyst quoted by SiliconANGLE was already flagging NAND price increases of 60%+ in some cases as an industry-wide cost being passed to customers. The question for every storage vendor is the same: how much of 2026's revenue growth is units, and how much is price?

Hyperscalers move from disk to flash
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

The largest data-centre operators have historically stored their bulk data on hard disks, designed in-house around parts from the disk makers. Everpure's pitch is that AI-era power and rack-space constraints make dense flash cheaper to run even when it costs more to buy — the release describes the second win as reclaiming "vital power and rack space." Two top-five design wins are evidence that argument is landing. They are not yet evidence of the revenue size: management's commitments speak of "tens of exabytes" and orders into calendar 2028, but the second customer's contribution this year is, in the CFO's word in November, "de minimis." The AP's account of the November 5 sell-off shows the other side of the argument: investors worried that data-centre construction could slow as borrowing gets more expensive.

From storage vendor to data platform — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The rename is a strategy statement: Everpure wants to be paid for managing data (Data Intelligence from the 1touch deal, Data Stream pipelines, Portworx, a Databricks connector), not just for holding it. The bull framing: subscription ARR of $2.2B, storage-as-a-service contract value (Evergreen//One and related offerings) up 57% in Q3, and software that spans on-premises, public cloud and hyperscale make this a platform with recurring economics; the company says its three newer businesses (Modern Data Software, Scale AI and Hyperscale Solutions) should reach about 20% of revenue by fiscal 2030, and the S&P 500 promotion confirms it now ranks among the large US companies. The bear framing: subscription revenue grew 19% in Q3 while product grew 77% — the business is becoming more hardware-driven, not less, in the very year it rebranded away from storage. Data-management software is a crowded field, and the newest of these products are early: in August the CEO said the DataStream AI data platform "saw its first sales this quarter." The way to judge it is the mix: if ARR growth climbs toward product growth over the next four quarters, the platform story is real — though the CFO has warned that RPO growth could moderate as last year's large deals are lapped.
