01 Company Overview

Petrobras is Brazil's national oil company: a listed, state-controlled producer that pumps oil from deep water beneath a thick layer of salt off Brazil's coast (the pre-salt), refines crude into diesel and gasoline for the Brazilian market, and sells natural gas. In the second quarter of 2026 it produced a record 3.34 million barrels of oil equivalent a day, 14.1% more than a year earlier, and ran its refineries at 101.2% of rated capacity, beating a record set in 2014. Sales revenue was $33.6B, up 59.8% on 2Q25, and net income attributable to shareholders was $10.4B, up 120%.

Two forces explain that quarter. The first is price: Brent crude averaged $104.52 a barrel in 2Q26 against $67.82 a year earlier, after what Petrobras's 1Q26 release calls "the start of the conflict in the Middle East". The second is volume: new floating production ships in the Búzios field, including P-79, which started on May 1, three months ahead of schedule. The stock moved accordingly. PBR closed 2025 at $11.85 and reached $22.03 at the end of April 2026; on October 2 it closed at $21.65, up 74.0% in a year. At that price the market values the whole company at $134.0B, about 5.5 times trailing earnings.

That low multiple is the price of the shareholder that owns the majority of the votes. The Brazilian Federal Government holds 50.26% of the voting shares and appoints the chair of the board. The same government has spent 2026 paying Petrobras subsidies to hold down diesel, gasoline and cooking-gas prices (R$13.3B received by the end of September), charging it an export tax on crude oil and diesel ($965M in 2Q26), and collecting R$161B from it in taxes, government take and withholdings in the first half alone. Brazil's general election was scheduled for October 4, two days after this report's data date, with a runoff on October 25. For a company whose pricing, dividends and leadership all run through Brasília, the result matters as much as Brent.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Pre-salt deepwater reserves Búzios scale (1.2M bpd) FPSO delivery pipeline Refining at 101.2% utilisation Integrated crude-to-pump chain Sovereign backing Net debt/EBITDA 1.14x

The moat is geology plus execution. Pre-salt reservoirs are large and productive, and Petrobras develops them by adding FPSOs one after another, with eight new production systems in the 2026–30 plan. P-79 started three months early, and output rose 14% in a year. The refining system protects the other end: Petrobras's plants ran above nameplate in 2Q26 and sold 742 thousand barrels a day of diesel at home. The weakness is that the moat belongs to the company, while the profits are shared with the state. Brazil collects through taxes, royalties, the export tax and, through its majority vote, dividend and pricing policy.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The controlling shareholder sets the terms. The Federal Government holds 50.26% of the votes and appoints the chair of the board that approves dividends and joins programmes such as the diesel subsidy. The October 4 and October 25, 2026 elections decide which administration holds that vote.
02
Windfall-style taxes are already here. The export tax on crude oil and diesel cost $122M in 1Q26 and $965M in 2Q26. A levy that grows with exports and prices transfers part of any oil-price windfall from shareholders to the Treasury.
03
A Brent-driven quarter is not a run-rate. Brent averaged $104.52 in 2Q26, against $63.69 in 4Q25. 2Q26 net income of $10.4B was more than three times 4Q25's $2.9B. If the conflict premium fades, earnings fall back toward 2025 levels: $19.6B of net income for the whole of 2025, against $25.6B over the trailing four quarters.
04
Two currencies. PBR is priced in dollars, but Petrobras's domestic sales, taxes and dividends are set in reais. Dividends declared in reais shrink in dollars if the real weakens: the 2Q26 average rate was R$5.05 per dollar, the October 2 PTAX rate R$5.2238.

Notable One-Off Items

Petrobras reports net income both with and without what it calls one-off events. In 2Q26, net income was $10.43B as reported and $11.07B excluding one-offs. Government measures run through the release line by line: the export tax on crude oil and diesel cost $965M, and the road-diesel subsidy programme brought in $1,923M. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At 5.5 times trailing earnings and 3.7 times EBITDA, you buy record production (3.34M boed, +14.1%), refineries running above nameplate, a 15% free-cash-flow yield and leverage down to 1.14x net debt/EBITDA. Even at 2025's $69 average Brent the company earned $19.6B. Dividends of 6.4% gross over the past year come on top. The Equatorial Margin discoveries and the Côte d'Ivoire blocks give a credible answer to "what comes after Búzios". If the political outcome leaves the governance and dividend framework intact, a 5–6x multiple on a growing, deleveraging producer is low.

Bear Case

The trailing numbers rest on one exceptional quarter at $104.52 Brent. The controlling shareholder has already shown, in 2026 alone, that it will tax exports, subsidise pump prices through the company and take R$161B in six months. An election two days after the data date decides who controls that vote. The ADS rose 83% in nine months and sits 1.7% below its five-year high, with an average analyst target only 5.7% above the price, and one recent upgrade came with a target below it. If Brent normalises and Brasília leans on pricing or dividends, the low multiple will turn out to have been fair.

Top 3 Risks

01
Political control of pricing, dividends and leadership. The Federal Government holds 50.26% of the votes. The October 2026 elections, the subsidy programmes (PMs 1,340, 1,358, 1,359, 1,363, 1,391) and the export tax are all reminders that minority holders own the cash flows only after the state has set its terms.
02
Oil-price normalisation. 2Q26 Brent of $104.52 was 64% above 4Q25's $63.69. Quarterly net income moved from $2.9B to $10.4B between those quarters. A return to 2025 prices takes most of that away, and the TTM P/E of 5.5 would rise accordingly.
03
Execution and frontier risk. Growth depends on new FPSOs arriving on time and on exploration replacing Búzios over the next decade. Frontier exploration in deep water off Amapá and West Africa is expensive and uncertain, and production is near the 3.4M boed peak the plan sets for 2028–29.
Industry Commentary
The 2026 oil shock — windfall for producers, problem for governments
CRITICAL VARIABLE

Brent averaged $63.69 in 4Q25, $80.61 in 1Q26 and $104.52 in 2Q26, according to Petrobras's releases; the 1Q26 release ties the rise to "the start of the conflict in the Middle East". For an exporter with low-cost barrels, that is a straight transfer to the bottom line: Petrobras's exports rose from $5.68B to $12.67B of quarterly revenue in a year. For a government that owns the producer and also faces voters at the pump, it is a dilemma. Brazil's answer, visible line by line in the filings, has been to tax the export side and subsidise the domestic side, with Petrobras as the channel for both.

State-controlled oil companies and the minority shareholder
STRUCTURAL DISCOUNT

Petrobras's owner has goals beyond profit, and 2026's filings show them: pump prices held down through subsidy programmes, an export tax, and R$161B of taxes and government take in six months. The company's history also includes the "Car Wash" (Lava Jato) corruption investigation into bribes allegedly paid to executives over inflated construction contracts, which ended in settlements worth billions. The current framework is a written Shareholder Remuneration Policy, a Business Plan with a US$75B gross-debt limit and a US$6B minimum cash balance, and subsidy receipts reported filing by filing. The question each election poses is whether that framework holds.

Deepwater as the low-cost frontier
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Oil and NGL output in Brazil was 2,689 thousand barrels a day in 2Q26, up 15.2% in a year, as new FPSOs came on in the pre-salt. The 2026–30 plan names its exploration highlights: the Southern and Southeastern Basins, the Equatorial Margin, and assets in Colombia, São Tomé and Príncipe and South Africa. In 2026 Petrobras has reported two discoveries in the Equatorial Margin, a gas discovery in the Sandia-1 well off Colombia (announced August 3; Petrobras operates with 44.44%, Ecopetrol holds 55.56%), approval to negotiate for four blocks in Ghana's offshore Keta Basin (August 21), and signed for eight blocks in Côte d'Ivoire. The plan's US$7.1B exploration budget is small next to the US$109B total, but it is the part that decides whether production stays near 3.4M boed after the 2028–29 peak.
