01 Company Overview

PACCAR designs, builds and finances heavy- and medium-duty commercial trucks under three nameplates — Kenworth and Peterbilt in North America and DAF in Europe — and runs a parts distribution business and a captive finance arm alongside them. Kenworth and Peterbilt together held a 30% retail share of the U.S./Canada Class 8 market in 2025 (Class 8 = the heaviest highway trucks, over 33,000 lb gross vehicle weight — the tractors that pull 53-foot trailers). FY2025 revenue was $28.44B, down 15.5% from $33.66B, and net income was $2.38B ($4.51 per share), down 43% — the company's 87th consecutive year of profit, but a trough year by any measure. It ranks #392 globally by market capitalisation at $65.64B.

This is a cycle stock, and the whole page turns on one question: where in the cycle is 2026? The bull reading is that 2025 was the bottom — deliveries have already climbed from 32,900 in Q4 2025 to 38,700 in Q2 2026 with roughly 42,000 guided for Q3, gross margin has recovered from 12.0% to 14.4%, and a friendlier EPA 2027 emissions regime gives the industry a strong 2027 without a boom-bust pre-buy. The bear reading is that 2026's order strength is the pre-buy — the ACT Research figures show August Class 8 orders down 35% month-over-month as surcharge-free 2026 build slots filled — and that once 2027-model trucks carrying $6,000–12,000 of emissions cost start shipping into a freight market that is only just recovering, demand air-pockets. The stock at 26× trailing and 18.8× forward earnings is priced somewhere between those two outcomes.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

PREMIUM BRANDS & RESALE VALUES DEALER NETWORK DENSITY PARTS ANNUITY ON INSTALLED BASE CAPTIVE FINANCE (A+/A1) LOCAL-FOR-LOCAL FOOTPRINT IN-HOUSE MX ENGINES 87 YEARS OF UNBROKEN PROFIT

The moat is a premium-brand truck wrapped in two service businesses that make money regardless of whether the truck sells. Kenworth and Peterbilt hold a 30% share of a market where the buyer is a professional fleet manager who tracks total cost of ownership to the penny: fuel economy, uptime, driver retention (a comfortable cab is a recruiting tool) and, critically, resale value. High resale values feed PFS's residuals and lower the effective lease cost, which supports pricing on the new truck, which supports the brand. Around that sits the dealer network and Parts — a growing installed base of trucks that will need components for a decade — and the finance arm that keeps the customer in the family. The result is a truck OEM that has stayed profitable through every downturn since 1938, including a 2025 trough in which revenue fell 15.5% and the company still earned a 9.0% net margin and paid $2.72 per share in dividends. What the moat does not do is insulate volume: PACCAR builds to order, and orders are a function of freight rates, interest rates and regulation.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The cycle is the business. FY2023 revenue $35.13B and EPS $8.76; FY2025 revenue $28.44B and EPS $4.51. That is a 49% earnings decline from peak to trough in a business with 30% share and no competitive loss. Truck & Other gross margin compressed to roughly 9% (derived) in Q2 2026 even as the recovery began. Anyone paying 26× trailing earnings is implicitly underwriting a recovery to something between the trough and the 2023 peak.
▸
EPA 2027 NOx rule (NOx = nitrogen oxides; the 2027 standard cuts the limit to 35 milligrams per horsepower-hour). The EPA affirmed the 35 mg limit but proposed letting fleets keep buying current engines while paying a nonconformance penalty (NCP) that management estimates at $6,000–7,000 per truck, versus an $8,000–12,000 OEM upcharge for fully compliant engines (industry sources). The bull says this smooths the cycle; the bear says August 2026 orders falling 35% month-over-month (ACT) as surcharge-free 2026 slots filled is the pre-buy ending in real time.
▸
Section 232 truck tariffs and the Mexico footprint. A 25% Section 232 tariff on imported medium- and heavy-duty trucks and parts took effect November 1, 2025. PACCAR builds Peterbilts in Mexicali, Mexico, as well as in Texas, Ohio and Canada. Management's line is that "local-for-local manufacturing" has created "advantages to PACCAR" and "a stable tariff operating environment," with tariff surcharges removed for 2026 and a price-cost benefit that "will carry forward in Q3." The vulnerability is the opposite side of that sentence: the benefit depends on competitors (Daimler and Traton are the largest Mexico-built Class 8 suppliers) being hurt more than PACCAR, and on the USMCA treatment of Mexicali output staying as it is. Neither is in PACCAR's control.
▸
European litigation overhang. PACCAR booked a $264.5M after-tax non-recurring charge in Q1 2025 for civil litigation in Europe, with management saying it was "making good progress on substantially resolving the EU civil litigation." The sources fetched do not identify the underlying case; note that the task brief's reference to "DOJ/EU emissions matters" was not verified — what the filings actually disclose is a European civil litigation charge, not an emissions action. Whether further charges follow is unknown.

Notable One-Off Items

European civil litigation charge, $264.5M after-tax, Q1 2025. This is the single biggest distortion in the trailing-five-quarter numbers. It took Q1 2025 net income to $505.1M ($0.96) from an adjusted $769.6M ($1.46), and FY2025 GAAP net income of $2.38B ($4.51) compares with adjusted $2.64B ($5.01). It has now rolled out of the TTM window (which runs Q3 2025 – Q2 2026), so the $4.75 TTM EPS above is clean. It matters for the YoY comparisons, though: Q1 2026's headline "+20% net income" versus Q1 2025 is really a decline against the adjusted base ($605.3M vs $769.6M).

Tariff surcharge and price-cost. PACCAR applied tariff surcharges in 2025 and removed them for 2026; management described a positive price-cost effect in Q1 and a tariff benefit in Q2 that "will carry forward in Q3." This is a favourable one-off in the sense that it depends on a policy regime (the Nov 1, 2025 Section 232 tariff) that could change with a signature.

Supplier constraints in Q2 2026 left "several hundred trucks" undelivered in the U.S./Canada — a small timing shift that flatters Q3 relative to Q2.

Extra dividend. FY2025 dividends declared were $2.72 per share ($1.43B), of which roughly $1.40 (derived) was the December extra dividend. The TTM yield of 2.25% therefore depends on the extra being repeated; the regular quarterly rate alone would yield roughly 1.1% (derived). PACCAR has paid an extra dividend in most years but it is discretionary and sized to the cycle. No share repurchase programme was mentioned in the releases fetched — PACCAR returns capital as dividends, not buybacks.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the units, because everything else follows from them. PACCAR delivered 32,900 trucks in Q4 2025, 33,000 in Q1 2026, 38,700 in Q2, and guides ~42,000 for Q3. Company-wide Truck, Parts & Other gross margin moved 12.0% → 13.1% → 14.4% over the same three quarters and is guided to 14.5% with "further expansion" in Q4. Net income moved $557M → $605M → $752M. This is what an operating-leverage recovery looks like at a build-to-order manufacturer: every incremental truck over the break-even level carries a gross margin far above the average, so a 17% sequential volume gain (Q1→Q2) produced a 24% profit gain. The second-half math in management's own forecast — 145,000 U.S./Canada Class 8 retail sales versus 105,000 in the first half — implies the fourth quarter is the strongest of the year.

Then separate what is cyclical from what is not. Parts did $1.67B of pretax income in FY2025 and PFS $485M; together that is roughly $2.16B of the company's ~$3.07B of operating income — about 70% of trough-year profit from the two segments that are not the truck cycle (derived). Parts' gross margin of 29.8% versus a Truck & Other gross margin of about 9% (derived) is a three-to-one spread, and Parts revenue grew 3% in a year when truck revenue fell by mid-teens. This is why PACCAR earned a 9% net margin at the bottom and why the stock never traded below $90 during the downturn. Valued on its own, the Parts and PFS stream could plausibly support a substantial share of today's $65.6B market cap; the market is paying for the truck recovery on top.

The EPA 2027 question, stated precisely. Historically a tightening emissions standard produces a pre-buy (fleets rush to buy cheaper current-generation trucks) followed by a post-buy hangover. The 2027 NOx rule adds an estimated $8,000–12,000 per compliant truck. The EPA's proposal to permit continued sales of current engines under nonconformance penalties of roughly $6,000–7,000 per truck narrows that gap and, in PACCAR's view, converts a spike-and-crash into "a continued improved cycle through the balance of the year with a stronger 2027." The evidence against: August Class 8 orders fell 35% month-over-month (ACT) and FTR explicitly called it the end of the pre-buy. The evidence for: the 12-month order total of 350,677 units against a 2026 retail forecast of 230,000–270,000 means the backlog is oversubscribed by roughly 35,000 units — orders can fall sharply for months while builds keep rising. The tiebreaker arrives in the September–October MY2027 order data and in the Q4 guide on October 20.

Tariffs, honestly. The Section 232 tariff of 25% on imported medium- and heavy-duty trucks has, so far, been a net positive for PACCAR: surcharges came off for 2026 and management reports a price-cost benefit persisting into Q3. This rests on PACCAR's Texas, Ohio and Canadian plants versus competitors' heavier Mexican footprints — Daimler Truck and Traton's International are the largest Mexico-built Class 8 suppliers. PACCAR's own Mexicali plant means the exposure is not zero, and the specific USMCA treatment of its Mexican output was not verified from the sources fetched. A "benefit" that depends on trade policy staying exactly where it is should be discounted accordingly.

Valuation. At $124.70 the stock trades at 26.3× trailing EPS of $4.75, 18.8× forward on consensus FY2026 EPS of $5.93, 22.2× trailing free cash flow, and yields 2.25% including the extra dividend. The trailing multiple is meaningless on its own for a cyclical — the same price is 14.2× the FY2023 peak EPS of $8.76. The honest framing is mid-cycle: if FY2027 earnings land midway between the trough ($4.51) and the peak ($8.76), roughly $6.60, the stock is at about 19× — a full but not extreme multiple for a business with a 30% share, a 30%-margin parts annuity and 87 consecutive years of profit. If 2027 is a post-pre-buy hangover and earnings stall near $6, the multiple is 21× on flat growth and the stock has little support above the $110 low target. The Street's 13-of-19 Hold count says most analysts see exactly this: a good business, correctly priced.

Bull Case

The trough is behind: deliveries up 17% sequentially, gross margin up 240 bps in two quarters, EPS at $1.43 and rising, 2026 build slots 90% full, backlog oversubscribed by ~35,000 units. The EPA's NCP framework removes the boom-bust pre-buy and sets up a stronger 2027. Tariffs are a relative advantage for PACCAR's U.S. footprint. Parts ($1.67B pretax, 29.8% gross margin) and PFS ($485M) already cover most of the dividend and a large share of the market cap on their own. At 18.8× forward earnings with a PEG under 1 and a 0.97 beta, the stock offers cyclical upside with defensive characteristics.

Bear Case

2026's order surge was the pre-buy, and it just ended: ACT's August orders fell 35% month-over-month as surcharge-free slots filled. 2027-model trucks carry $6,000–12,000 of incremental emissions cost into a freight market that has only just stabilised. The Truck segment's gross margin is still only ~9% (derived), so a volume stall in 2027 flows straight to earnings. Tariff benefits reverse the day the policy does. Trailing revenue is still down 10.6% and TTM EPS is 46% below the 2023 peak; a 26× trailing multiple leaves no room for the recovery to pause, and the extra dividend that gets the yield to 2.25% is discretionary.

Top 3 Risks

01
A 2027 post-pre-buy air pocket. If fleets front-loaded 2026 purchases and balk at MY2027 pricing, deliveries could fall from the Q3/Q4 2026 run-rate even as the industry forecast says otherwise. The September–October order data and the October 20 Q4 guide are the first tests; a Q4 delivery guide below Q3's ~42,000 would be the tell.
02
Tariff regime change. PACCAR's current price-cost advantage is a function of a 25% Section 232 tariff that penalises competitors' Mexican output more than its own Mexicali plant. A renegotiation, exemption, or USMCA carve-out would remove the tailwind — and a broadening of tariffs to inputs PACCAR imports would turn it into a headwind.
03
Freight recession relapse hitting PFS and Parts together. The two "non-cyclical" segments are less cyclical, not acyclical. A renewed downturn would lower truck utilisation (fewer Parts sales), push used-truck values down (PFS residuals and credit losses on a $22.3B book), and compress the ~$2.2B of pretax income that currently makes the trough tolerable. Parts pretax income was already slightly down in the first half of 2026.
  Industry Commentary
EPA 2027: Pre-Buy or Smoothed Cycle?
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The 2027 NOx standard (35 mg/hp-hr) is the single largest variable in the North American Class 8 market for the next 18 months. Compliant engines carry an estimated $8,000–12,000 OEM upcharge; the EPA's proposed nonconformance-penalty path lets current engines keep selling at roughly $6,000–7,000 per truck. The 2026 order season closed up 39% at 350,677 units (FTR), far above the 230,000–270,000 retail forecast — a backlog oversubscribed by ~35,000 units. August orders then fell 35% month-over-month (ACT) as surcharge-free slots filled. FTR reads that as the pre-buy ending; ACT reads it as a capacity constraint. Every truck OEM's 2027 depends on which is right.
Section 232 Truck Tariffs Redraw the North American Map
ONGOING HEADWIND
Since November 1, 2025, imported medium- and heavy-duty trucks and parts face a 25% tariff. Mexico is the largest source: Daimler Truck (Saltillo, Santiago Tianguistenco), Traton's International (Escobedo) and PACCAR's Peterbilt (Mexicali) all build there, and Transport Topics reported in February 2026 that OEMs were largely maintaining Mexican output regardless. The net effect so far has been higher industry pricing and a relative advantage for the OEM with the most U.S. capacity — PACCAR — but the tariff is a headwind for the industry's cost base and for the fleets that buy from it, and it is a policy lever that can be pulled in either direction without warning.
Freight Rates Have Turned — and Europe Is Quietly Recovering
EMERGING TAILWIND
Management attributes the order recovery to "improved freight rates" as "constrained industry freight capacity" finally tightens after a multi-year trucking recession — carriers deferred replacement for years and are now returning to normal cycles. Used-truck values are up ~4% YoY, which lowers the effective cost of a new truck and de-risks PFS residuals. In Europe, PACCAR raised its 2026 >16-tonne forecast from 280,000–320,000 to ~310,000 during the year, DAF is picking up awards, and Q2 European deliveries were described as strong. Bull framing: a synchronised North American and European upcycle with a parts annuity underneath, at 18.8× forward earnings. Bear framing: freight rate improvement that is driven by capacity exiting, not demand growing, is fragile — and every recovery in this industry has ended with an emissions rule change or a tariff. This one has both scheduled for 2027.
