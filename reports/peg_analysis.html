01 Company Overview

Public Service Enterprise Group is a two-piece company that most investors treat as one. The larger piece, PSE&G, is New Jersey's dominant regulated electric and gas utility — poles, wires, pipes, meters, and a state-approved rate of return on the capital sunk into them. The smaller piece, PSEG Power & Other, owns a 3,758 MW carbon-free nuclear fleet that sells into the PJM wholesale market. FY2025 revenue was $12.17B, up 18.3% YoY, with GAAP net income of $2.11B ($4.22 per share) and non-GAAP operating earnings of $2.03B ($4.05 per share). PSE&G contributed $1,745M of the $2,029M in operating earnings — 86%. At a $36.1B market capitalisation PEG is a mid-sized member of the S&P 500 utility complex, not a mega-cap.

The central debate of this report is whether PEG is a regulated utility that happens to own reactors, or a nuclear merchant story wearing a utility's clothes. The market spent 2024 pricing it as the latter — the stock ran from $57.99 in January 2024 to $94.30 in November 2024 on the thesis that data-centre demand would let PSEG contract its nuclear output at premium prices. Twenty-two months later the stock closed at $72.48, within 0.6% of its 52-week low, and not one megawatt of contracted nuclear offtake has been announced. The regulated business kept compounding the whole time. What has actually deflated is the option, not the utility. Everything below is an attempt to size each half honestly.

This is the compounding machine, and it works the way every regulated utility works: PSE&G spends capital on transmission, distribution, gas main replacement and clean-energy programmes; the New Jersey Board of Public Utilities (BPU) approves the spend into rate base — the pool of invested capital on which the utility is permitted to earn a return — and customers pay that return through rates. Year-end 2025 rate base was approximately $36 billion, up 7% from 2024, and management guides to a 6–7.5% rate-base CAGR through 2030. The capital plan behind it is $24–28B in total for 2026–2030, of which $22.5–25.5B is regulated; 2026 regulated investment alone is roughly $4.2B, a 13.5% step-up on 2025. Q2 2026 segment operating earnings were $342M against $332M a year earlier. The October 2024 New Jersey distribution base-rate case settlement is the current revenue foundation; the authorised return on equity from that settlement was not verified from any page fetched for this report and is therefore not quoted. The affordability counterweight is real and visible: PSE&G has filed to lower residential gas bills by 5% effective October 1, 2026, which is what a utility does when the political temperature on bills is rising.

Fourteen percent of earnings, and close to one hundred percent of the argument. The fleet produced 30.9 TWh in FY2025 — carbon-free, always-on, sitting inside the PJM market at the eastern end of the biggest data-centre build-out in the country. Two things drive the segment. First, the nuclear production tax credit (PTC) created by the Inflation Reduction Act, which works as a floor rather than a subsidy: if realised power prices fall below a threshold, the credit tops the revenue back up; if prices exceed it, the credit pays nothing. PSEG's FY2025 disclosure is explicit that output was sold at prices exceeding the PTC threshold — meaning the floor was not in the money, the fleet was earning market upside, and the downside protection sits unused underneath. That is the best possible configuration: participate on the way up, protected on the way down. Second, the contracting option. Q2 2026 operating earnings of $83M versus $52M show the segment improving, but the equity was priced for something much larger: multi-year offtake agreements selling nuclear output directly to hyperscalers at a premium to market. Management's language remains conditional — the company says it is "pursuing opportunities incremental to our long-term forecast, including the potential to contract our nuclear output under multi-year agreements," and at the Q2 2026 call CEO LaRossa said only that "there's more opportunities for long-term PPA type or utility-like agreements, we see an opportunity that might present itself." No contracted megawatts, counterparties, pricing or nuclear units have been announced on any page fetched for this report. Note also that the segment's GAAP result swings violently against the operating figure — Q2 2026 GAAP was a $(8)M loss against $83M of operating earnings, almost entirely mark-to-market and nuclear decommissioning trust noise (see Section 04).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Exclusive NJ franchise $36B regulated rate base 3,758 MW carbon-free nuclear IRA PTC price floor PJM location premium 15 yrs dividend growth Self-funded capex plan

The core advantage is structural and boring: PSE&G holds a legally exclusive franchise to deliver electricity and gas across the densest, wealthiest part of New Jersey. Nobody can build a competing distribution network, and the return on invested capital is set by regulators rather than by competition. That converts $4.2B a year of 2026 capital spending into a contractually visible earnings stream and underwrites the 6–8% operating-earnings growth target through 2030. Layered on top is something most regulated peers do not have — a paid-off, carbon-free nuclear fleet with a federal price floor beneath it and a scarcity premium above it. The moat is not that PSEG can out-compete anyone. It is that the regulated half cannot be competed with at all, and the unregulated half owns an asset class nobody is building more of quickly.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Affordability politics in New Jersey. Rising bills driven partly by data-centre load growth are a live state-level issue, and the utility's own response — filing to cut residential gas bills 5% from October 1, 2026 — is evidence of the pressure. Regulated ROEs and rate-case outcomes are political as much as financial, and New Jersey is not a forgiving jurisdiction when customers are angry.
02
The nuclear contract that keeps not arriving. The large-load pipeline is enormous on paper — PSE&G disclosed 9.4 GW as of August 2025, almost all data centres, up 47% in three months — but management itself expects only 10–20% of interconnection inquiries to materialise. Talks have been "ongoing" for well over a year with no announced megawatts, counterparty or price. Every quarter without a deal drains the option value the 2024 share price capitalised.
03
Rate-sensitivity and leverage. A beta of 0.52 makes PEG a bond proxy, and bond proxies de-rate when long yields rise. Debt-to-equity is 1.42 with $24.68B of total debt against $192M of cash, and the funding plan for $24–28B of capex assumes no new equity. If rates or construction costs move against that plan, the no-dilution promise is the first casualty.
04
PJM market design risk. LaRossa has publicly criticised PJM's capacity-auction governance and said that absent change "we will be living with the outcome of that process." Capacity prices, load-forecast methodology and the reliability backstop auction are all in flux — and the nuclear segment's economics sit directly downstream of decisions PSEG does not control.

Notable one-off items — read operating earnings, not GAAP. PSEG's GAAP net income is structurally noisy because the company marks its energy hedges to market each quarter. A hedge that locks in a future power price is an economic stabiliser, but accounting forces its unrealised value through the income statement, so GAAP earnings move inversely to the very price swings the hedges are meant to neutralise. Non-GAAP operating earnings strip out those mark-to-market (MTM) gains and losses, nuclear decommissioning trust (NDT) fund movements, and other material infrequent items. The gap is not cosmetic: in Q2 2026, GAAP net income fell to $334M ($0.67) from $585M ($1.17), a 43% decline — while operating earnings rose to $425M ($0.86) from $384M ($0.77). At the segment level the divergence is starker still: PSEG Power & Other posted a GAAP loss of $(8)M against $83M of operating earnings. For FY2025 the relationship reversed — GAAP $4.22 above operating $4.05 — which is exactly the point: the difference is direction-less noise. Guidance, the dividend and the 6–8% growth target are all set on the operating measure, so that is the measure this report leads with. It is also, unavoidably, a company-defined number: taking it at face value means accepting management's definition of what is infrequent.

RSI compares the size of recent gains with the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 a stock has risen hard and fast and is called "overbought"; below 30 it has fallen hard and fast and is "oversold". It measures momentum, not value — an oversold reading says the selling has been persistent, not that the shares are cheap.

Five years, and almost nothing to show for it in price. PEG closed October 2021 at $63.80 and September 10, 2026 at $72.48 — a price-only gain of 13.6% over five years, roughly 2.6% a year, before dividends. Add a yield that has run in the 3–4% range and the total return is respectable for a defensive holding, but the capital appreciation has been almost entirely a round trip. The path is far more interesting than the endpoints.

Three distinct regimes. From late 2021 through the end of 2023 the stock did nothing at all — it traded between roughly $56 and $70 for twenty-six straight months while the Fed raised rates and every bond proxy de-rated; the October 2022 close of $56.07 was the low of that stretch. Then came the AI-utility trade. From $57.99 in January 2024 the stock ran 63% in ten months to $94.30 in November 2024, as the market decided that any owner of always-on carbon-free generation inside PJM was about to be paid a scarcity premium by hyperscalers. Since that peak it has been a slow, relentless deflation: $84.49 by December 2024, a failed rally to $89.79 in July 2025, a second failed rally to $86.07 in February 2026, and then eight of the last nine months lower into a $72.48 close that sits 23.1% below the November 2024 high and 0.6% above the 52-week low of $72.08.

The 6-period monthly RSI tells the same story without the narrative. It peaked near 90 in November 2024 — as overbought as this stock has ever been on monthly data — and has printed 29.1 at the September 2026 reading, below the 30 oversold line and the weakest momentum reading of the entire five-year window. The 3-month average crossed below the 10-month average in mid-2026 and both are now declining. That is not a bottoming pattern; it is a downtrend that has become stretched.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 16.1× forward earnings and collecting 3.7% for a business whose regulated half — 86% of operating earnings — grows rate base 6–7.5% annually on a $24–28B capital plan that management says needs no new equity and no asset sales. That alone supports the 6–8% operating-earnings growth target through 2030 and a sixteenth consecutive dividend increase. The 3,758 MW nuclear fleet is thrown in nearly free at these levels, with an IRA production tax credit underneath it as a price floor and a 9.4 GW large-load interconnection pipeline above it. One signed multi-year offtake contract re-rates the Power segment and re-opens the 2024 narrative.

Bear Case

The nuclear contract has been "in discussions" for over a year with no megawatts, counterparty or price disclosed, and management's own conversion assumption for interconnection inquiries is 10–20%. Strip the option out and this is a 0.52-beta bond proxy at 18× trailing earnings with a 1.42 debt-to-equity ratio, $276M of free cash flow against a $1.3B annual dividend bill, and 4.53% ROIC — a rate-sensitive utility funding a large capital programme with debt. New Jersey affordability politics are tightening at exactly the moment data-centre load is pushing bills higher, and every analyst revision in the past three months has been a target cut.

Top 3 Risks

01
The nuclear contract never lands on commercial terms worth having. PSEG is a price-taker in a negotiation against hyperscalers who have alternatives, and PJM's rules — capacity auctions, load forecasts, the reliability backstop — keep shifting underneath the deal. A contract signed at market rates rather than a premium would confirm the bear case as surely as no contract at all.
02
Regulatory and affordability compression in New Jersey. Rate base only earns what the BPU allows it to earn. With residential bills rising and data centres widely blamed, the political path of least resistance is a tougher next rate case, a lower authorised return, or cost allocations that shift large-load expense onto the utility. The voluntary 5% gas bill cut is a signal of which way the wind is blowing.
03
Rates, leverage and the no-equity promise. $24.68B of debt, $192M of cash, $276M of free cash flow and a $24–28B capital programme to fund. The plan assumes no new equity issuance; if long rates rise or construction costs run, that assumption breaks and the fix is dilution, a slower capital plan, or a strained dividend — and a 0.52-beta stock de-rates on higher long yields regardless.

Industry Commentary

CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
New Jersey has a generation problem it cannot build its way out of quickly
LaRossa's framing is blunt: "The facts are that there has not been any new baseload generation built in New Jersey for quite some time." Meanwhile PSE&G's large-load interconnection queue hit 9.4 GW as of August 2025 — up 47% in three months, almost all data centres. Against that, PSEG's entire nuclear fleet is 3,758 MW. Even discounted to management's own 10–20% conversion assumption, the demand signal exceeds anything the existing supply stack can serve, which is why scarcity pricing is plausible — and why bills, and therefore politics, are the binding constraint rather than physics.
ONGOING HEADWIND
PJM market design is unsettled, and PSEG does not control it
The nuclear segment's economics run through PJM's capacity auction, its load forecast and its new bilateral contracting and reliability backstop processes — all of which are being actively rewritten. LaRossa has publicly questioned the governance ("How crazy does it sound that the governors have to get a Members Committee vote to allow a vote to take place?") and warned that absent reform "we will be living with the outcome of that process." He declined to give specifics on PSEG's own supply proposals in August 2026 precisely because "PJM's plans are in flux, including its load forecast." Unquantifiable timing risk sits on top of a quantifiable asset.
EMERGING TAILWIND
The PTC floor makes the nuclear fleet a one-way bet — and frames the whole debate
The IRA nuclear production tax credit tops up revenue when realised power prices fall below a threshold and pays nothing when they exceed it. PSEG's FY2025 disclosure is that output — 30.9 TWh — was sold at prices above the threshold, so the fleet captured market upside with the floor sitting unused beneath it. Bull read: you own a call option on data-centre power pricing with the downside insured by federal statute, and the market is currently paying you almost nothing for it — the stock has given back its entire 2024 AI premium. Bear read: an option with no expiry date and no announced counterparty is just a story, and 14% of operating earnings cannot carry a valuation on its own. The regulated 86% has to do the work, and at 16.1× forward earnings with a 3.7% yield it is priced to do exactly that — no more.
