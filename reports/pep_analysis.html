01 Company Overview

PepsiCo is a global convenient-foods and beverage company built on two roughly equal engines: a North American snack business (Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos, Quaker) and a beverage business (Pepsi, Gatorade, Mountain Dew, Starbucks ready-to-drink, poppi) that spans owned bottling in North America and franchise concentrate models internationally. Fiscal 2025 net revenue was $93.93 billion, up 2.3% YoY on organic growth of just 1.7%, with company-wide organic volume down 2% and net effective pricing up 4% — the clearest possible statement of the problem: PepsiCo has been buying revenue with price while losing units. On a TTM basis revenue is $96.90 billion (+5.62%), boosted by acquisitions and currency rather than volume.

At a $191.26 billion market capitalization PepsiCo is the 110th most valuable listed company in the world and the 64th largest in the United States, sandwiched between Analog Devices ($190.10B) and McDonald's ($187.90B). That is a demotion: PepsiCo's market cap peaked near $248.89 billion in 2022 and has compounded negatively every year since. Over the trailing twelve months PepsiCo's market cap fell 7.1% while Coca-Cola's rose 27.0% — a ~34-point divergence between the two names that most investors still treat as substitutes. That gap is the entire reason Elliott Investment Management is on the register.

PepsiCo's moat is physical, not digital. Frito-Lay's direct-store-delivery network — company drivers who merchandise the salty-snack aisle themselves, several times a week, in tens of thousands of outlets — is close to unreplicable at scale and is why PepsiCo has historically been able to hold roughly half of a fragmented US category while charging a brand premium. That network also creates a second, subtler advantage: because PepsiCo shows up with both a snack truck and a beverage truck, it can bundle shelf negotiations and foodservice contracts in a way that a pure-play snack or pure-play beverage competitor cannot. The financial signature of the moat is visible in the returns — ROE of 51.51% and ROIC of 19.38% on a business trading at 18.1x trailing earnings. The vulnerability is that a DSD network is a fixed-cost asset: it is a superb moat when volumes grow and a millstone when they shrink, because the trucks run whether or not the bags move. With convenient-foods volume at −2% and North America beverage volume at −4%, PepsiCo is currently paying for a moat it is not fully using.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Structural volume decline in North American snacking. Four volume declines in six quarters is not a demand blip; it is a category re-rating. Price cuts of up to 15% on the flagship brands in February 2026 produced flat volume by Q2 — meaning PepsiCo surrendered price without recovering units. With measured salty-snack elasticity around −0.9, the arithmetic says discounting alone does not restore the P&L; the recovery has to come from mix, formats and reformulation.

2. Capital-heavy North American bottling. PepsiCo owns its US bottling; Coca-Cola largely does not. The internal comparison is damning — International Beverages Franchise earned a 41.8% Q2 operating margin on concentrate economics while PBNA earned roughly 14.5% owning the assets. This is the structural gap Elliott is attacking, and until it is addressed PepsiCo's consolidated net profit margin of 10.78% will keep sitting far below Coca-Cola's 28.6%.

3. GLP-1 and the health re-rating of indulgent snacking. PwC-cited data puts GLP-1 usage at 21% of US households as of May 2026, up from 9% in January 2025, with roughly 11–12% of US adults using the drugs specifically for weight loss. Approximately 58% of PepsiCo's annual revenue comes from food brands, giving it the highest direct exposure of any mega-cap staples name. Hershey has flagged the same dynamic. Reformulation and portion control are the defence, but they take years and cost margin.

4. Private-label share and consumer trade-down. US store-brand unit share hit a record high in the first half of 2026 while national-brand units declined. Salty snacks are among the easiest categories for a shopper to trade down in — the product is a commodity input in a branded bag — and the lower-income cohort that drives snack volume is the same cohort most squeezed by the "rising inflationary pressures" Laguarta cited on the Q2 call.

Notable One-Off Items

Impairments dominate the year-over-year optics — do not read the headline growth. FY2025 carried $1,993 million of intangible asset impairments, primarily against the Rockstar energy brand (acquired 2020) with additional charges against Be & Cheery, the Chinese snacks acquisition. Those write-downs are why FY2025 reported EPS of $6.00 fell 14% while core EPS of $8.14 was flat in constant currency. They are also why every FY2026 comparison looks spectacular: Q2 FY26 reported diluted EPS of $2.18 was up 137% and reported operating profit rose 125%, purely from lapping roughly $1.86 billion of prior-year charges. Core EPS of $2.20 was up just 4%, and core operating margin of 16.8% was down 40 basis points. Reported and core are telling opposite stories; core is the one that matters.

Restructuring and footprint actions. PepsiCo has announced three US manufacturing plant closures and an approximately 20% reduction in US SKUs beginning early 2026, alongside what management calls a "record year of productivity savings in 2026" from automation, digitalisation and simplification. Restructuring cash outflows fell year-over-year, contributing to operating cash flow of $2.365 billion for the 24-week period versus $996 million a year earlier — a 137% improvement that is partly genuine and partly the absence of prior-year cash charges.

Currency and M&A. FY26 guidance embeds roughly a 1-point foreign-exchange tailwind and roughly a 1-point M&A contribution — meaning implied reported revenue growth of 4–6% against organic growth of only 2–4%. Roughly half of the reported top-line growth investors see this year is not operating performance. The poppi acquisition is the visible driver inside PBNA's +7% Q2 revenue against a −4% North America beverage volume result.

SodaStream. The task brief asked specifically about a SodaStream write-down. No SodaStream-specific impairment was identifiable in the FY2025 or Q2 FY26 disclosures reviewed for this build; the named impairments were Rockstar and Be & Cheery. Absence of a figure here should be read as "not verified," not as "confirmed none."

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten down and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. On a dividend-adjusted basis PEP went from $129.26 in September 2021 to $140.47 in August 2026 — a cumulative total return of roughly +8.7%, or about 1.7% annualised over five years. For a business that raised its dividend every one of those years, virtually all of the return came from the coupon; the price did the opposite of compounding. The shape matters more than the endpoint: PEP rose steadily through the 2022 inflation era, when pricing power was the market's favourite trait, peaking at $170.73 in April 2023. It then de-rated for two full years to a trough of $125.07 in May 2025 — a −26.7% drawdown — as GLP-1 adoption, volume declines and the collapse of the pricing narrative all landed at once. The Elliott stake and the December investor day produced a sharp +33% rally off that low to $166.48 in February 2026, and the entire move has since been surrendered: June 2026 closed at $135.40 and the stock now sits at $140.13, below its 200-day moving average of $148.99 and sitting almost exactly on its 50-day of $140.05.

Relative performance. The comparison that defines the stock is not the index — it is Coca-Cola. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 18.1x trailing and 15.9x forward earnings for a 0.36-beta business with 19.4% ROIC, a 4.23% dividend yield backed by 54 consecutive years of increases, and $9.28B of free cash flow — at a multiple below both the staples sector (23.1x) and the peer median (~29x). The international half of the company already works: EMEA +10%, LatAm +15%, APAC +12% and IBF +11% in Q2 reported revenue, with IBF earning a 41.8% operating margin. Elliott is on the register with $4B and has already extracted a 100-bps margin commitment, a 20% SKU cut, three plant closures and board refreshment; the North America supply-chain decision due late 2026 is a genuine option on structural change. If refranchising ever happens, the gap between PepsiCo's 10.8% net margin and Coca-Cola's 28.6% is the size of the prize.

Bear Case

The core North American business is shrinking in units and the fix is not working. Four volume declines in six quarters, price cuts of up to 15% on the flagship brands that produced flat volume, North America beverage volume −4% while Coca-Cola runs +5%, and core operating margin down 40 bps despite a "record productivity year." GLP-1 usage reached 21% of US households by May 2026 from 9% in January 2025, private-label unit share is at a record high, and 58% of revenue sits in exactly the categories being abandoned. Meanwhile the December investor day explicitly did not announce a bottling separation, so the structural catalyst remains hypothetical — and the stock has already round-tripped the entire activist rally. A 3.13 PEG on ~5% consensus EPS growth says you are not being paid much to wait.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume decline proves structural, not cyclical. If GLP-1 adoption and deliberate snacking behaviour permanently reset per-capita salty-snack consumption, no amount of price cutting or SKU rationalisation restores the volume base — and PepsiCo is left carrying a fixed-cost DSD network sized for a larger business. The 21%-of-households GLP-1 figure more than doubled in sixteen months; there is no evidence yet of it plateauing.
02
The activist catalyst fizzles. Elliott endorsed the December plan without securing a bottling separation. If the late-2026 supply-chain update is incremental rather than structural, the option value currently embedded in the $155–$183 bull targets deflates, and the stock re-rates toward the $134–$145 cluster where Bernstein, Wells Fargo and Citi already sit. Elliott could also simply exit.
03
Balance sheet limits the response. $53.21B of total debt against $10.72B of cash, a 0.93 current ratio, 2.39 debt-to-equity and a 77.58% payout ratio leave little room. FY26 buybacks are budgeted at only $1.0B against $7.9B of dividends. If earnings disappoint further, PepsiCo cannot buy its way out, and the dividend streak — the single largest reason income investors own it — becomes the constraint rather than the attraction.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
GLP-1 Drugs and the Snacking Volume Reset
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
PwC-cited data puts GLP-1 usage at 21% of US households as of May 2026, up from 9% in January 2025 — a doubling-plus in sixteen months. Roughly 11–12% of US adults use the drugs specifically for weight loss, versus ~7% in the UK. Users buy fewer sweet treats and cut back on salty snacks. With approximately 58% of PepsiCo revenue in food brands, PepsiCo carries the highest direct exposure of any mega-cap staples name; Hershey has independently flagged the same demand reshaping. This is not a pricing problem that discounting solves — one industry observer framed it as consumers moving "from snacking on autopilot to making much more deliberate decisions." The February 2026 price cuts of up to 15% on Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos and Tostitos produced flat volume by Q2, which is the empirical test of that framing and PepsiCo failed it.
Trade-Down to Private Label
ONGOING HEADWIND
US store-brand unit share hit a record high in the first half of 2026 while national-brand units declined. Salty snacks are structurally easy to trade down in, and the lower-income cohort driving snack volume is the one Laguarta identified as constrained by "rising inflationary pressures." Measured salty-snack price elasticity of about −0.9 means a 15% price cut should theoretically generate ~13.5% more units; PepsiCo got flat. That gap implies the loss is share and occasion, not price. Note the geographic split in the same data: Kraft Heinz North America organic sales fell 2.7% in Q2 while its emerging markets grew 8.5%; Mondelēz North America grew 3.4% versus Latin America +8.4% and AMEA +7.1%. The trade-down is a developed-market phenomenon, which is precisely why PepsiCo's international divisions are outgrowing its domestic ones.
Activist Pressure on Food Conglomerates — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND
Elliott's $4B PepsiCo position is part of a broader activist thesis that conglomerate food and beverage structures destroy value versus focused, asset-light operators — Kraft Heinz, Campbell's and Smucker have all cut plants amid the same slowdown. Bull framing: PepsiCo's own International Beverages Franchise proves the model, earning a 41.8% Q2 operating margin on concentrate economics while PBNA needs 4.8x the revenue to produce comparable profit. Refranchise North American bottling and the 18-point net-margin gap to Coca-Cola becomes addressable; Elliott has already banked a 100-bps three-year margin commitment, a 20% SKU cut and board refreshment. Bear framing: the December 9, 2025 investor day announced no bottling separation and no refranchising — only productivity. Elliott endorsed it anyway. The stock priced in the unlock by February at $166.48 and has given all of it back. Investors are now being asked to underwrite a structural change that management has explicitly declined to commit to, with the next update not due until late 2026.
