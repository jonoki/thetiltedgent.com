01 Company Overview

PepsiCo is a global convenient-foods and beverage company built on two roughly equal engines: a North American snack business (Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos, Quaker) and a beverage business (Pepsi, Gatorade, Mountain Dew, Starbucks ready-to-drink, poppi) that spans owned bottling in North America and franchise concentrate models internationally. Fiscal 2025 net revenue was $93.93 billion, up 2.3% YoY on organic growth of just 1.7%, with company-wide organic volume down 2% and net effective pricing up 4% — PepsiCo had been buying revenue with price while losing units. The third quarter of 2026 (12 weeks to September 5) showed the first turn: net revenue of $25.27 billion, up 5.6%, organic revenue up 3.1%, and total organic volume up 0.5%. But the growth came from abroad — organic revenue rose 9% in EMEA and Asia Pacific Foods, 7% at International Beverages Franchise and 6% in Latin America — while North American beverage volume fell 3% and North American foods revenue was flat. On a TTM basis revenue is $98.24 billion.

At a $171.66 billion market capitalization (1,362.7 million shares at the Oct 9, 2026 close of $125.97, our calculation), PepsiCo ranks #67 in the S&P 500 and #35 in the Nasdaq-100 on that date (our calculation). The stock has finished every year since 2022 lower on the chart's year-end closes — $180.66 (2022), $169.84 (2023), $152.06 (2024), $143.52 (2025) — and now sits 34% below its April 2023 month-end high of $190.89. Over the past year (Oct 9, 2025 close to Oct 9, 2026 close, price only) PepsiCo's shares fell 12.95% while Coca-Cola's rose 32.67%, a gap of about 46 points between two names most investors still treat as substitutes. On Oct 8 the company cut its 2026 core earnings outlook, saying the North American fix is taking longer than planned and that more structural cost cuts are coming.

This is the historical profit engine and now the problem child. Frito-Lay's structural advantage is a direct-store-delivery (DSD) route network — PepsiCo's own drivers stock the shelf — which historically produced snack operating margins well above beverages. That machine is now running in reverse: PFNA organic revenue fell 2% in FY2025 with core constant-currency operating profit down 6%. Q3 FY26 is the first quarter where units moved: PFNA organic volume rose 0.5%, but effective net pricing fell 1%, so net revenue was flat at $6,504M (vs $6,526M a year earlier). The release describes "savory snacks volume growth and volume market share improvement, offset by lower effective net pricing." Profit went the other way: core operating profit fell 12% to $1,382M, a core margin of about 21%, as the lower pricing came through. Laguarta said the new cost cuts are partly meant to "mitigate the impacts of rising input cost inflation." Units are finally up, but each one now earns less.

PepsiCo's moat is physical, not digital. Frito-Lay's direct-store-delivery network — company drivers who merchandise the salty-snack aisle themselves, several times a week, in tens of thousands of outlets — is close to unreplicable at scale and is why PepsiCo has historically been able to hold roughly half of a fragmented US category while charging a brand premium. That network also creates a second, subtler advantage: because PepsiCo shows up with both a snack truck and a beverage truck, it can bundle shelf negotiations and foodservice contracts in a way that a pure-play snack or pure-play beverage competitor cannot. The financial signature of the moat is visible in the returns — ROE of 52.19% and ROIC of 19.68% on a business trading at 15.85x trailing earnings. The vulnerability is that a DSD network is a fixed-cost asset: it is a superb moat when volumes grow and a millstone when they shrink, because the trucks run whether or not the bags move. Q3 FY26 showed both sides: North American foods volume turned up half a point, but only after price cuts, and PBNA organic volume fell 3%. PepsiCo is still paying for a moat it is not fully using.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Volume in North American snacking is coming back slowly and expensively. PFNA organic revenue fell 2% in FY2025. Price cuts of up to 15% on the flagship brands in February 2026 produced flat volume by Q2 and +0.5% PFNA organic volume in Q3, while effective net pricing fell 1% and core operating profit fell 12%. Discounting alone has not restored the P&L; the recovery has to come from mix, formats and reformulation, and CFO Steve Schmitt told investors on Oct 8 that it is taking more time than planned.

2. Capital-heavy North American bottling. PepsiCo owns its US bottling; Coca-Cola largely does not. The internal comparison is stark — International Beverages Franchise earned a 40.3% Q3 core operating margin on concentrate economics while PBNA earned about 13% owning the assets. This is the structural gap Elliott is attacking, and until it is addressed PepsiCo's consolidated net profit margin of 11.09% will keep sitting far below Coca-Cola's 28.6%.

3. GLP-1 and the health re-rating of indulgent snacking. PwC data puts the share of US households with a current GLP-1 user at 21% as of May 2026, up from 9% in January 2025. About 58% of PepsiCo's revenue comes from convenient foods, the categories GLP-1 users cut back on. Reformulation and portion control are the defence, but they take years and cost margin.

4. Private-label share and consumer trade-down. US store-brand unit share reached a record 23.8% in the first half of 2026 while national-brand units fell 0.5% (PLMA and Circana). Salty snacks are among the easiest categories for a shopper to trade down in — the product is a commodity input in a branded bag — and in Q3 Laguarta said new cost cuts are needed partly to offset rising input-cost inflation, which leaves less room to keep funding lower prices.

Notable One-Off Items

Impairments dominate the year-over-year optics — do not read the headline growth. FY2025 carried $1,993 million of intangible asset impairments, primarily against the Rockstar energy brand (acquired 2020) with additional charges against Be & Cheery, the Chinese snacks acquisition. Those write-downs are why FY2025 reported EPS of $6.00 fell 14% while core EPS of $8.14 was flat in constant currency. They are also why the FY2026 comparisons look strong: year-to-date reported EPS of $6.10 is up 47% on the 36 weeks of 2025, while core EPS of $6.15 is up just 5%. In Q3 alone reported EPS of $2.23 rose 17% and core EPS of $2.34 rose 2% (1.5% in constant currency); core operating margin of 16.9% was down 35 basis points. Reported and core are telling different stories; core is the one that matters.

Tariff refunds. The Q3 release says core operating profit growth of 3% included a 4-percentage-point favourable impact of tariff refunds — without them, core operating profit would have fallen. That is a one-off: refunds of tariffs paid in an earlier year do not recur, so the 2027 comparison has to be earned without them.

Restructuring and footprint actions. PepsiCo has announced three US manufacturing plant closures and an approximately 20% reduction in US SKUs beginning early 2026, and on Oct 8 said further structural cost reductions will be implemented in the coming months. Restructuring charges were $192 million in Q3 and $373 million year-to-date. Operating cash flow for the 36 weeks was $7.95 billion versus $5.47 billion a year earlier, and capital spending fell to $2.18 billion from $2.50 billion.

Currency and M&A. The updated FY26 guidance embeds roughly a 1.5-point foreign-exchange tailwind and roughly a 1.5-point M&A contribution — reported revenue growth of about 6% against organic growth of about 3%. Roughly half of the reported top-line growth this year is not operating performance. Acquisitions supplied about 6 points of PBNA's +5% Q3 revenue, against a −3% PBNA organic volume result.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 15.85x trailing and 15.0x forward earnings for a 0.36-beta business with 19.7% ROIC, a 4.70% dividend yield backed by 54 consecutive years of increases, and $10.47B of free cash flow — at a multiple below both the staples sector (21.4x) and the peer median (~29x). The international half of the company already works: Q3 organic revenue +9% in EMEA and Asia Pacific Foods, +7% at IBF and +6% in Latin America, with IBF earning a 40.3% core operating margin. And Q3 brought the first sign that the North American price cuts are working: PFNA organic volume +0.5% and, per the release, volume market-share gains in savory snacks. Elliott has already extracted a 100-bps margin commitment, a 20% SKU cut, three plant closures and board refreshment; the North America supply-chain decision due late 2026 is a genuine option on structural change. If refranchising ever happens, the gap between PepsiCo's 11.1% net margin and Coca-Cola's 28.6% is the size of the prize.

Bear Case

The North American fix is costly and, in the CFO's words on Oct 8, taking more time than planned. Price cuts of up to 15% on the flagship brands bought half a point of PFNA volume in Q3 while its core operating profit fell 12%; PBNA organic volume fell 3% while Coca-Cola ran +5% in its last reported quarter; core operating margin fell 35 bps even though one-off tariff refunds added 4 points to core operating profit growth; and 2026 core EPS growth was cut to +2.5% to +3.5%. US households with a GLP-1 user reached 21% by May 2026 from 9% in January 2025, private-label unit share is at a record high, and 58% of revenue sits in convenient foods. Meanwhile the December plan explicitly did not announce a bottling separation, so the structural catalyst remains hypothetical — and the stock has already given back the entire activist rally and more. A 2.75 PEG on low single-digit EPS growth says you are not being paid much to wait.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume decline proves structural, not cyclical. If GLP-1 adoption and deliberate snacking behaviour permanently reset per-capita salty-snack consumption, no amount of price cutting or SKU rationalisation restores the volume base — and PepsiCo is left carrying a fixed-cost DSD network sized for a larger business. The 21%-of-households GLP-1 figure more than doubled in sixteen months.
02
The activist catalyst fizzles. Elliott endorsed the December plan without securing a bottling separation. If the late-2026 supply-chain update is incremental rather than structural, the option value in the remaining bull targets ($157 at BNP Paribas Exane, $165 at Goldman Sachs) deflates, and the Street converges on the $132–$137 cluster where Deutsche Bank, Citi and JPMorgan already sit. Elliott could also simply exit.
03
Balance sheet limits the response. $51.88B of debt against $10.68B of cash, a 0.94 current ratio, 2.33 debt-to-equity and a 74.5% payout ratio on reported EPS leave little room. FY26 buybacks are budgeted at only $1.0B against $7.9B of dividends. If earnings disappoint further, PepsiCo cannot buy its way out, and the dividend streak — the single largest reason income investors own it — becomes the constraint rather than the attraction.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
GLP-1 Drugs and the Snacking Volume Reset
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
PwC data puts the share of US households with a current GLP-1 user at 21% as of May 2026, up from 9% in January 2025 — a doubling-plus in sixteen months. About 58% of PepsiCo revenue comes from convenient foods, the categories users cut back on. If the drugs change what people want to eat, discounting does not fix it: the February 2026 price cuts of up to 15% on Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos and Tostitos produced flat volume by Q2 and only +0.5% PFNA organic volume in Q3, so far very few units for the money.
Trade-Down to Private Label
ONGOING HEADWIND
US store-brand unit share reached a record 23.8% in the first half of 2026 while national-brand units fell 0.5% (PLMA and Circana). Salty snacks are structurally easy to trade down in, and price cuts of up to 15% bought PepsiCo flat volume in Q2 and +0.5% in Q3. Note the geographic split in the same data: Kraft Heinz North America organic sales fell 2.7% in Q2 while its emerging markets grew 8.5%; Mondelēz North America grew 3.4% versus Latin America +8.4% and AMEA +7.1%. The trade-down is a developed-market phenomenon, which is precisely why PepsiCo's international divisions are outgrowing its domestic ones.
Activist Pressure on Food Conglomerates — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND
Elliott's $4B PepsiCo position is part of a broader activist thesis that conglomerate food and beverage structures destroy value versus focused, asset-light operators — Kraft Heinz, Campbell's and Smucker have all cut plants amid the same slowdown. Bull framing: PepsiCo's own International Beverages Franchise proves the model, earning a 40.3% Q3 core operating margin on concentrate economics while PBNA earns about 13%. Refranchise North American bottling and the 17-point net-margin gap to Coca-Cola (11.1% vs 28.6%) becomes addressable; Elliott has already banked a 100-bps three-year margin commitment, a 20% SKU cut and board refreshment. Bear framing: the December 8, 2025 plan announced no bottling separation and no refranchising — only productivity. Elliott endorsed it anyway. The stock priced in the unlock by February at $169.74 and has given all of it back and more. Investors are being asked to underwrite a structural change that management has not committed to; the Q3 release of Oct 8 offered more cost cuts, not a structural decision, with the North America update still pending for late 2026.
