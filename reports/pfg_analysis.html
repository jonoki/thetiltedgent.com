01 Company Overview

Principal started life in 1879 as The Bankers Life Association, founded in Des Moines by Edward Temple. A century and a half later it is less a life insurer than a retirement plumber: it runs 401(k) plans for employers, manages money for institutions and individuals, runs pension businesses abroad, and sells group dental, disability and life cover to small and mid-sized employers. At the end of Q3 2026 it had $785.0B of assets under management and $1,840.3B of assets under administration. Trailing-twelve-month revenue was $16.63B (+5.2%) and GAAP net income $1.98B, or $9.00 per share.

The stock closed 2025 at $88.21 and settled on October 30, 2026 at $111.63 — up 26.6% year to date, and 34.9% above its October 30, 2025 close of $82.74 (Yahoo Finance). The run-up has two visible pieces. One is earnings growth: Q3 2026 non-GAAP operating EPS was $2.22, up 6% ($2.49 excluding significant variances, up 7%; significant variances are the one-off actuarial and market-driven items management strips out), and management said that on that adjusted basis EPS is up 12% year to date, at the top of its 9–12% target. The other is a news item from Seoul: in early September Korean press reported that Samsung Life is seeking roughly a 15% stake in the company, a deal sized at $3.6–4.4B, and on September 3 PFG closed at $118.51, up 6.5% on the day. When asked about it on the Q3 call, management said it does not comment on market rumours. The October 30 close is 5.8% below that September 3 close.

The Q3 print was a mixed one. Operating EPS of $2.22 was below the $2.46 consensus on Earnings Whispers, and pre-tax operating earnings fell in four of the five business units (Section 02). GAAP net income was different: $631.9M, or $2.91 a share, because the exited business, a block of reinsured policies Principal no longer sells, swung from a $251.8M loss a year ago to a $277.1M gain, mostly an accounting value change on a reinsurance contract. Over nine months GAAP EPS is $6.68 against operating EPS of $6.79, so the two measures are close this year, which they were not in 2025 (GAAP EPS for the year was $5.25). At $111.63 the stock trades at 12.4x trailing GAAP EPS of $9.00 and about 12.1x consensus FY2026 operating EPS of $9.25 (StockAnalysis).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

401(k) recordkeeping scale Employer switching cost Payroll-linked recurring deposits Small/mid-business distribution Cross-sell: plan + benefits + funds RBC ~380% capital cushion

The moat is inertia with a payroll attached. Once an employer's 401(k) plan, dental plan and disability cover sit with one provider, moving them means re-enrolling every employee, re-mapping payroll feeds and taking the blame if anything breaks. Few HR departments volunteer for that. Every pay cycle then deposits new money into the plan without anyone deciding to — recurring deposits rose 8% in Q3 2026, to about $13B. The vulnerability is that inertia protects the account, not the investment choice inside it: participants and plan sponsors can and do move money from Principal's active funds to cheaper index products while leaving the recordkeeping where it is. That is the −$10.7B quarter.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Fees ride the market, in both directions. Retirement and asset-management revenue is a percentage of balances. AUM was flat year over year (+0.1%) while net cash flow over the last twelve months was −$25.5B — the market held the balance up. A 20% equity drawdown would shrink the fee base without a single client leaving.
02
Active-equity outflows. −$10.7B of AUM net cash flow in Q3 2026 (−$11.1B in Q2), concentrated in select active equity strategies. Investment Management pre-tax earnings fell 3%. Private markets (about $0.8B of inflows in Q3) and active ETFs (about $0.5B) are the answer management offers; they are not yet big enough to be one.
03
GAAP versus operating. In Q3 2026 GAAP net income was $631.9M against $481.8M of non-GAAP operating earnings, because the exited business contributed $277.1M (a year earlier it lost $251.8M); excluding it, non-GAAP net income fell 24% to $354.8M. The gap runs in both directions from quarter to quarter, and FY2025 GAAP EPS of $5.25 was well below what management reported on its operating measure. Investors valuing on operating EPS are trusting that the excluded items keep being non-recurring.
04
A benefits book running below its normal loss ratio. Specialty Benefits had a 59.0% loss ratio in Q3 2026, under the 60–64% range management targets, and individual disability claims ran higher in the quarter, per the call. Loss ratios tend to drift back toward their long-run range; a return to it would take back part of the recent gain.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported figures. First, the exited business: in Q3 2026 GAAP net income was $631.9M, while non-GAAP net income excluding it was $354.8M, down 24% (the exited-business line was +$277.1M against −$251.8M a year earlier). Second, significant variances — actuarial and market-driven items management strips out — made the difference between $2.22 reported and $2.49 underlying operating EPS in Q3. Third, buybacks: with the share count down to 212.8M from 217.4M at year-end 2025, per-share growth runs ahead of total-dollar growth (operating earnings +2% vs operating EPS +6% in Q3).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $68.58 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $111.63, PFG gained 62.8% on price, before dividends. The shape is two regimes. For four years, 2021 to 2025, the stock went sideways in a wide band — roughly $65 to $93 on month-end closes — with sharp drops whenever markets wobbled. Then from late 2025 it broke out and climbed almost in a straight line: December 2025 $88.21, April 2026 $100.91, July 2026 $113.70. Since then it has stalled: the highest month-end close is still July's $113.70 and the October 30 close is 1.8% below it, while the highest daily close was $118.51 on September 3.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 74.3, after peaking at 84.8 in July 2026 — the highest reading in the five-year window. The 10-month moving average sits at about $104.00, 7% below the price. The price is still above its own recent average, but the momentum of the spring and summer has faded, and the Street's average target sits below the price.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 12x consensus 2026 earnings and ~2x book for a business earning a 16.6% operating ROE (excluding significant variances), with operating EPS up 12% year to date on that basis, returning $1.5–1.8B a year to shareholders and holding $1.5B of excess capital with an RBC ratio near 380%. The profit engine is retirement recordkeeping — sticky, payroll-fed, with recurring deposits up 8%. The Specialty Benefits loss ratio is under the company's own range and Beam adds a digital channel. And a strategic buyer — Samsung Life — is reportedly seeking a premium-priced 15% of the company, which would say something about what an informed insurer thinks the franchise is worth.

Bear Case

The stock sits 1.9% above the average analyst target ($109.50) and within 12% of the highest ($125); only 2 of 12 analysts rate it a Buy, and five of the eight post-print target changes were cuts. Q3 operating EPS of $2.22 missed the $2.46 consensus and four of five business units earned less than a year ago. Net flows were −$10.7B in Q3 and −$25.5B over twelve months — AUM is being held up by the market, not by clients. The Specialty Benefits loss ratio is below the company's own normal range and may drift back. Buybacks are now expected toward the low end of the range to pay for Beam. And part of the September gain rested on a reported Samsung stake that neither company has announced; if talks fail, that premium comes out.

Top 3 Risks

01
Equity-market exposure through fees. Retirement and asset-management fees are a percentage of balances. The same mechanism that held AUM flat on negative flows would shrink it in a drawdown — and PFG fell 17.0% in March 2023 and 12.1% in April 2025 when markets turned.
02
The Samsung premium rests on a press report. The 6.5% gain on September 3 came on a report of early-stage talks; management declined to comment on it at the Q3 call and neither company has announced anything. The deal structure is unknown, and a stake bought in the market is a different outcome for existing holders from new shares issued at a premium.
03
Active-management outflows. −$10.7B of Q3 net cash flow, concentrated in select active equity strategies, and Investment Management earnings down 3%. If the private-markets and ETF push does not scale, one-fifth of the earnings base stays flat to shrinking while the rest has to carry growth.
Industry Commentary
Retirement recordkeeping as a scale game
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Workplace retirement in the US has large, scaled recordkeepers, and Principal bought into that group — it agreed in 2019 to buy Wells Fargo's institutional retirement and trust business for $1.2B. Scale matters because recordkeeping is a fixed-cost technology business: each extra participant costs little to serve. The Q3 2026 numbers show the pipeline but not yet the payoff — net revenue rose 1% to $757.3M and recurring deposits rose 8% to about $13B, while pre-tax earnings fell 4% and the margin eased to 39.3% from 41.3%. Samsung Life's reported interest points the same way: a large Asian life insurer is reportedly looking for exposure to exactly this market.

Active to passive — the asset-management squeeze
ONGOING HEADWIND

Across the industry, money keeps moving from actively managed equity funds to lower-fee index products. Principal's −$10.7B Q3 net cash flow was concentrated in select active equity strategies. The industry's answer — private markets, active ETFs — is Principal's answer too (about $0.8B and $0.5B of inflows in Q3). Peer T. Rowe Price, an active-equity specialist, is worth less than PFG ($22.17B against $23.71B on October 30, our calculation from closing prices and shares outstanding).

Asian insurers buying into US retirement
EMERGING THEME

The Samsung Life report came paired with a Samsung Fire & Marine bid for the Lloyd's insurer Canopius — 7–9 trillion won (about $5.1–6.6B) of combined outbound deals, per the Korea Economic Daily as reported by Insurance Business, as Samsung's insurers look for growth beyond a saturated home market. The bull framing: a strategic buyer sets a reference price, and a 15% holder that plans to stay could steady the shareholder base. The bear framing: nothing is announced, management declined to comment, and a stock that trades above its average target partly on a rumoured premium has little room if the rumour fades. The Samsung story is an option, not a floor.
