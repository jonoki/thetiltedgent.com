01 Company Overview

Principal started life in 1879 as Bankers Life Insurance Company, set up by a group of Des Moines bankers. A century and a half later it is less a life insurer than a retirement plumber: it runs 401(k) plans for employers, manages money for institutions and individuals, runs pension businesses abroad, and sells group dental, disability and life cover to small and mid-sized employers. At the end of Q2 2026 it had $808.0B of assets under management and $1,892.4B of assets under administration. Trailing-twelve-month revenue was $15.69B (+3.7%) and GAAP net income $1.56B, or $7.03 per share.

The story of 2026 is that the market finally started paying for it. The stock closed 2025 at $88.21 and settled on September 21, 2026 at $116.47 — up 32.0% year to date, with the stock more than 40% above its level a year ago (+42.54% 52-week change). Two things did the work. The first was steady execution: Q2 2026 non-GAAP operating EPS rose 16% to $2.50, and operating ROE hit 16.4%. The second came from Seoul: in early September Korean press reported that Samsung Life is seeking roughly a 15% stake in the company, a deal sized at $3.6–4.4B — and on September 3 PFG closed up 7.28% at $118.51. Neither side has commented publicly; talks were described as early.

The tell for this report is the gap between two earnings numbers. Principal's own scorecard — operating EPS — was $8.55 for FY2025. The GAAP number for the same year was $5.25. For a life insurer and asset manager that gap is normal (bond-portfolio marks, hedge accounting, businesses being run off), but it means the headline P/E and the P/E management would quote are two different bets. At $116.47 the stock trades at 16.6x trailing GAAP EPS but only about 12.2x consensus FY2026 EPS of $9.55.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

401(k) recordkeeping scale (top-3 US) Employer switching cost Payroll-linked recurring deposits Small/mid-business distribution Cross-sell: plan + benefits + funds RBC ~400% capital cushion

The moat is inertia with a payroll attached. Once an employer's 401(k) plan, dental plan and disability cover sit with one provider, moving them means re-enrolling every employee, re-mapping payroll feeds and taking the blame if anything breaks. Few HR departments volunteer for that. Every pay cycle then deposits new money into the plan without anyone deciding to — recurring deposits rose 6% in Q2 2026. The vulnerability is that inertia protects the account, not the investment choice inside it: participants and plan sponsors can and do move money from Principal's active funds to cheaper index products while leaving the recordkeeping where it is. That is the −$11.1B quarter.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Fees ride the market, in both directions. Retirement and asset-management revenue is a percentage of balances. AUM grew 7% YoY while net flows were negative — the market did the work. A 20% equity drawdown would shrink the fee base without a single client leaving.
02
Active-equity outflows. −$11.1B of AUM net cash flow in Q2 2026, concentrated in US active equity. Investment Management pre-tax earnings grew just 1%. Private markets (+10% AUM) and active ETFs ($2B trailing inflows) are the answer management offers; they are not yet big enough to be one.
03
GAAP versus operating. FY2025 GAAP EPS was $5.25 against operating EPS of $8.55 — GAAP was 61% of the number management reports. In Q2 2026, GAAP net income of $403.4M was 74% of $547.0M of operating earnings, with the exited business alone accounting for about $132M of the difference ($535.0M non-GAAP net income excluding exited business, less $403.4M). Investors valuing on operating EPS are trusting that the excluded items keep being non-recurring.
04
A benefits book that has just had a very good year. Specialty Benefits earnings rose 25% on a 57.4% loss ratio — well below the 60–64% range management itself treats as normal. Loss ratios mean-revert; a return to the range would take back part of this year's growth.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $64.40 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $116.47, PFG gained 80.9% on price, before dividends (the annual dividend rose from $2.44 in 2021 to $3.08 in 2025). The shape is two regimes. For four years, 2021 to 2025, the stock went sideways in a wide band — roughly $65 to $93 on month-end closes — with sharp drops whenever markets wobbled. Then from late 2025 it broke out and climbed almost in a straight line: December 2025 $88.21, April 2026 $100.91, July 2026 $113.70. The Sep 21 close is the highest point on the monthly chart, though not the highest daily close (that was $118.51 on September 3).

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 79.4, in overbought territory, after peaking at 84.8 in July 2026 — the highest reading in the five-year window. The 10-month moving average sits at about $102.11, 12% below the price. That is what a trend looks like, not a signal to sell it; but it does mean the price has moved faster than its own history, while the Street's average target sits below it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 12x consensus 2026 earnings and ~2x book for a business earning a 16.4% operating ROE, growing operating EPS 16% in the latest quarter, returning $1.5–1.8B a year to shareholders and holding $1.6B of excess capital with an RBC ratio near 400%. The profit engine is retirement recordkeeping — sticky, payroll-fed, and growing margins (41.5%). Specialty Benefits is producing record earnings and Beam adds a digital channel. And a strategic buyer — Samsung Life — is reportedly willing to pay a premium for 15% of the company, which says something about what an informed insurer thinks the franchise is worth.

Bear Case

The stock has gone through the average analyst target ($111.83) and sits within 7% of the highest ($125); only 2 of 12 analysts rate it a Buy. On GAAP earnings it is a 16.6x stock, not a 12x one, and the gap between GAAP and operating EPS was $3.30 per share in FY2025. Net flows were −$11.1B in Q2 — AUM growth is coming from the market, not from clients. The Specialty Benefits loss ratio is below the company's own normal range and will likely revert. Monthly RSI of 79.4 says the move has been one-sided, and part of the September gain rests on a reported, unconfirmed Samsung stake. If talks fail, that premium comes out.

Top 3 Risks

01
Equity-market exposure through fees. Retirement and asset-management fees are a percentage of balances. The same mechanism that grew AUM 7% on negative flows would shrink it in a drawdown — and PFG fell 17.0% in March 2023 and 12.1% in April 2025 when markets turned.
02
The Samsung premium is unconfirmed. The +7.28% September 3 session came on a press report of early-stage talks that neither party has confirmed. The deal structure is unknown, and a stake bought in the market is a different outcome for existing holders from new shares issued at a premium.
03
Active-management outflows. −$11.1B of Q2 net cash flow, concentrated in US active equity, and Investment Management earnings up only 1%. If the private-markets and ETF push does not scale, one-fifth of the earnings base stays flat to shrinking while the rest has to carry growth.
Industry Commentary
Retirement recordkeeping as a scale game
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

US workplace retirement is consolidating around a few very large recordkeepers, and Principal bought its way into that group — Wells Fargo's institutional retirement and trust business in 2019 for $1.2B, the Ascensus ESOP book in 2024. Scale matters because recordkeeping is a fixed-cost technology business: each extra participant costs little to serve. The Q2 2026 numbers show the leverage — Retirement and Income Solutions margin up 60bps to 41.5% — and the 30% jump in transfer deposits shows the pipeline. Samsung Life's reported interest points the same way: a large Asian life insurer is reportedly looking for exposure to exactly this market.

Active to passive — the asset-management squeeze
ONGOING HEADWIND

Across the industry, money keeps moving from actively managed equity funds to lower-fee index products. Principal's −$11.1B Q2 net cash flow was concentrated in US active equity. The industry's answer — private markets, active ETFs — is Principal's answer too (private-markets AUM +10%, active ETFs $2B trailing inflows). Peer T. Rowe Price, an active-equity specialist, has seen its market cap fall 3.1% over the past year to $22.32B while PFG rose 38.4%: the market is paying for recordkeeping and benefits, not for active stock-picking.

Asian insurers buying into US retirement
EMERGING THEME

The Samsung Life report came paired with a Samsung Fire & Marine bid for the Lloyd's insurer Canopius — about $6.6B of combined outbound deals, per the Korea Economic Daily, as Samsung's insurers look for growth beyond a saturated home market. The bull framing: a strategic buyer sets a reference price, and a 15% anchor holder makes the register more stable. The bear framing: nothing is signed, and a stock that trades above its average target partly on a rumoured premium has left itself little room if the rumour fades. The honest position is that the Samsung story is an option, not a floor.
