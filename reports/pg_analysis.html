01 Company Overview

Procter & Gamble is the world's largest maker of household and personal-care products, selling across five reporting segments — Fabric & Home Care, Baby/Feminine & Family Care, Beauty, Health Care and Grooming — in about 180 countries and territories. Its portfolio is built almost entirely around daily-replenishment categories (Tide, Ariel, Downy, Pampers, Always, Bounty, Charmin, Pantene, Head & Shoulders, Olay, SK-II, Gillette, Crest, Oral-B, Vicks): products households use up and buy again every few weeks.

Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) net sales rose 3% to $87.0 billion, but that headline flattered the underlying picture: organic sales grew only 1%, with volume flat and roughly two points of the reported growth coming from a weaker dollar. Diluted EPS rose 2% to $6.62 and core EPS rose 1% to $6.89. The first quarter of fiscal 2027 (July–September 2026) broke the pattern: net sales rose 4% to $23.3 billion, organic sales also rose 4% with unit volume up 2.4%, and core EPS rose 1% to $2.00, as higher commodity costs and spending on product upgrades absorbed most of the sales gain. P&G is a member of the Dow Jones Industrial Average, and in April 2026 it raised its dividend for the 70th consecutive year; it has paid one for 136 consecutive years. That record is what earns it the “Dividend King” label (a company that has raised its dividend for 50 years or more).

P&G's moat is a self-reinforcing loop between scale and shelf. It spent $10.2 billion on advertising and $2.1 billion on R&D in FY2026 — sums no private-label manufacturer and few branded rivals can match — which sustains price premiums over store brands in categories where the consumer is buying a repeatable outcome (a clean shirt, a dry baby, a comfortable shave). Those premiums fund the trade terms and category-management partnerships that secure prime shelf and endcap space at Walmart, Costco, Kroger and Amazon, which in turn guarantees the volume that keeps unit procurement and manufacturing costs below competitors'. The result is a gross margin around 50% and a ROIC of about 17% on a business with no technology cycle. The counterpoint, and it is the central debate on the stock today, is that a moat expressed as price premium is only as strong as the consumer's willingness to pay it — and in FY2026 that willingness measurably weakened, with volume flat for the year. The September 2026 quarter, with volume up 2.4% and global value share in line with a year earlier, is a sign it is holding.

Private-label share gains in a stretched US consumer. Executives have said shoppers are using every last drop of shampoo and laundry detergent and buying more value packs (CNBC, Oct 22, 2026), and on the Q4 FY2026 call the CEO described the Family Care plan as defending against private label. Fabric & Home Care earns the most profit of the five segments ($5.6 billion of FY2026 segment net earnings), so share lost there costs the most dollars.

Retailer power and inventory de-stocking. In Q4 US consumption rose 2% while P&G’s shipments fell 1%, a gap the CFO put down to retailers cutting inventory and sales pulled into the previous quarter. P&G's ten largest customers bought about 43% of its FY2026 net sales, Walmart alone about 16% (10-K); when Walmart, Costco or Amazon reset assortment or working capital targets, P&G absorbs the swing with a one-to-two quarter lag it cannot control.

Input-cost and tariff exposure with limited pricing offset. FY2027 guidance now embeds about $1.4 billion after tax of higher raw-material, energy and transportation costs, plus $300 million from higher net interest, $150 million from lower non-operating income and $50 million from currency: $0.79 a share, an 11% drag on core EPS growth, up from $0.56 a share in July. In prior cycles P&G simply priced through; with private label gaining and the CFO calling the balance between pricing and product improvements fragile, that lever is more constrained than it was in 2022–23. Pricing added just 0.4% to sales in the September quarter.

Channel shift toward e-commerce and hard discount. Online marketplaces and hard-discount grocers weaken two of the moat's load-bearing pillars at once: infinite digital shelf space neutralises P&G's physical shelf advantage, and search-ranked comparison makes the store-brand price gap explicit at the moment of purchase. Q4's Prime Day timing distortion was a reminder that a growing share of volume now moves on promotional calendars P&G does not set.

Demographic erosion in family care. Falling birth rates in North America, Western Europe and China structurally shrink the diaper and baby-wipes base — a category where P&G is the global leader and therefore has the most to lose. Growth must be manufactured through premium mix (Pampers Premium Care, Ninjamas) rather than category expansion.

Notable One-Off Items. FY2026 earnings carry several non-recurring distortions worth stripping out. First, the restructuring program: P&G recognised more than half of a $1.0–1.6 billion pre-tax two-year non-core charge in FY2026, covering severance for up to 7,000 non-manufacturing roles, brand and category exits, and supply-chain consolidation. That is the bulk of the $0.27 gap between $6.62 GAAP EPS and $6.89 core EPS, and it is why Q4 GAAP EPS fell 15% while core EPS fell only 3%. A further $0.13–$0.17 per share of charges is guided for FY2027; the September quarter carried $192 million of before-tax restructuring costs in all (10-Q), and the part above P&G’s normal level is the $0.03 gap between $1.97 GAAP and $2.00 core EPS. Second, commodity, energy and transportation costs: gross margin fell 100 basis points in FY2026, and these costs are now guided at about $1.4 billion after tax in FY2027 (about $1 billion in July); with interest, non-operating income and currency the total headwind is $0.79 per share, up from $0.56 — recurring in nature but stepping up sharply, so year-on-year comparisons overstate the underlying deterioration. Third, currency added about two points to FY2026 reported sales, meaning reported revenue growth of 3% overstates the 1% organic result; investors should anchor on organic. Fourth, portfolio pruning — discontinued brands, product forms and ways of selling — is a 30–50 basis-point headwind already inside the FY2027 organic sales guide, self-inflicted but deliberate. Finally, the $3.9 billion all-cash Thorne acquisition, completed on October 1, 2026, adds 1 point to the FY2027 all-in sales guide (now 2–4%) and will bring integration costs and purchase-accounting effects into results from the December quarter; the purchase-price allocation is still preliminary.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a 24.3x P/E on one of the strongest cash generators in consumer staples — 50% gross margins, 17.2% ROIC, $15.3B of free cash flow over four quarters, a 2.7% yield backed by 70 consecutive annual increases, and a 0.37 beta that makes the stock a hiding place in a growth scare. The September 2026 quarter answered the question the last edition left open: volume grew 2.4%, all ten categories and six regions grew, and the most profitable segment, Fabric & Home Care, led with 4.3% volume growth. The full-year guide already absorbs a $0.79-a-share cost headwind, so restructuring savings and Thorne give management two levers, cost and mix, to beat it.

Bear Case

A 15x PEG (forward P/E of 23.0 against 1.5% guided core EPS growth) says you are paying a quality multiple for almost no growth. One good quarter follows a fiscal year with no volume growth; core gross margin fell another 150 bps in the quarter; the cost headwind grew from $0.56 to $0.79 a share in three months; and the CFO calls the balance between pricing and product improvements fragile. After an 11.8% rise since the last edition the stock trades at about the staples average, and a slow-growing, bond-like stock with a 2.7% yield is not obviously cheap at 24x trailing earnings when cash and bonds pay competitive yields.

  Industry Commentary — What Matters for P&G Right Now
The US Consumer Trade-Down and Private-Label Cycle
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the defining dynamic. In fiscal 2026 shoppers used every last drop of shampoo and detergent and bought more value packs, executives have said, and P&G’s volume did not grow for the year. The September 2026 quarter broke the pattern: volume +2.4%, with Fabric & Home Care volume up 4.3%. CFO Andre Schulten still describes a split economy: higher-income shoppers trade up to new products, even a $30 shampoo, while lower-income households try to make Tide and Pampers fit tight budgets (CNBC, Oct 22, 2026). P&G’s answer is product superiority plus promotions and new pack sizes: executives said premium launches such as Pampers Amore and Pantene’s Abundant & Strong line have gained share.
Tariffs, Commodities and the Return of Input-Cost Inflation
ONGOING HEADWIND
FY2026 gross margin fell 100 basis points to 50.2% and operating margin fell 160 basis points to 22.7%. In the September 2026 quarter core gross margin fell another 150 basis points: 140 of higher commodity costs, 140 of product reinvestment, 50 of less favourable mix and 10 of rounding and other items, offset in part by 120 of productivity savings, 50 of lower tariff costs and recoveries and 20 of higher pricing. FY2027 guidance now carries about $1.4 billion after tax of commodity, energy and transportation costs and $0.79 a share of total headwind, an 11% drag on core EPS growth, up from $0.56 (8%) in July. The difference from the 2022–23 inflation episode is the response: then P&G priced through it; now pricing added 0.4% to sales in the quarter, so the company is leaning on productivity, mix and the 7,000-role restructuring instead. Whether cost-out can offset cost-in is the biggest single driver of FY2027 earnings.
Defensive Rotation Mechanics — A Two-Way Option
EMERGING TAILWIND
With a 0.37 beta and a 2.7% yield, PG is one of the stocks investors buy when they get nervous, and the past year showed it: from a $137.62 low on January 7, 2026 the stock rallied about 21% to $167.25 by February 27 as investors rotated out of technology stocks into staples, then gave the whole move back by June 1. The October 2026 jump was different: it came on results. Bull framing: the buying from nervous investors now comes with a volume recovery attached, at a multiple near the staples average and below the index, and the Thorne acquisition adds a wellness business on top. Bear framing: money that comes in looking for shelter does not lift the stock’s valuation for good — it leaves as soon as investors want risk again, and in the meantime you own a company whose earnings are guided to grow 0–3%. Buying PG to steady a portfolio is a legitimate decision; buying it for long-term growth needs the volume recovery to last.
