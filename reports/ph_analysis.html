01 Company Overview

Parker-Hannifin makes motion and control technologies — hydraulics, pneumatics, filtration, fluid connectors, sealing, electromechanical drives and, increasingly, flight-critical aerospace hardware — sold into industrial, off-highway, energy, HVAC/refrigeration, transportation and aerospace & defense end markets. Fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026) produced record sales of $21.50 billion, up 8.3% YoY with 6.6% organic growth, record adjusted segment operating margin of 27.3%, and record adjusted diluted EPS of $32.31 (+18%). At a $129.0 billion market capitalization Parker is the 96th-largest US public company and roughly the 181st-largest in the world — a valuation that has more than doubled since 2024, driven far less by the industrial cycle than by what the company has done to its own mix.

The central fact about Parker today is that it is no longer best described as a cyclical distributor of motion-and-control components. Aerospace Systems generated $7.1 billion of FY2026 sales (33% of the total) at a 29.9% adjusted operating margin, and contributed roughly 36% of adjusted segment operating income (calculated from Parker's reported segment margins). Including the pending Filtration Group and CIRCOR aerospace acquisitions, management put aerospace & defense at ~37% of pro-forma sales. The £6.3 billion (~$8.8 billion) Meggitt acquisition, closed September 2022, roughly doubled the aerospace segment and is what turned a ~20% margin industrial into a business management now believes can reach a 30% adjusted segment operating margin by FY2031.

Parker's moat has two distinct halves that got welded together by Meggitt. On the industrial side it is distribution and breadth: roughly 10,000 independent distributor locations, hundreds of thousands of SKUs, and the ability to quote an entire hydraulic-plus-filtration-plus-sealing subsystem rather than a fitting. That produces pricing power in the aftermarket — where a machine is down and the buyer is not shopping — and it is why Parker sustains ~26% adjusted margins in a business most people would describe as commodity components. On the aerospace side the moat is certification and installed base: once a Parker fuel system, brake or ice-protection system is designed into an airframe, it is on that platform for a 25-to-40-year production and service life, and the qualification cost of replacing it is prohibitive. The aftermarket that follows is the highest-margin revenue in the company and effectively an annuity on flying hours. Layered over both is the Win Strategy, Parker's decade-plus operating system covering engaged people, premier customer experience, profitable growth and financial performance — the mechanism by which adjusted segment operating margin has gone from roughly 17% pre-Meggitt to 27.3% in FY2026, and the basis for management's raised FY2031 target of 30%, up 300 bps from the prior 27% goal that was reached three years early.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

Short-cycle industrial demand remains the soft spot. Two-thirds of revenue still sits in Diversified Industrial, where FY2026 organic growth was +3.1% in North America and +3.9% internationally, with EMEA essentially flat at +0.1% and Latin America down 3.3%. Interconnect, filtration and hydraulics ship on short lead times against distributor inventory, so a destocking cycle hits revenue almost immediately with no backlog to cushion it. The +16%/+24% Q4 order rates argue a recovery is underway, but Parker is changing to a 12-month rolling order calculation in FY2027, which will make the comparison harder to read in real time.

Valuation now embeds the aerospace story. At 35.9x trailing and 28.9x forward earnings, with a PEG of 3.44 and 25.0x EV/EBITDA, Parker trades at a multiple that assumes aerospace mix, margin expansion and aftermarket durability all persist. It is not priced as an industrial. Any evidence that the 30% FY2031 target is slipping — or that aerospace aftermarket growth normalizes toward build rates — removes the justification for the premium without any change to current earnings.

Serial-acquirer integration and re-leveraging risk. Parker has $11.8 billion of announced transactions in flight (Filtration Group and CIRCOR's Commercial & Defense Aerospace business) after just finishing paying down Meggitt. Long-term debt fell from $7.5B to $6.8B in FY2026; closing these deals reverses that. FY2027 guidance explicitly excludes both, so reported results will diverge from guidance and the GAAP-versus-adjusted gap — already $3.83 per share in FY2026 — will widen again on purchase accounting and amortization.

Tariffs, currency and end-market concentration. Q4 FY2026 included an $84 million reduction to cost of sales from IEEPA tariff refunds, with further refunds of uncertain timing and amount — a reminder that trade policy is currently a P&L swing factor in both directions. Management guides to a ~0.5% currency headwind for FY2027. And within aerospace, a meaningful slice of the backlog is exposed to a small number of platforms and to US and allied defense appropriations.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Meggitt purchase accounting is the single largest recurring distortion. FY2026 GAAP diluted EPS of $28.48 versus adjusted EPS of $32.31 leaves a $3.83 per share gap (~$480M pre-share-count), and reported segment operating margin of 24.5% versus adjusted 27.3% leaves 280 bps — the bulk of it acquisition-related intangible amortization from Meggitt, plus integration and restructuring costs. This is non-cash but it is also not going away soon, and it will step up again when Filtration Group and CIRCOR close. Investors comparing Parker's 28.9x forward P/E to peers should confirm which earnings basis is being used.

Tariff refunds. Q4 FY2026 included an $84 million reduction to cost of sales from International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) tariff refunds received from the US government. Management flagged additional refunds as pending with uncertain timing and amount. This inflated Q4 reported segment margin — reported margin rose 260 bps to 26.5% while adjusted rose only 110 bps to 28.0%, and the gap direction reverses in Q4 versus the full year, which is the signature of a one-time credit.

Acquisitions and currency in the growth rate. Reported FY2026 growth of 8.3% versus organic of 6.6% means roughly 170 bps came from Curtis Instruments (closed September 2025) and foreign exchange. FY2027 guidance assumes ~0.5% acquisition benefit against ~0.5% currency headwind, netting to organic ≈ reported. FY2025's higher net margin (17.79% versus FY2026's 16.97%) reflected a divestiture-related gain, so year-over-year GAAP net income growth of 3% understates operating progress; adjusted net income rose 16%.

Capital deployment. Nearly $2 billion returned to shareholders in FY2026 via dividends and buybacks, an 11% dividend increase, and long-term debt reduced from $7.5B to $6.8B — all before the announced $11.8B of pending acquisitions, which FY2027 guidance excludes.

08 Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

Parker has structurally changed what it is. Aerospace Systems now delivers 33% of sales at a 29.9% adjusted margin and roughly 36% of adjusted segment profit, backed by a record $8.5B backlog and a high-margin aftermarket annuity tied to flying hours rather than build rates — and the pending CIRCOR and Filtration Group deals push aerospace & defense to ~37% of pro-forma sales. Management just beat its FY2029 27% margin target three years early and raised the FY2031 goal to 30%, on a track record that makes that credible. Meanwhile the short-cycle industrial businesses — the perceived weak link — posted +16% and +24% Q4 order growth, implying the cyclical recovery is still ahead. A 70-year dividend streak, 28% payout ratio, 15.4% ROIC and ~1.5x net leverage give management room to keep compounding through acquisition.

BEAR CASE

At 35.9x trailing, 28.9x forward and a 3.44 PEG, essentially all of the transformation is in the price — and FY2027 guidance implies only ~8% adjusted EPS growth, below the 10%+ long-term algorithm. Two-thirds of revenue still sits in short-cycle industrial businesses that grew 3–4% organically in FY2026 with EMEA at +0.1%, and Parker is switching to a 12-month rolling order metric that makes that segment harder to monitor. The $3.83 GAAP-versus-adjusted EPS gap is largely Meggitt amortization and it will widen when $11.8B of new deals close, re-levering a balance sheet that took three years to repair. One analyst carries a $680 target — a 34% downside — and GuruFocus's model puts fair value near $717. If aerospace aftermarket growth normalizes or the industrial recovery stalls, the multiple has a long way to fall before it meets the industrial comps.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Multiple compression if the aerospace narrative wobbles. The premium to industrial peers is entirely a function of aerospace mix and margin durability. A single quarter of aftermarket growth decelerating toward OEM build rates, or any hint that the 30% FY2031 target is slipping, would compress a 28.9x forward multiple toward the mid-20s without any change to reported earnings — roughly a 15–20% price impact on its own.
02
Short-cycle industrial demand fails to convert from orders to shipments. Q4 order growth of +16% in North America and +24% internationally is the foundation of the "broadening recovery" underpinning FY2027 guidance. If those bookings reflect distributor restocking or tariff-driven pull-forward rather than end demand — and EMEA organic revenue was flat at +0.1% for the full year — the 5.5–8.5% organic guide is at risk from the largest two-thirds of the business.
03
Integration and leverage risk on $11.8B of pending M&A. Filtration Group and CIRCOR's aerospace business are excluded from guidance, will consume the deleveraging headroom rebuilt since Meggitt, and add purchase-accounting amortization that widens the GAAP-to-adjusted gap. Parker's integration record is strong, but the deals are being done at the top of the aerospace cycle and at scale — execution slippage would hit both earnings and the credibility that sustains the multiple.
  Industry Commentary
Aerospace aftermarket strength vs. soft industrial short-cycle
EMERGING TAILWIND
The defining split in Parker's results. Aerospace grew 13.4% organically in FY2026 — a fourth straight double-digit year — at a 29.9% adjusted margin, with aftermarket revenue up 17% in Q2 FY2026 and a record $8.5B backlog. Against that, Diversified Industrial grew 3.1% (North America) and 3.9% (International) organically, with EMEA at +0.1% and Latin America at −3.3%. The aerospace side is long-cycle, backlog-covered and aftermarket-rich; the industrial side ships short-lead-time hydraulics, fluid connectors and filtration against distributor inventory, which is why it turned first and will recover last. Q4 order growth of +19% company-wide is the first real evidence the industrial half is turning.
Defense spending and platform content
EMERGING TAILWIND
Parker describes defense as the second leg of the aerospace stool alongside commercial OEM and commercial aftermarket, with multi-year bookings on platforms including the F-35 and KC-46A. Elevated US and allied appropriations have lengthened the visibility on this revenue, and military aftermarket carries margin characteristics similar to commercial. The pending CIRCOR Commercial & Defense Aerospace acquisition is a direct bet on this continuing. The vulnerability is concentration: a small number of large platforms drive a disproportionate share of the backlog, and appropriations are a political variable, not an economic one.
Portfolio mix shift as the re-rating driver — bull vs. bear
ONGOING HEADWIND TO VALUATION
The bull framing: Parker's multiple expanded because its earnings quality changed. Adjusted segment operating margin went from roughly 17% pre-Meggitt to 27.3%, aerospace went from a minority contributor to ~36% of adjusted segment profit, and $12.8B of backlog replaced pure short-cycle exposure. On that view 28.9x forward is what a durable, aftermarket-weighted compounder with a 70-year dividend streak should cost, and the FY2031 30% target extends the runway.

The bear framing: the re-rating has already happened. The stock is up 266% in five years and 39% in twelve months, PEG is 3.44, FY2027 guides to only ~8% adjusted EPS growth, and two-thirds of revenue still comes from businesses growing 3–4%. The mix shift is real but it is finite — after CIRCOR and Filtration Group, aerospace is ~37% of sales and the next increment of margin has to come from execution, not from portfolio change. The Street's $680 low target and GuruFocus's ~$717 fair value estimate are both expressions of that view.
