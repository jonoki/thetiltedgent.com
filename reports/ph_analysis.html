01 Company Overview

Parker-Hannifin makes motion and control technologies — hydraulics, pneumatics, filtration, fluid connectors, sealing, electromechanical drives and, increasingly, flight-critical aerospace hardware — sold into industrial, off-highway, energy, HVAC/refrigeration, transportation and aerospace & defense end markets. On November 5, 2026 it reported a record first quarter of fiscal 2027 (the three months to September 30, 2026): sales of $5.99 billion, up 17.9% YoY, 11.5% organic growth (growth from existing businesses; the rest came from acquisitions, mainly Filtration Group, which closed August 13), record adjusted segment operating margin of 28.6% (adjusted strips out acquisition-related amortization and one-off costs) and record adjusted diluted EPS of $8.90 (+23%). The last full year, fiscal 2026 (ended June 30, 2026), had sales of $21.50 billion (+8.3%) and adjusted EPS of $32.31. At a $115.7 billion market capitalization (our calculation at the November 6 close: 126.10 million shares × $917.79) Parker ranks #103 in the S&P 500; the shares closed 16.6% below their August 6 intraday high of $1,099.94.

The central fact about Parker today is that it is no longer best described as a cyclical distributor of motion-and-control components. Aerospace Systems generated $1.87 billion of Q1 sales (31% of segment sales) at a 30.7% adjusted operating margin and contributed about 33% of adjusted segment operating income ($574 million of $1,721 million; our calculation from the release). The £6.3 billion (~$8.8 billion) Meggitt acquisition, closed September 2022, nearly doubled the aerospace segment. Parker's adjusted segment operating margin was 22.3% in fiscal 2022, the last full year before the deal, and 27.3% in fiscal 2026; management's target is 30% by FY2031. The newest deal goes the other way: Filtration Group, bought for $9.25 billion in cash and closed August 13, 2026, adds filtration revenue to the industrial side, and the pending purchase of CIRCOR's Commercial & Defense Aerospace business, for about $2.55 billion in cash, would add to aerospace.

Parker's moat has two distinct halves that got welded together by Meggitt. On the industrial side it is distribution and breadth: independent distributors around the world, a very wide catalogue of parts, and the ability to quote a whole hydraulic, filtration and sealing subsystem rather than one fitting. That gives Parker pricing power on replacement parts, where a machine is down and the buyer is not shopping around; Diversified Industrial earned a 27.6% adjusted margin in Q1 in a business most people would describe as commodity components. On the aerospace side the moat is certification and installed base: once a Parker fuel system or brake is designed into an aircraft, it generally stays on that aircraft type for as long as it is built and flown, because certifying a replacement is costly. The spare parts and repairs that follow earn high margins and recur with flying hours. Layered over both is the Win Strategy, Parker's business system covering engaged people, customer experience, profitable growth and financial performance. Adjusted segment operating margin has gone from 22.3% in fiscal 2022, the last full year before Meggitt, to 27.3% in FY2026, and management has raised its target by 3 percentage points to 30% by FY2031; the prior goal, 27% by FY2029 (set in May 2024), was reached three years early.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

Short-cycle industrial demand is recovering, but it has not been tested. Diversified Industrial is about 69% of Q1 segment sales ($4,152 million of $6,019 million; our calculation). Organic growth there was +9.4% in North America and +11.6% internationally, against +3.1% and +3.9% for fiscal 2026. Hydraulics, fluid connectors and filtration ship on short lead times against distributor inventory, so when distributors run down their stock, revenue falls almost immediately with little backlog to cushion it. Rolling 12-month orders were up 11% in North America and 14% internationally in Q1. Parker moved to that rolling measure in fiscal 2027, so they cannot be compared with the quarterly rates quoted in August.

Valuation still embeds the aerospace story. At 31.5x trailing and 24.9x forward earnings (on the midpoint of fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guidance), a PEG of 1.77 and about 22x EV/EBITDA (our calculation at the November 6 close), Parker's share price assumes that the aerospace mix, higher margins and the new acquisitions all deliver. It is not valued like a typical industrial company. Any evidence that the 30% FY2031 target is slipping — or that aerospace aftermarket growth slows toward new-aircraft build rates — removes the justification for the premium without any change to current earnings.

Debt has doubled and integration is under way. Filtration Group closed on August 13, 2026 for $9.25 billion in cash, most of it paid for with $7.75 billion of term loans; in September Parker sold about $2.4 billion of U.S. notes and €2.025 billion of euro notes to repay the 364-day loan (SEC Forms 8-K). Total debt went from $8.52 billion at June 30 to $17.48 billion at September 30, and net debt (debt less cash) from about $8.0 billion to $16.8 billion (our calculation from the release). The CIRCOR aerospace purchase is still pending. The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap was $1.98 per share in Q1 and the guidance implies about $6.80 for the year (midpoints, our calculation), mostly acquisition amortization.

Tariff refunds and currency. In Q1, net sales were cut by $27 million for IEEPA tariff-related returns to customers, partly offset by $6 million received from the U.S. government, a net $21 million ($0.17 per share) that Parker excludes from adjusted results and organic growth. In Q4 FY2026 the same refunds had cut cost of sales by $84 million, a reminder that trade policy can swing profit in both directions. Management expects currency to take about 0.5 points off fiscal 2027 sales growth.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Acquisition accounting is the largest recurring distortion. Q1 FY2027 GAAP diluted EPS of $6.92 against adjusted EPS of $8.90 leaves a $1.98 per share gap: amortization of acquired intangibles $1.48, acquisition-related expenses $0.61, integration costs $0.18, business realignment $0.13 and the $0.17 net tariff return added back, less a $0.59 tax effect (release reconciliation; the sum is our addition). Reported segment operating margin of 24.4% against adjusted 28.6% is a gap of 4.2 percentage points. This is mostly non-cash, and fiscal 2027 guidance shows it staying large: GAAP EPS of $29.65–$30.45 against adjusted $36.45–$37.25. Investors comparing Parker's forward P/E with peers should confirm which earnings basis is being used.

Tariff returns. In Q1, net sales were reduced by $27 million for IEEPA (International Emergency Economic Powers Act) tariff-related returns to customers, partly offset by $6 million received from the U.S. government: a net $21 million, or $0.17 a share, excluded from adjusted results and organic growth. The release notes the U.S. Supreme Court ruled in February 2026 that IEEPA tariffs were unauthorized. In Q4 FY2026 the refunds had instead reduced cost of sales by $84 million.

Acquisitions and currency in the growth rate. Reported Q1 growth of 17.9% against organic growth of 11.5% means acquisitions added 6.9 points (Filtration Group from August 13, plus Curtis Instruments) and currency took off 0.5. Fiscal 2027 guidance: reported sales growth of 16.0%–19.0%, organic 7.5%–10.5%, acquisitions about 9 points and currency about −0.5 points.

Capital deployment. In Q1 Parker paid $252 million of dividends ($2.00 a share, up 11% from $1.80), bought back $118 million of stock (against $522 million a year earlier) and spent $9.10 billion on acquisitions net of cash, funded by $12.48 billion of new borrowings while repaying $5.26 billion of debt.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward. Readings above 70 suggest the stock has run hard and may be due for a pause; below 30 suggests it has been beaten down and may be due for a bounce. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Over the five years from the November 2021 close of $302.06 to the November 6, 2026 close of $917.79, PH returned +203.8% on price alone, roughly 3.0x, before dividends. The S&P 500 (SPY, price only) rose 61.4% over the same window ($455.56 to $735.29). The path was not a straight line. The stock fell for most of 2022. 08 Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

Parker has structurally changed what it is. Aerospace Systems delivers 31% of segment sales at a 30.7% adjusted margin and about a third of adjusted segment profit, backed by a record $13.7B total backlog and a high-margin aftermarket tied to flying hours rather than build rates. Q1 showed the industrial half waking up too: organic growth of +9.4% in North America and +11.6% internationally, with rolling 12-month orders up 11% and 14%. Management raised fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS guidance to $36.45–$37.25, and Filtration Group adds aftermarket-rich filtration revenue (about $1.85B of sales in fiscal 2027, per management on the call). It beat its FY2029 27% margin target three years early and raised the FY2031 goal to 30%. A 70-year dividend streak and a 27.5% payout ratio leave room to keep buying businesses.

BEAR CASE

At 31.5x trailing and 24.9x forward earnings, much of the transformation is in the price — and Parker has just doubled its debt: total debt of $17.48B against $8.52B at June 30, with net debt of about $16.8B. Two-thirds of revenue still sits in short-cycle industrial businesses that grew 3–4% organically in fiscal 2026, so Q1's +9–12% needs to last. Aerospace order growth slowed to +14% from +18% on the rolling 12-month measure. The Q1 EPS beat of 8.1% did not hold the shares: they closed down 1.97% on the day and 16.6% below the August high. The $1.98 per-share gap between GAAP and adjusted earnings will stay wide as acquisition amortization grows. The lowest analyst target is $740, 19% below the November 6 close. If aerospace aftermarket growth slows or the industrial recovery stalls, the shares have a long way to fall before their valuation matches other industrial companies.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
The valuation drops if the aerospace story wobbles. The premium to industrial peers depends on aerospace mix and margin durability. One quarter of aftermarket growth slowing toward new-aircraft build rates, or any hint that the 30% FY2031 target is slipping, could pull the forward P/E from 24.9x toward the low 20s without any change in reported earnings: at the same earnings, 22x is a 12% lower share price and 21x a 16% lower one (our arithmetic).
02
Short-cycle industrial demand fades after the restock. Q1 organic growth of +9.4% in North America and +11.6% internationally, and rolling 12-month orders up 11% and 14%, underpin the 7.5%–10.5% organic guidance. If those orders reflect distributors restocking, or customers buying early ahead of tariffs, rather than real end demand, the guidance is at risk from roughly two-thirds of the business.
03
Debt and integration risk after Filtration Group. Total debt doubled to $17.48B in one quarter and the CIRCOR aerospace purchase is still to come. Interest costs rise with it, and the GAAP-to-adjusted gap widens with purchase-accounting amortization. Two large deals are being absorbed close together, and any slip in integration would hit both earnings and the confidence behind the share price's valuation.
  Industry Commentary
Aerospace strength and an industrial recovery
EMERGING TAILWIND
The defining split in Parker's results is narrowing. Aerospace grew 13.9% organically in Q1 FY2027 (+13.4% for fiscal 2026) at a 30.7% adjusted margin, with a record $13.7B of total backlog. Diversified Industrial grew 9.4% organically in North America and 11.6% internationally, against 3.1% and 3.9% for fiscal 2026, with EMEA at +4% (it was +0.1%) and Asia-Pacific at +23%. The aerospace side is long-cycle, backlog-covered and aftermarket-rich; the industrial side ships short-lead-time hydraulics, fluid connectors and filtration against distributor inventory, so it responds to the economy sooner. Rolling 12-month orders rose 13% company-wide (12% a quarter earlier): the industrial half is turning.
Defense demand
EMERGING TAILWIND
Defense is one of the markets Parker's aerospace business sells into, alongside commercial aircraft makers and commercial repairs. On the November 5 call management raised its fiscal 2027 outlook for sales to defense aircraft makers to high-single-digit growth. The pending CIRCOR Commercial & Defense Aerospace acquisition is a direct bet on this continuing. The risk is that defense budgets are set by politics, not the economy.
Portfolio mix shift as the valuation driver — bull vs. bear
ONGOING HEADWIND TO VALUATION
The bull framing: Parker's valuation rose because its earnings quality changed. Adjusted segment operating margin went from 22.3% in fiscal 2022, before Meggitt, to 28.6% in Q1, aerospace now brings about a third of adjusted segment profit, and Parker has a record $13.7B backlog. On that view 24.9x forward earnings is what a durable, aftermarket-weighted business with a 70-year dividend streak should cost, and the FY2031 30% target extends the runway.

The bear framing: the price has already moved a long way. The stock is up 204% in five years and 10% in twelve months, trailing P/E is 31.5x, and the company has just doubled its debt to buy a filtration business. The mix shift is real but finite — the next increment of margin has to come from execution, not from portfolio change. The lowest analyst target is $740.
