01 Company Overview

Every box that arrives on a doorstep started as a roll of containerboard. Packaging Corporation of America makes that board in its own mills and then turns most of it into corrugated boxes, displays and protective packaging in its own plants. The mill's output is largely sold to itself. A much smaller Paper segment makes uncoated freesheet office and printing papers. TTM revenue is $9.53B, up 10.3%; FY2025 revenue was $8.99B, up 7.23% from $8.38B.

Over the last twelve months PCA got bigger and leaner at once. On September 1, 2025 it closed a $1.8B cash purchase of Greif's containerboard business: two mills (Riverville in Amherst, Virginia and Massillon, Ohio) with about 800,000 tons of annual capacity, plus eight sheet-feeder and corrugated plants. Three months later, on December 3, 2025, it announced it would shut the No. 2 paper machine and kraft pulping at its Wallula, Washington mill, removing 250,000 tons of high-cost capacity. Then, in 2026, the industry started pushing price. After two rounds earlier in the year, PCA announced an unusually large $140-per-ton increase effective September 1. The stock trades at $238.31, near the top of its $191.50–$259.98 52-week range. A trailing P/E of 31 looks rich, but restructuring charges have dented the earnings it is measured on; the forward multiple is about 20. The market is pricing one question that is easy to ask and hard to answer: how much of that $140 actually sticks?

The operating numbers that actually matter

A containerboard company earns volume × (price − cost), and all three moved in 2026.

Greif deal
$1.8B
Paid for a business with ~$1.2B of sales and $212M of EBITDA in the 12 months to April 30, 2025. That is about 8.5x before synergies, or ~6.6x counting the $60M of pre-tax synergies PCA expects within two years of closing. The deal lifts the recycled share of PCA's mix from about 20% to about 30%.
Wallula reconfiguration
−250k tons
The No. 2 machine and kraft pulping are shut. The mill keeps 285,000 tons of recycled linerboard and medium on the No. 3 machine, at an expected cost about $125/ton below 2025 levels. Roughly 200 jobs were cut. At announcement, pre-tax restructuring charges were estimated at about $205M.
Sept 1 price increase
$140/ton
Roughly double what producers usually announce, per Packaging Dive; peers announced $80–$110. Fastmarkets RISI had recognised a net ~$100/ton gain so far in 2026, then showed prices flat in July and August. Truist's contacts thought the full $140 "might be a stretch."
Q3 2026 guidance
$2.91
EPS excluding special items, against $2.35 in Q2 2026: a 24% step-up in one quarter. It rests on one extra shipping day, finishing the first price increase and starting the second, better performance at the Jackson and Greif mills, and lower maintenance outages overall.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Mill-to-box vertical integration Replacement cost of a mill (~$1B) Consolidated North American supply Lower-cost mill system after Wallula Local box-plant network Price leadership in 2026

The moat is steel and permits. A new containerboard mill costs, in the words of a PCA executive, "every bit of $1 billion." Boxes are heavy, bulky and cheap per unit, so they have to be made close to the customer. That is why the industry consolidates instead of being disrupted, and why paying $1.8B for Greif's two running mills can beat building one. It is also why PCA could open with a $140 increase: in a market that lost nearly 10% of its capacity in 2025, someone has to go first. The weakness is that the moat protects the industry, not the price. Containerboard is a commodity, and when demand softens, the price takes the hit.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
PCA does not control whether its price sticks. Contract box prices are commonly tied to the prices Fastmarkets RISI publishes, and RISI recorded no change in July or August 2026. If RISI recognises only part of the $140 (Truist's contacts mentioned $50–$70), then both the Q3 guide and the $13.12 consensus EPS for 2027 depend on price that may never arrive.
02
Demand depends on who you ask. PCA reported record legacy shipments in Q2. But BofA's George Staphos called demand "lackluster and volumes weak," and International Paper's CEO said Q2 demand "was softer than expected." A price increase pushed into a soft market is the classic setup for having to give it back.
03
The costs PCA can't control are rising. Freight cost $0.26 per share in Q2 2026. Management expects freight to stay high, recycled fiber to keep climbing, and chemicals and electricity to rise. The Greif mills also raised PCA's exposure to recycled fiber, which gets more expensive with the same box demand that supports board prices.
04
Debt paid for Greif. $1.5B of the $1.8B was borrowed. Total debt is $4.40B against $593.8M of cash (net debt $3.74B), about 2.0x EBITDA. That is manageable, but the dividend takes 71% of GAAP earnings, which leaves less room if the cycle turns.

Notable One-Off Items

Two things distort the reported numbers. The first is Wallula. The December 2025 decision to shut the No. 2 machine and kraft pulping came with about $205M of estimated pre-tax restructuring charges, mostly non-cash, and Q4 2025 GAAP net income fell to $92.1M. The Q4 2025 special-item breakdown was not fetched, so pinning the whole gap on Wallula is likely but unverified. Special items continued into 2026: Q2 2026 carried $0.20 per share of charges, including $0.05 for Wallula and $0.03 for acquisition and integration. The second is Greif. Since September 2025 the results include a business with ~$1.2B of annual sales, so headline growth overstates organic growth. In Q2 2026 legacy corrugated shipments were up 4.1% per day and total shipments 24.3%. Q2 also included a $0.04 depreciation benefit from purchase-accounting adjustments on the acquired assets.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The industry cut nearly 10% of North American capacity in 2025. PCA took out 250,000 tons of its own highest-cost production and bought 800,000 tons of running capacity at about 6.6x EBITDA after synergies. Producers then pushed through two rounds of price increases before trying a third. If even part of the $140 sticks, the earnings math is steep. Consensus has EPS rising from $7.70 GAAP TTM to $10.45 in 2026 and $13.12 in 2027, which puts the stock at 18x 2027 earnings. Legacy volumes set a record in Q2, and the Q3 guide calls for a 24% jump in EPS from Q2. A 2.5% dividend pays you to wait.

Bear Case

Containerboard is a commodity, and the index that sets its price has not moved since June. BofA calls demand "lackluster" and International Paper calls it "softer than expected." A $140 increase announced into that kind of market tends to be partly recognised and then given back. The costs, meanwhile, are here now: freight, recycled fiber, chemicals and power are all rising. Packaging segment operating income fell year-over-year in Q2 despite 15% more sales, and the Greif deal added $1.5B of borrowing. At 31x trailing and 20x forward earnings, with a 71% payout ratio and a 3.5% FCF yield, the price already reflects much of the recovery.

Top 3 Risks

01
Price increases fall short. The Q3 guide assumes the first increase is fully in place and the second starts to land. Truist's channel checks suggested only $50–$70 of the latest $140 may be recognised. Any round that is only partly recognised comes straight out of 2026–2027 estimates.
02
Box demand. PCA's record shipments sit alongside peers saying demand is soft. If volumes weaken, the capacity cuts that support pricing may not be enough.
03
Rising costs during integration. Freight cost $0.26 per share in Q2 alone, and recycled fiber keeps rising just as the Greif mills have raised PCA's recycled mix to about 30%. The $60M synergy target now has more cost inflation to absorb than when the deal was announced.
Industry Commentary
Capacity discipline in North American containerboard
EMERGING TAILWIND

Packaging Dive reports that 2025 facility closures removed "nearly 10%" of North American containerboard production capacity. PCA's own Wallula move was about cost. As Truist's Michael Roxland wrote at the time, "these capacity adjustments are being driven by cost and not due to any incremental demand deterioration." Less supply against steady demand gave producers the confidence to announce three rounds of increases in 2026.

The price-realisation gap
KEY UNCERTAINTY

Fastmarkets RISI recognised a net ~$100/ton of increases in the first half of 2026: $20 in Q1, $30 in April and $50 in June. It then recognised nothing in July or August. The third wave is uneven, with PCA at $140 and most others at $80–$110. How much of it RISI recognises will decide whether PKG at $238 is early or late in the cycle.

Input costs
ONGOING HEADWIND

PCA's Q3 outlook expects freight to stay high, recycled fiber prices to keep rising, and chemical and electricity costs to rise, with wood fiber and natural gas stable. Recycled fiber is the awkward one. Its price rises with the same box demand that supports board prices, so part of any price gain goes straight back out as cost.
