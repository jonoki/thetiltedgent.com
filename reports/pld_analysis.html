01 Company Overview

Prologis is the world's largest owner, operator and developer of logistics real estate — the warehouses and distribution centers that sit between factories, ports and the customer's doorstep. It is structured as a REIT and generates the overwhelming majority of revenue from long-duration leases on industrial buildings, supplemented by a fee-earning fund-management arm (Strategic Capital). Full-year 2025 revenue was $8.79B, up 7.2% YoY, with Core FFO of $5.81 per diluted share. In the third quarter of 2026 (reported October 15) Core FFO was $1.60 per diluted share, up from $1.49 a year earlier, and management raised its 2026 Core FFO guidance to $6.25–$6.29. Prologis ranks #101 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization.

The company is in the middle of the largest transaction in its history: on August 4, 2026 it announced a recommended acquisition of SEGRO plc, the UK-listed European warehouse REIT, valuing SEGRO's equity at approximately $18.8B (1,031.7p per share; about £14.0B in press reports of the final offer). To help fund it, Prologis sold 15,000,000 new shares on August 4 for about $2.1B, or about $2.4B if the underwriters take up their 2,250,000-share option at the same price (computed). SEGRO's shareholders approved the scheme on September 28, 2026; court sanction and regulatory clearances remain, and completion is still targeted for H1 2027. On completion, combined AUM would be roughly $269B, the European portfolio would grow 47% and the European land bank 126%. Separately, Prologis has opened a genuinely new growth vector by converting land and secured electrical interconnects into data center capacity — the power pipeline stood at 5.8 GW at September 30, 2026, 1.7 GW of it with secured power.

The genuinely new leg of the story. Prologis owns land in exactly the places where power interconnects are scarce, and has been converting land parcels plus secured grid capacity into data center development — a use that clears far higher rents per square foot than warehousing. The power pipeline stood at 5.8 GW at September 30, 2026: 1.7 GW with secured power (680 MW of it under development) and 4.1 GW at an advanced stage (Q3 supplemental); management said on the Q3 call that roughly 1 GW is covered by letters of intent. Through Q2 the company had started $2.1B of data center projects in 2026 against a full-year goal of about $2B, including a 260-megawatt build-to-suit campus at roughly $800M (Q2 call). The honest counterweight is that data centers were under 1% of the global portfolio at Q2, so this is optionality rather than earnings. It is also a strategically defensive move: if AI-driven power demand keeps bidding up interconnected land, Prologis monetizes an asset it already owns. Management additionally argues AI capital spending itself creates warehouse demand — space for equipment staging, components and construction supply chains.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
IRREPLACEABLE INFILL LAND $4.9B LAND BANK (BOOK VALUE) SECURED POWER INTERCONNECTS A2/A CREDIT · 3.3% COST OF DEBT GLOBAL CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIPS STRATEGIC CAPITAL FLYWHEEL 16% EMBEDDED MARK-TO-MARKET (Q3)

Prologis's moat is not a technology or a brand — it is basis and balance sheet. The company owns land near ports, rail and dense population that cannot be replicated at today's prices, and it finances that land with debt costing a 3.3% weighted-average rate over a 7.6-year average term against A2/A ratings. That funding advantage is decisive in an asset class where returns are a spread over cost of capital: Prologis started development at a 6.6% stabilized yield in Q3 and acquired at a 4.2% cap rate, while a levered private competitor borrowing hundreds of basis points wider cannot make the same math work. Layered on top is the lease mark-to-market, 16% at September 30, 2026 (about $780M of embedded NOI at today's rents, CFO Tim Arndt on the Q3 call), which converts to reported growth mechanically as leases roll: Q3 rollover leases repriced +38.0% net effective and +23.4% cash. Finally, the Strategic Capital arm turns scale into a self-funding loop — stabilized assets are contributed to managed funds, the proceeds help fund higher-yielding development, and Prologis keeps the fees and the operating control. Adding SEGRO would extend all three legs: European footprint +47%, European land bank +126%, and combined AUM to ~$269B.

Interest rates and cap rates set the valuation, not operations. The 2022–23 drawdown — from $168.36 at end-2021 to $101.60 in September 2022, a −39.7% move — happened while occupancy and rent spreads were near record highs. REIT equity is a duration asset: when the discount rate moves, the multiple moves regardless of NOI. This quarter shows the same pattern in a smaller way: between August 19 and October 16 the shares fell 11.0% to $125.75 while 2026 Core FFO guidance was raised, and CFO Tim Arndt said on the Q3 call that the market is digesting the recent rise in Treasury yields, that the gap between buyers' and sellers' prices has widened, and that he expects transaction volumes to moderate in the near term. With shares at roughly 20.1x the midpoint of 2026 Core FFO guidance (computed), a further repricing is possible without any operational deterioration.

Supply digestion is not finished. US vacancy is 7.1% (down 10bps in Q3) — improving, but above Europe's 5.3%. US market rent growth in Q3 2026 was only +50bps for the quarter; Europe's rents rose 70bps in the quarter (Q3 call). Rexford's −14.6% one-year price return (−4.9% YTD) is the market's verdict on the weakest submarket (Southern California). If the post-COVID overbuild takes longer to absorb, the mark-to-market gap compresses and same-store NOI growth decelerates.

Execution and integration risk on SEGRO. SEGRO's shareholders approved the $18.8B cross-border, cash-and-stock acquisition on September 28, but UK court sanction, regulatory clearances and an LSE secondary listing remain, with closing targeted for H1 2027 — a long window of open risk. Prologis has already sold 15,000,000 shares (about $2.1B, about $2.4B with the underwriters' option) for general corporate purposes that the offering release said could include the SEGRO deal. Management itself guides the deal to be "broadly neutral to minimally dilutive" to Core FFO and AFFO per share in the first full year assuming run-rate synergies — meaning the base case is no near-term accretion, and any synergy shortfall is dilution. The stock component also dilutes existing holders at a ~20x FFO multiple.

Tenant concentration in a cyclical, tariff-exposed demand base. Warehouse demand is derived demand for physical goods flow. Tariff regimes, nearshoring shifts and consumer goods weakness all transmit directly into leasing decisions, and large 3PL and e-commerce tenants can defer expansion quickly. The Q3 supplemental lists Amazon at 5.6% and the top ten customers at 15.3% of net effective rent. Prologis's 75.9% retention in Q3 is healthy but implies roughly a quarter of expiring space turns over each cycle — with re-leasing downtime and capital cost each time.

Notable One-Off Items

Development gains and dispositions are the single largest GAAP distortion. Q3 2026 net earnings were $0.86 per diluted share (vs $0.82 a year earlier) against Core FFO of $1.60, a ~$0.74 per-share gap. Full-year GAAP guidance was cut to $4.05–$4.15 from $4.40–$4.55 in July, while Core FFO guidance rose to $6.25–$6.29 from $6.22–$6.30; realized development gains are still guided at $600–700M. The release does not itself explain the change in net earnings guidance; its guidance table lowers the plans for fund contributions (to $1.75–2.25B from $2.0–2.5B) and dispositions (to $1.75–2.5B from $2.25–2.75B). Investors should not extrapolate the GAAP EPS trajectory.

Promote income swings. Net promote income is guided at $0 for 2026, unchanged from July (it was guided to a $(50)M expense in January). Core FFO excluding promote was $1.61 in Q3 versus $1.60 reported, so promotes cost about a cent in the quarter; trailing-twelve-month net promote was an expense of $8.7M (Q3 supplemental). For FY2026, guided Core FFO and Core FFO ex-promote are identical at $6.25–6.29.

SEGRO transaction costs and currency. The pending $18.8B acquisition will bring deal costs, a GBP-denominated earnings stream and an LSE secondary listing. Prologis already carries meaningful European and Asian exposure, so reported results are exposed to translation effects; the Q3 release says 99% of forecast 2026 earnings are in US dollars or hedged (98% for 2027). The release again carries a UK Takeover Code disclosure tied to the SEGRO offer, which raises the bar on any future guidance revision.

FY2023 revenue growth of +32.56% is not organic — it reflects the 2022 Duke Realty acquisition annualizing, and comparisons spanning that year are not like-for-like.

Plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward. Above 70 means the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 means it has been beaten down and may be washed out. On monthly data these signals are slow — they mark multi-month regimes, not trading entries.

Five-year trajectory. From a November 2021 close of $150.75 (the first point in the series) to $125.75 on October 16, 2026, PLD returned −16.6% on price. Adding the dividends with ex-dividend dates in that stretch ($18.36/share, computed as the sum of the 20 quarterly payments from December 2021 through September 2026 as listed by Yahoo Finance, without reinvestment) lifts the total to approximately −4.4% — around −0.9% a year over 4.9 years. That is a poor outcome next to a 2026 Core FFO guided at $6.25–$6.29 against $5.81 in 2025, and a reading of the chart is that a lower valuation multiple, not weaker leasing, has been the main drag: the company's reported results have held up better than its share price.

The path was violently non-linear. The stock peaked at $168.36 in December 2021 at the height of the post-COVID logistics scramble, then fell −39.7% to $101.60 by September 2022 as the Fed's hiking cycle repriced every long-duration asset. It then spent roughly three full years — from late 2022 through late 2025 — oscillating in a $100.75–$133.30 band without making progress, a stretch that included a brutal −21.6% single-month drop in April 2024 on a demand-driven guidance reset. From October 2025 the stock broke out, running from $124.09 to a July 2026 close of $144.61 — a +16.5% move in nine months. It has since given back 13.0% of that: $139.92 at the end of August, $129.84 at the end of September and $125.75 on October 16. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Growth is contractually embedded, not hoped for: Q3 rollover leases repriced +38.0%, a 16% lease mark-to-market remains at September 30, 2026, and the quarter drove same-store NOI +5.7%/+5.9%, with occupancy at 95.6% and 60M sq ft leased in the quarter. Management has raised 2026 Core FFO guidance again, to $6.25–6.29 from $6.00–6.20 in January, and raised development starts guidance to $5.0–5.75B; Q3 starts carried a 6.6% yield against a 4.4% implied EBITDA/EV yield — a wide value-creation spread. On top sits genuine optionality: a 5.8 GW data center power pipeline (1.7 GW with secured power) on land already owned, and a SEGRO acquisition, now approved by SEGRO's shareholders, that lifts combined AUM to ~$269B. All of this at ~20.1x the Core FFO guidance midpoint with a 3.40% yield, 4.5x debt/EBITDA and a 3.3% cost of debt.

Bear Case

You are paying ~20.1x Core FFO and 2.14x book for a business whose price is set by long rates, with a beta of 1.32 — above the market's 1.00. The last five years proved the point: the stock returned only about −0.9% a year including dividends from November 2021 to October 2026, and the shares fell 11.0% in the two months after the last edition even as Core FFO guidance rose. US vacancy at 7.1% is still elevated, quarterly US market rent growth of +50bps is modest, and the SoCal bellwether (Rexford, −14.6% over a year) says the overbuild is not fully digested. The $18.8B SEGRO deal is guided to be at best neutral to year-one Core FFO, is partly funded with new Prologis shares (15,000,000 sold in August), and carries court and regulatory steps before it closes in H1 2027. The Q3 update also cut net earnings guidance and trimmed fund-contribution and disposition plans. Data centers are a small share of the portfolio — a story, not yet earnings. And free cash flow is negative $459.6M (trailing twelve months through Q2), so growth depends on continued access to disposition and third-party capital markets.

01
Interest-rate and cap-rate risk dominates everything else. A −39.7% drawdown from December 2021 to September 2022 occurred with operations at record levels, and management itself pointed to the recent rise in Treasury yields on the Q3 call. At ~20.1x Core FFO and a 1.32 beta, a renewed rise in long rates compresses the multiple and marks down portfolio values simultaneously — and the 3.40% dividend yield provides only partial support.
02
SEGRO integration and dilution. An $18.8B cross-border acquisition, funded substantially in stock, closing in H1 2027, guided to be "broadly neutral to minimally dilutive" to Core FFO and AFFO in year one assuming run-rate synergies. SEGRO's shareholders have approved it; any synergy shortfall, regulatory delay or GBP move could still turn the base case into dilution, and the UK Takeover Code disclosure in the Q3 release reduces guidance flexibility in the interim.
03
Demand normalization compressing the mark-to-market. The +38.0% net effective rent change on Q3 rollover leases is a function of a gap built during 2021–22's rent explosion (16% at September 30, 2026). That gap depletes as leases roll. If market rent growth stays near the +50bps quarterly pace while US vacancy sits at 7.1%, the roll-up rate decays over the next few years and same-store NOI growth converges toward low single digits — precisely the scenario the April 2024 −21.6% month was pricing.
  Industry Commentary
Interest Rates & the REIT Multiple
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

REIT equity value is a spread trade against long rates, and Prologis is the cleanest large-cap expression of that. The evidence is in the tape: $168.36 (Dec 2021) → $101.60 (Sep 2022), −39.7%, with occupancy and rent spreads at record levels the entire way down; then $100.75 (Oct 2023) → $133.30 (Dec 2023), +32.3% in eight weeks on nothing but a shift in rate expectations. The operating business is insulated — 3.3% weighted-average cost of debt, 7.6-year average maturity, 4.5x debt/EBITDA, $8.8B liquidity, A2/A ratings. The equity is not: at a 4.4% EBITDA/EV yield (computed implied-cap-rate proxy) versus a 3.40% dividend yield and a 1.32 beta, there is little valuation cushion if the discount rate backs up. Every operational win in 2026 can be overwritten by the rates market.

Digesting the Post-COVID Warehouse Overbuild
ONGOING HEADWIND

The 2021–22 rent explosion pulled forward an enormous wave of speculative development, and the market has spent three years absorbing it. The recovery is real but incomplete: US vacancy has fallen to 7.1%, Q3 2026 US net absorption was 63M sq ft, which CFO Tim Arndt called consistent with a normal year of 230–240M sq ft (Q3 call). But quarterly US market rent growth was only +50bps in Q3, while several markets (Dallas, Houston, Atlanta, Phoenix, Indianapolis, Northern California) were described on the call as on pace for 5–6% market rent growth. Europe, at 5.3% vacancy, saw rents rise 70bps in the quarter. The relevant tension: Prologis is raising development starts to $5.0–5.75B (Prologis share) into a market that still has 7.1% vacancy — a bet that the supply pipeline from merchant developers stays shut.

Data-Center Conversion Optionality — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND

Prologis discovered that its most valuable asset may not be the buildings but the grid interconnects attached to the land under them. The power pipeline stands at 5.8 GW, of which 1.7 GW has secured power and about 1 GW is covered by letters of intent (Q3 call). Through Q2 the company had started $2.1B of data center projects in 2026 against a ~$2B full-year goal, including a 260MW / ~$800M build-to-suit campus.

Bull framing: this is high-margin optionality on land already carried at historic cost, and it could re-rate Prologis from a cyclical goods-flow REIT toward a digital-infrastructure platform — a category the market pays a higher multiple for. SEGRO brings the same playbook in Europe, with a 3.0 GVA power bank and two data centre joint ventures with Pure DC.

Bear framing: data centers were under 1% of the global portfolio at Q2. None of the 2026 Core FFO guidance of $6.25–6.29 is attributed to them in the release. Conversions consume land that would otherwise support the logistics pipeline, hyperscaler demand is concentrated among a handful of counterparties, and the incremental returns have not been separately disclosed. If AI capex decelerates, the pipeline becomes an unrealizable option — and investors will have paid a premium multiple for it.
