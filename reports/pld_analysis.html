01 Company Overview

Prologis is the world's largest owner, operator and developer of logistics real estate — the warehouses and distribution centers that sit between factories, ports and the customer's doorstep. It is structured as a REIT and generates the overwhelming majority of revenue from long-duration leases on industrial buildings, supplemented by a fee-earning fund-management arm (Strategic Capital). Full-year 2025 revenue was $8.79B, up 7.45% YoY, with Core FFO of $5.81 per diluted share. Prologis ranks roughly #171 globally and #116 in the US by market capitalization, and is the second-largest holding in the S&P Real Estate Select Sector index (XLRE) at an 8.96% weight.

The company is in the middle of the largest transaction in its history: on August 3–4, 2026 it announced a recommended acquisition of SEGRO plc, the UK-listed European warehouse REIT, valuing SEGRO's equity at approximately $18.8B (≈£14.8B, 1,031.7p per share). On completion — targeted for H1 2027 — combined AUM would be roughly $269B, the European portfolio would reach 368M square feet (a 47% expansion of Prologis's European footprint), and the European land bank would grow 126%. Separately, Prologis has opened a genuinely new growth vector by converting land and secured electrical interconnects into data center capacity — the power pipeline stood at 5.8 GW as of Q2 2026.

Revenue Mix

The core engine: owning and leasing modern distribution facilities in supply-constrained submarkets near major consumption centers and ports. The economics are driven less by new leasing volume than by lease mark-to-market — the gap between below-market in-place rents signed years ago and today's market rents. In Q2 2026 that gap was still 17% on a net effective basis, meaning roughly a sixth of embedded rent growth is contractually locked in as leases roll. Q2 2026 delivered a net effective rent change of +36.9% and cash rent change of +22.3% on new and renewal leases, producing same-store NOI growth of +6.4% net effective / +8.5% cash. Period-end occupancy was 95.5% (up 20bps sequentially), average occupancy 95.0%, with 72.7% retention. Q2 leasing hit a record 67M square feet — the fourth record in seven quarters — following a record 228M sq ft signed across all of 2025. Customers span e-commerce, third-party logistics, retail replenishment and manufacturing. Management flagged that there is "no availability whatsoever in spaces larger than 1 million sq ft," a scarcity dynamic that supports pricing at the large-format end.

Prologis manages logistics real estate on behalf of sovereign wealth funds, pensions and insurers through open-ended and closed-end co-investment vehicles, collecting recurring asset-management fees plus promote income — performance fees earned when a fund clears a return hurdle. Q2 2026 produced $241.6M of Strategic Capital revenue and $26.2M of net promote income (gross promote revenue of ~$83M in the quarter). Promotes are lumpy and cycle-dependent: management entered 2026 guiding to a $(50)M net promote expense and by Q2 had raised that to $0M — a ~$50M swing that flows straight to Core FFO. The genuinely new leg of the story. Prologis owns land in exactly the places where power interconnects are scarce, and has been converting land parcels plus secured grid capacity into data center development — a use that clears far higher rents per square foot than warehousing. The power pipeline reached 5.8 GW in Q2 2026, up from roughly 2.9 GW two years earlier — a doubling — and management sees a >10 GW opportunity over ten years. Execution is running ahead of plan: $2.1B of data center starts year-to-date through Q2 2026 against a full-year goal of ~$2B, including a 260-megawatt build-to-suit campus at roughly $800M. The honest counterweight is that data centers remain under 1% of the global portfolio today, so this is optionality rather than earnings. It is also a strategically defensive move: if AI-driven power demand keeps bidding up interconnected land, Prologis monetizes an asset it already owns at a basis competitors cannot replicate. Management additionally argues AI capital spending itself creates warehouse demand — millions of square feet of space for equipment staging, components and construction supply chains.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
IRREPLACEABLE INFILL LAND 14,000-ACRE LAND BANK SECURED POWER INTERCONNECTS A2/A CREDIT · 3.3% COST OF DEBT GLOBAL CUSTOMER RELATIONSHIPS STRATEGIC CAPITAL FLYWHEEL 17% EMBEDDED MARK-TO-MARKET

Prologis's moat is not a technology or a brand — it is basis and balance sheet. The company owns land near ports, rail and dense population that cannot be replicated at today's prices, and it finances that land with debt costing a 3.3% weighted-average rate over a 7.9-year average term against A2/A ratings. That funding advantage is decisive in an asset class where returns are a spread over cost of capital: Prologis can start development at a 7.2% stabilized yield and acquire at a 4.1% cap rate — roughly a 20% discount to replacement cost — while a levered private competitor borrowing hundreds of basis points wider cannot make the same math work. Layered on top is the lease mark-to-market of 17% net effective, which converts to reported growth mechanically as leases roll: Q2 2026 signings repriced +36.9% net effective and +22.3% cash. Finally, the Strategic Capital arm turns scale into a self-funding loop — stabilized assets are contributed to managed funds at tight cap rates, the proceeds fund higher-yielding development, and Prologis keeps the fees and the operating control. Adding SEGRO would extend all three legs simultaneously: European footprint +47% to 368M sq ft, European land bank +126%, and combined AUM to ~$269B.

Interest rates and cap rates set the valuation, not operations. The 2022–23 drawdown — from $147.04 at end-2021 to $90.40 in September 2022, a −38.5% move — happened while occupancy and rent spreads were near record highs. REIT equity is a duration asset: when the discount rate moves, the multiple moves regardless of NOI. With shares at roughly 22.6x the midpoint of 2026 Core FFO guidance (computed), a repeat of that repricing is entirely possible without any operational deterioration.

Supply digestion is not finished. US vacancy is 7.2% — improving, but still well above Europe's 5.2% and above pre-2022 levels. Market rent growth in Q2 2026 was only +70bps in the US for the quarter; Europe's rents have recovered 160bps from last year's trough and management describes Europe as running about 12 months ahead of the US in the recovery. Rexford's −0.42% YTD return is the market's verdict on the weakest submarket (Southern California). If the post-COVID overbuild takes longer to absorb, the mark-to-market gap compresses and same-store NOI decelerates.

Execution and integration risk on SEGRO. An $18.8B cross-border, cash-and-stock acquisition requiring SEGRO shareholder approval, UK court sanction, regulatory clearances and an LSE secondary listing, closing in H1 2027, is a long window of open risk. Management itself guides the deal to be "broadly neutral to minimally dilutive" to Core FFO and AFFO per share in the first full year assuming run-rate synergies — meaning the base case is no near-term accretion, and any synergy shortfall is dilution. The stock component also dilutes existing holders at a ~22x FFO multiple.

Tenant concentration in a cyclical, tariff-exposed demand base. Warehouse demand is derived demand for physical goods flow. Tariff regimes, nearshoring shifts and consumer goods weakness all transmit directly into leasing decisions, and large 3PL and e-commerce tenants can defer expansion quickly. Prologis's 72.7% retention is healthy but implies roughly a quarter of expiring space turns over each cycle — with re-leasing downtime and capital cost each time.

Notable One-Off Items

Development gains and dispositions are the single largest GAAP distortion. Q2 2026 net earnings of $1,060.8M ($1.13/diluted share) versus Core FFO of $1.63/share is a ~$0.50 per-share gap, and FY2026 GAAP guidance was raised far more aggressively (from $3.70–4.00 in January to $4.40–4.55 in July) than Core FFO guidance was (from $6.00–6.20 to $6.22–6.30) — the difference is realized gains on asset sales, which January guidance pegged at $400–600M. Investors should not extrapolate the GAAP EPS trajectory.

Promote income swings. Net promote income was $26.2M in Q2 2026 (~$83M gross promote revenue), and full-year guidance moved from a $(50)M expense to $0M — a ~$50M positive swing. Core FFO excluding net promote was $1.60 in Q2 versus $1.63 reported. For FY2026, guided Core FFO and Core FFO ex-promote are now identical at $6.22–6.30, so promotes are not currently padding guidance.

SEGRO transaction costs and currency. The pending $18.8B acquisition will bring deal costs, a GBP-denominated earnings stream and an LSE secondary listing. Prologis already carries meaningful European and Asian exposure — development yields are disclosed separately for Europe (5.4%) and Asia (5.1%) — so reported results are exposed to translation effects. No specific FX gain/loss figure was verifiable at capture, and none is asserted here. Note also that the Q2 2026 guidance constitutes a profit forecast under UK Takeover Code Rule 28 because of the SEGRO offer, which raises the bar on any future revision.

No material impairments were identified in the Q2 2026 or FY2025 releases reviewed. Also worth flagging: FY2023 revenue growth of +32.56% is not organic — it reflects the 2022 Duke Realty acquisition annualizing, and comparisons spanning that year are not like-for-like.

Plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward. Above 70 means the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 means it has been beaten down and may be washed out. On monthly data these signals are slow — they mark multi-month regimes, not trading entries.

Five-year trajectory. From a September 2021 close of $109.11 to $141.30 on August 19, 2026, PLD returned +29.5% on price over five years. Adding the declared dividends over the period (roughly $16.66/share, computed as a simple sum of the verified quarterly rates for 2022–1H2026 and excluding reinvestment) lifts the total to approximately +45% — around 7.7% annualized. That is a poor outcome for a company that grew Core FFO per share, occupancy and rent spreads throughout, and it is the single most important fact about this chart: the entire shortfall is multiple compression driven by interest rates, not operational failure.

The path was violently non-linear. The stock peaked at $147.04 in December 2021 at the height of the post-COVID logistics scramble, then fell −38.5% to $90.40 by September 2022 as the Fed's hiking cycle repriced every long-duration asset. It then spent roughly three full years — from late 2022 through late 2025 — oscillating in a $92–$123 band without making progress, a stretch that included a brutal −21.6% single-month drop in April 2024 on a demand-driven guidance reset. Only from October 2025 did the stock break out, running from $121.27 to a July 2026 close of $144.61 — a +19% move in nine months — and it has still not reclaimed the December 2021 high on a monthly closing basis, four and a half years later.

Relative performance. On the verified one-year window, PLD returned +38.35% (total return) versus +20.87% for the S&P 500 via SPY and +13.68% for the real-estate sector via XLRE — roughly 1,750bps of alpha versus the index and 2,470bps versus its own sector. That is a decisive recent win. Stretch the window to five years and the picture inverts: a ~7.7% annualized total return badly lags the S&P 500's long-run experience over the same period, because PLD entered 2022 with a peak-cycle multiple and spent three years giving it back. The investment question is which regime is now operative — the 2022–24 rate-compression regime or the 2025–26 operating-momentum regime. Against the peer set, PLD's +38.35% one-year return leads every listed industrial REIT comparable (EGP, FR, TRNO, REXR), with Rexford's +4.56% at the bottom.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Growth is contractually embedded, not hoped for: a 17% net effective lease mark-to-market repriced Q2 signings +36.9% and drove same-store NOI +6.4%/+8.5%, with occupancy at 95.5% and record 67M sq ft leased. Management has raised 2026 Core FFO guidance twice, to $6.22–6.30 from $6.00–6.20, and doubled development starts guidance to $4.5–5.5B at a 7.2% yield against a 3.9% implied EBITDA/EV yield — a wide, self-funded value-creation spread. On top sits genuine optionality: a 5.8 GW data center power pipeline (doubled in two years, >10 GW opportunity) on land already owned, and a SEGRO acquisition that lifts combined AUM to ~$269B. All of this at ~22.6x forward Core FFO with a 3.04% yield, 4.7x debt/EBITDA and a 3.3% cost of debt.

Bear Case

You are paying ~22.6x forward Core FFO and 2.43x book for a business whose price is set by long rates, with a beta of 1.32 — higher than both the market and its own sector. The last five years proved the point: Core FFO, occupancy and rent spreads all compounded, and the stock still returned only ~7.7% annualized because the multiple compressed. US vacancy at 7.2% is still elevated, quarterly market rent growth of +70bps is modest, and the SoCal bellwether (Rexford, −0.42% YTD) says the overbuild is not fully digested. The $18.8B SEGRO deal is guided to be at best neutral to year-one Core FFO, issues stock at a full multiple, and carries a year of regulatory and integration risk before it closes in H1 2027. Data centers are <1% of the portfolio — a story, not yet earnings. And free cash flow is negative $459.6M, so growth depends on continued access to disposition and third-party capital markets.

01
Interest-rate and cap-rate risk dominates everything else. A −38.5% drawdown from December 2021 to September 2022 occurred with operations at record levels. At 22.6x forward Core FFO and a 1.32 beta, a renewed rise in long rates compresses the multiple and marks down portfolio values simultaneously — and the 3.04% dividend yield provides only thin support relative to a real-estate sector averaging 3.12%.
02
SEGRO integration and dilution. An $18.8B cross-border acquisition, funded substantially in stock, closing in H1 2027, guided to be "broadly neutral to minimally dilutive" to Core FFO and AFFO in year one assuming run-rate synergies. Any synergy shortfall, regulatory delay or GBP move turns that into outright dilution, and the Rule 28 profit-forecast constraint reduces guidance flexibility in the interim.
03
Demand normalization compressing the mark-to-market. The +36.9% net effective rent change is a function of a 17% embedded gap built during 2021–22's rent explosion. That gap depletes as leases roll. If market rent growth stays near the +70bps quarterly pace while US vacancy sits at 7.2%, the roll-up rate decays over the next few years and same-store NOI growth converges toward low single digits — precisely the scenario the April 2024 −21.6% month was pricing.
  Industry Commentary
Interest Rates & the REIT Multiple
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

REIT equity value is a spread trade against long rates, and Prologis is the cleanest large-cap expression of that. The evidence is in the tape: $147.04 (Dec 2021) → $90.40 (Sep 2022), −38.5%, with occupancy and rent spreads at record levels the entire way down; then $92.20 (Oct 2023) → $122.77 (Dec 2023), +33% in eight weeks on nothing but a shift in rate expectations. The operating business is insulated — 3.3% weighted-average cost of debt, 7.9-year average maturity, 4.7x debt/EBITDA, $7.6B liquidity, A2/A ratings. The equity is not: at a 3.9% EBITDA/EV yield (computed implied-cap-rate proxy) versus a 3.04% dividend yield and a 1.32 beta, there is little valuation cushion if the discount rate backs up. Every operational win in 2026 can be overwritten by the rates market.

Digesting the Post-COVID Warehouse Overbuild
ONGOING HEADWIND

The 2021–22 rent explosion pulled forward an enormous wave of speculative development, and the market has spent three years absorbing it. The recovery is real but incomplete: US vacancy has fallen to 7.2%, Q2 2026 US net absorption of 66M sq ft was the best since 2022, Southern California vacancy dropped 30bps sequentially to below 7%, and management estimates a run-rate of ~220M sq ft of US absorption annually. But quarterly market rent growth was only +70bps in the US, and Europe — at 5.2% vacancy with rents 160bps off the trough — is described by management as roughly 12 months ahead of the US, which is a polite way of saying the US is the laggard. The scarcity is concentrated at the top end, where management reports "no availability whatsoever in spaces larger than 1 million sq ft." The relevant tension: Prologis is raising development starts to $4.5–5.5B into a market that still has 7.2% vacancy — a bet that the supply pipeline from merchant developers stays shut.

Data-Center Conversion Optionality — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND

Prologis discovered that its most valuable asset may not be the buildings but the grid interconnects attached to the land under them. The power pipeline has gone from ~2.9 GW to 5.8 GW in two years, with a stated >10 GW decade-long opportunity, and execution is running ahead of plan — $2.1B of data center starts through Q2 2026 against a ~$2B full-year target, including a 260MW / ~$800M build-to-suit campus.

Bull framing: this is high-margin optionality on land already carried at historic cost, monetizable without new capital, and it re-rates Prologis from a cyclical goods-flow REIT toward a digital-infrastructure platform — a category the market pays a materially higher multiple for. SEGRO brings the same playbook in Europe, including a £1B West London data center JV.

Bear framing: data centers are under 1% of the global portfolio today. None of the 2026 Core FFO of $6.22–6.30 depends on them. Conversions consume land that would otherwise support the logistics pipeline, hyperscaler demand is concentrated among a handful of counterparties, and the incremental returns have not been separately disclosed. If AI capex decelerates, the 5.8 GW pipeline becomes an unrealizable option — and investors will have paid a premium multiple for it.
