01 Company Overview

Palantir builds operating software for data — Gotham (defense and intelligence), Foundry (companies and civilian government), Apollo (the system that installs and updates its software), and AIP, the Artificial Intelligence Platform that connects large language models to an organization's live "ontology", a working map of its assets, processes and decisions. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $4.475 billion, up 56% YoY, and growth has since sped up: Q3 2026 revenue of $2.313 billion grew 96% YoY, the first quarter above $2 billion, and US commercial revenue ($984 million, +148%) passed US government revenue ($953 million, +96%) for the first time. At a $413.7 billion market value, Palantir is priced at about 57 times its $7.29 billion of revenue over the last four quarters.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Ontology Layer Forward-Deployed Engineers FedRAMP High / IL6 Accreditation 20+ Yr Classified Track Record Sole-Source Enterprise Agreements Operational Switching Costs Bootcamp Go-To-Market

Palantir's moat is not the models — it explicitly does not train frontier LLMs — but the layer underneath them. The ontology maps a customer's messy operational reality (aircraft, patients, invoices, munitions, shipments) into a governed object graph with permissions, lineage and write-back to source systems. That is what makes an agent's output actionable rather than advisory, and it is the piece that generic model APIs and data warehouses do not supply. Once an ontology is built, it becomes the semantic spine of the enterprise: switching means re-deriving every workflow, permission and audit trail, which is why net dollar retention is 165%, against 125% at Snowflake. Layered on top are accreditations most software companies never obtain (FedRAMP High and Defense Department Impact Level 5/6 authorisation, cleared staff, classified deployment experience) and contract vehicles that get easier to buy through over time: the Army's $10B enterprise agreement and Maven's move to a program of record replace dozens of separate procurement fights with pre-agreed pricing and volume discounts. The forward-deployed engineers, once criticised as a consulting-style drag on margins, now build the reference workflows that make bootcamps convert, and the adjusted operating margin was 62% in Q3 2026.

Valuation is itself the primary vulnerability. At $171.70 the stock trades at about 57× trailing sales, 48× the revenue Palantir guides to for FY2026 and 120× trailing GAAP EPS. A price like that already assumes Palantir grows into a revenue base several times today's at software margins. A quarter that only meets expectations can hit the stock hard, and the record shows it: the shares fell 49% from a $207.52 intraday high on November 3, 2025 to a $106.37 low on June 25, 2026 while revenue kept growing, and they slipped 3.7% over the two sessions after a Q3 that beat and raised guidance.

Government concentration and political exposure. Government (US plus international) was 50% of Q3 2026 revenue ($1.146B of $2.313B, 10-Q), and the US federal part runs through a handful of very large contract vehicles. A stopgap budget (continuing resolution), spending cuts, a change in administration priorities, or a protest of a sole-source award can each dent a growth rate that the market values at 48× this year's revenue. Palantir's work in immigration enforcement, health data and targeting also carries reputational and regulatory tail risk: its Denver offices drew repeated protests in 2025 and January 2026, including over its immigration-enforcement work, before it moved its headquarters to Miami in February 2026; the company gave no reason for the move (Colorado Politics).

Competition is arriving from above and below. Anduril's $20 billion Army enterprise agreement in March 2026 — double the ceiling of Palantir's own $10B deal, consolidating 120 contracts around the Lattice operating system — is direct evidence that the Army will fund a second software-native prime. At the same time the big cloud providers (Microsoft Fabric, AWS, Google Cloud), Databricks at $6.9B of annualised revenue growing 80%, and the AI labs themselves are building tools for AI agents that run business workflows, which weakens the "only Palantir can do this" case on the commercial side.

Dilution and the GAAP-to-adjusted gap. FY2025 stock-based compensation was $684 million against only $75 million of buybacks, and in the first nine months of 2026 it was $786 million against $1.5 million of buybacks, so the share count keeps rising. The 15-point gap between the 62% adjusted operating margin and the 47% GAAP operating margin in Q3 2026 is the $319 million of stock pay plus $23 million of payroll taxes on it. Investors paying 48× this year's revenue are paying it on a share count that keeps growing.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items materially distort the reported profitability and deserve to be stripped out before extrapolating:

1. Stock-based compensation is the largest recurring "adjustment," and it is not a one-off. FY2025 SBC was $684 million (~15% of revenue), against buybacks of only $75 million; Q3 2026 SBC was $319 million (~14% of revenue). In Q3 2026, SBC and the payroll taxes on it ($23 million) account for the whole 15-point gap between the 62% adjusted operating margin and the 47% GAAP operating margin. GAAP is the better anchor.

2. Interest income lifts net income above operating income. Palantir earned $89 million of interest income in Q3 2026 and $233 million in the first nine months, on cash and marketable securities that reached $10.6 billion at September 30. In Q3, GAAP net income of $1.128B exceeded GAAP operating income of $1.085B; the difference is non-operating. The other non-operating line, "other income (expense), net", swings with items such as investment marks: a $26 million net loss in Q3 after a $160 million net gain in the first half. Such swings are not recurring earnings and can reverse.

3. Tax. The Q3 2026 tax provision was $13.6 million on $1.148 billion of pre-tax income, about 1.2%, far below the statutory rate. Palantir's own adjusted EPS assumes a 23% long-term tax rate, which is why adjusted EPS ($0.44) came out equal to GAAP EPS in Q3 even though it adds back stock pay. A normal tax rate would narrow the gap between operating and net margin.

Netting these out: the ~50% net margin is real but lifted by interest income and the low tax bill, and the 62% adjusted operating margin leaves out about 15 points of cost paid in shares.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent price movement has been upward. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen so fast it is often described as "overbought"; below 30 means the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation tool — PLTR has held above 70 for extended stretches while continuing to rise.

Five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 month-end close of $20.65, PLTR rose +731% to the November 4, 2026 close of $171.70 (price only; Palantir pays no dividend), but the path was anything but straight. The stock fell 69% to a $6.42 month-end close in December 2022, when rising interest rates hit unprofitable software stocks and investors doubted that Palantir's consulting-heavy model could scale. From that low the gain is +2,574%. The turn came from the business: GAAP profits every quarter from late 2022, then the April 2023 launch of AIP, which recast Palantir from custom software projects into a product for putting AI to work. The 2024–2025 run combined index inclusion (S&P 500 in September 2024, NASDAQ-100 that December) with faster growth, and peaked at $207.52 intraday on November 3, 2025. 2026 has been a round trip twice over: a 49% fall to $106.37 on June 25, a doubling to a record $212.94 intraday on October 12 after the Q2 report, and a slide to $171.70 by November 4, 19% below that high, even as Q3 beat and raised guidance.

Relative performance. Over the same window (November 30, 2021 to November 4, 2026), SPY, the S&P 500 index fund, returned +68% ($455.56 → $766.81) and QQQ, the Nasdaq-100 fund, +76% ($393.82 → $691.95), on a price basis with dividends excluded. PLTR's +731% is about 10.7 times SPY's. The last year looks very different: from November 4, 2025 to November 4, 2026 PLTR is down 10.0% ($190.74 → $171.70) while SPY rose 13.6% and QQQ 11.7%, even though quarterly revenue nearly doubled ($1.181B in Q3 2025 to $2.313B in Q3 2026). That gap is the whole debate in one line: the business got much bigger and the stock got cheaper relative to it, because the price a year ago already assumed the growth.

08 Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

Palantir is doing something rare for enterprise software at this size: growth sped up from 56% in FY2025 to 96% in Q3 2026 while the adjusted operating margin ran at 62%, a Rule of 40 score of 158 (growth plus margin; 40 is considered excellent). Net dollar retention of 165% means existing customers alone are growing faster than most software companies grow in total, and $13.4B of total remaining deal value (+55% YoY) is already contracted or awarded for coming quarters. The ontology, the FedRAMP High and IL6 accreditations and a $10B Army enterprise vehicle add up to a moat that neither the big cloud providers nor the AI labs can copy quickly, and $10.6B of cash and securities with no borrowings means the company never has to sell anything under pressure. If revenue reached $18B in FY2028, today's $413.7B market value would be about 23× that year's sales: expensive, but not absurd for the best-placed company in enterprise AI.

BEAR CASE

At 57× trailing sales, 48× this year's guided revenue, 120× trailing earnings and about 84× this year's free cash flow guidance, PLTR is priced for near-flawless execution for a decade, and the market has already shown it will not always pay that much: it cut the stock 49% in under eight months to June 2026 while revenue kept growing, and it sold the shares after a Q3 that beat and raised. Growth of 96% cannot last; Palantir's own Q4 guidance already implies about 88%, Q3 contract signings grew only 6%, and once growth heads toward 40–50% there is little to hold up the price. A fall to a still-generous 25× this year's revenue would mean roughly −48%. The bear case is not that Palantir is a bad company (RBC, at Underperform with a $130 target, grants the execution); it is that no realistic 2030 revenue and margin path justifies $413.7B today. Add steady dilution from stock pay ($786M in the first nine months of 2026), insider selling under trading plans, government at half of revenue and exposed to budget fights, Anduril's $20B Army vehicle showing the government will fund a rival, and international commercial growing only 21%, and the margin of safety is not thin: it is negative.

01
A lower valuation if growth slows. The biggest risk by far. At 48× this year's revenue, most of the market value is a bet on growth years out rather than on today's cash flow. The record so far: a 49% fall from November 2025 to June 2026 while revenue grew strongly, and a 3.7% two-session slide after a Q3 that beat and raised. With Q4 guidance implying growth slowing to about 88%, any sign of a sharper slowdown would test the price again.
02
Government concentration, appropriations and competitive displacement. Government was 50% of Q3 2026 revenue and the US federal piece is concentrated in a small number of very large vehicles. A stopgap budget (continuing resolution), a shift in administration priorities, a successful bid protest, or expansion of Anduril's $20B Army enterprise agreement into neighbouring missions would each hit the most visible half of the business. Political and reputational exposure around immigration, health and military targeting work adds to this.
03
Dilution, insider supply, and the GAAP-versus-adjusted gap. $684M of FY2025 stock compensation against $75M of buybacks, and $786M in the first nine months of 2026 against $1.5M, means each share's slice of the company keeps shrinking, and the 15-point gap between 62% adjusted and 47% GAAP operating margins is a real cost. Peter Thiel sold 2 million shares on March 2, 2026 under a trading plan, and insiders kept filing Form 144 notices of planned sales from August to November 2, 2026 (SEC EDGAR). Owning PLTR means accepting that the people with the most information keep selling.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
AI Software Revenue — The Proof Keeps Arriving
EMERGING TAILWIND
For three years the central question in enterprise AI has been whether anyone would turn model capability into recurring software revenue at scale. Palantir's Q3 2026 is another clear yes: US commercial revenue +148% to $984M, 686 US commercial customers (+29%), net dollar retention of 165%, and 64 deals above $10M in a single quarter (down from 73 in Q2). The contrast with the rest of the category is stark: Snowflake grew FY2026 product revenue 29% to $4.47B with 125% net revenue retention, and C3.ai's revenue fell 36% to $250M with a $470M net loss and a restructuring. Only Databricks, at $6.9B annualised and +80%, is growing in the same range. The takeaway: AI budgets are concentrating on the few platforms that can put AI agents to work on a company's own controlled data, and the gap between the winners and the rest is widening. The caution from Q3 is that even the winner's new signings can slow: total contract value grew only 6%.
Defense Budgets and the Rise of Software-First Defense Contractors
ONGOING HEADWIND & TAILWIND
The tailwind is real and lasting: the Pentagon raised Maven's contract ceiling to nearly $1.3B in 2025 and in March 2026 ordered it turned into a program of record with its own budget line (Maven now has more than 100,000 users, per CTO Shyam Sankar on the Q3 call), the Army consolidated 75 Palantir contracts into a $10B enterprise agreement, NATO bought Maven six months after setting out the requirement, and allied members have committed to raising defense spending toward 5% of GDP. US government revenue grew 96% YoY in Q3 2026 and international government 31%. But the same buying reform that opened the door for Palantir is now creating competitors: Anduril's $20B Army enterprise agreement in March 2026 consolidated 120 contracts around its Lattice software at twice Palantir's ceiling. Army CTO Gabe Chiulli has called enterprise contracts a key part of the Army's modernisation strategy, and it has now signed one with Anduril as well as Palantir. Defense is therefore both the most reliable growth engine and the place where the moat is most tested, and government is still half of revenue (50% in Q3), so the outcome matters a great deal.
Valuation — The Binding Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every other point in this analysis comes second to one number: PLTR trades at about 57× trailing sales and 48× the revenue it guides to for FY2026, against a software average in the mid-single digits. Trailing P/E is 120×. On adjusted free cash flow, the most flattering measure available, the stock is at about 84× the FY2026 guidance midpoint. The bull view: at 96% growth with a 62% adjusted operating margin and 165% net dollar retention, Palantir grows into its valuation faster than any comparable company; three years of 50% growth would take the price-to-sales ratio on this year's revenue from 48× to roughly 14× at today's price. The bear view: the price leaves no room for an ordinary quarter. The stock fell 49% between November 2025 and June 2026 while revenue kept growing, and slipped after a Q3 beat and raise, which shows that results alone will not defend the price. Seen this way, owning PLTR is less a bet on the business and more a bet that the market keeps paying today's price for confidence about 2030. That is not something the company controls.
