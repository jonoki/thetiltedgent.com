01 Company Overview

Palantir builds enterprise operating systems for data — Gotham (defense/intelligence), Foundry (commercial and civil government), Apollo (deployment infrastructure), and AIP, the Artificial Intelligence Platform that wires large language models into an organization's live operational "ontology" of assets, processes and decisions. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $4.475 billion, up 56% YoY, and the business has since accelerated rather than decelerated: Q2 2026 revenue of $1.935 billion grew 93% YoY, with US commercial up 149%. At a ~$411 billion market capitalization Palantir is roughly the 34th most valuable listed company on earth and the 11th largest on the NASDAQ — a valuation carried on approximately $6.2 billion of trailing revenue, which makes it, on almost any sales or earnings multiple, the most expensive large-cap in the NASDAQ-100.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Ontology Layer Forward-Deployed Engineers FedRAMP High / IL6 Accreditation 20+ Yr Classified Track Record Sole-Source Enterprise Agreements Operational Switching Costs Bootcamp Go-To-Market

Palantir's moat is not the models — it explicitly does not train frontier LLMs — but the layer underneath them. The ontology maps a customer's messy operational reality (aircraft, patients, invoices, munitions, shipments) into a governed object graph with permissions, lineage and write-back to source systems. That is what makes an agent's output actionable rather than advisory, and it is the piece that generic model APIs and data warehouses do not supply. Once an ontology is built, it becomes the semantic spine of the enterprise: switching means re-deriving every workflow, permission and audit trail, which is why net dollar retention is 157% rather than the 110–125% typical of enterprise software. Layered on top are accreditations most software companies will never obtain — FedRAMP High and DoD Impact Level 5/6 authorisation, decades of cleared personnel, and classified deployment experience — and a distribution advantage that compounds: the Army's $10B enterprise agreement and Maven's promotion to a program of record convert dozens of separate procurement fights into pre-negotiated pricing and volume discounts. The forward-deployed engineering model, historically criticised as a services-like drag on margins, has inverted into an advantage: it produces the reference workflows that make bootcamps convert, and adjusted operating margin has still reached 62%.

Valuation is itself the primary vulnerability. At $171.04 the stock trades at roughly 67× trailing sales, 50× forward sales and 146× trailing EPS. That is not a demanding multiple, it is a multiple that already assumes Palantir grows into a $30B+ revenue business at software margins. Any quarter that merely meets guidance — let alone one that decelerates — carries asymmetric downside, and the stock has already demonstrated this: it fell from a $207.52 high to a $106.37 low inside twelve months while fundamentals never wavered.

Government concentration and political exposure. Government (US plus international) is 51% of revenue, and the US federal component is dominated by a handful of very large vehicles. Continuing resolutions, budget sequestration, a change in administration priorities, or a protest of a sole-source award can each dent a growth rate that the market is capitalising at 50× sales. Palantir's work in immigration enforcement, health data and targeting also carries reputational and regulatory tail risk — the Denver-to-Miami headquarters move in February 2026 followed sustained local protest.

Competition is arriving from above and below. Anduril's $20 billion Army enterprise agreement in March 2026 — double the ceiling of Palantir's own $10B deal, consolidating 120 contracts around the Lattice operating system — is direct evidence that the Army will fund a second software-native prime. Simultaneously the hyperscalers (Microsoft Fabric, AWS, Google Cloud), Databricks at $6.9B ARR growing 80%, and the frontier labs themselves are building agentic workflow tooling that erodes the "only Palantir can do this" premise from the commercial side.

Dilution and the GAAP-to-adjusted gap. FY2025 stock-based compensation was $684 million against only $75 million of buybacks, so the share count grows structurally. The 15-point spread between the 62% adjusted operating margin and the 47% GAAP operating margin in Q2 2026 is almost entirely SBC. Investors paying 50× forward sales are paying that multiple on a share count that is not static.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items materially distort the reported profitability and deserve to be stripped out before extrapolating:

1. Stock-based compensation is the largest recurring "adjustment," and it is not a one-off. FY2025 SBC was $684 million (~15% of revenue), against buybacks of only $75 million. In Q2 2026, SBC accounts for essentially the entire 15-point gap between the 62% adjusted operating margin and the 47% GAAP operating margin. Investors should anchor on GAAP.

2. Interest income and investment marks are inflating net income above operating income. Palantir earned $229 million of interest income in FY2025 on its cash pile, and the balance has since grown to $9.2 billion. In Q2 2026, GAAP net income of $1.062B exceeded GAAP operating income of $912M — the difference is non-operating. Separately, gains on the company's SpaceX position contributed roughly $0.03 to Q2 GAAP EPS (about 7% of the $0.41 reported). Marks on private holdings are not revenue-quality earnings and can reverse.

3. Tax. Palantir's effective tax rate remains well below statutory as historical net operating losses are consumed, which flatters net margin relative to the eventual steady state. As those shields exhaust, the gap between operating and net margin will narrow.

Netting these out: the ~49% net margin is real but overstated by perhaps 8–12 points of non-operating and tax-timing benefit, and the 62% adjusted operating margin overstates economic profitability by roughly 15 points of equity dilution.

Five-year trajectory. From the September 2021 close of $24.04, PLTR delivered a total return of +611% to the August 12, 2026 close of $171.04 — but the path bears almost no resemblance to a straight line. The stock lost 73% of its value through 2022, bottoming at a $6.42 monthly close in December 2022 amid the rate-shock de-rating of unprofitable software and persistent doubts about whether Palantir's services-heavy model could ever scale. But the relative picture inverts over shorter windows: over the trailing twelve months PLTR is down roughly 6% while the S&P 500 has advanced — the stock has spent the last year de-rating even as revenue growth accelerated from 48% to 93%. That divergence is the entire debate in one sentence: the business got substantially better and the stock got slightly cheaper, because it started from a multiple that assumed the improvement.

08 Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

Palantir is doing something no enterprise software company at this scale has done: accelerating from 56% to 93% revenue growth while running a 62% adjusted operating margin — a Rule of 40 score of 155, versus a benchmark where 40 is considered excellent. Net dollar retention of 157% means the installed base alone compounds faster than most software companies grow in total, and $13.1B of remaining deal value plus $2.13B of quarterly US commercial bookings pre-fund the next several quarters. The ontology plus FedRAMP/IL6 accreditation plus a $10B Army enterprise vehicle constitute a moat that neither hyperscalers nor frontier labs can replicate quickly, and $9.2B of net cash with no debt means the company is never a forced seller of anything. If US commercial sustains even half its current rate, FY2028 revenue exceeds $18B and today's price is ~23× that year's sales — expensive, but not absurd for the best-positioned asset in enterprise AI.

BEAR CASE

At 67× trailing sales, 50× forward sales, 146× trailing earnings and ~88× forward free cash flow, PLTR is priced for near-flawless execution over a decade — and the market has already shown it will not pay this multiple indefinitely, cutting the stock 49% in seven months on no fundamental deterioration whatsoever. Growth of 93% is arithmetically unsustainable; the moment the rate steps down toward 40–50%, the multiple has no support, and a de-rate to a still-premium 25× forward sales implies roughly −50%. The bear case is not that Palantir is a bad company — RBC's $90 target explicitly concedes the execution — it is that no realistic 2030 revenue and margin path underwrites $411B today. Add structural equity dilution from $684M of annual SBC, persistent insider selling by both Thiel and Karp, 51% government revenue exposure to appropriations risk, Anduril's $20B Army vehicle proving the government will fund a rival, and international commercial growing at only 26%, and the margin of safety is not thin — it is negative.

01
Multiple compression on any growth deceleration. The dominant risk by an order of magnitude. At 50× forward sales, roughly 80% of the current market capitalisation is discounted future growth rather than current cash flow. A single quarter of merely in-line results — not a miss, just no beat-and-raise — is historically sufficient to trigger a 20–30% drawdown, as the 49% decline from October 2025 to June 2026 demonstrated against a backdrop of 71–93% revenue growth.
02
Government concentration, appropriations and competitive displacement. Government is 51% of revenue and the US federal piece is concentrated in a small number of very large vehicles. A continuing resolution, a shift in administration priorities, a successful bid protest, or expansion of Anduril's $20B Army enterprise agreement into adjacent mission areas would each hit the highest-margin, highest-visibility part of the business. Political and reputational exposure around immigration, health and targeting work compounds this.

  Industry Commentary — What Actually Matters Right Now
AI Software Monetisation — The Proof Finally Arrived
EMERGING TAILWIND
For three years the central question in enterprise AI has been whether anyone would convert model capability into recurring software revenue at scale. Palantir's Q2 2026 is the clearest affirmative data point yet: US commercial revenue +149% to $764M, 653 US commercial customers (+35%), net dollar retention of 157%, and 73 individual deals above $10M in a single quarter. The contrast with the rest of the category is stark — Snowflake grew FY2026 product revenue 29% to $4.47B with 125% net revenue retention, and C3.ai's revenue fell 36% to $250M with a $470M net loss and a restructuring. Only Databricks, at $6.9B annualised and +80%, is compounding in the same neighbourhood, and its margins are being compressed by agent workloads. The read-through: AI budgets are consolidating toward the handful of platforms that can put agents into production against governed operational data, and the gap between the winners and the rest is widening, not narrowing.
Defense Budgets and the Rise of the Software-Native Prime
ONGOING HEADWIND & TAILWIND
The structural tailwind is real and durable: the Pentagon has formalised Maven as a department-wide program of record after raising its ceiling past $1B, the Army consolidated 75 Palantir contracts into a $10B enterprise agreement, NATO procured Maven in six days, and allied members are committed to raising defense spending toward 5% of GDP. US government revenue reaccelerated to +90% YoY and international government to +42%. But the same procurement reform that created Palantir's opening is now being used to create competitors: Anduril's $20B Army enterprise agreement in March 2026 consolidated 120 contracts around Lattice at twice Palantir's ceiling. The Army has explicitly said enterprise vehicles are a modernisation strategy, not an exclusivity award. Defense is therefore both the most reliable growth engine in the model and the segment where the moat is most actively being tested — and government remains 51% of total revenue, so the outcome matters disproportionately.
Valuation and Multiple Compression — The Binding Constraint
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every other consideration in this analysis is subordinate to one number: PLTR trades at approximately 67× trailing sales and 50× forward sales, against a software sector median in the mid-single digits and a NASDAQ-100 aggregate near 5×. Trailing P/E is 146× versus ~33× for the index. On adjusted free cash flow — the most flattering metric available — the stock is at ~88× the FY2026 guidance midpoint. The bull framing: at 93% growth with a 62% adjusted operating margin and 157% net dollar retention, Palantir compounds into the multiple faster than any comparable asset; three years of 50% growth takes forward sales from 50× to roughly 15×, and the market has consistently underestimated the trajectory — every quarter since Q3 2024 has beaten and raised. The bear framing: the multiple leaves no room for the ordinary. The stock fell 49% between October 2025 and June 2026 while revenue growth ran 71–93%, which is the empirical proof that fundamentals alone will not defend the price. Positioned this way, PLTR is less an investment in a business and more a leveraged position on the market's willingness to keep paying for certainty about 2030. That willingness is not a company-controlled variable.
