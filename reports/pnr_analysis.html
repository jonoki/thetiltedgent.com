01 Company Overview

Pentair makes the equipment that moves and cleans water: pumps and filtration for homes, farms and industry, water softeners and commercial water treatment, and the pumps, heaters, filters and automation that run a backyard swimming pool. It was founded in 1966 in Minnesota by five former Litton Industries engineers — the name is "penta" for the five founders — merged with Tyco's flow-control business in 2012, spun its electrical division off as nVent in 2018 (completed April 30), and is today an Irish-incorporated company run from London with its US base in Golden Valley, Minnesota. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to September 30, 2026 was $3.97B, with a 41.2% gross margin, a 19.6% GAAP operating margin and GAAP EPS of $3.77 (our sums of the company's quarterly releases).

For five years this was a quiet compounder: it closed September 2025 at $110.76, up from $40.63 at the September 2022 low. Then the pool business broke. On the evening of July 14, 2026 Pentair pre-announced second-quarter sales of about $930 million, down 17%, blamed a destock in the pool distribution channel worth roughly $170 million of sales and $105 million of segment income in the quarter, cut its full-year outlook from 2–4% sales growth to a 4–7% decline, and disclosed that its recently appointed CFO had left on July 10. The stock fell 15.0% the next day, to $64.33, and kept falling, to a September month-end close of $52.99 and an intraday low of $51.13 on October 8. Then came the third quarter. Before the open on October 27, 2026 Pentair reported adjusted EPS of $1.18, above its own $1.05–$1.08 guide and the Nasdaq consensus of $1.07, with Pool sales down 18% rather than the 23–25% it had guided, and said third-quarter Pool performance "reflected the completion of the inventory realignment with our major channel partners." The stock rose 12.8% that day and 3.3% the next, closing October 28 at $64.37: back to its level the day after the July warning, and still 42% below the September 2025 month-end. The debate has moved a step on. The destock is over by the company's account, so the question is whether Pool returns to growth in 2027 or settles at a lower base — the share-loss reading a securities lawsuit alleges — while Pentair absorbs the $1.4B Taco acquisition it closed on October 1.

The two numbers that actually matter

For a business sold through distributors, sell-in (what Pentair ships to the channel) and sell-through (what homeowners and pool builders actually buy) can diverge for a season or two. Q2 2026 was sell-in collapsing; Q3, by the company's account, was the end of it. Judge the recovery on whether sell-in climbs back to sell-through in the 2027 stocking season.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base & replacement demand Distributor & dealer relationships Brand in pool equipment Water-treatment product breadth High segment margins (25–29% ROS) Moderate leverage before Taco (D/E 0.38)

The moat is the installed base. A pool pump or heater, once plumbed in, tends to be replaced with the same brand by the same service technician, and water-treatment systems carry recurring filter and service demand. That is why Pentair's segment margins stayed between 23% and 30% through the two worst quarters of the destock. The vulnerability is that the moat protects end-demand, not channel orders: distributors decide how much inventory to hold, and when they decide to hold less, the manufacturer's reported sales fall far harder than anything the homeowner did. The open question the lawsuit raises is whether the moat itself — share at the dealer — eroded as well.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Channel concentration in Pool. The company itself attributed the Q2 miss to inventory realignment "with major channel partners". When a few large distributors reset stocking levels at once, a segment that was 38% of Q2 2025 sales can lose 42% of its revenue in a quarter.
02
Credibility and litigation. Securities class actions (filed in the District of Minnesota and the Southern District of New York) cover purchases from March 11, 2025 to July 14, 2026 and allege that the 80/20 programme damaged Pool customer relationships, cost market share and was masked by above-normal rebates. These are allegations, not findings — but the guidance cut arrived eleven weeks after the April 28 Q1 report, with a CFO exit in between.
03
Rate-sensitive end market. New pools, remodels and big-ticket upgrades are discretionary and financed; the company named higher interest rates and inflation as part of the worsening pool conditions. Replacement demand is stickier, but it can be deferred with repairs.
04
Deal risk and more debt. The $1.425B Taco purchase — about 14% of the $10.28B market value — closed on October 1, paid for largely with $1.4B of term loans. That roughly doubles the $1.48B of debt on the September 30 balance sheet, against $74.0M of cash, and integration begins a month before the new CFO starts. A new HVAC-adjacent platform adds execution load while Pool is still recovering.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the picture. First, the destock itself: ~$170M of Q2 sales and ~$105M of Pool segment income that management attributes to channel inventory rather than end-demand, plus a smaller Q3 effect, now described as complete. If that reading is right, 2026 earnings understate normalised power; if the lawsuit's reading is right, prior years overstated it. Second, GAAP vs adjusted: the FY2026 GAAP EPS guide of about $3.76 sits $0.94 below the adjusted ~$4.70, and Q3 results included about $8M of tariff refunds under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA), the law the 2025 tariffs were imposed under. The adjustments are doing real work this year, and the forward P/E above uses the adjusted consensus. Third, Taco: from October 1, deal costs, purchase accounting and $1.4B of new term loans enter the numbers; the company's forecast of intangible amortisation does not yet include the deal. Separately: the TTM EPS of $3.77 includes discontinued operations ($0.03 in Q4 2025 and $0.07 in Q1 2026); on continuing operations it is $3.67, and the P/E would be 17.5.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 28, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the October 2021 month-end close of $73.97 to the October 28, 2026 settled close of $64.37, PNR lost 13.0% of its price, while SPY rose 68.2% and QQQ 87.9% over the same dates (price only, Yahoo closes). Dividends ($0.80 a share in 2021 rising to $1.08 annualised) soften that but do not change the sign. The endpoints hide two completely different stocks. From the September 2022 low of $40.63 the shares rose 173% to $110.76 by September 2025 as margins expanded — operating margin went from 15.4% in FY2022 to 23.3% in FY2025 on StockAnalysis's basis (20.5% in FY2025 on GAAP operating income) on almost unchanged revenue. Then most of the gain went away: −52.2% from the September 2025 month-end to the September 2026 month-end of $52.99, before the Q3 print lifted October by 21.5% to the banner date. From the September 2025 month-end to the banner date the stock is −41.9%, and −38.2% since December 31, 2025.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 36.3 at the banner date, up from 14.8 at the September month-end — level with the May 2026 low — and back above the 30 "oversold" line for the first time since February: all seven month-ends from March to September 2026 read below 30. The stock closed lower in seven of the eight months from February to September 2026, the only exception a June bounce of 8.2% that July's pre-announcement erased; October to date is up 21.5%, on the print. The 10-month moving average sits near $76.31, 18.6% above the price: the longer trend still points down, and one month's rebound does not turn it. On daily closes the 14-day RSI is 80.2, and the price is 13.6% above its 50-day average ($56.68) and 15.9% below its 200-day average ($76.55) (our calculations from Yahoo daily closes). For context, the 2022 selloff drove the monthly RSI far lower still, and the stock went on to nearly triple — but that was investors paying a lower multiple of earnings as interest rates rose, for a business whose earnings were intact, which is what Pool now has to show.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 17.1x trailing GAAP earnings and about 13.7x the 2026 adjusted consensus for a company whose two non-pool segments grew segment income 15% and 16% in the third quarter, whose Pool destock the company now calls complete, and which yields 6.3% in trailing free cash flow and 1.68% in dividends. If end-demand for replacement pool equipment is intact, 2027 laps a destocked base and channel orders climb back toward sell-through, on top of Taco's ~$540M of revenue. Q3 beat the company's own adjusted EPS guide, the full-year adjusted midpoint held, a permanent CFO starts in November, and BNP Paribas Exane dropped its Underperform after the print.

Bear Case

Pool sales down 42% and then 18% in successive quarters, a full-year outlook cut in July and a CFO leaving months into the job are not the fingerprints of a routine channel adjustment. The class action alleges the 80/20 programme cost Pool share and that rebates pulled revenue forward — if even part of that is true, the pre-2026 margins were flattered and the "normal" earnings base is lower than the bulls assume. Debt roughly doubled on October 1 to pay for Taco, trailing GAAP EPS is still falling ($3.77, from $3.98 a quarter ago), and a two-day 16.6% rebound leaves the price 15.7% below its 10-month average: the longer trend has not turned.

Industry Commentary
Pool channel inventory reset
CONSTRAINT EASING

Pool equipment is sold through distributors who build inventory in the first half for the summer season. When they decide to carry less — because of higher financing costs, slower new-pool construction or simply a reset in how many weeks of stock they want on hand — the manufacturer sees the cut in full and at once, even if homeowners are buying the same number of replacement pumps. Pentair's own language pointed at exactly that: a more pronounced inventory realignment with major channel partners than it had estimated, and worsening conditions including higher interest rates and inflation. In October the company said third-quarter Pool performance reflected the completion of that realignment, and Pool sales fell 18%, less than the 23–25% decline it had guided. The 2027 season's stocking orders, placed in late 2026 and early 2027, are the first real test of how much of the fall was inventory and how much was demand or share.

80/20 simplification — margin engine or franchise risk
ONGOING HEADWIND

Pentair's operating margin rose from 15.4% in FY2022 to 23.3% in FY2025 on StockAnalysis's basis (20.5% in FY2025 on GAAP operating income), on revenue that barely moved ($4,122M to $4,176M). That is the output of a deliberate programme to concentrate on the most profitable products and customers — the 80/20 approach the lawsuit targets. Pruning low-margin customers and SKUs can be exactly right. It can also hand shelf space and dealer loyalty to a competitor, which in pool equipment means Hayward and others. The plaintiffs allege the latter happened and was obscured by rebates. 2026 cuts both ways: segment income in Flow and Water Solutions rose 27% and 17% in Q2 and 15% and 16% in Q3, while Pool's fell 62% and then 38%.

Water, cooling and the Taco bet
EMERGING TAILWIND

Management's answer to a stalled pool franchise is diversification into hydronic heating and cooling: Taco adds roughly $540M of projected 2026 revenue at about 10.5x 2026E EBITDA (including tax benefits), with the interim CFO describing it as a new growth engine into energy efficiency, comfort cooling, HVAC and data-centre build-outs (CFO Dive, July 28, 2026). The bull framing: a business with 23–30% segment margins in water treatment and flow buys into faster-growing building and data-centre water loops at a reasonable multiple, lowering pool from 38% of sales toward a smaller share. The bear framing: a management team that cut its own full-year sales outlook by six to eleven points in July committed $1.425B near the low point of its own share price. The way to adjudicate is two reported numbers: Pool core sales growth in the first half of 2027, and Taco's contribution at the margin management promised.
