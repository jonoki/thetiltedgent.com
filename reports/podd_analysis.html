01 Company Overview

Insulet makes the Omnipod — a small, tubeless, wearable insulin pump (the "Pod") that sticks to the skin, delivers insulin for a few days and is then thrown away and replaced. The flagship Omnipod 5 is an automated insulin delivery system that pairs with a continuous glucose monitor. Because every Pod is disposable, the model looks less like selling hardware and more like selling a consumable: with its FY2025 results the company reported more than 600,000 estimated active Omnipod users globally. FY2025 revenue was $2.71B, up 30.7%, with GAAP EPS of $3.48 and adjusted EPS of $4.97.

Here is the puzzle. Revenue grew 30.7% in 2025, Q3 2026 beat on both sales and earnings, and management now guides to 21–22% constant-currency growth for 2026 (constant currency strips out exchange-rate moves). Yet the stock closed August 2025 at $339.88 and November 6, 2026 at $146.51 — a 56.9% fall in about fourteen months, including six straight monthly declines from December 2025 through May 2026 and a 20.12% one-day drop on August 5, 2026. Three things went wrong, and none of them is this year's growth rate. First, quality: two voluntary medical device corrections in March and May 2026 for Pods with tubing tears that could cause insulin to leak or be under-delivered — the second covering roughly 8.5% of 2025 Pod production. Second, the type 2 story: the market had been paying for Omnipod's expansion from type 1 into the far larger type 2 diabetes population, and in August management cut its U.S. outlook because type 2 customers were not staying on therapy as long as expected. Third, new in November, competition: on the Q3 call management said it expects 2027 growth modestly below the mid-teens and expects to give up a couple of points of share of new starts as new rivals arrive. The stock rose 6.56% on results day and lost all of it, and more, over the next two sessions. A growth stock is a bet on the next five years; all three problems are about the next five years, not the last quarter.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Tubeless, disposable form factor 600,000+ installed users Recurring Pod consumables CGM integration (Abbott Libre 3 Plus) Omnipod 5 in 20 countries 72% gross margin #1 new starts, U.S. & Europe (2025, company claim)

The moat is habit plus manufacturing scale. A diabetic who has learned to live with a patch pump, whose endocrinologist prescribes it and whose insurer covers it, does not switch lightly — and every one of them reorders consumables for years. That repeat revenue is why the stock used to sell for a high price relative to its earnings. The vulnerability is that the same scale cuts both ways: when a manufacturing defect gets in, it gets into millions of units. The May 2026 correction covered roughly 7 million Pods. The next leg of growth — type 2 patients, who are often older and less used to intensive insulin management — quit faster in the first 90 days than the company assumed. And its lead is about to be tested: in November management said it expects the market to get more competitive as new products arrive and expects to give up a couple of points of share of new starts. Habit protects users who have formed it; it does nothing for users who quit before it forms, or for new patients who have more choices.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Manufacturing quality. March 12, 2026: a correction for certain Omnipod 5 Pod lots (about 1.5% of annual Omnipod 5 production) with a tear in the internal tubing; 18 serious incidents linked to high blood glucose were reported when it was announced, no deaths. The FDA later classed it a Class I recall, its most serious type, and its page counts 29 serious injuries and no deaths as of April 17. May 26, 2026: a wider correction, for a tear in the cannula just above the skin, across Omnipod 5, DASH and Eros Pods in the U.S. and affected international markets — about 7 million Pods, 8.5% of 2025 production, 24 serious adverse event reports including hospitalisation and DKA, no deaths. The company says it has identified the cause and implemented corrective actions. A third event would test that claim in public.
02
Type 2 retention. The largest untapped market is also the one that stays on therapy least well in the first 90 days, per management. The August guidance cut was small on the headline (total growth 20–22% from 21–23%) but it changed what the market believed the type 2 population is worth. In November the CFO reported a modest improvement in retention, with utilization stable. One quarter does not settle it.
03
Litigation. A securities class action, Hu v. Insulet Corporation, alleges false and misleading statements about the quality and safety of Omnipod products over a class period of February 21, 2025 through May 26, 2026 (Levi & Korsinsky notices). The lead plaintiff's amended complaint is due December 10, 2026, and the company has recorded no accrual (10-Q). These are the plaintiffs' allegations, not findings.
04
Cash conversion. Nine-month free cash flow of $211.7M was down 36% from $329.5M despite 25% revenue growth; trailing-twelve-month (TTM) free cash flow is 8.2% of revenue against an 18.4% operating margin. The CFO tied the weaker 2026 figure to higher capital spending and cash tied up by the corrections.
05
New competitors. On the November call the CEO said she expects the market to become more competitive as new products arrive, and expects to give up a couple of points of share of new starts. U.S. starts were already flat in Q3.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the headline numbers. First, FY2024 GAAP EPS of $5.78 was higher than adjusted EPS of $3.24 — a $2.54 gap, meaning non-recurring items inflated GAAP that year. That is why GAAP EPS then "fell" 39.8% to $3.48 in FY2025 while adjusted EPS rose 53.4% to $4.97 — do not read the FY2025 GAAP decline as a business decline. Second, the device-correction charges — a $38.5M net charge over nine months — depress GAAP margins in the first half and then flatter them in Q3, when a $7.0M cut to the estimate helped lift GAAP EPS ($2.03) above adjusted EPS ($1.99); the release's reconciliation shows the correction line adding $0.05 a share to GAAP EPS. Third, in her international remarks the CFO cited about $5M of reserve adjustments in France and Canada, a benefit of 2.5 percentage points in the quarter.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 23.9x trailing GAAP earnings and about 22.3x the 2026 adjusted consensus for a business that still grew revenue 19.2% in Q3 with a 74.2% adjusted gross margin, 23% ROIC, 600,000+ users buying disposables, the #1 position in new starts in the U.S. and Europe in 2025 (by the company's count), and an international outlook raised twice in a row. A year ago the same stock closed at $323.40, about 53x today's earnings. Fewer Pods have needed replacing than the company first estimated (10-Q), and management reported a modest improvement in type 2 retention in Q3. The final Omnipod 6 filing is in, with launch expected in 2027 and fully closed-loop for type 2 planned for 2028. The board's $1B buyback is about 10% of the company's market value, with $400M expected in Q4. The lowest published analyst target ($160) is above the price.

Bear Case

The high price of the past was paid for two things — clean execution and a huge type 2 market — and 2026 dented both. Two corrections within eleven weeks, one classed by the FDA as its most serious type of recall, plus a class action alleging misstatements about quality, is a record that regulators, prescribers and payers remember. U.S. growth slowed to 14.7% in Q3 from 20.1% in Q2, the Q4 range is 10–13.5%, U.S. new starts were flat, and management now expects to lose share of new starts to incoming rivals. Its preliminary 2027 view went from a mid-teens framework to modestly below it, with consensus 2027 revenue growth at 12.2%. Nine-month free cash flow fell 36% while revenue rose 25%, and about $275M for the year is only 2.7% of the market value. At 23.9x GAAP earnings this is no longer priced as a hyper-growth stock — but it is not priced as a broken one either, and investors could still pay less for a slowing medical-device maker with a quality question.

Top 3 Risks

01
Another quality event. Two voluntary corrections in 2026 (March 12, May 26), and the first classed by the FDA as Class I. A third would undermine the claim that the root cause is fixed and hand competitors a sales argument.
02
Type 2 economics and new rivals. If first-90-day quitting does not improve, the type 2 market is smaller and costlier to serve than the old share price assumed, and U.S. growth keeps sliding from the 10–13.5% Q4 range. New competitors make that harder to reverse.
03
Litigation and cash conversion. The securities class action is pending, and nine-month free cash flow fell to $211.7M from $329.5M. Either one widening would pressure a stock already priced for doubt, and the $400M Q4 buyback will use about two-thirds of the cash on hand.
Industry Commentary
Quality at scale — the consumable-device trade-off
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A disposable device business makes enormous numbers of identical units, so a single manufacturing defect is a population event. Insulet's March correction covered about 1.5% of annual Omnipod 5 production; the May one covered roughly 7 million Pods across three product generations, about 8.5% of 2025 production, of which 60% had already been used or had expired by the time it was announced. The company recorded a $38.5M net charge over nine months, expects about $50M in total and says the cause is identified and corrected. The financial hit is small against $3.19B of revenue; the reputational and legal hit is what the stock is pricing. The class action's February 2025 – May 2026 class period frames the allegation: that quality problems pre-dated disclosure.

Type 2 diabetes — the prize and the problem
ONGOING HEADWIND

Omnipod's growth story since its Omnipod 5 expansion to type 2 patients has been that a simple, tubeless automated system could reach insulin users who would never wear a traditional tubed pump. The August call put a hard edge on that: per CEO Ashley McEvoy on the Q2 2026 call, utilization and retention among type 2 customers were weaker than anticipated, and the first 90 days on therapy are critical — once type 2 users get past them, retention stabilises as it does in type 1. That makes the problem an onboarding problem, and the remedies are operational: more customer care, a change to sales-force compensation, tighter sample use, and the Omnipod Discover software, which the company says more than 35,000 users and 5,000 healthcare professionals had adopted by November 2. In November management reported the first modest improvement in retention, said type 2 patients were over 40% of U.S. starts, and said it has seen no effect on type 2 starts from wider GLP-1 use. The pipeline answer is fully closed-loop delivery for type 2, planned for 2028 — less user work, fewer reasons to quit.

The valuation reset — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

On $6.13 of TTM GAAP EPS, the August 2025 peak price implied about 55x; the November 6 close implies 23.9x, and 22.3x the $6.58 adjusted consensus for 2026. The bull framing: the price already allows for a slowdown to mid-teens growth and a quality scare, while the business still grew 19.2% in Q3 with 72% gross margins and a raised international outlook, and the company is buying back stock — even the lowest published target ($160) is above the price. The bear framing: 23.9x GAAP is still a growth-company price, and U.S. growth guided to 10–13.5% for Q4 with consensus 2027 revenue growth of 12.2% is not the business the peak price paid for. The tests are concrete: the Q4 results and formal 2027 guidance (estimated February 2027), whether U.S. new starts recover, how much share new rivals take, and whether a year passes without a third correction.
