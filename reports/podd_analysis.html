01 Company Overview

Insulet makes the Omnipod — a small, tubeless, wearable insulin pump (the "Pod") that sticks to the skin, delivers insulin for a few days and is then thrown away and replaced. The flagship Omnipod 5 is an automated insulin delivery system that pairs with a continuous glucose monitor. Because every Pod is disposable, the model looks less like selling hardware and more like selling a consumable: with its FY2025 results the company reported more than 600,000 estimated active Omnipod users globally. FY2025 revenue was $2.71B, up 30.7%, with GAAP EPS of $3.48 and adjusted EPS of $4.97.

Here is the puzzle. Revenue has compounded at 20–30% a year, Q2 2026 beat on both sales and earnings, and management still guides to 20–22% constant-currency growth for 2026. Yet the stock closed August 2025 at $339.88 and September 21, 2026 at $140.53 — a 58.7% fall in thirteen months, including six straight monthly declines from December 2025 through May 2026 and a 20.12% one-day drop on August 5, 2026. Two things broke, and neither was the growth rate. First, quality: two voluntary medical device corrections in March and May 2026 for Pods with tubing tears that could cause insulin to leak or be under-delivered — the second covering roughly 8.5% of 2025 Pod production. Second, the type 2 story: the market had been paying for Omnipod's expansion from type 1 into the far larger type 2 diabetes population, and in August management cut its U.S. outlook because type 2 customers were not staying on therapy as long as expected. A growth stock is a bet on the next five years; both problems hit the next five years, not the last quarter.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Tubeless, disposable form factor 600,000+ installed users Recurring Pod consumables CGM integration (Abbott Libre 3 Plus) Omnipod 5 in 20 countries 71% gross margin #1 new starts, U.S. & Europe (2025, company claim)

The moat is habit plus manufacturing scale. A diabetic who has learned to live with a patch pump, whose endocrinologist prescribes it and whose insurer covers it, does not switch lightly — and every one of them reorders consumables for years. That annuity is why the stock traded at a premium multiple. The vulnerability is that the same scale cuts both ways: when a manufacturing defect gets in, it gets into millions of units. The May 2026 correction covered roughly 7 million Pods. And the next leg of growth — type 2 patients, who are often older and less used to intensive insulin management — turns out to churn faster in the first 90 days than the model assumed. Habit protects users who have formed it; it does nothing for users who quit before it forms.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Manufacturing quality. March 12, 2026: a correction for certain Omnipod 5 Pod lots (about 1.5% of annual Omnipod 5 production) with a tear in the internal tubing; 18 serious incidents linked to high blood glucose reported, no deaths. May 26, 2026: a wider correction, for a tear in the cannula just above the skin, across Omnipod 5, DASH and Eros Pods in the U.S. and affected international markets — about 7 million Pods, 8.5% of 2025 production, 24 serious adverse event reports including hospitalisation and DKA, no deaths. The company says it has identified the cause and implemented corrective actions. A third event would test that claim in public.
02
Type 2 retention. The largest untapped market is also the one that stays on therapy least well in the first 90 days, per management. The August guidance cut was small on the headline (total growth 20–22% from 21–23%) but it changed what the market believed about the addressable population's economics.
03
Litigation. A securities class action alleges misstatements about manufacturing quality controls over a class period of February 21, 2025 through May 26, 2026 (Levi & Korsinsky notices, July–August 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline August 31, 2026). These are the plaintiffs' allegations, not findings. Outcome and cost cannot be estimated from sources fetched.
04
Cash conversion. H1 2026 free cash flow of $145.4M was down 37% from $229.4M despite 28% revenue growth; TTM FCF margin is 9.6% against a 16.9% operating margin. Warranty costs explain part of it; the rest is not broken out in the sources fetched.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the headline numbers. First, FY2024 GAAP EPS of $5.78 was higher than adjusted EPS of $3.24 — a $2.54 gap, meaning non-recurring items inflated GAAP that year (the item is not identified in sources fetched for this report). Second, the device-correction warranty charges — $33.6M estimated for H1 2026, $25.0M of it in Q2 — depress GAAP margins and are excluded from adjusted figures. Third, the Q2 2026 GAAP net income of $95.0M vs $22.5M a year earlier reflects a weak comparison base; the adjusted figures ($115.0M, +37.4%) are the cleaner growth read.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 26.5x trailing and about 21.6x forward earnings for a business still growing revenue 20%+ with a 71% gross margin, 21% ROIC, 600,000+ users buying disposables, the #1 position in new starts in the U.S. and Europe (by the company's count), and an international line that just had its outlook raised. A year ago the same stock traded near 64x today's earnings. Both 2026 blows are the fixable kind: the corrections had identified causes and no deaths, and type 2 retention is an onboarding problem the company is attacking with care staff, incentives and software — before Omnipod 6 and fully closed-loop type 2 technology arrive. Management's preliminary 2027 view assumes none of that works. The lowest published analyst target ($144) is above the price.

Bear Case

The premium multiple was paid for two things — clean execution and a huge type 2 market — and 2026 dented both. Two corrections within eleven weeks, the second covering 8.5% of a year's production, plus a class action alleging misstatements about quality controls, is a quality record that regulators, prescribers and payers remember. U.S. growth is decelerating from 20.1% in Q2 to a 14–16% Q3 guide, consensus has 2027 revenue growth at 13.9%, and half-year free cash flow fell 37% while revenue rose 28%. At 26.5x GAAP earnings this is no longer priced as a hyper-growth stock — but it is not priced as a broken one either, and a decelerating medtech with a quality question can de-rate further.

Top 3 Risks

01
Another quality event. Two voluntary corrections in 2026 (March 12, May 26) with 42 combined serious adverse-event reports. A third would undermine the claim that the root cause is fixed and hand competitors a sales argument.
02
Type 2 economics. If first-90-day churn does not improve, the type 2 market is smaller and costlier to serve than the premium multiple assumed, and U.S. growth keeps sliding from the 14–16% Q3 guide.
03
Litigation and cash conversion. The securities class action has no estimable cost in sources fetched, and H1 2026 free cash flow fell to $145.4M from $229.4M. Either one widening would pressure a stock already priced for doubt.
Industry Commentary
Quality at scale — the consumable-device trade-off
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A disposable device business makes enormous numbers of identical units, so a single manufacturing defect is a population event. Insulet's March correction covered about 1.5% of annual Omnipod 5 production; the May one covered roughly 7 million Pods across three product generations, about 8.5% of 2025 production, of which 60% had already been used or had expired by the time it was announced. The company estimates $33.6M of warranty cost for H1 2026 and says the cause is identified and corrected. The financial hit is small against $3.05B of revenue; the reputational and legal hit is what the stock is pricing. The class action's February 2025 – May 2026 class period frames the allegation: that quality problems pre-dated disclosure.

Type 2 diabetes — the prize and the problem
ONGOING HEADWIND

Omnipod's growth story since its Omnipod 5 expansion to type 2 patients has been that a simple, tubeless automated system could reach insulin users who would never wear a traditional tubed pump. The August call put a hard edge on that: per CEO Ashley McEvoy, as reported in Investing.com's call transcript, utilization and retention among type 2 customers were weaker than anticipated, and the first 90 days on therapy are critical — once type 2 users get past them, retention stabilises as it does in type 1. That makes the problem an onboarding problem, and the remedies are operational: more customer care, a change to sales-force compensation, tighter sample use, and the Omnipod Discover software. The pipeline answer is the EVOLUTION 3 feasibility study of fully closed-loop delivery for type 2 — less user work, fewer reasons to quit.

The valuation reset — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

On $5.30 of TTM GAAP EPS, the August 2025 peak price implied about 64x; the September 21 close implies 26.5x, and 21.6x the $6.50 consensus for 2026. The bull framing: the multiple has already absorbed a slowdown to mid-teens growth and a quality scare, while the business still grows 20%+ with 71% gross margins and a raised international outlook — even the lowest published target ($144) is above the price. The bear framing: 26.5x GAAP is still a growth multiple, and U.S. growth guided to 14–16% for Q3 with consensus 2027 revenue growth of 13.9% is not the business the peak price paid for. The adjudicators are concrete and dated: the Q3 print (estimated November 5, 2026), whether U.S. growth stabilises, and whether a year passes without a third correction.
