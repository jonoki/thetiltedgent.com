01 Company Overview

PPG began in 1883 as the Pittsburgh Plate Glass Company and renamed itself PPG Industries in December 1968 to reflect a business that had long since outgrown glass. Today it is a pure paints-and-coatings company: the architectural paint on walls in Latin America and Europe, the sealants and transparencies on aircraft, the refinish paint in body shops, the coatings inside beverage cans and on car bodies coming off the assembly line. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 30, 2026 was $16.69B, with diluted EPS of $7.01. At a $27.59B market value it ranks #343 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation.

The story of the last five years is a stock that went nowhere while the company kept paying. PPG closed November 2021 at $154.17 and peaked at a $172.44 month-end in December 2021; it closed on November 2, 2026 at $124.89 — 19.0% below that starting point and 27.6% below the peak, after a 19.0% rise since the previous edition's September 21 close. Through all of it the dividend kept rising: StockAnalysis counts 55 consecutive years of dividend growth, and the current $2.96 annual rate yields 2.37%. The portfolio was reshaped underneath: the US and Canada architectural paint business (Glidden, Pittsburgh Paints, 750 company-owned stores) was sold to American Industrial Partners for $550M, closing December 2, 2024. What is left is a mix where aerospace, packaging and automotive coatings do the heavy lifting. The central debate was a pricing-power question: in Q2 2026 selling prices covered only about 90% of cost inflation and adjusted EPS (profit per share before one-off items) was essentially flat ($2.23 against $2.22). The third quarter answered much of it. On October 27, 2026 PPG reported organic sales (growth excluding currency and acquisitions) up 5% and a record third-quarter adjusted EPS of $2.25, up 6%. It said its pricing and other commercial actions fully offset cost inflation, and it raised the midpoint of its full-year adjusted EPS guidance within a narrower $7.90–$8.10 range. The margin problem has moved rather than gone: in Industrial Coatings, where many customers' prices follow a raw-material index with a delay, segment profit fell 14%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Aerospace qualification lock-in Formulas specified into car & can production Global manufacturing footprint Latin American paint brands (Comex) 55 years of dividend growth Gross margin 41.2%

Coatings are a small share of what a customer spends on the finished product and a large share of what can go wrong with it. A can lining that fails, an aircraft sealant that cracks, a car finish that peels — each costs the customer far more than the paint. That asymmetry is the moat: once a formulation is qualified onto an airframe or specified onto an assembly line, switching carries requalification cost and risk that no purchasing manager wants to own. It shows in a 41.2% gross margin on what is, chemically, a commodity input business. The vulnerability is that the moat protects volume more than it protects price — index-linked industrial contracts pass raw-material swings through with a lag, and Industrial Coatings' Q3 2026 margin squeeze is that lag in action.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Price lags cost in Industrial Coatings. Company-wide pricing fully offset cost inflation in Q3 2026 (about 90% in Q2), but Industrial's index-based contracts reset after the fact: segment profit fell 14% and the EBITDA margin dropped to 14.4% from 17.0%. The company guides Industrial margin lower again in Q4 on the same timing.
02
Refinish has stopped falling, not proven it will recover. Automotive refinish organic sales rose by a mid-single-digit percentage in Q3 2026 after declines earlier in the year, and the Q4 outlook cites stabilisation. Automotive OEM coatings (paint on new cars) fell by a low single-digit percentage organically in the quarter. A relapse would weigh on Performance Coatings, whose Q4 margin outlook leans on pricing and refinish stabilisation.
03
Price is carrying much of the growth. Of Q3's 7% sales growth, 5 points were organic (price +3, volume +2), 1 was currency and 1 came from acquisitions. Volume growth is the smaller part of the organic gain, and a stronger dollar would take away the currency help.
04
Leverage is real, if manageable. Debt-to-equity is 0.87 ($7.47B total debt on $8.63B of total equity, StockAnalysis), against $1.64B of cash and short-term investments. The 10-Q shows $686M of short-term and $6,155M of long-term debt at September 30, 2026. Not a stress point — StockAnalysis puts its Altman Z-score, a bankruptcy-risk gauge where higher is safer, at 3.75 — but it limits how aggressively buybacks can substitute for organic earnings growth.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the per-share history. First, the Q4 2024 loss: PPG reported a net loss of $(280)M, EPS $(1.21), in the quarter in which the US and Canada architectural sale to American Industrial Partners closed (December 2, 2024). That quarter has left the trailing window, but it still makes FY2025 EPS of $6.94 look “up 46.2%”, which is not a measure of underlying growth. Second, the adjusted-to-reported gap in Q3 2026 was $0.13 per share: acquisition-related amortization ($0.09), business restructuring ($0.05) and a resolved tax matter ($0.01), less a portfolio-optimization gain ($0.01) and an insurance recovery ($0.01). Restructuring is recurring enough at PPG to deserve scepticism as “one-off”. Third, discontinued operations (the sold US and Canada architectural business) lost $22M, or $0.10 per share, in Q3 2026, so total diluted EPS was $2.02 against $2.12 from continuing operations. Buybacks were modest in 2026 — about $150M in Q3 and about $320M year to date, against $790M in FY2025.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 17.8x trailing and about 15.6x consensus 2026 earnings for a company that has grown organic sales for several quarters running, is outgrowing its industry by 3 percentage points by management's count, is taking share in packaging and automotive paint, has an aerospace business growing double digits, and has raised its dividend for 55 consecutive years. The pricing squeeze that held Q2 earnings flat closed in Q3: management says commercial actions fully offset cost inflation, record third-quarter adjusted EPS was $2.25 and the full-year guidance midpoint went up. Average analyst target is $130.14, 4.2% above the price. If Industrial Coatings' index-linked prices catch up with costs and refinish keeps stabilising, margins have room to rebuild.

Bear Case

The bear case is that five years of this is a pattern, not a patch. Revenue is still below 2021, gross margin has edged down since FY2024 (41.91% in FY2024, 41.63% in FY2025, 41.18% trailing), and Industrial Coatings, the largest segment, just posted a 14% fall in profit with margin guided lower again in Q4. Winning volume by conceding price is not the same thing as having a moat. Automotive OEM coatings fell in Q3, refinish has only stabilised, and about three of the five points of organic growth came from price. The stock is up 19.0% since the previous edition, so a good deal of the recovery is in the price; the lowest analyst target ($115) is now below it, and the shares have not posted a month-end close above $125 since September 2024.

Industry Commentary
Raw-material inflation and the index-pricing lag
NARROWING TO ONE SEGMENT

Coatings makers buy resins, pigments, solvents and packaging, and sell to customers who often have contracts tied to raw-material indices. In a rising-cost environment that structure guarantees a lag. PPG announced global price increases in its Q1 2026 release; by Q2 pricing covered about 90% of cost inflation, and in Q3 the company said its commercial actions fully offset it. The lag now shows in one place: Industrial Coatings, where the EBITDA margin fell to 14.4% from 17.0% and Q4 guidance calls for more compression. The last time inflation outran pricing, in 2022, gross margin fell to 36.38% and EPS dropped 28%; this time gross margin is 41.18% TTM.

Automotive: refinish stabilising, OEM softer
MIXED

Refinish demand follows collision repair activity at body shops. After lower refinish volumes in the first half of 2026, PPG reported mid-single-digit organic growth in Q3 on higher prices and volumes, and its Q4 outlook cites refinish stabilisation. Automotive OEM coatings (paint on new cars) fell by a low single-digit percentage organically, though the company says share gains let its volumes outpace global automotive production. PPG attributes Performance Coatings' EBITDA margin recovery, to 23.9% from 21.7%, to higher selling prices and sales volumes.

Aerospace and packaging — the growth engines
TAILWIND

Two businesses are doing what the whole company needs to do. Aerospace grew organic sales by a double-digit percentage in Q3 2026 with an order backlog near $300 million, and packaging coatings also grew double digits, with volumes up more than 20% over two years combined on share gains. The bull framing: these are specification-driven, hard-to-displace businesses, and their growth keeps compounding while cyclical automotive and index-priced industrial work recover. The bear framing: together they are not large enough to offset margin leakage in Industrial Coatings. The test is whether adjusted EPS growth holds once Industrial's pricing catches up.
