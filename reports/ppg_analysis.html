01 Company Overview

PPG began in 1883 as the Pittsburgh Plate Glass Company and renamed itself PPG Industries in December 1968 to reflect a business that had long since outgrown glass. Today it is a pure paints-and-coatings company: the architectural paint on walls in Latin America and Europe, the sealants and transparencies on aircraft, the refinish paint in body shops, the coatings inside beverage cans and on car bodies coming off the assembly line. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 30, 2026 was $16.42B, up 4.99%, with diluted EPS of $6.99. At a $23.33B market value it ranks #1033 globally by market capitalisation.

The story of the last five years is a stock that went nowhere while the company kept paying. PPG closed September 2021 at $143.01 and peaked at a $172.44 month-end in December 2021; it closed on September 21, 2026 at $104.93 — 26.6% below its starting point and 39.1% below that peak. Through all of it the dividend kept rising: StockAnalysis counts 55 consecutive years of dividend growth, and the current $2.96 annual rate yields 2.82%. The portfolio was reshaped underneath: the US and Canada architectural paint business (Glidden, Pittsburgh Paints, 750 company-owned stores) was sold to American Industrial Partners for $550M, closing December 2, 2024. What is left is a mix where aerospace, packaging and automotive OEM coatings do the heavy lifting. The central debate now is a pricing-power question: in Q2 2026 PPG grew organic sales 4% and outgrew its industry by 300 basis points by management's account — yet adjusted EPS was flat, because selling prices covered only about 90% of cost inflation and automotive refinish slumped. Volume share is being won; margin is not yet following it.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Aerospace qualification lock-in Spec'd-in OEM & packaging formulations Global manufacturing footprint Latin American paint brands (Comex) 55 years of dividend growth Gross margin 41.2%

Coatings are a small share of what a customer spends on the finished product and a large share of what can go wrong with it. A can lining that fails, an aircraft sealant that cracks, a car finish that peels — each costs the customer far more than the paint. That asymmetry is the moat: once a formulation is qualified onto an airframe or specified onto an assembly line, switching carries requalification cost and risk that no purchasing manager wants to own. It shows in a 41.2% gross margin on what is, chemically, a commodity input business. The vulnerability is that the moat protects volume more than it protects price — index-linked industrial contracts pass raw-material swings through with a lag, and the Q2 2026 margin squeeze is that lag in action.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Price lags cost. Pricing covered ~90% of cost inflation in Q2 2026. Index-based contracts in Industrial Coatings reset after the fact, so a rising raw-material, energy or logistics bill hits margin first and is recovered later — if it is recovered. Q3 guidance calls for EBITDA margin flat to down 100 bps YoY.
02
Refinish is the high-margin hole. Automotive refinish volumes fell in both Q1 and Q2 2026, and management said recovery is slower than anticipated. Because refinish carries rich margins, a double-digit organic decline there took 300 bps off the Performance segment's EBITDA margin in one quarter.
03
Growth leans on currency and acquisitions. Of Q2's 7% sales growth, 4 points were organic and 1 point came from acquisitions; the remainder was currency. In Q1 2026, organic growth was 1% and foreign currency contributed 6%. A stronger dollar would reverse that tailwind.
04
Leverage is real, if manageable. Debt-to-equity is 0.87 with $7.47B of total debt against $1.61B of cash (StockAnalysis). Not a stress point at a 3.72 Altman Z-score, but it limits how aggressively buybacks can substitute for organic earnings growth.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items distort the per-share history. First, the Q4 2024 loss: PPG reported a net loss of $(280)M, EPS $(1.21), in the quarter in which the US and Canada architectural sale to American Industrial Partners closed (December 2, 2024). That quarter drops out of the trailing window, which is why TTM EPS is "up 59.8%" and FY2025 EPS of $6.94 is "up 46.2%" — neither is a measure of underlying growth. Adjusted EPS, which the company guides on, is roughly flat to up mid-single digits. Second, the adjusted-to-reported gap in Q2 2026 was $0.27 per share: acquisition-related amortization ($0.09), legacy environmental remediation ($0.08), business restructuring ($0.04), a legal settlement ($0.04) and portfolio optimization ($0.02). Environmental remediation and restructuring are recurring enough at PPG to deserve scepticism as "one-off". Buybacks were modest in 2026 — $75M in Q2 and $175M year to date, against $790M in FY2025.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 15.0x trailing and about 13.3x consensus 2026 earnings for a company that has grown organic sales for six straight quarters, is outgrowing its industry by 300 bps by management's count, is taking share in packaging and automotive OEM, has an aerospace business growing double digits, and has raised its dividend for 55 consecutive years. The Q2 margin squeeze is a timing problem — index-linked prices reset after costs rise — and management has put a date on full recovery (Q4 2026). Every one of 24 analyst targets sits above the current price, from +9.6% to +31.5%. If price-cost coverage reaches 100% and refinish finds a floor, the stock is trading under all its moving averages for a problem that fixes itself.

Bear Case

The bear case is that five years of this is a pattern, not a patch. Revenue is still below 2021, gross margin has slipped three years running, and 2026 growth leans on currency and acquisitions — Q1's organic growth was 1%. Winning volume by conceding price is not the same thing as having a moat. The headline EPS growth is a base effect from the Q4 2024 loss; adjusted EPS is flat year over year in Q2. Refinish, the high-margin business, is shrinking and management says recovery is slower than anticipated; leverage at 0.87x debt-to-equity limits the buyback lever, and a stock that has not posted a month-end close above $125 since September 2024 is telling you what the market thinks the earnings are worth.

Top 3 Risks

01
Price-cost gap persists. Pricing covered ~90% of cost inflation in Q2 2026 and Q3 margin is guided flat to down 100 bps. If the promised 100% coverage by Q4 slips, the $7.70–$8.10 EPS guide is the next thing to move.
02
Automotive refinish decline deepens. Double-digit organic declines in Q2 2026 in a high-margin business already cost Performance Coatings 300 bps of EBITDA margin. Management says recovery is slower than anticipated.
03
Currency reversal. Foreign currency contributed about 6 points of Q1 2026 sales growth and a large part of Q2's. With the architectural business now outside the US and Canada, a stronger dollar would flip the reported growth rate quickly.
Industry Commentary
Raw-material inflation and the index-pricing lag
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Coatings makers buy resins, pigments, solvents and packaging, and sell to customers who often have contracts tied to raw-material indices. In a rising-cost environment that structure guarantees a lag. PPG's Q1 2026 release announced global price increases against rising costs for raw materials, energy, logistics and packaging; by Q2 pricing covered about 90% of cost inflation. The last time this happened, in 2022, gross margin fell to 36.38% and EPS dropped 28%. This time the damage is smaller — gross margin is 41.20% TTM — but the direction is the same, and Q3 guidance does not yet show it turning.

Automotive refinish softness
ONGOING HEADWIND

Refinish demand follows collision repair activity at body shops. PPG reported lower refinish volumes in both Q1 and Q2 2026, with the Q2 decline described as double-digit organic and recovery slower than anticipated. The underlying causes of weaker collision-repair demand were not sourced this session. Because refinish is among PPG's richest-margin businesses, even a modest share of revenue can swing segment profitability, as the 300 bp Performance Coatings margin decline showed.

Aerospace and packaging — the growth engines
EMERGING TAILWIND

Two businesses are doing what the whole company needs to do. Aerospace grew double digits in both 2026 quarters and carried an order backlog into Q2; packaging coatings grew double digits in Q2 on share gains. The bull framing: these are specification-driven, hard-to-displace businesses, and their growth compounds while cyclical refinish and index-priced industrial work recover. The bear framing: together they are not large enough to offset margin leakage in the rest of the portfolio — which is exactly what the flat Q2 adjusted EPS shows. The test is whether adjusted EPS growth reappears once price-cost coverage reaches 100%.
