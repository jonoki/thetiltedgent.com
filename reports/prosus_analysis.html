01 Company Overview

Prosus is the Amsterdam-listed international arm of South Africa's Naspers, and it is two things at once. It is the largest outside shareholder in Tencent — 22.66% of the Chinese messaging-and-games giant at March 31, 2026, or 2,045.8 million Tencent shares after the sales disclosed up to September 25, 2026 — and it is an operator of consumer internet businesses in three regions: iFood (food delivery and payments in Brazil), Despegar (travel), OLX (classifieds), Just Eat Takeaway.com (JET, food delivery in Europe), eMAG (online retail in Romania), iyzico (payments in Turkey) and PayU (payments in India), plus minority stakes such as 22.31% of India's Swiggy. In the year to March 31, 2026 (FY26) those operating businesses produced revenue of $9.71B, up 57% (12% in local currency once acquisitions are stripped out) and ecosystem aEBITDA of $1.27B, up 84%.

The share price, though, is set by the Tencent stake and by how much the market discounts it. At the October 2 close, Prosus's Tencent shares were worth about €97.8B (2,045.8M × HK$421.20, converted at the ECB rate), against a Prosus market value of €72.2B. Investors are paying 74 cents for each euro of Tencent and getting iFood, OLX, JET and the rest — and $6.4B of net debt — for less than nothing. Closing that gap is the point of the open-ended buyback running since June 2022: Prosus sells a sliver of Tencent at full value and buys back its own shares at the discount, so each remaining Prosus share carries more Tencent. Management says it will spend $5B on it in FY27. It has not closed the gap. By the board's own account the discount stayed "broadly unchanged at around 43%" through FY26 even as $10B went into buybacks (AGM minutes, August 26, 2026). The shares fell 43.6% in the twelve months to October 2 (from €61.14), worse than Tencent's 37.7% fall in Hong Kong over the same dates, and the October 2 close of €34.50 was the lowest in 52 weeks. Company news added to the Tencent move: a subsidy war in Brazilian food delivery led management to guide iFood's FY27 aEBITDA to $100–150M from $400M (the shares fell 6.33% the day that letter came out), and JET's turnaround needs another year of investment.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

22.66% of Tencent iFood scale in Brazil OLX classifieds network effects Payments rails: iFood Pago, PayU, iyzico $5B/yr buyback capacity Investment-grade balance sheet

Prosus's strongest advantage is not one of its apps; it is access to cheap capital from Tencent. A 22.66% stake that pays more than $1B a year in dividends lets Prosus outspend almost any rival in its markets, which is exactly what it is doing in Brazil. Among the operating businesses the clearest moat is OLX: classifieds are winner-takes-most, because buyers go where the listings are and sellers go where the buyers are, which is why the business earns a 48% aEBITDA margin. iFood has scale, market leadership and a loyalty programme, Clube, that carried 45% of food-delivery volume by March 2026 — but scale in delivery is defended with money, and two large Chinese groups have come to test it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A discount that the buyback has not closed. Prosus trades at 0.74x its Tencent stake alone. Each year the buyback converts some of the discount into per-share Tencent exposure, but the discount has not gone away: Prosus says the combined Naspers–Prosus discount narrowed by 16 percentage points from June 2022 to March 2026, yet the board put it at around 43% at the end of FY26, broadly unchanged on the year. The shares fell further than Tencent over the last twelve months (−43.6% in euros vs −37.7% in Hong Kong dollars). Holding-company discounts are partly about governance, and ahead of the August 26, 2026 AGMs large investors objected to the dual-class structure and to Bloisi's pay, including a $100M "moonshot" award (Bloomberg); all resolutions still passed.
02
A delivery war in Brazil with no end date. iFood earned $400M of aEBITDA in FY26; the company guides $100–150M for FY27 because it will match rivals' spending with its own. Management's case is that the subsidies are "irrational" and temporary. If Meituan and DiDi treat Brazil as a long-term bet, the lower profit level becomes the normal one.
03
One stock drives the value. At the October 2 close the Tencent stake was worth 136% of Prosus's market cap. A 10% move in Tencent moves the stake by about €9.8B ($11.0B), more than the $8.3B analyst-consensus value for OLX on Prosus's NAV page. Tencent is a Chinese company: regulation, US–China relations and its own AI spending all pass straight through.
04
A big European turnaround bought at full price. JET cost €4.2B, its orders were still falling 7% year on year in the May letter, and Prosus expects to report measurable progress within 12 to 18 months. Uber's offer for Delivery Hero would make Uber a much larger force in European delivery, where JET competes.

Notable One-Off Items

FY26 profit attributable to shareholders was $11,638M on revenue of $9,705M — a holding company can earn more than its revenue because the profit comes from Tencent. Three items distort it. First, $4,858M of gains on partial disposals of equity-accounted investments ($6,447M in FY25), of which the Tencent sales account for $4.7B; the lower figure is why IFRS earnings from continuing operations fell 7% while core earnings rose. Second, $372M of other finance costs, mainly unrealised currency losses on euro bonds translated into dollars. Third, $477M of other losses, most of it in the "Other" segment. Core headline earnings strip these out and rose 13% to $8,340M; the buyback turned that into 24% growth per share (378 US cents vs 306).

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end of €31.65 (split-adjusted) to the October 2, 2026 close of €34.50, Prosus gained 9.0% on price, while the AEX rose 44.7% over the same dates. Dividends were small (the FY26 distribution is 28 euro cents) and do not change the picture. The five years had two halves. The first ended at a €20.21 month-end in October 2022, the low of the window, followed by a 41.9% rebound in November 2022, the largest monthly gain in the series. The second was a two-year climb to €59.94 in September 2025, when the monthly RSI hit 92.3, the most overbought reading in the series. Since then the stock has given back 42.4%.

The 6-period monthly RSI now reads 25.4, oversold and the lowest since April 2022 (23.2). The price sits below its 3-month average of €36.12 and well below its 10-month average of €39.73: the downtrend that began in late 2025 has not turned.

Against the asset it mostly owns, the gap is narrower but still the wrong way: over the twelve months to October 2, Prosus fell 43.6% in euros (€61.14 → €34.50) while Tencent fell 37.7% in Hong Kong dollars (HK$676.50 → HK$421.20), or about 35.6% in euros at Yahoo's EUR/HKD closes (9.1294 → 8.8245). Currency therefore widens the gap rather than explaining it: in the same currency Prosus underperformed its main asset by about eight percentage points. A holding company whose buyback is designed to grow Tencent exposure per share should, all else equal, outperform Tencent over time. Over this year it did not.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You can buy €46.77 of Tencent for €34.50 and get iFood, OLX, JET, eMAG, PayU, iyzico, Despegar and the Indian stakes for less than nothing. Management is buying $5B of its own shares a year at that discount, which raised core headline EPS 24% in FY26 on 13% underlying growth. The operating businesses now fund themselves ($275M of free cash flow excluding Tencent), OLX earns a 48% margin, iFood's fintech is scaling, and the lowest analyst target on the consensus page, €39, is above the price. If the Brazilian subsidy war fades — management calls rivals' losses of about $8 an order unsustainable — iFood's profit returns to FY26 levels and the discount has fewer reasons to persist.

Bear Case

Years of buybacks have not closed the discount — the board itself put it at around 43% at the end of FY26, broadly unchanged — and the shares fell further than Tencent this year. FY27 is a year of lower profit by management's own guidance — iFood down to $100–150M of aEBITDA, JET still in investment — and the group's free cash flow still depends on one Chinese dividend. The governance that investors complain about is not changing: Naspers controls the vote, and the August AGMs drew opposition from large Dutch, Norwegian and US funds while every resolution still passed. Meanwhile Meituan and DiDi are funding Brazil from far larger profit pools, and Uber is buying Delivery Hero next to JET. A 0.74x price on Tencent may be what a controlled holding company with a cash-burning food-delivery fight is worth.

Top 3 Risks

01
Tencent's share price. The stake is worth 136% of Prosus's market cap. Tencent fell 37.7% over the past year and is raising AI spending (AI-related capex of RMB79.2B in 2025). Every move in Hong Kong passes through to Amsterdam with leverage from the net debt.
02
The cost of defending iFood. Guidance already assumes iFood's aEBITDA falls by roughly two-thirds in FY27, and iFood is spending R$24B in the year to March 2027. If the competition does not retreat, that level of spending becomes permanent and Prosus's best cash generator outside Tencent shrinks.
03
A permanent holding-company discount. A controlled, dual-class structure, a large pay award under dispute and a stream of acquisitions (JET, Despegar, La Centrale, $8.0B deployed in FY26) give investors reasons to keep valuing the whole below the parts, however much stock is bought back.
Industry Commentary
Brazil's food-delivery war
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

iFood is Brazil's food-delivery leader; Prosus says it "expanded its market leadership" in FY26. Two Chinese groups are now competing for that market: Meituan with Keeta and DiDi with 99Food, both offering discounts that iFood's CEO put at around 50%, aimed at low-income neighbourhoods and small restaurants. iFood's position, laid out on the June 29 call, is that its commission and logistics margin are unchanged and ad revenue is still growing — only the cost of winning each order has risen — and that the subsidies will end. It is not waiting: iFood will spend R$24B in Brazil in its current fiscal year, 41% more than last year, and the profit guide reflects it. The outcome decides whether iFood is a $400M-a-year business or a $100M-a-year business for the next few years.

Consolidation in European delivery
ONGOING HEADWIND

European food delivery is consolidating into a few large groups. Prosus bought JET for €4.2B; Uber holds 24.99% of Delivery Hero with a further 11.8% through instruments and has offered €41.50 a share for the rest, a 151% premium to Delivery Hero's one-month average price before Prosus's first sale to Uber (Prosus, July 16). For Prosus the deal is a clean exit from a regulatory obligation and roughly €2.1B of cash. For JET it means facing a better-funded competitor in some markets just as it tries to return to growth after four years of falling orders.

The holding-company discount and the buyback
THE CORE DEBATE

Prosus describes its buyback as the largest of any tech company as a share of shares outstanding; it has cut Prosus's free-float share count by 32% since June 2022. The mechanics are simple: sell Tencent at full value, buy Prosus at a discount, and each remaining share owns more Tencent. That raised NAV per share 16 percentage points more than it would have risen otherwise, by Prosus's count. The bull reading: a discount that persists while the company keeps buying is a gift to the holders who stay, and the buyback is now funded partly by non-core sales (about $2B in FY26) rather than only Tencent. The bear reading: the discount reflects control, governance and capital-allocation risk that the buyback does not address, and the money spent defending iFood and rebuilding JET is money not spent on buybacks. The test is simple and public: price ÷ Tencent stake, 0.74x at October 2. If it does not rise as the buyback continues, the market is telling management the problem is not the share count.
