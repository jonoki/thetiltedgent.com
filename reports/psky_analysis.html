01 Company Overview

Paramount Skydance owns the CBS Television Network, a stack of cable channels — Nickelodeon, MTV, Comedy Central, BET, CMT among them — free-to-air networks abroad (Network 10, Channel 5, Telefe, Chilevisión), the Paramount Pictures studio and the Paramount+ streaming service, which ended Q2 2026 with 81.6 million subscribers. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $29.11B, up just 1.2%, and on a GAAP basis the company lost $621M over those four quarters (EPS −$0.58). At the September 21 close the whole equity was worth about $11.1B — less than half a year's revenue.

The company is barely a year old. On August 7, 2025, David Ellison's Skydance Media merged with Paramount Global, and the old PARA (Class B) and PARAA (Class A) shares stopped trading in favour of a single listed Class B line, PSKY, on Nasdaq. The terms: each old Class B share could elect $15.00 in cash or one new PSKY Class B share, with the cash elections capped at $4.3B in aggregate and prorated beyond that; each old Class A (voting) share could elect $23.00 in cash or 1.5333 PSKY Class B shares. Because the Class B conversion was one-for-one, the PARA price history runs straight into PSKY with no ratio adjustment — which is how the chart in Section 07 is built.

Then the little fish went after the whale. On February 27, 2026, Paramount agreed to buy Warner Bros. Discovery — Warner Bros. studios, HBO, CNN and the Discovery networks — for $31.00 per share in cash, an $81B equity value and $110B enterprise value, after outbidding a Netflix deal the WBD board had already signed. At the September 21 closes, WBD's equity was worth roughly seven times Paramount's own. The report that follows is really about one bet: whether a company worth $11B can digest one worth $77B, carrying the debt that requires, and come out with shares worth more than the $9.91 they fetch today.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Film & TV library CBS broadcast network Franchise IP (Star Trek, Top Gun, Mission: Impossible) Multi-year affiliate contracts Controlling shareholder with deep pockets Scale if WBD closes

A media company's moat is what it owns, not what it makes this year. Paramount owns a century of film, a broadcast network with live sports, and franchises people will pay to see again. Add Warner Bros. and HBO and the library becomes one of the deepest in the business — which is Ellison's argument that the company needs that scale to compete with Netflix, Amazon, Apple and Disney. The weakness is that a library is a stock of value, while the channels that monetise it — cable bundles — are a shrinking flow. Owning more of a melting asset with borrowed money raises the stakes rather than the moat.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage on a declining base. Arete Research cut the stock to Sell on July 9, 2026 with a $2 target, citing about $86 billion of gross debt and leverage of about six times for the combined company (Yahoo Finance), and management's inexperience running a "highly levered balance sheet". The largest segment is the one in decline, so the debt has to be serviced out of a cash flow that is shrinking at its core.
02
Thin profits today. On a GAAP basis the last four quarters lost $621M; operating margin is 6.75% and free cash flow was just $470M on $29.11B of revenue — a 1.61% FCF margin. There is little cushion before the WBD debt arrives.
03
The settlement is a five-year rulebook. Minimum film counts, $300M a year of extra U.S. film spending, a trustee to monitor compliance and an editorial-independence board over CBS News and CNN (NPR); press reports citing a settlement source add a $1.5B commitment to Hollywood film production over five years. Each is a constraint on the cost-cutting a leveraged buyer usually relies on. The agreement still needs a judge's approval.
04
Public holders don't vote. PSKY Class B shares carry no votes and the Ellison family controls the company. Once the deal closes, Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Abu Dhabi funds will own a large block of non-voting shares — 49.5% foreign ownership in total per NPR (the FCC approved this on September 18). Minority holders are passengers on the strategy.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

A note on the series first. Before August 7, 2025 there was no PSKY — the line is Paramount Global's Class B stock, PARA. Old Class B holders could take one PSKY share for each PARA share (or $15 cash, prorated), so the price level carries across the merger without a conversion factor, and that is how StockAnalysis, Yahoo Finance and Digrin all present it. One caveat: in the months before the close, PARA's price had a $15 cash-election floor-of-sorts attached to part of each holding, so those closes are not a pure read on the business.

With that said, the five years are brutal. From the September 2021 close of $39.51 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $9.91, the Class B line lost 74.9% of its price (dividends excluded). It was not a straight line down. The stock fell to the $10–13 range by early 2024 and stayed there until the Skydance deal was done — then a bidding war turned it into a momentum stock. The Class B line rallied from $12.57 at the end of July 2025 to $18.92 at the end of September 2025, when the 6-period monthly RSI hit 89.3, the highest reading in the series, and it touched an intraday high of $20.86 on September 23, 2025. Everything since has been the market pricing in the cost of winning. March 2026, the first full month after Paramount won WBD, was down 33.2%; July 2026, the month of the court pause and the Arete downgrade, was down 19.3% to $7.96, the lowest month-end close in the window.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The last obstacle is gone and the deal gets done at a price the market clearly doesn't believe. The new money — $47B of Ellison and RedBird equity — comes in at $16.02 a share, 62% above where public holders can buy today. The combined company puts Paramount+ and HBO Max, CBS and CNN, and the Paramount and Warner Bros. studios under one roof, with over $6B of targeted synergies on top of the more than $2.7B of standalone savings already in progress. Standalone Paramount is already turning: two profitable quarters in 2026, Paramount+ revenue growing mid-teens, the EBITDA guide raised. At 0.38x sales and about 7.8x EV/EBITDA, the stock prices a lot of failure. A 14% short float means any good news after closing can squeeze.

Bear Case

This is a leveraged bet on legacy media. Combined gross debt of up to $86–87B, about six times leverage by Arete's estimate, sits on top of a TV business shrinking 9% a year. The consent decree requires minimum film output and extra production spending for five years, which rules out the cost cuts a leveraged buyer usually depends on. Public shareholders don't vote, will end up as a minority beside the Ellisons and three sovereign funds, and have an average Street target at about today's price with as many Sells as Holds. Settlement day told the story: WBD rose to within 20 cents of the offer and PSKY closed lower.

Top 3 Risks

01
Debt load after closing. Committed acquisition debt of $49B on top of WBD's own $32.02B and Paramount's $16.20B. If linear TV declines faster than streaming and studios grow, interest cover gets tight fast — the thesis behind Arete's $2 target.
02
Integration under a consent decree. Five years of minimum film counts, extra U.S. production spending, separate operation of the two studios, a trustee, and an independent board over CBS News and CNN. The promised synergies must come from elsewhere.
03
Governance and dilution. Non-voting public shares, a controlling family, and a cap table about to be reshaped by roughly 2.9B new shares plus any ticking-fee equity. Minority holders have no say in the next capital decision.
Industry Commentary
Linear TV decline vs. the streaming land-grab
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Every legacy media company has the same problem: the cable bundle that pays the bills is shrinking, and streaming, which is meant to replace it, earns less per viewer. Paramount's Q2 2026 shows both sides — TV Media revenue down 9%, Direct-to-Consumer up 9%, total up less than 1%. Ellison's answer is scale: combine two libraries, two studios and two streamers, then compete with Netflix, Amazon, Apple and Disney as a peer. Netflix's signed deal for the studio and streaming business, and Comcast's bid in the auction, show the industry believes the assets are worth owning. The question is the price and the debt that paid it.

Regulation, politics and foreign capital
ONGOING HEADWIND

The Justice Department cleared the deal in June 2026, and Ellison says competition authorities in nearly 70 jurisdictions approved it. Twelve states sued anyway. The settlement ended the case, but not the scrutiny. California Attorney General Rob Bonta said: "The settlement is not a vote of support for this merger." Connecticut's attorney general said his state "wanted and demanded full divestiture of CNN and CBS News". NPR reports the FCC approved foreign ownership of up to 100% on September 18, with sovereign funds of Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Abu Dhabi among the investors. The Ellison family's closeness to the Trump administration, which NPR notes, is both a political asset and a political risk for a company that will own two national newsrooms.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The most unusual number in this report is $16.02. That is the price at which the controlling family and its partners agreed to buy tens of billions of dollars of new PSKY stock — against a $9.91 market close. The bull framing: the people with the most information and control are paying 62% above market, and the public float can buy the same shares at a steep discount before the combined company reports a single quarter. The bear framing: the price was fixed on February 27, before the court fight, the ticking fee and the settlement's spending commitments, and the market has marked it down ever since; a rights offering at $16.02 that no rational holder would take up shows how far the market has moved away from that price. The way to decide is the first two combined quarters: EBITDA versus the $3.8–3.9B standalone guide plus WBD, and whether the debt starts coming down.
