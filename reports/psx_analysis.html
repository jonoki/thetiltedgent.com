01 Company Overview

Phillips 66 is an integrated downstream energy company operating five reportable segments — Refining, Midstream, Marketing & Specialties, Chemicals, and Renewable Fuels — spun out of ConocoPhillips in May 2012. It generated $132.4B of revenue in FY2025, down 7.5% year over year, the third consecutive annual decline as refining margins compressed; on a trailing-twelve-month basis through June 30, 2026 revenue has rebounded to $152.2B (+14.4% YoY) as crack spreads inflected sharply higher.

The company ranks #183 globally by market capitalisation at $95.77B, and the stock has roughly doubled over the last twelve months (+96.5% 52-week change) on the back of a violent upswing in refining margins. But the defining feature of the last eighteen months has not been the commodity cycle — it has been governance. Elliott Management's activist campaign, a $2.5B stake and the first successful Elliott proxy vote against a major U.S. corporation, has reframed the entire investment debate around whether Phillips 66 should remain an integrated conglomerate at all.

A NOTE ON EARNINGS QUALITY
FY2025 GAAP earnings of $4.4B ($10.79/sh) were roughly 70% higher than adjusted earnings of $2.6B ($6.44/sh), largely because of gains on $3.5B of asset dispositions. Screening tools quoting a trailing P/E on GAAP numbers will materially misrepresent the underlying business at both ends of this cycle.

The core cyclical engine. Phillips 66 processed 186.9 million barrels of crude in Q2 2026 at 96% utilisation — implying roughly 2.1 million barrels per day of net crude capacity (derived from throughput ÷ days ÷ utilisation; the company does not quote a single headline capacity figure in the release). Clean product yield — the proportion of each barrel that comes out as high-value gasoline, diesel and jet rather than low-value residual fuel — ran 86% in Q2 2026 (87% in Q1), with a 98% market capture rate against indicator margins.

Realized margin is the whole story: $10.11/bbl in Q1 2026 → $24.08/bbl in Q2 2026 (Q4 2025 was $12.48/bbl). That single line item took segment earnings from $208M to $3,086M sequentially. Phillips 66's chemicals exposure sits entirely in Chevron Phillips Chemical Company (CPChem), a 50/50 joint venture with Chevron producing olefins and polyolefins (ethylene, polyethylene) and specialty chemicals. Because it is equity-accounted rather than consolidated, it contributes earnings without contributing revenue — a structural reason the segment looks small on a revenue mix and large on an asset-value basis, and one of the reasons Elliott argues the sum of the parts exceeds the whole. Global O&P capacity utilisation was 91% in Q2 2026 (94% in Q1), with Q3 guidance in the low 90s. Q2 carried −$29M of lower-of-cost-or-market (LCM) inventory adjustments.

Two large growth projects are mid-construction and both are expected to reach full operations in 2027: the Golden Triangle Polymers Project at Orange, Texas, and the Ras Laffan Polymers Project in Qatar. Global polyethylene has been in a heavy oversupply cycle; these assets are advantaged on U.S. and Qatari feedstock but land into a market that has to absorb substantial new capacity.

Rodeo Utilisation
Built around the Rodeo Renewable Energy Complex in California — the conversion of a conventional crude refinery into one of the world's largest renewable diesel and sustainable aviation fuel plants. The economics are policy-driven rather than crude-driven: profitability turns on the blender's tax credit regime, California's Low Carbon Fuel Standard credits, and RIN prices under the federal Renewable Fuel Standard, all set against feedstock costs for used cooking oil, tallow and soybean oil.

That policy sensitivity produced a violent swing: the segment lost $380M on an adjusted basis across FY2025, lost a further $41M in Q1 2026, then earned +$544M in Q2 2026 — with Rodeo running at 106% of nameplate and production rising from 40 MBD to 53 MBD. A single quarter of Renewable Fuels earnings more than erased a full year of losses. Investors should treat this line as an option on policy, not a durable earnings stream, until multiple quarters of positive contribution accumulate.

REPLACEMENT-COST BARRIERS

Phillips 66's moat is physical rather than commercial. No meaningful greenfield refinery has been permitted in the United States in decades, and the replacement cost of a ~2.1 million b/d refining system plus an integrated Permian-to-Gulf-Coast NGL chain is far above where the equity has traded through most of this cycle. Within that fixed asset base the company's genuine differentiator is operational: 99% crude utilisation in FY2025 marked a third consecutive year above the industry average, clean product yield of 86–87% keeps a high share of each barrel in the premium product slate, and a 98% market capture rate means the system actually harvests the indicator crack rather than leaking it to logistics and quality discounts. Adjusted controllable costs have been driven from $6.98/bbl in 2022 to $5.57/bbl, with a $5.50/bbl target for 2027 — a real, measurable structural improvement rather than a cyclical one.

The midstream franchise is the higher-quality half of the moat. Its earnings are fee-based rather than margin-based, its volumes hit records in FY2025 (NGL transportation +22%, fractionation +23%) and again in Q2 2026 (1,020 MBD fractionation), and the DCP integration delivered $500M of annual run-rate synergies with a path from $3.7B of segment EBITDA in 2024 to $4.5B by 2027. Elliott's entire thesis rests on the observation that this is a better business than the one it is bolted to — and that the market has been unwilling to pay a midstream multiple for midstream cash flows sitting inside a refiner.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Margin-taker, not margin-maker. Phillips 66 does not set crack spreads; it harvests them. Refining adjusted earnings went $427M (FY2025) → $208M (Q1 2026) → $3,086M (Q2 2026) on realized margins of $12.48 → $10.11 → $24.08 per barrel. Cost discipline moves the number by a dollar or two a barrel; the market moves it by fourteen. Every valuation anchored on trailing earnings at either extreme is wrong in a predictable direction.

2. Contested governance and an unresolved structure. Elliott holds two board seats won in May 2025 and continues to argue for separation of midstream and chemicals. Management has refused, citing up to $10B of tax leakage, and is instead deploying more capital into midstream ($975M vs $822M for refining in FY2025). This is a stalemate, not a resolution — and stalemates in activist situations tend to resolve either through a strategic action or through renewed escalation.

3. Policy dependence in Renewable Fuels. The Rodeo conversion was a large, largely irreversible capital commitment whose returns depend on RIN prices, LCFS credits and blender's credits — all of which are set by regulators and legislatures, not markets. A −$380M FY2025 loss followed by a +$544M quarter is not a business with a stable cost of capital attached to it.

4. Chemicals oversupply and 2027 start-up risk. CPChem utilisation slipped from 94% to 91% and the segment absorbed LCM inventory write-downs in Q2 2026. Golden Triangle and Ras Laffan both reach full operations in 2027 into a global polyethylene market that has been absorbing heavy capacity additions. Advantaged feedstock helps; it does not immunise the JV against a prolonged trough.

Notable One-Off Items

One-off items are unusually material here and run in both directions:

Los Angeles refinery accelerated depreciation. FY2025 carried $964M of pre-tax accelerated depreciation ($239M of it in Q4 2025) tied to the decision to cease operations at the Los Angeles refinery. This is the single largest reconciling item between FY2025 GAAP EPS of $10.79 and adjusted EPS of $6.44.

Asset sale gains. FY2025 portfolio dispositions totalled $3.5B — Gulf Coast Express, Rockies Express, the Discovery pipeline interest (to Williams), and the 65% interest in the Germany/Austria retail network for $1.6B. Gains on these sales inflated GAAP earnings without adding to recurring earnings power. A further $64M of post-closing adjustments landed in Q2 2026. Offsetting these, FY2025 acquisitions also totalled $3.5B, including the remaining 50% of WRB Refining.

Q2 2026 special items. Marketing & Specialties carried +$69M of pre-tax special items, Refining −$24M, and Chemicals absorbed −$29M of lower-of-cost-or-market inventory adjustments. Reported EPS of $9.55 versus adjusted $9.41 means special items were, unusually, a modest net positive in the quarter.

Turnaround expense. $123M in Q2 2026, $178M in Q1 2026, $135M in Q4 2025, against full-year 2026 guidance of $550–600M and Q3 2026 guidance of $100–120M. Turnarounds are recurring in aggregate but lumpy by quarter, and quarter-on-quarter earnings comparisons are distorted by their timing.

Working capital and mark-to-market. Q2 2026 operating cash flow of $7.3B included $2.9B from working capital — a source of cash that reverses. Management also cited mark-to-market impacts as a contributor to the refining result. Neither is repeatable earnings power.

Proxy contest costs. Phillips 66 incurred costs defending the Elliott campaign through 2025, but no separately disclosed dollar figure was verified from a live source for this build. Corporate & Other ran −$1,465M in FY2025 and −$407M in Q2 2026, with Q3 2026 guidance of $325–350M; the proxy costs sit inside that line without being broken out.

Five-year trajectory. On a dividend-adjusted basis PSX has gone from $58.72 (Sep 2021) to $240.00 (Aug 20, 2026) — a total return of roughly +309%, or about 32% annualised. But that line is not a smooth compounding story; it is three distinct regimes stacked end to end. From late 2021 through the end of 2022 the stock rode the post-COVID refining margin explosion from $58.72 to $91.95. Through 2023 and early 2024 it ground higher to a March 2024 peak of $151.19 on strong distillate cracks. It then gave back a third of its value, bottoming at $99.65 in April 2025 as refining margins collapsed and FY2024 adjusted EPS fell to $4.99. From that low it has risen 141% in sixteen months, with the decisive move coming in the last two months: $169.05 (Jun 2026) → $211.68 (Jul 2026) → $240.00 as the Q2 margin surge landed.

What the RSI is saying. Six-period monthly RSI is deep in overbought territory after the July–August advance. On monthly data that is not a sell signal — cyclical stocks can hold overbought readings through an entire margin upcycle — but it does mean the stock is priced for the current margin environment to persist. The comparable readings in this series came around the late-2022 and early-2024 peaks; both were followed by multi-month consolidations rather than crashes.

Relative performance. Over the last twelve months PSX is up +96.5% against roughly +20.6% total return for the S&P 500 (VOO, one year to Aug 21, 2026) — an outperformance of roughly 76 percentage points. Within the refining group it has been strong but not exceptional: over the same period Marathon Petroleum's market cap rose 103.4%, Valero's 129.5% and PBF's 202.6%, against PSX's 94.0%. PSX's conglomerate structure damped both the drawdown into 2025 and the recovery out of it — which is, in miniature, exactly the argument Elliott is making.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Phillips 66 is generating extraordinary cash at a valuation the market still applies to a mediocre refiner: TTM free cash flow of $6.39B and operating cash flow of $8.93B against a $95.77B market cap, with total debt cut $6.6B in a single quarter to $20.6B and debt-to-capital down to 39%. Operationally the company is genuinely better than it was — 99% crude utilisation, 98% market capture, controllable costs down from $6.98 to $5.57/bbl. And there is an embedded free option: a fee-based midstream business on a path to $4.5B of EBITDA by 2027, sitting inside a refiner, with an activist holding two board seats arguing it is worth substantially more on its own. If margins hold and the structure changes, both the earnings and the multiple move in the same direction.

Bear Case

You are buying a deeply cyclical business two months after realized margins more than doubled, 2.8% off a 52-week high, at a price 7.6% above the average analyst target, with six Holds and two Sells among twenty analysts. The trailing P/E of 13.70 is calculated on peak-cycle EPS of $17.51 — FY2025 adjusted EPS was $6.44, and FY2024 diluted EPS was $4.99. Q2's $7.3B of operating cash flow included $2.9B of working capital that reverses. Meanwhile the governance question is unresolved rather than settled: management is deploying more capital into the very segment Elliott wants sold, buybacks have fallen from $4.01B (FY2023) to $1.21B (FY2025), and CPChem's two large polyethylene projects land into an oversupplied market in 2027.

Top 3 Risks
01
Crack spread mean reversion. Realized margin went from $10.11/bbl (Q1 2026) to $24.08/bbl (Q2 2026). Nothing about Phillips 66's asset base changed in ninety days. If margins revert toward the $12–13/bbl range seen as recently as Q4 2025, refining earnings fall by billions and the trailing P/E that currently reads 13.70 re-prices against a far smaller earnings base.
02
An unresolved activist stalemate. Elliott has two directors and a public thesis worth roughly $40B; management has a public rebuttal citing $10B of tax leakage and is allocating more capital to midstream than to refining. Neither side has conceded. Renewed escalation, a second proxy contest, or a value-destructive compromise are all live outcomes, and the de-staggering proposal's failure means board turnover remains slow.
03
Policy risk in Renewable Fuels and regulatory risk in refining. Rodeo's swing from a −$380M FY2025 drag to +$544M in one quarter was driven by RIN, LCFS and blender's credit economics, not by operations. The same mechanism works in reverse. Simultaneously, the Los Angeles refinery closure — and the $964M of accelerated depreciation it triggered — is a reminder that Californian and European regulatory regimes can strand refining assets faster than they can be depreciated.
  Industry Commentary
Refining Crack Spreads & Global Capacity Closures
EMERGING TAILWIND
The crack spread — the differential between crude cost and refined product prices — is the single variable that explains most of PSX's earnings variance, and it has inflected hard. Phillips 66's realized margin went $12.48/bbl (Q4 2025) → $10.11/bbl (Q1 2026) → $24.08/bbl (Q2 2026), taking refining adjusted earnings from $208M to $3,086M in one quarter. The whole group moved together: MPC's market cap is up 103.4% over twelve months, VLO's 129.5%, PBF's 202.6%. The structural argument is that Western refining capacity has been closing — Phillips 66 is itself ceasing operations at its Los Angeles refinery — while global product demand has not fallen commensurately, leaving surviving complex refiners with pricing power. The specific global capacity-closure statistics behind that argument were not verified from a live source for this build and should be checked independently.
Activist Pressure on Conglomerate Structures in Energy
ONGOING HEADWIND
The Phillips 66 campaign is the reference case for a broader trend: activists arguing that integrated energy conglomerates trade at a discount to the sum of their parts because fee-based midstream cash flows are valued at commodity multiples. Elliott put $2.5B behind the thesis, claimed ~$40B of trapped value, and in May 2025 won two of four contested board seats — its first successful proxy vote against a major U.S. corporation. Management's defence was substantive rather than rhetorical: $13.6B returned to shareholders since July 2022, a 67% total shareholder return versus 42% for weighted proxy peers, $3.5B of dispositions against a $3B target, controllable costs cut 15% with a further target of $5.50/bbl by 2027, and a warning of $10B of tax leakage from separation. The de-staggering proposal failed at its 80% threshold. The result is influence without control on one side and a mandate without a majority on the other — a structure that historically does not stay stable.
Renewable Fuels Policy & RIN Economics — Bull vs Bear
POLICY-DEPENDENT
Bull framing: the Rodeo conversion is finished, running at 106% of nameplate and 53 MBD, and delivered +$544M in Q2 2026 — a single quarter that more than erased the segment's entire −$380M FY2025 loss. If RIN and LCFS credit economics stay supportive, a business the market has been valuing at less than zero becomes a multi-hundred-million-dollar annual contributor and Phillips 66 owns one of the largest renewable diesel and SAF assets in the world.

Bear framing: nothing in that swing was earned by operations. Renewable fuel margins are set by compliance credit prices and blender's credits — instruments that legislatures and regulators can reprice or repeal. A business whose quarterly earnings can move by $585M on policy rather than performance does not deserve a durable multiple, and the capital sunk into the conversion is not recoverable if the policy regime turns. The honest position is that this segment is an option, and options should be sized as options.
