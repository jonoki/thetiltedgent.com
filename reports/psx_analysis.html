01 Company Overview

Phillips 66 is an integrated downstream energy company (it refines, moves and sells fuel rather than drilling for oil) operating five reportable segments — Refining, Midstream, Marketing & Specialties, Chemicals, and Renewable Fuels — spun out of ConocoPhillips in May 2012. It generated $132.4B of revenue in FY2025, down 7.5% year over year as refining margins compressed; over the twelve months to September 30, 2026 revenue has rebounded to $168.2B (+27.5% YoY) as crack spreads (the gap between what a refiner pays for crude and what it gets for gasoline and diesel) widened sharply.

The company ranks #111 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation at $106.73B, and the stock has roughly doubled over the last twelve months (+98.3% price change, Oct 29, 2025 to Oct 29, 2026) on the back of a violent upswing in refining margins. But the defining feature of the last eighteen months has not been the commodity cycle — it has been governance. Elliott Management's activist campaign — a $2.5B stake and a 2025 proxy fight that won it two board seats — has reframed the entire investment debate around whether Phillips 66 should remain an integrated conglomerate at all.

A NOTE ON EARNINGS QUALITY
FY2025 GAAP earnings of $4.4B ($10.79/sh) were roughly 70% higher than adjusted earnings of $2.6B ($6.44/sh), largely because of gains on $3.5B of asset dispositions. Screening tools quoting a trailing P/E on GAAP numbers will materially misrepresent the underlying business at both ends of this cycle.

The core cyclical engine. Phillips 66 processed 186.9 million barrels of crude in Q2 2026 at 96% utilisation — implying roughly 2.1 million barrels per day of net crude capacity (derived from throughput ÷ days ÷ utilisation; the company does not quote a single headline capacity figure in the release). FY2025 crude utilisation was 99%, the third consecutive year above the industry average. Clean product yield — the proportion of each barrel that comes out as high-value gasoline, diesel and jet rather than low-value residual fuel — ran 86% in Q2 2026 and again in Q3 (87% in Q1), with a 98% market capture rate in Q2 (the share of the benchmark refining margin the company actually earned).

Realized margin is the whole story: $10.11/bbl in Q1 2026 → $24.08/bbl in Q2 → $38.00/bbl in Q3 2026 (Q4 2025 was $12.48/bbl), which the company put down mainly to wider market crack spreads. That single line item took segment adjusted earnings from $208M in Q1 to $3,086M in Q2 and $6,060M in Q3. Crude utilisation rose to 98% in Q3, with a record third-quarter distillate (diesel and jet) yield of 41% and record Central Corridor processed inputs of 848 thousand b/d. Adjusted controllable costs ran $5.57/bbl in Q2 2026, versus $6.98/bbl in 2022 and a stated $5.50/bbl target for 2027 — a 21% structural cost reduction. Turnaround (planned maintenance) expense was $79M in Q3, against $123M in Q2 and $178M in Q1. Integrating WRB Refining, whose remaining 50% Phillips 66 bought on October 1, 2025, has delivered more than $50M of near-term synergies. On April 28, 2026 it bought the assets of the closed Lindsey Oil Refinery in the UK for $115M, to be folded into its neighbouring Humber refinery rather than restarted on their own, and it is ceasing operations at its Los Angeles refinery — the latter driving $964M of pre-tax accelerated depreciation through FY2025.

The stable, fee-based counterweight to refining and the single most contested asset in the company. In Q3 2026 it earned $702M before tax (Q2: $785M), with adjusted EBITDA of $977M down from $1,046M on lower gas transportation margins, fractionation of about 1 million b/d, wellhead gas volumes of 4.6 Bcf/d and the Freeport LPG export dock at 105% utilisation. Asked on the Q3 call whether midstream could grow to half of company EBITDA, Mark Lashier did not rule it out, noting that midstream is growing faster than the other segments. Midstream gathers, processes, transports and fractionates natural gas and NGLs across a wellhead-to-export value chain: Permian gathering and processing, long-haul NGL takeaway, Sweeny-area fractionation, and LPG export docks. The 2023 acquisition of the remainder of DCP Midstream transformed the segment's scale — management has since booked $500M of annual run-rate DCP synergies and grown segment adjusted EBITDA from $2.1B in 2021 to $3.7B in 2024, with a stated path to $4.5B by 2027. FY2025 NGL transportation volumes rose 22% and fractionation volumes 23%, both records, and Q2 2026 fractionation hit 1,020 MBD with NGL throughput to market of 943 MBD and record LPG export volumes.

Capital is flowing here, not to refining: FY2025 midstream capex of $975M versus $822M for refining. Recent additions include the $2.2B EPIC NGL acquisition (Jan 2025), the Pinnacle Midstream Permian bolt-on, Dos Picos II (220 million cubic feet of gas a day, at full production by Q2 2026), and two projects announced in May 2026 and expected online in 2028: the Zeus Gas Plant (300 million cubic feet a day, Permian) and a third Coastal Bend NGL fractionator (100 thousand barrels a day, Robstown, near Corpus Christi). A separate Coastal Bend expansion project expects first volumes in Q4 2026 and a ramp to 350 thousand barrels a day in early 2027, and the Q3 2026 release reported a final investment decision on the Western Gateway Pipeline, expected in service in 2029. Elliott's core argument is that investors pay more for each dollar of steady pipeline fees than for a refiner's swinging margins, and that inside the conglomerate this business is valued like refining.

Phillips 66's chemicals exposure sits entirely in Chevron Phillips Chemical Company (CPChem), a 50/50 joint venture with Chevron producing olefins and polyolefins (ethylene, polyethylene) and specialty chemicals. Because it is equity-accounted rather than consolidated, it contributes earnings without contributing revenue — a structural reason the segment looks small on a revenue mix and large on an asset-value basis, and one of the reasons Elliott argues the sum of the parts exceeds the whole. Global O&P capacity utilisation was 92% in Q3 2026 (91% in Q2, 94% in Q1). Segment earnings fell to $311M from $404M in Q2, and adjusted EBITDA to $423M from $528M, on lower polyethylene margins partly offset by higher volumes and lower costs. On the Q3 call management flagged a seasonally slower Q4 and start-up costs for Golden Triangle showing up at CPChem.

Two large growth projects are mid-construction and both are expected to reach full operations in 2027: the Golden Triangle Polymers Project at Orange, Texas, and the Ras Laffan Polymers Project in Qatar. Global polyethylene has been in a heavy oversupply cycle; these assets are advantaged on U.S. and Qatari feedstock but land into a market that has to absorb substantial new capacity.

Phillips 66's moat is physical rather than commercial. No meaningful greenfield refinery has been permitted in the United States in decades, and the replacement cost of a ~2.1 million b/d refining system plus an integrated Permian-to-Gulf-Coast NGL chain is far above where the equity has traded through most of this cycle. Within that fixed asset base the company's genuine differentiator is operational: 99% crude utilisation in FY2025 marked a third consecutive year above the industry average, clean product yield of 86–87% turns a high share of each barrel into the most valuable fuels, and a 98% market capture rate means the system earns nearly all of the benchmark refining margin rather than losing it to transport costs and crude-quality discounts. Adjusted controllable costs have been driven from $6.98/bbl in 2022 to $5.57/bbl, with a $5.50/bbl target for 2027 — a real, measurable structural improvement rather than a cyclical one.

The midstream franchise is the higher-quality half of the moat. Its earnings are fee-based rather than margin-based, its volumes hit records in FY2025 (NGL transportation +22%, fractionation +23%) and again in Q2 2026 (1,020 MBD fractionation, held at about 1 million b/d in Q3), and the DCP integration delivered $500M of annual run-rate synergies with a path from $3.7B of segment EBITDA in 2024 to $4.5B by 2027. Elliott's entire thesis rests on the observation that this is a better business than the one it is bolted to — and that investors have been unwilling to value pipeline cash flows the way they value a pipeline company while those cash flows sit inside a refiner.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Margin-taker, not margin-maker. Phillips 66 does not set crack spreads; it harvests them. Refining adjusted earnings went $427M (FY2025) → $208M (Q1 2026) → $3,086M (Q2 2026) → $6,060M (Q3 2026) on realized margins of $12.48 (Q4 2025) → $10.11 → $24.08 → $38.00 per barrel. Cost discipline moves the number by a dollar or two a barrel; the market moved it by almost twenty-eight in two quarters. Every valuation anchored on trailing earnings at either extreme is wrong in a predictable direction.

2. Contested governance and an unresolved structure. Elliott's two directors, elected in May 2025 after a campaign to separate midstream and chemicals, remain on the board, and Elliott said in March 2026 that it would stay engaged with management and the board. Management has refused, citing up to $10B of tax leakage, and is instead deploying more capital into midstream ($975M vs $822M for refining in FY2025). This is a stalemate, not a resolution — and stalemates in activist situations tend to resolve either through a strategic action or through renewed escalation.

3. Policy dependence in Renewable Fuels. The Rodeo conversion was a large, largely irreversible capital commitment whose returns depend on RIN prices, LCFS credits and blender's credits — all of which are set by regulators and legislatures, not markets. A −$380M FY2025 loss followed by a +$544M quarter and then a $191M one is not a business whose earnings anyone can forecast with confidence.

4. Chemicals oversupply and 2027 start-up risk. CPChem utilisation slipped from 94% in Q1 2026 to 91% in Q2 and 92% in Q3, and Q3 earnings fell on lower polyethylene margins. Golden Triangle and Ras Laffan both reach full operations in 2027 into a global polyethylene market that has been absorbing heavy capacity additions. Advantaged feedstock helps; it does not immunise the JV against a prolonged trough.

Notable One-Off Items

One-off items are unusually material here and run in both directions:

Los Angeles refinery accelerated depreciation. FY2025 carried $964M of pre-tax accelerated depreciation ($239M of it in Q4 2025) tied to the decision to cease operations at the Los Angeles refinery. This is the single largest reconciling item between FY2025 GAAP EPS of $10.79 and adjusted EPS of $6.44.

Asset sale gains. FY2025 portfolio dispositions totalled $3.5B — Gulf Coast Express, Rockies Express, the Discovery pipeline interest (to Williams), and the 65% interest in the Germany/Austria retail network for $1.6B. Gains on these sales inflated GAAP earnings without adding to recurring earnings power. A further $64M of post-closing adjustments landed in Q2 2026. Offsetting these, FY2025 acquisitions also totalled $3.5B, including the remaining 50% of WRB Refining.

Special items, Q2 and Q3 2026. In Q2, Marketing & Specialties carried +$69M of pre-tax special items (a $110M gain on asset sales less a $41M legal accrual) and Refining −$24M (a legal accrual); reported EPS of $9.55 against adjusted $9.41. In Q3 the only special item was a $29M Refining impairment, so reported and adjusted EPS were both $14.08 per share and the quarter's result carries no one-off gains.

Turnaround expense. $79M in Q3 2026, $123M in Q2, $178M in Q1 and $135M in Q4 2025. Turnarounds (planned maintenance shutdowns) are recurring in aggregate but lumpy by quarter, and quarter-on-quarter earnings comparisons are distorted by their timing.

Working capital. Q2 2026 operating cash flow of $7.3B included $2.9B from working capital (money freed from inventories and receivables, which tends to reverse), and Q3's $7.3B included a further $1.1B; excluding working capital, Q3 operating cash flow was $6.2B. Management also cited mark-to-market impacts as a contributor to the Q2 refining result. Neither is repeatable earnings power.

Proxy contest costs. Phillips 66 incurred costs defending the Elliott campaign through 2025. Corporate & Other ran −$1,465M in FY2025, −$407M in Q2 2026 and −$372M in Q3; the proxy costs sit inside that line without being broken out.

RSI measures how much of recent price movement has been upward versus downward. Above 70 the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been sold hard and may be washed out. It describes momentum, not value.

Five-year trajectory. On actual month-end closes PSX has gone from $74.78 (Oct 29, 2021) to $273.61 (Oct 29, 2026) — a price return of +265.9%, or about 29.6% a year, dividends excluded; the S&P 500 ETF (SPY) returned +67.5% on the same dates. But that line is not a smooth compounding story; it is three distinct regimes stacked end to end. From late 2021 through the end of 2022 the stock rode the post-COVID refining margin explosion from $74.78 to $104.08. Through 2023 and early 2024 it ground higher to a March 2024 peak of $163.34 on strong distillate cracks. It then gave back over a third of its value, bottoming at $104.06 in April 2025 as refining margins collapsed and FY2024 diluted EPS fell to $4.99. From that low it has risen about 163% in eighteen months, most of it in the last four: $169.05 (Jun 2026) → $211.68 (Jul) → $246.58 (Aug) → $255.31 (Sep) → $273.61 (Oct 29) as the Q2 and Q3 margin surges landed. The last point is the Oct 29 close, not a month-end.

What the RSI is saying. Six-period monthly RSI reads about 91 at the Oct 29 close, the highest reading in this five-year series, after rising every month since June. On monthly data that is not a sell signal — cyclical stocks can hold overbought readings through an entire margin upcycle — but it does mean the stock is priced for the current margin environment to persist. Daily momentum has already cooled: the stock closed at $273.61 against an intraday high of $299.39 on Oct 19, and the 14-day daily RSI was 48.5, neutral.

Relative performance. Over the last twelve months (Oct 29, 2025 to Oct 29, 2026 closes, price only) PSX is up +98.3% against +11.9% for the S&P 500 ETF (SPY) — an outperformance of roughly 86 percentage points. Within the refining group it has been strong but not the strongest: over the same dates Marathon Petroleum rose 121.0%, Valero 125.3% and PBF 121.5%. PSX's conglomerate structure damped both the drawdown into 2025 and the recovery out of it — which is, in miniature, exactly the argument Elliott is making.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Phillips 66 is generating extraordinary cash at a modest valuation: TTM free cash flow of $12.36B and operating cash flow of $15.03B against a $106.73B market cap, a trailing P/E of 8.7×, total debt cut by $8.6B in two quarters to $18.5B and debt-to-capital down to 34%. The cash is now coming back to shareholders: $2.3B of buybacks in Q3 alone and two dividend increases in 2026. Operationally the company is genuinely better than it was — 98% crude utilisation in Q3, a third-quarter record distillate yield, controllable costs down from $6.98 to $5.57/bbl. And there is an embedded free option: a fee-based midstream business on a path to $4.5B of EBITDA by 2027, sitting inside a refiner, with an activist holding two board seats arguing it is worth substantially more on its own. If margins hold and the structure changes, both the earnings and the price investors pay for each dollar of them move up together.

Bear Case

You are buying a deeply cyclical business after realized margins went from $10.11 to $38.00 a barrel in two quarters, with seven Holds and two Sells among twenty analysts and consensus expecting FY2027 EPS about 11% below FY2026. The trailing P/E of 8.74 is calculated on peak-cycle EPS of $31.31 — FY2025 adjusted EPS was $6.44, and FY2024 diluted EPS was $4.99. Q2 and Q3 operating cash flow included $4.0B of working capital that tends to reverse. The refiners showed how fast sentiment can turn on Oct 27, when PSX fell 7.0% and Valero 9.2% in a session the S&P 500 rose. Meanwhile the governance question is unresolved rather than settled: management is deploying more capital into the very segment Elliott wants sold, the head of Refining retires at year-end with no successor named, and CPChem's two large polyethylene projects land into an oversupplied market in 2027.

Top 3 Risks
01
Crack spread mean reversion. Realized margin went from $10.11/bbl (Q1 2026) to $24.08/bbl (Q2) and $38.00/bbl (Q3 2026). Nothing about Phillips 66's asset base changed in six months. If margins revert toward the $12–13/bbl range seen as recently as Q4 2025, refining earnings fall by billions and the trailing P/E that currently reads 8.74 rises sharply as the high-margin quarters drop out of the trailing twelve months.
02
An unresolved activist stalemate. Elliott has two directors and a public thesis worth roughly $40B; management has a public rebuttal citing $10B of tax leakage and is allocating more capital to midstream than to refining. Neither side has conceded. Renewed escalation, a second proxy contest, or a value-destructive compromise are all live outcomes, and the de-staggering proposal's failure means board turnover remains slow.
03
Policy risk in Renewable Fuels and regulatory risk in refining. Rodeo's swing from a −$380M FY2025 drag to +$544M in Q2 2026, and back down to $191M in Q3, was driven by RIN, LCFS and blender's credit economics, not by operations. The same mechanism works in reverse. Simultaneously, the Los Angeles refinery closure — and the $964M of accelerated depreciation it triggered — is a reminder that Californian and European regulatory regimes can strand refining assets faster than they can be depreciated.
  Industry Commentary
Refining Crack Spreads & Global Capacity Closures
EMERGING TAILWIND
The crack spread — the differential between crude cost and refined product prices — is the single variable that explains most of PSX's earnings variance, and it has inflected hard. Phillips 66's realized margin went $12.48/bbl (Q4 2025) → $10.11/bbl (Q1 2026) → $24.08/bbl (Q2) → $38.00/bbl (Q3 2026), taking refining adjusted earnings from $208M to $6,060M in two quarters. The whole group moved together: over the twelve months to Oct 29, 2026, MPC's share price is up 121.0%, VLO's 125.3% and PBF's 121.5%, against 98.3% for PSX. They also fell together: on Oct 27 MPC lost 7.4%, VLO 9.2%, PBF 8.6% and PSX 7.0% while the S&P 500 ETF rose. The structural argument is that Western refining capacity has been closing — Phillips 66 is itself ceasing operations at its Los Angeles refinery — while global product demand has not fallen commensurately, leaving surviving complex refiners with pricing power.
Activist Pressure on Conglomerate Structures in Energy
ONGOING HEADWIND
The Phillips 66 campaign is the reference case for a broader trend: activists arguing that integrated energy conglomerates trade below what their pieces would be worth separately, because steady pipeline fees get valued like swinging commodity earnings. Elliott put $2.5B behind the thesis, claimed ~$40B of trapped value, and in May 2025 won two of four contested board seats. Management's defence was substantive rather than rhetorical: $13.6B returned to shareholders since July 2022, a 67% total shareholder return versus 42% for weighted proxy peers, $3.5B of dispositions against a $3B target, controllable costs cut 15% with a further target of $5.50/bbl by 2027, and a warning of $10B of tax leakage from separation. The de-staggering proposal failed at its 80% threshold. The result is influence without control on one side and a management team that kept its strategy without a decisive shareholder vote behind it on the other — a structure that historically does not stay stable.
Renewable Fuels Policy & RIN Economics — Bull vs Bear
POLICY-DEPENDENT
Bull framing: the Rodeo conversion is finished, running at 106% of nameplate and 53 MBD, and delivered +$544M in Q2 2026 — a single quarter that more than erased the segment's entire −$380M FY2025 loss — and stayed profitable in Q3 at $191M. If RIN and LCFS credit economics stay supportive, a business the market has been valuing at less than zero becomes a multi-hundred-million-dollar annual contributor and Phillips 66 owns one of the largest renewable diesel and SAF assets in the world.

Bear framing: nothing in that swing was earned by operations. Renewable fuel margins are set by compliance credit prices and blender's credits — instruments that legislatures and regulators can reprice or repeal. A business whose quarterly earnings can move by $585M on policy rather than performance does not deserve a high, stable valuation, and the capital sunk into the conversion is not recoverable if the policy regime turns. The honest position is that this segment is an option, and options should be sized as options.
