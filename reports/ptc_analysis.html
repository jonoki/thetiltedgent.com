01 Company Overview

Somewhere in every aircraft, tractor, pump and pacemaker there is a digital twin of the thing — a 3D model, a bill of materials, a change history of who approved which revision and when. PTC sells the software that holds that record. Creo and Onshape are its CAD tools; Windchill, Arena, Codebeamer and ServiceMax sit on the PLM side. FY2025 (year ended September 30, 2025) revenue was $2.74B, up 19.18%, with diluted EPS of $6.08 and free cash flow of $856.7M.

The number management wants you to watch is not revenue. PTC sells multi-year subscriptions, and under the accounting rules a chunk of each term licence is booked up front — so reported revenue swings with contract length, not with demand. The cleaner gauge is ARR: $2.448B at constant currency on June 30, 2026, up 9.1% and above the top of the 8–9% guide. Q3 FY2026 revenue, by contrast, fell 7% to $600M, partly because of the divestiture and partly, per management on the call, the shortened duration of a single large contract expansion. Two businesses left in March: PTC sold Kepware and ThingWorx — its industrial-connectivity and IoT units — to TPG for $523M in cash, and turned the after-tax proceeds into buybacks.

And yet the stock is 37% below its July 2025 month-end high of $214.81. In July 2025 reports said Autodesk was weighing a bid; the shares jumped, Autodesk walked away within days, and the takeover premium has been bleeding out since, accelerated in 2026 by the market's broad doubt about what AI does to seat-priced software. The question for a buyer at $135.28: is a 9% ARR grower with 84.5% gross margins and a shrinking share count a value trade, or is the market right that the new-business engine underneath the ARR is idling?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

PLM system-of-record lock-in Decades of engineering data Recurring subscription base 84.5% gross margin Defense & regulated-industry installs Cloud-native CAD (Onshape)

The moat is data gravity. A PLM system is not an app a manufacturer uses; it is where the company's product history lives — every revision, every approval, every supplier part number, often with regulatory audit trails attached. Barua's own framing, "our structural advantage is at the data and process level," is the right one. It is why ARR keeps compounding at high single digits through a year in which the stock lost a third of its value. The vulnerability is the other side of the same coin: a moat that protects the installed base does not by itself generate new business, and new business is exactly what the market questioned in 2026.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
New business looked flat. Per a June 12, 2026 Trefis analysis, when management was asked about new business excluding ARR already "banked" from deferred contracts, the CFO acknowledged it was essentially flat year-over-year. Per Trefis, the guided second-half step-up rested largely on deferred ARR already "banked" rather than fresh wins. Q3's ~$60M net new ARR beat expectations, but one quarter does not settle it.
02
AI is still a promise, not a line item. Management called AI monetisation "early stages" with customers starting pilots, and stand-alone AI revenue a "medium- to long-term" opportunity. If AI lets engineering teams do the same work with fewer seats before PTC can charge for the AI, seat-based CAD revenue is the first place it shows.
03
Reported numbers are noisy by construction. Term-licence revenue timing, the March 2026 divestiture and a $462.6M gain on sale all distort the income statement this year. TTM EPS of $10.32 includes that gain; the FY2026 non-GAAP guide is $7.87–$8.42. Anyone screening PTC on trailing P/E is looking at a flattered number.
04
The balance sheet is being used. Debt rose to $1,423.3M at June 30, 2026 from $1,197.4M at September 30, 2025 while cash rose to $351.5M, as buybacks outran free cash flow. Current ratio is 1.03. This is deliberate and still modest leverage (debt/equity 0.46), but the cushion is thinner than a year ago.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $119.79 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $135.28, PTC gained 12.9% on price — and it pays no dividend, so that is the whole return. The endpoints hide a round trip: a 2022 range between roughly $105 and $127, a steady 2023–24 climb to $200.06 in November 2024, a takeover-rumour spike to $214.81 in July 2025, and then a slide of 47% from that peak to the $113.61 June 2026 month-end — the lowest month-end close since September 2022.

The 6-period monthly RSI sat at or below about 30 through the first half of 2026 — 21.6 in April and 16.0 at the June low, the most oversold reading in the five-year series — and has since recovered to 41.5, below neutral. The 10-month moving average sits at about $144.63, above the current price: the July–August rally reached it and the September selloff pushed the stock back underneath. Momentum has healed from extreme; the long trend has not turned.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 16.5x consensus FY2026 earnings and 12.45x EV/EBITDA for a business whose ARR grew 9.1% in constant currency, beat its own guide, and got its full-year guide raised — with 84.5% gross margins, a 6.4% free-cash-flow yield, and a board retiring roughly 4% of the share count this year. The moat is the data: PLM systems of record do not get switched out on a whim, and displacement wins doubled year-over-year. The lowest target in StockAnalysis's consensus set, $140, sits above $135.28. If Q4 delivers the $79–92M of net new ARR the guide requires, the "flat new business" worry that drove the 2026 de-rating is answered with a number.

Bear Case

The ARR beat rests partly on contracted ARR already banked, and per a Trefis account of the spring earnings call the CFO answered "Approximately" when asked whether new business excluding that was flat. AI monetisation is, in management's own words, early-stage — while the risk that AI trims engineering seats is live now. Reported revenue fell 7% last quarter, the trailing P/E is flattered by a $462.6M one-off gain, and the buyback is being funded partly with new debt. The July 2025 peak was a takeover premium that evaporated within a week; there is no bidder under the stock today. A 5.25% short interest says a meaningful share of the market sees 9% growth as the ceiling, not the floor.

Top 3 Risks

01
Q4 net new ARR shortfall. The FY2026 guide needs $79–92M in Q4 against ~$60M in Q3. Q4 is seasonally the largest quarter, but a miss here would reopen the new-business question at the worst possible moment — alongside the first FY2027 guide.
02
AI compresses seats before it lifts price. CAD is 41% of ARR and seat-based. If generative design tools let customers do the same engineering with fewer licences, the installed-base moat protects the relationship but not the revenue per customer.
03
Sector-level de-rating. No company release was found for the September 4–8 drop, and a same-day headline tied it to sector-wide selling. Software multiples are moving as a block on AI sentiment, and a 1.00 beta understates how fast PTC has moved with them this year.
Industry Commentary
AI and the seat-priced engineering-software model
CRITICAL QUESTION

Engineering software has long been sold per seat. The 2026 market question is whether AI-assisted design shrinks the number of seats a manufacturer needs faster than vendors can charge for the AI itself. PTC's answer is that its advantage sits "at the data and process level" — the model is only as useful as the product data it can read, and PTC holds that data. The evidence so far: API calls to Onshape from AI start-ups tripling, an MCP server for Onshape, a near-seven-figure ServiceMax AI deal cited as PTC's largest yet — and management's own caution that stand-alone AI revenue is a medium- to long-term opportunity. None of this yet shows up as a disclosed AI revenue or ARR figure.

Focus by subtraction
STRATEGIC SHIFT

Selling Kepware and ThingWorx to TPG ended PTC's bet on industrial IoT. The logic is concentration: two product groups, CAD and PLM, under one "Intelligent Product Lifecycle" story, with the proceeds returned through buybacks rather than redeployed into acquisitions. The cost is a year of messy comparisons — revenue down, EPS up, cash flow down on divestiture taxes — that makes the income statement nearly useless for screening until FY2027.

The valuation reset
EMERGING TAILWIND

At $135.28 PTC trades at 16.5x consensus FY2026 EPS and a 6.4% FCF yield. The bull framing: a high-margin, high-retention system of record growing ARR at 9% with a shrinking share count is being priced like a no-growth business because its whole sector is out of favour. The bear framing: the discount is not a mistake — it prices the risk that 9% is peak growth for a seat-based model entering an AI transition, and the stock's 2025 high was a bid that never came. The adjudicator is dated and reported: Q4 FY2026 net new ARR on November 4.
