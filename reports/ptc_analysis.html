01 Company Overview

Somewhere in every aircraft, tractor, pump and pacemaker there is a digital twin of the thing — a 3D model, a bill of materials, a change history of who approved which revision and when. PTC sells the software that holds that record. Creo and Onshape are its CAD tools; Windchill, Arena, Codebeamer and ServiceMax sit on the PLM side. FY2026 (year ended September 30, 2026) revenue was $2.73B, down 0.2%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $8.93 and free cash flow of $872.7M.

The number management pointed investors to is not revenue. PTC sells multi-year subscriptions, and under the accounting rules a chunk of each term licence is booked up front — so reported revenue swings with contract length, not with demand. The cleaner gauge is ARR: $2.545B at constant currency on September 30, 2026, up 9.7%, above the top of the 9–9.5% guide. Q4 FY2026 revenue, by contrast, fell 25% to $673M, with licence revenue of $267M against $484M a year earlier while support and cloud revenue held at $382M. Two businesses left in March: PTC sold Kepware and ThingWorx — its industrial-connectivity and IoT units — to TPG for $523M in cash, and turned the after-tax proceeds into buybacks.

Then the question changed. On October 5, 2026, French energy-management group Schneider Electric agreed to buy PTC for $205 a share in cash, about $22.6B of equity value and 42.3% above the $144.03 close on October 2. The stock closed that day at $192.26 and has closed between $192.26 and $195.54 ever since. It had been a long road: in July 2025 reports said Autodesk was weighing a bid and the shares jumped; Autodesk walked away within days, the month still ended at a $214.81 close, and by June 2026 the stock had fallen 47% to $113.61 amid the market's doubts about what AI does to software priced per user seat. A buyer at $195.07 is no longer betting on software but on a closing: $9.93 (5.1%) to the offer price, against $51.04 (26.2%) back to the last pre-deal close if the deal breaks.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

PLM system-of-record lock-in Decades of engineering data Recurring subscription base 83.3% gross margin Defense & regulated-industry installs Cloud-native CAD (Onshape)

The moat is data gravity. A PLM system is not an app a manufacturer uses; it is where the company's product history lives — every revision, every approval, every supplier part number, often with regulatory audit trails attached. It is why ARR kept growing at high single digits through a fiscal year in which the share price fell 30.9% ($203.02 at the end of September 2025 to $140.30 a year later), and it is what Schneider Electric is paying for: about 21 times estimated 2027 adjusted operating profit by its own count, or 13 times once it reaches its targeted savings. The vulnerabilities below now sort into two groups: what could stop the deal, and what the business would look like if it did.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The deal needs US national-security clearance. Closing requires a yes from holders of a majority of the outstanding shares, the end of the US antitrust waiting period, and approval by CFIUS. PTC sells to defense contractors and Schneider is French; Investing.com's earnings preview, citing analysts, said PTC's US defense and federal business could draw scrutiny during the review. The proxy says the two companies have filed a draft CFIUS notice and an antitrust filing. The merger agreement sets no fee for Schneider to pay if the deal falls through.
02
$205 is close to a ceiling. The proxy's background section shows Schneider going from $190 on September 8 to $200 on September 17 to $205 on September 19, which it called its "best and final offer". An unnamed Company Y proposed $180–190 in cash and stock on August 31, and Company Z declined. A rival would also have to cover the $700M fee PTC owes Schneider if it leaves for a better offer.
03
The waiting has a cost. The merger's deadline for completion is April 4, 2027, and it can extend in three-month steps to January 4, 2028 if regulatory conditions are still open. Holders earn no dividend, get no buybacks, and now get no earnings calls or guidance from the company.
04
If the deal fails, the old questions return. Net new ARR of about $97M in Q4 answered part of the 2026 doubt about new business, but one quarter does not settle it, and AI seat risk is untouched. On the Q3 call management spoke of early traction for its AI products and called stand-alone AI revenue a medium- to longer-term opportunity.
05
The balance sheet was used. Debt rose to $1,609.8M at September 30, 2026 from $1,197.4M a year earlier, with cash of $232.9M, as buybacks outran free cash flow. Debt to equity is 0.47.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A signed, all-cash offer at $205 for a stock at $195.07: 5.1% if it closes, about 5.7% annualised if that happens on September 30, 2027, with no dividend along the way. Schneider has a fully committed temporary bridge loan and both boards approved unanimously, and PTC owes Schneider a $700M fee if it leaves for a better offer. The business has kept delivering: ARR grew 9.7% at constant currency in FY2026, above the top of the guide, with about $97M of net new ARR in Q4 and full-year free cash flow of $872.7M. Evercore, in the proxy, values PTC at $160–$210 on projected future cash (a discounted-cash-flow range), so the offer sits near its top.

Bear Case

You are risking about $51 to make about $10. At $195.07, against $144.03 if the stock went back to its last pre-deal close, the market is pricing the deal at roughly 84% odds, before counting what the money could earn elsewhere (our arithmetic: $51.04 ÷ $60.97). The deal needs CFIUS clearance for a French buyer of software used by defense contractors, and the completion deadline can run to January 2028. The price is capped near $205 while the wait costs you interest. Holders no longer get guidance, calls or buybacks, and the stand-alone case behind the offer still rests on seat-priced software in an AI transition, with Q4 revenue down 25%.

Industry Commentary
AI and the seat-priced engineering-software model
CRITICAL QUESTION

Engineering software has long been sold per seat. The 2026 market question is whether AI-assisted design shrinks the number of seats a manufacturer needs faster than vendors can charge for the AI itself. PTC's answer is that its advantage sits at the level of data and process — the model is only as useful as the product data it can read, and PTC holds that data. The evidence so far: API calls to Onshape from AI start-ups tripling, a connector for AI coding tools in Onshape, a near-seven-figure ServiceMax AI deal cited as PTC's largest yet — and management's own caution that stand-alone AI revenue is a medium- to longer-term opportunity. None of this shows up as a disclosed AI revenue or ARR figure in the Q4 release.

Focus by subtraction
STRATEGIC SHIFT

Selling Kepware and ThingWorx to TPG ended PTC's bet on industrial IoT. The logic was concentration: two product groups, CAD and PLM, under one Intelligent Product Lifecycle story, with the proceeds returned through buybacks rather than redeployed into acquisitions. The cost was a year of messy comparisons, with revenue flat and GAAP EPS up 47% on a one-off gain. Schneider then bought the simpler company.

What $205 says about seat-priced software
MARKET SIGNAL

At the $144.03 close before the offer, the stock was 33% below its $214.81 high. Schneider agreed to pay 42.3% more, at 22.7 times FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS, and it did so with management projections in hand showing ARR of $2.78B in FY2027 and $3.42B in FY2029 (proxy, prepared by management before the deal). The proxy shows why the price looks the way it does: an unnamed Company X had proposed $228 a share in cash and stock in July 2025 before talks ended, and Company Y's proposal in August 2026 was $180–190.
