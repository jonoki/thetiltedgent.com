01 Company Overview

PayPal Holdings operates a two-sided digital payments network connecting consumers and merchants, spanning branded checkout (the orange-and-blue PayPal button a shopper actively chooses at checkout), unbranded card processing through Braintree (white-label acquiring that runs a large merchant's card payments invisibly, with no PayPal branding on the page), peer-to-peer transfer through Venmo, cross-border remittance through Xoom, and a set of adjacent products including PayPal Credit, Honey, Hyperwallet and Paidy. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $33.17B, up 4.32% year over year — the fourth consecutive year of decelerating growth from the +18.26% posted in 2021. The company carries a market capitalisation of $46.97B at the October 28, 2026 close (830.66M shares, 10-Q cover, × $56.55; our calculation), ranking it #254 in the S&P 500 — down from about $274B at the end of 2020 (companiesmarketcap.com) and roughly $360B at its 2021 peak (Reuters).

That last pair of numbers is the entire story. PayPal is not being analysed here as a fintech leader; it is being analysed as a broken-growth business. The stock has lost about three-quarters of its value since October 2021 (−75.7% from the October 2021 month-end close to the October 28, 2026 close) while revenue kept compounding, which means the market did not stop believing PayPal has a business — it stopped believing the business grows. The job of this report is to establish numerically whether the survivor is cheap or correctly priced.

2026 so far. Enrique Lores replaced Alex Chriss as CEO on March 1. On July 15 Reuters reported a $60.50-a-share offer, more than $53B in all, from Stripe and the private-equity firm Advent International; the stock closed 17.2% higher that day. Reuters later reported that PayPal's board saw the offer as inadequate, and on August 28 Bloomberg reported that the bidders had dropped their pursuit; the stock closed 12.7% lower. PayPal, Stripe and Advent declined to comment on the Bloomberg report. The third-quarter results on October 27 beat on profit and raised the full-year outlook, but revenue grew only 2% and volume through the PayPal button grew 1–2%. The stock closed 2.0% higher that day. The October 28 close of $56.55 is 6.5% below the reported $60.50 offer.

02 Core Businesses

Before the segments: three definitions carry this entire report, and retail commentary routinely conflates them.

Total Payment Volume (TPV) is the gross dollar value of payments processed across all of PayPal's rails — the top-line "how much money moved" number. Transaction take rate is transaction revenue divided by TPV: the fraction of each processed dollar PayPal keeps as revenue. Transaction margin dollars is transaction revenue minus transaction expense and transaction credit losses — the actual gross profit generated by moving money, and the single most important number management guides on. TPV can rise while take rate falls, and the product of the two can rise while transaction margin dollars stall. Branded checkout means the shopper consciously selects PayPal and PayPal owns the customer relationship — high take rate. Unbranded processing means Braintree quietly acquires card payments for a large merchant, competing directly with Adyen, Stripe and Fiserv on price — very thin take rate. Active accounts counts accounts that transacted at least once in the trailing twelve months.

Q3 2026 operating metrics. PayPal discloses these only in its earnings release, call and 10-Q. For the quarter to September 30, 2026 (release, 27 Oct 2026): TPV $487.1B, +6% (+7% currency-neutral); transaction margin dollars $4.0B, +2% (+3% excluding interest on customer balances); active accounts 440M, +0.6%; transactions per active account 60.8 over the trailing twelve months, +6%. On the call the CEO put branded checkout volume growth at 1–2% for four quarters running, the CFO said Braintree volume grew 10%, and Venmo volume grew by double digits for an eighth straight quarter. Volume up 6%, margin dollars up 2%: that gap is the mix shift this report is about. Reported gross profit (table below) is the closest audited proxy for transaction margin dollars.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
2-SIDED NETWORK STORED CREDENTIAL MERCHANT ACCEPTANCE RISK & FRAUD DATA BUYER PROTECTION TRUST CROSS-BORDER RAILS REGULATORY LICENSING

PayPal's moat is real but it is narrower than its scale suggests, and it is concentrated almost entirely in the branded checkout business. The durable assets are a stored payment credential held by hundreds of millions of consumers, near-universal merchant acceptance in online retail, two decades of proprietary fraud and risk data that lets PayPal underwrite transactions competitors would decline, and — the underrated one — buyer protection, which is the actual reason many consumers choose the PayPal button on an unfamiliar merchant's site. That trust asset is genuinely hard to replicate. Against that, the unbranded Braintree business has essentially no moat at all: it is a commodity acquiring service sold on price and integration quality against extremely well-capitalised competitors, and it is the part of the business growing fastest. A company whose moated segment is losing relative share while its unmoated segment grows is, structurally, a company whose blended economics deteriorate even when total volume rises. That is precisely what the gross-margin series shows.

Checkout disintermediation by platform owners. Apple Pay is the default on the device the consumer already holds; Shop Pay is native to the merchant's own storefront; Click to Pay is owned by the card networks whose rails PayPal rides. None of these needs to win outright to damage PayPal — each simply needs to take incremental share of the click. Every branded checkout click lost is replaced, at best, by a much thinner unbranded processed dollar.

Structural take-rate compression. The revenue-versus-gross-profit divergence documented above (6.9% vs 3.7% four-year CAGR) is not a one-off; it is what happens when the mix shifts toward large-merchant processing. Absent a genuine reversal in branded volume growth, this is a permanent drag on transaction margin dollars regardless of how large TPV becomes.

Flat account base. Growth in a two-sided network normally comes from adding accounts. PayPal abandoned its long-term account-target framework in 2022 and has since managed the base rather than grown it, leaning on transactions per account instead. Active accounts were 440 million at September 30, 2026, up 0.6% in a year, while transactions per account rose 6%. Engagement per user is the better metric for revenue, but a flat or shrinking account base removes the compounding optionality that justified the old multiple.

Regulatory and credit exposure. PayPal holds customer balances, extends consumer and merchant credit, and operates across dozens of licensing regimes. Interest earned on balances is levered to the policy rate; credit losses are levered to the consumer cycle. Neither is under management's control, and both flow directly into transaction margin dollars. In September 2026 PayPal received a civil investigative demand from the FTC (a formal request for documents and answers) about how it advertises and manages fees and purchase protection; it says it is cooperating (Q3 2026 10-Q).

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

FY2022 is the obvious distortion. Net income collapsed to $2,419M (EPS $2.09) from $4,169M ($3.52) in FY2021 even though revenue grew 8.46% and operating income fell only 6.4%, to $4,039M. A gap that wide between operating income and net income points to large non-operating charges rather than a business event. Every growth rate computed off a 2022 base in third-party screens is distorted by this.

The trailing period is the second distortion, and it runs the other way. TTM (September 2026) net income of $4,727M is 9.7% below FY2025's $5,233M, but TTM EPS of $5.24 is only 3.1% below FY2025's $5.41. The 6.5 percentage-point difference is share count, not performance. Anyone reading the EPS line alone would conclude earnings are roughly flat; earnings are in fact down nearly ten percent. This is the single most important adjustment in the report and it is developed further in the buyback discussion in Section 08.

The same effect flattered FY2025. Net income rose 26.19% while EPS rose 35.59% — implying roughly a 6.9% reduction in the diluted share count. Roughly nine of those thirty-six percentage points of EPS growth were bought, not earned.

The two EPS lines now point in opposite directions. PayPal guides 2026 non-GAAP EPS (its adjusted figure, which leaves out items such as restructuring and stock-based pay) to about $5.60, up from $5.31 in 2025, while its GAAP guidance is about $5.07, down from $5.41. The guidance assumes about $570M of adjustments for the year; Q3 alone carried $255M of "restructuring and other" charges (10-Q). The headline beat is on the adjusted line.

In plain English: RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been. Above 70 the stock has risen persistently and may be stretched; below 30 it has fallen persistently and may be washed out. It says nothing about value.

Five-year trajectory. PYPL closed October 2021 at $232.59 and closed at $56.55 on October 28, 2026 — a price return of −75.7%. Dividends paid over the window total $0.56 per share (four $0.14 payments since the programme began in November 2025), so adding them back, not reinvested, gives about −75.4%. Over the same five years revenue grew from $25.37B (FY2021) to $34.30B (twelve months to September 2026), and earnings per share grew from $3.52 to $5.24. The business got bigger and more profitable per share while the equity lost three-quarters of its value. That is the definition of a de-rating (investors paying less for each dollar of earnings), and it is the reason valuation — not growth — is now the only question that matters here. The multiple compressed from about 66× FY2021 EPS at the October 2021 close to 10.8× trailing earnings today.

Relative performance. Over the same dates (October 29, 2021 close to October 28, 2026 close, price only) SPY, a fund that tracks the S&P 500, returned +68.2% and QQQ, which tracks the Nasdaq-100, +87.9% (Yahoo Finance daily closes). Against PYPL's −75.7% that is a gap of more than 140 percentage points. The underperformance is not concentrated in a single crash: on calendar-year closes the stock fell in every year the window touches except 2024 (2021 from the October close, 2026 to October 28), which is the signature of a steady de-rating rather than a shock. 08 Summary & Key Risks

THE VALUATION QUESTION, STATED DIRECTLY

PYPL trades at 10.79× trailing earnings ($56.55 ÷ $5.24 TTM GAAP EPS), 9.67× forward earnings, 7.81× EV/EBITDA (company value including net debt, over operating profit before depreciation and amortisation), 1.37× sales, and generates a 13.4% free cash flow yield ($7.58 FCF per share on a $56.55 price). On every one of those measures it is cheap in absolute terms and roughly a third of the Nasdaq-100's multiple. So: is a low multiple on slow-growing transaction margin dollars cheap, or is it a value trap (a stock that looks cheap and stays cheap because the business behind it is shrinking)?

The honest answer is that the multiple is correct for the growth rate, and the growth rate is the thing at risk. Consider the arithmetic. Consensus has 2026 revenue at $34.55B (+4.14%) and non-GAAP EPS at $5.54 (+4.30%) (stockanalysis, read 29 Oct 2026); PayPal itself guides non-GAAP EPS to about $5.60 and GAAP EPS to about $5.07, a 6.3% fall from 2025. A business growing adjusted earnings 4–5% and shrinking its GAAP earnings deserves a low multiple; 10.8× is not a mispricing, it is a fair price for stagnation. The PEG ratio of 1.43 makes the point — adjusted for growth, PYPL is not cheap relative to the market despite a headline P/E a third of it.

The deeper problem is what sits underneath even that modest EPS number. TTM net income of $4,727M is 9.7% below FY2025's $5,233M. TTM EPS of $5.24 is only 3.1% below FY2025's $5.41. The 6.5-point difference is the buyback. In Q3 alone GAAP net income fell 14% while weighted diluted shares fell from 960M to 858M (release). Strip out the repurchase and the operating business is shrinking in absolute profit, not merely growing slowly. On a four-year view the same wedge appears in the gross-profit line: FY2021→FY2025 revenue compounded at 6.9% while gross profit compounded at 3.7% — the take-rate compression from the unbranded mix, showing up exactly where theory says it should.

That is the value-trap mechanism in full. A buyback funded by a 13.4% FCF yield can hold EPS flat for several years while the underlying profit pool erodes. The multiple stays optically low the whole time, the "cheap" screen keeps firing, and the share price still goes nowhere because earnings per share are being manufactured rather than earned. The five-year chart is what that looks like: down 75.7% while EPS rose from $3.52 to $5.24.

What would settle it. One number: transaction margin dollars growing faster than TPV. If branded checkout volume reaccelerates, or if Venmo, value-added services and advertising scale enough to offset the Braintree mix, then gross profit growth converges back toward revenue growth, the buyback adds to per-share growth instead of making up for shrinking profit, and a 10.8× multiple on a business compounding high single digits is materially too cheap. If it does not, the buyback shrinks the company one share at a time and the multiple never rises. The first reading since the last edition came in on the wrong side: in Q3 2026 transaction margin dollars grew 2% while TPV grew 6%, and management expects branded checkout to stay at 1–2% in the fourth quarter. The raised outlook — about $15.7B of transaction margin dollars for 2026, per the CFO — rests on costs and the share count, not on the mix.

Bull Case

A 13.4% free cash flow yield, a 23.73% ROIC, only about $3.97B of net debt and a 9.78% total shareholder yield mean PayPal can return nearly a tenth of its market capitalisation to shareholders every year without straining the balance sheet — and at 10.8× earnings every dollar of that lifts EPS a lot. Cost discipline is real (headcount down 23% from the 2021 peak; operating margin up from 14.68% in FY2022 to 18.70% in FY2025; $700M of yearly savings on track by the end of 2026, per Reuters), and in October management raised its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to about $5.60. A buyer was reportedly willing to pay $60.50 a share in July. If branded checkout stabilises and Venmo's double-digit volume growth turns into revenue, gross-profit growth converges back to revenue growth and the multiple has room to expand from a third of the index.

Bear Case

Revenue growth has decelerated for four straight years (18.3% → 8.5% → 8.2% → 6.8% → 4.3%) and was 2% in Q3 2026, below what analysts expected; gross profit compounded at only 3.7% against revenue's 6.9%; TTM net income is 9.7% below FY2025 and PayPal's own GAAP EPS guidance for 2026 is down 6%. Branded checkout volume has grown 1–2% for four quarters. The mix shift toward unbranded Braintree volume is structural, not cyclical, and it converts headline volume growth into no profit growth. The buyback is masking absolute earnings decline, not amplifying growth. Apple Pay, Shop Pay and network wallets attack the only high-margin part of the business, and Venmo's revenue from its volume has yet to show in the consolidated numbers. 36 of 43 analysts rate it Hold or below, and the takeover bid that lifted the stock in July is gone.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Structural take-rate compression from the branded/unbranded mix. Braintree volume grows faster than branded checkout at a fraction of the take rate, so TPV and revenue can keep rising while transaction margin dollars stall — exactly the pattern visible in the 6.9% revenue versus 3.7% gross-profit four-year CAGRs. This is the single risk that determines whether 10.8× is cheap or correct, and it is not cyclical. Q3 2026 showed it again: TPV +6%, transaction margin dollars +2%.
02
Buyback-masked earnings decline. Shares outstanding fell 8.79% year over year, turning a 9.7% fall in trailing net income into a 3.1% fall in EPS. Screens and headlines will read the EPS line. If operating profit keeps eroding, the repurchase merely delays recognition while consuming the free cash flow that constitutes the entire bull case — and the accretion math stops working the moment the multiple falls further.
03
Checkout disintermediation by parties that own the distribution. Apple owns the device, Shopify owns the storefront, and Visa and Mastercard own the rail. None has to beat PayPal outright; each only needs to capture incremental clicks. Losses here hit the highest-margin dollars in the business and show up with a lag in the gross-margin line rather than in any single announcement — which is why the deterioration was visible in the FY2021–FY2023 margin series long before it was visible in revenue.
  Industry Commentary
The Commoditisation of Payment Acceptance
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Merchant acquiring has become a scale-and-price business. Stripe, Adyen, Fiserv and a long tail of processors compete for large-merchant volume on basis points, and the marginal economics approach the cost of the underlying interchange plus a thin spread. PayPal's participation in that market through Braintree is strategically defensible — it keeps PayPal present at large merchants and preserves the option to convert unbranded volume into branded checkout — but it is financially dilutive at the margin. The evidence is in the audited series: reported gross margin fell from 46.99% (FY2021) to 39.59% (FY2023), a 740 basis-point compression, during a period when revenue grew every single year. Growing revenue while losing 740bp of gross margin is the numerical fingerprint of commoditisation, and it recovered only partially (to 41.48% in FY2025; 40.55% over the twelve months to September 2026).
Distribution Now Beats Product in Consumer Payments
ONGOING HEADWIND
The decisive change since 2021 is that the parties controlling the surfaces where payment happens — operating systems, e-commerce platforms and card networks — have all launched their own checkout products. PayPal's historical advantage was that it was the only trusted, universally accepted alternative to typing a card number. That advantage was a product advantage. It is now competing against distribution advantages it cannot replicate: Apple Pay is pre-installed, Shop Pay is embedded in the merchant's own cart, Click to Pay is written into the network rules. PayPal's counter-assets — buyer protection, two decades of fraud data, and a stored credential base measured in the hundreds of millions — are genuine, but they defend a position rather than expand one. This is why the active-account base has been managed rather than grown since the 2022 abandonment of the account-target framework, and why transactions per account became the metric management chose to emphasise instead. Of the two, transactions per account is the one that drives revenue, while the account count is the one that drives the long-term optionality the old multiple was built on.
Bull vs Bear: What the Next Four Quarters Decide
EMERGING TAILWIND (CONDITIONAL)
Bull framing: the margin trough is behind it. Gross margin bottomed at 39.59% in FY2023 and has recovered to 41.48% (FY2025); operating margin rose from 14.68% (FY2022) to 18.70% (FY2025); ROIC is 23.73%; free cash flow is $6.30B against a $46.97B market capitalisation. Value-added services and the advertising initiative earn money from assets PayPal already owns, at little extra cost. On that path, 10.8× trailing earnings and 7.81× EV/EBITDA are very low for a business earning 24% on capital.

Bear framing: the recovery in margin was cost-cutting, and cost-cutting ends. Headcount is down 23% from the 2021 peak and cannot fall indefinitely; TTM operating income of $6,069M is already 2.2% below FY2025's $6,203M (stockanalysis basis) and TTM net income 9.7% below. Consensus has 2026 adjusted EPS growing 4.3%, and PayPal guides GAAP EPS down about 6%. On that path the low multiple is not an opportunity, it is an accurate quotation for a business whose profit pool is being competed away one basis point of take rate at a time.

The resolution is a single disclosure line. If transaction margin dollars grow faster than TPV over the next four quarters, the bull framing is right and the stock is materially mispriced. In Q3 2026 they did not: +2% against +6%. If they do not, the bear framing is right and no amount of buyback fixes it. Everything else in the PayPal debate — Venmo, crypto, agentic commerce, the checkout redesign — is commentary on that one number.
