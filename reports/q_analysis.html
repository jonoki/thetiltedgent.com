01 Company Overview

Most people have never heard of Qnity, and that is the point of it. It makes the consumables a chip has to pass through on its way to existing — CMP pads and slurries, photoresists, cleaning chemistries — plus the plating, thermal-interface and packaging materials that wire finished chips to boards. It is not a new business. It is DuPont's electronics arm, handed to DuPont shareholders as a separate company: the separation completed on November 1, 2025, one Qnity share for every two DuPont shares held on the October 22 record date, roughly 209 million shares in all. Regular trading on the NYSE under the single letter "Q" began on November 3, 2025, and the stock went straight into the S&P 500, taking Eastman Chemical's seat.

One year in, the listed history is short and loud. The stock's first month-end close was $81.09 (November 2025); it roughly doubled to a $163.31 June 2026 close, touched $177.28 on June 22, and has since fallen more than a quarter to $117.00. The results have gone the other way: Q3 2026 net sales rose 22% to $1.556B, adjusted EPS rose 55% to $1.46 against a Nasdaq consensus of $1.18, and on November 4 management raised full-year guidance again. The central question for a materials supplier is whether this is a cycle or a lasting change in what each chip needs — whether the industry's shift toward what management calls "shrink and stack" (smaller transistors plus advanced packaging, AI circuit boards and thermal management) has permanently raised the materials content per chip, or whether Qnity is riding one very strong spending wave that will ebb like every other one.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Approved materials at leading chip plants Consumable, per-layer revenue Advanced-node position (3nm, 2nm) Advanced-packaging content 30%+ adj. EBITDA margin Global footprint, ~10,000 staff

The moat in semiconductor materials is qualification. A fab that has tuned a process around a particular pad, slurry or resist does not swap it to save a few percent — the cost of a batch of spoiled chips dwarfs the materials bill. Once a material is designed into a node (a generation of chip manufacturing), it tends to ride that node for its life. That is why Qnity's margins sit above 30% at the EBITDA line and why the 25% growth in the advanced-nodes portfolio in Q3 matters: each qualification at 3nm and 2nm is a multi-year stream of repeat sales. The vulnerability is that qualification protects the position, not the volume. When chip and circuit-board production slows, consumption falls with it — and roughly 80% of the revenue sits in one region.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A cyclical business under a long-term growth story. Volume drove the growth — 22% for the company, 29% in Interconnect. Volume is exactly what reverses in a semiconductor downturn. The business's own history shows it: revenue fell 15.1% in 2023 (from $4.755B to $4.035B), before the AI ramp.
02
Asia concentration. About 80% of revenue comes from Asia Pacific. Trade restrictions, tariffs or a regional disruption would hit the majority of the business at once.
03
Standalone costs and leverage. The company carries about $4.0B of debt (net leverage about 1.7x) and booked $123M of transformation, integration and other charges in the first nine months of 2026, $83M of it for IT independence. GAAP EPS in Q3 was $0.87 against $1.46 adjusted — those charges, amortization and legacy costs account for the gap.
04
Input costs and seasonality. On the Q2 call management flagged about $20M of input-cost pressure from logistics and energy. It has not shown yet: Q3 gross margin was 47.8%, up 2.7 points from a year earlier. The new guide implies Q4 sales of $1.47–$1.53B (the full-year range minus the first nine months), below Q3's $1.556B, with CFO Ken Rizvi citing "normal seasonality on the consumer portion of our business".
05
Inherited chemical liabilities. Under its separation from DuPont, Qnity bears 44% of DuPont's after-tax share of certain PFAS ("forever chemical") liabilities. The Q3 10-Q carries $44M of indemnification liability for the North Carolina settlement of September 10, 2026 and $43M for New Jersey, and says exposure could range up to about $100M above the amount accrued at September 30.

Notable One-Off Items

The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is the main distortion. In Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $0.87 (down 14%) while adjusted EPS was $1.46 (up 55%). The release's bridge from GAAP to adjusted earnings for the quarter adds back $53M of transformation, integration and other charges, $50M of amortization of intangibles, $39M of indirect legacy costs and $5M of debt repricing losses and fees, less a $23M tax effect. The transformation line is the cost of standing up systems that used to belong to DuPont: $123M over nine months, $83M of it for IT independence. These should fade as the company becomes fully standalone, but until they do the trailing P/E overstates how expensive the stock is on underlying earnings. Capital returns are small: $50M of dividends and $75M of buybacks over nine months, the buybacks at an average $128.55 a share.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A materials supplier approved for use in the most advanced chip production — its advanced-nodes portfolio grew 25% in Q3 — with the fastest-growing piece of the business sitting where AI changes what goes into a chip: advanced packaging, AI circuit boards and thermal management. Q3 2026 delivered 22% organic growth, a 32.3% adjusted EBITDA margin and a raised guide. On the $4.95–$5.05 adjusted EPS guide the stock trades around 23.4x, falling to about 20.2x on 2027 consensus, and it is 28% below its June close while all ten covering analysts rate it a Buy with the lowest target 28% above the price. As separation costs roll off, GAAP earnings converge toward adjusted, and the 44.2x trailing P/E stops looking like the real number.

Bear Case

Strip out the AI narrative and this is a semiconductor-consumables business whose revenue fell 15.1% in 2023, now growing on volume — the component that reverses first in a downturn. It carries ~$4B of debt from its separation, ~80% of revenue from one region, an ROE of about 7%, a trailing P/E of 44.2x on GAAP earnings and possible PFAS costs beyond what it has accrued. The stock doubled in seven months and has since lost 28% from that close despite two beat-and-raise quarters, falling 7.36% the day after the latest one. Wall Street's unanimous Buy on a one-year-old stock has not been tested by a down cycle.

Top 3 Risks

01
A turn in the AI-hardware spending cycle. Interconnect grew 30% on a 29% volume increase. If AI server and advanced-packaging builds slow, that volume goes into reverse, and it is now almost half of sales.
02
Geographic concentration. About 80% of revenue from Asia Pacific leaves the company exposed to trade policy and regional disruption in a way a diversified industrial is not.
03
Standalone execution. A CFO in the job since October 1, $123M of transformation costs in nine months, about 1.7x net leverage and a short public track record. Any stumble in the stand-up shows directly in GAAP earnings and the gap to adjusted.
Industry Commentary
Shrink and stack
LONG-TERM TAILWIND

CEO Jon Kemp frames Qnity's position around the industry's shift "toward shrink and stack": performance gains now come from packaging chips together as well as from shrinking transistors. For a materials supplier that means more steps, more layers and more material per finished device. The Q3 numbers are consistent with that — the packaging-heavy Interconnect segment grew 30% while the wafer-side segment grew 15% — but one strong year is not proof of a permanent shift.

Spin-off mechanics
FADING DISTORTION

Spin-offs start life with a shareholder base that did not choose them, costs for systems they no longer share with the parent, and a balance sheet designed by the parent. Two of the three are measurable at Qnity: nine-month transformation, integration and other charges were $123M, and debt stands near $4B. These effects fade with time. Until they do, GAAP and adjusted earnings will tell different stories, and the reader should know which one they are looking at.
