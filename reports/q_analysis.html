01 Company Overview

Most people have never heard of Qnity, and that is the point of it. It makes the consumables a chip has to pass through on its way to existing — CMP pads and slurries, photoresists, cleaning chemistries — plus the plating, thermal-interface and packaging materials that wire finished chips to boards. It is not a new business. It is DuPont's electronics arm, handed to DuPont shareholders as a separate company: the separation completed on November 1, 2025, one Qnity share for every two DuPont shares held on the October 22 record date, roughly 209 million shares in all. Regular trading on the NYSE under the single letter "Q" began on November 3, 2025, and the stock went straight into the S&P 500, taking Eastman Chemical's seat.

Ten months in, the listed history is short and loud. The stock's first month-end close was $81.09 (November 2025); it more than doubled to a $163.31 June 2026 close on the AI-packaging trade, touched $177.28 intramonth, and has since given back a quarter of that to $121.82. The fundamentals did not follow the price down: Q2 2026 net sales rose 22% to $1.429B, adjusted EPS rose 53% to $1.19, and management raised full-year guidance on August 4. The central question for a materials supplier is whether this is a cycle or a mix shift — whether the industry's move from "shrink" to "stack" (advanced packaging, AI circuit boards, thermal management) has permanently raised the materials content per chip, or whether Qnity is riding one very strong capex wave that will ebb like every other one.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Qualified-in materials at leading fabs Consumable, per-layer revenue Advanced-node position (3nm, 2nm) Advanced-packaging content ~30% adj. EBITDA margin Global footprint, ~10,000 staff

The moat in semiconductor materials is qualification. A fab that has tuned a process around a particular pad, slurry or resist does not swap it to save a few percent — the cost of a yield excursion dwarfs the materials bill. Once a material is designed into a node, it tends to ride that node for its life. That is why Qnity's margins sit around 30% at the EBITDA line and why the 20%+ growth in the advanced-nodes portfolio matters: each qualification at 3nm and 2nm is a multi-year annuity. The vulnerability is that qualification protects the position, not the volume. When wafer starts or board builds slow, consumption falls with them — and roughly 80% of the revenue sits in one region.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Cyclical volume under a secular story. Volume drove the growth — 23% for the company, 28% in Interconnect. Volume is exactly what reverses in a semiconductor downturn. The business's own history shows it: revenue fell 15.1% in 2023 (from $4.755B to $4.035B), before the AI ramp.
02
Asia concentration. About 80% of revenue comes from Asia Pacific. Trade restrictions, tariffs or a regional disruption would hit the majority of the business at once.
03
Standalone costs and leverage. The company carries about $4.0B of debt (net leverage ~2.0x) and booked $70M of transformation and IT-independence costs in the first half of 2026. GAAP EPS in Q2 was $0.59 against $1.19 adjusted — separation costs are doing heavy lifting in the gap.
04
Input costs and mix. Management flagged ~$20M of input-cost pressure (logistics, energy), gross-margin pressure from mix and R&D, supply-chain dynamics in memory, and muted sequential Q4 growth on consumer-electronics seasonality.

Notable One-Off Items

The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is the main distortion. In Q2 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $0.59 (down 34%) while adjusted EPS was $1.19 (up 53%) — the difference is largely separation-related charges and $70M of transformation and IT-independence costs in the first half, the cost of standing up systems that used to belong to DuPont. These should fade as the company becomes fully standalone, but until they do the trailing P/E overstates how expensive the stock is on underlying earnings. Management also flagged an elevated Q3 2025 comparison base (~$40M of accelerated sales), which will flatter nothing in the next print. Capital returns are small: $34M of dividends and $50M of buybacks in the first half.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A materials supplier with qualified positions at the leading edge — advanced nodes up 20%+, adoption at 3nm, activity at 2nm — and the fastest-growing piece of the business sitting exactly where AI changes the bill of materials: advanced packaging, AI circuit boards and thermal management. Q2 2026 delivered 22% organic growth, a 30.2% adjusted EBITDA margin and a raised guide. On the $4.40–$4.60 adjusted EPS guide the stock trades around 27x, falling to ~22.6x on 2027 consensus, and it is 25% below its June close while all eight covering analysts rate it a Buy with the lowest target 23% above the price. As separation costs roll off, GAAP earnings converge toward adjusted, and the 43.7x trailing P/E stops looking like the real number.

Bear Case

Strip out the AI narrative and this is a semiconductor-consumables business whose revenue fell 15.1% in 2023, now growing on volume — the component that reverses first in a downturn. It carries ~$4B of debt from its separation, ~80% of revenue from one region, a ROE under 7%, and a trailing P/E of 43.7x on GAAP earnings. The stock doubled in seven months and has been cut by a quarter in three with no change in reported results, which says the holder base is trading momentum, not owning a franchise. Sell-side unanimity on a ten-month-old stock has not been tested by a down cycle.

Top 3 Risks

01
A turn in the AI-hardware capex cycle. Interconnect grew 30% on a 28% volume increase. If AI server and advanced-packaging builds slow, that volume goes into reverse, and it is now almost half of sales.
02
Geographic concentration. About 80% of revenue from Asia Pacific leaves the company exposed to trade policy and regional disruption in a way a diversified industrial is not.
03
Standalone execution. A new CFO from October 1, $70M of transformation costs in six months, ~2.0x net leverage and a short public track record. Any stumble in the stand-up shows directly in GAAP earnings and the gap to adjusted.
Industry Commentary
From "shrink" to "stack"
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Management frames its position around the industry's move from "shrink to stack": performance gains now come as much from packaging chips together as from shrinking transistors. For a materials supplier that means more steps, more layers and more material per finished device. The Q2 numbers are consistent with that — the packaging-heavy Interconnect segment grew 30% while the wafer-side segment grew 15.5% — but one strong year is not proof of a permanent shift.

Spin-off mechanics
FADING DISTORTION

Spin-offs start life with a shareholder base that did not choose them, costs for systems they no longer share with the parent, and a balance sheet designed by the parent. Two of the three are measurable at Qnity: first-half transformation and IT-independence costs were $70M, and debt stands near $4B. The third — how far the register has turned over from DuPont holders — was not sourced. These effects fade with time. Until they do, GAAP and adjusted earnings will tell different stories, and the reader should know which one they are looking at.
