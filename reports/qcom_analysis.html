01 Company Overview
Qualcomm designs the application processors, modems and radio-frequency front-ends that sit at the centre of most premium Android smartphones, and separately licenses the foundational 3G/4G/5G patent portfolio that nearly every handset maker on earth must pay for. In fiscal 2026, which ended September 27, 2026, revenue was $43.12B, down 2.6% YoY, and non-GAAP EPS fell to $10.54 from $12.03 as phone-chip sales shrank faster than car and industrial chips grew. The company is worth about $177B at the November 6, 2026 close.
The investment question is a race between two clocks. Apple, historically one of Qualcomm's largest customers, is replacing Qualcomm's modems with its own: phone-chip revenue fell 26% YoY in the September quarter, the 10-K now says Qualcomm expects Apple to primarily use its own modems, and CFO Akash Palkhiwala said on the November 4 call that December will no longer be the company's biggest quarter. In July management said Apple product revenue in fiscal 2027 would come in below its earlier guide of a little over $2B. Against that, automotive reached a first $2B quarter, up 89%, Amazon signed a multi-generation custom AI chip deal worth up to $60B over ten years, and Apple, Huawei and Transsion all renewed their patent licences. Management still targets $5B of data-centre revenue in fiscal 2027 and $15B in fiscal 2029, starting with more than $650M in the December quarter. The stock fell 9.49% on the first session after the results and rose 5.52% on the second.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Cellular Standard-Essential Patents Device-Level Royalty Model Modem-RF Systems Integration Premium Android Incumbency Auto Design-Win Lock-In Standards-Body Position Scale R&D (~$10B/yr)
Qualcomm's moat is two moats bolted together, and they are weakening at very different speeds. The licensing moat is the durable one: because Qualcomm's patents are essential to the 3G/4G/5G standards themselves, every handset maker pays a royalty on the device price whether or not it buys a Qualcomm chip — which is why QTL still earned a 70% pre-tax margin in a quarter when chip revenue shrank 8%, and why Apple renewed its licence even as it stops buying the chips. The chip moat is narrower and easier to attack: it rests on how hard it is to put a leading-edge processor, a 5G modem and the radio front-end into one power-efficient package, and on 20 years inside Samsung's and the Chinese makers' flagship phones. But "hard" is not "impossible," and Apple has now shown that a customer with enough money can build its own modem, ship it and scale it. Every other large customer is watching. What remains most intact is the royalty stream, now renewed with Apple, Huawei and Transsion with the next cycle starting in fiscal 2030, and the long lag on car-chip design wins, which gives that business years of visibility.
Key Vulnerabilities

1. Customer insourcing — Apple's exit. The 10-K names Apple and Samsung as the two customers each above 10% of fiscal 2026 revenue, and says Qualcomm expects Apple to primarily use its own modems, significantly reducing chip revenue. In July management said fiscal-2027 Apple product revenue would come in below its earlier guide of a little over $2B, and that growth in non-handset lines in fiscal 2027 should replace all of Apple's fiscal-2026 product revenue. Management says the timing is now fully built into its plan.

2. China and Android concentration. Outside Apple and Samsung, much of Qualcomm's premium chip revenue comes from Chinese phone makers — Xiaomi, Oppo, vivo, Honor — several of which run their own chip programmes and all of which are exposed to Chinese consumer demand and to US–China export policy. The 10-K says a significant portion of revenue comes from Chinese customers. Management called the June quarter the low point and says revenue has grown since; a Bernstein analyst asked on the call whether some of that is buying ahead of Qualcomm's price increases, and Amon said Qualcomm has been pricing newer platforms and new phones, which he said makes those increases less subject to buying ahead.

3. Licensing and regulatory risk. The Apple, Huawei and Transsion renewals remove the nearest risk, and the next cycle does not begin until fiscal 2030. What remains is the long-run pressure on the model: royalties only benefit from phone price increases below the per-device price cap, and Qualcomm has a long history of antitrust and patent-licensing litigation across the US, EU, China and Korea.

4. Data-centre execution against entrenched competitors. The $5B and $15B targets require Qualcomm to take real share in a market where NVIDIA holds an estimated 80%+ and Broadcom owns many of the custom-chip relationships. Two customers make up most of fiscal 2027's $5B, so a slip at either one shows up at once, and the High Bandwidth Compute products do not arrive until mid-2027. The Amazon warrant shows what a customer of that size can ask for.

Notable One-Off Items

The $5.7B tax valuation allowance release is the single most important distortion on this page. Fiscal 2026 GAAP results include a $5.7B income tax benefit, worth $5.32 a share, from releasing a valuation allowance on deferred tax assets. The result is an income tax benefit of $4.21B on $9.41B of pre-tax income: taxes added to reported earnings rather than subtracting from them. Any trailing GAAP metric on this page — EPS of $12.70, the 13.02x trailing P/E, the 31.6% net margin, the 52.8% ROE — is lifted by this item and will not repeat. Use the forward multiple and non-GAAP EPS for comparison purposes.

The mirror image sits in fiscal 2025. GAAP net income was $5,541M (EPS $5.01) despite higher revenue, because income tax expense was $7,122M. Comparing fiscal 2025 with fiscal 2026 GAAP EPS without adjusting for both sides produces a badly misleading growth rate.

Restructuring and a legal settlement. Restructuring and related charges rose to $332M in fiscal 2026 from $39M, substantially all severance, with most of it ($235M) in the fourth quarter. Separately, a $120M payment to a customer from a legal settlement reduced GAAP revenue in the quarter; the non-GAAP figures exclude it.

M&A. Qualcomm closed Alphawave in December 2025, setting aside about $2.3B of cash for the purchase, and Modular on July 28, 2026, paying up to 19.2M Qualcomm shares (8-K filed June 24, 2026). Goodwill rose to $16.37B from $11.36B.

Arm Holdings litigation. On October 13, 2026 a Delaware federal jury found that Arm breached its architecture licence with Qualcomm and interfered with Qualcomm's relationship with Meta, awarding nominal damages, and sided with Arm on four counts tied to a separate licence. A bench trial on Qualcomm's good-faith claim ran alongside it, and the remedies phase is pending. Amon said the case has no implications for Qualcomm's CPU roadmap. An earlier dispute over the Nuvia licence ended in final judgment for Qualcomm on September 30, 2025; Arm filed a notice of appeal on October 1, 2025.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 17x fiscal-2027 consensus earnings and collecting a 2.2% yield for a business that generated $8.65B of free cash flow in fiscal 2026, earns a 24.5% ROIC, and has just locked in its patent royalties: Apple, Huawei and Transsion renewed, with no major phone-maker renewal due for several years and management saying the rates held. The diversification is measurable: automotive +89% YoY to a first $2B quarter with a run rate near $8B, automotive and IoT together +36%, and Amazon's multi-generation custom chip deal worth up to $60B over ten years. If management delivers $5B of data-centre revenue in fiscal 2027 and its fiscal-2029 non-GAAP EPS target above $18, today's price is about 9x that year's earnings, with a $19.6B buyback authorisation (11% of market cap) shrinking the share count while you wait.

Bear Case

One of its largest customers is leaving. Phone-chip revenue fell 26% YoY, still makes up 49% of revenue, and management no longer expects December to be its biggest quarter. Fiscal-2027 consensus EPS of $9.60 sits below the $10.54 just earned. Gross margin fell to 53.1% from 55.4%, and custom data-centre chips will lower it further by management's own account. The offsets are concentrated: two hyperscalers provide most of the $5B, and Amazon got a 25-million-share warrant as part of its deal. Capex more than doubled to $2.49B and free cash flow fell to $8.65B from $12.82B. Reported earnings are inflated by a non-recurring $5.7B tax release; strip it out and the stock trades near 22x trailing earnings rather than 13x. Twenty-three of 37 analysts rate it Hold for a reason.

01
The Apple transition runs faster or deeper than guided. Management revised its iPhone share assumption down in July and now says the timing is fully built into its plan. Another revision, or Apple bringing radio front-end parts in-house as well as the modem, would break the fiscal-2027 growth claim and force a further reset of the earnings base.
02
The fiscal-2027 non-handset plan arrives late. The bull case rests on non-handset revenue growing more than 60% YoY in fiscal 2027 and exceeding 50% of QCT revenue. That requires two hyperscaler custom-chip programmes, the ~$8B automotive run rate and the IoT pipeline all to convert on time. Management says supply, not demand, is the limit above $5B, which makes a manufacturing slip the one to watch.
03
China demand and geopolitical exposure. Management says the June quarter was the low point for Android phones in China and revenue has grown since, while planning for flat to slightly lower handset units in 2027. With Chinese phone makers at the core of the remaining premium Android business, weak consumer demand, faster local chip substitution or new export controls or tariffs would hit the largest surviving revenue pool directly.
  Industry Commentary
Customer Insourcing — the Structural Threat to Merchant Silicon
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The defining dynamic in mobile semiconductors is that the largest customers have become the largest competitors. Apple's C-series modem programme has moved from a low-volume experiment to displacing Qualcomm across the iPhone line-up; the 10-K says Qualcomm expects Apple to primarily use its own modems. Qualcomm's own numbers show the damage: phone-chip revenue down 26% YoY in the September quarter, and Apple product revenue guided below a little over $2B for fiscal 2027. Patent royalties survive it, since Apple has renewed its licence. The read-across is broader than Apple: Samsung runs Exynos, Google runs Tensor, and several Chinese phone makers have their own chip programmes. Chip sellers depending on a handful of giants that are building their own face a one-way loss of content, and investors price that in long before it shows up in the accounts, which is what the 17.2x forward P/E against a ~28x peer average represents.
On-Device and Agentic AI — the Upgrade Cycle That Has Not Yet Materialised
EMERGING TAILWIND
The industry's stated hope is that running large AI models on the device — for latency, cost and privacy — forces a hardware refresh across phones, PCs and glasses, and that the NPU (neural processing unit) becomes the spec that sells the device. Qualcomm is well placed for that world: it sells premium Android processors, has the Snapdragon X line in PCs, and supplies chips for smart glasses. On the November call Amon said the first phones built around AI agents launched on Snapdragon, and that leading AI companies are targeting phones by 2028 able to run 100-billion-parameter models locally with all-day battery life. The problem is timing: management still plans for flat to slightly lower handset units in 2027, so the AI refresh has not yet shown up in units. This is a real tailwind with an unproven arrival date.
China Handset Demand and Geopolitical Risk — Where Bull and Bear Actually Collide
ONGOING HEADWIND
With Apple content shrinking, Chinese Android phone makers become a larger share of a smaller pie — and that is both the bull case and the bear case. Management says channel inventories remain thin, the June quarter was the low point, and revenue has grown into the September and December quarters; a Bernstein analyst asked on the call whether some of that is buying ahead of price increases. The bull reading: China troughed, Apple is a known and now-quantified drag, non-handset revenue grows more than 60% in fiscal 2027, and you own $8.65B of free cash flow and a 70%-margin royalty stream at 17.2x forward earnings with a buyback worth 11% of the market cap. The bear reading: the China recovery could be pre-buying, the Apple guide was already cut once, the fiscal-2027 offsets are concentrated in two customers, reported earnings are lifted by a one-time $5.7B tax release, and export-control or tariff escalation could remove the remaining growth pool quickly. The stock's 52-week range of $121.99 to $259.92 is the market refusing to choose between these two readings.
