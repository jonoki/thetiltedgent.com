01 Company Overview
Qualcomm designs the application processors, modems and radio-frequency front-ends that sit at the centre of most premium Android smartphones, and separately licenses the foundational 3G/4G/5G patent portfolio that nearly every handset maker on earth must pay for. The company reported fiscal 2025 revenue of $44.28B, up 13.7% YoY, but the TTM figure has since flattened to $44.07B (+1.9%) as the handset business rolls over. It is the world's 125th most valuable listed company by market capitalisation at roughly $173B — a rank that has gone essentially nowhere in five years while the broader semiconductor complex re-rated violently higher.
The investment question is unusually binary right now. Qualcomm's single largest product customer is insourcing the one component Qualcomm sold it, and management said on the July 29, 2026 call that its share of the next iPhone launch will be "materially lower than our prior estimate of 20%", with Apple-related revenue falling roughly 50% sequentially from the September to the December quarter and an EPS headwind exceeding $1.50 in fiscal 2027. Against that, automotive grew 61% YoY to a record $1.59B in the June quarter and management now targets $40B of non-handset revenue by fiscal 2029 — roughly double the target set in November 2024 — plus a brand-new data-centre business guided to $5B in FY27 and $15B in FY29. The stock is priced as though the first story wins.
Chief Executive Officer
Cristiano R. Amon
President and CEO since June 2021, a Qualcomm engineer since 1995 and formerly president of the QCT chip division. He is the architect of the diversification strategy — automotive, PC, industrial IoT and now data centre — and framed the 2026 data-centre entry as "the ideal and logical time for Qualcomm to enter the market as agentic workloads are reshaping AI economics."

Qualcomm licenses its 3G/4G/5G standard-essential patent portfolio to essentially every handset manufacturer, collecting a royalty on the wholesale price of the device regardless of whose chip is inside. QTL generated $881M of pre-tax profit on $1,278M of revenue — a 69% EBT margin — and contributes a wildly disproportionate share of consolidated earnings on 13% of revenue. Crucially, this stream is largely insulated from the Apple chip loss: Apple pays QTL royalties under a separate licence even when it uses its own modem. Management flagged one structural limit worth understanding: royalties benefit from handset average selling price increases only below the contractual per-device price cap, so above-cap ASP inflation provides no QTL uplift. The renewal cycle on the major licence agreements is the recurring risk here, and each renegotiation is a step-down risk rather than a step-up opportunity.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Cellular SEP Portfolio Device-Level Royalty Model Modem-RF Systems Integration Premium Android Incumbency Auto Design-Win Lock-In Standards-Body Position Scale R&D (~$9B/yr)
Qualcomm's moat is genuinely two moats bolted together, and they are weakening at very different speeds. The licensing moat is the durable one: because Qualcomm's patents are essential to the 3G/4G/5G standards themselves, every handset maker pays a royalty on the device price whether or not it buys a Qualcomm chip — which is why QTL still earned a 69% pre-tax margin in a quarter when the chip business shrank 5%. The chip moat is narrower and more contestable: it rests on the fact that integrating a leading-edge application processor, a 5G modem and the RF front-end into one power-optimised package is genuinely hard, and on 20 years of incumbency inside Samsung's and the Chinese OEMs' flagship programmes. But "hard" is not "impossible," and Apple has now demonstrated over roughly two product cycles that a well-capitalised customer can build its own modem, ship it, and scale it. Every other large customer is watching that demonstration. The moat that remains most intact — and least appreciated in the current multiple — is that the royalty stream survives the chip loss, and that automotive design wins locked in today are effectively contracted revenue for the 2029–2032 window.
Key Vulnerabilities

1. Customer insourcing — the Apple cliff. Qualcomm's 10-Q shows two customers at 23% and 20% of FQ3'26 revenue. Management guided Apple-related revenue down roughly 50% sequentially into the December quarter and quantified a fiscal-2027 EPS headwind of more than $1.50 — on a fiscal-2026 consensus base of roughly $10.50, that is a mid-teens percentage hit to earnings from one customer decision. The prior working assumption of 20% modem share in the next iPhone is now explicitly stale to the downside.

2. China and Android concentration. Outside Apple and Samsung, the bulk of Qualcomm's premium chip revenue comes from Chinese OEMs — Xiaomi, Oppo, vivo, Honor — several of which run their own silicon programmes and all of which are exposed to Chinese consumer demand and to US–China export policy. Management called the June quarter the "low point" for the China handset market, which is an admission of how much the near-term print depends on a single geography's inventory cycle.

3. Licensing renewal and regulatory risk. QTL is a renegotiation business, and the arithmetic of each renewal is asymmetric — licensees push rates down, never up. Management also flagged that royalties only benefit from handset ASP increases below the per-device price cap, capping the natural growth of the annuity. Qualcomm has a long history of antitrust and FRAND litigation across the US, EU, China and Korea, and the licensing model is a standing regulatory target.

4. Data-centre execution risk against entrenched incumbents. The $5B FY27 / $15B FY29 data-centre targets require Qualcomm to take meaningful share in a market where NVIDIA holds an estimated 80%+ and Broadcom owns the custom-ASIC relationships. Two hyperscaler wins is a start, not a franchise, and the first HBC solutions do not ship until mid-2027. If those targets slip, the diversification bridge over the Apple gap gets materially longer.

Notable One-Off Items

The $5.7B tax valuation allowance release is the single most important distortion on this page. Per the FQ3'26 10-Q, Qualcomm released a $5.7 billion valuation allowance on federal deferred tax assets after Treasury Notice 2026-07 allowed it to reduce corporate alternative minimum tax (CAMT) exposure using previously capitalised domestic R&D expenses, eliminating what it had expected to be perpetual CAMT exposure. The result is an annual effective tax rate for fiscal 2026 of a 40% benefit — i.e. taxes are adding to reported earnings rather than subtracting from them. Any trailing GAAP metric on this page — TTM EPS of $8.59, the 19.29x trailing P/E, the 21.0% net margin, the 33.8% ROE — is flattered by this item and will not repeat. Use the forward multiple and non-GAAP EPS for comparison purposes.

The mirror image sits in fiscal 2025. FY2025 GAAP net income was $5,541M (EPS $5.01) against FY2024's $10,142M (EPS $8.97) despite revenue rising 13.7% and operating income rising to $12,394M — a gap consistent with a large prior-year tax charge now being reversed. Comparing FY25 to FY26 GAAP EPS without adjusting for both sides of this item produces a badly misleading growth rate.

Restructuring. Modest: $68M in FQ3'26 and $97M for the nine months, substantially all severance. Not material to the earnings picture, but the direction of travel is a company trimming headcount in the handset organisation while hiring into data centre.

M&A. Qualcomm completed the acquisition of Modular Inc. during the quarter to strengthen AI software capabilities for the data-centre push. Terms were not disclosed in the materials reviewed.

Arm Holdings litigation — status not verified. The Arm v. Qualcomm dispute over the Nuvia architecture licence has been a recurring overhang. The current status of this litigation could not be verified from a live source in this build. Do not assume a resolution in either direction; check the latest 10-Q "Legal Proceedings" section and court dockets before assigning it any value or risk weight.

Five-year trajectory. Qualcomm has been a spectacular round trip and a mediocre investment. The stock finished 2021 near $183 on the back of a 5G upgrade supercycle and a fiscal-2021 revenue year of $33.6B (+42.7%), then lost roughly 40% of its market value in 2022 as the handset cycle inverted — fiscal-2023 revenue fell 19.0% to $35.8B and the shares bottomed near $107 in October 2023. The 2024 recovery was real but incomplete: revenue rebounded 8.8% in FY24 and 13.7% in FY25 to $44.28B, yet the shares ended 2025 around $170 — still below the December 2021 level four years later. From the Aug 17, 2021 reference point to the Aug 17, 2026 close of $162.18, the price return is roughly +9%, or something in the region of +22% total return including reinvested dividends. Over the same window the semiconductor complex multiplied several times over. Qualcomm did not participate in the AI trade until it tried to join it in 2026 — and the market's verdict on that attempt is currently a 38% drawdown from the June high.
Relative performance. Against the PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) and the NASDAQ-100, QCOM has been a persistent and severe underperformer over the full five years — the entire semiconductor re-rating of 2023–2026 was driven by AI datacentre exposure that Qualcomm did not have. Its 19.3x trailing and 17.9x forward multiples versus estimated peer-group averages near 38x and 28x are the market's quantification of that gap. The one window of outperformance was the spring 2026 data-centre rally, when the stock roughly doubled off the $121.99 low to a $259.92 high before giving back the majority of it. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 17.9x forward earnings and collecting a 2.3% yield for a business generating $10.4B of free cash flow, earning a 25.6% ROIC, and holding a licensing annuity that earns a 69% pre-tax margin and survives the loss of Apple's chip business entirely. The diversification is measurable, not hypothetical: automotive +61% YoY to a record $1.59B with the exit-FY26 run-rate target raised to ~$7B, IoT +9%, a >$7B industrial design-win pipeline, and non-handset QCT revenue up 28% YoY. If management's FY27 bridge holds — non-handset growth above 60% offsetting the Apple decline, $5B of data-centre revenue, and a path to >$18 FY29 non-GAAP EPS — the stock is trading at a single-digit multiple of its target year, with a $20.6B buyback authorisation (12% of market cap) shrinking the share count while you wait.

Bear Case

The largest customer is leaving, and management has now conceded it is leaving faster than they thought: Apple share of the next iPhone materially below the prior 20% estimate, Apple revenue down ~50% sequentially into the December quarter, and a fiscal-2027 EPS headwind exceeding $1.50 on a ~$10.50 base. Handsets — still 51% of revenue — fell 20% YoY, the total handset market is guided down "low teens," and the remaining Android exposure is concentrated in Chinese OEMs that are themselves building silicon. The offsets are back-end-loaded and unproven: the FY29 data-centre target requires taking ~5% share from NVIDIA and Broadcom, and the first HBC products do not ship until mid-2027. Meanwhile the reported earnings that make the multiple look cheap are inflated by a non-recurring $5.7B tax valuation-allowance release. Twenty-three of 37 analysts rate it Hold for a reason.

01
The Apple transition runs faster or deeper than guided. Management has already revised its iPhone share assumption downward once and described FY27 Apple revenue as "much lower" than previously guided. A second revision — or Apple insourcing RF front-end content on top of the modem — would break the FY27 growth claim outright and force a further reset of the earnings base.
02
The FY27 non-handset bridge fails to arrive on schedule. The entire bull case rests on non-handset revenue growing more than 60% YoY in fiscal 2027 and exceeding 50% of QCT revenue. That requires the two hyperscaler custom-silicon programmes, the ~$7B automotive run-rate and the IoT pipeline all to convert on time. Semiconductor design-win-to-revenue slippage is the norm, not the exception, and a one-to-two-quarter delay in any of the three leaves a visible earnings hole.
03
China demand and geopolitical exposure. Management has explicitly called the June quarter the low point for the Chinese handset market and guided to a double-digit sequential recovery — a forecast, not a fact. With Chinese OEMs representing the core of the remaining premium-Android franchise, any combination of weak Chinese consumer demand, accelerated domestic silicon substitution, or new export-control or tariff measures hits the largest surviving revenue pool directly.
  Industry Commentary
Customer Insourcing — the Structural Threat to Merchant Silicon
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The defining dynamic in mobile semiconductors is that the largest customers have become the largest competitors. Apple's C-series modem programme has moved from a low-volume experiment to displacing Qualcomm across the iPhone line-up, and Qualcomm has now quantified the damage: share of the next launch "materially lower" than the prior 20% estimate, Apple revenue down roughly 50% sequentially from the September to the December quarter, and a fiscal-2027 EPS headwind above $1.50. Qualcomm's 10-Q shows customer concentration at 23% and 20% of revenue. The read-across is broader than Apple: Samsung runs Exynos, Google runs Tensor, and several Chinese OEMs have domestic silicon programmes. Merchant chip vendors selling into a handful of vertically integrating giants face a permanent, one-way erosion of content — and the market prices that erosion into the multiple long before it shows up in the P&L, which is exactly what the 17.9x forward P/E versus a ~28x peer average represents.
On-Device and Agentic AI — the Upgrade Cycle That Has Not Yet Materialised
EMERGING TAILWIND
The industry's stated hope is that running large models locally — for latency, cost and privacy — forces a hardware refresh across phones, PCs and glasses, and that the NPU (neural processing unit) becomes the spec that sells the device. Qualcomm is arguably the best-positioned company for that world: it ships the highest-volume premium NPUs in Android, has the Snapdragon X franchise in PCs with the Microsoft "Project Solara" enterprise collaboration and growing Chromebook share, and supplies the Snapdragon START smart-glasses reference platform into a category shipping roughly 600 million eyewear units a year. Cristiano Amon's framing — "agentic workloads are reshaping AI economics" — is also the justification for the data-centre entry, since inference cost pressure is what creates room for a challenger. The problem is timing: the handset market is still guided down "low teens," so the AI refresh has not yet shown up in units. This is a real tailwind with an unproven arrival date.
China Handset Demand and Geopolitical Risk — Where Bull and Bear Actually Collide
ONGOING HEADWIND
With Apple content shrinking, Chinese Android OEMs become a larger share of a smaller pie — and that is simultaneously the bull case and the bear case. Management says channel inventories have "thinned down," that the June quarter was "the low point," and guides to double-digit sequential China growth in the September quarter; they also note Chinese OEM memory sourcing is running consistent with historical trends, implying the memory-cost squeeze on bill-of-materials budgets should ease. The bull reading: China troughed, Apple is a known and now-quantified drag, non-handset revenue compounds at 60%+ into FY27, and you own $10.4B of free cash flow and a 69%-margin royalty annuity at 17.9x forward earnings with a 12%-of-market-cap buyback running. The bear reading: the China recovery is a management forecast, the Apple guide has already been cut once, the FY27 offsets are all back-end-loaded into businesses Qualcomm does not yet lead, reported earnings are propped up by a one-time $5.7B tax release, and export-control or tariff escalation could remove the remaining growth pool overnight. The stock's 52-week range of $121.99 to $259.92 — a 113% spread — is the market openly refusing to choose between these two readings.
