01 Company Overview

Start with one number: 39.0%. That was Ferrari's EBITDA margin in Q2 2026 (€755M on €1,938M of revenue), after 38.8% for FY2025 (€2.77B on €7.15B). Mass-market carmakers do not earn that. Luxury houses do. Ferrari builds cars, but it prices like a luxury house: it ships fewer cars than it could sell, so the waiting list does the selling and the price sticks. FY2025 revenue was €7,146M, up 7.0%, with diluted EPS of €8.96. By market value it is the #6 company in Italy (companiesmarketcap's country ranking, the home market of the FTSE MIB) and #342 globally (companiesmarketcap, which shows $73.07B). On 175.92M shares outstanding (StockAnalysis) × $416.13, the market cap is $73.21B.

The premise to check is scarcity pricing: fewer cars, a richer mix, and a multiple priced for luxury rather than autos. The numbers support it. In Q2 2026 Ferrari delivered 3,366 cars, 3.7% fewer than a year earlier, yet Cars and spare parts revenue rose 8% and EBIT rose 10%. Management says the order book "entirely covers 2027" (CEO Benedetto Vigna, July 30, 2026). Measured from June 30, 2026, that is roughly 18 months of production already spoken for. The market pays about 39x trailing earnings. Toyota trades at 8.3x, Porsche AG at 13.3x and LVMH at 17.8x (companiesmarketcap, TTM). The debate is not whether the business is excellent. It is whether a multiple more than twice LVMH's can hold when growth has slowed to single digits. The October 2025 Capital Markets Day tested exactly that, and the stock lost 15% in one session.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Deliberate under-supply Order book covering 2027 Personalisation pricing Special Series allocation Scuderia Ferrari / F1 heritage Brand licensing & lifestyle Anchor-holder control (48.5% votes)

The moat is allocation power. Ferrari chooses who can buy which car, and the most limited models (Special Series, supercars like the F80) go to existing clients. The waiting list therefore enforces loyalty, and new buyers have to work their way up. That is why the order book can run into 2027 while Q2 deliveries fall, and why revenue per car rises when volume does not. The same mechanism explains the valuation. A carmaker's earnings rise and fall with the economic cycle. Ferrari's earnings behave more like a luxury house's, where demand runs ahead of supply at the top of the market, so pricing holds. The weakness is that scarcity pricing works only while the brand stays wanted. Everything that protects the brand (F1 results, product hits, discipline on volume) is also a cost line.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The multiple is the risk. At ~39x trailing and ~37x forward earnings, the stock prices in steady mix-led compounding. When the October 2025 Capital Markets Day set a 2030 revenue target of €9B (Capital Brief puts the implied revenue growth at about 4.9% a year), the Milan line fell as much as 16.18% intraday and closed 15.41% lower at €354 in a single session. Nothing in the business had broken. The market simply re-priced the growth rate.
02
Electrification cuts both ways. The 2030 plan cut the full-electric share of production to 20%, from 40% in the 2022 plan, with hybrids and combustion cars at 40% each. The first electric Ferrari, the Luce, is now fully presented. Whether collectors pay combustion-level prices for an electric Ferrari is untested. "An addition, not a transition" is a hedge, not an answer.
03
Geography and FX. Q2 2026 Americas deliveries fell 206 units (−21%), and Mainland China/HK/Taiwan fell 89 (−32%). Management calls these planned. A reporter in euros that sells in dollars and yen also carries translation risk, which the company hedges. 2026 guidance is "based on current visibility on the Middle East crisis effects", a region inside Ferrari's EMEA reporting area.
04
Control is not contestable. Exor and the Piero Ferrari Trust hold 48.5% of votes through loyalty shares. That keeps the brand discipline stable. It also means no takeover premium will ever be paid, and minority holders have no real say on strategy.

Notable One-Off Items

Ferrari reports no adjustments: adjusted EBITDA, EBIT, net profit and EPS "equaled the reported ones" in the periods presented. Three items still flatter or distort the numbers. First, temporary lower D&A during the model change-over lifted the Q2 2026 EBIT margin to 31.2%. Management guides D&A higher as new models enter production. Second, the Patent Box tax benefit lowered the Q2 effective tax rate to 23.0%. Third, buybacks and cancellations: on July 16, 2026 Ferrari cancelled 16,644,606 treasury common shares and 6,686,115 special voting shares. It is executing the second tranche of a ~€3.5B buyback programme running to 2030, announced at the October 2025 Capital Markets Day, and reports purchases weekly (the latest on September 21, 2026). Share count fell 0.97% YoY, so per-share growth runs slightly ahead of profit growth.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Scarcity pricing is working in the numbers. Q2 2026 deliveries fell 3.7%, yet revenue rose 8% (11% at constant currency), EBIT rose 10%, and the EBITDA margin held at 39.0%. The order book covers all of 2027, about 18 months of forward production from June 30. Guidance was raised, not cut, in a year of model change-over. Net industrial debt is just €131M after more than €800M returned in a single quarter, and a ~€3.5B buyback runs to 2030. The stock has already absorbed a 31% de-rating from its September 2025 level to the January 2026 trough on a plan that disappointed on growth rate, not on quality. Strong Buy consensus from 14 analysts averages $462 (+11%).

Bear Case

At ~39x trailing and ~37x forward earnings, RACE is valued at more than twice LVMH and several times Porsche AG on the assumption that mix and personalisation keep compounding. Management's own 2030 plan implies about 5% annual revenue growth, and a single session in October 2025 showed what that gap does to the price. The EV transition is untested at Ferrari prices. Americas and Greater China deliveries fell 21% and 32% in Q2. The Q2 margin was helped by temporary lower D&A that management expects to reverse. And with 48.5% of votes held by two anchor holders, there is no control premium to fall back on.

Top 3 Risks

01
Multiple compression on slower growth. The stock is priced as a luxury compounder. With revenue growth guided at ~6% for 2026, any sign that personalisation or mix is peaking hits the multiple, not just the estimates. October 2025 (−15.4% in a day in Milan) is the template.
02
Brand dilution through volume or the electric line. Scarcity is a choice that has to be re-made every year. A Luce that has to be pushed rather than allocated, or deliveries raised to meet growth targets, would weaken the mechanism the valuation rests on.
03
FX and regional demand. A euro reporter selling in dollars and yen, with guidance explicitly conditioned on the Middle East crisis, and Q2 deliveries down 21% in the Americas and 32% in Greater China. Most of this is planned change-over, but it has to show up again as volume in 2027.
Industry Commentary
Luxury multiple versus auto multiple
STRUCTURAL SUPPORT

The market pays roughly 8x earnings for Toyota and 13x for Porsche AG, but about 39x for Ferrari. The reason sits in the margin and the order book. A carmaker's profit depends on selling enough cars to cover its plants, so margins fall when demand weakens. Ferrari builds fewer cars than it has orders for, sells the scarcest models only to existing clients, and has personalisation lifting the price of every car. The result is a 39% EBITDA margin and a demand buffer running into 2027. Those are the traits of a luxury house. The practical test is simple: in Q2 2026 volume fell and profit rose. A mass-market carmaker cannot do that.

Luxury sports cars and electrification
OPEN QUESTION

Ferrari cut its 2030 full-electric production target to 20% from 40% because demand for high-performance luxury EVs has been weaker than expected. Hybrids and combustion cars are each set at 40%. Rivals are also moving: McLaren said in September 2026 it would invest £500M in UK manufacturing and launch its first SUV (Reuters, via StockAnalysis news). The luxury segment is broadening, which tests whether Ferrari's pricing power is unique or cyclical.

Formula 1 as a revenue line and a brand line
NEAR-TERM DRAG

The prior season's championship ranking sets Ferrari's share of F1 commercial revenues. In Q2 2026 that share fell and partly offset higher sponsorship. On track in 2026, Scuderia Ferrari has scored podiums but is not contending for the drivers' title. After the Spanish Grand Prix, Lewis Hamilton conceded his title hopes 101 points back (The Guardian, via StockAnalysis news). The financial effect is small against a €7.6B revenue base.
