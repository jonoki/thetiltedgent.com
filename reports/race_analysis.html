01 Company Overview

Start with one number: 39.7%. That was Ferrari's EBITDA margin in Q3 2026 (€753M on €1,896M of revenue), up from 37.9% a year earlier, after 38.8% for FY2025 (€2.77B on €7.15B). Mass-market carmakers do not earn that. Luxury houses do. Ferrari builds cars, but it prices like a luxury house: it ships fewer cars than it could sell, so the waiting list does the selling and the price sticks. FY2025 revenue was €7,146M, up 7.0%, with diluted EPS of €8.96. On November 3, 2026 it raised its 2026 guidance again, to about €7.65B of revenue and adjusted EPS of at least €9.85. On 175.42M shares outstanding (StockAnalysis) × $416.13, the market cap is $73.00B.

The premise to check is scarcity pricing: fewer cars, a costlier mix of models, and a multiple (the price paid per euro of earnings) priced for luxury rather than autos. The numbers support it. In Q3 2026 Ferrari shipped 3,368 cars, 1.0% fewer than a year earlier, yet Cars and spare parts revenue rose 7% (10% at constant currency) and EBIT rose 12%. CEO Benedetto Vigna said in the results release: "Our order book now reaches into 2028, and we still have two more launches to come this year." In July the company said its order book "entirely covers 2027". Measured from September 30, 2026, that is more than 15 months of production already spoken for. The market pays about 39x trailing earnings. Toyota trades at 9.7x, Porsche AG at 19.6x and LVMH at 18.6x (StockAnalysis trailing P/E fields, rescaled to November 6 closes). The debate is not whether the business is excellent. It is whether a multiple more than twice LVMH's can hold when growth has slowed to single digits. The October 2025 Capital Markets Day tested exactly that, and the stock lost 15% in one session. After the Q3 print the shares rose 2.9% on the day and 4.3% the next, then gave back 3.8% over the following two sessions, closing on November 6 at the same $416.13 as the previous edition.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Deliberate under-supply Order book into 2028 Personalisation pricing Special Series allocation Scuderia Ferrari / F1 heritage Brand licensing & lifestyle Anchor-holder control (48.5% votes)

The moat is allocation power. Ferrari chooses who can buy which car, and the most limited models (Special Series, supercars like the F80) go to existing clients. The waiting list therefore enforces loyalty, and new buyers have to work their way up. That is why the order book can run into 2028 while shipments slip, and why revenue per car rises when volume does not. The same mechanism explains the valuation. A carmaker's earnings rise and fall with the economic cycle. Ferrari's earnings behave more like a luxury house's, where demand runs ahead of supply at the top of the market, so pricing holds. The weakness is that scarcity pricing works only while the brand stays wanted. Everything that protects the brand (F1 results, product hits, discipline on volume) is also a cost line.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The multiple is the risk. At ~39x trailing and ~38x forward earnings, the stock assumes steady growth from pricier models and more personalisation. When the October 2025 Capital Markets Day set a 2030 revenue target of €9B (about 5% a year on the company's own plan), the Milan line fell as much as 16.18% intraday and closed 15.41% lower at €354 in a single session. Nothing in the business had broken. The market simply paid less for slower growth.
02
Electrification cuts both ways. The 2030 plan cut the full-electric share of the model mix to 20%, from 40% in the 2022 plan, with hybrids and combustion cars at 40% each. The first electric Ferrari, the Luce, reaches its first customers in Q4 (Vigna, Q3 call). Whether collectors pay combustion-level prices for an electric Ferrari is untested. "An addition, not a transition" is a hedge, not an answer.
03
Geography and FX. Q3 2026 shipments to Mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan fell 105 units (−42%), while the Americas were flat (+5). Vigna put the China swing down to a model changeover. A company that reports in euros but sells in dollars and yen also sees those sales rise or fall in euro terms as exchange rates move. It hedges that risk, and currency was a drag in the quarter. 2026 guidance is based on current visibility on the Middle East crisis effects, a region inside Ferrari's EMEA reporting area.
04
Control is not contestable. Exor and the Piero Ferrari Trust hold 48.5% of votes through loyalty shares. That keeps the brand discipline stable. It also means no takeover premium will ever be paid, and minority holders have no real say on strategy.

Notable One-Off Items

Ferrari reports no adjustments: adjusted EBITDA, EBIT, net profit and EPS "equaled the reported ones" in the periods presented (Q3 2026 release). Three items still shape the numbers. First, depreciation and amortisation rose to €190M in Q3 2026 from €167M as new models enter production; the company says this is in line with the start of production of new models and is a factor in the guidance. Second, the Patent Box tax benefit kept the Q3 effective tax rate at 23.0% (22.0% a year earlier). Third, buybacks and cancellations: on July 16, 2026 Ferrari cancelled 16,644,606 treasury common shares and 6,686,115 special voting shares. Q3 repurchases were €173M. RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $260.46 to the November 6, 2026 settled close of $416.13, RACE gained 59.8% on price. Dividends come on top. The endpoints hide two regimes. From mid-2022 to mid-2025 the stock rose almost without a break: investors moved from paying an auto multiple towards paying a luxury one while margins widened. Since the October 2025 Capital Markets Day it has been one long argument about the price of slower growth. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Scarcity pricing is working in the numbers. Q3 2026 shipments slipped 1.0%, yet revenue rose 7% (10% at constant currency), EBIT rose 12% and the EBITDA margin reached 39.7%. The order book now reaches into 2028, and guidance was raised again, with revenue now about €7.65B and adjusted EPS at least €9.85. Net industrial cash was €219M at September 30 after €173M of buybacks in the quarter, and a ~€3.5B buyback runs to 2030. The stock has already fallen 31% from its September 2025 close to the January 2026 trough, on a plan that disappointed on growth rate, not on quality. The 12-analyst consensus is Buy, with an average target of $464 (+11.5%).

Bear Case

At ~39x trailing and ~38x forward earnings, RACE is valued at more than twice LVMH and about twice Porsche AG on the assumption that pricier models and personalisation keep lifting profits. Management's own 2030 plan implies about 5% annual revenue growth, and a single session in October 2025 showed what that gap does to the price. The EV transition is untested at Ferrari prices. Shipments to Mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan fell 42% in Q3, and total shipments are down 3.0% over nine months. Depreciation and amortisation rose to €190M in the quarter from €167M as new models start production. And with 48.5% of votes held by two anchor holders, there is no takeover premium to fall back on.

Top 3 Risks

01
The price of slower growth. The stock is priced like a luxury house. With revenue growth guided at ~7% for 2026, any sign that personalisation or mix is peaking hits the multiple (the price paid per euro of earnings), not just the estimates. October 2025 (−15.4% in a day in Milan) is the template.
02
Brand dilution through volume or the electric line. Scarcity is a choice that has to be re-made every year. A Luce that has to be pushed rather than allocated, or deliveries raised to meet growth targets, would weaken the mechanism the valuation rests on.
03
FX and regional demand. A euro reporter selling in dollars and yen, with guidance explicitly conditioned on the Middle East crisis, and Q3 shipments to Mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan down 42%. Management puts the China swing down to a model changeover, but it has to show up again as volume in 2027.
Industry Commentary
Luxury multiple versus auto multiple
LASTING SUPPORT

The market pays roughly 10x earnings for Toyota and 20x for Porsche AG, but about 39x for Ferrari. The reason sits in the margin and the order book. A carmaker's profit depends on selling enough cars to cover its plants, so margins fall when demand weakens. Ferrari builds fewer cars than it has orders for, sells the scarcest models only to existing clients, and has personalisation lifting the price of every car. The result is a near-40% EBITDA margin and a demand buffer running into 2028. Those are the traits of a luxury house. The practical test is simple: in Q3 2026 shipments slipped 1.0% and EBIT rose 12%. A mass-market carmaker cannot do that.

Luxury sports cars and electrification
OPEN QUESTION

Ferrari cut the full-electric share of its 2030 model mix to 20% from 40%. Automotive World tied the cut to wealthy buyers' reluctance to give up combustion engines, a retreat it said mirrors Porsche and Mercedes-Benz. Hybrids and combustion cars are each set at 40%. Rivals are also moving: McLaren said in September 2026 it would invest £500M in UK manufacturing and launch its first SUV (Reuters, via StockAnalysis news). The luxury segment is broadening, which tests whether Ferrari's pricing power is unique or cyclical.

Formula 1 as a revenue line and a brand line
NEAR-TERM DRAG

The prior season's championship ranking sets Ferrari's share of F1 commercial revenues. In Q2 2026 that share fell and partly offset higher sponsorship. Ferrari's raised 2026 guidance assumes second place in the Formula 1 Championship (Q3 release). The financial effect is small against a €7.65B revenue base. The brand effect of a title win or a long drought is harder to measure, and it matters more.
