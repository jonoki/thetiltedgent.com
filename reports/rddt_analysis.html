01 Company Overview

Reddit is the internet's largest collection of people arguing, advising and explaining things to each other in public — organised into communities ("subreddits") around almost any topic. It makes money the way most free platforms do: advertising, which was 94.7% of Q2 2026 revenue. The rest, "other revenue", is mostly the newer business of licensing that archive of human conversation to AI developers. FY2025 revenue was $2.20B, up 69.4%, and the company swung from a $484M net loss in FY2024 (the IPO year, heavy with stock compensation) to $530M of net income in FY2025. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 2026 is $2.78B and net income $871M.

That is the most unusual shape in the S&P 500's newer members: a twenty-year-old website that only became a profitable public company in the last two years, and is still growing revenue at more than 60% a year — Q2 2026 was +61%, the eighth straight quarter above 60% according to LightShed Partners' Rich Greenfield. Yet the stock closed at $150.85 on September 18, 2026, 34.4% below its $229.87 close at the end of 2025. The debate is not about the numbers already reported. It is about the pipe the users arrive through. A large share of Reddit's growth came from Google sending searchers to Reddit threads; as Google answers more questions itself with AI Overviews, that pipe narrows. With the Q2 2026 results CEO Steve Huffman said "search referrals were choppy in the quarter," and the shares fell about 11% after hours despite a beat on every line. Reddit was then added to the S&P 500 effective August 18, 2026, jumped 12.63% to $178.09 on the announcement day, and has since given the whole pop back.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

20 years of human-written archive Community network effects Volunteer moderation Purchase-intent audience 91% gross margin $2.8B cash, no debt to speak of

The moat is the one thing AI cannot manufacture: provably human opinion at scale. A thread where forty people who own a product argue about it is worth more to a searcher than a summary — and worth more to an AI model as training data. Communities are also sticky in the classic network sense: the value of a subreddit is the people already in it, and volunteer moderators run it for free. The vulnerability is that Reddit does not control the front door. Its audience growth has leaned on Google, and every question Google now answers on its own page is a visit Reddit never sees. The moat protects the content; it does not guarantee the traffic.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Search dependence. Huffman told investors "search referrals were choppy in the quarter and traffic was more volatile later in the quarter," and said Google's AI Overviews "has yet to make a similar level of positive impact" as the old ten blue links. The stock fell about 11% after hours on July 30 on that line, not on the numbers.
02
US saturation. US daily users grew 6% while the US user pays 5.2x what an international user does. If US growth stalls, revenue growth has to come from raising ad prices on the same audience, or from converting cheaper international users — both slower levers than the last two years.
03
One customer is three relationships. Google is the traffic source, a licensing customer (reported $60M a year) and the competitor answering questions above Reddit's link. The renewal talks reported in July are a negotiation with the party holding most of the cards.
04
Volatility is the product for now. Beta of 2.02, 14.38% of the float sold short, and monthly moves of −35% (March 2025) and +81% (October 2024) in a 30-month life as a public company. The index addition changed the holder base, not the temperament.

Notable One-Off Items

Two things distort the headline profit. First, tax: StockAnalysis shows an effective tax rate of 0.78% and only $6.86M of tax paid on $878M of pretax income over the trailing year. A company that lost money for most of its life carries tax losses forward; that shield will not last forever, and when a normal rate arrives, EPS falls even if the business does not. Second, the share count: StockAnalysis shows shares up 8.43% year over year, while the company reports fully diluted shares of 207.0M, up only 0.2% — the difference is in the definition (basic vs fully diluted), and the Q2 buyback of $235M is aimed at holding dilution from stock compensation flat. FY2024's $484M loss was the IPO year and is not comparable to today's run-rate.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value. The first reading appears in September 2024, once six monthly moves exist.

Reddit priced its IPO at $34.00 and closed its first partial month, March 2024, at $49.32. At the September 18, 2026 close of $150.85 the stock is up 206% from that first month-end and 344% from the IPO price. That headline hides a round trip: the stock more than quadrupled to $229.99 by September 2025, closed 2025 at $229.87, and has since lost a third of its value while the business kept growing at 60%+. In under thirty months it has had two separate drawdowns of more than 40% measured on month-end closes.

The 6-period monthly RSI reached 96.4 in January 2025 — about as stretched as the measure gets — and the stock promptly fell 47% in two months. It has not been oversold on this measure since: the low reading was 35.3 in March 2026, and it sits at 44.7 now, neutral. The 10-month moving average, around $162.67, is above the price; the 3-month average, $146.44, is just below it. The long trend is still down; the short one has flattened.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 35x trailing and about 20x FY2026 consensus earnings for a company growing revenue 61%, with 91% gross margins, a 28% operating margin that was 14% a year ago, $1.02B of trailing free cash flow and $2.77B of net cash. It has beaten and guided above consensus while the stock fell a third. In a world filling with AI-written text, a twenty-year archive of real people's opinions becomes scarcer and more valuable — to users, to advertisers and to AI companies that must license it. If Huffman's push to turn search visitors into daily app users works, the Google risk shrinks each quarter, and the market is pricing a traffic problem that the users figures do not yet show: global DAUq still grew 18%.

Bear Case

The growth came through a door someone else owns. US daily users — the ones worth $11.85 a quarter — grew only 6%, and the CEO himself called search referrals choppy as Google's AI answers keep users on Google's page. ARPU gains cannot compound forever on a slowing US base, the Q3 guide already implies growth falling to the high 40s, and net income is flattered by a 0.78% tax rate that will normalise. Fourteen percent of the float is short, beta is 2, and the index-inclusion bid has already come and gone. A 35x multiple on a business whose traffic source is also its biggest competitor is not cheap.

Top 3 Risks

01
Search referrals shrink as AI answers grow. The single risk that has driven the stock this year. Management acknowledged it on the Q2 call; the Q3 print on October 29 is the next read on logged-out traffic.
02
The Google licensing renewal. A reported $60M-a-year deal with a counterparty that is also the traffic source. Talks are ongoing; the outcome and timing are not disclosed.
03
Growth normalisation meets a growth multiple. Q3 guidance implies ~47–49% growth versus 61% in Q2, and the tax shield on earnings is temporary. Either can compress EPS growth faster than the multiple adjusts, in a stock with a 2.02 beta.
Industry Commentary
AI answers vs. the open-web traffic model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For twenty years Google sent searchers out to other sites. AI Overviews answer the question on Google's own page, and news publishers have complained loudly about lost referrals. Reddit has held up far better — global users still grew 18% — because people add "reddit" to their searches precisely to find human answers. But Huffman's own words, that the AI results have "yet to make a similar level of positive impact," describe the same force. Reddit's answer is to become a destination rather than a result: app users are, in his words, "worth multiples more than the search referral traffic."

Paying for the training data
ONGOING NEGOTIATION

AI developers need large amounts of human-written text, and the best sources are starting to charge. Reddit licenses to Google (reported $60M a year) and OpenAI. Regulators are paying attention to the balance of power: the UK Competition and Markets Authority's chief executive Sarah Cardell said it is "crucial that content publishers, including news organizations, have appropriate bargaining power over how their content is used." For Reddit this line is only 5.3% of revenue today; its value is as leverage in the traffic conversation.

Index inclusion and the new holder base
STRUCTURAL SUPPORT

Reddit joined the S&P 500 on August 18, 2026. Index membership brings a permanent passive holder base, which usually dampens volatility over time. The first month did not show it: the announcement pop to $178.09 was fully erased within a month, and short interest remains above 14% of float. The bull framing: a profitable 60% grower now owned by every index fund, trading at 20x forward earnings. The bear framing: the index added a stock whose next leg of growth depends on a partner it cannot control. Both will be judged on one number — US daily users.
