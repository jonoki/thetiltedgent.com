01 Company Overview

Reddit is one of the internet's largest collections of people arguing, advising and explaining things to each other in public — organised into communities ("subreddits") around almost any topic. It makes money the way most free platforms do: advertising, which was 94.6% of Q3 2026 revenue. The rest, "other revenue", is mostly the newer business of licensing that archive of human conversation to AI developers. FY2025 revenue was $2.20B, up 69.4%, and the company swung from a $484M net loss in FY2024 (the IPO year, heavy with stock compensation) to $530M of net income in FY2025. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 is $3.09B and net income $992M.

That is an unusual shape for an S&P 500 member: a twenty-year-old website that only became a profitable public company in the last two years, and whose revenue grew more than 60% a year for eight straight quarters before Q3 2026 slowed to +53%. Yet the stock closed at $96.15 on November 3, 2026, 58.2% below its $229.87 close at the end of 2025. The debate is not about the revenue already reported. It is about the pipe the users arrive through. A large share of Reddit's audience growth came from Google sending searchers to Reddit threads; as Google answers more questions itself with AI, that pipe is narrowing. With the Q3 results on October 29, Reddit reported US daily users down 2% from a year earlier, and CEO Steve Huffman said that if October's rate of decline continued, total daily users could fall by a low double-digit percentage in Q4. The stock fell 29.22% the next session. Reddit had joined the S&P 500 effective August 18, 2026, after jumping 12.63% to $178.09 on August 14, the session after the announcement; it now trades 46% below that close.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

20 years of human-written archive Community network effects Volunteer moderation Purchase-intent audience 91% gross margin $3.0B cash, no borrowings

The moat is the one thing AI cannot manufacture: provably human opinion at scale. A thread where forty people who own a product argue about it is worth more to a searcher than a summary — and worth more to an AI model as training data. Communities are also sticky in the classic network sense: the value of a subreddit is the people already in it, and volunteer moderators run it for free. The vulnerability is that Reddit does not control the front door. Its audience growth has leaned on Google, and every question Google now answers on its own page is a visit Reddit never sees. The moat protects the content; it does not guarantee the traffic.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Search dependence. On the Q3 call Huffman said "The biggest impact was on logged out and web traffic," and that "If this rate of decline continues, current trends would suggest that total DAUqs could fall in Q4 by a low double-digit percentage from our current base of 130 million DAUqs." The stock fell 29.22% the next session; 24/7 Wall St. put the fall down to the search-traffic slump and the soft outlook, which overshadowed results that beat.
02
A shrinking US audience. US daily users fell 2% while a US user pays 5.7x what an international user does. Revenue growth now has to come from raising ad prices on the same or a smaller US audience, or from converting cheaper international users — both slower levers than the last two years.
03
One customer is three relationships. Google is the traffic source, a licensing customer (reported $60M a year) and the competitor answering questions above Reddit's link. Huffman said on the Q3 call that the renewal talks are active and could end without a deal.
04
Volatility is the product for now. Beta of 2.05, 15.28% of the float sold short at the October 15 settlement, and monthly moves of −35.6% (October 2026), −35.2% (March 2025) and +81% (October 2024) in about 31 months as a public company. The index addition changed the holder base, not the temperament.

Notable One-Off Items

Two things distort the headline profit. First, tax: StockAnalysis shows an effective tax rate of 1.46% over the trailing year, and CFO Drew Vollero said on the call: "Our Q3 tax expense was approximately $5.8 million with an effective tax rate of about 2%." A company that lost money for most of its life carries tax losses forward; that shield will not last forever, and when a normal rate arrives, EPS falls even if the business does not. Second, the share count: StockAnalysis shows shares outstanding up 3.49% year over year, while the company reports 206.3M fully diluted shares at September 30, 2026, down 0.3% from June — the difference is in the definition (shares issued today vs every share that options and stock awards could add), and the buyback ($340M in the first nine months of 2026) is aimed at holding dilution from stock pay flat. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 20x reported trailing earnings (and about 12x StockAnalysis's FY2026 consensus, which is on an adjusted basis) for a company that grew revenue 53% last quarter, with 91% gross margins, a 29% operating margin, $1.20B of trailing free cash flow — about 6.5% of its market value — and $3.00B of net cash. Revenue beat its own guidance, and the Q4 guide still implies growth of 38–42%. The users who pay most — logged-in and app users, over 80% of monetisation by the COO's count — grew from Q2 in both the US and abroad. If the search visitors now leaving were the least valuable ones, the market has priced a user loss as if it were a revenue loss. In a world filling with AI-written text, a twenty-year archive of real people's opinions becomes scarcer and more valuable — to users, to advertisers and to AI companies that must license it.

Bear Case

The growth came through a door someone else owns, and the door is closing. US daily users — the ones worth $13.82 a quarter — fell 2%, and the CEO himself warned that total daily users could fall by a low double-digit percentage this quarter as Google sends fewer searchers. Revenue growth has gone from 61% to 53% and is guided to 38–42%; higher ad prices cannot carry growth forever on a shrinking US audience, and net income is flattered by a tax rate near 2% that will normalise. Fifteen percent of the float is short, beta is 2, and twenty brokers cut their targets between October 30 and November 2. A business whose traffic source is also its biggest competitor deserves a discount; the open question is how big.

Industry Commentary
AI answers vs. the open-web traffic model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For twenty years Google sent searchers out to other sites. AI Overviews answer the question on Google's own page. Reddit held up far better than most — global users still grew 12% in Q3 — because people add "reddit" to their searches precisely to find human answers. But in Q3 the same force reached Reddit's US numbers: US logged-out daily users, the ones who arrive from search, fell 4% year over year (Q3 release), and Huffman said on the call that "Google has really shifted in their relationship with the rest of the Internet." Reddit's answer is to become a destination rather than a result: by COO Jennifer Wong's count, logged-in and app users already carry over 80% of its monetisation.

Paying for the training data
ONGOING NEGOTIATION

AI developers need large amounts of human-written text, and the best sources are starting to charge. Reddit licenses to Google (reported $60M a year) and OpenAI. For Reddit this line is only 5.4% of revenue today ($48M in Q3, +35%); its value is as leverage in the traffic conversation, and Huffman said on the Q3 call that Reddit would only do licensing deals that make sense for it, which could mean no deal.

Index inclusion and the new holder base
STRUCTURAL SUPPORT

Reddit joined the S&P 500 on August 18, 2026. Index membership means index funds buy the shares and hold them, which usually dampens swings over time. The first months did not show it: the announcement pop to $178.09 was erased within a month, the stock is now 46% below it, and short interest was 15.28% of float at the October 15 settlement. The bull framing: a profitable company growing revenue above 50%, now owned by every S&P 500 index fund, at about 20x reported trailing earnings. The bear framing: the index added a stock whose next leg of growth depends on a partner it cannot control. Both will be judged on one number — daily users.
