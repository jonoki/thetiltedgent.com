01 Company Overview

Reliance Industries is India's most sprawling listed company: one balance sheet that holds an oil refinery and petrochemicals complex at Jamnagar, a mobile network the company ranks first in India (Jio, 533.3 million customers), a retailer with 20,169 stores, gas fields off India's east coast, the JioStar media business, a fast-growing packaged-goods arm, and a clean-energy factory complex still being commissioned. In the year to March 31, 2026 (FY26) it booked gross revenue of ₹11,75,919 crore ($124.0 billion at the company's own conversion), EBITDA of ₹2,07,911 crore and net profit of ₹95,754 crore. (One crore is ten million rupees; one lakh crore is a trillion rupees.)

The investment question is a hand of three different games played with one stack. The refinery is a global cyclical whose margins swing with diesel and jet-fuel spreads; in the April–June 2026 quarter those spreads hit records after the Middle East conflict and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, and O2C (oil-to-chemicals) EBITDA rose 17.2%. Jio and Retail are domestic growth businesses that now produce more than half of group EBITDA. And New Energy plus the AI data-centre build at Jamnagar are the next decade's bets, funded from today's cash flow — capex was ₹1,44,271 crore in FY26. The market has not rewarded the mix lately: the stock closed at ₹1,167.70 on October 1, 2026, its lowest close of the past year, 25.6% below its December 2025 month-end and almost exactly where it stood five years earlier, while the Nifty 50 index rose about 27% over the same five years. The catalyst everyone is watching is the Jio Platforms IPO: its draft prospectus was filed with India's market regulator SEBI on June 19, 2026, and SEBI issued its observation letter on it, the regulator's go-ahead, on August 28, 2026 (Reliance's exchange filing that day). No offer dates had been announced by October 1.

The latest quarter. Reliance filed its April–June 2026 (Q1 FY27) results after the market closed on July 17, 2026. EPS of ₹15.48 came in 3.4% above the ₹14.97 average analyst estimate compiled by Yahoo Finance, and the stock closed 0.3% lower in the first session afterwards (₹1,327.20 on July 17 to ₹1,323.10 on July 20). Chairman Mukesh Ambani called it "a steady start to FY27" and said it "gives me reason to be optimistic about the year ahead as we move forward with phased commissioning of new energy projects and unlock value through the Jio IPO" (Q1 media release). The group gives no earnings or capex guidance: asked about full-year capex at the July 17 analyst meet, CFO V. Srikanth said "I have stayed away from commenting very specifically on capex", pointing instead to its debt-to-EBITDA and credit-rating limits. The one new target is in Retail, which set a three-year objective (FY27–FY29) to double operating EBITDA, with FY27 framed as the year of building online scale (results presentation, July 17, 2026).

The two numbers that tie it together

For a conglomerate that spends this heavily, watch whether the spending is paid for by the businesses, and what is left for the parent's shareholders.

Capex vs operating cash (FY26)
₹1,44,271 cr
FY26 capex excluding spectrum, against operating cash flow of ₹1,92,113 crore. Cash spent on property, plant, spectrum and intangibles was ₹1,22,916 crore, leaving about ₹69,197 crore of free cash flow (our calculation from the cash-flow statement). Q1 FY27 capex was ₹38,682 crore.
Net debt (Jun 30, 2026)
₹1,22,914 cr
Gross debt ₹3,69,705 crore less cash of ₹2,46,791 crore. That is 0.60x trailing EBITDA, inside the company's own ceiling of under 1x. S&P rates Reliance A− and Moody's Baa1, which the chairman noted are two notches above India's sovereign rating; Moody's upgraded its foreign-currency debt to Baa1 during the April–June quarter (Q1 media release). Since then it has borrowed at home: ₹12,000 crore of five-year bonds at 7.47% on September 16 and ₹13,000 crore of ten-year bonds at 7.90% on October 1, both rated AAA/Stable by CRISIL and CARE (exchange filings).
Profit that leaks to minorities
₹2,250 cr
Of Q1 FY27 net profit of ₹23,196 crore, ₹2,250 crore belonged to outside shareholders in subsidiaries such as Jio and Retail; ₹20,946 crore belonged to Reliance's own shareholders. A Jio listing would make that split visible at a market price.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

533M-customer Jio network Nationwide 5G network (285M users) Integrated Jamnagar O2C complex 20,169-store retail footprint A− / Baa1 credit, above the sovereign Operating cash flow ₹1.92 lakh cr Cross-selling across 396M retail customers

The moat is scale paid for in advance. Jio's network and spectrum are sunk costs that a challenger would have to match before charging a rupee; the retail footprint now links more than 2,500 stores to two-hour delivery; and the refinery's flexibility showed in Q1 FY27, when it shifted to Russian and Latin American crude and placed products in short markets such as Singapore, Australia and East Africa. The company says it keeps capex within operating cash flow, so the old businesses pay for the new ones. The weakness is the same feature seen from the other side: capital allocation is controlled by one family, and minority shareholders have to trust that the next ₹1.4 lakh crore a year earns more than its cost.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The refinery is a policy business in a crisis. When fuel prices spike, the Indian state acts: SAED came back on diesel, petrol and jet fuel, Reliance diverted propane and butane to LPG for state-owned retailers, and Jio-bp held pump prices and absorbed under-recoveries. Record cracks did not all reach the income statement.
02
Retail margins are going backwards. Retail EBITDA fell 1.1% YoY with the margin down 80 bps to 7.9% which management puts down to investment in digital commerce and the fast build-out of hyper-local delivery. Until those orders scale, that cost weighs on the whole retail business. Retail CFO Dinesh Taluja told analysts on July 17 that the dark-store build would continue "in the next nine to 10 months" and that "the percentages for the short term could come down"; the plan to double operating EBITDA by FY29 sets no margin target.
03
Returns lag the size. Trailing return on equity is about 8.5% (our calculation: ₹74,727 crore of TTM profit to owners ÷ ₹8,80,365 crore of net worth). That return sits on five years of capex totalling ₹6,48,428 crore, which the chairman said is more than any company in India.
04
Mature gas fields. KG D6 output fell 7.4% YoY and gas realisations fell. The E&P segment's 79% margin will shrink in rupees unless new wells offset decline.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort comparisons. First, the ₹8,924 crore gain on sale of listed investments in Q1 FY26 inflates last year's first quarter: Q1 FY27 profit after tax rose 6.1% on a recurring basis but fell 24.6% including it. Second, the 1:1 bonus issue allotted on October 29, 2024 doubled the share count to 13.53 billion, so pre-2024 per-share figures must be halved to compare. Third, the demerger of the consumer-brands business into Reliance Consumer Products depresses reported Retail growth (7.4% reported vs 11.6% adjusted). Earlier, in July 2023, the financial-services arm was demerged as Jio Financial Services, one share for each Reliance share held on the July 20, 2023 record date.

HOW TO READ THE GAP
●
A 43% gap is a bet on the sum of the parts. Valued piece by piece — Jio, Retail, O2C, New Energy — a conglomerate can look worth more than its share price. Read the gap as a measure of how much of that value the market thinks the parent's shareholders will ever see.
●
The stock fell; the targets sit far above it. The share price fell 25.6% from the December 2025 month-end to October 1, 2026, yet the lowest target is still above the close. Either the targets are slow to move, or the market is applying a discount the analysts do not.
●
One dissenter. Yahoo shows a single Strong Sell among 25 ratings, against 24 Buy or Strong Buy and no Holds.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 1, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of ₹1,170.48 (on today's share basis) to the October 1, 2026 settled close of ₹1,167.70, Reliance's price moved −0.2% in five years. Dividends of ₹25 per current share over the window add roughly two percentage points. Over the same span the Nifty 50 rose 26.9% (17,671.65 at the October 2021 month-end to 22,421.95 on October 1, 2026). The path was a range, not a trend: month-end closes between ₹1,071.86 (February 2023) and ₹1,570.40 (December 2025), with two failed breakouts — mid-2024 and late 2025 — each given back within months.

The 6-period monthly RSI stands at 24.7 on the October 1 close, the lowest reading in the five-year series, after 26.3 at the September month-end. The 10-month moving average, about ₹1,311.86, sits 12% above the price. In poker terms the stock has been card-dead for nine months: down 25.6% since December while the business reported record quarterly EBITDA. Oversold readings say the selling has been one-sided; they do not say it is over.

Relative performance

Against its home market, Reliance has lagged badly. Over the five years from the October 2021 month-end to October 1, 2026 the stock's price is −0.2% against +26.9% for the Nifty 50. In calendar 2026 to date, it is −25.6% against −14.2% for the Nifty 50 (26,129.60 at the December 2025 month-end). Over the past year it is down 14.7%. The valuation tells the same story from the other side: at 21.1x trailing earnings Reliance trades only slightly above the Nifty 50's 19.19x despite owning Jio and a 20,169-store retailer. That is the conglomerate discount at work, and the Jio IPO is the event most likely to test it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 21.1x trailing earnings, 18.3x this fiscal year's consensus and 16.4x next year's for a group whose largest earner, Jio, grew EBITDA 15.1% at a 53.3% margin, whose refinery just printed a record revenue quarter, and whose balance sheet carries net debt of only 0.60x EBITDA with ratings above the sovereign. The stock sits at a 52-week closing low with the deepest RSI reading of five years, while every one of 25 analyst targets on Yahoo sits above the price (mean ₹1,674.72). The latest quarter beat the consensus EPS estimate by 3.4%, SEBI has now issued its observation letter on the Jio prospectus, and Retail has set itself a target of doubling operating EBITDA by FY29. A Jio listing could put a public price on more value than the whole group's ₹15.80 lakh crore market cap reflects, New Energy is moving from building to selling, and FY26 still produced about ₹69,000 crore of free cash flow after ₹1.2 lakh crore of cash capex.

Bear Case

Five years of capex totalling ₹6,48,428 crore have produced a flat share price and an 8.5% return on equity. Retail margins are falling as the business pays to build quick delivery, and Retail's own CFO says the margin percentage could come down in the short term. The list of new ventures keeps growing (a fighter-engine partnership with Rolls-Royce in August, explosives and fertilisers added to the company's objects the same month) while management declines to guide on capex. KG D6 gas is in decline, and the refinery's windfall is being taxed and price-controlled as it arrives. The Jio IPO may raise money for Jio rather than return it to Reliance shareholders, and minority interests already take a slice of profits (₹2,250 crore in Q1). A 0.51% dividend yield means holders depend entirely on management's next bets — AI data centres and batteries — earning more than their cost, and the price chart says the market has been unwilling to assume it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Capital allocation and the holding-company discount. Group capex of ₹1,44,271 crore in FY26 and new commitments (120 GWh of batteries, 20 GW of solar, AI compute) leave little room for payouts. The list widened after the print: an intended aero-engine partnership with Rolls-Royce (August 14) and shareholder approval to enter ammonium nitrate, explosives and fertilisers (August 20), and the CFO has declined to give a capex figure. If returns stay near 8.5%, the discount to analysts' sum-of-the-parts can persist indefinitely.
02
Energy-shock policy risk. The same crisis that lifted cracks brought SAED back, forced LPG diversions and pump-price restraint, and cut domestic polymer demand by 21.7%. A prolonged shock could see more intervention; a quick resolution would take the cracks back down. The export levies move every fortnight: on September 16 the diesel duty was cut to ₹20 a litre from ₹25 and jet fuel to ₹15 from ₹19.
03
Execution on the IPO and new businesses. SEBI issued its observation letter on August 28, but the timing and pricing of the Jio IPO had not been announced by October 1, and New Energy has promised commissioning "this year". Delays on either would remove the two catalysts the bull case relies on.
Industry Commentary
The Hormuz shock: windfall and windfall tax
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

In April–June 2026 global oil demand fell 4.8 million barrels a day to 99.1 mb/d and refinery throughput fell 4.7 mb/d, according to Reliance's own market review, while Brent averaged $104.5. Middle-distillate cracks reached all-time highs. For a complex coastal refinery that can switch crudes, that is the best margin environment in years. But the state's priority is supply at stable prices: hence the SAED levies, the LPG directives, and pump prices held steady at Reliance's own Jio-bp outlets. Reliance's results show the trade-off: record O2C revenue, EBITDA up a more modest 17.2%, and margin down 100 bps to 8.4%. Management expects the tightness to last: at the July 17 analyst meet Srinivas Tuttagunta, COO of Refining & Marketing, pointed to refining capacity lost in Kuwait and Bahrain and to Russia having "lost more than 40% of its capacity", and concluded that "all these point towards reasonably strong cracks in our view, and that is what the market suggests."

Indian telecom: Jio pricing up while adding users
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Jio's ARPU has risen from ₹208.8 a year earlier to ₹215.6 while it kept adding subscribers (net 35.2 million in twelve months) and pushed churn down to 1.6%. Bharti Airtel is the other large listed operator ($113.2B market value at the October 1 close) and Vodafone Idea is far smaller ($14.3B). Jio's own numbers show it lifting ARPU while still adding customers. The next leg — fixed broadband via JioAirFiber, enterprise and cloud, AI services — is what an IPO prospectus will have to sell.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At 21.1x trailing earnings and 1.79x book, Reliance sits between the oil-and-gas index (10.44x, 1.29x) and the Nifty 50 (19.19x, 2.75x): the market prices it as half refiner, half consumer company. The bull framing: the consumer half already earns over 50% of EBITDA and grows in the mid-teens, so the blended multiple undervalues it, and a listed Jio will force the comparison. The bear framing: a parent that keeps reinvesting at single-digit returns deserves a discount to its parts, and listing a subsidiary does not change who allocates the cash. The way to tell the two apart is not the P/E but the return on equity: watch whether 8.5% starts rising as New Energy and AI assets begin to earn.
