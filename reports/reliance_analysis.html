01 Company Overview

Reliance Industries is India's most sprawling listed company: one balance sheet that holds an oil refinery and petrochemicals complex at Jamnagar, a mobile network the company ranks first in India (Jio, 542.4 million customers, listed on the stock market since October 28, 2026 with Reliance still the majority owner), a retailer with 19,947 stores, gas fields off India's east coast, the JioStar media business, a fast-growing packaged-goods arm, and a clean-energy factory complex still being commissioned. In the year to March 31, 2026 (FY26) it booked gross revenue of ₹11,75,919 crore ($124.0 billion at the company's own conversion), EBITDA of ₹2,07,911 crore and net profit of ₹95,754 crore. (One crore is ten million rupees; one lakh crore is a trillion rupees.)

The investment question is a hand of three different games played with one stack. The refinery is a global cyclical whose margins swing with diesel and jet-fuel spreads; in July–September 2026 those spreads reached historic highs with shipping through the Strait of Hormuz still disrupted, and O2C (oil-to-chemicals) EBITDA rose 67.9%, making the refinery the group's largest earner again at 40% of EBITDA. Jio and Retail are domestic growth businesses: they produced just over half of group EBITDA in April–June and 45% in July–September, when refining profit jumped and Retail's fell. And New Energy plus the AI data-centre build at Jamnagar are the next decade's bets, funded from today's cash flow — capex was ₹1,44,271 crore in FY26 and ₹76,835 crore in the first half of FY27. The market had not rewarded the mix: the stock ended October at ₹1,164.90, 25.8% below its December 2025 month-end. The quarter changed the mood: on November 2, the first session after the results, the stock rose 11.6%, its biggest one-day gain since April 2020, and it closed at ₹1,307.90 on November 3, 2026, still 16.7% below that December month-end. Since the November 2021 month-end it is up 17.8%, against 40.2% for the Nifty 50 index. The catalyst everyone was watching has happened: Jio Platforms listed on October 28, 2026 after selling ₹30,188 crore of new shares at ₹1,119 each, and the company says the listing made Jio India's second most valuable company, worth over ₹13 lakh crore ($137 billion).

The latest quarter. Reliance reported its July–September 2026 (Q2 FY27) results after the market closed on Friday, October 30, 2026 (PTI's report on them ran at 8:49 pm India time). EPS of ₹18.88 came in 9.1% above the ₹17.31 average analyst estimate compiled by Yahoo Finance, and profit of ₹29,550 crore (+33.8% YoY) and EBITDA of ₹62,409 crore (+23.9%) were both quarterly records. The stock closed 11.6% higher in the first session afterwards (₹1,164.90 on October 30 to ₹1,300.00 on November 2). Chairman Mukesh Ambani said "During 2Q FY27, our businesses delivered a strong performance despite a complex operating environment" and called Jio's listing "a historic moment in Reliance’s value creation journey" (Q2 media release). The group still gives no earnings or capex guidance: asked about full-year capex at the July 17 analyst meet, CFO V. Srikanth said "I have stayed away from commenting very specifically on capex". The Q2 results presentation offers three markers instead: oil-to-chemicals is on a pace of over ₹80,000 crore of EBITDA a year on its first-half results; New Energy projects are "poised for accelerated operationalization in coming quarters", with an earnings contribution from FY28; and in Retail, FY27 builds online scale and FY28–FY29 turn that scale into value.

The two numbers that tie it together

For a conglomerate that spends this heavily, watch whether the spending is paid for by the businesses, and what is left for the parent's shareholders.

Capex vs operating cash (FY26)
₹1,44,271 cr
FY26 capex excluding spectrum, against operating cash flow of ₹1,92,113 crore. Cash spent on property, plant, spectrum and intangibles was ₹1,22,916 crore, leaving about ₹69,197 crore of free cash flow (our calculation from the cash-flow statement). Q1 FY27 capex was ₹38,682 crore and Q2 ₹38,153 crore. In the first half of FY27 operating cash flow was ₹93,055 crore and cash capex ₹52,813 crore, about ₹40,242 crore of free cash flow (our calculation, half-year cash-flow statement).
Net debt (Sep 30, 2026)
₹1,17,140 cr
Gross debt ₹3,84,952 crore less cash of ₹2,67,812 crore. That is 0.47x the quarter's EBITDA at an annual rate (the company's measure), inside its own ceiling of under 1x. S&P rates Reliance A− and Moody's Baa1, which the chairman noted are two notches above India's sovereign rating; Moody's upgraded its foreign-currency debt to Baa1 during the April–June quarter (Q1 media release). Since then it has borrowed at home: ₹12,000 crore of five-year bonds at 7.47% on September 16 and ₹13,000 crore of ten-year bonds at 7.90% on October 1, both rated AAA/Stable by CRISIL and CARE (exchange filings). After the quarter ended, Jio used ₹27,500 crore of its IPO money to repay foreign-currency loans.
Profit that leaks to minorities
₹4,002 cr
Of Q2 FY27 net profit of ₹29,550 crore, ₹4,002 crore belonged to outside shareholders in subsidiaries such as Jio and Retail (₹2,250 crore in Q1); ₹25,548 crore belonged to Reliance's own shareholders. The new shares Jio sold in its IPO enlarge the outside holders' slice of Jio's profit from the October–December quarter on.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

542M-customer Jio network Nationwide 5G network (300M users) Integrated Jamnagar O2C complex 19,947-store retail footprint A− / Baa1 credit, above the sovereign Operating cash flow ₹1.92 lakh cr Cross-selling across 400M+ retail customers

The moat is scale paid for in advance. Jio's network and spectrum are sunk costs that a challenger would have to match before charging a rupee; JioMart now fulfils orders from more than 5,000 stores in over 1,000 cities; and the refinery's flexibility showed again in Q2 FY27, when it bought crude from Latin America, North America and the Mediterranean to sidestep pricier Gulf grades and freight rates up 15–20 times, and sent its exports to whichever markets paid the most. The company says it keeps capex within operating cash flow, so the old businesses pay for the new ones. The weakness is the same feature seen from the other side: capital allocation is controlled by one family, and minority shareholders have to trust that the next ₹1.4 lakh crore a year earns more than its cost.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The refinery is a policy business in a crisis. When fuel prices spike, the Indian state acts: SAED came back on diesel, petrol and jet fuel, Reliance diverted propane and butane to LPG for state-owned retailers, and Jio-bp held pump prices and absorbed under-recoveries. Record cracks did not all reach the income statement.
02
Retail margins are going backwards. Retail EBITDA before a one-time charge fell 8.6% YoY in Q2 FY27 with the margin down 120 bps to 7.8%, which management puts down to planned spending on online commerce, input-cost inflation it absorbed for customers, and festivals falling in the next quarter. Until online orders scale, that cost weighs on the whole retail business. Retail CFO Dinesh Taluja told analysts on July 17 that the dark-store build would continue "in the next nine to 10 months" and that "the percentages for the short term could come down"; the plan to double operating EBITDA by FY29 sets no margin target.
03
Returns lag the size. Trailing return on equity is about 9.1% (our calculation: ₹82,110 crore of TTM profit to owners ÷ ₹9,06,204 crore of net worth at September 30, 2026). That return sits on five years of capex totalling ₹6,48,428 crore, which the chairman said is more than any company in India.
04
Mature gas fields. KG D6 output fell 9.0% YoY in Q2 FY27 and gas realisations fell. The E&P segment's 81% margin will shrink in rupees unless new wells offset the decline.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort comparisons. First, the ₹8,924 crore gain on sale of listed investments in Q1 FY26 inflates last year's first half: 1H FY27 profit of ₹52,746 crore is 20.0% above 1H FY26 on a recurring basis (₹43,951 crore) but level with the ₹52,875 crore reported. Q2 is unaffected. Second, a ₹108 crore one-time impairment in Retail in Q2 FY27 (7-Eleven, per the results presentation). Third, the 1:1 bonus issue allotted on October 29, 2024 doubled the share count to 13.53 billion, so pre-2024 per-share figures must be halved to compare. Fourth, the demerger of the consumer-brands business into Reliance Consumer Products: Retail's 6.4% growth is measured against a year-earlier figure adjusted for it. Earlier, in July 2023, the financial-services arm was demerged as Jio Financial Services, one share for each Reliance share held on the July 20, 2023 record date.

HOW TO READ THE GAP
●
A 27% gap is a bet on the sum of the parts. Valued piece by piece — Jio, Retail, O2C, New Energy — a conglomerate can look worth more than its share price. Jio's listing now puts a market price on the biggest piece; read the remaining gap as a measure of how much of that value the market thinks the parent's shareholders will see.
●
Estimates up, some targets down. The stock rose 12.0% between the two editions and Jefferies raised its EBITDA estimates after the beat, yet Nomura and Emkay lowered their targets. The average Yahoo target, ₹1,666, sits slightly below the ₹1,674.72 it showed on October 6.
●
No dissent in the count. Every rating in Bloomberg's compilation is Buy, yet Yahoo's lowest target sits only 3.2% above the close and Avendus Spark's is below it.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 3, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of ₹1,110.09 (on today's share basis) to the November 3, 2026 settled close of ₹1,307.90, Reliance's price rose 17.8% in five years. Dividends of ₹25 per current share over the window add roughly two percentage points. Over the same span the Nifty 50 rose 40.2% (16,983.20 at the November 2021 month-end to 23,810.85 on November 3, 2026). The path was a range, not a trend: month-end closes between ₹1,071.86 (February 2023) and ₹1,570.40 (December 2025), with two failed breakouts — mid-2024 and late 2025 — each given back within months.

The 6-period monthly RSI reached 24.5 at the October 2026 month-end, the lowest reading in the five-year series, and the November 2 jump lifted it to 50.9 on the November 3 close, back to neutral. The 10-month moving average, about ₹1,302.83, now sits just below the price (by 0.4%). In poker terms the stock was card-dead for ten months — down 25.8% from the December 2025 month-end to the October 2026 month-end while the business reported record quarterly EBITDA — then won one big pot. One session does not make a trend.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 21.6x trailing earnings, 19.1x this fiscal year's consensus and 17.7x next year's for a group that just reported its most profitable quarter ever, whose largest consumer business, Jio, grew EBITDA 16.6% at a 54.1% margin and is now listed at a value the company puts above ₹13 lakh crore, and whose balance sheet carries net debt of only 0.47x annualised EBITDA with ratings above the sovereign. The quarter beat the consensus EPS estimate by 9.1%, the refinery is running at over ₹80,000 crore of EBITDA a year on its first-half pace, and every rating in Bloomberg's count is Buy with Yahoo's average target at ₹1,666 (+27.4%). Reliance's Jio stake alone is worth close to half the group's ₹17.70 lakh crore market cap at the listing value, New Energy is moving from building to selling, and the past twelve months produced about ₹68,000 crore of free cash flow after ₹1.2 lakh crore of cash capex.

Bear Case

Five years of capex totalling ₹6,48,428 crore have produced a share price up 17.8% against 40.2% for the Nifty 50, and a 9.1% return on equity. The record quarter came from refining spreads set by a war, which can close as fast as they opened. Retail went backwards: EBITDA down 8.6% before a ₹108 crore write-down, 222 fewer stores, and Retail's own CFO said in July the margin percentage could come down in the short term. New Energy's earnings are now dated FY28, not the FY27 Anant Ambani gave at the June AGM. The list of new ventures keeps growing (a fighter-engine partnership with Rolls-Royce, explosives and fertilisers added to the company's objects) while management declines to guide on capex. KG D6 gas is in decline. The Jio IPO raised money for Jio, not for Reliance shareholders — ₹27,500 crore of it repaid Jio's loans — and outside shareholders already took ₹4,002 crore of Q2 profit, a slice the IPO enlarges. A 0.46% dividend yield means holders depend on management's next bets — AI data centres and batteries — earning more than their cost.

Top 3 Risks

01
Capital allocation and the holding-company discount. Group capex of ₹1,44,271 crore in FY26 and ₹76,835 crore in the first half of FY27, plus new commitments (120 GWh of batteries, 20 GW of solar, AI compute), leave little room for payouts. The list widened after the print: an intended aero-engine partnership with Rolls-Royce (August 14) and shareholder approval to enter ammonium nitrate, explosives and fertilisers (August 20), and the CFO has declined to give a capex figure. If returns stay near 9%, the discount to analysts' sum-of-the-parts can persist indefinitely.
02
Energy-shock policy risk. The same crisis that lifted cracks brought SAED back, forced LPG diversions and pump-price restraint, and cut domestic polymer demand (down 21.7% in April–June and 3.5% in July–September). A prolonged shock could see more intervention; a quick resolution would take the cracks back down. The export levies move every fortnight: on September 16 the diesel duty was cut to ₹20 a litre from ₹25 and jet fuel to ₹15 from ₹19.
03
Execution on New Energy and AI. The Jio listing is done (October 28). What remains are New Energy, which the Q2 presentation dates to an earnings contribution from FY28, and the first 120 MW of AI compute promised by the end of 2026. Further delays would remove the catalysts the bull case now relies on.
Industry Commentary
The Hormuz shock: windfall and windfall tax
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

In July–September 2026 global oil demand fell 3.4 million barrels a day from a year earlier to 102.5 mb/d and refinery throughput fell 3.5 mb/d to 80.9 mb/d, after attacks on refineries in the Middle East and Russia, according to Reliance's own market review, while Brent averaged $97. Fuel cracks reached historic highs. For a complex coastal refinery that can switch crudes, that is the best margin environment in years. But the state's priority is supply at stable prices: hence the SAED levies and the fuel sold below cost at Reliance's own Jio-bp outlets. This quarter more of the windfall got through: O2C EBITDA rose 67.9% and its margin 160 bps to 10.9%, against 8.4% in April–June. Management expected the tightness to last: at the July 17 analyst meet Srinivas Tuttagunta, COO of Refining & Marketing, pointed to refining capacity lost in Kuwait and Bahrain and to Russia having "lost more than 40% of its capacity", and concluded that "all these point towards reasonably strong cracks in our view, and that is what the market suggests."

Indian telecom: Jio pricing up while adding users
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Jio's ARPU has risen from ₹211.4 a year earlier to ₹218.8 while it kept adding subscribers (36 million in twelve months) and cut churn to 1.6% from 1.9%. Bharti Airtel is the other large listed operator ($136.0B market value at the November 3 close) and Vodafone Idea is far smaller ($14.1B). Jio's own numbers show it lifting ARPU while still adding customers. The next leg — fixed broadband via JioAirFiber, which the company says holds over 78% of the fixed-wireless market, enterprise and cloud, AI services — is what Jio's new public shareholders are now paying for.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At 21.6x trailing earnings and 1.95x book, Reliance trades above the Nifty 50 on earnings (19.71x) but below it on book value (2.92x), and far above the oil-and-gas index (9.93x, 1.36x): the market prices it as half refiner, half consumer company. The bull framing: the consumer half earned 45–51% of EBITDA in the last two quarters and Jio's EBITDA grew 16.6% in July–September, so a single price-to-earnings ratio for the whole group undervalues it, and a listed Jio now forces the comparison every trading day. The bear framing: a parent that keeps reinvesting at single-digit returns deserves a discount to its parts, and listing a subsidiary does not change who allocates the cash. The way to tell the two apart is not the P/E but the return on equity: watch whether 9.1% starts rising as New Energy and AI assets begin to earn.
