01 Company Overview

Rheinmetall started life in 1889 as the Rheinische Metallwaaren- und Maschinenfabrik, set up to supply ammunition to the German Empire. Today it is a defence company and nothing else: the car-parts business it ran for decades was reclassified as a discontinued operation in December 2025 and agreed in June 2026 to be sold to a subsidiary of the Munich investor AEQUITA, with closing expected in the fourth quarter. What remains builds armoured vehicles (Boxer, Puma, Lynx), military trucks, tank and artillery guns, ammunition and the powder that goes into it, air-defence systems (Skynex, Skyranger), battlefield digital networks, satellites and, since buying the Bremen shipbuilder NVL in February 2026, warships. FY2025 sales were €9,935M, up 29%, with an operating result of €1,841M (18.5% margin). The shares have sat in Germany's DAX since 2023 and the EURO STOXX 50 since 2025.

The poker frame is simple: the order book is a monster hand, and the market has spent a year arguing about the price of the chips. Rheinmetall's backlog was €80.5B at June 30, 2026, about seven years of trailing sales, and management guides to roughly €50B of sales by 2030 at a margin above 20%. Yet the stock closed at €956.90, down 51.8% from its €1,984.50 month-end peak of September 2025, after a year that brought a slow first quarter, the government's cancellation of the F126 frigate programme and a first half with €1.6B of negative operating free cash flow. The quarter itself was not the problem: the second-quarter operating result of €562M came in almost 20% above the €469.9M market expectation the company cited. Over five years it is still up more than elevenfold. The question now is whether the backlog turns into cash at the margins promised, or whether 2025 priced in a decade of perfect execution.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

€80.5B backlog Ammunition & powder capacity Boxer, Puma & Leopard 2 A8 work German home customer (38% of sales) Plants across Europe incl. the east Skynex / Skyranger air defence Multi-year framework contracts

The real moat is capacity that already exists. Armies rearming in a hurry buy from suppliers whose factories are already running, and Rheinmetall has them, from Unterlüß to Spain, Hungary and South Africa, with more being built, such as the new ammunition plant in Lithuania named in the H1 report. Framework agreements then lock in call-offs for years: the frame backlog alone was €22.9B at end-2025. Germany's own budget adds visibility: the July 2 investor recap cited a 2027 draft defence budget of close to €145B, about 21% above 2026. The weakness is the flip side of the same fact: a handful of governments decide the revenue, and they can delay, re-scope or cancel, as the F126 showed.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Governments write the cheques and can tear them up. Germany took 38% of H1 2026 sales and, by the CEO's account on the August 6 call, about 60% of the backlog. The F126 cancellation cost up to €300M of 2026 sales; the next test is Arminius, the Boxer order, which still needs parliamentary approval.
02
Cash is lagging profit. H1 operating free cash flow was −€1,616M while the operating result was +€786M. The company targets a cash conversion rate (free cash flow ÷ operating result) above 40% for 2026 and above 50% on a three-year rolling basis by 2030. The balance sheet went from net cash of €369M to net debt of €2.72B in six months, part of it funding NVL.
03
Ukraine is a customer, not just a reason. The FY2025 release named Ukraine the most important customer country for Weapon and Ammunition, the highest-margin segment. Any change in the war, or in who pays for Ukraine's ammunition, reaches the profit line directly.
04
Execution at a scale never attempted. Going from €9.9B of sales in 2025 to about €50B by 2030 means new plants, a workforce the company plans to take from 34,000 to roughly 60,000–70,000 (headcount, continuing operations; September 2026 presentation) and a shipbuilder it has owned since February. Construction of several energetics plants has already slipped into 2027.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the car-parts exit: the civilian business lost €341M in FY2025 (mostly an impairment when it was classified for sale) and €160M in H1 2026, which is why total EPS (€15.62 TTM) sits far below continuing EPS (€26.47). The sale to AEQUITA is priced provisionally at €350M. Second, NVL: the shipbuilder added €334M of sales and €33M of operating result from March and raised goodwill from €1,354M to €2,198M, so H1 growth is not all organic. Third, convertible bonds: holders converted €239M of bonds by the end of June and the last €4M in early July, raising the share count by 787,033 to 46,789,567 (about 1.7%) and moving that debt into equity.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of €84.78 to the October 2, 2026 close of €956.90, Rheinmetall's share price rose 1,028.7%, a multiple of 11.3. Dividends of €32.90 per share were paid along the way (€3.30, €4.30, €5.70, €8.10 and €11.50, each May from 2022 to 2026). The DAX rose 65.3% over the same window. The shape is three acts: a step change in February–March 2022, when the shares gained 45.1% and then 43.8% in consecutive months around Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine; a steady climb through 2023 and 2024; and a vertical 2025, when the stock went from €614.60 at end-2024 to €1,984.50 at end-September 2025, a 223% gain in nine months. Since then it has given back 51.8%.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how stretched 2025 was: it reached 97.4 in May 2025, the highest reading in the series. The unwind took it to 26.4 in June 2026, the lowest, and it stands at 29.8 now, still in oversold territory. The price is 24.6% below its 10-month moving average of about €1,269, so the long-term trend is still down. No month-end close since January 2026 (€1,781.50) has been higher.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a backlog of €80.5B, seven years of sales, at 0.56 times its face value, with management guiding to sales roughly five times 2025's level by 2030 at margins above 20%. Profit is already compounding faster than sales: operating result +74% in H1 on sales +39%, a record 17.1% Q2 margin, and the highest-margin segment (ammunition, 23.7% in H1, 29% in FY2025) is the one adding capacity fastest. Germany's draft 2027 defence budget is up about 21%, NATO's core target is rising to 3.5% of GDP, and the lowest analyst target MarketScreener shows is above the banner-date close. The 2026 cash drain is mostly inventory and advance-payment timing; a €3B-plus Arminius down payment would reverse much of it in a single quarter.

Bear Case

The 2025 price assumed flawless execution, and 2026 has delivered the opposite: an 8% first quarter, a cancelled frigate programme, guidance cut by €300M, and €1.6B of cash out the door in six months while net debt rose to €2.72B. A backlog is a promise from governments, and governments re-scope. About 60% of it is German, the profit engine's most important customer country in 2025 was Ukraine, and the €50B 2030 target requires a workforce roughly double today's and plants that are already running late. Even after a 52% fall the shares trade at 36 times trailing continuing earnings, 61 times total earnings, and a free-cash-flow yield of 0.5%. If the war in Ukraine winds down or budgets slip, the multiple has further to compress.

Top 3 Risks

01
Procurement decisions. The F126 showed that a signed programme can disappear; Arminius, the single largest order in the pipeline, still needs a parliamentary vote. One delayed or downsized German programme moves both the order book and the 2026–27 sales trajectory.
02
Cash conversion. H1 operating free cash flow of −€1,616M against a 2026 cash-conversion target above 40%. If advance payments do not arrive in Q4 as management expects, the balance sheet, now at 0.52 debt-to-equity, keeps absorbing the growth.
03
Ukraine exposure and the peace question. Ukraine was the most important customer country of the highest-margin segment in 2025. A ceasefire would not end European rearmament, but it would change the urgency, and the stock is priced on urgency.
Industry Commentary
European budgets: the tailwind is written into law
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Rheinmetall's September 2026 investor presentation notes that NATO is lifting its core spending target to 3.5% of GDP by 2035, and that Germany is ordering now to reach 3.5% as early as 2029. The July 2 recap cited a German 2027 draft defence budget of close to €145B, about 21% above 2026. The EU's SAFE loan programme is already producing orders: Romania alone placed Lynx vehicles (298), four naval vessels (€920M) and medium-calibre ammunition under it in H1 2026. Demand is not the question for this industry. Turning it into deliveries, on time and at the promised margin, is.

Procurement reality: budgets are not orders
ONGOING HEADWIND

The 2026 lesson for European defence stocks is that a bigger budget does not guarantee any one contractor's programme. The F126 frigate was cancelled outright over delays and expected cost overruns; Rheinmetall's recap said the ministry also cited possible recourse issues with the shipbuilder Damen. On the August 6 call a Morgan Stanley analyst asked about press reports of a German draft showing lower cash outlays for ammunition and land systems; Papperger answered that most tank-ammunition contracts were already signed, that nobody had called a halt to the talks on further rounds, and that with the ammunition backlog as high as it is he saw no impact over the next years. Across the listed peer group, one-year price returns run from +8.2% (Saab) to −57.8% (Renk), averaging −19.9%, while the DAX rose 3.3%: one way to read it is that the market has stopped paying for orders and started waiting for deliveries.

Valuation after the unwind: bull and bear framing
DEBATE

At €956.90 Rheinmetall trades at 36.2 times trailing continuing EPS of €26.47, with an average analyst target 68% higher. The bull framing: earnings are growing far faster than the multiple is falling. Guidance implies another year of roughly 40% sales growth at a higher margin, so the multiple on 2026 earnings will be much lower than the trailing one, and the 2030 plan does not need to be met in full for today's price to look low. The bear framing: the 2030 plan is a five-fold scale-up dependent on a few governments, the first half burned €1.6B of cash, and a P/E in the mid-30s still assumes most of it goes right. The test is not the P/E. It is whether Q4 brings the Arminius signature, the down payment and a cash-conversion figure back above 40%. All three are dated, and the market will know by early 2027.
