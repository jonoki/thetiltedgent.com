01 Company Overview

Richemont owns Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels, and that is most of the story. Its four Jewellery Maisons (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Buccellati and Vhernier) sold €16.5B of the Group's €22.4B in the year to March 31, 2026, and earned €5.0B of operating profit at a 30.5% margin. Everything else (Vacheron Constantin, Jaeger-LeCoultre, IWC and the other watchmakers, plus Montblanc, Chloé and the fashion houses) made €5.9B of sales and roughly €11M of combined operating profit. Richemont is, in practice, a jewellery company with a watch and fashion portfolio attached. It reports in euros, its shares trade in Swiss francs on the SIX Swiss Exchange, and at the November 20, 2026 close of CHF 169.00 the market valued the whole group at about CHF 99.9B ($118.4B).

The latest print, for the six months to September 30, 2026 (fiscal H1 2027, released November 13), shows the jewellery engine still growing fast, but slower than in the spring. Group sales rose 16% in euros (17% at constant exchange rates) to €12.28B; the Jewellery Maisons grew 19% at constant rates, and operating profit rose 33% to €3.13B, a 25.5% margin. The second quarter was the slower one: sales of €5.95B grew 13% against 20% in the first quarter, and jewellery grew 14% against 24%. Reuters reported that total second-quarter sales beat the €5.89B consensus from Visible Alpha, while jewellery missed the 16% growth an RBC poll expected. Part of the profit gain is one-time: €229M of positive tariff-related items sit in gross profit, and without them operating profit was about €2.9B, a 23.6% margin. The shares fell 3.8% on the day (CHF 176.15 → 169.45, November 13) after trading as low as 160.30. Management gives no numeric guidance: Johann Rupert told analysts, "I don't want you to build your models and then blame us later on." The question for the next print is how much of the margin gain holds without one-off items. In FY2026, gold prices, a weaker dollar and US duties cut the gross margin from 66.9% to 64.4%, and operating profit rose only 1% on 5% higher sales; in H1 FY2027, without the tariff items, gross margin still fell 70 basis points (0.7 percentage points).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Cartier & Van Cleef icons Pricing power (5–6% in FY26) 78% direct-to-client Own workshops & manufacture €7.7B net cash Family control (50.6% votes)

The moat is designs that do not go out of style. The lines that carried FY2026 were long-running icons (Love and Panthère at Cartier, Alhambra and Perlée at Van Cleef & Arpels), extended with new pieces each year, so the Maisons do not have to reinvent the range each season and can raise prices gradually. In FY2026 that meant 5–6% price increases (Bos) while volume still grew, and jewellery sales rose by double digits at constant rates in every quarter. Richemont sells 78% of its goods directly (H1 FY2027, up 2 percentage points), through its own boutiques and websites, so it controls price and presentation. It also builds rather than buys: Bos said the Group prefers to add its own workshops "rather than to acquire". The weakness is concentration. One business area earns all the profit, and its main input, gold, is priced in a market Richemont does not control. Rupert's own summary of FY2026 was that gold and the dollar hurt more than tariffs.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Gold and currency squeeze the margin. FY2026 gross margin fell 250 basis points to 64.4%; in H1 FY2027 it was 66.5%, but 70 basis points lower than a year earlier once €229M of one-time tariff items are removed, with gold about 40% above the prior year in the half (CFO Burkhart Grund, results call). The company named "Adverse exchange rate movements, combined with higher raw material costs, and to a lesser extent, additional US customs duties". Rupert on the call: "When the gold price went up, it was basically the dollar down and vice versa. This time, Luca, we had both up."
02
Two of three business areas barely earn. The watchmakers made a 3.4% margin and Other lost €96M in FY2026. If jewellery demand cools, nothing else in the Group carries the profit line.
03
US tariffs and the Middle East. Rupert put the tariff cost at "300 million" and said: "I can give you my word, we did not use pricing to offset tariffs." Middle East & Africa was 9% of FY2026 sales, fell 3% in the March quarter after the conflict began and fell 2% in the six months to September; the Chairman's FY2026 outlook: "uncertainty is likely to persist, not least in relation to developments in the Middle East."
04
Outside shareholders do not control the company. Compagnie Financière Rupert owns 10.18% of the capital and 50.60% of the votes through the unlisted B shares. That keeps the long view; it also means no bid premium and limited say for holders of the listed A shares. The September 2026 appointment of Anton Rupert as Co-Deputy Chairman keeps the family at the top of the Board.

Notable One-Off Items

The half-year to September 2026 carries one one-time item: €229M of positive tariff-related items booked in gross profit, worth about 190 basis points of gross margin. Without it operating profit was about €2.9B (a 23.6% margin, up 140 basis points) and gross margin fell 70 basis points. CFO Burkhart Grund said on the call: "We don't expect any additional tariff refunds in the second half of the year." In FY2026, three items distorted the numbers. First, €164M of non-recurring costs inside operating profit: a €99M charge for impairments of non-current assets and the €59M write-down on the Baume & Mercier sale. Second, the currency hedge: a €374M gain sits in finance income, offset by €534M of exchange losses on monetary items, so operating profit shows the full currency hit while the hedge result shows up lower in the income statement. Third, YNAP: FY2025 profit included a €1.0B loss on the online business; FY2026 profit from discontinued operations was a €20M gain, which is why total profit rose 27% while profit from continuing operations fell 8%. Richemont also publishes headline earnings for its Johannesburg listing: diluted headline EPS was €6.114 in FY2026 (against €6.327 in FY2025) and €4.26 in H1 FY2027 (against €3.00).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels are still growing fast: +19% at constant rates over the half-year, +14% in the second quarter, with the Americas up 23% and Japan up 30% at constant rates. Jewellery earned a 35.3% margin in the half (33.4% without one-time items) and the Maisons have shown they can raise prices 5–6% without losing volume. The Group sits on €7.7B of net cash after paying its dividend, has sold YNAP and Baume & Mercier and raised its ordinary dividend 10% with a special on top. The watchmakers' profit tripled to €150M and their growth reached +12% in Q2, so the weakest area is becoming less of a drag. If the margin holds without one-off items, the forward multiple of about 24x on rising earnings looks reasonable against Hermès at 28.5x trailing; 20 of 27 analysts rate it a Buy, with an average target 22% above the price.

Bear Case

At about 26x trailing earnings the stock already prices in the jewellery boom, and the second quarter showed growth cooling from 24% to 14%, below what analysts polled by RBC expected. Half-year operating profit rose 33%, but €229M came from one-time tariff items, and without them gross margin fell 70 basis points as gold stayed about 40% above the prior year; management will not guide. One business area earns the profit; the watchmakers make 8.8% and the fashion houses sit near break-even. Mainland China fell 3% in the half, the Middle East conflict cut tourist spending in the region, and US duties add cost the Chairman says was not passed on in prices. Control sits with the Rupert family's partnership, so outside holders have little influence. The analyst panel is less unanimous than the label: about a quarter say Hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margin squeeze from gold and currencies. The half-year operating margin of 25.5% includes €229M of one-time tariff items; without them it was 23.6%, and gross margin fell 70 basis points. The CFO said no further tariff refunds are expected in the second half, and that gold was about 40% above the prior year, so the next full results (May 2027) have no such help.
02
A jewellery slowdown. With three-quarters of sales and essentially all the profit in one business area, any cooling in Cartier or Van Cleef demand, from the US consumer, Chinese clients or tourists, hits the whole group. Jewellery growth already slowed from +24% in the first quarter to +14% in the second.
03
Geopolitics and trade. US duties cost about 300 million per the Chairman, the Middle East region is 9% of sales (and fell 2% in the half-year), and a Swiss-made, euro-reporting, dollar-selling group is exposed to every currency move between them.
Industry Commentary
Jewellery is where luxury is growing
STRUCTURAL SUPPORT

Richemont's own numbers show a split market. Jewellery Maisons grew 14% at constant rates in FY2026, 24% in Q1 FY2027 and 14% in Q2 (19% over the half); the Other area, mostly fashion and accessories, grew 3% in FY2026 and 9% in the half. Bos told analysts in May that in America there has been "a refocus towards jewellery, watches, you know, more iconic products with a long-term value compared to probably more fashion and accessories". Analysts on the call noted rivals moving into jewellery and buying workshops; Bos said Richemont prefers to build its own capacity.

Swiss watches: a slow recovery
OPEN QUESTION

The FY2026 release called the last two years "a challenging 24-month period for the watch market". Richemont's watchmakers went from −7% at constant rates in Q1 FY2026 to +8% in Q1 FY2027 and +12% in Q2. Margins are following: 8.8% in the half to September 2026 (7.7% without one-time items), against 3.4% for FY2026. Selling Baume & Mercier, which Richemont describes as having an "accessible positioning in the luxury watch segment", leaves a smaller, higher-priced watch group.

China: consolidation, not a rebound
NEAR-TERM DRAG

China, Hong Kong and Macau combined grew 3% in FY2026, led by Hong Kong, and by double digits in Q1 FY2027; in the half to September 2026 mainland China fell 3% while Hong Kong and Macau grew strongly (Bos on the results call; Investing.com transcript). Management is not planning on a return to the old boom: brands over-expanded into smaller cities, Bos said (on November 13: "The confidence factor in mainland China is still not at its all-time high," Reuters), and Cartier cut five points of sale in the country in FY2026 while upgrading its main stores. Local jewellery brands have gained ground; Bos's answer is new collections from the existing Maisons rather than buying Chinese brands.
