01 Company Overview

Richemont owns Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels, and that is most of the story. Its four Jewellery Maisons (Cartier, Van Cleef & Arpels, Buccellati and Vhernier) sold €16.5B of the Group's €22.4B in the year to March 31, 2026, and earned €5.0B of operating profit at a 30.5% margin. Everything else (Vacheron Constantin, Jaeger-LeCoultre, IWC and the other watchmakers, plus Montblanc, Chloé and the fashion houses) made €5.9B of sales and roughly €11M of combined operating profit. Richemont is, in practice, a jewellery company with a watch and fashion portfolio attached. It reports in euros, its shares trade in Swiss francs on the SIX Swiss Exchange, and at the October 2, 2026 close of CHF 172.85 the market valued the whole group at about CHF 102.2B ($123.1B).

The latest print shows the jewellery engine still accelerating. In the quarter to June 30, 2026 (Richemont's fiscal Q1 2027), Group sales rose 20% at constant exchange rates (+17% in euros) to €6.33B, the fastest of the last five quarters, and the Jewellery Maisons grew 24%, which the company called "a seventh consecutive quarter of double-digit growth". Every region grew by double digits except the Middle East & Africa, which still grew 3% despite the conflict there. The shares rose 6.7% on the day (CHF 183.35 → 195.60, July 15). The question for the next print is margins, not demand. In FY2026, gold prices, a weaker dollar and US duties cut the gross margin from 66.9% to 64.4%, and operating profit rose only 1% on 5% higher sales. Management gives no numeric guidance; asked about FY2027 margins in May, CFO Burkhart Grund said: "No, I mean, I do not think I can seriously answer that."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Cartier & Van Cleef icons Pricing power (5–6% in FY26) 77% direct-to-client Own workshops & manufacture €8.5B net cash Family control (50.6% votes)

The moat is designs that do not go out of style. The lines that carried FY2026 were long-running icons (Love and Panthère at Cartier, Alhambra and Perlée at Van Cleef & Arpels), extended with new pieces each year, so the Maisons do not have to reinvent the range each season and can raise prices gradually. In FY2026 that meant 5–6% price increases (Bos) while volume still grew, and jewellery sales rose by double digits at constant rates in every quarter. Richemont sells 77% of its goods directly, through its own boutiques and websites, so it controls price and presentation. It also builds rather than buys: Bos said the Group prefers to add its own workshops "rather than to acquire". The weakness is concentration. One business area earns all the profit, and its main input, gold, is priced in a market Richemont does not control. Rupert's own summary of FY2026 was that gold and the dollar hurt more than tariffs.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Gold and currency squeeze the margin. FY2026 gross margin fell 250 basis points to 64.4%. The company named "Adverse exchange rate movements, combined with higher raw material costs, and to a lesser extent, additional US customs duties". Rupert on the call: "When the gold price went up, it was basically the dollar down and vice versa. This time, Luca, we had both up."
02
Two of three business areas barely earn. The watchmakers made a 3.4% margin and Other lost €96M in FY2026. If jewellery demand cools, nothing else in the Group carries the profit line.
03
US tariffs and the Middle East. Rupert put the tariff cost at "300 million" and said: "I can give you my word, we did not use pricing to offset tariffs." Middle East & Africa was 9% of FY2026 sales and fell 3% in the March quarter after the conflict began; the Chairman's FY2026 outlook: "uncertainty is likely to persist, not least in relation to developments in the Middle East."
04
Outside shareholders do not control the company. Compagnie Financière Rupert owns 10.18% of the capital and 50.60% of the votes through the unlisted B shares. That keeps the long view; it also means no bid premium and limited say for holders of the listed A shares. The September 2026 appointment of Anton Rupert as Co-Deputy Chairman keeps the family at the top of the Board.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the FY2026 numbers. First, €164M of non-recurring costs inside operating profit: a €99M charge for impairments of non-current assets and the €59M write-down on the Baume & Mercier sale. Second, the currency hedge: a €374M gain sits in finance income, offset by €534M of exchange losses on monetary items, so operating profit shows the full currency hit while the hedge result shows up lower in the income statement. Third, YNAP: FY2025 profit included a €1.0B loss on the online business; FY2026 profit from discontinued operations was a €20M gain, which is why total profit rose 27% while profit from continuing operations fell 8%. Richemont also publishes headline earnings for its Johannesburg listing: diluted headline EPS was €6.114, against €6.327 in FY2025.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Cartier and Van Cleef & Arpels are growing fast: +24% at constant rates in the latest quarter, the seventh double-digit quarter in a row, with every region up. Jewellery earns a 30% margin and the Maisons have shown they can raise prices 5–6% without losing volume. The Group sits on €9.1B of net cash, has sold YNAP, is selling Baume & Mercier and raised its ordinary dividend 10% with a special on top. The watchmakers are recovering in sequence (+8% in Q1), so the weakest area is becoming less of a drag. If the half-year margin holds up, the forward multiple of about 25x on rising earnings looks reasonable against Hermès at 30x trailing; 17 of 26 analysts rate it a Buy, with an average target 22% above the price.

Bear Case

At 31x trailing earnings the stock already prices in the jewellery boom. FY2026 showed how little of a sales surge reaches profit when gold and the dollar move the wrong way: sales +5%, operating profit +1%, gross margin −250 basis points, and management will not guide. One business area earns the profit; the watchmakers make 3.4% and the fashion houses lose money. China is being right-sized, not regrown, the Middle East conflict cut tourist spending in the region, and US duties add cost the Chairman says was not passed on in prices. Control sits with the Rupert family's partnership, so outside holders have little influence. The analyst panel is less unanimous than the label: a third say Hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Margin squeeze from gold and currencies. The November 13 interim results are the first test of whether Q1's sales growth reached profit. Another half like H2 FY2026 (18.1% operating margin) would challenge a 25x forward multiple.
02
A jewellery slowdown. With three-quarters of sales and essentially all the profit in one business area, any cooling in Cartier or Van Cleef demand, from the US consumer, Chinese clients or tourists, hits the whole group. Comparisons get harder after +24%.
03
Geopolitics and trade. US duties cost about 300 million per the Chairman, the Middle East region is 9% of sales, and a Swiss-made, euro-reporting, dollar-selling group is exposed to every currency move between them.
Industry Commentary
Jewellery is where luxury is growing
STRUCTURAL SUPPORT

Richemont's own numbers show a split market. Jewellery Maisons grew 14% at constant rates in FY2026 and 24% in Q1 FY2027; the Other area, mostly fashion and accessories, grew 3% and 9%. Bos told analysts in May that in America there has been "a refocus towards jewellery, watches, you know, more iconic products with a long-term value compared to probably more fashion and accessories". Analysts on the call noted rivals moving into jewellery and buying workshops; Bos said Richemont prefers to build its own capacity.

Swiss watches: a slow recovery
OPEN QUESTION

The FY2026 release called the last two years "a challenging 24-month period for the watch market". Richemont's watchmakers went from −7% at constant rates in Q1 FY2026 to +8% in Q1 FY2027, led by the Americas and Japan, while China, Hong Kong and Macau combined still declined. Margins have not followed yet: 3.4% for FY2026. Selling Baume & Mercier, which Richemont describes as having an "accessible positioning in the luxury watch segment", leaves a smaller, higher-priced watch group.

China: consolidation, not a rebound
NEAR-TERM DRAG

China, Hong Kong and Macau combined grew 3% in FY2026, led by Hong Kong, and by double digits in Q1 FY2027. Management is not planning on a return to the old boom: brands over-expanded into smaller cities, Bos said, and Cartier cut five points of sale in the country in FY2026 while upgrading its main stores. Local jewellery brands have gained ground; Bos's answer is new collections from the existing Maisons rather than buying Chinese brands.
