01 Company Overview

ResMed makes the machines and masks that keep people with obstructive sleep apnea breathing through the night, and it wires almost all of them to the cloud. It reports two segments — Sleep & Breathing Health (devices, masks and accessories) and Residential Care Software. Fiscal 2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) revenue was $5.653B, up 10% reported and 8% in constant currency, with GAAP diluted EPS of $10.43 and non-GAAP EPS of $11.17 (+17%). The company says more than 35 million of its devices are cloud-connected and more than 37 million patients sit in its AirView ecosystem.

This is the hand the market keeps misreading. In 2021 ResMed's closest rival, Philips Respironics, recalled millions of breathing devices over degrading foam, and a 2024 US consent decree restricted its sale of new CPAP machines in the United States. ResMed was left holding most of the table. Then, in August 2023, the fear flipped: weight-loss drugs (GLP-1s) might shrink the patient pool itself, and the stock fell from a $222.35 July close to $159.59 in a single month. It climbed back to $274.51 by August 2025, only to lose 30.6% into a $190.57 May 2026 close — through a quarter of 11% revenue growth, an earnings beat, a CFO handover and fresh GLP-1 worry. The settled close is now $224.99. The company's own counter-evidence: patients with an OSA diagnosis who were prescribed a GLP-1 drug showed a 1,070 bps higher likelihood of starting PAP therapy. The debate is whether GLP-1s are a leak in the funnel or a new door into it — and whether the July 2026 ventilator recall is a one-year dent or a crack in the quality story.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

35M+ connected installed base Recurring mask & accessory resupply AirView / myAir adherence data Rival weakened by 2021 recall FDA-cleared product portfolio 62.4% non-GAAP gross margin Net cash balance sheet (D/E 0.13)

The moat is not the blower — it is the loop. A patient is diagnosed, a provider sets up a connected device, AirView reports nightly usage to the provider and, in many cases, the insurer, and the resupply schedule for masks and cushions runs off that data. Every part of the loop makes the next part cheaper for ResMed and more annoying to replace for the provider. The 2021 Philips recall and the 2024 consent decree handed ResMed years in which its main rival was restricted from selling new CPAP machines in the US. The vulnerability is that the loop starts with a diagnosis — and anything that shrinks the number of people who need one attacks the top of the funnel, not the margin.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
GLP-1 drugs and the size of the patient pool. Some research suggests weight-loss drugs may reduce sleep-apnea severity over time. ResMed's own data points the other way — GLP-1 patients are more likely to start PAP — but that is company-sourced and early. This is the thesis behind the August 2023 crash and a large part of the 2026 drawdown.
02
Quality is now a two-sided story. ResMed's franchise was built partly on a rival's recall. The FDA Class I recall of the Astral 100/150 ventilators in July 2026 — a component that may leak and damage circuitry — puts the same scrutiny on ResMed. The ventilator line is small, but a second quality event in the sleep line would hit the moat itself.
03
Growth is slowing into fiscal 2027. FY2027 revenue guidance of $5.75–$5.85B compared with an LSEG consensus of $5.92B, per Medical Device Network. The MatrixCare sale and the Astral suspension explain much of the gap, but the headline growth rate a screen sees will be low single digits.
04
Philips' eventual return. The consent decree restricts Philips until requirements are met; it is not a permanent exit. When the rival is back at full strength in the US, some of the share ResMed gained by default is contestable. Timing was not sourced this session.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $263.55 to the September 18, 2026 settled close of $224.99, RMD lost 14.6% of its price. Adding back the dividends declared per share over fiscal 2022–2026 ($9.88 in total) brings the total return to roughly −11% (estimate). That start date matters: September 2021 was the height of the Philips-recall windfall, when the market priced in a rival's absence at full value. The five years since have been a range with two trap doors — the August 2023 GLP-1 scare that took the stock to a $141.22 October 2023 low, and the 2026 slide from $274.51 (August 2025) to $190.57 (May 2026) — each followed by a recovery that did not quite reach the prior high.

The 6-period monthly RSI bottomed at 22.2 in May 2026, close to the 21.9 of October 2023 and above the 19.9 of April 2022, and has since recovered to 49.0 — dead neutral. The 10-month moving average sits at about $225.53, almost exactly on the current price: the trend is neither up nor down. The stock is waiting for a reason.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 21.6x trailing and 18.6x forward earnings for the category leader in sleep-apnea therapy: 62.4% non-GAAP gross margin, 26% ROIC, net cash, $1.65B of free cash flow, a 10% dividend raise and more than $1.85B of planned capital returns in fiscal 2027. Earnings roughly doubled over fiscal 2022–2026 while the stock fell. The company's own data says GLP-1 patients are more likely to start PAP, not less; the Astral recall is a known, sized ~$75M headwind in a small ventilator line; and the fiscal 2027 revenue "miss" is largely a portfolio choice (the MatrixCare sale). If the installed base keeps buying masks at double-digit growth, the 9.19% short interest is fuel.

Bear Case

The bear case does not dispute this year's numbers; it disputes the size of the patient pool in five years. If GLP-1 drugs reduce sleep-apnea severity at scale, fewer people get diagnosed, fewer devices get placed, and the mask annuity slowly runs off. The stock's best years were priced on a rival's recall that will not last forever, and ResMed now has a Class I recall of its own. FY2027 revenue guidance came in below consensus, Residential Care Software grew only 2% cc in Q4, and 11 of 19 analysts rate the stock Hold. A near-market multiple is not cheap for a business whose long-run demand curve is genuinely in question.

Top 3 Risks

01
GLP-1 demand erosion. The thesis behind both big drawdowns in the window (August 2023, March–May 2026). The evidence so far is company-sourced and runs the other way, but it is early, and the market will not give credit until diagnosis and new-patient device growth prove it over several years.
02
Quality and regulatory exposure. The July 2026 Astral Class I recall has a $41.9M provision and a ~$75M revenue impact expected in fiscal 2027. A quality problem spreading to the core sleep devices or masks would damage the franchise that benefited from Philips' recall.
03
Competitive normalisation. Philips operates under a consent decree that restricts, rather than ends, its US sleep business. As it re-qualifies, the share ResMed gained by default becomes contestable on price and features.
Industry Commentary
GLP-1 drugs vs. the sleep-apnea funnel
CRITICAL DEBATE

Weight-loss drugs that treat obesity and type 2 diabetes have been the overhang on this stock since 2023, because some researchers suggest they may reduce the severity of sleep apnea over time. The market acted on that twice: a 28.2% one-month fall in August 2023 and a 30.6% slide from August 2025 to May 2026. ResMed's counter is data from its own ecosystem — OSA-diagnosed patients prescribed a GLP-1 were 1,070 bps more likely to start PAP therapy — which reads as GLP-1s pulling people into doctors' offices where sleep apnea gets diagnosed. Both can be true at once: more diagnoses in the near term, less severe disease in the long term. The quarterly tell is Americas device growth: +6% to $459M in Q4 FY2026, per the release.

Recalls cut both ways
ONGOING

The Philips Respironics recall of June 2021 — driven by degrading sound-abatement foam — and the April 9, 2024 consent decree restricting its new CPAP and BiPAP sales in the US reshaped this industry in ResMed's favour. The July 2026 FDA Class I recall of ResMed's own Astral 100 and 150 ventilators is far smaller in scale — a single ventilator line, five serious injuries and no deaths reported as of June 23, 2026 — but it reminds investors that the same regulatory machinery applies to the leader. The provision was taken in Q4 FY2026; the revenue cost lands in fiscal 2027.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

TIKR notes that ResMed traded at about 17x forward earnings in May 2026 against a five-year average of 29.4x; at $224.99 it is 18.6x. The bull framing: this is a category leader with 60%-plus gross margins, a recurring consumables stream and net cash, priced at roughly the multiple of an ordinary industrial because of a theme rather than a result. The bear framing: the old 29x multiple was the anomaly — a premium paid for a rival's absence and an uncontested patient pool, neither of which is guaranteed. The way to decide is not the P/E; it is several consecutive quarters of Americas device growth, which is the closest public read on new-patient starts.
