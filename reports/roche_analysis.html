01 Company Overview

Roche is a Basel drug and diagnostics company, founded in 1896, and Switzerland's most valuable listed company. It runs two divisions: Pharmaceuticals (prescription medicines, including the US arm Genentech and a majority stake in Japan's Chugai) and Diagnostics (the lab machines and tests hospitals run every day). FY2025 sales were CHF 61.52B, up 7% at constant exchange rates (CER) and 2% in francs. IFRS diluted EPS was CHF 16.04; Roche's preferred core EPS was CHF 19.46.

The share price has made a full round trip. It closed December 2021 at CHF 379.10, the high of this five-year window, then fell 41.8% to CHF 220.60 at the end of April 2024, a stretch in which COVID-test sales collapsed and a flagship Alzheimer's drug failed. Then two late-stage trial wins in November 2025, in multiple sclerosis and early breast cancer, gave the stock its best month of the window (+18.7%). It closed October 2, 2026 at CHF 345.70. The question now is whether the new pipeline can outgrow three drags: older drugs losing patent protection, a strong Swiss franc that shrinks every dollar of sales, and US drug-pricing policy, which matters because the United States is 51.8% of pharma sales.

One quirk to know first. Roche has two listed securities. The bearer shares (RO) carry the votes, and a founding-family pool holds 64.97% of them. The participation certificates (ROP) carry no vote but the same dividend and the same claim on profits. They replaced the old non-voting Genussscheine (ticker ROG) one-for-one on March 17, 2026. There are 702.6 million certificates against 106.7 million bearer shares, so ROP is by far the larger line and the one this report tracks.

The latest print: first half 2026, reported July 23. Group sales of CHF 30.36B were in line with the roughly CHF 30.31B analysts expected, while core operating profit of CHF 11.86B (+10% at CER) beat expectations, Reuters reported; the eye drug Vabysmo was the soft spot, missing forecasts on weaker US demand and competition. On the call CFO Alan Hippe put the currency effect at minus 8 percentage points, the gap between +6% at constant rates and −2% in francs. CEO Thomas Schinecker pointed to the pipeline: "Overall, the phase III success rate in this year is 80%, and with that significantly above the average of the industry." Roche confirmed its 2026 outlook: mid-single-digit sales growth and high-single-digit core EPS growth, both at CER, and a further dividend increase in francs. The stock closed 5.1% higher that day (CHF 332.40 → 349.50).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Diagnostics installed base Drug + test under one roof Biologics manufacturing scale Three research engines (Roche, Genentech, Chugai) Family control, long horizon 39th straight dividend increase

Roche's edge is that it sells both the medicine and the test that decides who gets it. In the first half of 2026 the FDA approved its Ventana PTEN assay, the first companion diagnostic to assess a key protein in prostate cancer, used to pick patients for combination treatment with Truqap. Its Elecsys pTau217 blood test for Alzheimer's sits next to its own experimental Alzheimer's drug, trontinemab, now headed into the Phase III PrevenTRON study. The family pool's 64.97% of the vote means no activist can force a break-up or a quick sale, which buys patience for long trials. The flip side is that certificate holders have no say at all.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The franc eats the growth. H1 2026 sales grew 6% at constant rates but fell 2% in francs; core EPS was +9% at CER and −2% in CHF. Roche earns mostly in dollars and euros and reports, pays dividends and is priced in francs. A strong franc does not hurt the business, but it does hurt the numbers shareholders are paid in.
02
The United States is half the pharma business. US sales were CHF 12,229M of CHF 23,629M in H1 2026. US pricing policy therefore moves the stock: on October 1, 2025, after Pfizer agreed a deal with the US government that swissinfo said gave it three years' protection from tariffs, Roche closed 8.6% higher (CHF 259.90 → 282.30). Genentech signed its own agreement with the US government on December 19, 2025, trading Medicaid prices in line with other wealthy countries for a three-year tariff exemption.
03
Patent cliffs keep coming. Patent-expired products fell 8% at CER to CHF 2.6B in H1 2026, and Perjeta (−7%) is shrinking as patients move to Phesgo. The breast-cancer franchise is next: at Roche's Pharma Day on September 28, 2026, Pharma CEO Teresa Graham said Roche expects "the HER2 franchise to peak at CHF 9 billion this year." Today's biggest earners, such as Ocrevus and Hemlibra, will face the same cycle in time. Growth depends on the pipeline arriving on schedule.
04
Big trials can fail. On November 14, 2022 Roche said its Phase III GRADUATE studies of gantenerumab in early Alzheimer's "did not meet their primary endpoints of slowing clinical decline"; the stock fell 7.4% that month. Write-downs of acquired drugs come with the territory: H1 2026 included CHF 1.0B of impairments on stopped compounds from the Inflazome, Carmot and Good Therapeutics deals.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between IFRS and core earnings is mostly write-downs and amortisation of acquired drugs. In 2024 IFRS EPS fell to CHF 10.31 under heavy impairments, then rebounded 56% to CHF 16.04 in 2025, which the Finance Report attributes partly to "lower charges for the impairment of goodwill and intangible assets". That swing is why Roche, and most analysts, track core EPS. H1 2026 again included CHF 1.0B of impairments, after Roche stopped developing compounds acquired with Inflazome, Carmot and Good Therapeutics. Currency is the other distortion: H1 2026 IFRS diluted EPS fell 8% in francs (CHF 8.52 vs 9.23) while rising 5% at constant rates.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 18.0x trailing core earnings and 17.0x forward for a business growing sales 6% and core EPS 9% at constant rates, with a 39.0% core operating margin, a 4.5% free-cash-flow yield and a dividend raised 39 years in a row. The growth drivers (CHF 11.0B, +12%) are four times the size of the shrinking patent-expired group (CHF 2.6B, −8%). The pipeline has started to pay off: giredestrant's FDA decision is due November 30, fenebrutinib's US filing in both forms of MS was accepted under priority review, Gazyva won a new FDA approval on September 25 and three more FDA decisions are due by November, and Tam-Peli and the obesity assets add options. At its September 28 Pharma Day Roche set an aim of up to 20 new molecular entities on the market by 2030, and since the July print Bernstein, Barclays and Goldman Sachs have all raised their targets. If the franc stops rising, constant-rate growth starts to show up in francs.

Bear Case

Five years of flat price say the market has watched Roche promise pipeline renewal before. Gantenerumab failed, COVID sales vanished, and acquired drugs keep being written down. At 22.6x IFRS earnings the stock is no longer the bargain it was at CHF 220. Half the pharma business is in the US, where pricing policy is being rewritten, and the franc has turned 6% growth into a 2% decline in reported sales. Even giredestrant is not a sure thing beyond early breast cancer: in March 2026 its persevERA trial in advanced disease missed its primary endpoint. Jefferies, with a CHF 230 target, called the September Pharma Day free of surprises and sees little beyond giredestrant and fenebrutinib toward 2028. Roche's obesity data so far come from Phase II trials, and H1 2026 brought CHF 1.0B of write-downs on stopped programmes, including a GLP-1/GIP drug from the Carmot deal. Certificate holders also have no vote: the family pool's 64.97% decides capital allocation.

Top 3 Risks

01
US drug pricing and tariffs. The US is 51.8% of pharma sales. The 8.6% one-day jump on October 1, 2025, when a peer's deal with Washington reduced uncertainty, shows how much of the valuation rides on US policy.
02
A strong Swiss franc. H1 2026 sales were +6% at CER but −2% in CHF, and core EPS +9% vs −2%. Dividends and the share price are in francs, so a dollar weakening against the franc lowers both.
03
Pipeline execution. The bull case leans on giredestrant (decision due November 30, 2026), fenebrutinib, Gazyva's autoimmune expansion and the obesity combination. One high-profile failure, as with gantenerumab in November 2022, can take 7% off the stock in a month.

US drug pricing: the deal-by-deal era
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The US sets the profit pool for every large drugmaker, and in 2025 the rules started being written company by company. Swissinfo reported on October 1, 2025 that Pfizer had won three years' protection from tariff surcharges in exchange for a series of concessions, and that analysts welcomed the reduced uncertainty for the whole industry. Roche closed up 8.6% that day. Roche's own agreement followed on December 19, 2025: Genentech committed to Medicaid prices comparable to those in other wealthy countries and to selling its flu medicines through TrumpRx.gov, and received a three-year exemption from tariffs. For Roche, with US sales of CHF 12,229M in H1 2026 (51.8% of pharma), any further US price concession applies to half of the drug business.

The Swiss franc as a permanent headwind
ONGOING HEADWIND

Roche's H1 2026 release put it in the headline: +6% sales growth at constant exchange rates, "-2% in CHF due to the significant appreciation of the Swiss franc". By the ECB's reference rates, one franc bought USD 1.2097 on October 2, 2026, up from USD 1.1980 two days earlier and from USD 1.0692 five years before, though below the USD 1.2571 of a year earlier. Every Swiss multinational that reports in francs faces the same translation effect.

Diagnostics and drugs converging
EMERGING TAILWIND

Precision medicine needs a test to pick each patient, and Roche runs a large drug business and a diagnostics division under one roof. In H1 2026 alone it won FDA approval for the first companion diagnostic for PTEN protein in prostate cancer and a CE mark for the pTau217 Alzheimer's blood test, launched the Axelios 1 sequencer, and agreed to buy PathAI for AI-based pathology. Pathology Lab, the unit closest to drug selection, grew 11% at CER, the fastest in the division.
