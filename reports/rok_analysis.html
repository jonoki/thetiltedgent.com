01 Company Overview

Rockwell Automation calls itself a global leader in industrial automation and digital transformation. It owns the Allen-Bradley controller franchise and the FactoryTalk software stack, and works through a global network of systems integrators and service engineers. It sells the programmable logic controllers, drives, motor-control centers, safety devices, industrial software and lifecycle services that run discrete, hybrid and process manufacturing plants: automotive, food and beverage, life sciences, semiconductors, warehouse automation and energy. Fiscal 2026 (ended September 30, 2026) revenue was $8,989 million, up 8% on both a reported and an organic basis (organic strips out currency moves and deals), after a flat fiscal 2025 of $8,342 million at the bottom of a destocking cycle, when distributors and machine builders ran down their inventories instead of ordering. Even so, fiscal 2026 revenue is still 0.8% below the $9,058 million of fiscal 2023.

The central debate is simple to state and hard to settle: is Rockwell a cyclical machinery company enjoying a rebound, or a better business that deserves a permanently higher price for its earnings? The November 5 results gave each side something. Adjusted EPS of $3.88 for the September quarter beat the $3.72 Nasdaq consensus, fiscal 2026 adjusted EPS of $13.42 came in above the $13.00–$13.30 guidance, and Software & Control grew 18.3% in the year at a 33.8% margin. Fiscal 2027 guidance is for 6–9% organic sales growth and adjusted EPS of $14.50–$15.50. Against that, quarterly sales of $2,332 million fell short of the roughly $2.41 billion analysts expected, after Rockwell booked an $85 million refund to customers as a reduction of sales. The tariff items also added $0.11 to the adjusted EPS figure, annual recurring revenue grew 5%, which CEO Blake Moret said "did not meet our expectations", and management guided the December quarter to adjusted EPS of around $3. The shares fell 14.15% on the day and another 1.36% on November 6, to $375.86, which is 24.1% below the June 2026 monthly closing high of $495.08. The stock now trades at 30.0× trailing P/E against 23.9× for the S&P 500, with a beta of 1.52.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Allen-Bradley installed base Distributor + integrator network Engineer retraining cost FactoryTalk software add-ons Plant-floor safety certification North American share concentration Long equipment life

Rockwell's moat is switching cost expressed in human capital. A controller architecture chosen for a plant tends to stay in service for many years, and controls engineers trained on Allen-Bradley ladder logic and Studio 5000 are costly to retrain on another maker's system. Replacing the platform means requalifying safety systems, retraining maintenance staff, and rewriting validated code — for a regulated pharmaceutical or food plant, a change-control exercise nobody undertakes casually. That is why a segment as commoditised-looking as motor control can sustain an 18–20% operating margin, and why Software & Control earned a 33.8% margin on top of it in fiscal 2026: the software is sold into an installed base that has already paid the switching cost. The financial evidence is in the returns — ROE of 38.73% and ROIC of 21.04% on a business whose fiscal 2026 revenue was still just below its fiscal 2023 level.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The cycle, not the company, sets the top line. FY2024 revenue fell 8.77% to $8,264M and FY2025 grew about 1%, two years in which a very good business produced no growth because distributors and machine builders were working off inventory. FY2026 revenue of $8,989M is still 0.8% below FY2023's $9,058M, so the +8% is measured off a depressed base. Whatever the software story, the reported revenue line still follows a machinery cycle.
02
Scale disadvantage against Siemens, ABB and Schneider. Rockwell is a roughly $9 billion revenue company competing against automation divisions embedded inside far larger European electrical groups that can cross-subsidise, bundle electrification with automation, and fund software R&D from a wider base. Rockwell's answer has been partnerships and bolt-on acquisitions rather than scale, which leaves it dependent on defending North America rather than winning globally.
03
Lifecycle Services is the weak segment. Sales fell 6.0% in FY2026 (organic −2%) and it returned to organic growth only in the September quarter, at +2%. Services are meant to be the steady, repeat business that smooths out the hardware cycle. Moret said delays in projects are still holding back recurring revenue here, and with services only 22.8% of segment sales, the case for the stock rests on the other two segments.
04
Balance sheet leaves little cushion. Debt-to-equity of 1.00 with $3.55B of total debt against $433M of cash, and a current ratio of 1.08 at June 30, 2026, is a thin liquidity position for a company whose sales fell 8.77% in a single year as recently as fiscal 2024. Buybacks of $1,256M and dividends of $618M in fiscal 2026 took more than the year's $1,701M of free cash flow. The high ROE is partly a product of that debt, not purely of the operations.

Notable One-Off Items. Three items distort the comparisons above. First, tariff refunds: in Q4 Rockwell received $103M of refunds on IEEPA tariffs (US tariffs imposed under emergency powers) plus $5M of interest, and accrued $85M to refund customers, booked as a reduction of sales and kept outside the segments. The net effect added about $0.11 to both Q4 and full-year EPS, GAAP and adjusted. CFO Christian Rothe said net tariff refund items will be an 11-cent headwind in fiscal 2027, even as sales growth gets about a 1-point benefit from lapping the refund. Second, the dissolution of the Sensia joint venture, completed in fiscal 2026, added about half a point to Q4 margin and, per Rothe, takes $100 million off fiscal 2027 revenue, split evenly between Q1 and Q2, about 1.5 points of reported growth. Third, the fiscal 2025 base is unusually weak for GAAP: net income attributable to Rockwell was $138M in Q4 FY2025 and $869M for the year, depressed by a non-cash impairment charge on Sensia and an accounting change and accrual increase for legacy asbestos liabilities (Q4 FY2025 release, November 6, 2025), which is why GAAP EPS rose 63% in fiscal 2026 while adjusted EPS rose 27%.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Rockwell has done something hard: the three segments' combined operating margin rose from 20.4% to 23.2% in fiscal 2026, adjusted EPS rose 27% and free cash flow reached $1,701M. Software & Control, 31.1% of segment sales with a 33.8% margin and +18.3% growth, is turning an installed base into something closer to software economics. Management guides to 6–9% organic growth and $14.50–$15.50 of adjusted EPS in fiscal 2027, and Moret says that forecast does not rely on a broad recovery in customer capital spending; semiconductor and data-center sales are expected to grow in the mid-teens. After a 24.1% fall from the June month-end high, the stock trades at 25.1× estimated fiscal 2027 adjusted earnings, with a 21% return on invested capital and 16 years of dividend increases on stockanalysis.com's count.

Bear Case

Strip out the story and this is a $9 billion machinery company whose fiscal 2026 revenue is still 0.8% below fiscal 2023, trading at 30.0× trailing earnings versus 23.9× for the index, with a 2.56 PEG, a 1.52 beta and debt equal to equity. The September-quarter beat included about $0.11 of tariff items: without them adjusted EPS would have been $3.77 against the $3.72 Nasdaq consensus. Sales missed, annual recurring revenue growth of 5% fell short of management's own expectations, and fiscal 2027 needs recurring revenue to speed up to 7% while memory-chip costs rise. Lifecycle Services fell 6.0% for the year. The December quarter is guided to about $3 of adjusted EPS, and 15 of 28 analysts rate the stock Hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Earnings and the price paid for them can fall together. At 30.0× trailing and 25.1× estimated fiscal 2027 adjusted EPS, ROK is still priced above the market. If organic growth comes in near the low end of 6–9%, a range that already includes about a point of help from lapping the tariff refund, both the earnings number and what investors pay for it could fall. The 14% drop on November 5 shows how quickly that can happen.
02
Capex deferral by manufacturing customers. Automation is discretionary capital spending that plant managers can postpone for four quarters without consequence. Moret says the fiscal 2027 forecast does not need a broad capital-spending recovery, but tariff uncertainty or a pause in new plant commitments would show up first in Intelligent Devices orders and then in Lifecycle Services projects, where he already cites delays.
03
Competitive share loss to larger integrated rivals. Siemens, ABB and Schneider can bundle electrification, automation and software from a far larger revenue base and fund software R&D that Rockwell must part-fund through acquisitions. Rockwell's advantage is strongest in existing North American plants; new plants built anywhere else are a fair fight it does not automatically win.

The destocking hangover is over, but sales are not yet back to FY2023
ONGOING HEADWIND
Rockwell's FY2024 revenue fell 8.77% to $8,264M and FY2025 grew about 1% to $8,342M as distributors and machine builders worked off inventory built during the 2021–22 shortage panic. Fiscal 2026's +8% is measured off that low base, and at $8,989M revenue is still 0.8% below the $9,058M of FY2023. Fiscal 2027 guidance of 6–9% organic growth would take sales above the fiscal 2023 level, at about $9.4–$9.7 billion on the reported range. The fall in the stock after the November results shows investors are not yet paying for that outcome.
Software mix is doing the margin work
EMERGING TAILWIND
The story is visible in the segment table more than the headline. Software & Control generated $256M of operating earnings on $754M of Q4 sales, a 34.0% margin, versus 21.6% for Intelligent Devices and 16.4% for Lifecycle Services. For the year it was 45% of segment profit on 31% of segment sales, and it made more operating profit than the hardware segment despite being 33% smaller. It supplied $244M of the $400M rise in segment operating earnings in FY2026, as the combined segment margin moved from 20.4% to 23.2%. The caveat: annual recurring revenue grew 5%, below what management wanted, so much of the segment's growth is controllers rather than software subscriptions.
Bull versus bear: a valuation standoff, not a fundamental one
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
What is striking about the analyst distribution, 12 Buy, 15 Hold and 1 Sell with an average target of $448.76 and a median of $452, is that almost nobody disputes the business quality. All seven November 6 target changes we list were cuts. Bulls and Holds agree on a 21% return on invested capital and on the software mix; they disagree on whether 30.0× trailing earnings and a 2.56 PEG already pay for it. For the bull, the fall from $495.08 in June to $375.86 has taken out the excess and left a business guiding to about 12% EPS growth at the midpoint. For the bear, a sales miss and a recurring-revenue shortfall at a stock this expensive explain why it fell, and a lower price does not change the cycle. Both cases rest on the same facts; only the entry price separates them.
