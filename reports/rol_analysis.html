01 Company Overview

Rollins is the parent of Orkin and a family of pest-control brands — HomeTeam Pest Defense, Western Pest Services, Clark, Critter Control, Fox, Northwest Exterminating, Waltham, Orkin Canada, Orkin UK, Orkin Australia and more — serving more than 2.8 million customers from over 850 locations with roughly 22,000 employees. The business is a route: a technician drives a van to the same house or restaurant every month or quarter, and the customer pays for the absence of a problem. FY2025 revenue was $3.76B, up 10.99%, and the Q3 2026 release counts 100 consecutive quarters of revenue growth. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $3.99B, net income $526.1M and GAAP EPS $1.09 (StockAnalysis), a cent lower than at the Q2 print because Q3 EPS fell 2.9% year on year.

So why has the stock halved? Because for years the market paid Rollins the price of a sure thing. In January 2026 the stock traded at about 45 times free cash flow (Motley Fool); it closed that month at $63.34. Then the certainty wobbled. Q1 2026 grew organically 6.6% against a 7–8% full-year plan; margins slipped; the CFO resigned in May; on July 22 Q2 missed and organic growth guidance came down to at least 6%; and on October 21, 2026, after the close, Q3 missed again. Revenue grew 6.2% to $1.09B, only 4.4% of it organic (growth from existing businesses, not acquisitions); residential organic growth slowed to 2.3%; adjusted EPS of $0.34 fell 2.9% and came in a cent under the $0.35 Nasdaq consensus; and the full-year organic outlook was cut a second time, to 4% to 6%. Management's explanation is new: people now ask AI chatbots for a pest-control company, and the answers point them to a wider set of local providers than a traditional search engine did. The stock had already fallen to an intraday low of $29.29 on October 5; it rose 5.6% the day after the print and closed Friday, October 23 at $34.82, down 38.7% in a year while the S&P 500 (SPY) rose 13.1% (Yahoo closes, price only). Nothing here is broken in the way a bankruptcy is broken. What broke was the premium. At the poker table, the player who pays top dollar for a "lock" loses the most when it turns out to be merely a strong hand — and that is the trade this report examines.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Route density Recurring service contracts Orkin brand Multi-brand, multi-channel Serial acquirer of local operators Termite warranty renewals Family control, long horizon

The real moat is density. A technician who can service eight homes on one street earns more per hour than a competitor driving across town for one, so the biggest operator in a neighbourhood can price lower and still make more. Rollins compounds that by buying local operators and folding their customers into existing routes — $163M of acquisitions in the first nine months of 2026. The balance sheet funds it: ROIC (return on the capital invested in the business) is 23.1% and debt is 1.08x EBITDA. The vulnerability is that density protects the cost of serving a customer, not the cost of finding one. When new customers come through a search ad, the price of that ad is set by everyone bidding for the same click, and that is the channel that broke in Q2 and weakened further in Q3. Now the search itself is changing: an AI chatbot asked for a pest-control company names a wider and shifting set of local firms, so a national brand's search ranking counts for less.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Digital lead generation is a rented channel, and it is changing. The Q2 shortfall came from brands "more reliant on consumer-initiated demand through search, digital media and inbound calls"; in Q3 the CEO said AI-driven search recommendations "vary significantly across platforms, markets and can even yield different results for the same search run on different days." Rollins does not own those channels; it bids for them, and it has begun buying ads on ChatGPT and Google Gemini. Selling and marketing ran at 14.1% of Q2 revenue — the cost of buying growth is already large.
02
Costs were built for a bigger season. The CFO said in July the cost structure "remained positioned for a stronger growth environment entering peak season." Technicians are hired ahead of summer; if the demand does not arrive, they are paid anyway. Operating margin fell 120 bps in Q1, 110 bps in Q2 and 140 bps in Q3, when staffing cuts helped but insurance claims costs rose.
03
Weather is a revenue line. Q1 2026 growth was held back by unfavourable January weather (Q1 earnings-call coverage); Q2 one-time services were hit by lower pest pressure (StockAnalysis transcript summary). Bugs follow heat and rain, and no management team controls either.
04
Management turnover mid-stumble. The CFO resigned in May, a new CFO took over in June, and the miss followed in July. Continuity was not the issue in the numbers, but it adds noise to the guidance the market now distrusts.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP and adjusted figures sit close together: Q3 2026 GAAP EPS $0.33 vs adjusted $0.34, operating margin 20.5% vs 21.2%. The gap is about $7.3M of pre-tax acquisition-related and other costs (CFO, October 22 call). Q3 cash flow was lifted by a $63M tax refund tied to the company's tax-credit planning strategy; without it, free cash flow would have been 109% of net income instead of 149%. That partly reverses the first half, when cash flow was depressed by $40M of tax-payment timing and $9M from moving the 2035 senior notes to semi-annual interest (Q1 2026 release). Q3 margins also carried higher insurance and claims costs against a favourable Q3 2025; the CFO said that, normalised for both, the quarter's incremental EBITDA margin would have exceeded 25% instead of the reported −2.8%.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $35.23 to the October 23, 2026 settled close of $34.82, ROL lost 1.2% of its price; over the same dates SPY rose 65.5% and QQQ 82.4% (Yahoo closes, price only). Adding back the $2.99 per share of dividends with ex-dates in that window (20 payments in Yahoo's dividend history, not reinvested) turns it into a gain of about 7.3%. The endpoints hide the shape: a four-year climb from $30.85 (January 2022) to $63.34 (January 2026) — a doubling — then a fall to $30.12 in September 2026, the lowest month-end close in the window, that handed back the entire gain.

The 6-period monthly RSI fell to 12.9 at the September month-end — the most oversold reading in the five-year series — and stands at 29.2 at the October 23 close, its sixth straight reading below 30 (May 28.8, June 22.0, July 18.7, August 17.1, September 12.9). It peaked at 76.9 in November 2025, two months before the top. The 10-month moving average sits near $46.17, 32.6% above the price; the 3-month average is $33.67, just under it. On daily closes the picture is similar: $34.82 is 3.3% above the 50-day average ($33.72) and 26.8% below the 200-day ($47.58). October's bounce, +15.6% from the September close so far, would be the largest monthly gain since October 2022 if it held to month-end; it describes the speed of the rebound, not where the bottom is.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business did not break; the price of certainty did. Revenue still grew 6.2% in a quarter management called disappointing, ROIC is 23.1%, trailing free cash flow is $672M on $26M of capex, the existing residential customer count is still growing, and the board has just raised the dividend about 10%. Commercial (+6.6% organic) is fine; the problem is how new homeowners find a pest-control company, and management is already moving money to the new search channels — website traffic was down only 4% year on year in September against 13% in August. Staffing is already being aligned with lower demand, and management tied $120M of Q3 buybacks to its "conviction in the intrinsic value of the business." At about 25x free cash flow — down from 45x — a family-controlled route business that has grown revenue for 100 straight quarters is priced as a normal company for the first time in years, and a return toward the 7–8% organic target would make that look cheap.

Bear Case

Thirty-two times trailing earnings is still a premium, and the premium was built on a growth rate the company has now cut twice in three months. Organic guidance fell from 7–8% to at least 6% to 4–6%; operating margins fell 120, 110 and 140 bps in the first three quarters of 2026; EPS has missed the Nasdaq consensus in three of the last four quarters. Management itself says "the customer discovery landscape may be changing structurally, and we are not assuming a return to historical patterns." The analyst panel went from 12 Buys and no Sells in June to 7 and 2. Short interest rose by half in the second half of September. If 4–6% organic is the new normal, a PEG of 4.15 has further to compress.

Top 3 Risks

01
Residential demand stays soft. Residential is 45% of revenue and grew 2.3% organically in Q3, down from 3.6% in Q2. The low end of the new 4–6% range assumes leads worsen from Q3 levels; management will not discuss 2027 until the Q4 report, so the market has no outlook for next year to lean on.
02
Continued margin compression. Operating margin fell 120 bps in Q1, 110 bps in Q2 and 140 bps in Q3 2026, and the new guide allows only "slightly positive to 10%" incremental margins for 2026 (the share of each extra dollar of revenue kept as EBITDA), against a long-run target of 30–35%. Labour is the largest cost (employee expenses were 30.5% of Q2 revenue in cost of service alone); a service business cannot cut technicians as fast as demand falls without hurting retention and service quality, and in Q3 termite growth was already held back by staffing constraints.
03
The valuation could fall further. The P/E is 31.9 and the PEG 4.15 even after a 45% fall from the January 2026 month-end. The stock was never priced on earnings alone; it was priced on reliability. Another miss would test how far a "quality" multiple can fall once the market stops paying for the streak.
Industry Commentary
Paying for customers you used to earn
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Rollins's own words split its growth engine in two: brands fed by "consumer-initiated demand through search, digital media and inbound calls" slowed, while "relationship-based channels, such as home builders and door-to-door sales, delivered solid organic growth" (Q2 release). The difference is who owns the customer relationship at the start. A builder partnership or a door-knock produces a customer Rollins found; a search lead is a customer Rollins bought, at an auction price it does not control. In Q3 the auction itself changed: when a homeowner asks an AI chatbot rather than a search engine, the answer names a wider, shifting set of local firms, and a national brand's ad budget and search ranking count for less. Piper Sandler's downgrade, reported September 24, cited the growing impact of agentic artificial intelligence and private-equity investment on the pest-control industry (as reported by AskTraders). Selling and marketing took 14.1% of Q2 revenue. The long-run question for residential is whether that mix can shift toward owned channels fast enough — HomeTeam's 2 million built-in Taexx installations are one example of an owned channel.

A fragmented market still being consolidated
ONGOING DYNAMIC

The largest consolidators named in a June 2026 industry tracker are Rollins, Rentokil Initial (which bought Terminix in a roughly $6.7B deal in December 2022, at about 19.8x adjusted EBITDA per the tracker), EQT-backed Anticimex, Goldman Sachs–owned Aptive Environmental, and family-owned Arrow Exterminators and Cook's Pest Control. Private-equity-backed platforms compete with Rollins for the same local businesses, which pushes up acquisition prices. Rollins's advantage is patient, family-controlled capital and a balance sheet at about 1x debt/EBITDA; its constraint is that bought growth (2–3 points a year, per guidance) does nothing for organic growth, which is the number the market now trades on.

The re-rating — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

Price-to-free-cash-flow has moved from about 45x in January 2026 to 24.8x today while free cash flow rose ($650M FY2025, $672M TTM, the latter helped by a $63M tax refund). The bull framing: this is a sentiment reset on a business whose cash generation is intact; a recurring-revenue compounder with 23.1% ROIC rarely trades at a 4% FCF yield for long. The bear framing: the old multiple was the anomaly, not the new one, and if AI search permanently raises the cost of finding customers, the market is right to stop paying for a streak. The way to decide is not the multiple but two reported numbers — residential organic growth and operating margin. Q3 moved both the wrong way; September's web traffic is the only early sign the other way.
