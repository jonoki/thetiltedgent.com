01 Company Overview

Rollins is the parent of Orkin and a family of pest-control brands — HomeTeam Pest Defense, Western Pest Services, Clark, Critter Control, Fox, Northwest Exterminating, Waltham, Orkin Canada, Orkin UK, Orkin Australia and more — serving more than 2.8 million customers from over 850 locations with roughly 22,000 employees. The business is a route: a technician drives a van to the same house or restaurant every month or quarter, and the customer pays for the absence of a problem. FY2025 revenue was $3.76B, up 10.99%, and the Q2 2026 release carried the headline "99th Consecutive Quarter of Revenue Growth." Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $3.92B (+9.9%), net income $531.7M and GAAP EPS $1.10 (+8.9%).

So why has the stock halved? Because for years the market paid Rollins the price of a sure thing. In January 2026 the stock traded at about 45 times free cash flow (Motley Fool); it closed that month at $63.34. Then the certainty wobbled. Q1 2026 grew organically 6.6% against a 7–8% full-year plan; margins slipped; the CFO resigned in May; and on July 22, 2026 Q2 missed — adjusted EPS $0.32 against a $0.34 consensus, residential organic growth 3.6% against the 5.4% the Street expected — and organic growth guidance came down to at least 6%. The stock fell 9.3% the next day and closed Monday, September 21 at $32.35, down 41.7% in 52 weeks while earnings per share are still growing. Nothing here is broken in the way a bankruptcy is broken. What broke was the premium. At the poker table, the player who pays top dollar for a "lock" loses the most when it turns out to be merely a strong hand — and that is the trade this report examines.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Route density Recurring service contracts Orkin brand Multi-brand, multi-channel Serial acquirer of local operators Termite warranty renewals Family control, long horizon

The real moat is density. A technician who can service eight homes on one street earns more per hour than a competitor driving across town for one, so the biggest operator in a neighbourhood can price lower and still make more. Rollins compounds that by buying local operators and folding their customers into existing routes — $117M of acquisitions in Q2 2026 alone. The balance sheet funds it: ROIC is 23.5% and debt is 1.07x EBITDA. The vulnerability is that density protects the cost of serving a customer, not the cost of acquiring one. When new customers come through a search ad, the price of that ad is set by everyone bidding for the same click, and that is exactly the channel that broke in Q2.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Digital lead generation is a rented channel. The Q2 shortfall came from brands "more reliant on consumer-initiated demand through search, digital media and inbound calls." Rollins does not own those channels; it bids for them. Selling and marketing ran at 14.1% of Q2 revenue — the cost of buying growth is already large.
02
Costs were built for a bigger season. The CFO said the cost structure "remained positioned for a stronger growth environment entering peak season." Technicians are hired ahead of summer; if the demand does not arrive, they are paid anyway. Operating margin fell 110 bps in Q2 and 120 bps in Q1.
03
Weather is a revenue line. Q1 2026 growth was held back by unfavourable January weather (Q1 earnings-call coverage); Q2 one-time services were hit by lower pest pressure (StockAnalysis transcript summary). Bugs follow heat and rain, and no management team controls either.
04
Management turnover mid-stumble. The CFO resigned in May, a new CFO took over in June, and the miss followed in July. Continuity was not the issue in the numbers, but it adds noise to the guidance the market now distrusts.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $35.33 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $32.35, ROL lost 8.4% of its price over five years. Adding back roughly $2.9 per share of dividends (our estimate, summed from StockAnalysis's annual dividend-per-share figures) brings the total return to roughly flat. The endpoints hide the shape: a four-year climb from $30.85 (January 2022) to $63.34 (January 2026) — a doubling — then a round trip in eight months that handed back the entire gain.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 14.2 at the September 21 close — the most oversold reading anywhere in the five-year series, after four straight month-end readings below 30 (May 28.8, June 22.0, July 18.7, August 17.1). It peaked at 76.9 in November 2025, two months before the top. The 10-month moving average sits near $48.91, more than 50% above the price, and the 3-month average ($35.46) is still falling. Momentum is as stretched to the downside as it has been in five years; that describes the speed of the fall, not where it ends.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business did not break; the price of certainty did. Revenue grew 7.9% in the "bad" quarter, EPS is up 8.9% on a trailing basis, ROIC is 23.5%, free cash flow is $619M on $28M of capex, and the dividend has grown 23 years running. Commercial (+7.2% organic) and termite (+8.9%) are fine; the problem is one channel inside one line, and management says lead volume recovered from late June. Costs that were set for a bigger season can be reset within a season. At 25x free cash flow — down from 45x — a family-controlled route business that has grown revenue for 99 straight quarters is priced as a normal company for the first time in years, and a return to 7%+ organic growth would make that look cheap.

Bear Case

Twenty-nine times trailing earnings is still a premium, and the premium was built on a growth rate the company has now missed twice in a row. Organic guidance fell from 7–8% to at least 6%, margins fell 110–120 bps in each quarter, and the channel that failed — digital, search-driven customer acquisition — is one Rollins rents rather than owns. The analyst panel went from 12 Buys to 8 and from 1 Sell to 2, and the lowest target ($32) is already the price. RSI at 14 says the selling was fast, not that it is finished. If 6% organic is the new normal, a PEG of 3.45 has further to compress.

Top 3 Risks

01
Residential demand stays soft. Residential is 45% of revenue and grew 3.6% organically in Q2. If digital lead volume does not recover, the "at least 6%" organic guide is at risk, and the stock is priced on that guide.
02
Continued margin compression. Operating margin fell 120 bps in Q1 and 110 bps in Q2 2026. Labour is the largest cost (employee expenses were 30.5% of Q2 revenue in cost of service alone); a service business cannot cut technicians as fast as demand falls without hurting retention and service quality.
03
Multiple compression is not finished. The P/E is 29.4 and the PEG 3.45 even after a 42% fall. The stock was never priced on earnings alone; it was priced on reliability. Another miss would test how far a "quality" multiple can fall once the market stops paying for the streak.
Industry Commentary
Paying for customers you used to earn
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Rollins's own words split its growth engine in two: brands fed by "consumer-initiated demand through search, digital media and inbound calls" slowed, while "relationship-based channels, such as home builders and door-to-door sales, delivered solid organic growth." The difference is who owns the customer relationship at the start. A builder partnership or a door-knock produces a customer Rollins found; a search lead is a customer Rollins bought, at an auction price it does not control. Selling and marketing already consumes 14.1% of quarterly revenue. The long-run question for residential is whether that mix can shift toward owned channels fast enough — HomeTeam's 2 million built-in Taexx installations are one example of an owned channel.

A fragmented market still being consolidated
ONGOING DYNAMIC

The largest consolidators named in a June 2026 industry tracker are Rollins, Rentokil Initial (which bought Terminix in a roughly $6.7B deal in December 2022, at about 19.8x adjusted EBITDA per the tracker), EQT-backed Anticimex, Goldman Sachs–owned Aptive Environmental, and family-owned Arrow Exterminators and Cook's Pest Control. Private-equity-backed platforms compete with Rollins for the same local businesses, which pushes up acquisition prices. Rollins's advantage is patient, family-controlled capital and a balance sheet at about 1x debt/EBITDA; its constraint is that bought growth (2.2 points of Q2) does nothing for organic growth, which is the number the market now trades on.

The re-rating — bull and bear framing
OPEN QUESTION

Price-to-free-cash-flow has moved from about 45x in January 2026 to 25.1x today while free cash flow barely changed ($650M FY2025, $619M TTM). The bull framing: this is a sentiment reset on a business whose cash generation is intact; a recurring-revenue compounder with 23.5% ROIC rarely trades at a 4% FCF yield for long. The bear framing: the old multiple was the anomaly, not the new one, and the market has simply stopped paying for a streak. The way to decide is not the multiple but two reported numbers — residential organic growth and operating margin — over the next two quarters. Neither has yet given a clean signal.
