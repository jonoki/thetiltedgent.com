01 Company Overview

Roper Technologies buys and holds niche vertical-market software companies — legal practice management, government project accounting, insurance distribution, freight brokerage networks, utility billing, autism-therapy practice software — alongside a smaller portfolio of technology-enabled products such as water meters, ultrasound accessories and surgical navigation systems. FY2025 revenue was $7,903M, up 12.3% from $7,039M in FY2024; revenue for the twelve months to September 2026 was $8.43B, and the first nine months of 2026 grew 9.1% to $6,376.0M. At the October 23, 2026 close of $438.14 the market value is $43.38B (99.00M shares; our calculation). The stock is down 8.7% over the past year (from $479.96 on October 23, 2025), while SPY, the S&P 500 fund, rose 13.1% and QQQ, the Nasdaq-100 fund, rose 15.4% over the same dates.

The central debate is straightforward and it cuts both ways. Roper is a serial acquirer: it pays large multiples for small, sticky software businesses, records most of the price as goodwill and intangibles, and then amortises those intangibles through the income statement. That amortisation is a real accounting charge but not a recurring cash cost, so GAAP earnings genuinely understate the cash the portfolio throws off: FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $14.20 against adjusted diluted EPS of $20.00 (Q4 2025 release), a gap of $5.80 per share, or roughly $625M, that is dominated by amortisation of acquired intangibles. That is the bull's framing, and it is defensible. The bear's framing uses the same fact differently: organic revenue growth is guided at about 6% for 2026 (7% in Q3), so the difference between that and the 8%-plus headline comes from buying other people's revenue with borrowed money. Net debt was 2.9× EBITDA at the end of September (down from 3.4× in June, management said on the Q3 call), goodwill and intangibles are 90% of total assets, and ROIC is 6.90% — which is at or below most estimates of the company's cost of capital. Both statements are true simultaneously. Everything below is an attempt to weigh them.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SYSTEM-OF-RECORD LOCK-IN NICHE VERTICAL SCALE RECURRING REVENUE BASE NEGATIVE WORKING CAPITAL CAPEX-LIGHT (0.5% OF SALES) DECENTRALISED OPERATING MODEL $6B+ M&A CAPACITY

The real moat operates at the level of the individual subsidiary, not the parent. A law firm running Aderant, a defence contractor running Deltek, an ABA therapy practice running CentralReach — each of these is a system of record that touches billing, compliance and payroll. Ripping it out means re-implementing a business process, retraining staff and risking a revenue interruption, for a piece of software that costs a rounding error relative to the firm's payroll. That is why these businesses renew at very high rates and can push price above inflation for years. Roper's contribution on top is deliberately light: it is a capital allocator, not an operator, and it says so. The financial signature of that model is unusual and genuinely attractive — capital expenditure of about $45M against $8.43B of revenue over the last twelve months (stockanalysis), about 0.5% of sales (this excludes capitalised software, which Roper also deducts in its own free cash flow: $46.8M in the first nine months of 2026), a current ratio of 0.43 because customers pay for software annually in advance, and free cash flow of $2.58B, or 30.5% of revenue, equal to about 78% of EBITDA ($3.29B). Deferred revenue funds the working capital; there is essentially no plant to maintain. Cash conversion of that quality is the single strongest argument that GAAP earnings understate the business.

The honest counterweight: the moat protects the installed base, not the growth rate. A system of record that nobody leaves and nobody new adopts still produces 4–5% organic growth in perpetuity. Roper's compounding has never come from the software growing fast; it has come from buying more of it. That makes the moat a durability asset and the M&A machine a growth asset — and only one of those is under the company's control.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The model requires perpetual M&A, and M&A requires a price. Organic growth of about 6% against reported growth of 8%-plus (the 2026 guidance) means roughly a quarter of top-line growth is purchased. Management said on the Q3 2026 call that it has more than $6B of capacity to deploy over the next twelve months, and that the pipeline leans toward deals Roper approaches itself rather than large auctions. Under $50M went into acquisitions in the first half of 2026 and $436M into three bolt-ons in Q3 — against $3,280.4M in the first nine months of 2025 (cash paid for acquisitions, Q3 release). A capital allocator that cannot allocate capital is, temporarily, an ordinary mid-single-digit-growth software company with leverage; Q3 is the first sign the engine is turning over again.
▸
Balance-sheet composition. At September 30, 2026: goodwill of $21,555.0M and other intangibles of $9,455.7M against total assets of $34,338.3M — 90.3% of the balance sheet is purchase accounting. Shareholders' equity of $19,172.6M is therefore backed by negative tangible book value of roughly $11.8B (equity less goodwill and intangibles; our calculation). This is not inherently a scandal — it is what buying software companies looks like — but it means any impairment is an equity event, and it is why ROE of 13.23% sits on a base that is mostly other people's businesses at the price Roper paid.
▸
Leverage is down to 2.9× — with help. Net debt/EBITDA fell from 3.4× at June 30 to 2.9× at September 30, management said on the Q3 call, as $1.2B of Indicor proceeds arrived and buybacks paused, while $436M still went into acquisitions. Total debt is $9,719.7M ($1,415.1M due within a year, $8,304.6M long-term) against $311.0M of cash. The first-half buyback — $2,724.1M for 7.865M shares — is what had pushed leverage up. The same balance sheet cannot fund $6B of acquisitions, renewed buybacks and a lower leverage ratio all at once.
▸
ROIC below plausible cost of capital. Reported ROIC is 6.90%, against ROE of 13.23% and ROA of 4.29%. For a company whose entire identity is capital allocation, a mid-single-digit return on invested capital is the most uncomfortable number on the page. The defence — that ROIC is depressed by goodwill from deals still ramping, and that incremental deals earn far more than the blended average — is plausible and is exactly what Constellation Software's bulls say too. It is also unfalsifiable from outside the company.

Notable One-Off Items

There are three material distortions in this reporting period and they all sit in the earnings line rather than the cash line.

The Indicor equity-investment gain, $835M, Q2 2026. Roper carved out its industrial businesses into Indicor in 2022 and retained a minority stake. Indicor agreed to sell its instrumentation businesses, and Roper marked its retained interest, producing an $835M gain that lifted Q2 2026 GAAP net earnings to $1,168.5M against operating income of just $584.7M — net income more than double operating income, which should always trigger a second look. GAAP diluted EPS of $11.62 compares with adjusted DEPS of $5.38, and the gain is excluded from adjusted. Every trailing GAAP ratio on the page above — P/E, net margin, ROE — is distorted by it. The cash arrived in Q3: Roper received $1.2B from Indicor's sale of its instrumentation businesses (Q3 release). Management said on the Q3 call that Indicor has agreed to sell its remaining flow-control businesses to Xylem, with about $600M of pre-tax proceeds to Roper expected in Q4. In Q3 itself the gap ran the usual way: GAAP diluted EPS of $4.88 against adjusted $5.89.

Amortisation of acquired intangibles — recurring, non-cash, and the whole argument. This is not a one-off, but it belongs here because it is the largest single wedge between GAAP and cash. Roper carried $9,455.7M of other intangible assets as of September 30, 2026, from deals including CentralReach ($842.0M of identifiable intangibles plus $48.0M of trade names, on a $1,850.0M purchase price completed April 23, 2025) and Subsplash ($365.1M plus $26.0M, on $800.0M completed July 25, 2025). Those amortise through operating expense. The FY2025 gap between GAAP EPS of $14.20 and adjusted EPS of $20.00 — about $625M pre-share-count — is dominated by it. The bull says: this is an accounting entry for cash spent years ago, ignore it. The bear says: the cash is being spent, continuously, on the next deal — Roper deployed roughly $3.3B in the first nine months of 2025 alone — so treating the amortisation as costless while treating the acquired revenue as free is double-counting. The honest answer is that amortisation is the wrong timing for a real cost, not a fake cost.

The 2025 acquisition cohort. Verified from Roper's business-combinations footnote: CentralReach ($1,850.0M, April 23, 2025 — announced March 24, 2025 at a $1.65B net purchase price after expected tax benefits, which is why two different numbers circulate; Application Software), Subsplash ($800.0M, July 25, 2025; Network Software), Convoy/Flexport freight technology ($250.0M, July 30, 2025), Orchard Software ($174.0M, July 28, 2025), Muni-Link ($118.0M, February 19, 2025), Outgo ($39.4M, May 15, 2025), plus HerculesAI/Zero Cognitive Systems (August 8, 2025) and Spectrum AI (August 15, 2025), both undisclosed. Combined goodwill recognised on the bolt-ons alone was $448.0M.

Buyback. 7.865M shares for $2,724.1M in the first half of 2026 at an average of $346.34 (Q2 10-Q) — about 7% of the share count — and then none in Q3, management said, to keep capacity for acquisitions. That is a large repurchase executed near the lows (the May 2026 month-end close was $325.53), and it is the clearest evidence management thought the derating overshot. It also used up the balance-sheet capacity that funds acquisitions, which is why leverage reached 3.4× in June; the Indicor cash and the pause brought it back to 2.9×.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 conventionally mean a stock has risen quickly enough to be considered "overbought"; below 30, "oversold". It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation measure — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a year, and an expensive one overbought.

Over the five years from the October 2021 month-end close of $487.87 to the October 23, 2026 close of $438.14, ROP has delivered a price return of −10.2% — before dividends (a 0.83% yield at the October 23 close) — against +65.5% for SPY and +82.4% for QQQ over the same dates. That is a poor outcome for a stock that spent most of the period being described as one of the highest-quality compounders in the market, and the shape of it matters more than the total. The stock ground higher through 2022–2024 while other software stocks swung widely, made the highest month-end close on this chart, $589.58 in March 2025, and then fell almost without interruption for fourteen months to a low month-end close of $325.53 in May 2026 — a 44.8% drawdown (month-end closes). The recovery since has been jagged: +30.4% to the end of August, the chart's worst month in September (−17.7%), then +25.4% in October to date. At $438.14 the stock is 34.6% above the May low and has made back about two-fifths of the drawdown.

The derating is the story, and it should be stated plainly. Revenue over that fourteen-month decline did not fall; it grew every quarter, from $1,883M in Q1 2025 to $2,109M in Q2 2026 and $2,172M in Q3. Adjusted DEPS rose. What changed was the multiple the market was willing to pay for mid-single-digit organic growth funded by leverage. Read plainly, the market was repricing the category of serial software acquirers: the cost of the debt that funds the deals, the competition from private-equity buyers for the same assets, and the possibility that AI makes small vertical software franchises less defensible than the last decade assumed. September's 17.7% fall came with no results, guidance or SEC filing from Roper, and the 9.7% jump on a modest October beat shows how much of the price now turns on each quarter's evidence that the model still works.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The cash economics are genuinely better than GAAP shows: $2.58B of free cash flow on $8.43B of revenue (30.5%), roughly 78% of EBITDA, against capex of about 0.5% of sales, from a portfolio of systems of record with negative working capital. The FY2025 GAAP-to-adjusted EPS gap of ~$5.80 per share is overwhelmingly amortisation of intangibles bought years ago. At 19.6× the raised FY2026 adjusted DEPS guidance and 17.8× 2027 consensus, the market is paying an ordinary multiple for an above-average asset. Organic growth reached 7% in Q3, net leverage is already down to 2.9×, management has $6B+ of deployment capacity with about $600M more of Indicor proceeds expected in Q4, and it bought back about 7% of the shares near the lows in the first half.

Bear Case

Strip out M&A and this is a roughly 6% organic grower (2026 guidance) with ROIC of 6.90%, 2.9× net leverage, and 90% of its balance sheet in goodwill and intangibles — negative tangible book value of roughly $11.8B. Compounding requires a permanent flow of accretive deals, and in the first nine months of 2026 Roper paid $463.2M for acquisitions against $3,280.4M a year earlier. Part of Q3's organic lift is CentralReach entering the base, and the Network Software and Technology Enabled Products EBITDA margins fell again in Q3. The derating is not a mistake the market is making about accounting; it is a repricing of a model whose growth is purchased, whose cost of capital has risen, and whose acquired assets may be less defensible in an AI-native software market.

Top 3 Risks

01
The M&A engine stalls. Under $50M deployed in H1 2026 and $436M in Q3, against more than $6B of stated capacity. If private sellers do not reset to prices Roper will pay, the company becomes a mid-single-digit-growth software holding company at about 3× leverage — and the multiple that implies is lower than today's.
02
Return on capital does not improve. ROIC of 6.90% is at or below most estimates of cost of capital. If incremental deals do not clear that bar by a wide margin, every dollar deployed destroys value even while it adds revenue and adjusted EPS. This is the risk the adjusted-earnings framing is least equipped to detect.
03
AI erodes vertical-software defensibility. Roper's moat is that nobody rebuilds a niche system of record. If AI materially lowers the cost of rebuilding vertical workflow software, the renewal rates and pricing power that justify 20-plus-times-EBITDA acquisition multiples (CentralReach: about $1.65B net for about $75M of expected EBITDA, roughly 22×, per Roper's March 24, 2025 release) come under pressure — and the goodwill on the balance sheet becomes an impairment question rather than an accounting convention.
Industry Commentary
The cost of capital repriced the serial-acquirer model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For a decade, buying vertical software with cheap debt was close to a free lunch: the assets grew steadily on their own, threw off cash, and the debt cost less than the earnings yield of what it bought. That arithmetic broke. Roper carries $9.72B of debt against $311.0M of cash at 2.9× net debt to EBITDA (management, Q3 call), down from 3.4× in June after the Indicor cash arrived and buybacks paused. The market's verdict: ROP is down 8.7% over the past year while SPY rose 13.1%, and it fell 17.7% in September with no results or guidance from the company before the Q3 print pulled it back. The price is being set on the strategy's cost of money, not on the execution, which keeps beating.

Private-market valuations are resetting — slowly
ONGOING HEADWIND

Roper competes for assets primarily with private-equity sponsors, not with strategic buyers. On the Q3 call management said it has more than $6B to deploy over the next twelve months and that its pipeline leans toward deals it approaches itself rather than large auctions. The evidence is the deal log: roughly $3.3B deployed in the first nine months of 2025 (CentralReach $1,850.0M, Subsplash $800.0M, six bolt-ons totalling $648.5M) versus under $50M in the first half of 2026 and $436M in Q3 (three bolt-ons for Procare, Strata and iPipeline). Discipline is the correct response, and Q3 is a restart. But a capital allocator earns its multiple by allocating capital, and 2026 is still far below 2025's pace.

AI: the same technology is both the threat and the option
EMERGING TAILWIND

Management said on the Q3 call that 17 or 18 of its 21 software businesses now have AI agent products on the market; the release does not break out AI revenue. Read the bull and bear implications side by side. Bull: systems of record sitting on proprietary vertical data — legal time entry, government contract accounting, insurance policy records, ABA therapy notes — are among the best-positioned assets in software for AI monetisation, because the data is captive, the workflows are repetitive and the customers have no alternative vendor to buy the feature from. Every incremental AI SKU sold into an existing base is close to pure margin. Bear: the same technology lowers the cost of building the ninth-best practice-management system for a niche vertical, which is precisely the barrier that makes those franchises worth 20-plus times EBITDA to acquire. Roper has bought two AI-adjacent businesses (HerculesAI and Spectrum AI, both August 2025, both undisclosed) — small enough to read as option premium rather than strategy. The resolution will not be visible in the 2026 numbers, which is exactly why the multiple has to guess.
