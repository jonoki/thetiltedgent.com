01 Company Overview

Roper Technologies buys and holds niche vertical-market software companies — legal practice management, government project accounting, insurance distribution, freight brokerage networks, utility billing, autism-therapy practice software — alongside a smaller portfolio of technology-enabled products such as water meters, ultrasound accessories and surgical navigation systems. FY2025 revenue was $7,903M, up 12.3% from $7,039M in FY2024; the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $8,280M, up 10.9%. At a $40.28B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #652 globally — a rank that has fallen materially, because the stock is down 22.3% over the past 52 weeks and the market cap is down 28.6% once buybacks are netted out.

The central debate is straightforward and it cuts both ways. Roper is a serial acquirer: it pays large multiples for small, sticky software businesses, records most of the price as goodwill and intangibles, and then amortises those intangibles through the income statement. That amortisation is a real accounting charge but not a recurring cash cost, so GAAP earnings genuinely understate the cash the portfolio throws off: FY2025 GAAP diluted EPS was $14.20 against adjusted diluted EPS of roughly $20.00, a gap of about $5.80 per share, or roughly $625M, that is dominated by amortisation of acquired intangibles. That is the bull's framing, and it is defensible. The bear's framing uses the same fact differently: organic revenue growth is only about 5–6%, so the difference between 6% and the 8–10% headline comes from buying other people's revenue with borrowed money. Net debt sits at 3.4× EBITDA, goodwill and intangibles are 87% of total assets, and ROIC is 6.66% — which is at or below most estimates of the company's cost of capital. Both statements are true simultaneously. Everything below is an attempt to weigh them.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SYSTEM-OF-RECORD LOCK-IN NICHE VERTICAL SCALE RECURRING REVENUE BASE NEGATIVE WORKING CAPITAL CAPEX-LIGHT (0.6% OF SALES) DECENTRALISED OPERATING MODEL $5B+ M&A CAPACITY

The real moat operates at the level of the individual subsidiary, not the parent. A law firm running Aderant, a defence contractor running Deltek, an ABA therapy practice running CentralReach — each of these is a system of record that touches billing, compliance and payroll. Ripping it out means re-implementing a business process, retraining staff and risking a revenue interruption, for a piece of software that costs a rounding error relative to the firm's payroll. That is why these businesses renew at very high rates and can push price above inflation for years. Roper's contribution on top is deliberately light: it is a capital allocator, not an operator, and it says so. The financial signature of that model is unusual and genuinely attractive — capital expenditure of roughly $50M against $8.28B of revenue (operating cash flow $2.67B less free cash flow $2.62B, about 0.6% of sales), a current ratio of 0.55 because customers pay for software annually in advance, and free cash flow of $2.62B, or 31.6% of revenue, equal to about 81% of adjusted EBITDA. Deferred revenue funds the working capital; there is essentially no plant to maintain. Cash conversion of that quality is the single strongest argument that GAAP earnings understate the business.

The honest counterweight: the moat protects the installed base, not the growth rate. A system of record that nobody leaves and nobody new adopts still produces 4–5% organic growth in perpetuity. Roper's compounding has never come from the software growing fast; it has come from buying more of it. That makes the moat a durability asset and the M&A machine a growth asset — and only one of those is under the company's control.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
The model requires perpetual M&A, and M&A requires a price. Organic growth of 5–6% versus reported growth of 8–10% means roughly a third of top-line growth is purchased. Management said on the Q2 2026 call that it has over $5B of annualised deployment capacity but that it is being "patient and disciplined" and expects deal activity to "break loose later this year and into 2027." Only two small bolt-ons totalling under $50M were completed in the first half of 2026 — against roughly $3.3B deployed in the first nine months of 2025. A capital allocator that cannot allocate capital is, temporarily, an ordinary 5%-growth software company with leverage.
▸
Balance-sheet composition. At June 30, 2026: goodwill of $21,330.7M and other intangibles of $9,347.3M against total assets of $35,169.5M — 87.2% of the balance sheet is purchase accounting. Shareholders' equity of roughly $18.6B (derived from the reported 0.61 debt-to-equity ratio on $11,319.4M of debt) is therefore backed by negative tangible book value of roughly $12B. This is not inherently a scandal — it is what buying software companies looks like — but it means any impairment is an equity event, and it is why ROE of 13.07% sits on a denominator that is mostly other people's businesses at the price Roper paid.
▸
Leverage at 3.4× in a higher-rate world. Net debt/EBITDA rose from 3.1× at Q1 2026 to 3.4× at Q2 2026 — largely because the company spent $1.2B on buybacks in Q2 alone at an average of about $341 per share. Management has now signalled a near-term preference for de-leveraging over buybacks. That is prudent, and it is also an admission: the same balance sheet cannot simultaneously fund $5B of acquisitions, a buyback and a lower leverage ratio.
▸
ROIC below plausible cost of capital. Reported ROIC is 6.66%, against ROE of 13.07% and ROA of 4.23%. For a company whose entire identity is capital allocation, a mid-single-digit return on invested capital is the most uncomfortable number on the page. The defence — that ROIC is depressed by goodwill from deals still ramping, and that incremental deals earn far more than the blended average — is plausible and is exactly what Constellation Software's bulls say too. It is also unfalsifiable from outside the company.

Notable One-Off Items

There are three material distortions in this reporting period and they all sit in the earnings line rather than the cash line.

The Indicor equity-investment gain, $835M, Q2 2026. Roper carved out its industrial businesses into Indicor in 2022 and retained a minority stake. AMETEK agreed to buy the instrumentation businesses, and Roper marked its retained interest, producing an $835M gain that lifted Q2 2026 GAAP net earnings to $1,168.6M against operating income of just $584.7M — net income more than double operating income, which should always trigger a second look. GAAP diluted EPS of $11.62 compares with adjusted DEPS of $5.38, and the gain is excluded from adjusted. Every trailing GAAP ratio on the page above — P/E, net margin, ROE — is distorted by it. Roper expects roughly $1.2B of after-tax proceeds from the transaction, which is real cash and real M&A firepower.

Amortisation of acquired intangibles — recurring, non-cash, and the whole argument. This is not a one-off, but it belongs here because it is the largest single wedge between GAAP and cash. Roper capitalised $9,347.3M of other intangible assets as of June 30, 2026, from deals including CentralReach ($842.0M of identifiable intangibles plus $48.0M of trade names, on a $1,850.0M purchase price completed April 23, 2025) and Subsplash ($365.1M plus $26.0M, on $800.0M completed July 25, 2025). Those amortise through operating expense. The FY2025 gap between GAAP EPS of $14.20 and adjusted EPS of roughly $20.00 — about $625M pre-share-count — is dominated by it. The bull says: this is an accounting entry for cash spent years ago, ignore it. The bear says: the cash is being spent, continuously, on the next deal — Roper deployed roughly $3.3B in the first nine months of 2025 alone — so treating the amortisation as costless while treating the acquired revenue as free is double-counting. The honest answer is that amortisation is the wrong timing for a real cost, not a fake cost.

The 2025 acquisition cohort. Verified from Roper's business-combinations footnote: CentralReach ($1,850.0M, April 23, 2025 — announced March 24, 2025 at a $1.65B net purchase price after expected tax benefits, which is why two different numbers circulate; Application Software), Subsplash ($800.0M, July 25, 2025; Network Software), Convoy/Flexport freight technology ($250.0M, July 30, 2025), Orchard Software ($174.0M, July 28, 2025), Muni-Link ($118.0M, February 19, 2025), Outgo ($39.4M, May 15, 2025), plus HerculesAI/Zero Cognitive Systems (August 8, 2025) and Spectrum AI (August 15, 2025), both undisclosed. Combined goodwill recognised on the bolt-ons alone was $448.0M.

Buyback. 9.0M shares for $3.2B in the first eight months of 2026 — roughly 8% of shares outstanding, at an average near $355 per share in Q2 (3.6M shares for $1.2B at ~$341). That is a genuinely large, genuinely accretive repurchase executed near the lows, and it is the clearest evidence management thinks the derating overshot. It also consumed the balance-sheet capacity that funds acquisitions, which is why leverage went from 3.1× to 3.4× and why the stated priority has now shifted to de-leveraging.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 conventionally mean a stock has risen quickly enough to be considered "overbought"; below 30, "oversold". It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation measure — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a year, and an expensive one overbought.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $487.87 to the September 4, 2026 close of $407.28, ROP has delivered a price return of −16.5% — before the roughly 0.7–1.0% annual dividend. That is a poor outcome for a stock that spent most of the period being described as one of the highest-quality compounders in the market, and the shape of it matters more than the total. The stock ground higher through 2022–2024 while the software complex was volatile, made an all-time monthly closing high of $589.58 in March 2025, and then fell almost without interruption for fourteen months to a low monthly close of $325.53 in May 2026 — a 44.8% drawdown. The rally since then (+25.1% off the low, driven by the Q2 beat and the $3.2B buyback) has recovered roughly a third of it.

The derating is the story, and it should be stated plainly. Revenue over that fourteen-month decline did not fall; it grew every quarter, from $1,883M in Q1 2025 to $2,109M in Q2 2026. Adjusted DEPS rose. Free cash flow rose. What changed was the multiple the market was willing to pay for mid-single-digit organic growth funded by leverage — and that repricing was not company-specific. Constellation Software, the closest structural analogue to Roper anywhere in public markets, has seen its market capitalisation fall 33.4% over its own trailing year on 17.7% revenue growth. When two serial acquirers on different continents with different growth rates derate together, the market is repricing the category: the cost of the debt that funds the deals, the price competition from private-equity buyers for the same assets, and the possibility that AI makes small vertical software franchises less defensible than the last decade assumed.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The cash economics are genuinely better than GAAP shows: $2.62B of free cash flow on $8.28B of revenue (31.6%), roughly 81% of EBITDA, against capex of about 0.6% of sales, from a portfolio of systems of record with negative working capital. The FY2025 GAAP-to-adjusted EPS gap of ~$5.80 per share is overwhelmingly amortisation of intangibles bought years ago. At 18.3× guided FY2026 adjusted DEPS — versus a five-year history of trading at more than twice that multiple — the market is now paying a below-market price for an above-market-quality asset. Management has $5B+ of deployment capacity, ~$1.2B of Indicor proceeds arriving, a private-market valuation reset in front of it, and it just repurchased 8% of the shares near the lows.

Bear Case

Strip out M&A and this is a 5–6% organic grower with ROIC of 6.66%, 3.4× net leverage, and 87% of its balance sheet in goodwill and intangibles — negative tangible book value of roughly $12B. Compounding requires a permanent flow of accretive deals, and in the first half of 2026 Roper deployed under $50M against $3.3B in the first nine months of 2025. Segment operating margins fell year over year in all three segments. The derating is not a mistake the market is making about accounting; it is a repricing of a model whose growth is purchased, whose cost of capital has risen, and whose acquired assets may be less defensible in an AI-native software market. Constellation Software derating 33.4% simultaneously says this is structural, not idiosyncratic.

Top 3 Risks

01
The M&A engine stalls. Under $50M deployed in H1 2026 against $5B+ of stated capacity. If private sellers do not reset to prices Roper will pay, the company becomes a 5%-growth software holding company at 3.4× leverage — and the multiple that implies is lower than today's.
02
Return on capital does not improve. ROIC of 6.66% is at or below most estimates of cost of capital. If incremental deals do not clear that bar by a wide margin, every dollar deployed destroys value even while it adds revenue and adjusted EPS. This is the risk the adjusted-earnings framing is least equipped to detect.
03
AI erodes vertical-software defensibility. Roper's moat is that nobody rebuilds a niche system of record. If AI materially lowers the cost of rebuilding vertical workflow software, the renewal rates and pricing power that justify 20-plus-times-EBITDA acquisition multiples come under pressure — and the goodwill on the balance sheet becomes an impairment question rather than an accounting convention.
Industry Commentary
The cost of capital repriced the serial-acquirer model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For a decade, buying vertical software with cheap debt was close to a free lunch: the assets compounded at 5–8% organically, threw off cash, and the debt cost less than the earnings yield of what it bought. That arithmetic broke. Roper carries $11.32B of debt against $364.9M of cash at 3.4× net EBITDA, and has now prioritised de-leveraging over buybacks. The market's verdict is visible in two places at once: ROP is down 22.3% over 52 weeks and Constellation Software's market cap is down 33.4% over its own trailing year despite 17.7% revenue growth. When the two purest expressions of the same strategy derate together on different continents, the repricing is of the strategy, not the execution.

Private-market valuations are resetting — slowly
ONGOING HEADWIND

Roper competes for assets primarily with private-equity sponsors, not with strategic buyers. Management's Q2 commentary — sponsor conversations are now "real conversations," valuations are resetting, activity should "break loose later this year and into 2027" — is a polite way of saying that sellers' price expectations have not yet fallen far enough. The evidence is the deal log: roughly $3.3B deployed in the first nine months of 2025 (CentralReach $1,850.0M, Subsplash $800.0M, six bolt-ons totalling $648.5M) versus under $50M in the first half of 2026. Discipline is the correct response and management deserves credit for it. But a capital allocator earns its multiple by allocating capital, and for two quarters it has not.

AI: the same technology is both the threat and the option
EMERGING TAILWIND

Roper is shipping agentic products across 21 software verticals and explicitly models no meaningful 2026 revenue from them. Read the bull and bear implications side by side. Bull: systems of record sitting on proprietary vertical data — legal time entry, government contract accounting, insurance policy records, ABA therapy notes — are among the best-positioned assets in software for AI monetisation, because the data is captive, the workflows are repetitive and the customers have no alternative vendor to buy the feature from. Every incremental AI SKU sold into an existing base is close to pure margin. Bear: the same technology lowers the cost of building the ninth-best practice-management system for a niche vertical, which is precisely the barrier that makes those franchises worth 20-plus times EBITDA to acquire. Roper has bought two AI-adjacent businesses (HerculesAI and Spectrum AI, both August 2025, both undisclosed) — small enough to read as option premium rather than strategy. The resolution will not be visible in the 2026 numbers, which is exactly why the multiple has to guess.
