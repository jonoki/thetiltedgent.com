01 Company Overview

Revvity is what was left of PerkinElmer after PerkinElmer sold itself in pieces. In 2022–23 the company sold its applied, food and enterprise-services businesses — and the PerkinElmer name — to New Mountain Capital for about $2.45 billion. The part that stayed public, the life-sciences and diagnostics business, needed a new name; it picked Revvity ("revolution" plus vita, Latin for life), and on May 16, 2023 the ticker changed from PKI to RVTY. It is the same listed company and the same share series — which is why the price chart below runs straight through the rename. Today Revvity sells instruments, reagents, software and screening tests to drug developers, academic and government labs, and clinical laboratories: FY2025 revenue was $2.86B, split almost exactly in half between Life Sciences ($1.43B) and Diagnostics ($1.43B).

The story of the last five years is a hangover and then a sober-up. Revenue peaked at $3.83B in FY2021, when pandemic testing was pouring through the Diagnostics business, then fell 13.5% in FY2022 and 17.0% in FY2023 as that testing disappeared. It has been flat to slightly up since: $2.755B in FY2024, $2.856B in FY2025. The stock followed the revenue down — from a $201.06 month-end close in December 2021 to $82.85 in October 2023 — and then spent two and a half years going nowhere. What changed in 2026 is the direction of the news: guidance raised in August, a lower-margin China business put up for sale, and an AI-driven drug-discovery bid for its screening instruments. The stock has gone from an $86.62 close at the end of April 2026 to $143.59 — up 66% in under five months, and now trading above every analyst average target on file. The question this report keeps coming back to: how much of the next two years has the market already paid for?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Newborn-screening installed base Consumable reagents on placed instruments High-content screening franchise Signals scientific software Regulated diagnostic assays Sales reach in 160+ countries

The durable part of Revvity is the razor-and-blade shape of much of its revenue: once a newborn-screening lab or a pharma screening group has validated a workflow on Revvity instruments, it keeps buying the reagents that run on them. Newborn-screening reagents growing high single digits in Q2 2026 despite falling birth rates is the clearest evidence of that stickiness. The weaker part is that Revvity is a mid-sized player in a field of giants — Thermo Fisher ($243.6B market cap) and Danaher ($151.7B) are each roughly ten to fifteen times its $16.0B size — and much of Life Sciences depends on customers' research budgets, which are cyclical and, in the case of academic and government labs, political.

Research budgets are someone else's decision. On the Q4 2025 call management named NIH funding changes, tariffs and pharma-policy uncertainty among 2025's headwinds, and nothing fetched says they have gone away. 02
China is being exited, not fixed. The immunodiagnostics sale removes a shrinking business, but it takes until the end of 2027 to close, and the company keeps a minority stake. Until then the declining business still sits in reported numbers.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 close of $173.29 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $143.59, the stock lost 17.1% of its price. The $0.28 annual dividend adds back only about $1.40 a share over the period, so total return is roughly −16%. The shape is a U: a 58.8% slide from the $201.06 December 2021 month-end to the $82.85 October 2023 low as pandemic testing revenue vanished, two and a half years of range-trading between roughly $87 and $128, and then a 2026 break-out that has taken the stock 66% off its April low.

The 6-period monthly RSI now reads 81.2 — the highest reading anywhere in the five-year series and firmly overbought. The extreme at the other end was 17.0 in October 2023. The 10-month moving average is about $107.87, 25% below the price: the trend is unambiguously up, and stretched. Momentum this strong can persist, but it is the same signal that sat at 17 near the bottom — the price has moved faster than the fundamentals, in both directions.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The pandemic hangover is over and the business underneath is growing again: pro forma organic growth guided to 4–5% for 2026 and 4–6% for Q3, Diagnostics up 11% in Q2, adjusted operating margin near 29%, free-cash-flow conversion of 117% in Q2, and a €500M debt repayment already done. The China exit removes the one part that was shrinking. The AI angle is concrete rather than a slide: high-content screening orders grew double digits and demand is running ahead of production capacity. On FY2026 adjusted EPS the stock is about 27x, and consensus has adjusted EPS growing 10% in 2027. If margins reach the upper 20s on growing revenue, earnings can catch up with the price.

Bear Case

The price already made the move. At $143.59 the stock is about 12% above the average analyst target, the 6-month RSI is 81 (the highest in five years), and the rally has come from a 66% re-rating in under five months rather than a matching change in earnings. On GAAP, the stock trades at 68.7x trailing EPS with ROIC of 4.4%. Life Sciences shrank 3% last quarter, research budgets depend on NIH funding and pharma policy, and part of Q2's beat came from tariff refunds that will not repeat. RBC's question — whether 6–8% long-term organic growth is durable — is the right one, and the answer is not in yet.

Industry Commentary
AI as a buyer of lab equipment
EMERGING TAILWIND

Most AI stories in 2026 are about software replacing people. Revvity's is the opposite: AI-driven drug discovery needs more experimental data, generated fast, which means automated cell imaging and screening. On the Q2 2026 call management described "Lab-in-the-Loop" workflows in which AI models become part of the experiment cycle, reported double-digit order growth in high-content screening, and said non-traditional AI customers are now ordering instruments alongside pharma and biotech. KeyBanc's $165 target cites the same thing. The open question is scale: screening instruments are one line inside a $359M-a-quarter segment that shrank last quarter.

China diagnostics and research-budget policy
ONGOING HEADWIND

Revvity's decision to sell its China immunodiagnostics business followed double-digit declines there and what the company called persistent policy-induced headwinds; it concluded that the investment needed to defend the position would be better spent elsewhere. In the US, management's list of 2025 headwinds included changes in NIH funding, evolving tariffs and pharma-policy uncertainty. Tariffs have cut both ways this year — Revvity received $16M of refunds in Q2 — which is a reminder of how much of this sector's earnings path is set outside the companies.

The valuation debate — bull and bear framing
THE CENTRAL QUESTION

At $143.59 Revvity trades at about 25–27x adjusted earnings, 20.9x EV/EBITDA and a ~3.5% free-cash-flow yield. The bull framing: this is a business re-rating from "post-COVID problem" to "AI-exposed tools company with 29% adjusted margins", and multiples in that bucket are higher than where the stock started the year. The bear framing: a 66% move in five months on 4–5% organic growth is sentiment, not arithmetic; the consensus target of about $128 says the Street has not followed; and a six-month RSI of 81 has historically been a place to be careful rather than greedy. The way to settle it is not the multiple — it is whether Life Sciences grows again in Q3 and whether margins reach the upper 20s on reported, not just pro forma, numbers.
