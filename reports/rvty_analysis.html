01 Company Overview

Revvity is what was left of PerkinElmer after PerkinElmer sold itself in pieces. In 2022–23 the company sold its applied, food and enterprise-services businesses — and the PerkinElmer name — to New Mountain Capital for about $2.45 billion. The part that stayed public, the life-sciences and diagnostics business, needed a new name; it picked Revvity ("revolution" plus vita, Latin for life), and on May 16, 2023 the ticker changed from PKI to RVTY. It is the same listed company and the same share series — which is why the price chart below runs straight through the rename. Today Revvity sells instruments, reagents, software and screening tests to drug developers, academic and government labs, and clinical laboratories: FY2025 revenue was $2.86B, split almost exactly in half between Life Sciences ($1.43B) and Diagnostics ($1.43B).

The story of the last five years is a hangover and then a sober-up. Revenue peaked at $3.83B in FY2021, when pandemic testing was pouring through the Diagnostics business, then fell 13.5% in FY2022 and 17.0% in FY2023 as that testing disappeared. It has been flat to slightly up since: $2.755B in FY2024, $2.856B in FY2025. The stock followed the revenue down — from a $201.06 month-end close in December 2021 to $82.85 in October 2023 — and then spent two and a half years going nowhere. What changed in 2026 is the direction of the news: guidance raised in August and again in November, the China immunodiagnostics business put up for sale, and an AI-driven drug-discovery bid for its screening instruments. The stock has gone from an $86.62 close at the end of April 2026 to $159.43 — up 84% in about six months — and sits 6% above the average analyst target, even after a wave of target increases that followed the Q3 report. The question this report keeps coming back to: how much of the next two years has the market already paid for?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Newborn-screening installed base Consumable reagents on placed instruments High-content screening franchise Signals scientific software Regulated diagnostic assays Sales reach in 160+ countries

The durable part of Revvity is the razor-and-blade shape of much of its revenue: once a newborn-screening lab or a pharma screening group has validated a workflow on Revvity instruments, it keeps buying the reagents that run on them. Newborn-screening reagents growing high single digits in Q2 2026 despite falling birth rates is the clearest evidence of that stickiness. The weaker part is that Revvity is a mid-sized player in a field of giants — Thermo Fisher ($261.7B market cap) and Danaher ($165.3B) are roughly nine to fifteen times its $17.8B size — and much of Life Sciences depends on customers' research budgets, which are cyclical and, in the case of academic and government labs, political.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Research budgets are someone else's decision. On the Q4 2025 call management named NIH funding changes, tariffs and pharma-policy uncertainty among 2025's headwinds. Life Sciences swung from a 3% organic decline in Q2 2026 to 9% growth in Q3, which shows how quickly it moves; a cut in academic or government spending lands here first.
02
China is being exited, not fixed. The immunodiagnostics sale removes a shrinking business, but it takes until the end of 2027 to close, and the company keeps a minority stake. Until then the declining business still sits in reported numbers.
03
GAAP earnings are thin. TTM GAAP EPS is $2.25 against FY2026 adjusted guidance of $5.40–$5.45. Return on equity is 3.45% and ROIC 4.53%. The adjusted numbers are what the market prices, but the gap between them and GAAP is more than half of earnings.
04
The price has run ahead of the average target. At $159.43 the stock is 6.4% above the $149.87 average analyst target and level with the $160 median. The eight targets raised after the Q3 print run from $160 to $190, so the Street has followed part of the way.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the 2026 figures. First, the China immunodiagnostics sale: the company now guides and reports on a pro forma basis that excludes a business worth about 6% of FY2025 revenue, so reported and pro forma numbers differ. Second, tariff refunds: $16M received in Q2 2026 added about $0.11 to pro forma adjusted EPS, and management chose to reinvest roughly half; the Q3 release does not mention any. Third, the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is wide — Q3 2026 GAAP EPS of $0.56 versus adjusted $1.38 — so which EPS you use changes the P/E from ~71x to ~30x. FY2023 GAAP net income of $693M was also well above the years around it and should not be read as a normal year.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the chart window, from the November 2021 month-end close of $182.16 to the November 4, 2026 settled close of $159.43, the stock lost 12.5% of its price; over the same dates the SPY S&P 500 fund rose 68.3% and the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ 75.7% (price only). The extreme at the other end was 16.9 in October 2023. The 10-month moving average is about $119.89, 25% below the price: the trend is unambiguously up, and stretched. On daily closes the 14-day RSI is 55.4, close to neutral, so the monthly reading measures the size of the six-month run rather than the last few weeks. Momentum this strong can persist, but it is the same signal that sat at 17 near the bottom — the price has moved faster than the fundamentals, in both directions.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The pandemic hangover is over and the business underneath is growing again: 7% pro forma organic growth in Q3 against a 4–6% guide, full-year guidance raised twice (now about 5% organic growth and $5.40–$5.45 of adjusted EPS), Life Sciences back to 9% growth with Signals software up in the high teens, a 29.4% pro forma adjusted operating margin, and the €500M note already repaid. The China exit removes the one part that was shrinking. The AI angle is concrete rather than a slide: high-content screening growth accelerated again and the backlog grew across almost every instrument group. On FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS of $5.93 the stock is about 27x, and eight brokers raised targets to between $160 and $190 after the print. If margins hold near 29% on growing revenue, earnings can catch up with the price.

Bear Case

The price already made the move. At $159.43 the stock is about 6% above the average analyst target and the 6-period monthly RSI is 85.5, the highest in five years. The shares are up 84% since April while the midpoint of full-year adjusted EPS guidance has risen about 1.4% since August ($5.35 → $5.425): investors are paying more for each dollar of expected profit, not just getting more profit. On GAAP, the stock trades at 70.9x trailing EPS with ROIC of 4.5%. Research budgets depend on NIH funding and pharma policy, and 11 of 18 analysts still rate it Hold after raising their targets. Whether growth can stay above the roughly 5% guided for 2026 is the open question, and the November 13 Investor Day is the next chance to answer it.

Valuation has outrun the average target. A price above the consensus average means further gains need analysts to keep raising numbers, and the post-print target raises now have to be earned in Q4 and in the Investor Day targets.

Industry Commentary
AI as a buyer of lab equipment
EMERGING TAILWIND

Most AI stories in 2026 are about software replacing people. Revvity's is the opposite: AI-driven drug discovery needs more experimental data, generated fast, which means automated cell imaging and screening. On the Q2 2026 call management described "Lab-in-the-Loop" workflows in which AI models become part of the experiment cycle, reported double-digit order growth in high-content screening, and said non-traditional AI customers are now ordering instruments alongside pharma and biotech. On the Q3 call CEO Prahlad Singh said AI-related demand is beginning to show up across a wider cross section of the catalogue, and the CFO said the backlog grew across almost every instrument group. The open question is scale: screening instruments are one line inside a $380M-a-quarter segment.

China diagnostics and research-budget policy
ONGOING HEADWIND

Revvity's decision to sell its China immunodiagnostics business followed double-digit declines there and what the company called persistent policy-induced headwinds; it concluded that the investment needed to defend the position would be better spent elsewhere. In the US, management's list of 2025 headwinds included changes in NIH funding, evolving tariffs and pharma-policy uncertainty. Tariffs have cut both ways this year — Revvity received $16M of refunds in Q2 — which is a reminder of how much of this sector's earnings path is set outside the companies.

The valuation debate — bull and bear framing
THE CENTRAL QUESTION

At $159.43 Revvity trades at about 27–30x adjusted earnings (FY2027 and FY2026 consensus), 22.3x EV/EBITDA and a ~3.3% free-cash-flow yield. The bull framing: investors now value it as an AI-exposed tools company with 29% adjusted margins rather than a post-COVID problem. The bear framing: an 84% move in six months on about 5% organic growth is sentiment, not arithmetic; the average target of about $150 sits below the price; and a 6-period monthly RSI of 85, the highest in the five-year chart, says the run has been unusually one-sided. The way to settle it is not the multiple — it is whether Life Sciences keeps growing after Q3's 9% and whether margins hold near 29% on reported, not just pro forma, numbers.
