01 Company Overview

Most people know Samsung from the phone in their pocket or the TV on their wall. The stock market knows it as something else: a memory-chip maker, with an estimated 39.4% of global DRAM revenue in the first half of 2026 (company estimate on DRAMeXchange data). In the third quarter of 2026 the chip division, Device Solutions, booked ₩147.1 trillion of sales and ₩106.5 trillion of operating profit — 99% of the whole company's ₩107.4 trillion. The phones, TVs and appliances that carry the brand lost money in the same quarter.

The numbers moved so fast that the scale is hard to hold in your head. Operating profit was ₩12.2 trillion in Q3 2025 and ₩107.4 trillion in Q3 2026, up 783%, and Samsung says it is the first tech company in the world to pass ₩100 trillion in a quarter. Quarterly revenue more than doubled to ₩195.3 trillion. Earnings per share for the quarter, ₩11,462, were 1.7 times the company's whole-year figure for 2025 (₩6,605). Against forecasts the quarter was mixed (FnGuide consensus, via JoongAng Daily): operating profit was above the ₩106 trillion expected and in line with the ₩107.4 trillion Samsung flagged in its October 8 preliminary guidance, but revenue of ₩195.25 trillion was 2.5% under the ₩200.3 trillion expected and net profit of ₩75.14 trillion was 14% under the ₩87.4 trillion expected. The gap opens below operating profit: Samsung booked net finance expense of ₩9.5 trillion in Q3 against net finance income of ₩4.7 trillion in Q2. The cause of the boom is the AI build-out: data-center servers need vast amounts of DRAM, flash storage and HBM, the industry is short of all three, and prices have climbed quarter after quarter. The share price followed, then gave back a good part of it: from ₩71,300 at the end of November 2021 to ₩189,900 on November 9, 2026, up 166% in won, but down 31.2% from the ₩276,000 of the previous edition (October 2). It peaked at ₩374,500 during the session of June 19, 2026. Since then two sell-offs that hit chip stocks worldwide, on October 13–15 (worries about interest rates and AI financing, with fears of Chinese competition earlier in the week) and November 5–6 (after the US midterm elections, on fears of tighter rules for AI data centers), took the stock down with the whole Korean market. The question for this report is the oldest one in memory chips: how long does a shortage last once everyone starts building factories to end it?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

39.4% DRAM share (1H26 est.) Manufacturing scale ₩197.6T net cash Memory + foundry + packaging Multi-year supply contracts #1 TV brand 20 years OLED panel leadership

The moat in chips is the cost of a seat at the table: Samsung spent ₩29.0T on capex in Q3 alone and ₩56.9T in the first nine months of 2026, expects about ₩84.4T for the full year (company release), and the half-year report notes that in foundry only about five companies with large capex budgets dominate the market. Samsung both makes memory and runs a foundry, and the Q3 release credits demand for HBM base dies with lifting foundry earnings. It also has a balance sheet that can keep building through a downturn. On the Q2 call (July 30) memory sales head Jaejune Kim said Samsung had "already finalized agreements with the top five global data center customers"; the contracts run five years, with a further year that can be added at each annual negotiation, carry "substantial advance payments," and for mainstream products set "minimum price floors." By the Q3 call (October 29) JoongAng Daily reported 10 major customers signed and 10 more in talks, some close to finalizing, and Kim said the target of covering 60% to 70% of capacity with long-term agreements is "looking increasingly achievable." Korea Investment & Securities' Chae Min-sook has argued that five-year contracts of this kind reduce the chance of a repeat of past oversupply (Seoul Economic Daily). The vulnerability is the same as ever: memory is a commodity sold at whatever price the shortage allows, and the shortage is what everyone, Samsung included, is spending to end.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The cycle has turned before. In the last downturn, annual operating profit fell from ₩43.4T in 2022 to ₩6.6T in 2023 (StockAnalysis annual data), and Q2 2025 profit was just ₩4.7T. Today's 55.0% operating margin (Q3 2026; 14.1% a year earlier) is a price, not a permanent feature. BNK Investment & Securities' Lee Min-hee cut the rating to hold in September on the view that producers are expanding capacity aggressively while memory prices have little room left to rise, and on October 29 warned that the pace of AI infrastructure spending may have peaked in the third quarter (JoongAng Daily).
02
Playing catch-up in HBM. CNBC described Samsung as seeking to catch up with domestic rival SK hynix in high-bandwidth memory, the highest-value AI product. Samsung is shipping HBM4 (60% of its Q3 HBM sales, per JoongAng Daily) and has sent HBM4E samples to customers, but in the most profitable corner of the market it has not been the leader. On the Q2 call Jaejune Kim said Samsung expects its second-half HBM share to be "broadly in line with our overall DRAM market share."
03
The rest of the company is squeezed by its own success. MX/Networks lost ₩0.6T in Q3 and VD/DA lost ₩0.2T, with higher component, raw-material and logistics costs behind it. If memory prices fall, those units recover, but the group's profit falls far more, because DS is 99% of it.
04
Labour and politics take a share. CNBC reported that Samsung agreed this year to scrap its bonus cap and set aside 10.5% of operating profit for bonuses after a weeks-long union protest. At Q3's ₩107.4T of operating profit, that is a large and rising cost.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items matter for reading the figures. First, swings below operating profit: net finance expense was ₩9.5T in Q3 against net finance income of ₩4.7T in Q2, so pre-tax profit (₩98.5T) ran ₩8.9T below operating profit instead of above it, and net profit (₩75.1T) came in 14% under the FnGuide forecast of ₩87.4T (JoongAng Daily). Second, one-off tariff refunds: the release says non-recurring items, including US tariff refunds, lifted Harman's earnings. Third, bonus provisions: on the Q2 call CFO Sooncheol Park said Q2 carried the whole first-half accrual for a special incentive of about 10.5% of first-half operating profit; part of it was added to the value of inventory and reaches cost of sales as that inventory is sold from Q3. Citi had put the extra bonus cost in its Q3 forecast at about ₩5T (Seoul Economic Daily, September 3). Fourth, share buybacks for staff, not for cancellation: in the first half the board approved three repurchases worth ₩2.50T, ₩3.57T and ₩7.17T of common shares to fund employee stock compensation, per the half-year report — these hand shares to staff rather than shrinking the count — and Q3 cash flow shows ₩12.5T spent on treasury stock. Separately, on April 2, 2026 Samsung cancelled 73,359,314 common and 13,603,461 preferred treasury shares.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of ₩71,300 to the November 9, 2026 close of ₩189,900, the shares rose 166% in won, before dividends. For a dollar investor the won's fall from ₩1,191 to ₩1,342 per dollar over the same window cut that to about +136% (our calculation from Yahoo's KRW=X closes). Almost none of it came in the first four years: the stock closed January 2025 at ₩52,400, below where it started, and has risen 3.6-fold since. The best month in the series was May 2026 (+43.8%), the worst March 2026 (−22.8%), July 2026 (−21.4%) was the second worst and October 2026 (−16.2%) the fourth — the sign of a stock that now moves like a leveraged bet on AI memory.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 5.9 times trailing earnings and 4.0 times this year's consensus for a producer of an estimated 39% of the world's DRAM revenue in a market its own management calls undersupplied, with ₩197.6T of net cash, a 16.7% trailing free-cash-flow yield and up to ₩110T of shareholder returns planned for 2026, ₩55–75T of it still to be decided in January. For 2027 Samsung says AI-driven demand will far exceed industry supply; on the Q2 call memory sales head Jaejune Kim said the shortage gets worse in 2027; Yuanta sees bottlenecks into 2028; and multi-year contracts with the biggest data-center buyers, backed by advance payments and price floors, make this cycle less dependent on day-to-day market prices than the last. Q3 was the first ₩100T operating-profit quarter. If the shortage lasts two more years rather than one, today's price looks cheap even if investors pay far less for each won of peak profit.

Bear Case

Low multiples on peak earnings are how memory stocks always look at the top. Operating margin went from 14.1% to 55.0% in a year; it can go back. Every producer is adding capacity — Samsung itself spent ₩56.9T in nine months, expects about ₩84.4T for 2026 and plans a second Texas fab, and Samsung and SK hynix have announced ₩800T for a new hub — while Chinese makers push into ordinary DRAM and NAND. Samsung trails SK hynix in HBM, the part of the market most insulated from that. The share price is also hostage to sentiment about AI and to how Korea's market works: it lost 31.2% between October 2 and November 9, in sell-offs set off by interest-rate and AI-financing worries and by the US midterm result, and BNK warns that AI infrastructure spending may have peaked in the third quarter. Q3 itself fell short on revenue and net profit, and Samsung has not yet said how it will pay out the remaining ₩55–75T.

Top 3 Risks

01
Memory prices peak. 99% of Q3 operating profit came from the chip division. TrendForce, cited by Financial News, expected DRAM contract prices to rise 13–18% in Q3, a smaller increase than in Q2; Kiwoom expects ordinary DRAM price gains of around 3% in Q4. Slowing increases are the first stage of every memory downturn.
02
AI spending slows. The late-July chip sell-off reflected concerns that AI infrastructure spending may be "peaking faster than expected," Forrester's Charlie Dai said (CNBC). On November 5 the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% and Micron 17.46% on fears of tighter rules for AI data centers (JoongAng Daily). Samsung's server-heavy mix makes it more exposed to the data-center buyers' budgets than in any past cycle.
03
Market structure and concentration. With Samsung and SK hynix around half the KOSPI's weight, index funds, leveraged ETFs and margin calls amplify every move. Korea has tightened the rules for leveraged ETFs, with new cash-deposit requirements taking effect on July 31 (CNBC), but the kind of investors who drove the swings have not gone away. Meritz Securities' Lee Jin-woo said "The Korean stock market is in a more vulnerable position than other markets in terms of supply and demand" (JoongAng Daily, November 6).
Industry Commentary
The AI memory shortage
CURRENT TAILWIND

Factory capacity used for HBM is capacity not making ordinary DRAM. That is the core of the shortage case Yuanta's Baek Gil-hyun makes: "given that HBM is eating into conventional DRAM capacity and that the spread of artificial intelligence inference is driving demand for high-capacity memory, supply constraints are likely to ease later than expected" (Seoul Economic Daily translation). Samsung's own outlook says the same in plainer terms: undersupply despite softer phone and PC demand. On the Q2 call memory sales head Jaejune Kim pointed to the more-than-three-year lead time from new fab construction to wafer output and said Samsung considers it "unlikely to see any significant increase in incremental supply through 2028." On the Q3 call Samsung said its HBM output for 2027 is already booked and expects supply to stay tight through at least 2028 (JoongAng Daily). Samsung and SK hynix together produce about two-thirds of the world's memory chips, according to the AP. Reuters calculated that the two will hold a combined $263B of net cash by year-end.

Capacity, China and the shape of the next downturn
BUILDING HEADWIND

The response to a shortage is construction. SK hynix announced ₩100T of new Korean investment in July (CNBC), Samsung is building in Taylor, Texas, and the two have a combined ₩800T plan for a new hub in Korea's southwest. China is the second source of supply: the AP reported investor concern over the strong market debut of ChangXin Memory Technologies and over reports of a state-owned Chinese company mass-producing domestic lithography machines, and Kiwoom expects CXMT and YMTC to compete hard for share in ordinary memory. In mid-October Samsung and SK hynix shares also fell on concerns about intensifying competition from China (Yahoo Finance, October 15). The bull framing: long-term contracts with the five largest data-center buyers and HBM's appetite for capacity stretch this cycle beyond the usual two years. The bear framing: every previous memory boom ended with too many factories, and this one has more money behind its factories than any before it.

Korea's market reforms and the shareholder-return turn
STRUCTURAL CHANGE

Korean equities have long traded at a discount to other developed markets — the "Korea discount" — which Siblis Research says has narrowed sharply as earnings expectations improved. At Samsung, shareholder returns are rising with profits. The half-year report shows 86.96 million treasury shares cancelled in April 2026. Then came the ₩90–110T 2026 return and the ₩30T third-quarter dividend (₩4,601 a share, paid November 27); on October 29 Samsung said ₩55–75T is still to come, including a substantial buyback and cancellation program and a closer look at the preferred shares, which close 20.5% below the common. Yuanta's analysts argue the next three-year return policy, due in January 2027, could be larger still. One caveat from CFO Sooncheol Park on the Q2 call: customer prepayments under the long-term memory contracts and share buybacks for staff "may affect FCF," the free cash flow that the current policy returns half of. Siblis Research notes the other side of the reform story: with two chipmakers at around 60% of the market, a KOSPI valuation is mostly a verdict on the memory cycle.
