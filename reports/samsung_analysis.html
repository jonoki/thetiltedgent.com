01 Company Overview

Most people know Samsung from the phone in their pocket or the TV on their wall. The stock market knows it as something else: a memory-chip maker, with an estimated 39.4% of global DRAM revenue in the first half of 2026 (company estimate on DRAMeXchange data). In the second quarter of 2026 the chip division, Device Solutions, booked ₩127.5 trillion of sales and ₩89.2 trillion of operating profit — 99.7% of the whole company's ₩89.5 trillion. The phones, TVs and appliances that carry the brand lost money in the same quarter.

The numbers moved so fast that the scale is hard to hold in your head. Operating profit was ₩4.7 trillion in Q2 2025 and ₩89.5 trillion in Q2 2026, up 1,814%. Quarterly revenue more than doubled to ₩171.5 trillion. Earnings per share for the quarter, ₩10,849, beat the company's whole-year figure for 2025 (₩6,605). Operating profit also beat analysts' forecast of ₩88.13 trillion (LSEG SmartEstimate, via CNBC), while revenue came in a shade under the ₩172.65 trillion expected. The cause is the AI build-out: data-center servers need vast amounts of DRAM, flash storage and HBM, the industry is short of all three, and prices have climbed quarter after quarter. The share price followed: from ₩69,800 at the end of October 2021 to ₩276,000 on October 2, 2026, up 295% in won, with most of that gain made in the last twelve months. It peaked at ₩374,500 during the session of June 19, 2026, then fell 21.4% in July in a market rout before steadying. The question for this report is the oldest one in memory chips: how long does a shortage last once everyone starts building factories to end it?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

39.4% DRAM share (1H26 est.) Manufacturing scale ₩167.6T net cash Memory + foundry + packaging Multi-year supply contracts #1 TV brand 20 years OLED panel leadership

The moat in chips is the cost of a seat at the table: Samsung spent about ₩28.0T on capex in the first half of 2026 alone, and the half-year report notes that in foundry only about five companies with large capex budgets dominate the market. Samsung both makes memory and runs a foundry, and the Q2 release credits demand for HBM base dies with lifting foundry earnings. It also has a balance sheet that can keep building through a downturn. On the Q2 call, memory sales head Jaejune Kim said Samsung has "already finalized agreements with the top 5 global data center customers" and is in the final stages of talks with five more. The contracts run five years, with a further year that can be added at each annual negotiation, carry "substantial advanced payments," and for mainstream products set "minimum price floors"; he expects contracted DRAM and NAND volumes to reach 60% to 70% of planned capacity. Korea Investment & Securities' Chae Min-sook has argued that five-year contracts of this kind reduce the chance of a repeat of past oversupply (Seoul Economic Daily). The vulnerability is the same as ever: memory is a commodity sold at whatever price the shortage allows, and the shortage is what everyone, Samsung included, is spending to end.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The cycle has turned before. In the last downturn, annual operating profit fell from ₩43.4T in 2022 to ₩6.6T in 2023 (StockAnalysis annual data), and Q2 2025 profit was just ₩4.7T. Today's 52% operating margin is a price, not a permanent feature: BNK Investment & Securities' Lee Min-hee cut the rating to hold in September on the view that producers are expanding capacity aggressively while memory prices have little room left to rise.
02
Playing catch-up in HBM. CNBC described Samsung as seeking to catch up with domestic rival SK hynix in high-bandwidth memory, the highest-value AI product. Samsung is shipping HBM4 and sampling HBM4E, but in the most profitable corner of the market it has not been the leader. On the Q2 call Jaejune Kim said Samsung expects its second-half HBM share to be "broadly in line with our overall DRAM market share."
03
The rest of the company is squeezed by its own success. MX/Networks lost ₩0.7T in Q2 on component costs, and VD/DA broke roughly even. If memory prices fall, those units recover, but the group's profit falls far more, because DS is 99.7% of it.
04
Labour and politics take a share. CNBC reported that Samsung agreed this year to scrap its bonus cap and set aside 10.5% of operating profit for bonuses after a weeks-long union protest. At Q2's ₩89.5T run rate, that is a large and rising cost — and the Q2 release already refers to incentive-related provisions.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items matter for reading the figures. First, bonus provisions: CNBC reported that the Q2 figures deduct one-off expenses for employee bonus provisions, and the company's release says Foundry earnings improved "prior to incentive-related provisions," so underlying chip profit was higher than reported. On the call CFO Soon-Cheol Park said Q2 carried the whole first-half accrual for a special incentive of about 10.5% of first-half operating profit, none having been booked in Q1; part of it was capitalised into inventory and reaches cost of sales as that inventory is sold from Q3. Citi put the extra bonus cost in its Q3 forecast at about ₩5T (Seoul Economic Daily, September 3). Second, share buybacks for staff, not for cancellation: in the first half the board approved three repurchases worth ₩2.50T, ₩3.57T and ₩7.17T of common shares to fund employee stock compensation, per the half-year report — these hand shares to staff rather than shrinking the count. Separately, on April 2, 2026 Samsung cancelled 73,359,314 common and 13,603,461 preferred treasury shares. Third, net finance income of ₩4.7T in Q2 2026 (₩0.9T a year earlier) sits between operating and pre-tax profit, so pre-tax profit (₩94.4T) ran above operating profit.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of ₩69,800 to the October 2, 2026 close of ₩276,000, the shares rose 295% in won, before dividends. For a dollar investor the won's fall from ₩1,174 to ₩1,361 per dollar over the same window cut that to about +241% (our calculation from Yahoo's KRW=X closes). Almost none of it came in the first four years: the stock closed January 2025 at ₩52,400, below where it started, and has risen 5.3-fold since. The best month in the series was May 2026 (+43.8%), the worst March 2026 (−22.8%), and July 2026 (−21.4%) was the second worst — the sign of a stock that now moves like a leveraged bet on AI memory.

The 6-month RSI shows how stretched it got. It reached 94.9 in February 2026, the highest reading in the series, and stayed above 84 at the May and June month-ends. It now reads 65.0: still above neutral, no longer extreme. The 10-month moving average is ₩248,270, and the price sits 11% above it — the long uptrend is intact, but the last three month-ends (₩262,500, ₩260,000, ₩268,500) and the latest close of ₩276,000 are a range, not a climb.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 12.2 times trailing earnings and 5.8 times this year's consensus for a producer of an estimated 39% of the world's DRAM revenue in a market its own management calls undersupplied, with ₩167.6T of net cash, an 8.2% trailing free-cash-flow yield and up to ₩110T of shareholder returns planned for 2026. The company says that shortage lasts through the second half; memory sales head Jaejune Kim says the shortage gets worse in 2027; Yuanta sees bottlenecks into 2028; and multi-year contracts with the biggest data-center buyers, backed by advance payments and price floors, make this cycle less spot-priced than the last. Q3 consensus points to the first ₩100T operating-profit quarter. If the shortage lasts two more years rather than one, today's price looks cheap even on a sharply lower peak multiple.

Bear Case

Low multiples on peak earnings are how memory stocks always look at the top. Operating margin went from 6.3% to 52.2% in a year; it can go back. Every producer is adding capacity — Samsung itself spent ₩28T in six months and plans a second Texas fab, and Samsung and SK hynix have announced ₩800T for a new hub — while Chinese makers push into ordinary DRAM and NAND. Samsung trails SK hynix in HBM, the part of the market most insulated from that. The share price is also hostage to Korea's market plumbing: July showed how leveraged retail money can take 20% off the stock in days. The near term is softer too: Citi cut its Q3 estimate to 9% below consensus on currency and bonus costs. BNK's ₩270,000 target is below today's price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Memory prices peak. 99.7% of Q2 operating profit came from the chip division. TrendForce, cited by Financial News, expected DRAM contract prices to rise 13–18% in Q3, a smaller increase than in Q2; Kiwoom expects ordinary DRAM price gains of around 3% in Q4. Slowing increases are the first stage of every memory downturn.
02
AI spending slows. The late-July chip sell-off reflected concerns that AI infrastructure spending may be "peaking faster than expected," Forrester's Charlie Dai said (CNBC). Samsung's server-heavy mix makes it more exposed to the data-center buyers' budgets than in any past cycle.
03
Market structure and concentration. With Samsung and SK hynix around half the KOSPI's weight, index funds, leveraged ETFs and margin calls amplify every move. Korea has tightened the rules for leveraged ETFs, with new cash-deposit requirements taking effect on July 31 (CNBC), but the ownership base that drove the swings has not gone away.
Industry Commentary
The AI memory shortage
CURRENT TAILWIND

Factory capacity used for HBM is capacity not making ordinary DRAM. That is the core of the shortage case Yuanta's Baek Gil-hyun makes: "given that HBM is eating into conventional DRAM capacity and that the spread of artificial intelligence inference is driving demand for high-capacity memory, supply constraints are likely to ease later than expected" (Seoul Economic Daily translation). Samsung's own outlook says the same in plainer terms: undersupply despite softer phone and PC demand. On the Q2 call memory sales head Jaejune Kim pointed to the more-than-three-year lead time from new fab construction to wafer output and said Samsung considers it "unlikely to see any significant increase in incremental supply through 2028." Samsung and SK hynix together produce about two-thirds of the world's memory chips, according to the AP. Reuters calculated that the two will hold a combined $263B of net cash by year-end.

Capacity, China and the shape of the next downturn
BUILDING HEADWIND

The response to a shortage is construction. SK hynix announced ₩100T of new Korean investment in July (CNBC), Samsung is building in Taylor, Texas, and the two have a combined ₩800T plan for a new hub in Korea's southwest. China is the second source of supply: the AP reported investor concern over the strong market debut of ChangXin Memory Technologies and over reports of a state-owned Chinese company mass-producing domestic lithography machines, and Kiwoom expects CXMT and YMTC to compete hard for share in ordinary memory. The bull framing: long-term contracts with the five largest data-center buyers and HBM's appetite for capacity stretch this cycle beyond the usual two years. The bear framing: every previous memory boom ended with too many factories, and this one has more money behind its factories than any before it.

Korea's market reforms and the shareholder-return turn
STRUCTURAL CHANGE

Korean equities have long traded at a discount to other developed markets — the "Korea discount" — which Siblis Research says has narrowed sharply as earnings expectations improved. At Samsung, shareholder returns are rising with profits. The half-year report shows 86.96 million treasury shares cancelled in April 2026. Then came the ₩90–110T 2026 return and the ₩30T third-quarter dividend. Yuanta's analysts argue the next three-year return policy, due in January 2027, could be larger still. One caveat from CFO Soon-Cheol Park on the Q2 call: customer prepayments under the long-term memory contracts and share buybacks for staff "may affect FCF," the free cash flow that the current policy returns half of. Siblis Research notes the other side of the reform story: with two chipmakers at around 60% of the market, a KOSPI valuation is mostly a verdict on the memory cycle.
