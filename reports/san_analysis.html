01 Company Overview

Santander is a Spanish bank with a global footprint: 182.5 million customers across Spain, the UK, Portugal, the US, Mexico, Brazil, Chile, Argentina and a consumer-finance arm spanning Europe and the Americas, run as five global businesses — Retail & Commercial Banking, Openbank (consumer finance and digital banking), Corporate & Investment Banking, Wealth Management & Insurance, and Payments. Reuters calls it the euro zone's largest lender by market value, and it carries an 18.2% weight in Spain's IBEX 35 index. FY2025 attributable profit was €14.1B. The latest reported period is the first half of 2026 (H1'26), published July 22, 2026: revenue €30.8B (+6%), record underlying profit €7.33B (+15%), reported profit €8.97B (+31%) including a €1.9B gain on selling its Polish bank, and an underlying RoTE of 15.6%.

2026 is the year Santander reshaped its map. It sold Santander Bank Polska to Erste Group (completed January 9, ~€7B cash), bought TSB in the UK (completed April 30, 4+ million customers), bought Webster Financial in the US (announced February 3, completed August 20, valued at $12.2B and paid partly in new Santander shares), and on July 30 announced an all-share offer for the ~10% of Santander Brasil it does not own. Meanwhile it keeps buying back its own stock: the share count fell from 14.89 billion in June 2025 to 14.56 billion after the September 2026 cancellation, even after issuing 330 million new shares for Webster. Executive chair Ana Botín's line on the half: "We performed strongly in the first half, adding twelve million customers year-on-year. Revenue grew 6% and costs fell, with ONE Transformation driving further improvements in operating leverage and supporting record profits." The stock has done the talking too: the NYSE ADS went from a $3.79 month-end in October 2021 to $13.36 — +252.5% in five years. The question now is whether 2028's targets — RoTE above 20%, profit above €20B — can carry it further, or whether the re-rating is done.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

182.5M customers €1.13T customer deposits Shared global tech platform Europe–Americas diversification Spain retail scale Capital-light wealth & CIB

Santander's edge is scale in many markets, run on one machine. Most global banks either stay home or run each country as its own bank. Santander's ONE Transformation programme puts every market on common platforms — the Gravity back-end is fully live in Spain, Mexico and Chile, and one global app now runs in Spain, Mexico, Brazil and Chile — so it builds a product once and deploys it across its markets. That is how revenue grew 6% while costs fell. Diversification is the other half: a bad year in Argentina or Brazil is absorbed by Spain, the UK and the US, which is why the CEO says results are "less volatile than peers."

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Latin American credit and currencies. Brazil and Argentina carry the group's highest credit costs, and their earnings are translated into euros at volatile exchange rates — Reuters notes the footprint "exposes it to Latin American currency swings." The CFO flagged pressure from a slower-than-expected rate cut cycle in Brazil, particularly among corporates and SMEs, and CEO Grisi told the call that Santander had stopped lending in Argentina.
02
Integration load. TSB (UK) and Webster (US) are being absorbed at the same time, while the Santander Brasil buy-in is pending. Each promises synergies — at least £400M for TSB — but integrations are where banks lose customers and add costs.
03
Capital tighter after Webster. CET1 falls from 14.0% toward 12.8–13% at year-end once Webster's c.150bps is absorbed, leaving less room above the 12–13% operating range for extraordinary buybacks.
04
UK conduct and consumer finance. The UK car-finance commission issue cost €245M gross in H1'26, and Openbank's profit fell 20.6%. Consumer lending is the part of the group that turns first in a downturn.

Notable One-Off Items

Santander strips "non-recurring items" out of its underlying numbers, and in 2026 they are large. Q1'26: a €1,895M net capital gain on selling ~49% of Santander Bank Polska to Erste (completed January 9; about €7B cash, +95bps of CET1). Q2'26: €250M of restructuring costs for the TSB integration. H1'25 comparatives carry €456M of Poland's own results in the same line. That is why reported H1 profit grew 31% while underlying grew 15%. Inside underlying profit, €245M gross of UK motor-finance provisions (mostly Q1) weighed on Openbank. Capital: TSB cost 55bps of CET1 in Q2; Webster costs c.150bps in H2. RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end of $3.79 to the October 2, 2026 settled close of $13.36, the Santander ADS rose 252.5% on price alone. The first half of the window went nowhere: the ADS sank to $2.32 in September 2022, the five-year low, and was still near $4 at the end of 2023. The move came in 2025 — a 25.3% jump in February 2025, the largest monthly gain in the series, and a climb from $4.56 at the end of 2024 to $11.73 at the end of 2025. 2026 has been choppier: the Webster announcement month (February) and March gave back ground, then the ADS made its five-year month-end high of $14.56 in August as Webster closed, before slipping 5.0% in September and another 3.4% into October 2.

The 6-period monthly RSI peaked at 96.1 in September 2025, an extreme reading, and was above 80 from June to August 2026. It has cooled to 60.3 — no longer overbought on this measure. The 3-month moving average (~$13.92) has rolled over above the price, while the 10-month average (~$13.07) is still below it: the long trend is intact, the short one has turned down.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Santander is growing revenue 6% while cutting costs, has taken its efficiency ratio to 42.8%, and is printing record underlying profit with EPS up 20% as buybacks shrink the share count. TSB and Webster add scale in the UK and US at the same time, with at least £400M of TSB synergies and a 2028 plan for RoTE above 20% and profit above €20B. At 11.0x trailing and 11.4x forward earnings and 1.87x tangible book, the €171.9B market value is about 8.6 times the 2028 profit target of €20B (our arithmetic), the payout policy is shifting toward cash dividends from 2027, and 80% of the 25 covering firms rate it a Buy, while the Bloomberg average target sits 17% above the price.

Bear Case

The easy re-rating is done: the ADS has more than tripled in five years, and price to tangible book went from 1.28x to 1.91x between June 2025 and June 2026 (company figures). Q2 was in line, not a beat, guidance was reiterated rather than raised, and the stock fell 3.2% over the two sessions after the print. Capital drops toward 12.8–13% once Webster is absorbed, two integrations and a Brazil buy-in run at once, Openbank's profit fell 20.6%, Argentina and Brazil are lifting credit costs, and the reported earnings flatter the trend — the €1.9B Poland gain inflates trailing EPS by about €0.13 a share. The October 1 sector sell-off showed how much of the price rides on the Spanish banking trade as a whole.

Top 3 Risks

01
Execution on two integrations at once. TSB (UK) and Webster (US) are both in their first year; missing the synergy and RoTE milestones would undercut the 2028 plan, which the market is already paying for.
02
Latin American credit and currency. Brazil (€1,093M of H1 profit) faces slower rate cuts and corporate stress; Argentina has already raised the group's cost of risk. Weaker local currencies shrink those profits in euros.
03
Less capital headroom. CET1 heading from 14.0% to 12.8–13% leaves less excess to distribute above the 50% payout; a credit shock or regulatory change would compete directly with buybacks.
Industry Commentary
Rates stayed higher for longer — good for deposit-funded banks
TAILWIND

Santander's H1 report describes a rate backdrop that turned rather than eased: the ECB raised its deposit rate to 2.25% in June 2026 as energy prices rose with the conflict in the Middle East, the Bank of England held at 3.75%, and the Federal Reserve held at 3.50–3.75%. For a bank that earns most of its revenue from the spread between loans and deposits, that is supportive. CFO García Cantera described NII as "stronger and more resilient" than the Investor Day guidance assumed, helped by the "higher for longer rate environment in most markets", with Brazil the exception. Santander says more than 95% of group revenue is linked to customer activity.

European bank consolidation, with Santander as buyer and seller
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

In twelve months Santander sold its Polish bank at 2.2x tangible book, bought TSB and Webster, and bid for the rest of Santander Brasil — capital moved out of a market where it was a minority-listed player and into markets where it wants scale (the UK and the US) or full ownership (Brazil). Spain's banks trade as a group: on October 1 BBVA, CaixaBank, Sabadell and Santander all fell about 4% in the same session. Santander's diversification outside Spain did not protect it on that day.

Valuation after the re-rating
KEY DEBATE

At 1.87x tangible book on a 15.6% underlying RoTE, Santander is priced for its returns to keep climbing. The bull framing: management's own arithmetic — RoTE "close to 17%" at normalised capital, a path to above 20% by 2028, and EPS growth boosted by buybacks — would make today's multiple look modest. The bear framing: every lever in that plan (TSB, Webster, Brazil, ONE Transformation) has to work at once, and the stock already reflects much of it. The sell side is firmly in the bull camp — 20 Buys among the 25 firms in Santander's coverage table — but the Bloomberg average target of €13.85 implies 17% upside, less than one strong year of the 2025 run.
