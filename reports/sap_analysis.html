01 Company Overview

SAP sells the software that runs the back office of large companies — finance, procurement, supply chain, manufacturing, HR — and is now selling it as a subscription instead of a licence. Two numbers carry the story. Current cloud backlog (CCB) was €24.1B at September 30, 2026, up 28% (26% at constant currencies), and the Q3 2026 non-IFRS cloud gross margin was 74.3%, down 0.8 percentage points (pp) year on year (0.7pp at constant currencies; IFRS 73.9%, down 0.7pp). The order book keeps compounding in the mid-20s; the margin on delivering it is edging down, not up. In Q3 SAP also passed €10B of quarterly revenue for the first time (€10,270M, +13%) and moved its 2026 cloud revenue and cloud-and-software revenue outlooks to the upper end of their ranges. FY2025 revenue was €36.80B, up 7.68%, with IFRS diluted EPS of €6.10; cloud revenue was €21.02B, +23%.

SAP is the largest listed software company in Europe and, at the October 26, 2026 close, the #1 company in Germany by market value (our calculation: $273.96B against about $223.5B for Siemens, about $50B ahead), a constituent of the DAX. That makes it Europe's software champion. The idea that the ERP migration tailwind is a clean 2027 cliff needs a correction: under SAP's own maintenance policy, mainstream maintenance for SAP Business Suite 7, which includes ECC 6.0, ends December 31, 2027, but paid extended maintenance runs to the end of 2030, and RISE with SAP customers who move to SAP's private-cloud edition by the end of 2030 can buy a transition option to 2033, which SAP said on October 13, 2026 may now run to the end of 2035 under further conditions. The deadline is a forcing function, not a wall. The stock has already lived through the debate: it closed $304.10 in June 2025, $154.11 in June 2026 (−49%), and $238.55 on October 26, 2026 (+55% from the June 2026 close) — so the question is not whether cloud grows, but how long 25%-ish backlog growth lasts once the migration wave is through, and whether AI adds revenue or only defends it.

Currency, read carefully. SAP reports in euros; the NYSE ADR trades in dollars at 1 ADR = 1 ordinary share (20-F, FY2025). Every per-share number on this page is converted at the ECB reference rate of 1.1188 USD per EUR on October 26, 2026 (1.1490 at the previous edition's September 21 date, so the euro is weaker and each euro of profit is worth fewer dollars). FX exposure: 39% of FY2025 revenue was earned in the Americas (US €11.54B, 31% of group), 46% in EMEA (Germany €5.83B, 16%), and the remaining ~14% in Asia Pacific Japan. A stronger euro therefore cuts reported euro growth and raises the dollar value of each euro of profit at the same time — SAP's own guidance assumes 1.13 USD per EUR on average for 2026.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

System of record for finance & supply chain Multi-year cloud contracts (€24.1B CCB) Migration path owned by SAP Integrator ecosystem Proprietary business-process data A1 / A+ balance sheet

The moat is switching cost at the system-of-record layer. An ERP holds a company's general ledger, inventory, orders and payroll; replacing it is a multi-year project that touches every department, which is why most ECC customers weigh "which SAP" rather than "whether SAP". The migration itself strengthens the moat: a RISE contract is a multi-year subscription with the hosting bundled in, so SAP moves from selling a licence that customers maintain to running the system for them. The AI pitch builds on that position — management argues that agents are only as good as the business data and process context under them, and SAP holds both. The vulnerability is that the switching cost protects the installed base, not the price of the next contract, and that AI agents may make it cheaper to wrap a legacy core than to replace it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A deadline-driven wave has a back end. Part of the 25%+ backlog growth is customers moving ahead of the end-2027 ECC mainstream-maintenance date. Management already guides CCB growth to slightly decelerate in 2026. Once the bulk of the installed base has converted, cloud growth has to come from new customers, expansion and AI rather than conversions, and the market will price the business on that lower run-rate.
02
The deadline is softer than the headline. Paid extended maintenance to 2030, a RISE transition option to 2033 (which SAP said on October 13, 2026 may run to 2035), and independent third-party support providers all let a hesitant customer wait. A slower, longer migration is still revenue, but it spreads the tailwind thinner.
03
Cloud gross margin is moving the wrong way. 74.3% non-IFRS in Q3 2026, −0.8pp YoY, and 74.7% (−0.4pp) over the first nine months. If AI features ship bundled rather than priced separately, margin dilution is the cost of defending the base.
04
AI monetisation is not yet a reported number. SAP discloses Cloud ERP Suite and CCB, not AI revenue. The statements list AI partnerships (Anthropic, AWS, NVIDIA, Palantir, Accenture and others, May 12) and AI/data acquisitions (Reltio, Dremio and Prior Labs closed; TechWolf agreed on October 6, closing expected in Q4 2026).

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, the 2024 transformation programme: €3,144M of restructuring expense in FY2024, against €3M in FY2025. That explains why IFRS EPS fell to €2.65 in 2024 and rebounded 130% to €6.10 in 2025; the 2025 growth rate is not a run-rate. Second, IFRS against non-IFRS: in Q3 2026 basic IFRS EPS (€1.96, +14%) was again above non-IFRS EPS (€1.89, +19%), though non-IFRS grew faster. Third, buybacks and M&A: a €10B repurchase programme (€4.2B spent by September 30 on 25.2M shares at €164.79 average) is shrinking the share count, while the Reltio, Dremio and Prior Labs acquisitions add backlog and dilute 2026 operating profit by over €100M (together they lowered Q3 non-IFRS operating profit by about €39M: Reltio −€9M, Dremio −€14M, Prior Labs −€16M).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $135.04 to the October 26, 2026 settled close of $238.55, the SAP ADR gained 76.6% on price (dividends excluded). The path went down, up, then down again, and has now turned up. The latest close is 21.6% below that June 2025 peak and 55% above the June 2026 close. The ADR is priced in dollars on a euro business, so some of every move is currency. The 5-year window is an ADR-in-USD series. The home-market XETRA line in EUR will look different.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A €24.1B current cloud backlog growing 26% at constant currencies means most of next year's cloud revenue is already under contract. The Cloud ERP Suite is +28% cc and 89% of cloud. Management guides total cc growth to accelerate in 2027, with opex growing at only 80–90% of revenue growth, which is operating leverage by design. FCF is heading to about €10B, the balance sheet is rated A1/A+, and a €10B buyback runs to end-2027. At 30.9x trailing and 29.8x forward USD EPS, the stock has recovered 55% from its June 2026 close; the case now rests on the ERP migration deadline still lying ahead and on a guide moved towards the upper end of its ranges. If AI agents need clean, process-level business data, SAP owns the data they need.

Bear Case

The backlog is strong because a deadline is pulling it forward, and management still guides deceleration. Cloud gross margin is falling (74.3%, −0.8pp) while acquisitions dilute profit by over €100M. Support revenue (25% of nine-month sales, the highest-margin stream) is shrinking 8% a year, and SAP says that decline will accelerate. AI revenue is not disclosed, so "AI monetisation" is still a claim, not a number. And the deadline is softer than advertised — 2030 extended maintenance and a transition option to 2033, now extendable to 2035, spread the wave out. On that view 30x forward earnings is not cheap for growth that peaks with the migration.

Top 3 Risks

01
Post-migration growth cliff. CCB growth is guided to slow slightly in 2026; Q3 came in at +26% cc, above 2025's 25%. If Q4 shows a sharper slowdown, the market will read it as the migration wave peaking before 2027 rather than after.
02
Margin cost of AI. Non-IFRS cloud gross margin fell 0.8pp YoY in Q3, and the July operating-profit guidance cut absorbed acquisition dilution. If AI is priced into the suite rather than sold on top, it defends revenue while diluting margin.
03
Currency. ~31% of revenue comes from the US and the guidance assumes 1.13 USD/EUR on average. SAP's own table shows a −1.5pp FX drag on FY2026 cloud growth and −2.0pp on operating-profit growth at September 30 rates. For a USD holder of the ADR a stronger euro partly offsets this, but reported euro growth — the number the market trades on — takes the hit.
Industry Commentary
The ERP migration wave and the 2027 deadline
TAILWIND, TIME-LIMITED

The on-premise ECC installed base has to go somewhere before end-2027, when mainstream maintenance for SAP Business Suite 7 ends (SAP's maintenance policy). SAP's answer is RISE with SAP: a bundled subscription that moves the customer to S/4HANA Cloud, with SAP running the infrastructure. Its results show the mechanism clearly. In Q3 the Cloud ERP Suite grew 28% cc while software support fell 6% cc, and SAP says the support decline will accelerate as more customers transform to the cloud. The question for the multiple is duration: extended maintenance to 2030 and a transition option to 2033 (2035 under SAP's October 13 update) mean the wave can be long and shallow rather than short and steep. A long, shallow wave is better for sustaining the backlog. It is worse for anyone betting on a 2027 peak-and-fade.

AI: defend the system of record, then charge for it
UNPROVEN IN THE NUMBERS

SAP's 2026 strategy is branded the "Autonomous Enterprise": Joule as the AI interface, Joule Agents that execute workflows, and Business Data Cloud as the data layer — now reinforced by Reltio (master data), Dremio (lakehouse) and Prior Labs (tabular foundation models), plus partnerships with Anthropic, AWS, NVIDIA, Palantir and Accenture announced May 12, 2026. The bull reading is that agents are only useful if they sit on accurate business data, which SAP owns, so AI deepens lock-in. The bear reading is that AI agents make it easier to automate around an ERP core rather than buy more modules from its vendor. Neither is settled by the numbers: SAP reports no standalone AI revenue, and the measurable costs — cloud margin down 0.8pp in Q3, more than €100M of acquisition dilution — arrive before the revenue.

The 2026 software de-rating
SENTIMENT HEADWIND

SAP's ADR fell 49% from June 2025 to June 2026 while its reported CCB growth stayed in the mid-20s — the price moved far more than the fundamentals. This report does not assign the fall to a single cause. The rebound since (to $238.55, 55% above the June 2026 close) came with two backlog prints, Q2 and Q3, both at +26% cc. At the October 26, 2026 close SAP ($273.96B) was the largest company in Germany by market value, about $50B ahead of Siemens (our calculation). For this stock the backlog number moves the price, and the Q4 results are the next one.
