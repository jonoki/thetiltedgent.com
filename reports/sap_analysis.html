01 Company Overview

SAP sells the software that runs the back office of large companies — finance, procurement, supply chain, manufacturing, HR — and is now selling it as a subscription instead of a licence. Two numbers carry the story. Current cloud backlog (CCB) was €22.9B at June 30, 2026, up 27% (26% at constant currencies), and the Q2 2026 non-IFRS cloud gross margin was 74.6%, down 0.6pp year on year (IFRS 74.3%, −0.5pp). The order book is compounding in the mid-20s; the margin on delivering it is edging down, not up. FY2025 revenue was €36.80B, up 7.68%, with IFRS EPS of €6.10; cloud revenue was €21.02B, +23%.

SAP is the largest listed software company in Europe and the #2 company in Germany by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap, behind Siemens by about $3.3B), a constituent of the DAX, and #74 in the world at $241.5B. The premise that it is "Europe's software champion" checks out. The premise that the ERP migration tailwind is a clean 2027 cliff needs a correction: on third-party summaries of SAP's maintenance policy, mainstream maintenance for ECC 6.0 (EHP 6–8) ends December 31, 2027, but paid extended maintenance runs to 2030, and customers who sign RISE with SAP get a private-cloud transition path reported to run to 2033. The deadline is a forcing function, not a wall. The stock has already lived through the debate: it closed $304.10 in June 2025, $154.11 in June 2026 (−49%), and $209.22 today — so the question is not whether cloud grows, but how long 25%-ish backlog growth lasts once the migration wave is through, and whether AI adds revenue or only defends it.

Currency, read carefully. SAP reports in euros; the NYSE ADR trades in dollars at 1 ADR = 1 ordinary share (20-F, FY2025). Every per-share number on this page is converted at the ECB reference rate of 1.1490 USD per EUR on September 21, 2026. FX exposure: 39% of FY2025 revenue was earned in the Americas (US €11.54B, 31% of group), 46% in EMEA (Germany €5.83B, 16%), and the remaining ~14% in Asia Pacific Japan. A stronger euro therefore cuts reported euro growth and raises the dollar value of each euro of profit at the same time — SAP's own guidance assumes 1.13 USD per EUR.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

System of record for finance & supply chain Multi-year cloud contracts (€22.9B CCB) Migration path owned by SAP Integrator ecosystem Proprietary business-process data A1 / A+ balance sheet

The moat is switching cost at the system-of-record layer. An ERP holds a company's general ledger, inventory, orders and payroll; replacing it is a multi-year project that touches every department, which is why most ECC customers weigh "which SAP" rather than "whether SAP". The migration itself strengthens the moat: a RISE contract is a multi-year subscription with the hosting bundled in, so SAP moves from selling a licence that customers maintain to running the system for them. The AI pitch builds on that position — management argues that agents are only as good as the business data and process context under them, and SAP holds both. The vulnerability is that the switching cost protects the installed base, not the price of the next contract, and that AI agents may make it cheaper to wrap a legacy core than to replace it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
A deadline-driven wave has a back end. Part of the 25%+ backlog growth is customers moving ahead of the end-2027 ECC mainstream-maintenance date. Management already guides CCB growth to slightly decelerate in 2026. Once the bulk of the installed base has converted, cloud growth has to come from new customers, expansion and AI rather than conversions, and the market will price the business on that lower run-rate.
02
The deadline is softer than the headline. Paid extended maintenance to 2030, a RISE transition option reported to run to 2033, and independent third-party support providers all let a hesitant customer wait. A slower, longer migration is still revenue, but it spreads the tailwind thinner.
03
Cloud gross margin is moving the wrong way. 74.6% non-IFRS in Q2 2026, −0.6pp YoY, while SAP accelerates R&D and pays for AI compute. If AI features ship bundled rather than priced separately, margin dilution is the cost of defending the base.
04
AI monetisation is not yet a reported number. SAP discloses Cloud ERP Suite and CCB, not AI revenue. The Q2 statement lists AI partnerships (Anthropic, AWS, NVIDIA, Palantir, Accenture and others, May 12) and two AI/data acquisitions (Dremio, Prior Labs), but the sources fetched contain no euro figure for AI revenue or attach rates.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, the 2024 transformation programme: €3,144M of restructuring expense in FY2024, against €3M in FY2025. That explains why IFRS EPS fell to €2.65 in 2024 and rebounded 130% to €6.10 in 2025; the 2025 growth rate is not a run-rate. Second, tax: in Q2 2026, IFRS profit after tax (€2,209M) exceeded non-IFRS (€1,828M) because tax-exempt income lowered the IFRS tax rate to 26.5%, so the +30% IFRS EPS growth in the quarter overstates the underlying +6% non-IFRS EPS growth. Third, buybacks and M&A: a €10B repurchase programme (€2.6B spent by June 30 on 16.28M shares at €161.16 average) is shrinking the share count, while the Reltio, Dremio and Prior Labs acquisitions add backlog (Reltio under 1pp of CCB growth) and dilute 2026 operating profit by over €100M.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A €22.9B current cloud backlog growing 26% at constant currencies means most of next year's cloud revenue is already under contract. The Cloud ERP Suite is +27% cc and 88% of cloud. Management guides total cc growth to accelerate in 2027, with opex growing at only 80–90% of revenue growth, which is operating leverage by design. FCF is heading to about €10B, the balance sheet is net cash and rated A1/A+, and a €10B buyback runs to end-2027. At 27.2x trailing and 25.5x forward USD EPS, after a 49% drawdown, you buy a business whose ERP migration deadline still lies ahead. If AI agents need clean, process-level business data, SAP owns the data they need.

Bear Case

The backlog is strong because a deadline is pulling it forward, and management already guides deceleration. Cloud gross margin is falling (74.6%, −0.6pp) while R&D and AI compute ramp and acquisitions dilute profit by over €100M. Support revenue (26% of TTM sales, the highest-margin stream) is shrinking 8% a year, and SAP says that decline will accelerate. AI revenue is not disclosed, so "AI monetisation" is still a claim, not a number. And the deadline is softer than advertised — 2030 extended maintenance and a 2033 transition option spread the wave out. On that view 25x forward earnings is not cheap for growth that peaks with the migration.

Top 3 Risks

01
Post-migration growth cliff. CCB growth is guided to slightly decelerate in 2026. If Q3 or Q4 show a sharper slowdown, the market will read it as the migration wave peaking before 2027 rather than after.
02
Margin cost of AI. Cloud gross margin −0.6pp YoY in Q2, operating-profit guidance cut for acquisition dilution, R&D spending accelerating. If AI is priced into the suite rather than sold on top, it defends revenue while diluting margin.
03
Currency. ~31% of revenue comes from the US and the guidance assumes 1.13 USD/EUR. SAP's own table shows a −1.5pp FX drag on FY2026 cloud growth and −2.0pp on operating-profit growth at June 30 rates. For a USD holder of the ADR a strong euro partly offsets this, but reported euro growth — the number the market trades on — takes the hit.
Industry Commentary
The ERP migration wave and the 2027 deadline
TAILWIND, TIME-LIMITED

The on-premise ECC installed base has to go somewhere before end-2027, when mainstream maintenance for EHP 6–8 ends (third-party summaries of SAP's policy). SAP's answer is RISE with SAP: a bundled subscription that moves the customer to S/4HANA Cloud, with SAP running the infrastructure. Its results show the mechanism clearly. Cloud ERP Suite +27% cc, software support −7% cc, and a statement that the support decline will accelerate "as a consequence of an acceleration of customers transforming to the cloud." The question for the multiple is duration: extended maintenance to 2030 and a reported 2033 transition option mean the wave can be long and shallow rather than short and steep. A long, shallow wave is better for sustaining the backlog. It is worse for anyone betting on a 2027 peak-and-fade.

AI: defend the system of record, then charge for it
UNPROVEN IN THE NUMBERS

SAP's 2026 strategy is branded the "Autonomous Enterprise": Joule as the AI interface, Joule Agents that execute workflows, and Business Data Cloud as the data layer — now reinforced by Reltio (master data), Dremio (lakehouse) and Prior Labs (tabular foundation models), plus partnerships with Anthropic, AWS, NVIDIA, Palantir and Accenture announced May 12, 2026. The bull reading is that agents are only useful if they sit on accurate business data, which SAP owns, so AI deepens lock-in. The bear reading is that AI agents make it easier to automate around an ERP core rather than buy more modules from its vendor. Neither is settled by the numbers: SAP reports no standalone AI revenue in the sources fetched, and the measurable costs — cloud margin down, R&D up, >€100M of acquisition dilution — arrive before the revenue.

The 2026 software de-rating
SENTIMENT HEADWIND

SAP's ADR fell 49% from June 2025 to June 2026 while its reported CCB growth stayed in the mid-20s — the price moved far more than the fundamentals. The fetched sources do not establish a single cause, and this report does not assign one. The market-cap table shows the damage across the group: on September 21, 2026 SAP ($241.5B) sits below CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks and not far above Salesforce ($194.6B), and trails Siemens for the #1 spot in Germany by about $3.3B. The July–August rebound (+43% in two months) came straight after a backlog print. For this stock the backlog number moves the price, and the October 21 print is the next one.
