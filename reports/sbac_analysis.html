01 Company Overview

SBA Communications is a landlord whose tenants hang radios in the air. It owns 45,252 wireless sites at September 30, 2026 — 17,340 in the US and its territories and 27,912 internationally — and rents vertical space on them to mobile carriers under long, escalating leases. It was founded in 1989 as Steven Bernstein and Associates, went public in June 1999, and is structured as a REIT. Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $2.85B (+2.3%), and the number that matters for a tower REIT is not GAAP EPS but AFFO per share: $3.19 in Q3 2026, down 3.3% from $3.30 a year earlier, against a full-year 2026 guide raised on November 2 to $12.15–$12.37.

2026 has had two stories. The first was a round trip. On April 2, 2026, Bloomberg reported that SBA was exploring a potential sale after preliminary takeover interest from large infrastructure funds; the stock closed that day at $204.04, up 18.93%. Barron's then reported (as relayed by Yahoo Finance) interest from KKR and Brookfield centred on about $250 per share. On June 15 Green Street reported the take-private talks had stalled and were effectively dead, and by the end of September the stock had fallen to $160.12, the lowest month-end close on the five-year chart. The second story is SpaceX. In October the Wall Street Journal reported SpaceX would buy a nationwide block of 800 MHz spectrum from Grain Management for about $8 billion, and Bloomberg reported SpaceX negotiating leases on tens of thousands of US towers; on November 4 SpaceX picked Qualcomm to supply base stations for a Starlink Mobile ground network starting in 2027. A fourth national carrier would add a tenant to towers that lost Sprint and EchoStar, but SBA has announced no SpaceX lease. The stock is at $195.48, 22% above the September close. Underneath, the operating question is unchanged: domestic leasing revenue is shrinking (−3.4% YoY in Q3) as the Sprint and EchoStar churn washes through, and international revenue is growing (+13.3%). Whether a new tenant shows up decides whether 15.9x AFFO is cheap.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

45,252 permitted sites Long leases with escalators Zoning & permitting barriers High margins (69% Adj. EBITDA) Central America scale (Millicom sites) Investment-grade balance sheet (BBB)

A tower's moat is where it stands. A carrier that needs coverage in a specific spot either rents space on the structure already there or tries to permit a new one — and SBA's model is to keep adding tenants to towers whose cost is already sunk. That is why the Adjusted EBITDA margin sits near 69%. The vulnerability is the other side of the same coin: the US customer base is three carriers, and when two of the historic tenants disappear — Sprint into T-Mobile, EchoStar out of the network business — there has been nobody to replace the rent at the same site. That is why SpaceX's plans matter so much to the stock: a fourth national network would need sites. The moat protects the tower; it does not protect the tenant count.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customer concentration and consolidation churn. The 2026 outlook carries $55–56M of Sprint consolidation churn and $56M of EchoStar churn, all domestic. Every carrier merger removes a tenant from overlapping sites. The domestic line fell 3.4% YoY in Q3 despite escalators and new leasing.
02
Leverage in a rate-sensitive structure. Net debt is 6.4x annualised Adjusted EBITDA, and FY2026 net cash interest is guided at $487–$493M against $1.918–$1.928B of Adjusted EBITDA — roughly a quarter of EBITDA (computed at midpoints). The July notes priced at 4.875%–5.450% and the November tower-securities refinancing is assumed at 5.25%; replacing older, cheaper debt at those rates is a slow drag on AFFO.
03
Emerging-market exposure. International is 29.6% of Q3 revenue, but it brings currency swings (Q3 international leasing revenue grew 13.3% as reported, 9.8% excluding currency moves, and the effect can run the other way) and churn from carrier bankruptcies and restructurings, which management expects to stay elevated through 2027.
04
A reported tenant is not a signed one. The stock is up 22% from its September close, with its biggest gains on the days of the SpaceX spectrum and tower-talk reports, but SBA has announced no lease and SpaceX has named no tower partner. The April bid showed what happens when reported interest does not become a deal: the stock gave back almost the whole move within two months.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, Q4 2025 net income of $370.4M ($3.47 per share) was roughly double the prior-year quarter's $178.8M ($1.61); the company's Q4 2025 release says net income included a $226.3M gain on the sale of substantially all of its operations in Canada (and the intercompany-loan FX loss shrank to $10.3M from $77.8M). That one-off still sits inside the trailing EPS and explains why consensus expects FY2026 EPS to fall 20% to $7.80 while AFFO per share guidance is roughly flat-to-down. Treat the 21.1x trailing P/E with care. Second, EchoStar: SBA's 2026 outlook removes all contracted EchoStar revenue while the claims are fought in Dish Wireless's bankruptcy, so any recovery would be upside not in guidance. Third, buybacks: SBA restarted them in Q3, buying 1.7M shares for $300M at an average $179.68, and has $0.8B of authorisation left; the guidance assumes no more this year, so any further buying is a lever not in the per-share guide.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 4, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $343.80 to the November 4, 2026 settled close of $195.48, SBAC lost 43.1% of its price (dividends excluded); from the December 2021 peak of $389.02 the fall is 49.8%. Over the same span annual revenue rose from $2.31B (FY2021) to $2.82B (FY2025). This was not a shrinking-business story; it was a valuation story — the market has been paying less for each dollar of tower revenue, and no rally since — late 2023, autumn 2024, spring 2025, the April 2026 bid or the October 2026 SpaceX run — has regained the December 2023 month-end of $253.69.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 53.4 at the November 4 close — neutral, up from 34.6 at the September month-end. Its lowest readings came in September 2023 (24.3) and May 2023 (24.6); the April 2026 deal spike took it to 60.3, not into overbought territory. The 10-month moving average, at about $188.75, sits below the price, and the price is above both its 50-day ($179.61) and 200-day ($190.27) averages: the October rally lifted the stock back above its longer trend, though it remains below the April 2026 month-end of $221.20.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 15.9x this year's guided AFFO per share for 45,252 sites, a 69% Adjusted EBITDA margin, an investment-grade rating and a dividend that is only ~41% of AFFO, raised 13% this year. SpaceX has bought low-band spectrum and picked a base-station supplier for a US ground network starting in 2027 across tens of thousands of sites; any operator building that network needs towers, and SBA owns 17,340 of them in the US. New spectrum is coming too, starting with the upper C-band auction in April 2027. The Sprint and EchoStar churn is a known, finite, disclosed number in the 2026 bridge, with Sprint churn falling below $20M in 2027, and management is buying back stock ($300M at $179.68 in Q3, $0.8B still authorised).

Bear Case

AFFO per share is falling (−3.3% in Q3, and the FY2026 guide midpoint of $12.26 is below the trailing $12.46), domestic leasing is shrinking, and three US carriers hold the pricing power. SpaceX has signed nothing with SBA and has named no tower partner, and the stock has already risen 22% from its September close, its biggest gains coming on the SpaceX reports. International churn stays elevated through 2027 and currency swings cut both ways. Leverage of 6.4x means interest cost — about a quarter of EBITDA — reprices higher as cheap debt rolls. And the private-equity bid is gone: the people with the most diligence on the assets decided not to pay ~$250, which makes that number a ceiling reference, not a floor.

Top 3 Risks

01
Domestic churn outlasts the plan. The 2026 outlook assumes $111–112M of Sprint and EchoStar churn. Any further carrier consolidation, or a slow carrier staying slow (management describes one of the three as "really slow"), would extend the domestic decline beyond 2026.
02
Rates and refinancing. At 6.4x net debt / EBITDA with FY2026 net cash interest guided at $487–$493M, every refinancing at today's 4.9%–5.5% coupons compresses AFFO per share. Investors also value the stock partly as a steady income stream, so higher rates can hit the share price and the cash flow at once.
03
Emerging-market execution. International is 29.6% of Q3 revenue and the growth engine, but it carries currency translation, carrier bankruptcies and consolidation churn. Management expects international churn "to remain elevated in 2027" and to normalise in 2028 — a forecast, not a result.
Industry Commentary
Carrier consolidation: fewer tenants per tower
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A tower's economics are a function of how many carriers hang equipment on it. The US market's move to three national carriers, and EchoStar's exit, removes tenants that cannot be replaced one-for-one at the same site. SBA's November 2026 bridge quantifies it: escalators add $72–74M group-wide and new leases $53–56M, while Sprint churn (−$55–56M), EchoStar churn (−$56M) and regular churn (−$59M to −$63M) take back more than all of it. Non-organic revenue — acquisitions and new builds, $81M, $77M of it international — and a $34M currency gain are what make the total grow.

Private buyers versus the stock market's price
ONGOING DEBATE

The April–June 2026 episode was a live test of whether private infrastructure capital values towers above the public market. The answer was "yes, but not enough": interest around ~$250 a share versus a $172.11 March close, but no deal once the buyers had to find the equity. The stock fell to $160.12 by the end of September and stood at $195.48 on November 4, after the SpaceX reports. The same arithmetic applies to any future bidder, and to the company itself, which chose buybacks in Q3 because, in Cavanagh's words, "we believe our stock is a great value".

Satellites, edge compute and the next tenant
EMERGING TAILWIND

Management repeated its view on the Q3 call: "Satellites are a complement to terrestrial wireless networks." Cavanagh pointed to "a satellite provider's recent purchase of low-band 800 megahertz spectrum" as support — the spectrum SpaceX agreed to buy from Grain Management — and SpaceX has since picked Qualcomm to supply base stations for tens of thousands of ground sites from 2027. The second new tenant is edge compute: SBA has "several edge sites up and running with a handful of different companies building out distributed AI inference capacity", with modest revenue so far. The bull framing: two new kinds of tenant arrive just as consolidation churn ends. The bear framing: neither is a signed, material contract, and none of it is in the numbers. The way to judge it is simple — watch for a signed agreement and a line in the guidance bridge.
