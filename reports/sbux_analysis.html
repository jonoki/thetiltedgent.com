01 Company Overview

Starbucks is the world's largest coffeehouse operator, running 41,607 stores globally at the end of fiscal 2026 (September 27, 2026) — 32% company-operated and 68% licensed — across three reporting segments: North America, International, and Channel Development (packaged coffee, ready-to-drink beverages and single-serve pods sold through grocery and foodservice channels rather than through Starbucks stores). Fiscal 2026 revenue was $37.91B, up 2.0% YoY, held back by the conversion of the China business into a licensed joint venture in April 2026. Among the 53 US-listed restaurant companies StockAnalysis tracks, it is the second-largest by market value, behind McDonald's ($188.0B at the November 2, 2026 close).

This is a turnaround that has started to show up in the numbers. Fiscal 2025 was the worst operating year in the company's modern history: comparable store sales fell 1% globally, GAAP operating margin fell 710 basis points (bps; 100 bps is one percentage point) to 7.9%, and GAAP EPS fell 51% to $1.63. Fiscal 2026 reversed most of that: global comparable sales rose 6.2%, GAAP operating margin recovered to 9.7%, GAAP EPS rose 36% to $2.21 and non-GAAP EPS (which strips out restructuring and other one-off items) rose 28% to $2.72, above the company's own $2.55–$2.65 guidance. Management now guides to $3.15–$3.25 of non-GAAP EPS for fiscal 2027. Everything below is best read against that path: what CEO Brian Niccol's "Back to Starbucks" plan has fixed, what it cost, and what the market is now paying for it.

The engine, and the segment where the turnaround is actually being decided. 16,781 U.S. stores (40% of the global portfolio) plus Canada. Fiscal 2026 segment revenue was $28.90B, +5.6%, at a 12.0% segment operating margin; in Q4 it was $7.33B, +6.2% YoY, at 12.3% (+780 bps), with North America comparable sales up 7.9%. The diagnosis two years ago was operational, not brand-level: mobile order-and-pay had congested store floors, service times had stretched, the menu had bloated, and management had been renting sales with discounts. The fix — branded "Green Apron Service" — deliberately adds labor hours, re-sequences order handling, simplifies the menu, and strips out promotional discounting. On the Q3 FY2026 call management called Green Apron Service the main driver of the sales recovery. Two supporting programs: the Coffee House Uplift store-remodel effort, which passed 1,000 locations by the Q3 call, and digital menu boards, which management targeted for 80–90% of stores by September 2026 to sell more in the weak afternoon hours. In Q4 the growth came more from spending per visit than from visits: average ticket rose 5.0% (the release cites product mix, food add-ons, pricing and more delivery sales) and transactions 2.7%, down from 4.5% in Q3. On September 22, 2026 the board approved closing about 250 North America coffeehouses that were not meeting the company's standards or financial targets, for about $300M of charges (Starbucks 8-K, September 24, 2026); $270.2M of restructuring and impairment charges landed in the segment in Q4. Management guides North America comparable sales to grow about 4% in fiscal 2027.

The single largest structural change in the story. On April 2, 2026 Starbucks closed a joint venture with Chinese private-equity firm Boyu Capital: Boyu took 60% of the China retail operations, Starbucks retained 40% plus ownership of the brand and the intellectual-property licence to the JV. Roughly 8,000 company-operated China coffeehouses converted to a licensed model, with a stated shared aspiration to grow toward as many as 20,000 locations. Starbucks booked a $536.3M pre-tax gain on the divestiture in Q3 FY2026. The November 3, 2025 announcement (filed as an 8-K exhibit) priced Boyu's entry at a cash-free, debt-free enterprise value of approximately $4 billion, and Starbucks said it expects the total value of its China retail business to exceed $13 billion. The mechanical consequence: International segment revenue fell 37% YoY in Q4 FY2026, to $1.31B, mainly because the China stores' sales no longer count as Starbucks revenue, while segment operating margin rose to 19.7% (+890 bps), because collecting licence fees and royalties earns a far higher margin than running 8,000 stores. For fiscal 2026 the segment's margin was 17.7%. International comparable sales grew 0.9% in Q4 (transactions −2.8%, ticket +3.7%). Packaged coffee, single-serve pods, and ready-to-drink (RTD) bottled and canned beverages sold outside Starbucks stores — largely through the Global Coffee Alliance with Nestlé and an RTD partnership with PepsiCo. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Global Brand Equity 41,607-Store Density Rewards Program Scale Mobile Order & Pay Green Coffee Sourcing Nestlé / PepsiCo Distribution Real-Estate Site Selection Pricing Power

Starbucks' moat is a habit moat rather than a technology moat. Its durable advantages are location density (16,781 U.S. stores means it is physically closest to the most morning commuters), a loyalty program that pre-loads customer cash and encourages repeat visits, and enough brand strength to raise prices without losing the customer — the comps of the years before the turnaround were carried mostly by ticket increases, which is only possible with real pricing power. The Channel Development segment extends that brand into grocery aisles at a 46.8% operating margin (fiscal 2026) without owning the distribution. Crucially, the moat was never the thing that broke. FY2025's collapse came from operations — congested stores, long service times, a bloated menu, and discount-dependent traffic — not from customers deciding the brand no longer had a claim on them. Fiscal 2026, in which North America transactions grew 3.6% while discounting was being removed, is the strongest evidence so far that the problem was execution rather than a fading brand. Q4 tempered it: transactions still grew, but by 2.7%, and ticket did more of the work. The bear side of that argument is in the first vulnerability below, and it is not dismissible.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Generational trade-down — structural or fixable? The genuine bear thesis is that younger consumers have permanently reset what they will pay for a coffee, and that Starbucks' $6–8 handcrafted beverage is on the wrong side of that reset. The evidence for it: Dutch Bros growing revenue 29.6% with a much cheaper, drive-thru-first format, and Luckin at 36,000+ stores and +36.4% revenue growth at a price point a fraction of Starbucks China's. The evidence against it: North America transactions grew 4.5% in Q3 FY2026 and 3.6% over the full year while discounting was being pulled, which is not what a brand losing a generation looks like. But Q4 transactions slowed to +2.7% with ticket at +5.0%, and the fiscal 2027 guide of about 4% North America comps implies slower growth again. A year of positive comps is not proof — the test is whether visits keep growing once the easy comparisons are gone.
02
Labor cost, and an unresolved union. Starbucks Workers United says it represents about 12,000 baristas, at roughly 550 to 600 U.S. stores (about 6% of Starbucks' company-operated U.S. stores), and has been without a first contract for over four years. An open-ended unfair-labor-practice strike that began November 13, 2025 peaked in December at more than 4,500 baristas at 230 stores and had wound down by April 2026, when bargaining was set to resume. The union presented a contract proposal in February 2026 and called a consumer boycott on August 25, 2026, still without a contract. Separately and independently, the turnaround itself is a deliberate labor-cost increase: Green Apron Service adds hours to store schedules. Both vectors push the same direction on store-level margin.
03
Balance sheet: negative equity and an over-covered dividend. Debt is $13.28B ($22.41B including operating-lease obligations) against $3.68B cash at September 27, 2026, and total shareholders' equity is negative $7.67B (book value per share −$6.73) after a decade of aggressive buybacks. That makes ROE and D/E mathematically meaningless. The current ratio is 0.76 and the four dividends of the past year ($2.48) came to 112% of fiscal 2026 GAAP EPS of $2.21 — the dividend is still being paid out of cash flow rather than earnings. Fiscal 2026 free cash flow of $3.72B covered the $2.82B of dividends paid, and the quarterly dividend rises to $0.63 in November; with buybacks set to resume in fiscal 2027, the cushion is thinner than the 2.38% yield suggests.
04
China is now an equity stake, not a controlled business. Retaining 40% converts China from an operating problem into a line-item Starbucks no longer controls. That makes the income statement less risky and lifted International margin to 19.7% in Q4, but it also caps the upside: if the JV succeeds in reaching 20,000 stores, Starbucks captures 40% of the economics plus a royalty rather than 100%. China comps are also no longer separately reported, which permanently reduces disclosure on what was one of the most-watched numbers in the story.
04 Key Financial Metrics

Start with comparable store sales, because that is the metric the market actually trades. Comps break into two components that most write-ups collapse into one number: transactions (how many customers came in) and average ticket (how much each spent). A comp held up by ticket while transactions fall means you are raising price on a shrinking customer base — that is materially worse than the headline. A comp led by transactions means people are coming back. That distinction is the entire argument.

Now the cost of the fix — and why the trailing P/E is not what it looks like. Adding labor hours and removing discounts works, but it is expensive. Starbucks' own GAAP operating margin went 15.0% (FY24) → 7.9% (FY25) → 9.7% (FY26) (company releases: FY25 was 7.9%, down 710 bps). StockAnalysis's operating-income series, used for the normalization below, went 15.36% (FY23) → 14.18% (FY24) → 9.90% (FY25) → 10.92% (FY26). GAAP EPS went $3.58 (FY23) → $3.31 (FY24) → $1.63 (FY25) → $2.21 (FY26); non-GAAP EPS went $2.13 (FY25) → $2.72 (FY26). Because fiscal 2026 ended on September 27, its $2.21 is also the TTM EPS, so the trailing P/E of 47.2x still sits on earnings well below their pre-2025 level. Margin is recovering: Q4 FY2026 GAAP operating margin was 10.8% (+790 bps) and non-GAAP was 14.0% (+460 bps), with non-GAAP EPS +54% YoY to $0.80, and management guides fiscal 2027 non-GAAP operating margin to 13.5–14.0%.

Illustrative normalization — the number that matters. The following is arithmetic on stated inputs, not a forecast. On roughly $38B of revenue: at FY2024's 14.2% operating margin, operating income is ~$5.4B; at FY2023's 15.4%, ~$5.85B (both StockAnalysis's figures; Starbucks' own FY2024 margin was 15.0%). Assuming net income of 68–75% of operating income (Starbucks kept 73–75% in FY2023–FY2024 on those figures; the 68% low end allows for interest on today's $13.28B of debt) gives net income of roughly $3.7B–$4.4B, or $3.20–$3.85 of normalized EPS on 1.14B shares. At $104.40 that is 27x–33x fully normalized earnings — against McDonald's at 21.6x and the Restaurants industry aggregate at 26.3x trailing earnings (StockAnalysis, November 2, 2026 close). Management's fiscal 2027 guidance of $3.15–$3.25 non-GAAP EPS (a 53-week year, with slightly more than $0.10 from the extra week) already lands at the bottom of that range, and puts the stock at ~32.6x next year's earnings at the midpoint. The honest conclusion is not "SBUX is expensive." It is that the market has already paid for a largely complete recovery, and the remaining return has to come from margins beyond the old normal or from new stores — not from investors paying a higher multiple for a rebound they already expect.

Notable One-Off Items — these are material and they distort both years badly.

Fiscal 2025: roughly $892M of restructuring and impairment costs tied to the Back to Starbucks restructuring announced in September 2025, including the closure of 627 stores, over 90% of them in North America, plus a simplification of the support organization. That charge is most of the gap between FY25 GAAP operating margin of 7.9% and the underlying run-rate.

Q3 FY2026: a $536.3M pre-tax gain on the China divestiture, offset by $302.6M of restructuring and impairments, $44.1M of China transaction costs and $18.1M of transformation costs. Net of those, GAAP EPS of $0.91 came in above non-GAAP EPS of $0.85 — an unusual inversion that means the headline GAAP number flatters the quarter. The effective tax rate also fell to 26.4% from 31.8%. For that quarter the $0.85 non-GAAP figure was the better guide to underlying earnings than the GAAP print.

Q4 FY2026: $272.8M of restructuring and impairments ($270.2M of it in North America, where about 250 company-operated coffeehouses closed in September), $5.5M of China transaction and transition costs and $6.8M of transformation costs — $285.1M of operating income in all, which is why GAAP EPS of $0.59 trails non-GAAP EPS of $0.80. Restructuring and impairments for the whole of fiscal 2026 came to $688.6M.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The diagnosis was right and the fix is working. Fiscal 2026 North America comps of +6.6% were led by transactions (+3.6%), not ticket (+3.0%), achieved while discounting was being removed, and the company met or beat every full-year sales, margin and earnings target it set at Q3. That argues the problem was throughput and service, not a fading brand. Q4 non-GAAP operating margin rose 460 bps to 14.0% and non-GAAP EPS rose 54%, so the cost of the added labor hours is already being outrun by the sales they bring in. Management's fiscal 2027 guide — non-GAAP EPS of $3.15–$3.25, margin of 13.5–14.0%, buybacks resuming — would put earnings back near the bottom of the $3.20–$3.85 normalized range a year early. China has been turned from an unwinnable price war into a 40% equity stake plus a royalty, lifting International segment margin to 19.7% in Q4. Free cash flow was $3.72B in fiscal 2026, and arabica coffee futures are down about a quarter from a year ago. On those numbers, today's 47.2x trailing multiple says more about depressed past earnings than about the price.

Bear Case

The recovery is real and already priced. At $104.40 the stock trades at 38.4x fiscal 2026 non-GAAP EPS, ~32.6x the midpoint of the fiscal 2027 guide and 27–33x a fully normalized $3.20–$3.85 — well above McDonald's 21.6x trailing and 20.2x forward, for a business guiding to about 4% North America comps and revenue down about 1% next year. Q4 also showed growth leaning back on ticket (+5.0%) rather than visits (+2.7%). A year of recovery does not settle the structural question either: Dutch Bros is growing revenue 29.6% and Luckin 36.4% at cheaper price points, and Starbucks now captures only 40% of whatever China becomes. The dividend took 112% of fiscal 2026 GAAP EPS, equity is negative $7.67B, and the current ratio is 0.76 — there is little balance-sheet cushion if comps slow before margin fully recovers, especially once buybacks resume.

Top 3 Risks

01
Comps slow before margin recovers. Fiscal 2027 laps a year of +6.2% global comps, and management already guides North America to about +4%. If transaction growth fades back toward zero while the added Green Apron labor hours stay in the cost base, the company ends up with weaker sales and fiscal 2027's expectations still built into the share price.
02
Investors pay less per dollar of earnings. The market is paying about 32.6x earnings that management has guided to but not yet delivered. If investors valued SBUX like McDonald's, at 21.6x, the $3.20 midpoint of the fiscal 2027 guide would be worth about $69 a share — below the 52-week low — without the turnaround failing at all. The share price is doing more work than the operations.
03
Labor costs and the union, with coffee as the offset. An unresolved Workers United contract could land as a wage step-up in the same year the Green Apron hours are fully in the cost base. Coffee is moving the other way: front-month arabica futures closed at 292.10 US¢/lb on November 2, 2026, down 25.5% from a year earlier and 21.4% since the previous edition (Yahoo Finance, ICE contract). If that reverses while wages rise, the path to normalized earnings gets materially longer.
  Industry Commentary
Green Coffee Costs — Falling Fast
EASING HEADWIND

Front-month arabica futures (the ICE contract for the nearest delivery month) closed at 292.10 US¢/lb (≈$2.92/lb) on November 2, 2026 — up 1.2% from October 2, but down 25.5% from the October 31, 2025 close of 392.05¢ and down 21.4% since late August (Yahoo Finance daily closes). On the Q3 FY2026 call management had flagged coffee inflation as a cost headwind it expected to ease in Q4; Q4 non-GAAP operating margin of 14.0% (+460 bps) came in well above the full-year 11.9%. Management guides fiscal 2027 non-GAAP operating margin to 13.5–14.0%. For a company whose gross margin is 22.43% and whose investment case rests on rebuilding margin, the direction of this one commodity carries unusual weight.

Labor: The Turnaround Is a Deliberate Cost Increase, and the Union Is Unresolved
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Two labor pressures push the same direction. First, by design: Green Apron Service adds staffing hours, routines and coaching to store schedules, and on the Q3 FY2026 call management described labor hours as being managed so as not to cap growth — hours are being spent to serve more customers faster. That is part of why FY2025's GAAP operating margin fell to 7.9% from 15.0% and GAAP EPS halved to $1.63, and why fiscal 2026's recovery to 9.7% GAAP still left margin well short of where it was. Second, involuntarily: Starbucks Workers United says it represents about 12,000 baristas at roughly 550 to 600 U.S. stores (about 6% of the company-operated U.S. stores) and has gone more than four years without a first contract. Its open-ended unfair-labor-practice strike, begun November 13, 2025, had wound down by April 2026, when bargaining was set to resume. The union's February 2026 proposal asks for a $17 wage floor, 4% annual raises, at least three workers on the floor at all times and resolution of hundreds of pending unfair-labor-practice charges (Northwest Labor Press, April 3, 2026); on August 25, 2026 it called a consumer boycott. The union's headline demands — more hours and better staffing — are directionally the same thing Green Apron Service is already doing, which is why a settlement is plausible; it is also why a settlement would likely be priced into wages rather than absorbed for free.

The Price-Point War — and What It Means for Bulls and Bears
EMERGING TAILWIND / STRUCTURAL QUESTION

The competitive set has split by price. Luckin surpassed 36,000 stores globally with TTM revenue of $8.24B growing 36.4% — larger than Starbucks' entire International segment's fiscal 2026 revenue — at a price point a fraction of Starbucks China's. In the U.S., Dutch Bros is growing revenue 29.6% to $1.88B from a cheaper, drive-thru-first format that skews younger. The bear reads this as structural: a generation has repriced what coffee is worth, Starbucks' premium positioning is on the wrong side of it, and the company has already conceded China by selling 60% of it. The bull reads it as containment: Starbucks exited a price war it could not win at an acceptable structural outcome (40% of the equity, plus brand royalties, plus a path to 20,000 JV stores, plus International segment margin of 19.7% in Q4), and then showed in North America — the market that actually matters at 76.4% of segment revenue — that it can grow transactions 3.6% over a full year while raising ticket 3.0% and removing discounts. Both readings fit the same data. Q4 FY2026 did not settle it: transactions grew 2.7% but ticket led at 5.0%. The tiebreaker is now fiscal 2027, against a year of +6.6% North America comps: if visits keep growing, the structural thesis weakens; if growth comes only from price, the dependence on discounts and price increases was never cured.
