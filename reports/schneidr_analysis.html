01 Company Overview

Schneider Electric makes the equipment that moves electricity from the grid to the place it is used, and the software that runs it: switchgear, circuit breakers, power distribution, backup power (UPS), cooling for data centers, building controls, and industrial automation from motor drives to the AVEVA software suite. It calls itself a "global energy technology leader". FY2025 revenue crossed €40B for the first time (€40.15B, +9% organic), with adjusted EBITA of €7.52B (18.7% of revenue) and a record €4.6B of free cash flow. It is a member of the CAC 40 and the Euro Stoxx 50.

The 2026 story is acceleration. H1 2026 revenue was a record €21.23B, up 14.0% organic, and Q2 revenue of €11.46B was a record quarter, up 16.5% organic, led by North America (+23.1%) and China & East Asia (+19.7%). Data centers drive it: in Q2, Systems sales (prefabricated solutions, cooling, 3-phase UPS) grew 28% organic. Management raised the 2026 target on July 30 to 10–13% organic revenue growth and 14–19% organic adjusted EBITA growth, and the stock rose 10.8% that day. The half beat the brokers' forecasts that Schneider gathered in late June and early July (published July 7, 2026): Q2 revenue of €11.46B against €10.88B expected, H1 adjusted EBITA of €4.09B against €3.82B, and adjusted EPS of €4.79 against €4.44. At €303.00 it closed the banner date at the highest point on the five-year monthly chart (the highest daily close of the window was €311.00 on August 12, 2026), up 21.0% in a year while the CAC 40 fell 2.0%. The debate is no longer whether the business is good. It is how much of the price depends on one buyer group (data-center builders) keeping up its spending, and whether a run of software acquisitions changes what kind of company this is.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

1 million partners Presence in 100+ countries Full data-center power & cooling stack Square D · APC · AVEVA brands Software layer (AVEVA, ETAP, RIB) A-grade credit ratings Balanced four-region footprint

The moat is specification and distribution. An electrical engineer who designs a building, factory or data hall around one maker's breakers and switchboards, and an electrician who stocks that maker's parts, tend to stay with it: the parts must fit, be certified, and be serviceable for decades. Schneider reaches that market through about one million partners (its own figure). In data centers it sells the whole power chain, from medium-voltage gear to UPS, cooling and monitoring software, and it says it has worked with data-center customers for more than 20 years. The vulnerability is that the fastest-growing part of the business, large Systems projects for a concentrated set of data-center builders, is also the most cyclical and the lowest-margin part.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One end market sets the multiple. Data Center & Networks was the end market with the strongest demand in Q2 (up triple-digit, strongest in North America), and North America, where data centers drove Energy Management, was 40% of Q2 revenue. On the H1 call the CEO said Schneider works with about 200 data-center customers, a few of whom are a large part of the opportunity. On September 14, 2026, Schneider fell 6.5% in a single session while Eaton fell 7.6%, Vertiv 7.7% and Legrand 6.7%, and the CAC 40 only 0.8%: when the market reprices data-center exposure, Schneider moves with that group, not with its index.
02
Metal costs and tariffs. In H1 2026, raw materials (mainly copper and silver) cost €330M and net tariffs €104M; €280M of product price increases did not cover them, leaving net price at −€154M. Management expects net price to turn positive in H2. If metal prices keep rising, the price increases must keep pace.
03
A busy acquisition calendar. In 2026 Schneider agreed to buy Cognite (all cash, $3.1B), about 90% of AiDASH ($350M enterprise value) and, on September 24, Shelly Group (€1.2B offer). Net debt was €15.36B at June 30, 2026, against €8.15B at the end of 2024; the largest single step was €5.5B paid in 2025 for the remaining minority stake in Schneider Electric India. The A-grade ratings were re-affirmed after Cognite, but every deal adds integration work and leverage.
04
The valuation already assumes the plan. At €303.00 the shares trade at 32.2 times trailing adjusted earnings and yield 1.4%. The medium-term plan (7–10% organic growth a year to 2030, +250 basis points of margin) has to be delivered on top of a 2026 that is already running above it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Electrification is a decades-long trend and Schneider is one of the few companies that sells the whole electrical chain in every region. H1 2026 showed growth everywhere at once: data centers, grid, semiconductors, a recovering automation business, and all four regions. Margins are rising (19.3% adjusted EBITA, +120bps organic), price increases are now landing, free cash flow is a record for a first half, and management raised its 2026 target mid-year. The half beat the brokers' own pre-results forecasts on revenue, adjusted EBITA and adjusted EPS, and they lifted their 2026 adjusted EPS estimate by about 4% afterwards. The CEO's framing is that data centers use only about 1.5% of the world's electricity (heading to 3% or more), and the rest (homes, buildings, factories, infrastructure) is electrifying too. If the 2026–2030 plan of 7–10% organic growth and +250bps of margin holds, earnings growth in the mid-teens can carry a premium multiple, and the buyback and the progressive dividend add to the return.

Bear Case

At 32 times trailing adjusted earnings and a 1.4% yield, the price already assumes the good years continue. Systems (the data-center projects) is the fastest-growing and lowest-margin part of the mix, and it depends on a small group of very large buyers whose spending can pause. The September 14 session, when Schneider, Eaton, Vertiv and Legrand all fell 6.5–7.7% together, shows how quickly that exposure is repriced. Metal costs and tariffs ate more than the price increases in H1. And the group is spending on software (Cognite $3.1B, AiDASH, Shelly €1.2B) with net debt already up to €15.4B; every deal priced at today's multiples raises the bar for returns.

Top 3 Risks

01
A slowdown in data-center spending. Data-center demand led Q2, and North America (40% of Q2 revenue), where data centers drove Energy Management, grew 23.1% organic. A pause by a few large builders would hit the fastest-growing part of the business first, and the stock trades with data-center peers on such news.
02
Acquisition and leverage risk. Three software and smart-home deals agreed in 2026, net debt of €15.36B at June 30, and a stated commitment to A-grade ratings. Integration slips or overpaying would show up in ROCE, which stood at 15.1% in FY2025.
03
Input costs and geopolitics. Copper and silver costs (−€330M in H1), tariffs (−€104M), and the Middle East (under 5% of 2025 revenue, flagged by management as a disruption for H2 with possible supply-chain and inflation effects).
Industry Commentary
AI data centers: the power and cooling bottleneck
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Every AI data hall needs power distribution, backup power and cooling before a single chip runs, and denser racks of graphics processors need more of all three per square metre. On the H1 call the CEO said rising power density per rack will require different power architectures, and that Schneider plans capacity two to three years out from customers' own gigawatt forecasts. To add capacity faster it has turned to partners: an agreement with Foxconn to build prefabricated data-center modules in North America, and a planned prefab factory in France linked to SoftBank's AI infrastructure plans there. The demand is real in the numbers (Systems +28% organic in Q2); the open question is duration, which no supplier controls.

Electrification beyond data centers
BROADENING

The CEO's own framing on the H1 call: "Data Centers represent only 1.5% of electricity consumption even with this boom that will go to 3%, 3%-plus, but 97% of the electricity consumption in the world come from the other segment." H1 2026 backed that up: grid (Power & Grid) and transportation infrastructure grew strongly, semiconductor-plant demand was up triple-digit in Q2, India grew strongly across all four end markets, and discrete automation recovered in China and the US. Residential construction in the US and China remains the weak spot.

Metals, tariffs and pricing power
COST HEADWIND

Electrical equipment is copper- and silver-intensive, and both cost more in 2026. Schneider's H1 bridge shows the race: +€280M from product price increases against −€330M of raw-material inflation and −€104M of net tariffs. Strong productivity (+€535M) more than made up the gap, which is why the margin still rose.
