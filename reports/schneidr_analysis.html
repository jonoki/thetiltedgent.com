01 Company Overview

Schneider Electric makes the equipment that moves electricity from the grid to the place it is used, and the software that runs it: switchgear, circuit breakers, power distribution, backup power (UPS), cooling for data centers, building controls, and industrial automation from motor drives to the AVEVA software suite. It calls itself a "global energy technology leader". FY2025 revenue crossed €40B for the first time (€40.15B, +9% organic), with adjusted EBITA of €7.52B (18.7% of revenue) and a record €4.6B of free cash flow. It is a member of the CAC 40 and the Euro Stoxx 50.

The 2026 story is acceleration. H1 2026 revenue was a record €21.23B, up 14.0% organic, and Q2 revenue of €11.46B was a record quarter, up 16.5% organic, led by North America (+23.1%) and China & East Asia (+19.7%). Data centers drive it: in Q2, Systems sales (prefabricated solutions, cooling, 3-phase UPS) grew 28% organic. Management raised the 2026 target on July 30 to 10–13% organic revenue growth and 14–19% organic adjusted EBITA growth, and the stock rose 10.8% that day. The half beat the brokers' forecasts that Schneider gathered in late June and early July (published July 7, 2026): Q2 revenue of €11.46B against €10.88B expected, H1 adjusted EBITA of €4.09B against €3.82B, and adjusted EPS of €4.79 against €4.44. Q3 kept the pace: revenue of €11.46B, up 16.9% organic (October 16 release), with demand in the pure Data Center segment again up triple-digit, and the 2026 target raised a second time, to 12–14% organic revenue growth and 18–21% organic adjusted EBITA growth. The shares went the other way. On October 5 Schneider agreed to buy the US industrial-software company PTC for $205 a share in cash, about $22.6B for the equity, and the stock fell 10.0% that day; a €6.0B sale of new shares on October 20 and a sell-off of AI-infrastructure suppliers on October 22 followed. At €237.50 the shares closed the banner date 21.6% below the October 2 close and 24.0% below the August 13 intraday high, and down 4.6% over a year while the CAC 40 fell 2.3%. The debate is no longer whether the business is good. It is how much of the price depends on one buyer group (data-center builders) keeping up its spending, and whether a $22.6B software purchase, paid for partly with new shares and debt, changes what kind of company this is at a price that works for shareholders.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

1 million partners Presence in 100+ countries Full data-center power & cooling stack Square D · APC · AVEVA brands Software layer (AVEVA, ETAP, RIB) A-grade credit ratings Balanced four-region footprint

The moat is specification and distribution. An electrical engineer who designs a building, factory or data hall around one maker's breakers and switchboards, and an electrician who stocks that maker's parts, tend to stay with it: the parts must fit, be certified, and be serviceable for decades. Schneider reaches that market through about one million partners (its own figure). In data centers it sells the whole power chain, from medium-voltage gear to UPS, cooling and monitoring software, and it says it has worked with data-center customers for more than 20 years. The vulnerability is that the fastest-growing part of the business, large Systems projects for a concentrated set of data-center builders, is also the most cyclical and the lowest-margin part.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One end market sets the share price. Data Center & Networks was the end market with the strongest demand in Q2 (up triple-digit, strongest in North America), and North America, where data centers drove Energy Management, was 40% of Q2 revenue. On the H1 call the CEO said Schneider works with about 200 data-center customers, a few of whom are a large part of the opportunity. On September 14, 2026, Schneider fell 6.5% in a single session while Eaton fell 7.6%, Vertiv 7.7% and Legrand 6.7%, and the CAC 40 only 0.8%: when investors mark down data-center suppliers, Schneider moves with that group, not with its index. It happened again on October 22, 2026: as European stocks fell on concerns over AI-infrastructure spending, Schneider closed down 5.4% and Legrand lost nearly 6% while the CAC 40 fell 1.4% (DPA).
02
Metal costs and tariffs. In H1 2026, raw materials (mainly copper and silver) cost €330M and net tariffs €104M; €280M of product price increases did not cover them, leaving net price at −€154M. Management expects net price to turn positive in H2. If metal prices keep rising, the price increases must keep pace.
03
A busy acquisition calendar. In 2026 Schneider agreed to buy Cognite (all cash, $3.1B), about 90% of AiDASH ($350M enterprise value), Shelly Group (€1.2B offer, September 24) and, on October 5, PTC (all cash, $22.6B for the equity, $23.7B enterprise value). PTC is being paid for with €6.0B of new shares, placed on October 20, and new debt, and the buyback pauses in 2027 and 2028. Net debt was €15.36B at June 30, 2026, before PTC, against €8.15B at the end of 2024; the largest single step was €5.5B paid in 2025 for the remaining minority stake in Schneider Electric India. S&P and Moody's re-affirmed the A-category ratings on October 6, after the PTC agreement, but every deal adds integration work and leverage.
04
The valuation already assumes the plan. At €237.50 the shares trade at 25.2 times trailing adjusted earnings and yield 1.8%, down from 32.2 times at the October 2 close. The medium-term plan (7–10% organic growth a year to 2030, +250 basis points of margin) still has to be delivered on top of a 2026 that is already running above it, now alongside the integration of PTC.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items move the comparisons. First, H1 2025 included a €207M fine paid in a French legal case, which depresses the prior-year free cash flow (hence +244% reported vs +140% like-for-like). Second, restructuring is running high on purpose: €127M in H1 2026 and around €450M expected for FY2026, part of €500M of extra restructuring planned over 2025–2027. Third, H1 2026 other operating expenses of €147M were mainly an impairment of capitalised development costs in Industrial Automation, plus M&A and integration costs. Adjusted figures exclude these; IFRS net income includes them. The minority buyout in Schneider Electric India (December 2025) lowered the share of profit going to minority holders (€60M in H1 2026 vs €97M), adding to Group-share earnings.

How the half compared with the forecasts. Schneider's pre-results consensus (published July 7, 2026; 23 brokers, submissions gathered June 26 – July 6) expected Q2 revenue of €10,882M, up 10.6% organic; the company reported €11,459M, 5.3% above, and +16.5% organic. Brokers then raised their numbers: between the July and the August consensus, 2026 adjusted EPS went from €9.97 to €10.39 (+4.2%), 2026 adjusted EBITA from €8,462M to €8,815M, 2026 organic growth from +10.4% to +12.9%, and 2027 adjusted EPS from €11.71 to €12.23 (+4.4%). Rating moves since the H1 print. Of the actions listed on MarketScreener's broker-research page between the results and October 2, most were Buy ratings kept: UBS, Deutsche Bank and Bernstein on July 31, Goldman Sachs on August 4, DZ Bank in August, and JP Morgan and Jefferies on September 24. After the PTC deal. JPMorgan cut the stock from Overweight to Neutral and its target from €345 to €310 on October 6; it did not question the operating outlook but had reservations about the new capital-allocation strategy, the €800M revenue-synergy goal and the financing, while judging the share-price fall excessive (BFM Bourse, from Zonebourse, October 6). Barclays (analyst George Featherstone) kept Overweight and cut its target from €356 to €326 in a note dated October 16, expecting the negative sentiment over PTC to persist for now despite positive surprises and higher group targets. UBS (analyst Andre Kukhnin) kept Buy and raised its target from €335 to €350 in a note dated October 21, raising his operating-profit estimates and calling the shares an attractive entry point after an excessive fall (both dpa-AFX, via boerse.de). Schneider gives a full-year target rather than quarterly guidance; the next test is the FY2026 results on February 18, 2027.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of €148.90 to the October 30, 2026 close of €237.50, Schneider rose +59.5% on price. Adding back the €17.65 of dividends paid over the period (not reinvested) makes it about +71%. The CAC 40 rose 16.7% on price over the same window. The path had four phases: a 2022 slide to €112.90 in June 2022, the low of the window; a near-uninterrupted climb from late 2023 to €245.35 in January 2025; after a 2025 pause, a volatile 2026 that peaked at €294.60 at the end of August; and an 18.7% fall in October, the largest monthly fall on the chart, after the PTC deal.

That is a sharp loss of momentum, not a forecast: it is close to, but not below, the 30 "oversold" line. The lowest reading in the window was 29.9 in April 2025, the last time the stock was oversold.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Electrification is a decades-long trend and Schneider is one of the few companies that sells the whole electrical chain in every region. H1 2026 showed growth everywhere at once: data centers, grid, semiconductors, a recovering automation business, and all four regions. Margins are rising (19.3% adjusted EBITA, +120bps organic), price increases are now landing, free cash flow is a record for a first half, and management raised its 2026 target twice, in July and again in October after a Q3 with revenue up 16.9% organic and data-center demand up triple-digit. The half beat the brokers' own pre-results forecasts on revenue, adjusted EBITA and adjusted EPS, and they lifted their 2026 adjusted EPS estimate by about 4% afterwards. The CEO's framing is that data centers use only about 1.5% of the world's electricity (heading to 3% or more), and the rest (homes, buildings, factories, infrastructure) is electrifying too. If the 2026–2030 plan of 7–10% organic growth and +250bps of margin holds, earnings growth in the mid-teens can justify paying more for each euro of earnings than for the average company, and the progressive dividend adds to the return. PTC brings a large recurring software business that Schneider expects to lift growth, gross margin and adjusted EPS, and the October fall took the shares from 32 to 25 times trailing adjusted earnings.

Bear Case

At 25 times trailing adjusted earnings and a 1.8% yield, the price still assumes the good years continue. Systems (the data-center projects) is the fastest-growing and lowest-margin part of the mix, and it depends on a small group of very large buyers whose spending can pause. The September 14 session, when Schneider, Eaton, Vertiv and Legrand all fell 6.5–7.7% together, shows how quickly investors mark that business down, and October 22 did it again. Metal costs and tariffs ate more than the price increases in H1. And the group is spending heavily on software (PTC $22.6B, Cognite $3.1B, AiDASH, Shelly €1.2B), paid for with new shares, debt and a two-year pause in buybacks, with net debt already €15.4B at June 30 before PTC. JPMorgan downgraded the stock over how it now allocates capital, and every deal bought at today's high prices raises the bar for returns.

Top 3 Risks

01
A slowdown in data-center spending. Data-center demand led Q2, and North America (40% of Q2 revenue), where data centers drove Energy Management, grew 23.1% organic; in Q3 North America was again 40% of revenue and grew 19.9%. A pause by a few large builders would hit the fastest-growing part of the business first, and the stock trades with data-center peers on such news.
02
Acquisition and leverage risk. Four software and smart-home deals agreed in 2026, the largest PTC at a $23.7B enterprise value; net debt of €15.36B at June 30, before PTC; €6.0B of new shares already issued; and a stated commitment to A-grade ratings. Integration slips or overpaying would show up in ROCE, which stood at 15.1% in FY2025.
03
Input costs and geopolitics. Copper and silver costs (−€330M in H1), tariffs (−€104M), and the Middle East (under 5% of 2025 revenue; the Q3 release says disruption there may affect Q4, with possible pressure on supply chains and inflation).
Industry Commentary
AI data centers: the power and cooling bottleneck
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Every AI data hall needs power distribution, backup power and cooling before a single chip runs, and denser racks of graphics processors need more of all three per square metre. On the H1 call the CEO said rising power density per rack will require different power architectures, and that Schneider plans capacity two to three years out from customers' own gigawatt forecasts. To add capacity faster it has turned to partners: an agreement with Foxconn to build prefabricated data-center modules in North America, and a planned prefab factory in France linked to SoftBank's AI infrastructure plans there. The demand is real in the numbers (Systems +28% organic in Q2, +22% in Q3, with data-center demand up triple-digit again), and the CEO says it is "providing increasing visibility into 2027 and 2028" (Q3 release). Investors are less sure: on October 22 European stocks fell on concerns over AI-infrastructure spending, and Schneider and Legrand were among the large fallers (DPA). The open question is duration, which no supplier controls.

Electrification beyond data centers
BROADENING

The CEO's own framing on the H1 call: "Data Centers represent only 1.5% of electricity consumption even with this boom that will go to 3%, 3%-plus, but 97% of the electricity consumption in the world come from the other segment." H1 2026 backed that up: grid (Power & Grid) and transportation infrastructure grew strongly, semiconductor-plant demand was up triple-digit in Q2, India grew strongly across all four end markets, and discrete automation recovered in China and the US. Residential construction in the US and China remains the weak spot.

Metals, tariffs and pricing power
COST HEADWIND

Electrical equipment is copper- and silver-intensive, and both cost more in 2026. Schneider's H1 bridge shows the race: +€280M from product price increases against −€330M of raw-material inflation and −€104M of net tariffs. Strong productivity (+€535M) more than made up the gap, which is why the margin still rose. Management expects net price to turn positive in H2 as increases accelerate; pricing power in a tight market is the industry's protection, and the H2 numbers will show whether it holds.
