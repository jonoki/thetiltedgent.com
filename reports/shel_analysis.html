01 Company Overview

Shell is one of the Western oil and gas integrated majors, and the one whose distinguishing asset is a global LNG portfolio. It reports five segments — Integrated Gas, Upstream, Marketing, Chemicals & Products, and Renewables & Energy Solutions. FY2025 revenue was $266.89B, down 6.13%, with income attributable to shareholders of $17.84B. The trailing twelve months to September 30, 2026 look very different: revenue $321.30B and net income $32.71B (StockAnalysis, the sum of the last four quarters), almost all of the jump coming in the last two quarters, when revenue rose 44.7% YoY to $94.66B in Q2 and 36.2% YoY to $92.85B in Q3 2026 on a Middle East supply shock. Q3 2026, reported on October 29, brought Adjusted Earnings of $10.81B (Q2: $9.84B; Q3 2025: $5.43B), a new $5.0B buyback and a trimmed 2026 capex guide of $23–25B. Its market value is $311.4B (our calculation); the NYSE ADS is the line priced on this page.

The question this report asks: why does Shell still trade at about half the valuation multiple of ExxonMobil and Chevron while producing roughly 1.6 to 2.1 times as much free cash flow for each dollar of market value? On StockAnalysis's data, repriced by us to the October 30 close, SHEL trades at 5.2x EV/EBITDA and a 12.0% FCF yield; XOM sits at 10.0x and 5.6%, CVX at 8.4x and 7.4%. Shell has also shrunk its share count 5.87% in a year versus 3.70% at Exxon (Chevron's count rose 10.04%). The gap is real on the numbers. The harder part is the "why" — and the honest answer is that some of the gap is earned: more debt (debt/equity 0.38 vs 0.13–0.19), more earnings volatility from refining and trading, European governments' readiness to tax windfall profits (as in 2022), and a London-first investor base. Integrated Gas is the supposed differentiator — but in Q3 2026 the biggest earner was Chemicals & Products ($3.86B of Adjusted Earnings, on record refining margins), ahead of Upstream ($3.19B) and Integrated Gas ($3.04B). We use Shell's own measures throughout: CFFO, Adjusted Earnings and gearing.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Global LNG portfolio & trading book Integrated value chain Deepwater operating know-how Retail brand & network Scale: $58.0B TTM operating cash flow Capex discipline ($23–25B FY26 guide)

Shell's edge is flexibility. Owning liquefaction, shipping, regas access and a trading desk means that when a supply shock hits — in September Reuters reported LNG buyers looking for new suppliers as the US-Israeli war against Iran cut Gulf shipments, and Shell Trading's chief economist said the energy market's shock absorbers were weakening — Shell can move cargoes to where the price is highest. A US major with a mostly domestic, production-weighted portfolio captures the commodity price; Shell also captures the spread, the price gap between markets. The vulnerability is the same thing viewed from the other side: spread income is episodic, and investors pay less for each dollar of one-off profit.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Earnings volatility. Chemicals & Products went from $550M of Adjusted Earnings in Q3 2025 to $3,855M in Q3 2026; CFFO was $6.06B in Q1 2026, then $21.43B in Q2 and $21.04B in Q3. A business whose quarterly cash flow can triple is priced on its normal quarter, not its best one.
02
European tax and policy risk. Europe has taxed energy windfalls before: Shell expected the EU's 2022 solidarity contribution (33% of profits above a company's average) and the UK's Energy Profits Levy, expanded to 35%, to cut its Q4 2022 earnings by about $2B (Oil & Gas 360, January 2023). A levy aimed at exactly the kind of crisis profits Shell is now earning would cap the upside the bull case needs.
03
More leverage than the US peers. Debt/equity is 0.38 against 0.13 at XOM and 0.19 at CVX (StockAnalysis). Shell's own gearing measure improved to 16.6% at September 30, 2026 (net debt $39.73B), from 18.7% at June 30 and 18.8% a year earlier, even after the ARC Resources deal (completed Sep 2, 2026, part-paid in Shell shares). In Q4 Shell also expects a non-cash loss of about $6.4B (its estimate at September 30, subject to change) from removing its Netherlands defined benefit pension plan from its books.
04
Legal legacy. The UK High Court has allowed claims that Shell's witnesses sought to mislead the court to be examined now, ahead of the main trial of the Bille community's Nigeria oil-pollution case, rather than afterwards as Shell had argued (Leigh Day, the community's lawyers, Sep 10, 2026). The community seeks aggravated damages; no amount has been given.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the trailing figures. First, identified items: Shell's Q3 2026 identified items were a net gain of $0.8B (Q2 2026: a $0.4B gain; Q3 2025: a $0.1B loss), which is why Q3 income attributable to shareholders ($12.06B) sits above Adjusted Earnings ($10.81B). Second, a pension item still to come: Shell's Q3 release expects a non-cash loss, estimated at $6.4B at September 30, 2026 and subject to change, in Q4 when it removes its Netherlands defined benefit pension plan from its books; it would reduce reported income, not cash. Third, cash flow and portfolio moves: Q3 CFFO of $21.04B came mainly from Adjusted EBITDA and $3.4B of net cash inflows related to derivatives, partly offset by tax and emissions-related outflows (Shell Q3 2026 results). Jiffy Lube proceeds, the RISEC power-plant sale and Tri Star Energy are portfolio moves, while ARC Resources (~$13.9B equity value, completed Sep 2) goes the other way.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 9.4x trailing earnings, 8.3x FY2026 forward earnings and 5.2x EV/EBITDA for a company with a 12.0% FCF yield, an 8.65% shareholder yield, a share count falling 5.87% a year and a higher ROCE (15.4%) than either ExxonMobil (13.4%) or Chevron (9.4%). Gearing is down to 16.6% and the buyback has been raised to $5.0B for the next three months. The LNG portfolio — 7.43 Mt liquefied in Q3 2026, with LNG Canada Phase 2 set to double that plant's capacity — is the asset the world is short of, and Shell also trades the spread. If the market paid even Chevron's 8.4x EV/EBITDA, the equity would be worth far more than today's price; and every quarter the gap persists, the buyback retires shares more cheaply.

Bear Case

The discount is not a mistake; it is a price for volatility and jurisdiction. Chemicals & Products went from $550M to $3.9B of Adjusted Earnings in a year on record refining margins, CFFO has ranged from $6.1B (Q1) to $21.4B (Q2) and $21.0B (Q3), and TTM earnings are riding a Middle East supply shock that consensus already expects to fade (FY2027 EPS −9.8%). Leverage is two to three times the US peers', ARC Resources added ~$13.9B of equity value (cash plus Shell shares), a $6.4B non-cash pension loss is expected in Q4, Europe has taxed energy windfalls before (2022), and Q3 capex nearly doubled from Q2. A low price for each dollar of peak-cycle earnings is how a company tied to the commodity cycle looks near the top.

Top 3 Risks

01
Normalisation of the 2026 windfall. Q3 2026 produced $10.81B of Adjusted Earnings against $5.43B a year earlier. Consensus already models FY2027 EPS falling 9.8% from FY2026. If the supply shock eases faster than that, the 12.0% FCF yield is a trailing number, not a forward one.
02
Policy and tax in Europe. Europe taxed the 2022 energy windfall (about a $2B hit to Shell's Q4 2022 earnings, by Shell's estimate), and a repeat on 2026's crisis profits is possible. It is one of the few explanations for the valuation gap that could get worse rather than better.
03
Capital allocation drift. ARC Resources (~$13.9B, completed Sep 2) and the LNG Canada Phase 2 investment decision (Sep 29) arrive together with a buyback raised to $5.0B. All are coherent with the LNG strategy; all compete for the same cash, and the 2026 cash-capex guide is $23–25B.
Industry Commentary
The valuation gap — why it persists
CENTRAL QUESTION

The gap is real on the numbers: on October 30, 2026 SHEL traded at 5.22x EV/EBITDA vs 9.96x for XOM and 8.39x for CVX, with a 12.00% FCF yield vs 5.62% and 7.41%, and a faster-shrinking share count (our repricing of StockAnalysis data). Why the gap persists: (1) earnings quality — more of Shell's profit comes from refining and trading, which the market pays less for; (2) leverage — 0.38 debt/equity vs 0.13–0.19; (3) jurisdiction — a London primary listing and a European policy environment that has taxed energy windfalls before (2022); and (4) the payout mix — Shell returns more through buybacks than dividends, and its dividend yield (2.78%) is below Chevron's (3.03%), which matters to income-focused holders. None of these is new, which is why a single strong quarter has not closed the gap. What would: several quarters of stable CFFO in a normal commodity market, with the buyback sustained.

LNG: a tight market
EMERGING TAILWIND

The war against Iran cut 36 million tonnes of Middle East LNG supply, but new capacity elsewhere left the net loss this year at only about 5 million tonnes, or 1–1.5% of global supply, according to Shell's head of LNG marketing and trading (Reuters, September 21, 2026), while buyers seek alternatives to Gulf cargoes. In Europe, gas storage was about 74.9% full on October 26, below typical levels for the time of year, and prices rose above €100 per megawatt-hour on October 28 for the first time since December 2022 (Xinhua). This is a strong backdrop for a portfolio LNG player, but periods like this pull forward new supply, and Shell's own LNG Canada Phase 2 decision (September 29) is part of that response.

Capital discipline
ONGOING THEME

Shell has cut spending while paying out heavily: FY2025 capex of $18.95B against $22.99B in FY2023, while buybacks ran at $14.7–19.0B a year from 2022 through 2025 (StockAnalysis cash-flow data). The open question for 2026–27 is whether a price spike loosens that discipline — Shell's 2026 cash-capex guide, lowered to $23–25B from $24–26B with Q3 results (it includes items beyond StockAnalysis's capex line), is the number to hold it to.
