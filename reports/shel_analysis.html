01 Company Overview

Shell is one of the five Western oil and gas integrated majors, and the one whose distinguishing asset is a global LNG portfolio. It reports five segments — Integrated Gas, Upstream, Marketing, Chemicals & Products, and Renewables & Energy Solutions. FY2025 revenue was $266.89B, down 6.13%, with income attributable to shareholders of $17.84B. The trailing twelve months to June 30, 2026 look very different: revenue $296.60B (+9.0%) and net income $25.97B (+90.9%), almost all of it from one quarter — Q2 2026, when revenue jumped 44.7% YoY to $94.66B. At a $267.85B market value it ranks #60 globally and #3 in the United Kingdom by companiesmarketcap's country ranking (behind HSBC and Arm; among FTSE 100 constituents on that list only HSBC is larger). Its home index is the FTSE 100; the NYSE ADS is the line priced on this page.

The question this report asks: why does Shell still trade at half the multiple of ExxonMobil and Chevron while throwing off roughly twice the free-cash-flow yield? On StockAnalysis's September 21 data, SHEL trades at 5.4x EV/EBITDA and an 11.8% FCF yield; XOM sits at 10.1x and 4.7%, CVX at 8.5x and 6.7%. Shell has also shrunk its share count 6.42% in a year versus 3.89% at Exxon (Chevron's count rose 12.01%). The premise holds on the numbers. The harder part is the "why" — and the honest answer is that some of the gap is earned: more leverage (debt/equity 0.40 vs 0.16–0.19), more earnings volatility from refining and trading, a European political bid for windfall taxes, and a shareholder register that is London-first. Integrated Gas is the supposed differentiator — but in H1 2026 it was Upstream and Chemicals & Products that drove the jump, not LNG. We use Shell's own measures throughout: CFFO, Adjusted Earnings and gearing.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Global LNG portfolio & trading book Integrated value chain Deepwater operating know-how Retail brand & network Scale: $49.1B TTM operating cash flow Capex discipline ($24–26B FY26 guide)

Shell's edge is optionality. Owning liquefaction, shipping, regas access and a trading desk means that when a supply shock hits — Reuters reports LNG buyers scrambling to diversify as the war involving Iran cuts Gulf shipments, and Shell itself has warned that 36 million tonnes of lost LNG are exhausting market buffers — Shell can move cargoes to where the price is highest. A US major with a mostly domestic, production-weighted portfolio captures the commodity price; Shell also captures the spread. The vulnerability is the same thing viewed from the other side: spread income is episodic, and investors capitalise episodic income at a lower multiple.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Earnings volatility. Chemicals & Products went from $118M to $2,877M of Adjusted Earnings in a year; Q1 2026 CFFO was $6.06B and Q2 was $21.43B. A business whose quarterly cash flow can triple is priced on its normal quarter, not its best one.
02
European tax and policy risk. Headlines on September 18, 2026 had Shell falling as Europe revived windfall-tax politics. The US majors face no equivalent debate at home. The headline (GuruFocus) was seen without detail; no specific levy was verified. A levy aimed at exactly the kind of crisis profits Shell is now earning would cap the upside the bull case needs.
03
More leverage than the US peers. Debt/equity is 0.40 against 0.16 at XOM and 0.19 at CVX (StockAnalysis). Shell's own gearing measure was 18.7% at June 30, 2026 (net debt $41.75B), down from 23.2% at March 31 — so the direction is right, but the ARC Resources deal (completed Sep 2, 2026; ~$2.5B of net debt and leases assumed, part-paid in Shell shares) lands after that date.
04
Legal legacy. A UK High Court has ruled that allegations that witnesses for Shell gave false or misleading evidence can be examined before the full trial of a long-running Nigeria oil-spill case (Premium Times, Sep 10, 2026). No financial estimate is available.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the trailing figures. First, identified items: H1 2026 carried a net $2.0B loss of identified items (H1 2025: $1.2B loss), which is why Q2 IFRS income ($10.82B) and Adjusted Earnings ($9.84B) diverge, and in opposite directions quarter to quarter (Q1 2026: $5.69B IFRS vs $6.92B adjusted). Second, working capital: Q2 CFFO of $21.43B included a $3.4B working-capital inflow; Q1's $6.06B was depressed by the opposite, so the half-year ($27.50B) is the fairer read. Third, portfolio moves: Jiffy Lube ($1.3B, completed Jun 30), Sprng Energy ($1.8B, agreed Jul 2026) and RISEC ($715M, agreed Sep 10) bring in disposal proceeds, while ARC Resources (~$13.9B equity value, completed Sep 2) goes the other way. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 10.4x trailing earnings, 8.4x forward and 5.4x EV/EBITDA for a company with an 11.8% FCF yield, a 9.66% shareholder yield, a share count falling 6.42% a year and a higher ROCE (13.3%) than either ExxonMobil (10.7%) or Chevron (9.4%). Gearing is back to 18.7%. The LNG portfolio — 15.6 Mt liquefied in H1 2026, +17% — is the asset the world is short of, and Shell also trades the spread. If the market paid even Chevron's 8.5x EV/EBITDA, the equity would be worth far more than today's price; and every quarter the gap persists, the buyback retires shares more cheaply.

Bear Case

The discount is not a mistake; it is a price for volatility and jurisdiction. Chemicals & Products went from $118M to $2.9B in a year, Q1 CFFO was $6.1B and Q2 was $21.4B, and TTM earnings are riding a Middle East supply shock that consensus already expects to fade (FY2027 EPS −11%). Leverage is twice the US peers', ARC Resources added ~$13.9B of equity value (cash plus Shell shares), Europe is talking windfall taxes again, and Integrated Gas — the supposed differentiator — grew H1 Adjusted Earnings only 6.8%. A cheap multiple on peak-cycle earnings is how a cyclical looks near the top.

Top 3 Risks

01
Normalisation of the 2026 windfall. Q2 2026 produced $9.84B of Adjusted Earnings against $4.26B a year earlier. Consensus already models FY2027 EPS falling 11% from FY2026. If the supply shock eases faster than that, the 11.8% FCF yield is a trailing number, not a forward one.
02
Policy and tax in Europe. Windfall-tax politics resurfaced on September 18, 2026 (headline only; no specific proposal verified). US peers do not carry the equivalent home-market risk, and it is one of the few explanations for the valuation gap that could get worse rather than better.
03
Capital allocation drift. ARC Resources (~$13.9B, completed Sep 2) and a possible LNG Canada Phase 2 FID arrive together. Both are coherent with the LNG strategy; both compete with the $3B-a-quarter buyback that underpins the per-share story.

The valuation gap — why it persists
CENTRAL QUESTION

The premise checks out on the numbers: on September 21, 2026 SHEL traded at 5.40x EV/EBITDA vs 10.06x for XOM and 8.53x for CVX, with an 11.78% FCF yield vs 4.69% and 6.70%, and a faster-shrinking share count. Our reading of why the gap persists (analysis, not sourced fact): (1) earnings quality — more of Shell's profit comes from refining and trading, which the market capitalises at lower multiples; (2) leverage — 0.40 debt/equity vs 0.16–0.19; (3) jurisdiction — a London primary listing and a European policy environment that periodically revisits windfall taxes; and (4) the payout mix — Shell returns more through buybacks than dividends, and its dividend yield (3.24%) is still below Chevron's (3.50%), which matters to income-focused holders. None of these is new, which is why a fresh cash-flow beat alone has not closed the gap. What would: several quarters of stable CFFO through a normal commodity tape, with the buyback held at $3B+.

LNG: the tightest market in energy
EMERGING TAILWIND

Shell has warned that around 36 million tonnes of lost LNG supply are exhausting market buffers, and Reuters reports buyers seeking alternatives to Gulf cargoes as the war involving Iran disrupts shipments. European gas storage was reported at about 67% of capacity in mid-September against the EU's 90% pre-winter target, and European gas prices hit a four-year high (Barron's, ~Sep 10, 2026). For a portfolio LNG player this is the best possible tape — but tapes like this pull forward new supply, and Shell's own Phase 2 FID at LNG Canada is part of that response.

Big Oil's capital discipline
ONGOING THEME

Across the majors, production has kept rising despite deep spending cuts, with cash returned to shareholders prioritised since the 2020 crash (OilPrice.com, Sep 2026). Shell is an example: FY2025 capex of $18.95B against $22.99B in FY2023, while buybacks ran at $14–19B a year from 2022 through 2025. The open question for 2026–27 is whether a price spike loosens that discipline — Shell's $24–26B cash-capex guide for 2026 (which includes items beyond StockAnalysis's capex line) is the number to hold it to.
