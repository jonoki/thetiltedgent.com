01 Company Overview
Shopify sells the software and financial plumbing that lets a merchant run an online (and increasingly offline) store — storefront, checkout, payments, shipping, capital, and a point-of-sale terminal. Unlike Amazon, it does not compete with its own customers for the sale. FY2025 revenue was $11,556M, up 30.1% year over year, on top of $8,880M in FY2024 (+25.8%) and $7,060M in FY2023 (+26.1%). Over the twelve months to September 2026, revenue reached $14,167M, up 32.4%. Shopify ranks #28 in the Nasdaq-100 by market value.
The important thing to understand before reading anything else on this page: Shopify's revenue is not the top of its funnel. GMV — gross merchandise volume, the total dollar value of everything sold across every merchant on the platform — is. In Q3 2026 Shopify's merchants transacted $119,145M of GMV and Shopify booked $3,742M of revenue against it. That ratio, roughly 3.1%, is the take rate (the share of merchant sales Shopify keeps as its own revenue). Everything that matters about this business is either GMV growth or take-rate expansion, and Section 02 is built around that split.

02 Core Businesses
Shopify reports exactly two revenue lines, and they behave nothing like each other. Subscription Solutions is recurring plan fees — high margin, predictable, slower. Merchant Solutions is payments, shipping, capital and hardware — most of the revenue, most of the growth, and roughly half the gross margin, because a large slice of it is money that passes straight through Shopify to the card networks. Understanding that difference is the single most useful thing in this report, and it is why revenue growth and gross-profit growth do not match.

Revenue Mix
Payments (Shopify Payments), shipping labels, merchant lending (Shopify Capital), currency conversion, tax services, POS hardware and Shop Pay Installments. This is where GMV converts into dollars. Against Q3 2026 GMV of $119,145M, Merchant Solutions revenue of $2,906M implies a merchant-solutions take rate of 2.44% — roughly three-quarters of Shopify's total 3.14% take. It grew 35% in Q3 2026, faster than the company.

Here is the catch, and it is the whole point. Merchant Solutions produced $1,120M of gross profit on $2,906M of revenue — a 38.5% gross margin. Subscription Solutions produced $661M on $836M — 79.1%. The reason is interchange: the fee the card-issuing bank charges on every card transaction. Shopify collects the full payment fee from the merchant as revenue, then remits interchange and network fees to Visa, Mastercard and the issuing banks as cost of revenue. That money is booked as revenue on the way in and cost on the way out, and Shopify keeps only the spread. So a dollar of Merchant Solutions revenue is worth about 38 cents of gross profit; a dollar of Subscription revenue is worth about 80 cents.

Because the low-margin half is growing faster (+35% vs +20% in Q3 2026), blended gross margin falls every year even when nothing goes wrong: 50.36% (FY2024) → 48.07% (FY2025) → 47.49% (twelve months to September 2026). That mechanically drives a wedge between the two growth rates investors quote. In Q3 2026, revenue grew 32% but gross profit grew 28%. For the full year FY2025, revenue grew 30.1% while gross profit grew 24.2% ($4,472M → $5,555M) — a six-point gap. On the Q3 call CFO Jeff Hoffmeister said the same for Q4: "We expect Q4 revenue growth in the low 30s year-over-year," and "We expect gross profit dollars to grow in the high 20s."

Payments Penetration
Shop Pay is Shopify's one-click accelerated checkout: a shopper who has bought from any Shopify merchant has their card and address on file, and can complete a purchase at any other Shopify merchant without re-typing anything. That is the flywheel — every new merchant adds shoppers to the vault, and every vaulted shopper makes every existing merchant convert better, which makes Shopify's platform worth more to the next merchant.

The commercial expression of the flywheel is attach rate — the proportion of merchants (or of GMV) that also use a given Shopify product rather than a third party. The critical one is GPV (gross payments volume) as a percentage of GMV: how much of the merchandise flowing across the platform is settled through Shopify Payments rather than through Stripe, PayPal or a bank acquirer. Every point of that penetration turns a software customer into a payments customer as well.

What Shopify disclosed: the release gives no GPV dollar figure, but on the Q3 2026 call CFO Jeff Hoffmeister said: "Payments penetration surpassed 68%, up 3 points year-over-year, and Shop Pay GMV grew 50% year-over-year." The aggregate take rate has held at about 3.1% for three quarters: 3.15% in Q1 2026 ($3,170M / $100,743M), 3.10% in Q2 ($3,583M / $115,567M) and 3.14% in Q3 ($3,742M / $119,145M), against 3.09% in Q3 2025. The revenue growth is coming from GMV, not from taking much more per dollar.

Debt / Equity
Shopify Capital advances working capital to merchants and takes repayment as a fixed percentage of their future sales. Because Shopify sees the merchant's entire order flow and settles the payments, its underwriting information is genuinely better than a bank's — and repayment is collected at source. That is a real structural edge.

It is also lending, and it sits inside a company the market values at a software multiple. A software business has essentially no balance-sheet loss content; a lender does. If merchant defaults rise in a consumer downturn, the losses land in Merchant Solutions cost of revenue and compress a gross margin that is already the diluted half of the business. Shopify's corporate balance sheet is very strong — at September 30, 2026, $4.73B of cash and marketable securities, no borrowings, $188M of lease liabilities, debt-to-equity of 0.01 and a current ratio of 4.86 — so this is not a solvency question. It is an earnings-quality question: a slice of Shopify's gross profit is lending income, which carries the risk of loans going bad, not software margin, and it should not be valued at the same multiple of profit.

What the release shows: loans and merchant cash advances, net, of $2,350M at September 30, 2026, up from $1,784M at December 31, 2025; $1,700M of purchases and originations in Q3 2026 (versus $996M a year earlier); and a transaction and loan losses expense of $120M (versus $148M), which combines payment and lending losses. No loss rate for Capital alone is reported (Q3 2026 release, November 3, 2026). Since April 2026 the merchant cash advance cash flows sit in investing rather than operating cash flow, which lifts reported operating cash flow against earlier periods (10-Q).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
Shop Pay vaulted-shopper network Merchant switching cost App & theme ecosystem Payments data → underwriting Not a marketplace competitor Checkout conversion advantage Omnichannel (POS + online)
Shopify's moat is a two-sided network dressed up as software. On the merchant side, once a store's catalogue, theme, apps, historical order data, payments settlement and staff training all live inside Shopify, migrating means rebuilding the business during a period when it cannot sell — a switching cost measured in weeks of revenue, not licence fees. On the shopper side, Shop Pay's stored credentials mean every additional Shopify merchant makes checkout faster for shoppers who have already bought elsewhere on the platform, and faster checkout measurably converts better. The two sides reinforce: merchants join for conversion, which grows the shopper vault, which improves conversion for the next merchant. Layered on top is an informational moat that is genuinely hard to replicate — because Shopify both hosts the store and settles the payment, it can underwrite Shopify Capital on live order flow rather than on filed accounts, and collect repayment before the merchant ever touches the cash. And unlike Amazon, Shopify does not sell against its own customers. The vulnerability in all of this is that the moat protects the merchant relationship, not the take rate — and the take rate is what the valuation depends on.
KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. Take-rate compression, not GMV loss, is the real risk. Take rate was 3.15% in Q1 2026, 3.10% in Q2 and 3.14% in Q3 — flat, with no clear expansion. Payments pricing is competitive and largely a markup over interchange; if Stripe, Adyen or PayPal price aggressively for Shopify merchants, Shopify loses margin on volume it still hosts. A change of a tenth of a percentage point in take rate on $119.1B of quarterly GMV is roughly $119M of quarterly revenue — larger than most single things management controls.

2. Mix shift keeps pulling gross margin down. Blended gross margin has fallen 50.36% → 48.07% → 47.49% across FY2024, FY2025 and the twelve months to September 2026, because the low-margin half grows faster. This is not a mistake and not reversible without slowing Merchant Solutions. It means revenue growth overstates the growth in what Shopify keeps, and an investor anchoring on the revenue line is paying for card fees that pass straight through to the banks.

3. Consumer-discretionary and small-business credit exposure. GMV is downstream of consumer spending on discretionary goods, and Shopify's merchant base skews toward small and mid-size businesses — the cohort most exposed in a downturn. Shopify Capital adds a second exposure: the same recession that reduces GMV also raises merchant default rates on advances that Shopify has already funded. A beta of 2.66 (StockAnalysis, five-year) is the market's summary of this.

4. Shopping through AI assistants could cut out the storefront. If shoppers increasingly buy through AI assistants and aggregators rather than by visiting a brand's site, the value of hosting the storefront falls and the value of being in the payment path may or may not survive. Shopify is positioning to be the merchant-of-record (the seller legally behind the sale) behind such purchases, and on the Q3 2026 call Harley Finkelstein said "AI traffic is up 2.6x year-over-year" and "Orders are growing even faster at 3.3x." The question is still open.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

This is the most important section on the page for anyone quoting the P/E. Shopify's net income and its operating income routinely diverge by hundreds of millions to billions of dollars, and the gap is almost entirely below the operating line — marks on financial assets and tax movements, not trading performance. The figures below are reported; the arithmetic is derived from them.

FY2022 — a $2.6B gap. Operating loss of −$822M against a net loss of −$3,460M. Roughly $2.6B of the loss sat below the operating line. FY2022 also contained the 10-for-1 stock split (effective June 29, 2022) and the $2.1B Deliverr acquisition, agreed in May 2022 and completed on July 8, 2022.

FY2023 — the logistics exit. Shopify completed the sale of the majority of Shopify Logistics to Flexport on June 6, 2023, receiving stock representing an incremental ~13% equity interest in Flexport (taking Shopify to a high-teens ownership stake); no cash consideration was disclosed in the release. This unwound the Deliverr purchase made barely a year earlier for $2.1B. The shape of the year: an operating loss of −$1,418M on $7,060M of revenue, yet net income of $132M (diluted EPS $0.10), so net income landed $1.55B above the operating result. Treat FY2023 as a distorted base year; comparisons that use it as a starting point will flatter subsequent growth.

FY2024 — flattered by $944M. Operating income $1,075M, net income $2,019M. Net income exceeded operating income by $944M. FY2024's headline EPS of $1.55 is therefore not a clean operating number.

FY2025 — held back by $237M. The reverse. Operating income rose 37% to $1,468M, yet net income fell to $1,231M and EPS fell from $1.55 to $0.94. A reader looking only at EPS would conclude 2025 was a bad year; operating income says the opposite.

2026 — swings of about $1B a quarter. Q1: operating income $382M, net loss −$581M (diluted EPS −$0.45). Q2: operating income $488M, net income $1,502M ($1.16). Q3: operating income $568M, net income $462M ($0.36), after a $127M unrealized loss on equity and other investments. All three quarters sit inside the TTM EPS of $1.63 that produces the 93.9× trailing P/E on this page.

Conclusion for valuation: Shopify's trailing P/E is close to meaningless as a signal. Over the twelve months to September 2026, net income of $2,126M sits just above operating income of $2,069M only because a loss and a gain of about $1B each cancelled out. Use gross profit, operating income and free cash flow. That is why Section 08 and the valuation discussion below price the business on gross profit rather than on earnings or revenue.

In plain English: RSI compares the size of recent monthly gains to recent monthly losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been sold hard and may be washed out. It measures momentum, not value.

Five years, and nothing to show for it. SHOP closed November 2021 at $152.18 (split-adjusted) and November 4, 2026 at $153.03. That is a price return of +0.6% in just under five years, about 0.1% a year — and Shopify pays no dividend, so price return is total return. In that time revenue went from $4,612M (FY2021) to $14,167M over the twelve months to September 2026, roughly tripling. The whole gap between those two facts is the price investors pay per dollar of business: an extraordinary multiple in 2021, a merely very high one now.

The path was violent. From the November 2021 peak of $152.18 the stock fell to $26.94 by September 2022 — a decline of 82.3% in ten months, through the 10-for-1 split in June 2022. From that low it has climbed to $153.03, a gain of +468%. An investor's entire experience of this stock is determined by which of those two points they started from, which is the single most important practical observation on this page.

Relative performance. Over the same window (November 30, 2021 close to November 4, 2026), the S&P 500 fund SPY returned +68.3% and the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ +75.7% on price, against SHOP's +0.6%. 08 Summary & Key Risks
VALUATION — ON GROSS PROFIT, NOT REVENUE
Quoting SHOP on revenue is misleading, because 77.7% of that revenue is Merchant Solutions and a large slice of Merchant Solutions revenue is card interchange that Shopify collects and immediately hands to the issuing banks. Gross profit is the honest denominator — it is what Shopify actually keeps.

At the $153.03 close on 1,286.5M shares (10-Q cover, October 30, 2026), market capitalisation is roughly $196.9B (our calculation). Cash and marketable securities of $4.73B less $188M of lease liabilities leaves net cash of $4.54B, giving an enterprise value (market value plus debt, minus cash) of about $192.3B. Against TTM gross profit of $6,728M, that is 28.6× EV / gross profit. On the Q3 2026 run-rate ($1,781M × 4 = $7,124M) it is 27.0×. For comparison, EV/Revenue is 13.6× and price-to-sales 13.9× — both roughly half the gross-profit multiple, which is exactly the distortion being corrected for.

What growth rate does 29× gross profit imply? Take a five-year horizon and assume the business ends it valued at 15× EV/gross profit — a reasonable multiple for a mature, high-quality business that needs little capital. For an investor to earn 10% a year from $153.03, enterprise value must reach roughly $310B in 2031 ($192.3B × 1.15, holding net cash and share count flat). At 15× that requires gross profit of $20.7B, up from $6.73B today — a 25.1% compound annual gross-profit growth rate, sustained for five consecutive years. At a more generous 20× the requirement falls to 18.1% a year. And to simply not lose money — flat enterprise value at 15× — gross profit must still nearly double, growing 13.8% a year.

Set that against what management is guiding: Q4 2026 gross profit dollars growing "in the high 20s," after TTM gross profit growth of 29.0% and FY2025 growth of 24.2%. The stock at $153.03 is priced for roughly today's gross-profit growth rate, unbroken, for five more years, while the multiple falls by almost half. That is not an impossible ask, but it leaves no margin for a single slowing quarter, and it explains both the $216 high target and the $130 low target on MarketBeat's panel.

Bull Case

GMV is growing 29% and revenue 32% — Shopify is taking share of global commerce, not just riding it, and it does so without competing with its own merchants the way Amazon does. Free cash flow of $2.66B TTM and a 22% margin in Q3 show the model turns growth into cash, a 26.66% ROIC shows it needs little capital to do so, and $4.73B of cash with no borrowings keeps every option open. Payments penetration passed 68% and Shop Pay GMV grew 50% in Q3, per the CFO. If enterprise wins keep landing, the high-margin Subscription line speeds up, the mix drag on gross margin eases, and revenue growth and gross-profit growth converge instead of diverging. MarketBeat's average target of $178.79 implies 16.8% upside.

Bear Case

You are paying 28.6× gross profit and 93.9× trailing earnings for a business whose take rate has not risen (3.15% → 3.10% → 3.14% this year) and whose gross margin keeps falling (50.36% → 48.07% → 47.49%) because its fastest-growing revenue is card fees that pass through to the banks at about half the margin. Trailing EPS is close to uninformative — 2026's quarters swung by about $1B between operating income and net income. The price return since November 2021 is +0.6% against +68.3% for SPY, which is what happens when a great business is bought at a great multiple. Management called part of Q3's cash-flow margin gain temporary. A 2.66 beta means a market drawdown is amplified, GMV is tied to discretionary consumer spending, Shopify Capital adds credit losses precisely when GMV falls, and shopping through AI assistants is an open threat to the value of hosting a storefront at all.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Valuation leaves no room for slowing growth. At 28.6× EV/gross profit, a 10% annual return requires 25.1% compound gross-profit growth for five straight years even if the multiple falls to 15×. A single quarter guiding gross profit to the low twenties would force a repricing — as June 2026's $114.18 month-end low showed, this stock can lose a third of its value while the business is still speeding up. The first session after the Q3 beat brought an 8.74% fall.
02
The mix shift is a lasting gross-margin headwind. In Q3 2026 Merchant Solutions grew 35% versus Subscription's 20%, at a 38.5% margin versus 79.1%. Every quarter of outperformance in the payments business lowers blended gross margin. The Q4 outlook spells out the consequence: revenue growth in the low thirties, gross profit in the high twenties. Investors who anchor on the revenue line overpay.
03
Consumer-cycle and merchant-credit exposure, magnified by a 2.66 beta. GMV is discretionary consumer spending on goods, transacted mostly by small and mid-size merchants. A downturn hits the numerator (GMV), the take (fewer high-margin add-ons), and Shopify Capital's loss rate simultaneously — three exposures to one macro variable, inside a business the market values on a software multiple that assumes none of them.
  Industry Commentary
Interchange is a permanent tax on the payments half of the business
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every card transaction carries interchange — the fee the shopper's issuing bank keeps — plus network fees to Visa and Mastercard. Shopify books the merchant's full payment fee as revenue and remits that interchange as cost of revenue, which is why Merchant Solutions runs at a 38.5% gross margin against Subscription's 79.1% (Q3 2026). This is not an efficiency problem Shopify can engineer away; it is the economics of standing between a merchant and a card network. Blended gross margin is the mix-weighted average of the two, and since the 38% half grew 35% in Q3 versus the 79% half's 20%, blended margin falls by construction. Consolidated gross margin has already gone 50.36% → 48.07% → 47.49%.
The take rate has stopped expanding
ONGOING HEADWIND
Q1 2026: $3,170M revenue on $100,743M GMV = 3.146%. Q2 2026: $3,583M on $115,567M = 3.101%. Q3 2026: $3,742M on $119,145M = 3.141%, against 3.091% a year earlier. Revenue growth is coming almost entirely from merchant volume rather than from Shopify capturing more of each dollar. The bull case has always assumed both engines run together — GMV growth and a rising take rate as more merchants add payments and Capital. If take rate stays flat, revenue growth is capped near GMV growth, and gross-profit growth sits below that because of mix. Shopify gives payments penetration only as a percentage on its calls (above 68% in Q3) and publishes no payments volume in dollars, which makes this the hardest part of the thesis to check from public data.
Enterprise and AI-assistant shopping — the bull/bear fork
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two forces could resolve the mix problem in either direction. Bull: Commerce Components sells Shopify's checkout stack to very large retailers who pay large platform fees, feeding the 79.1%-margin Subscription line and easing the mix drag — and if shoppers buy through AI assistants, being the trusted seller-of-record behind the assistant's purchase could be worth more than owning the storefront. On the Q3 call Harley Finkelstein said "AI traffic is up 2.6x year-over-year." Bear: if AI assistants and aggregators handle the purchase, the storefront Shopify hosts becomes a commodity backend, merchants question the plan fee, and Shopify is left competing purely on payment spread — the lowest-margin, most contested part of its business. The market is paying 28.6× gross profit for the bull outcome. The $130 low target on MarketBeat's panel is roughly what the bear outcome looks like.
