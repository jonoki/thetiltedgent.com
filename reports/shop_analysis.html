01 Company Overview
Shopify sells the software and financial plumbing that lets a merchant run an online (and increasingly offline) store — storefront, checkout, payments, shipping, capital, and a point-of-sale terminal. It is the largest independent commerce platform outside of the marketplace model, and unlike Amazon it does not compete with its own customers for the sale. FY2025 revenue was $11,556M, up 30.1% year over year, on top of $8,880M in FY2024 (+25.8%) and $7,060M in FY2023 (+26.1%). On a trailing-twelve-month basis through June 2026, revenue reached $13,269M, up 32.5%. CompaniesMarketCap ranks Shopify the 104th most valuable public company in the world as of August 2026.
The important thing to understand before reading anything else on this page: Shopify's revenue is not the top of its funnel. GMV — gross merchandise volume, the total dollar value of everything sold across every merchant on the platform — is. In Q2 2026 Shopify's merchants transacted $115,567M of GMV and Shopify booked $3,583M of revenue against it. That ratio, roughly 3.1%, is the take rate (the share of merchant sales Shopify keeps as its own revenue). Everything that matters about this business is either GMV growth or take-rate expansion, and Section 02 is built around that split.

02 Core Businesses
Shopify reports exactly two revenue lines, and they behave nothing like each other. Subscription Solutions is recurring plan fees — high margin, predictable, slower. Merchant Solutions is payments, shipping, capital and hardware — most of the revenue, most of the growth, and roughly half the gross margin, because a large slice of it is money that passes straight through Shopify to the card networks. Understanding that difference is the single most useful thing in this report, and it is why revenue growth and gross-profit growth do not match.

Revenue Mix
Payments (Shopify Payments), shipping labels, merchant lending (Shopify Capital), currency conversion, tax services, POS hardware and Shop Pay Installments. This is where GMV converts into dollars. Against Q2 2026 GMV of $115,567M, Merchant Solutions revenue of $2,781M implies a merchant-solutions take rate of 2.41% — roughly three-quarters of Shopify's total 3.10% take. It grew 37% in Q2 2026, faster than the company.

Here is the catch, and it is the whole point. Merchant Solutions produced $1,069M of gross profit on $2,781M of revenue — a 38.4% gross margin. Subscription Solutions produced $639M on $802M — 79.7%. The reason is interchange: the fee the card-issuing bank charges on every card transaction, typically a large majority of the total card cost. Shopify collects the full payment fee from the merchant as revenue, then remits interchange and network fees to Visa, Mastercard and the issuing banks as cost of revenue. That money is booked as revenue on the way in and cost on the way out, and Shopify keeps only the spread. So a dollar of Merchant Solutions revenue is worth about 38 cents of gross profit; a dollar of Subscription revenue is worth about 80 cents.

Because the low-margin half is growing faster (+37% vs +22%), blended gross margin falls every year even when nothing goes wrong: 50.36% (FY2024) → 48.07% (FY2025) → 47.77% (TTM). That mechanically drives a wedge between the two growth rates investors quote. In Q2 2026, revenue grew 34% but gross profit grew 31%. For the full year FY2025, revenue grew 30.1% while gross profit grew 24.2% ($4,472M → $5,555M) — a six-point gap. Management's own Q3 2026 guidance says the quiet part out loud: revenue growing at a "low-thirties percentage rate" while gross profit dollars grow at a "mid-to-high twenties percentage rate."

GPV Penetration
Shop Pay is Shopify's one-click accelerated checkout: a shopper who has bought from any Shopify merchant has their card and address on file, and can complete a purchase at any other Shopify merchant without re-typing anything. That is the flywheel — every new merchant adds shoppers to the vault, and every vaulted shopper makes every existing merchant convert better, which makes Shopify's platform worth more to the next merchant.

The commercial expression of the flywheel is attach rate — the proportion of merchants (or of GMV) that also use a given Shopify product rather than a third party. The critical one is GPV (gross payments volume) as a percentage of GMV: how much of the merchandise flowing across the platform is settled through Shopify Payments rather than through Stripe, PayPal or a bank acquirer. Every point of that penetration converts a pure-software customer into a payments customer at roughly triple the take rate.

Honest gap: Shopify did not disclose a GPV figure or a GPV-as-%-of-GMV penetration rate in the Q1 2026 or Q2 2026 releases I fetched, and I did not obtain it from any other source. No GPV number is stated on this page. What can be stated is the aggregate: take rate was 3.10% in Q2 2026 ($3,583M / $115,567M) and 3.15% in Q1 2026 ($3,170M / $100,743M). The absence of visible take-rate expansion quarter over quarter is itself worth noting — the recent revenue acceleration is coming from GMV, not from taking more per dollar.

Debt / Equity
Shopify Capital advances working capital to merchants and takes repayment as a fixed percentage of their future sales. Because Shopify sees the merchant's entire order flow and settles the payments, its underwriting information is genuinely better than a bank's — and repayment is collected at source. That is a real structural edge.

It is also lending, and it sits inside a company the market values at a software multiple. A software business has essentially no balance-sheet loss content; a lender does. If merchant defaults rise in a consumer downturn, the losses land in Merchant Solutions cost of revenue and compress a gross margin that is already the diluted half of the business. Shopify's corporate balance sheet is pristine — $4.95B total cash against $178M total debt, debt-to-equity of 0.01, current ratio 5.35 — so this is not a solvency question. It is an earnings-quality question: a slice of Shopify's gross profit is credit spread, not software margin, and it should not be capitalised at the same multiple.

Honest gap: Shopify did not break out Capital originations, the outstanding loan balance, or a loss rate in the releases fetched. No figures are invented here.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
SOURCES OF MOAT
Shop Pay vaulted-shopper network Merchant switching cost App & theme ecosystem Payments data → underwriting Not a marketplace competitor Checkout conversion advantage Omnichannel (POS + online)
Shopify's moat is a two-sided network dressed up as software. On the merchant side, once a store's catalogue, theme, apps, historical order data, payments settlement and staff training all live inside Shopify, migrating means rebuilding the business during a period when it cannot sell — a switching cost measured in weeks of revenue, not licence fees. On the shopper side, Shop Pay's stored credentials mean every additional Shopify merchant makes checkout faster for shoppers who have already bought elsewhere on the platform, and faster checkout measurably converts better. The two sides reinforce: merchants join for conversion, which grows the shopper vault, which improves conversion for the next merchant. Layered on top is an informational moat that is genuinely hard to replicate — because Shopify both hosts the store and settles the payment, it can underwrite Shopify Capital on live order flow rather than on filed accounts, and collect repayment before the merchant ever touches the cash. And critically, unlike Amazon, Shopify does not sell against its own customers, which is the single most-cited reason brands choose it. The vulnerability in all of this is that the moat protects the merchant relationship, not the take rate — and the take rate is what the valuation depends on.
KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. Take-rate compression, not GMV loss, is the real risk. Take rate was 3.15% in Q1 2026 and 3.10% in Q2 2026 — no visible expansion. Payments pricing is competitive and largely a spread business over interchange; if Stripe, Adyen or PayPal price aggressively for Shopify merchants, Shopify loses margin on volume it still hosts. A ten-basis-point take-rate change on $115.6B of quarterly GMV is roughly $116M of quarterly revenue at near-100% incremental margin — larger than most things management can control.

2. Mix shift is structurally deflating gross margin. Blended gross margin has fallen 50.36% → 48.07% → 47.77% across FY2024, FY2025 and TTM, entirely because the low-margin half grows faster. This is not a mistake and not reversible without slowing Merchant Solutions. It means revenue growth systematically overstates economic growth, and any investor anchoring on the revenue line is paying for pass-through interchange.

3. Consumer-discretionary and small-business credit exposure. GMV is downstream of consumer spending on discretionary goods, and Shopify's merchant base skews toward small and mid-size businesses — the cohort most exposed in a downturn. Shopify Capital adds a second-order exposure: the same recession that reduces GMV also raises merchant default rates on advances that Shopify has already funded. Beta of 2.59 is the market's summary of this.

4. Agentic and AI-mediated commerce could disintermediate the storefront. If shoppers increasingly buy through AI assistants and aggregators rather than by visiting a brand's site, the value of hosting the storefront falls and the value of being in the payment path may or may not survive. Shopify is positioning to be the merchant-of-record layer behind such flows, but this is an unresolved structural question, not a solved one — and it was explicitly cited in Jefferies' 2026 bull case on the stock.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

This is the most important section on the page for anyone quoting the P/E. Shopify's net income and its operating income routinely diverge by hundreds of millions to billions of dollars, and the gap is almost entirely below the operating line — marks on financial assets and tax movements, not trading performance. The figures below are all directly sourced; the arithmetic is mine.

FY2022 — a $3.0B gap. Operating loss of −$476M against a net loss of −$3,460M. Roughly $3.0B of the loss sat below the operating line. FY2022 also contained the 10-for-1 stock split (effective June 29, 2022) and the $2.1B Deliverr acquisition made in May 2022.

FY2023 — the logistics exit. Shopify completed the sale of the majority of Shopify Logistics to Flexport on June 6, 2023, receiving stock representing an incremental ~13% equity interest in Flexport (taking Shopify to a high-teens ownership stake); no cash consideration was disclosed in the release. This unwound the Deliverr purchase made barely a year earlier for $2.1B. Shopify stated it would detail the P&L impact on its Q2 2023 call. I did not obtain the specific impairment or loss-on-sale figure from a fetched source, so no number is stated here. What is verifiable is the shape of the year: FY2023 operating income of $260M on $7,060M of revenue — a 3.68% operating margin, versus 14.04% the following year — and net income of only $132M, i.e. net income landed below operating income. Treat FY2023 as a distorted base year; comparisons that use it as a starting point will flatter subsequent growth.

FY2024 — flattered by $772M. Operating income $1,247M, net income $2,019M. Net income exceeded operating income by $772M. FY2024's headline EPS of $1.55 is therefore not a clean operating number.

FY2025 — penalised by $667M. The reverse. Operating income rose 52% to $1,898M, yet net income fell to $1,231M and EPS fell from $1.55 to $0.94. A reader looking only at EPS would conclude 2025 was a bad year. Operationally it was the best year in the company's history.

Q2 2026 — a $1.0B swing in a single quarter. Operating income $488M; net income $1,502M. Over $1.0B of the quarter's profit came from below the operating line. That single quarter is inside the TTM EPS of $1.48 that produces the 99.6× trailing P/E on this page.

Conclusion for valuation: Shopify's trailing P/E is close to meaningless as a signal. Over the TTM, net income of $1,928M actually sits below operating income of $2,366M despite the enormous Q2 gain — the volatility runs in both directions. Use gross profit, operating income and free cash flow. That is why Section 08 and the valuation discussion below price the business on gross profit rather than on earnings or revenue.

In plain English: RSI compares the size of recent monthly gains to recent monthly losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been sold hard and may be washed out. It measures momentum, not value.

Five years, and almost nothing to show for it. SHOP closed September 2021 at $135.58 (split-adjusted) and August 2026 at $147.37. That is a total return of +8.7% over five years, a compound annual rate of roughly 1.7% — and Shopify pays no dividend, so price return is total return. In that same five years revenue went from $4,612M (FY2021) to a $13,269M TTM run-rate, nearly tripling. The entire gap between those two facts is multiple compression: investors were paying an extraordinary multiple in 2021 and are paying a merely very high one now.

The path was violent. From the November 2021 peak of $152.18 the stock fell to $26.94 by September 2022 — a decline of 82.3% in ten months, through the 10-for-1 split in June 2022. From that low it has compounded to $147.37, a gain of +447%. An investor's entire experience of this stock is determined by which of those two points they started from, which is the single most important practical observation on this page.

Relative performance. I did not fetch index return data for the NASDAQ-100 or the S&P 500 over this window, so no comparative index return is stated here rather than estimated. What can be said without a number: SHOP's beta of 2.59 means the stock has historically moved about two and a half times the market, and a +8.7% five-year return from a high-beta growth name over a period that included a major technology drawdown and recovery is, on its face, a poor risk-adjusted outcome. 08 Summary & Key Risks
VALUATION — ON GROSS PROFIT, NOT REVENUE
Quoting SHOP on revenue is misleading, because 77.6% of that revenue is Merchant Solutions and a large slice of Merchant Solutions revenue is card interchange that Shopify collects and immediately hands to the issuing banks. Gross profit is the honest denominator — it is what Shopify actually keeps.

At the $147.37 close on 1.29B shares, market capitalisation is roughly $190.1B. Net cash is $4.95B less $178M of debt, or $4.77B, giving an enterprise value of about $185.3B. Against TTM gross profit of $6,338M, that is 29.2× EV / gross profit. On the Q2 2026 run-rate ($1,708M × 4 = $6,832M) it is 27.1×. For comparison, the headline EV/Revenue is 13.9× and the P/S is 14.29× — both roughly half the gross-profit multiple, which is exactly the distortion being corrected for.

What growth rate does 29× gross profit imply? Take a five-year horizon and assume the business exits at 15× EV/gross profit — a defensible terminal multiple for a mature, high-quality, capital-light compounder. For an investor to earn 10% a year from $147.37, enterprise value must reach roughly $301B in 2031. At 15× that requires gross profit of $20.1B, up from $6.34B today — a 26.1% compound annual gross-profit growth rate, sustained for five consecutive years. At a more generous 20× exit multiple the requirement falls to 18.9% a year. And to simply not lose money — flat enterprise value at a 15× exit — gross profit must still nearly double, growing 14.3% a year.

Set that against what management is actually guiding: Q3 2026 gross profit dollars growing at a "mid-to-high twenties percentage rate," and FY2025 gross profit that grew 24.2%. The stock at $147.37 is priced for roughly a continuation of today's gross-profit growth rate, unbroken, for five more years, with a full multiple de-rating along the way. That is not an impossible ask — Shopify has grown gross profit at better than 24% for three straight years — but it leaves no margin for a single decelerating quarter, and it explains both the $220 bull target and the $110 bear target in the analyst range.

Bull Case

GMV is compounding at 32% and revenue at 34% — Shopify is taking share of global commerce, not just riding it, and it does so without competing with its own merchants the way Amazon does. Free cash flow of $2.35B TTM at an 18% quarterly margin proves the model converts growth into cash, ROIC of 26.24% proves it does so without capital, and $4.95B of cash against $178M of debt means every option stays open. If enterprise Commerce Components lands and Shop Pay penetration keeps rising, the high-margin Subscription line reaccelerates, the mix drag on gross margin reverses, and revenue growth and gross-profit growth converge upward instead of diverging. Consensus at $171.15 implies 16.1% upside and the sell-side has been raising, not cutting.

Bear Case

You are paying 29.2× gross profit and 99.6× trailing earnings for a business whose take rate went down quarter over quarter (3.15% → 3.10%) and whose gross margin has fallen three years running (50.36% → 48.07% → 47.77%) because its fastest-growing revenue is half-margin pass-through. Trailing EPS is close to uninformative — Q2 2026 alone carried a $1.0B gap between operating income and net income. The five-year total return is +8.7%, or 1.7% a year, which is what happens when a great business is bought at a great multiple. Beta of 2.59 means a market drawdown is amplified, GMV is levered to discretionary consumer spending, Shopify Capital adds credit losses precisely when GMV falls, and agentic commerce is an unresolved threat to the value of hosting a storefront at all.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
Valuation leaves no room for deceleration. At 29.2× EV/gross profit, a 10% annual return requires 26.1% compound gross-profit growth for five straight years even after de-rating to 15×. A single quarter guiding gross profit to the low twenties would force a repricing — as June 2026's $114.18 low showed, this stock can lose a third of its value on multiple alone while the business is still accelerating.
02
The mix shift is a structural, permanent gross-margin headwind. Merchant Solutions grew 37% versus Subscription's 22%, at 38.4% margin versus 79.7%. Every quarter of outperformance in the payments business mathematically lowers blended gross margin. Q3 guidance already spells out the consequence: revenue in the low thirties, gross profit in the mid-to-high twenties. Investors who anchor on the revenue line are systematically overpaying.
03
Consumer-cycle and merchant-credit exposure, magnified by a 2.59 beta. GMV is discretionary consumer spending on goods, transacted mostly by small and mid-size merchants. A downturn hits the numerator (GMV), the take (fewer high-margin add-ons), and Shopify Capital's loss rate simultaneously — three exposures to one macro variable, inside a business the market values on a software multiple that assumes none of them.
  Industry Commentary
Interchange is a permanent tax on the payments half of the business
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every card transaction carries interchange — the fee the shopper's issuing bank keeps — plus network fees to Visa and Mastercard. Shopify books the merchant's full payment fee as revenue and remits that interchange as cost of revenue, which is why Merchant Solutions runs at a 38.4% gross margin against Subscription's 79.7%. This is not an efficiency problem Shopify can engineer away; it is the economics of standing between a merchant and a card network. It caps blended gross margin at roughly the mix-weighted average of the two, and since the 38% half is growing at 37% versus the 80% half's 22%, blended margin falls by construction. Consolidated gross margin has already gone 50.36% → 48.07% → 47.77%.
The take rate has stopped expanding
ONGOING HEADWIND
Q1 2026: $3,170M revenue on $100,743M GMV = 3.146%. Q2 2026: $3,583M on $115,567M = 3.101%. Two quarters is not a trend, but it is a datapoint that the recent revenue acceleration is coming entirely from merchant volume rather than from Shopify capturing more of each dollar. The bull case has always assumed both engines run together — GMV growth and take-rate expansion from payments and Capital attach. If take rate is flat or drifting down, revenue growth is capped at GMV growth, and gross-profit growth is capped below that by mix. Shopify did not disclose GPV or a Shopify Payments penetration rate in either release fetched, which makes this the hardest part of the thesis to verify from public data.
Enterprise and agentic commerce — the bull/bear fork
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two forces could resolve the mix problem in either direction. Bull: Commerce Components sells Shopify's checkout stack to very large retailers who pay large platform fees, feeding the 79.7%-margin Subscription line and reversing the mix drag — and MarketBeat's own coverage frames Jefferies' 2026 upgrade explicitly around Shopify's positioning for agentic commerce, where being the trusted merchant-of-record behind an AI assistant's purchase could be worth more than owning the storefront. Bear: if AI assistants and aggregators mediate the purchase, the storefront Shopify hosts becomes a commodity backend, merchants question the plan fee, and Shopify is left competing purely on payment spread — the lowest-margin, most contested part of its business. The market is currently paying 29.2× gross profit for the bull resolution. The $110 low target on the street is roughly what the bear resolution looks like.
