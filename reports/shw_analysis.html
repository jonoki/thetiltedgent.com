01 Company Overview
Sherwin-Williams develops, manufactures, distributes and sells paint, coatings and related products to professional, industrial, commercial and retail customers across North and South America, the Caribbean, Europe, Asia and Australia. It is best understood not as a chemicals company but as a distribution business that happens to make paint: roughly 4,853 company-owned Paint Stores Group locations put the product, the color-matching, the credit terms and the same-day availability directly in front of the professional contractor, bypassing the retail shelf that every rival must rent. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $23.574B, up 2.1% year over year — a record top line achieved in a demand environment management itself described as soft — with GAAP diluted EPS of $10.26 and adjusted diluted EPS of $11.43.
On a TTM basis the company now runs $24.91B of revenue (+7.0% YoY) and $2.68B of net income. At a $77.50B market capitalization SHW ranks #167 in the S&P 500 by market value. Third-quarter 2026, reported before the market opened on October 27, was a sales beat and a profit squeeze: consolidated net sales rose 7.9% to $6.860B, but gross margin fell to 47.8% from 49.2% on higher raw-material and supply-chain costs and the lower-margin Suvinil business, and adjusted EPS of $3.63 (up 1.1%) came in $0.02 under the $3.65 Nasdaq consensus. Management narrowed full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $11.85–$12.15 from $11.80–$12.20 and said customer sentiment and its leading indicators point to muted demand into 2027. The stock fell 2.73% on the day and closed October 28 at $320.80, 4.3% below its close before the report.

The moat is owned distribution, and it is unusually durable because it is expensive and slow to replicate. A professional painting crew's economics are dominated by labor, not material: a wasted hour driving to a second store to match a color costs more than the paint itself. Sherwin's ~4,853 store network puts a location inside a short drive of nearly any US job site, with contractor-specific credit accounts, jobsite delivery, color matching and — critically — consistency of formulation across stores, so a crew painting a 300-unit apartment complex gets the same batch behavior in every city. That convenience translates into pricing power: PSG pushed mid-single-digit price again in Q3 2026 on top of positive volume, and an 8% increase took effect September 1, 2026, in a market where end demand is soft. Owning the shelf also means Sherwin keeps the retail spread that Behr surrenders to Home Depot and Valspar's retail line partly surrenders to Lowe's — the arithmetic reason PSG earned a 24.3% segment margin in Q3 2026 while CBG earned 15.6% and PCG 13.4%. Layer on scale purchasing of titanium dioxide, resins and solvents, and the result is a trailing gross margin of 48.60% — roughly 19 points above a typical specialty-chemicals peer. That combination is what the market pays a premium multiple for: SHW trades near 29.5x trailing earnings versus PPG at 16.9x and RPM at 18.4x for broadly similar end markets, and the delta is almost entirely the distribution moat.
Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Housing turnover dependency — asymmetric across segments. Existing-home sales ran at a 3.99M seasonally adjusted annual rate in September 2026, up 1.5% month over month but down 2.2% year over year, with the 30-year fixed mortgage averaging 6.86% (Freddie Mac, as reported by the National Association of Realtors). DIY paint demand and new-residential volume track that number closely — people paint when they move. Consumer Brands is the direct casualty; Paint Stores is partly insulated because residential repaint and commercial maintenance are driven by the age of the housing stock rather than by transactions. But a multi-year freeze in turnover caps the volume line for the whole architectural business and forces the company to grow on price and share alone, which is exactly what the 2025–2026 numbers show.
▸
Raw-material and tariff cost cycle. Paint economics are levered to titanium dioxide, acrylic resins, solvents and packaging — petrochemical derivatives with their own cycle. In Q3 2026 higher raw-material and supply-chain costs, plus the lower-margin Suvinil business, cut consolidated gross margin to 47.8% from 49.2% despite mid-single-digit pricing, and on the October 27 call CEO Heidi Petz said "we expect raw material inflation to be in the low to mid single digit range for 2027". Tariffs on imported inputs and on packaging compound the problem. Sherwin has historically recovered input inflation with a lag via price increases, and the September 1 8% PSG increase is that mechanism operating — but the lag itself is a margin risk, and pushing price into a soft-volume market invites trade-down and share loss at the margin.
▸
Balance-sheet thinness engineered by buybacks. Relentless repurchase has shrunk shareholders' equity to $3.98B at September 30, 2026 against $13.98B of debt including operating lease liabilities ($11.80B of borrowings plus $2.19B of leases, Q3 10-Q; StockAnalysis’s total is $14.80B) — a debt-to-equity ratio of 3.52x and a current ratio of just 0.71. The 63.7% ROE is mostly a product of the small equity base; ROIC of 16.6% is the honest number and is good but not extraordinary. Net debt is roughly $13.72B against $4.78B of TTM EBITDA (StockAnalysis), about 2.9x on those two figures, with interest coverage of 7.49x. This is a manageable structure at current rates and cash generation, but it removes the cushion: a genuine demand shock hits earnings and leverage ratios simultaneously.
▸
Channel concentration and legacy legal exposure. Consumer Brands' economics depend heavily on a small number of home-center relationships, notably the Lowe's partnership — a renegotiation or loss would be a step-change to that segment, and Behr's exclusive Home Depot arrangement shows how binary these relationships are. Separately, Sherwin-Williams carries long-running environmental remediation obligations (a net $15.3M environmental provision in FY2025) and historical lead-pigment litigation matters disclosed in its filings.

Notable One-Off Items

The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap at Sherwin-Williams is large and recurring enough that it deserves explicit accounting. Valspar purchase-accounting amortization is the biggest single item: $1.03 per share pre-tax ($0.78 after tax) in FY2025 and $0.21 per share in Q3 2026 ($0.20 a year earlier), with $0.82 per share embedded in the FY2026 GAAP guidance. It is a non-cash charge from a 2017 acquisition, but it has recurred every year since — treating it as purely "one-off" flatters the earnings picture, and it falls disproportionately on Performance Coatings, which is why that segment's reported 13.4% Q3 margin becomes 16.2% adjusted.

Restructuring and severance: FY2025 carried $111.0M pre-tax ($0.34 per share after tax). Q3 2026 severance and restructuring was $0.04 per share, the same as a year earlier, and FY2026 guidance now embeds $0.11 per share. On the October 27 call CEO Heidi Petz said "We expect cost savings of approximately $75 million in 2027," about $25 million of it from the second- and third-quarter actions.

Acquisition effects: the Suvinil Brazilian architectural paints acquisition (October 2025) is the main driver of Consumer Brands' 21.7% Q3 2026 net sales growth. That number should not be read as organic momentum — the company says North American demand was soft. Suvinil also weighs on margins: management says nine-month gross margin was flat year over year once Suvinil's dilution is excluded, against a reported 48.6% versus 49.0%.

Currency: translation helped Consumer Brands' Q3 2026 sales, but foreign-currency transaction losses hurt that segment's profit, so the two effects pull in opposite directions; strip both out when judging underlying volume.

Environmental and litigation: FY2025 included a net $15.3M environmental provision — small relative to $23.6B of revenue. Historical lead-pigment litigation remains a disclosed contingency in the company's filings (see the most recent 10-Q commitments-and-contingencies footnote).

Five-Year Trajectory

From the October 2021 month-end close of $316.61 to the October 28, 2026 close of $320.80, SHW returned +1.3% on price — about 0.3% a year, with dividends adding just under another percentage point a year at the current yield of about 1%. Five years of essentially no price return for a business of this quality, and the path was far rougher than the endpoints suggest. The stock peaked at a $352.16 monthly close in December 2021, fell 41.9% to a $204.75 trough in September 2022 as the Fed's tightening cycle repriced every housing-levered asset, then nearly doubled — +94.1% — into a ten-year monthly-close high of $397.40 in November 2024 on rate-cut optimism. It has spent the twenty-three months since going sideways-to-down, closing 2025 at $324.03 and moving between $303.84 and $362.59 at month-ends through 2026. The trailing 52-week price change is -9.49% while TTM EPS grew 6.2%: multiple compression (investors paying less for each dollar of earnings) from a very high base rather than business deterioration.

Technically the setup is weak but not washed out. At $320.80 the stock sits below both its 50-day moving average of $330.11 and its 200-day of $332.97, with the 50-day below the 200-day. It sits 35% of the way up the $289.86–$377.77 52-week range, 15.1% below the high and 10.7% above the low, and the 14-day RSI on daily closes is 42.6 — neither stretched nor washed out. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Sherwin-Williams keeps showing the moat works in a bad demand environment: with existing-home sales at a 3.99M annual rate, it still grew Q3 2026 sales 7.9% with 3.9% Paint Stores same-store sales, and management says gross margin was flat year to date once Suvinil is excluded. The 8% price increase effective September 1 is, in CEO Heidi Petz's words, "tracking as planned", and management expects about $75M of cost savings in 2027. A 48.60% gross margin, 16.64% ROIC and $3.08B of free cash flow fund a buyback that bought back 7.1M shares in the first nine months of 2026. The trailing multiple is down to 29.5x from 32.6x at the last edition, and 24.1x on FY2027 consensus EPS; if mortgage rates ease toward 6% and housing turnover normalizes, the extra profit that comes from selling more paint through the same stores and plants, absent for four years, arrives on top of price and share gains already in the run rate.

Bear Case

You are paying 29.51x trailing and 24.08x forward earnings — a PEG of 2.33 — for a business growing revenue at 7.0% with help from an acquisition, about 1.7 times PPG's 16.90x. The trailing twelve months delivered a -9.49% return, and the stock sits 19.3% below its November 2024 month-end high nearly two years on. Q3 showed the cost side biting: gross margin fell 140bp to 47.8%, Consumer Brands' adjusted margin fell to 18.3% from 23.3%, adjusted EPS grew only 1.1%, and management guided to muted demand into 2027 with low-to-mid single-digit raw-material inflation next year. The balance sheet has no cushion — $13.98B of debt including leases against $3.98B of equity, a 0.71 current ratio and $264.2M of cash. If price increases fail to stick into soft volume, or housing stays frozen, both earnings and the multiple compress together.

  Industry Commentary
Housing Turnover Lock-In CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the defining macro variable for Sherwin-Williams and it has been working against the company for four years. September 2026 existing-home sales ran at a 3.99M seasonally adjusted annual rate — up 1.5% month over month but down 2.2% year over year — with the 30-year fixed mortgage averaging 6.86%, up from 6.67% in August, and inventory at 4.8 months' supply. The median existing-home price of $417,500 was up 1.3%, a 39th consecutive month of annual price gains, which is precisely the problem: prices high, rates high, transactions frozen. Households holding low fixed-rate mortgages have little reason to move, and every transaction that does not happen is a house that does not get painted before listing and does not get repainted by its new owner. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun put the latest turn plainly: "Further increases in mortgage rates in recent weeks mean fewer buyers can qualify to purchase a home." Critically, the damage is asymmetric: DIY and new-residential volume, concentrated in Consumer Brands, take the direct hit, while residential repaint and commercial maintenance in Paint Stores are driven by the age of the housing stock rather than by transactions and have proven far more resilient. That asymmetry is why PSG still posted 3.9% same-store sales growth in Q3 2026 in a market this soft, while management described continued softness in DIY.
Raw-Material and Tariff Cost Pressure ONGOING HEADWIND
Paint is a petrochemical product wearing a consumer label. Titanium dioxide, acrylic resins, solvents and packaging drive cost of goods sold, and management said higher raw-material and supply-chain costs weighed on Q3 2026, when gross margin came in at 47.8% versus 49.2% a year earlier despite mid-single-digit price increases in Paint Stores; on the call it guided to raw-material inflation in the low to mid single digits for 2027. Tariffs on imported inputs and packaging compound the pressure. The historical playbook is recovery-with-a-lag through list price increases, and the 8% Paint Stores Group increase effective September 1, 2026 is that mechanism running in real time — but pushing 8% into a market where volume is only growing low-single-digits is an aggressive bet on customer captivity. The margin history frames the stakes: gross margin bottomed at 42.10% in FY2022 during the last input-cost spike and has since climbed to 48.60% on a trailing basis, meaning about six and a half points of margin — and a very large share of the earnings recovery since 2022 — is cost-cycle rather than structural. That is recoverable, but it is also losable.
Share Gains Through the Owned Store Network EMERGING TAILWIND
The structural story running underneath the cycle is consolidation of the professional contractor toward Sherwin's network. Company-owned-store distribution accounted for 40.66% of 2025 US paints and coatings revenue, and that is the channel Sherwin is built on: 4,853 Paint Stores plus 310 Consumer Brands stores and 317 Performance Coatings branches. In a soft market this channel gains share, because the independent dealer and the smaller regional brand lose the contractor first when jobs get scarce and every hour counts. The evidence is in the numbers: North America architectural coatings is a $25.31B market growing at 3.30% CAGR, and Sherwin's Paint Stores Group grew net sales 4.7% with 3.9% same-store sales in Q3 2026 — comfortably above market. In the Q3 release CEO Heidi Petz said the company kept winning customers and grew sales in all three segments while market volumes were muted, and that spread supports her.

Bull versus bear implications. The bull reading is that Sherwin is compounding share and price through a long housing slump, so that when turnover eventually unlocks, volume operating leverage lands on a structurally larger base — and the 24.08x forward multiple is a reasonable price for that optionality. The bear reading is that share gains and price increases are the only growth levers currently working, that Q3's headline growth was flattered by the Suvinil acquisition and currency translation while margins fell, and that a 29.51x trailing multiple with a 2.33 PEG on 7.0% revenue growth prices in an unlock that may be years away. Both readings are consistent with the same facts; the disagreement is entirely about how long an investor is willing to wait, which is exactly why the Street splits 15 Buy against 14 Hold with a $329–$420 target range.
