01 Company Overview
Sherwin-Williams develops, manufactures, distributes and sells paint, coatings and related products to professional, industrial, commercial and retail customers across North and South America, the Caribbean, Europe, Asia and Australia. It is best understood not as a chemicals company but as a distribution business that happens to make paint: roughly 4,841 company-owned Paint Stores Group locations put the product, the color-matching, the credit terms and the same-day availability directly in front of the professional contractor, bypassing the retail shelf that every rival must rent. Fiscal 2025 revenue was $23.574B, up 2.1% year over year — a record top line achieved in a demand environment management itself described as soft — with GAAP diluted EPS of $10.26 and adjusted diluted EPS of $11.43.
On a TTM basis the company now runs $24.41B of revenue (+5.8% YoY) and $2.69B of net income. At an $85.33B market capitalization SHW ranks #294 globally and #148 among US companies by market value. Second-quarter 2026, reported July 28, was materially better than the run-rate: consolidated net sales of $6.789B rose 7.5%, adjusted EPS of $3.70 beat the $3.52 consensus, and management raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $11.80–$12.20 from the $11.50–$11.90 range given in January.

The moat is owned distribution, and it is unusually durable because it is expensive and slow to replicate. A professional painting crew's economics are dominated by labor, not material: a wasted hour driving to a second store to match a color costs more than the paint itself. Sherwin's ~4,841 store network puts a location inside a short drive of nearly any US job site, with contractor-specific credit accounts, jobsite delivery, color matching and — critically — consistency of formulation across stores, so a crew painting a 300-unit apartment complex gets the same batch behavior in every city. That convenience translates into pricing power: PSG pushed mid-single-digit price in Q2 2026 on top of positive volume, and put through a further 8% increase effective September 1, 2026, in a market where end demand is soft. Owning the shelf also means Sherwin keeps the retail spread that Behr surrenders to Home Depot and Valspar's retail line partly surrenders to Lowe's — the arithmetic reason PSG earns a 24.6% segment margin while CBG earns 16.1% and PCG 13.9%. Layer on scale purchasing of titanium dioxide, resins and solvents, and the result is a gross margin of 48.98% — roughly 19 points above a typical specialty-chemicals peer. That combination is what the market pays a premium multiple for: SHW trades near 32.6x trailing earnings versus PPG at 16.2x and RPM at 21.6x for broadly similar end markets, and the delta is almost entirely the distribution moat.
Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Housing turnover dependency — asymmetric across segments. Existing-home sales ran at a 4.06M seasonally adjusted annual rate in July 2026, down 1.7% month over month and up only 0.7% year over year, against a 30-year fixed mortgage averaging 6.54%. DIY paint demand and new-residential volume track that number closely — people paint when they move. Consumer Brands is the direct casualty; Paint Stores is partly insulated because residential repaint and commercial maintenance are driven by the age of the housing stock rather than by transactions. But a multi-year freeze in turnover caps the volume line for the whole architectural business and forces the company to grow on price and share alone, which is exactly what the 2025–2026 numbers show.
▸
Raw-material and tariff cost cycle. Paint economics are levered to titanium dioxide, acrylic resins, solvents and packaging — petrochemical derivatives with their own cycle. Management flagged a "moderate rise" in raw material costs pressuring gross margin in Q2 2026, where consolidated gross margin slipped to 49.2% from 49.4% a year earlier despite mid-single-digit pricing. Tariffs on imported inputs and on packaging compound the problem. Sherwin has historically recovered input inflation with a lag via price increases, and the September 1 8% PSG increase is that mechanism operating — but the lag itself is a margin risk, and pushing price into a soft-volume market invites trade-down and share loss at the margin.
▸
Balance-sheet thinness engineered by buybacks. Relentless repurchase has shrunk shareholders' equity to $3.85B against $15.03B of total debt — a debt-to-equity ratio of 3.90x and a current ratio of just 0.73. The famous 65.1% ROE is substantially a denominator artifact; ROIC of 16.4% is the honest number and is good but not extraordinary. Net debt is roughly $14.74B against $4.7B of TTM EBITDA (2.73x), with interest coverage of 7.71x. This is a manageable structure at current rates and cash generation, but it removes the cushion: a genuine demand shock hits earnings and leverage ratios simultaneously.
▸
Channel concentration and legacy legal exposure. Consumer Brands' economics depend heavily on a small number of home-center relationships, notably the Lowe's partnership — a renegotiation or loss would be a step-change to that segment, and Behr's exclusive Home Depot arrangement shows how binary these relationships are. Separately, Sherwin-Williams carries long-running environmental remediation obligations (a net $15.3M environmental provision in FY2025) and historical lead-pigment litigation matters disclosed in its filings. Verification note: no current lead-pigment reserve balance was obtainable from a live source for this build, so no figure is asserted here — check the latest 10-Q contingencies footnote before sizing that exposure.

Notable One-Off Items

The GAAP-versus-adjusted gap at Sherwin-Williams is large and recurring enough that it deserves explicit accounting. Valspar purchase-accounting amortization is the biggest single item: $1.03 per share pre-tax ($0.78 after tax) in FY2025 and $0.20 per share in Q2 2026, with $0.81 per share embedded in the FY2026 GAAP guidance. It is a non-cash charge from a 2017 acquisition, but it has now recurred for nine straight years — treating it as purely "one-off" flatters the earnings picture, and it falls disproportionately on Performance Coatings, which is why that segment's reported 14.3% Q2 margin becomes 17.3% adjusted.

Restructuring and severance: FY2025 carried $111.0M pre-tax ($0.34 per share after tax). Q2 2026 severance and restructuring was a much smaller $0.07 per share, down from $0.18 in Q2 2025, with the actions expected to deliver roughly $17M of annualized savings. FY2026 guidance embeds $0.07 per share of restructuring. The trend is decelerating, which is a genuine positive.

Acquisition effects: the Suvinil Brazilian architectural paints acquisition is the dominant driver of Consumer Brands' 21.5% Q2 2026 net sales growth. That number should not be read as organic momentum — strip the acquisition and North American CBG growth is far more modest. Suvinil is also why CBG's reported segment margin (21.6%) sits nearly three points below adjusted (24.5%).

Currency: a genuine tailwind in the most recent quarter — +1.6% to Consumer Brands net sales and +2.0% to Performance Coatings. That reverses if the dollar strengthens, and it is worth stripping out when judging underlying volume.

Environmental and litigation: FY2025 included a net $15.3M environmental provision — small relative to $23.6B of revenue. Historical lead-pigment litigation remains a disclosed contingency in the company's filings; no current reserve balance was verifiable from a live source for this build and none is estimated here. Five-Year Trajectory

From the September 2021 monthly close of $267.94 to the August 19, 2026 close of $353.59, SHW returned +31.97% on price — roughly a 5.7% annualized price CAGR, with dividends adding on the order of another percentage point per year at the current sub-1% yield. That is a positive but distinctly unspectacular five years for a business of this quality, and the path was far rougher than the endpoints suggest. The stock peaked at a $337.87 monthly close in December 2021, fell 41.4% to a $197.83 trough in September 2022 as the Fed's tightening cycle repriced every housing-levered asset, then nearly doubled — +98.2% — into an all-time monthly-close high of $392.01 in November 2024 on rate-cut optimism. It has spent the twenty-one months since going sideways-to-down, closing 2025 at $322.48 and trading in a $303.84–$360.85 band through the first eight months of 2026. Net of all that, the trailing 52-week price change is -1.29%: two years of essentially zero return while earnings ground higher, which is multiple compression from a very high base rather than business deterioration.

Technically the setup is constructive but not extended. At $353.59 the stock sits above both its 50-day moving average of $337.87 and its 200-day of $334.05, with the 50-day above the 200-day — a positive configuration. It sits 71% of the way up the $289.86–$379.65 52-week range, 6.9% below the high and 22.0% above the low. The +2.41% move on August 19 fits the post-Q2 recovery pattern.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Sherwin-Williams is proving the moat works in the worst possible demand environment: with existing-home sales stuck at a 4.06M annual rate, it still delivered 7.5% consolidated growth and 4.2% Paint Stores same-store sales in Q2 2026, beat consensus by $0.18, and raised full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $11.80–$12.20. The 8% price increase effective September 1 tests — and so far validates — genuine pricing power that only owned distribution confers. A 48.98% gross margin, 16.39% ROIC and $3.21B of free cash flow fund a buyback that has taken share count down 1.78% year over year. If mortgage rates ease toward 6% and housing turnover normalizes, operating leverage on volume that has been absent for four years arrives on top of price and share gains already in the run rate.

Bear Case

You are paying 32.62x trailing and 27.71x forward earnings — a PEG of 2.37 — for a business growing revenue at 5.8%, roughly double the multiple of PPG at 16.19x on comparable growth. The trailing twelve months delivered a -1.29% return precisely because that premium has been compressing, and the stock still sits 9.8% below its November 2024 high nearly two years on. Growth quality is questionable: Q2's headline was flattered by the Suvinil acquisition (most of Consumer Brands' 21.5%) and by favorable FX (+1.6% CBG, +2.0% PCG), while gross margin actually declined 20bps on raw-material inflation. The balance sheet has no cushion — $15.03B of debt against $3.85B of equity, a 0.73 current ratio and $293.5M of cash. If price increases fail to stick into soft volume, or housing stays frozen, both earnings and the multiple compress together.

  Industry Commentary
Housing Turnover Lock-In CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the defining macro variable for Sherwin-Williams and it has been working against the company for four years. July 2026 existing-home sales ran at a 4.06M seasonally adjusted annual rate — down 1.7% month over month, up only 0.7% year over year — with the 30-year fixed mortgage averaging 6.54% and inventory at 4.6 months' supply. The median existing-home price of $434,100 was up 2.0%, marking a 37th consecutive month of annual price gains, which is precisely the problem: prices high, rates high, transactions frozen. Millions of households holding sub-4% mortgages have no rational reason to move, and every transaction that does not happen is a house that does not get painted before listing and does not get repainted by its new owner. NAR's chief economist has framed the relief threshold explicitly — affordability improves significantly if rates return near 6%. Critically, the damage is asymmetric: DIY and new-residential volume, concentrated in Consumer Brands, take the direct hit, while residential repaint and commercial maintenance in Paint Stores are driven by the age of the housing stock rather than by transactions and have proven far more resilient. That asymmetry is why PSG still posted 4.2% same-store sales growth in a market this soft.
Raw-Material and Tariff Cost Pressure ONGOING HEADWIND
Paint is a petrochemical product wearing a consumer label. Titanium dioxide, acrylic resins, solvents and packaging drive cost of goods sold, and management explicitly flagged a "moderate rise" in raw-material costs pressuring Q2 2026 gross margin, which came in at 49.2% versus 49.4% a year earlier despite mid-single-digit price increases in Paint Stores. Tariffs on imported inputs and packaging compound the pressure. The historical playbook is recovery-with-a-lag through list price increases, and the 8% Paint Stores Group increase effective September 1, 2026 is that mechanism running in real time — but pushing 8% into a market where volume is only growing low-single-digits is an aggressive bet on customer captivity. The margin history frames the stakes: gross margin bottomed at 42.10% in FY2022 during the last input-cost spike and has since climbed to 48.98%, meaning roughly seven points of margin — and a very large share of the earnings recovery since 2022 — is cost-cycle rather than structural. That is recoverable, but it is also losable.
Share Gains Through the Owned Store Network EMERGING TAILWIND
The structural story running underneath the cycle is consolidation of the professional contractor toward Sherwin's network. Company-owned-store distribution accounted for 40.66% of 2025 US paints and coatings revenue, and Sherwin is overwhelmingly the operator of that channel with 4,841 stores plus 308 Consumer Brands stores and 317 Performance Coatings branches. In a soft market this channel gains share, because the independent dealer and the smaller regional brand lose the contractor first when jobs get scarce and every hour counts. The evidence is in the numbers: North America architectural coatings is a $25.31B market growing at 3.30% CAGR, and Sherwin's Paint Stores Group grew net sales 5.1% with 4.2% same-store sales in Q2 2026 — comfortably above market. CEO Heidi Petz's framing that the company is "outperforming the market" despite macro challenges is supported by that spread.

Bull versus bear implications. The bull reading is that Sherwin is compounding share and price through the worst housing market in decades, so that when turnover eventually unlocks, volume operating leverage lands on a structurally larger base — and the 27.71x forward multiple is a reasonable price for that optionality. The bear reading is that share gains and price increases are the only growth levers currently working, that Q2's headline was flattered by the Suvinil acquisition and 1.6–2.0% currency tailwinds, and that a 32.62x trailing multiple with a 2.37 PEG on 5.8% revenue growth prices in an unlock that may be years away. Both readings are consistent with the same facts; the disagreement is entirely about how long an investor is willing to wait, which is exactly why the Street splits 14 Buy against 11 Hold with a tight $340–$420 target range.
