01 Company Overview

Siemens is Europe's industrial technology heavyweight: factory automation and the software that designs and runs factories, the electrical gear inside buildings, power grids and data centers, trains and rail signalling, and — through its listed subsidiary Siemens Healthineers — medical imaging and diagnostics. Founded by Werner von Siemens in Berlin in 1847, it is a DAX 40 and EURO STOXX 50 member. In fiscal 2026 (year to September 30, 2026) the group booked €83.4B of revenue (up 8% on a comparable basis) and €9.5B of net income, against €10.4B in fiscal 2025, which included a €2.1B gain on the Innomotics sale. Basic EPS was €10.94 (fiscal 2025: €12.25). The U.S. supplied 28% of nine-month fiscal 2026 revenue, Germany 15% and China 9%.

The story right now has two halves. The operating half is strong: the quarter to September 30, 2026 brought record orders of €34.8B (+58% comparable, a book-to-bill of 1.47), an order backlog of about €143B, record free cash flow of €12.1B for the year, and a first fiscal 2027 outlook of €10.20–10.80 EPS pre PPA (earnings per share before the amortisation of acquisition-related assets), which leaves out Healthineers. Management also raised its medium-term targets. Data-center customers are the hottest pocket: CEO Roland Busch said on the call that data-center orders passed €8.5B in fiscal 2026 and data-center revenue rose about 60% to €4.6B. The structural half is a break-up in slow motion. Siemens spun off Siemens Energy in 2020 and has since sold down to a 4.98% stake (September 8, 2026); it owns about 67% of Siemens Healthineers and now plans to hand a 34.1% stake in Healthineers to its own shareholders, one Healthineers share for every two Siemens shares, subject to a vote at the February 2027 annual meeting. What is left is meant to be a focused automation, software and electrification company. The share price has risen with that plan — up 80.2% from the November 2021 month-end close to €254.65 — and the stock now trades at 23.3x trailing earnings, so the question is how much of that focus is already in the price.

Fiscal Q4 was a record on orders, profit and cash, and the shares closed +1.7% on results day (€277.00 → €281.70, Xetra closes) after trading as high as €291.45 during the session; the DAX rose 0.4%. They then slid over the next six sessions to €254.65 on November 20, 9.6% below the results-day close, against a 7.4% fall for the DAX (26,771.88 → 24,802.78); November 20 alone was −4.6% for Siemens and −2.6% for the DAX. On November 16, when Siemens fell 2.5% and the DAX 0.6% (close to close), dpa-AFX, as carried by finanztrends.de, put Siemens among the weakest DAX stocks as uncertainty around Nvidia, the coming Anthropic share listing and rising inflation and interest-rate worries weighed on markets. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

€143B order backlog Automation + design software in one stack €6.1B recurring software revenue Grid & data-center electrification Long-term rail service contracts Global installed base Industrial net debt 0.3x EBITDA

The moat is breadth plus lock-in. A factory that is designed in Siemens software, controlled by Siemens automation and powered through Siemens switchgear has a single vendor at three layers, and replacing any of them means re-engineering and downtime. The software piece is the newest and most valuable layer: €6.1B of recurring revenue growing 12%, extended by the Altair and Dotmatics acquisitions (net cash spent on acquisitions was €9.97B in the first nine months of fiscal 2025). The backlog quantifies the rest — about €143B of signed work, much of it multi-year rail and grid projects. The weakness of breadth is that it is also a conglomerate, and the market has historically priced conglomerates below the sum of their parts; the Siemens Energy exit and the Healthineers spin-off are management's answer to that.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Automation is cyclical. Digital Industries revenue fell 4% in fiscal 2025 and segment profit dropped from €3.50B to €2.64B. The recovery in fiscal 2026 is real (+9% comparable over nine months, +10% in Q4) but it is a cycle, and the short-cycle automation business drove much of Q3's order growth.
02
Data-center concentration. Smart Infrastructure's comparable orders rose 42% in Q3 and 39% in Q4 on large data-center contracts, and data centers are about 20% of the segment's revenue. That is the strongest growth engine in the group, and it depends on a capital-spending cycle that Siemens does not control.
03
Mobility's margin. At 8.2% for nine months and 9.5% in Q4, rail earns well under the 16.8% Q4 margin of the industrial business as a whole. Its large multi-year orders lock in revenue but also lock in execution risk on long projects.
04
Currency and trade. The U.S. supplied 28% of nine-month revenue. Currency translation took one percentage point from both order and revenue growth in Q3, and U.S. tariff refunds flattered profit in the same quarter — trade policy moves the numbers in both directions.

Notable One-Off Items

Full-year net income was €9.5B against €10.4B in fiscal 2025, which included a €2.1B gain on the Innomotics sale (Q4 FY2026 release). Three things distort the year-on-year comparisons. First, Innomotics: the sale of that business accounts for most of the €2.09B of discontinued-operations income in the first nine months of fiscal 2025, which is why nine-month net income fell 18% this year (€7.04B versus €8.55B) even as income from continuing operations rose 7%. Siemens says the Innomotics gain plus Altair and Dotmatics effects were worth €2.23 per share in fiscal 2025. Second, Q3 FY2026 gains: U.S. tariff refunds lifted profit, primarily at Healthineers; Siemens Financial Services booked a €156M gain on selling a stake in a U.K. equity investment; and divestment gains outside the industrial business were about €0.2B. Third, buybacks: Siemens spent €2.66B on its own shares in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 and cut its share count from 800 million to 782 million issued shares (as of March 3, 2026), so per-share figures grow faster than profit. Since the July 1 launch of the current program, €1.7B has been spent (CFO, Q4 call). In Q4, U.S. tariff refunds added about half a point to Smart Infrastructure's margin.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Siemens is turning from a conglomerate into a focused industrial-tech company just as its best markets are booming. Fiscal Q4 orders hit a record €34.8B (+58% comparable), Smart Infrastructure's orders rose 39% to €8.7B on data-center demand, Digital Industries' margin reached 20.9% with €7.2B of software revenue for the year, and the order backlog stands at about €143B — 1.7 years of revenue. A record €12.1B of free cash flow funds a rising dividend (€5.50 proposed) and a buyback that has already spent €1.7B since July. Management guides fiscal 2027 EPS pre PPA to €10.20–10.80, about 13% growth at the midpoint on a comparable base without Healthineers, and raised its medium-term revenue-growth target to 7–11%. The Healthineers spin-off hands shareholders the medtech asset directly and leaves a cleaner, higher-margin core that peers such as Schneider and ABB suggest the market can reward.

Bear Case

At 23x trailing earnings and about 20x consensus fiscal 2027 EPS the good news is not cheap, and the market's verdict on a record quarter with a new outlook was a 1.7% gain on results day followed by a 9.6% slide in six sessions, with Vertical Research and Oddo BHF cutting to Hold. The stock is up 80% in five years and the average target is about 22% above the close. Digital Industries is a cyclical business that shrank in fiscal 2025, the data-center order surge depends on other companies' capital budgets, tariff refunds helped Smart Infrastructure's Q4 margin, and Mobility earns a 9.5% margin, the lowest in the group. The spin-off still needs two shareholder votes, and removing Healthineers takes nearly 29% of segment revenue out of the consolidated group, so fiscal 2027 profit has to be compared on the €9.27 base, not the €11.76 reported.

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation leaves little room for a stumble. A 23.3x trailing P/E, two downgrades to Hold after the print and a 9.6% slide in the six sessions after a record quarter show how little patience the market has; the fiscal 2027 guide of €10.20–10.80 EPS pre PPA (excluding Healthineers) now has to be met.
02
Cycle exposure in automation and data centers. Digital Industries revenue fell 4% in fiscal 2025 and its profit by about a quarter; Smart Infrastructure's fastest growth comes from a concentrated set of data-center customers. A pause in either cycle hits the two highest-margin businesses at once.
03
Spin-off execution. The Healthineers separation is, in Siemens' own word, an "unprecedented" transaction structure, and it still needs approval by both companies' shareholders; in April final regulatory confirmation was outstanding, and in August the CFO said binding tax rulings had arrived. The terms changed on November 12 (a 34.1% stake in Healthineers, one Healthineers share for every two Siemens shares), and a delay would prolong the conglomerate structure the market is trying to price out.
Industry Commentary
Electrification for AI data centers
STRONG TAILWIND

The AI build-out needs power before it needs chips: switchgear, transformers, busways and grid connections. Siemens' numbers show it directly. Smart Infrastructure's Q4 FY2026 orders rose 39% comparable to a record €8.7B, data-center orders passed €8.5B for the fiscal year (triple-digit growth) and data-center revenue rose about 60% to €4.6B, and management guides the segment to 11–13% revenue growth and a 19.5–20.5% margin in fiscal 2027. The demand has lifted the electrical-equipment group unevenly: ABB is up 17.4% over the year to November 20, while Schneider is down 0.8% and Eaton down 5.1%. The open question for every supplier is duration: orders this large are a capital-spending wave, and waves end. On the call CFO Veronika Bienert said Siemens will watch U.S. permitting, which could cause some hesitation, while CEO Roland Busch said he sees no slowdown in data-center demand over the next couple of years, with about 18 months of order visibility.

Industrial software and the automation cycle
RECOVERING

Factory automation had a downturn: Siemens' Digital Industries revenue fell 4% in fiscal 2025 and profit fell from €3.50B to €2.64B. Fiscal 2026 brought a recovery, with Q4 comparable revenue growth of 10% and a Q4 margin of 20.9%, and Siemens has used the trough to buy more software — Altair and Dotmatics — so that a larger share of the segment earns recurring revenue (€6.1B ARR, +12%; €7.2B of software revenue, +13%). Siemens says its digital business grew 16% in fiscal 2026, and it guides Digital Industries to 8–12% growth and a 20–22% margin in fiscal 2027. In late September the CFO still described car makers as subdued while semiconductors grew above average (Reuters), so car and chemical customers are the part to watch.

The European conglomerate break-up
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

Siemens is the clearest case of a European industrial group simplifying itself: Healthineers listed in 2018, Siemens Energy spun off in 2020 and now below a 5% stake, Innomotics sold in fiscal 2025, and a Healthineers spin-off planned for 2027. The bull framing: each step removes a business the market valued poorly inside the group and leaves automation, software and electrification — the businesses with the highest margins — to be valued on their own. The bear framing: the price has already risen with the plan. The stock is up 80% since late 2021 and trades at 23x trailing earnings, and once Healthineers is gone the group gets smaller and more cyclical, not just more focused. The November 12 outlook gave the first look at what the focused company is guided to earn: €10.20–10.80 EPS pre PPA for fiscal 2027, without Healthineers.
