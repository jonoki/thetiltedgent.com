01 Company Overview

Siemens is Europe's industrial technology heavyweight: factory automation and the software that designs and runs factories, the electrical gear inside buildings, power grids and data centers, trains and rail signalling, and — through its listed subsidiary Siemens Healthineers — medical imaging and diagnostics. Founded by Werner von Siemens in Berlin in 1847, it is a DAX 40 and EURO STOXX 50 member. In fiscal 2025 (year to September 30, 2025) the group booked €78.9B of revenue and €10.4B of net income, with basic EPS of €12.25. Over the last twelve months to June 30, 2026, revenue was €81.1B; the U.S. supplied 28% of nine-month fiscal 2026 revenue, Germany 15% and China 9%.

The story right now has two halves. The operating half is strong: the quarter to June 30, 2026 brought record orders of €27.9B (+14% on a comparable basis), a book-to-bill of 1.34, a record €132B order backlog and a raised full-year EPS outlook. Data-center customers are the hottest pocket: Smart Infrastructure's data-center orders more than doubled to about €6B in the first nine months. The structural half is a break-up in slow motion. Siemens spun off Siemens Energy in 2020 and has since sold down to a 4.98% stake (September 8, 2026); it owns about 67% of Siemens Healthineers and plans to hand 30% of Healthineers' shares directly to its own shareholders, subject to a vote at the February 2027 annual meeting. What is left is meant to be a focused automation, software and electrification company. The stock has re-rated accordingly — up 96.8% from the October 2021 month-end close to €276.10 — and now trades at 27.0x trailing earnings, so the question is how much of that focus is already in the price.

Since the August print. The record quarter still sent the shares down 4.5% on results day (€285.95 → €273.10, Xetra closes); they recovered 2.5% the next session. Brokers read the numbers as a beat — BofA raised its price objective on August 7, citing a fiscal Q3 beat and the upgraded outlook (MT Newswires) — but the research house Alphavalue headlined its results-day note "Order beat largely discounted, raised outlook not enough to sustain current valuations". In a Börsen-Zeitung interview reported by Reuters on September 29, CFO Veronika Bienert said Siemens was seeing a slight improvement in its operating conditions, with semiconductors, aviation and defence growing above average while car makers stayed subdued and energy-intensive industries such as chemicals held back on investment, and said she was confident of meeting the targets given to the market. Mobility added two large rail awards on September 22, and the share buyback launched on July 1 had bought back 3,931,057 shares by September 27.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

€132B order backlog Automation + design software in one stack €5.7B recurring software revenue Grid & data-center electrification Long-term rail service contracts Global installed base Industrial net debt 0.6x EBITDA

The moat is breadth plus lock-in. A factory that is designed in Siemens software, controlled by Siemens automation and powered through Siemens switchgear has a single vendor at three layers, and replacing any of them means re-engineering and downtime. The software piece is the newest and most valuable layer: €5.7B of recurring revenue growing 11% organically, extended by the Altair and Dotmatics acquisitions (net cash spent on acquisitions was €9.97B in the first nine months of fiscal 2025). The backlog quantifies the rest — €132B of signed work, much of it multi-year rail and grid projects. The weakness of breadth is that it is also a conglomerate, and the market has historically priced conglomerates below the sum of their parts; the Siemens Energy exit and the Healthineers spin-off are management's answer to that.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Automation is cyclical. Digital Industries revenue fell 4% in fiscal 2025 and segment profit dropped from €3.50B to €2.64B. The recovery in fiscal 2026 is real (+9% comparable over nine months) but it is a cycle, and the short-cycle automation business drove much of Q3's order growth.
02
Data-center concentration. Smart Infrastructure's 42% comparable order jump in Q3 was driven by large data-center contracts in the U.S. and Europe. That is the strongest growth engine in the group, and it depends on a capital-spending cycle that Siemens does not control.
03
Mobility's margin. At 8.2% for nine months, rail earns roughly half the margin of the rest of the industrial business. Its large multi-year orders lock in revenue but also lock in execution risk on long projects.
04
Currency and trade. The U.S. supplied 28% of nine-month revenue. Currency translation took one percentage point from both order and revenue growth in Q3, and U.S. tariff refunds flattered profit in the same quarter — trade policy moves the numbers in both directions.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the year-on-year comparisons. First, Innomotics: the sale of that business accounts for most of the €2.09B of discontinued-operations income in the first nine months of fiscal 2025, which is why nine-month net income fell 18% this year (€7.04B versus €8.55B) even as income from continuing operations rose 7%. Siemens says the Innomotics gain plus Altair and Dotmatics effects were worth €2.23 per share in fiscal 2025. Second, Q3 FY2026 gains: U.S. tariff refunds lifted profit, primarily at Healthineers; Siemens Financial Services booked a €156M gain on selling a stake in a U.K. equity investment; and divestment gains outside the industrial business were about €0.2B. Third, buybacks: Siemens spent €2.66B on its own shares in the first nine months of fiscal 2026 and cut its share count from 800 million to 782 million issued shares (as of March 3, 2026), so per-share figures grow faster than profit.

Relative performance

Against its home market Siemens has been a clear outperformer. Over the year to October 2, 2026 the stock rose 14.5% on price against 3.3% for the DAX (which includes dividends) and 10.5% for the EURO STOXX 50 price index. Since the October 2021 month-end it has returned about 124% with dividends against the DAX's 60.8%. Year to date (from the December 30, 2025 close) it is up 15.5% against the DAX's 3.0%. Among industrial peers the picture is more mixed: over the same twelve months ABB (+40.4%), Rockwell Automation (+30.3%) and Schneider Electric (+21.0%) all did better on price, Eaton (+15.8%) roughly matched it, and the rail and medtech comparisons — Alstom (−33.1%), GE HealthCare (−16.3%), Philips (−13.9%) — were far weaker. The market is paying up for automation and electrification and marking down rail and medtech, the two businesses Siemens is least focused on.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Siemens is turning from a conglomerate into a focused industrial-tech company just as its best markets are booming. Smart Infrastructure's orders rose 42% comparable in Q3 on data-center demand, Digital Industries is recovering from its fiscal 2025 trough with software growing 15% and €5.7B of recurring revenue, the Industrial Business margin hit a record 17.3%, and the order backlog stands at €132B — 1.6 years of revenue. Free cash flow of €11.85B over twelve months funds a rising dividend and a buyback that has already cut the share count to 782 million issued shares. The Healthineers spin-off, with tax rulings now in hand, hands shareholders the medtech asset directly and leaves a cleaner, higher-margin core that peers such as Schneider and ABB suggest the market will reward.

Bear Case

At 27x trailing earnings and 21x consensus fiscal 2027 EPS, the good news is in the price, and Evercore downgraded to Hold on October 2. The stock has nearly doubled in five years, the average target is only about 11% above the close, and the market's own verdict on Q3 — a record quarter with a raised outlook — was a 4.5% fall. Digital Industries is a cyclical business that shrank in fiscal 2025, the data-center order surge depends on other companies' capital budgets, Mobility earns half the group's industrial margin, and Q3 profit was flattered by tariff refunds and disposal gains. The spin-off still needs two shareholder votes, and removing Healthineers takes nearly 29% of segment revenue out of the consolidated group.

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation leaves little room for a soft fiscal 2027 guide. A 27.0x trailing P/E and Evercore's October 2 downgrade to Hold set up the November 12 outlook as the key test; Q3 showed the stock can fall on a record quarter.
02
Cycle exposure in automation and data centers. Digital Industries revenue fell 4% in fiscal 2025 and its profit by about a quarter; Smart Infrastructure's fastest growth comes from a concentrated set of data-center customers. A pause in either cycle hits the two highest-margin businesses at once.
03
Spin-off execution. The Healthineers separation is, in Siemens' own word, an "unprecedented" transaction structure, and it still needs approval by both companies' shareholders; in April final regulatory confirmation was outstanding, and in August the CFO said binding tax rulings had arrived. A delay would prolong the conglomerate structure the market is trying to price out.
Industry Commentary
Electrification for AI data centers
STRONG TAILWIND

The AI build-out needs power before it needs chips: switchgear, transformers, busways and grid connections. Siemens' numbers show it directly. Smart Infrastructure's Q3 FY2026 orders rose 42% comparable to a record €8.0B on large data-center contracts in the U.S. and Europe, nine-month data-center orders more than doubled to about €6B, and management lifted the segment's growth and margin guidance. The same demand has re-rated the whole electrical-equipment group — ABB is up 40.4% and Schneider 21.0% over the year to October 2. The open question for every supplier is duration: orders this large are a capital-spending wave, and waves end.

Industrial software and the automation cycle
RECOVERING

Factory automation had a downturn: Siemens' Digital Industries revenue fell 4% in fiscal 2025 and profit fell from €3.50B to €2.64B. Fiscal 2026 has brought a recovery, with nine-month comparable revenue growth of 9% and a Q3 margin of 18.7%, and Siemens has used the trough to buy more software — Altair and Dotmatics — so that a larger share of the segment earns recurring revenue (€5.7B ARR, +11% organic). Siemens says its digital business grew 18% in the first nine months, ahead of a 15% target. After Q3, Berenberg noted the market's disappointment that Digital Industries' targets were not raised alongside Smart Infrastructure's, and in late September the CFO still described car makers as subdued while semiconductors grew above average, so this is the line to watch in November.

The European conglomerate break-up
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

Siemens is the clearest case of a European industrial group simplifying itself: Healthineers listed in 2018, Siemens Energy spun off in 2020 and now below a 5% stake, Innomotics sold in fiscal 2025, and a Healthineers spin-off planned for 2027. The bull framing: each step removes a business the market valued poorly inside the group and leaves automation, software and electrification — the businesses with the highest margins — to be valued on their own. The bear framing: the re-rating has already happened. The stock has nearly doubled since 2021 and trades at 27x trailing earnings, and once Healthineers is gone the group gets smaller and more cyclical, not just more focused. The November 12 fiscal 2027 outlook is the first chance to see what the focused company is guided to earn.
