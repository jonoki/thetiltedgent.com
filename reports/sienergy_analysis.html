01 Company Overview

Siemens Energy builds the heavy machinery of electricity: gas turbines and the long service contracts that come with them, grid equipment (transformers, switchgear, high-voltage direct-current links), industrial machinery (compressors, steam turbines, electrolysers) and, through Siemens Gamesa, wind turbines. Two numbers carry the story. The order backlog reached a record €164B at September 30, 2026, the end of its fiscal year, up 18.2% in a year and about 3.7 times fiscal 2026 revenue of €44.2B. And the wind business, the source of almost every loss in the company's short history, broke even for the year before special items (€8M, against a €1,364M loss in fiscal 2025), with a profit in each of the last two quarters.

The share price has lived through both ends of the cycle. It closed €8.37 at the end of October 2023, the month the company said it was seeking German government guarantees after quality failures at Siemens Gamesa. It closed €180.58 at the end of April 2026, 21.6 times that level, after two years of fast-rising orders for gas turbines and grid equipment, with the US — including data-centre projects — the main source of growth. At €131.28 the stock sits 27.3% below that April month-end, after a fall to €113.84 on November 5 and a 10.8% jump on November 11, the day it reported a record fiscal 2026: revenue €44.2B (+15.5% comparable), profit before special items €5,486M (a 12.4% margin, against 6.0% a year earlier), net income €4,129M and basic EPS €4.44. In the fourth quarter (July–September) basic EPS was €1.49 against a €1.34 consensus forecast cited by Investing.com. The debate over the stock is about how long the power-equipment boom lasts, not about the results (sections 03 and 08).

The print (Nov 11, 2026). The company met or beat every target of the fiscal 2026 outlook it had raised in April: revenue growth 15.5% comparable (guide 14–16%), margin before special items 12.4% (guide 10–12%), net income €4.13B (about €4B) and free cash flow pre tax €8.8B (about €8B). For fiscal 2027 it guides to comparable revenue growth of 14–16%, a margin before special items of 14–16%, net income of about €5B and free cash flow pre tax of about €10B; for fiscal 2030 it targets a 20–22% margin, with comparable revenue growth of 12–14% a year from fiscal 2026. It proposes a dividend of €2.30 per share (€0.70 a year earlier) and is expanding its buyback: up to a further €9B of shares by the end of fiscal 2030, beside the €6B plan running since March 2026, of which €3.98B had been spent by November 6. Earlier in the autumn, on August 25, the company said it would prepare the separation of Transformation of Industry (compressors, steam turbines, electrolysers), keeping a minority stake; Siemens AG, which spun the company off in 2020, cut its voting stake to 4.98% on September 8; and in July the company announced it will drop the licensed Siemens name and become Omterra.

Currency. Siemens Energy reports in euros and its shares trade in euros on Xetra; every per-share figure here is in EUR. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base of gas turbines Decades-long service contracts Factory capacity in a supply-short market €164B backlog HVDC & transformer know-how Prepayment-funded balance sheet

The moat is scarcity plus the installed base. Gas turbines are a concentrated market — Barclays (July 2026) put Siemens Energy's share at around 40%, while Bruch, on the Q4 call, put its global share at around 30% — and customers pay reservation fees for production slots (Q3 and Q4 releases), which is why free cash flow is running ahead of profit. Each turbine sold also feeds the service business for decades, at an average agreement length of 17 years. The newer orders carry better prices than the ones being delivered now — Ferraro said new-unit backlog margins are 26 percentage points higher than in fiscal 2022 — so margins rise as those newer orders are delivered. The vulnerability is that scarcity is a feature of the cycle, not of the company: if capacity across the industry catches up with demand, or demand forecasts for data centres fall, pricing power goes first and the prepayments that fund the balance sheet go next.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Peak-cycle pricing. Barclays (Jul 7, 2026, downgrade to Underweight) argued the valuation is "pricing in indefinite peak-cycle economics": it estimated sustainable gas-turbine demand at 80–90 GW a year, about 15% below the current yearly pace of orders, and noted Siemens Energy's market share had risen to around 40% against a historical 25–27%. Management's view is the opposite: at its Sep 30 pre-close call it described a gas-turbine market of "around 110 to 120 gigawatts" a year, and on the Q4 call Bruch said "We continue to see a gas turbine market at a structurally higher level". The new fiscal 2030 margin target of 20–22% builds on that view.
02
Wind's order book is thin. Siemens Gamesa broke even for the year with a fiscal 2026 book-to-bill of 0.55 and a €31B backlog, and the company assumes its revenue shrinks 1–3% in fiscal 2027. A turnaround built on cost cuts needs new orders, and offshore orders arrive in large, irregular blocks: Q4's one Baltic Sea order was worth around €1.7B.
03
Cash flow that depends on order momentum. €28.6B of customer prepayments sit on the balance sheet. Barclays expects equity free cash flow to peak in 2026 and net working capital to become "a material headwind" from 2028. If orders slow, the cash generation slows with them, before profit does.
04
One customer country. The US supplied 40.7% of fiscal 2026 orders (€27.7B of €68.1B) and 41.0% of Q4's. Data-centre customers account for about €15B of the €164B backlog (Ferraro, Q4 call). US power policy, tariffs on imported equipment, or a pause in data-centre construction would land on this order book first.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, special items: Q4 FY2026 included a positive €184M, mainly the reversal of earlier write-downs on aeroderivative gas turbine assets at Gas Services, so Q4 profit (€1,725M) is above profit before special items (€1,540M); for the full year special items were −€82M. This is why the company headlines profit before special items. Second, prepayments: fiscal 2026 operating cash flow of €9,958M includes a €5,812M increase in contract liabilities — customer advance payments and reservation fees tied to new orders — so cash is running well ahead of profit. Third, buybacks: €3.98B has been spent on 26.5M shares since March 2026 (first tranche €2.0B at €158.50 average, second €1.0B at €154.63, third €985M at €133.28 to Nov 6), which reduces the share count against a 861.1M issued base; treasury shares stood at €3,097M at cost on September 30.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A €164B backlog — about 3.7 years of revenue — is being delivered at rising margins: 12.4% in fiscal 2026 against 6.0% a year earlier, a 14–16% guide for fiscal 2027 and a 20–22% target for fiscal 2030, with the CFO saying new-unit backlog margins are 26 percentage points above fiscal 2022's. Gas and grid are sold out into a power-demand cycle driven by data centres, electrification and grid upgrades, yet data-centre customers are only about €15B of the backlog, and wind has broken even. Cash is ahead of profit (€8.8B pre-tax free cash flow in fiscal 2026, about €10B guided for 2027), funding a dividend more than tripled to €2.30 and up to €9B more of buybacks through 2030. The Transformation of Industry separation and the Omterra rebrand cut the group down to its fastest-growing businesses and end, by 2030 at the latest, a licence fee that cost about €300M last year. At 20.5 times fiscal 2027 consensus EPS, 27.3% below the April month-end, the consensus target sits 46% above the price.

Bear Case

At 30.0x trailing earnings the price still assumes peak conditions last. Barclays' case (July 2026) is that they won't: gas-turbine demand settling 15% below today's yearly pace, Siemens Energy's market share well above its historical 25–27%, and data-centre orders already covering three to four years. Grid prices are levelling off — "the pricing environment in Grid is flattening out," Bruch said on the Q4 call — and Grid orders fell 19.3% in Q4 against a strong prior year. The cash flow that looks so strong is partly customer prepayments, which reverse when orders slow. Wind's book-to-bill was 0.55 for the year, with revenue guided lower for 2027, and 40.7% of fiscal 2026 orders came from one country. No month-end since April has regained April's close, and the shares lost 21.7% between the previous edition and their November 5 low.

Top 3 Risks

01
Order cycle turns. Gas Services' fiscal 2026 book-to-bill was 2.26 (orders €32.8B against revenue €14.5B), and Q4's 1.26. A return toward 1 — from industry capacity catching up or a slowdown in data-centre construction — would cap pricing on new orders and slow the prepayments that fund the buyback.
02
Wind relapse. Siemens Gamesa made €8M before special items in fiscal 2026 — break-even — and the company assumes a 0–2% margin on lower revenue in 2027. Any new quality issue or loss-making offshore contract would land on a business with no margin for error.
03
US exposure. The US supplied 40.7% of fiscal 2026 orders. Trade policy, tariffs on imported grid and turbine equipment, and the dollar (orders priced in USD, results reported in EUR) all feed straight into this order book.
Industry Commentary
Power demand and the gas-turbine squeeze
TAILWIND

Siemens Energy's own description of the market is short: "Electricity demand is in a growth era, requiring sustained investment in generation, grids, and energy infrastructure" (Bruch, Q4 call). The Q4 release expects the gas turbine market "to remain robust, supported by data centers, government programs for grid stability and coal phase-out, as well as industrial growth." At year-end the company counted 65 GW of firm gas-turbine orders and 39 GW under slot reservations, and Bruch said "Our growth thesis does not depend on AI": data-centre customers are about €15B of the €164B backlog (Ferraro). The constraint is supply. Barclays (July) counts more than 70 GW of data-centre-related orders or slot reservations across the turbine makers, covering the next three to four years, and the company calls "value over volume" its guiding commercial principle (pre-close call, Sep 30). Firm pricing today is why the bull and bear cases both turn on one question — how long the shortage lasts.

The grid bottleneck
TAILWIND, LONGER-DATED

New generation is useless without the wires and transformers to connect it, and grid equipment has the same shortage as turbines with a longer runway: Grid Technologies grew revenue 36.3% in Q4 and 26.2% in fiscal 2026, holds a €52B backlog, and plans another 22–24% in fiscal 2027. Grid customers are mostly utilities and transmission operators working through multi-year investment plans, which makes this the steadier half of the growth story. Prices are no longer climbing as fast: Bruch said on the Q4 call that Grid pricing is flattening out.

Wind: from crisis to break-even
ORDERS UNPROVEN

Siemens Gamesa lost €1,781M before special items in FY2024 and €1,364M in FY2025 while it fixed quality problems and costs, then broke even in FY2026 (+€8M). Two profitable quarters in a row say the cost side is fixed; the order side is not yet: fiscal 2026 orders were €5.9B against €10.6B of revenue, and one offshore order in the Baltic Sea, worth around €1.7B, made most of Q4's €2.4B. For the group, wind is no longer the swing factor in the earnings — gas and grid carry them — but it is still the most likely source of an unpleasant surprise.
