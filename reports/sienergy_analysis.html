01 Company Overview

Siemens Energy builds the heavy machinery of electricity: gas turbines and the long service contracts that come with them, grid equipment (transformers, switchgear, high-voltage direct-current links), industrial machinery (compressors, steam turbines, electrolysers) and, through Siemens Gamesa, wind turbines. Two numbers carry the story. The order backlog reached a record €162B at June 30, 2026, up 19.3% in a year and about 3.9 times trailing revenue. And in Q3 of fiscal 2026 (April–June) the wind business, the source of almost every loss in the company's short history, made a profit before special items for the first time since fiscal 2022 (€75M).

The share price has lived through both ends of the cycle. It closed €8.37 at the end of October 2023, the month the company said it was seeking German government guarantees after quality failures at Siemens Gamesa. It closed €180.58 at the end of April 2026, 21.6 times higher, after two years of fast-rising orders for gas turbines and grid equipment, with the US — including data-centre projects — the main source of growth. At €145.42 the stock sits 19.5% below that April month-end, even though the quarter reported since then was the best in its history: orders €17.9B, revenue €11.4B (+18.5% comparable), profit before special items €1,623M (14.2% margin, against 5.1% a year earlier), and basic EPS €1.28 against a €1.16 forecast compiled by Investing.com. The argument against the stock since then has been about valuation, not results (section 05).

Since the Q3 print (Aug 5, 2026). Management confirmed the fiscal 2026 outlook it had raised on April 23, and now expects the margin before special items to land toward the upper end of 10–12%. On August 25 the company said it would prepare the legal and operational separation of Transformation of Industry (compressors, steam turbines, electrolysers; €5.7B of FY2025 revenue), keeping a minority stake. Siemens AG, which spun the company off in 2020, cut its voting stake to 4.98% on September 8, and Matthias Rebellius, one of its two original representatives on the supervisory board, stepped down at the end of September. On September 24 the company started a third €2B buyback tranche, part of a plan to repurchase up to €6B of shares by the end of fiscal 2028. At a pre-close call on September 30 it reaffirmed the outlook and said fourth-quarter group margins should stay around the first-half level. And in July it announced it will drop the licensed Siemens name and become Omterra.

Currency. Siemens Energy reports in euros and its shares trade in euros on Xetra; every per-share figure here is in EUR. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base of gas turbines Decades-long service contracts Factory capacity in a supply-short market €162B backlog HVDC & transformer know-how Prepayment-funded balance sheet

The moat is scarcity plus the installed base. Gas turbines are a concentrated market — Barclays puts Siemens Energy's share at around 40% — and customers now pay reservation fees for production slots (Q3 release), which is why free cash flow is running ahead of profit. Each turbine sold also feeds the service business for decades, at an average agreement length of 17 years. The newer orders carry better prices than the ones being delivered now, so margins rise as the backlog turns over. The vulnerability is that scarcity is a feature of the cycle, not of the company: if capacity across the industry catches up with demand, or demand forecasts for data centres fall, pricing power goes first and the prepayments that fund the balance sheet go next.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Peak-cycle pricing. Barclays (Jul 7, 2026, downgrade to Underweight) argued the valuation is "pricing in indefinite peak-cycle economics": it estimated sustainable gas-turbine demand at 80–90 GW a year, about 15% below the current run-rate, and noted Siemens Energy's market share had risen to around 40% against a historical 25–27%. Management's view, restated at its Sep 30 pre-close call, is a gas-turbine market of "around 110 to 120 gigawatts" a year, with 10 to 20 GW of possible upside from data centres, and orders booked today carry better margins than the backlog now being delivered.
02
Wind still lacks a new order book. Siemens Gamesa's first profitable quarter came with a 0.38 book-to-bill and a backlog down to €31B. A turnaround built on cost cuts needs new orders, and offshore orders arrive in large, irregular blocks.
03
Cash flow that depends on order momentum. €28.1B of customer prepayments sit on the balance sheet. Barclays expects equity free cash flow to peak in 2026 and net working capital to become "a material headwind" from 2028. If orders slow, the cash generation slows with them, before profit does.
04
One customer country. The US supplied 40.6% of nine-month orders and 39.7% comparable order growth in Q3. US power policy, tariffs on imported equipment, or a pause in data-centre construction would land on this order book first.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, special items: Q3 FY2025's profit included a positive €458M, mainly from the demerger of the energy business out of Siemens Limited, India, so year-on-year profit growth looks smaller than the underlying gain, which is why the company headlines profit before special items (€1,623M vs €497M). Q3 FY2026 special items were −€59M. Second, prepayments: nine-month free cash flow pre tax of €7,163M includes a €5,359M increase in contract liabilities — customer advance payments and reservation fees tied to new orders — so cash is running well ahead of profit. Third, buybacks: €3.17B has been spent on 20.3M shares since March 2026 (first tranche €2.0B at €158.50 average, second €1.0B at €154.63, third under way at €144.25 to Oct 2), which reduces the share count against a 861.1M issued base.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A €162B backlog — nearly four years of revenue — is being delivered at rising margins: 14.2% in Q3 against 5.1% a year earlier, with the FY2028 target at 14–16% and the CFO saying today's orders carry higher margins than yesterday's. Gas and grid are sold out into a power-demand cycle driven by data centres, electrification and grid upgrades, and wind has turned profitable. Cash is ahead of profit (€7.2B pre-tax FCF in nine months), funding €6B of buybacks with net cash to spare. The Transformation of Industry separation and the Omterra rebrand cut the group down to its fastest-growing businesses and end, by 2030 at the latest, a licence fee that cost about €300M last year. After a 19.5% pullback from the April month-end, the consensus target is 36% above the price.

Bear Case

At 46.9x trailing earnings the price assumes peak conditions last. Barclays' case is that they won't: gas-turbine demand settling 15% below the current run-rate, Siemens Energy's market share at around 40% against a historical 25–27%, and data-centre orders already covering three to four years. The cash flow that looks so strong is partly customer prepayments, which reverse when orders slow. Wind's book-to-bill was 0.38 in its first profitable quarter, and 41% of nine-month orders came from one country. No month-end since April has regained April's close, and management itself guides to a softer Q4.

Top 3 Risks

01
Order cycle turns. Gas Services' Q3 book-to-bill was 2.65. A return toward 1 — from industry capacity catching up or a slowdown in data-centre construction — would cap pricing on new orders and slow the prepayments that fund the buyback.
02
Wind relapse. Siemens Gamesa is at −€15M before special items for nine months, with break-even guided for the year and a shrinking backlog. Any new quality issue or loss-making offshore contract would land on a business with no margin for error.
03
US exposure. The US supplied 40.6% of nine-month orders. Trade policy, tariffs on imported grid and turbine equipment, and the dollar (orders priced in USD, results reported in EUR) all feed straight into this order book.
Industry Commentary
Power demand and the gas-turbine squeeze
TAILWIND

Siemens Energy's own description of the market is short: "Global demand for electricity - and consequently for our products - remained strong in the third quarter" (Bruch, Q3 release). It booked 73 turbines in Q3, and Bruch said medium-sized turbines "are often selected for distributed power generation and data center applications, where customers require reliable power solutions that can be deployed quickly." The constraint is supply. Barclays counts more than 70 GW of data-centre-related orders or slot reservations across the turbine makers, covering the next three to four years, and Bruch says the company prioritises "value over volume". Firm pricing today is why the bull and bear cases both turn on one question — how long the shortage lasts.

The grid bottleneck
TAILWIND, LONGER-DATED

New generation is useless without the wires and transformers to connect it, and grid equipment has the same shortage as turbines with a longer runway: Grid Technologies grew revenue 28.6% in Q3 with orders up another 27.6% and a €51B backlog. Grid customers are mostly utilities and transmission operators working through multi-year investment plans, which makes this the steadier half of the growth story.

Wind: from crisis to break-even
UNPROVEN

Siemens Gamesa lost €1,781M before special items in FY2024 and €1,364M in FY2025 while it fixed quality problems and costs. Its return to a quarterly profit says the cost side is fixed; the order side is not yet. Offshore orders come in multi-billion blocks, and Q3 had none. For the group, wind is no longer the swing factor in the earnings — gas and grid carry them — but it is still the most likely source of an unpleasant surprise.
