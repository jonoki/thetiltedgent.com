01 Company Overview

Smucker's name was built on spreads out of Orrville, Ohio, where the company was founded in 1897. The business that pays the bills today is U.S. Retail Coffee — $3.30B of the $9.05B in fiscal 2026 net sales (year ended April 30, 2026), roughly 36.5% of the total. Behind it sit peanut butter and other spreads plus frozen handheld foods (the Frozen Handheld & Spreads segment), Pet Foods, a foodservice arm (Away From Home) and, since November 2023, Sweet Baked Snacks — Hostess, of Twinkies fame.

That last line item is the story of this stock. On September 11, 2023 Smucker agreed to buy Hostess Brands for $5.6 billion in cash and stock, including assumed debt; the deal completed on November 7, 2023. In the three reporting quarters from January 2025 to January 2026, Smucker wrote down $2.94B of that price in impairment charges — about 53% of the purchase price (our calculation from the company's disclosed charges). By the third write-down the goodwill of the Sweet Baked Snacks unit was gone entirely. Those charges are why GAAP earnings per share read −$11.57 in FY2025 and −$1.30 in FY2026, and why the trailing P/E looks absurd at 59.1x, while management's adjusted EPS was $10.12 and $9.15 in the same two years.

The poker read: the Hostess hand was played and lost, and the chips are already off the table — the charges are non-cash, recognised and (the goodwill at least) exhausted. The live question is the next hand, and fiscal 2027 has opened with two beats and two raises. On August 26, 2026, Q1 adjusted EPS rose 71% to $3.24 (of which $0.84 was a one-off tariff refund) and full-year adjusted EPS guidance went to $10.50–$11.00 from $9.75–$10.25. On November 24, 2026, before the market opened, Q2 adjusted EPS came in at $3.20, up 52%, against a Nasdaq consensus of $2.57 ($0.22 of it from tariff refunds), on net sales down 2% to $2.28B, and guidance went up again to $10.80–$11.20. Coffee segment profit nearly doubled on lower costs while Coffee sales fell 5% as lower bean costs are passed on in lower prices. The stock fell 1.45% on the day (close to close) and closed at $130.06 on November 25, 8.5% above the previous edition's $119.88.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Category-leading household brands Retail shelf space & distribution Coffee cost pass-through Scale in green-coffee buying Low-beta demand (β 0.25)

Smucker's moat is the old consumer-staples moat: brands people buy on autopilot and the shelf space those brands command. It works where the brand is the category — coffee, peanut butter, spreads — and it is what lets the coffee segment reprice when bean costs move. Hostess was supposed to add a snacking brand with the same autopilot quality. The write-downs are the company's own admission that it did not: a brand moat that needs heavier promotion to hold volume, and still loses 11 points of volume/mix in a quarter, is not the same asset as a jar of Smucker's.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Sweet Baked Snacks is still shrinking. After $2.94B of impairments, the goodwill is gone but the Hostess trademark remains on the books after three partial write-downs. Q2 FY2027 sales fell 10% on 11 points of lost volume/mix; segment profit rose 54%, but from lower costs and marketing, not from selling more. Further underperformance can still produce trademark charges.
02
Coffee margin is a timing gain. Segment profit up 124% in Q1 and 96% in Q2 is what a lag between falling costs and falling prices looks like, with tariff refunds on top. Lower net pricing already took 3 points off Q2 coffee sales. If competitors cut shelf prices faster, the margin window closes.
03
Leverage from the deal. Total debt was $6,704.7M at October 31, 2026 (long-term $6,395.7M, current portion $150.0M, short-term borrowings $159.0M, per the Q2 10-Q; StockAnalysis shows $6.92B), against $44.1M of cash. Debt-to-equity is 1.14 and the current ratio 0.95. CFO Tucker Marshall put leverage at 2.9 times net debt to EBITDA on the call (debt minus cash, against a year of earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization). Net cash outflows related to debt were $36.5M in Q2 ($267.3M of net short-term repayments in the half), and buybacks only $0.8M ($6.5M in the half).
04
One-offs flatter FY2027. Tariff refunds added $0.84 to Q1 adjusted EPS and $0.22 to Q2 ($1.06 in the first half). The raised $10.80–$11.20 guide includes a net benefit of about $0.75 from them, after planned investments. They will not repeat.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 25, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Five years, almost no progress on price: from the November 2021 close of $126.47 to the November 25, 2026 settled close of $130.06, SJM moved +2.8%, against +57.3% for SPY and +61.0% for QQQ over the same dates (price only; dividends are left out of all three). For a holder the dividend has done most of the work: $4.44 a share over the past twelve months. In between, the stock ran to a $158.46 month-end high in December 2022, fell to $96.44 in March 2026 (−39.1% from that high) and has since recovered 34.9%. Over the past year (November 25, 2025 close to November 25, 2026) it is up 29.6%, against 6.2% for SPY.

The 6-period monthly RSI hit its lowest reading of the window — 17.5 in November 2023, the month the Hostess deal closed — after three straight monthly declines starting with the September 2023 announcement month. Its highest reading was 81.1 in December 2022, at the price peak. It stands at 65.5 now, after 56.6 at the September close and 65.3 at October's. The price sits above both the 3-month (≈$126.29) and the 10-month (≈$115.54) moving averages: the uptrend that began at the March low has held through the second beat and raise. On daily closes it is 2.9% above its 50-day average ($126.35) and 14.4% above its 200-day ($113.64).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The bad news is booked. $2.94B of Hostess write-downs are behind the company, the Sweet Baked Snacks goodwill is at zero, and the GAAP P/E of 59.1 hides a stock trading at about 11.8x consensus FY2027 EPS with a 3.44% dividend yield. Management has raised adjusted EPS guidance in both quarters of the year so far, free cash flow is guided at ~$1.1B (roughly 7.9% of the market cap, our calculation), and the core — coffee, peanut butter, spreads, pet — earned 21–30% segment margins in FY2026. Hostess profit grew 54% in Q2; if the business merely stops shrinking, the earnings base is clean and the multiple is a staples multiple on a low-beta stock.

Bear Case

The beats lean on two things that fade: tariff refunds ($1.06 of first-half adjusted EPS) and a coffee margin that widened because costs fell faster than shelf prices, while Coffee sales are already falling (−5% in Q2). The company itself guides net sales down for the year. Hostess sales are still falling (−10% in Q2), Pet profit fell 17%, the trademark can still be written down again, and $6.7B of debt against $44M of cash leaves little room for buybacks — first-half repurchases were $6.5M. A company that paid $5.6B for a snack-cake business that earned $97M of segment profit in FY2026 has shown how it allocates capital; the market is entitled to remember.

Top 3 Risks

01
Coffee margin normalisation. Coffee is 36.5% of sales, and its segment profit more than doubled in Q1 and nearly doubled in Q2 on the gap between costs and prices. As net price realisation falls through FY2027 — the company's own guidance, already −3 points in Q2 — that margin is the most likely line to give back.
02
Further Hostess impairment. The goodwill is gone, but the Hostess indefinite-lived trademark was written down three times ($320.9M across two FY2025 quarters, $454.2M in FY2026) and still carries value. Continued volume declines would put it back in the test.
03
Balance-sheet leverage. Debt-to-equity 1.14, current ratio 0.95, $44.1M of cash and 2.9 times net debt to EBITDA per the CFO, with a term loan due March 5, 2027. Deleveraging competes with buybacks and dividend growth for the ~$1.1B of expected free cash flow.
Industry Commentary
Sweet snacks in a weak-volume market
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

CFO Tucker Marshall tied the third write-down to "near-term underperformance and revised long-term expectations," and Mark Smucker said stabilisation "is taking longer than we expected." In Q2 FY2027 volume/mix fell another 11 points, even as segment profit rose 54%. Smucker's answer is to shrink to profitability: fewer SKUs, less promotion, one less plant.

Coffee: pricing up, pricing down
CYCLE

Coffee makes Smucker's earnings cyclical in a way the jam jar does not suggest. Higher green-coffee costs forced price increases that added about 10 points to Q1 FY2027 coffee sales; by Q2 lower net pricing was taking 3 points off, and the Q2 segment margin was 37.5%, double the year before. Segment profit is widest in the lag between the two. Investors should read coffee margins as a timing effect first and a structural improvement second.

The valuation gap — GAAP vs adjusted
FRAMING

Trailing GAAP EPS of $2.20 against guided adjusted EPS of $10.80–$11.20 is a 5x gap, almost entirely non-cash impairment. The bull framing: impairments are sunk cost; cash flow ($1.51B TTM FCF) is the truth, and it supports the $4.48 dividend comfortably. The bear framing: the impairments were cash spent in 2023 — $5.6B went out the door, and the write-downs just record that roughly half of it bought nothing. Both are right. The way to adjudicate is Sweet Baked Snacks segment profit and total debt, both reported every quarter.
