01 Company Overview

SK hynix makes memory chips — the parts of a computer that hold data while it is being worked on (DRAM) and the parts that store it (NAND flash). Its signature product is HBM, the stacked memory that sits next to the processors inside AI servers. In the first quarter of 2026 it ranked first in HBM with a 56.4% market share and second in DRAM overall at 29.1%, according to IDC data cited in its July 2026 US prospectus. FY2025 revenue was ₩97.15 trillion, up 47%, with operating profit of ₩47.21 trillion — and 2026 has dwarfed that: the first half alone brought in ₩131.9 trillion of revenue and ₩98.2 trillion of operating profit. Even so, the latest quarter fell short of expectations: Q2 2026 revenue of ₩79.32 trillion and operating profit of ₩60.54 trillion were records, but analysts had expected about ₩84 trillion and ₩64 trillion (LSEG SmartEstimates, cited by CNBC and Quartz), misses of about 6% and 5%. Three weeks later the board approved a ₩40 trillion buyback of about 3.3% of the shares, all to be cancelled (August 19, 2026).

Memory is the most cyclical corner of the chip business: prices are set by supply and demand, not by contracts, so the same factories that lose money in a glut print money in a shortage. SK hynix lived both ends inside this report's five-year window. In FY2023 it posted an operating loss of ₩7.73 trillion; in Q2 2026 it posted an operating margin of 76% on ₩79.3 trillion of quarterly revenue. The stock followed: from a ₩75,000 month-end close in December 2022 to ₩2,650,000 at the end of June 2026, then down 35% in July alone. At the October 2 close of ₩1,841,000 the company is worth about $986 billion, second in Korea only to Samsung Electronics. The central question is not whether the current numbers are extraordinary — they are — but how long a market where, in the company's words, customer demand exceeds supply can last before new capacity, Chinese entrants and Samsung's HBM catch-up bring prices back down.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

HBM process & packaging know-how Qualified at the largest AI-chip customers Scale — one of three DRAM majors Net cash ₩69.4T Multi-year supply agreements Solidigm QLC eSSD portfolio

The memory industry has consolidated to three large DRAM makers — Samsung, SK hynix and Micron held 90% of Q2 2026 DRAM revenue between them on Counterpoint's figures. A new fab costs tens of trillions of won and years to build, and HBM adds a second barrier: stacking a dozen dies and keeping yields high is a packaging skill as much as a chip-making one, and every new generation has to pass months of qualification at the customer. SK hynix's edge has been getting there first. The company lists "superior quality, stable supply capabilities based on high yield, cost competitiveness" as the basis of its HBM leadership. The weakness is that none of this changes the commodity nature of conventional memory: when supply catches up, prices fall for everyone, and the lead in HBM is measured in quarters, not decades.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The cycle has not been repealed. FY2023 brought an operating loss of ₩7.73 trillion and a net loss of ₩9.11 trillion (stockanalysis). Q2 2026 profitability rests on DRAM prices up about 30% and NAND prices up in the mid-50s percent in a single quarter. Samsung and China's CXMT are adding capacity too (KED Global; Counterpoint); SK hynix itself is spending in the high ₩40 trillion range this year.
02
The HBM lead is shrinking. Counterpoint has SK hynix at 50% of HBM revenue in Q2 2026, down from 58% in Q1, as Samsung rose from 21% to 33%. HBM is the product that justified the premium narrative; losing share there matters more than the headline profit suggests.
03
Customer concentration in one end-market. HBM, AI-server DRAM and eSSD drove the record quarter — all tied to the capital budgets of a handful of cloud and AI companies. The company itself says demand is supported by revenue from AI services; if that spending pauses, the products with the highest prices are the first exposed.
04
Korean market plumbing. The Kospi fell 10.84% on July 28, 2026 and recorded an unprecedented second consecutive circuit breaker the next day, amid an Asia-wide chip sell-off and leverage-fuelled selling (KED Global). Korean regulators have since tightened rules on single-stock leveraged ETFs tied to Samsung and SK hynix. Those flows can move the stock far more than any change in the business.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the 2026 figures. First, the Kioxia exit: Q2 net profit of ₩93.92 trillion exceeds operating profit of ₩60.54 trillion because of about ₩63.3 trillion of investment-asset gains tied to the Kioxia share sale and investment valuations (Q2 earnings call; Seoul Economic Daily). That gain alone equals about 39% of trailing net profit (pre-tax gain against after-tax profit; our calculation), so the trailing P/E of 8.1 overstates how cheap the recurring business is. Second, new shares: the July 2026 Nasdaq offering issued 17,790,000 new common shares — about 2.5% of issued shares — sold as American depositary shares (ADS) at $149 each, one ADS being a tenth of a share, for about US$26.2 billion of net proceeds earmarked for general purposes including capex. Shares outstanding rose from 711,075,500 to 728,865,500. Third, the FY2025 shareholder return: a ₩1,500 special dividend and the cancellation of 15.3 million treasury shares, a ₩14.3 trillion programme in all (FY2025 results release; AGM release). A fourth is under way: the ₩40 trillion buyback approved on August 19, 2026 (about 24.07 million shares, 3.3% of the 730,492,365 issued, bought on the open market from August 20 to November 19 and then cancelled) will shrink the share count again (6-K, August 19, 2026).

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of ₩103,000 to the October 2, 2026 settled close of ₩1,841,000, SK hynix rose 1,687% on price — roughly 17.9 times the starting value, against +135.8% for the KOSPI over the same window. Almost all of it came late. The stock spent 2022 falling to a ₩75,000 month-end low in December, was still below ₩200,000 as recently as April 2025, and then rose nearly fifteenfold to the June 2026 month-end of ₩2,650,000. Since then it has given back 30.5% of that month-end level.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how stretched the run became: 95.3 in February 2026, the highest reading in the five-year series, and above 80 at most month-ends from June 2025 to June 2026. The lowest reading, 27.9, came at the December 2022 trough. July's 35.2% fall reset it to the high 50s; it stands at 61.3 now. The 10-month moving average, around ₩1,605,500, is below the price — the long-term trend is still up even after the July break.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 5.2x this year's consensus earnings for a company that just earned a 76% operating margin, holds ₩69.4 trillion of net cash before the US$26.2 billion ADS proceeds, and has signed multi-year supply agreements with around 10 customers. Management describes a structural shift in which AI is lifting demand for both HBM and ordinary memory at once, and says customer demand exceeds what it can supply. HBM4 shipments began in Q2 and ramp in the second half. Thirty-seven of 38 rated analysts say Buy, the average target is 70.6% above the price, and the company is buying back and cancelling about 3.3% of its shares for ₩40 trillion by November 19, with fixed and special dividends under review for the Q3 results. If the shortage lasts through 2027, today's price discounts a downturn that may not come on schedule.

Bear Case

Single-digit P/Es are what memory stocks look like at the top. The trailing multiple is flattered by a ₩63.3 trillion one-off gain, the forward one assumes prices that rose about 30% (DRAM) and in the mid-50s percent (NAND) in a single quarter stay there. SK hynix lost 8 points of HBM share in one quarter as Samsung caught up, fell from first to second in DRAM, and is spending in the high ₩40 trillion range on new capacity alongside Samsung and China's CXMT. FY2023's ₩7.73 trillion operating loss is three years old. Even the record Q2 missed consensus by about 5% on operating profit. And a possible US listing of Solidigm, reported by Bloomberg in late September, would add another layer to the SK group's ownership pyramid — the stock closed 5.0% lower at the next session. And the price itself is set in a market of leveraged single-stock ETFs and back-to-back circuit breakers, where the stock rose 30% in a single day in July; a beta of 2.39 cuts both ways.

Top 3 Risks

01
Memory prices turn. Revenue more than tripled in a year, and in Q2 prices did most of the work (DRAM prices up about 30% against high single-digit bit growth). When new fabs at SK hynix, Samsung and CXMT come on line, the same operating leverage that took margins from negative in FY2023 to 76% works in reverse.
02
HBM share loss. Counterpoint's Q2 2026 figures show the share falling from 58% to 50% in one quarter, with Samsung expected to gain further as its HBM4 ramps. HBM is where the premium sits; a second quarter of share loss would undercut the case that SK hynix deserves more than a commodity multiple.
03
AI capex pause and market structure. Demand rests on the spending of a few AI and cloud buyers; in July a chip sell-off, leveraged-ETF selling and a Kospi crash took 35% off the stock in a month despite record results. The persistent ADS premium in New York adds a second, less-arbitraged price for the same company.
Industry Commentary
An AI-driven memory shortage
CURRENT TAILWIND

SK hynix's own framing in its Q2 release: as AI moves toward agents that carry out complex tasks, the demand base for memory is broadening, and "a structural shift is occurring where demand for both AI memory and conventional memory is expanding in tandem." Counterpoint's Q2 2026 memory tracker says DRAM demand still exceeds supply and expects conventional DRAM prices to rise further. The numbers bear it out for now: SK hynix's revenue grew 257% year on year in Q2, its DRAM revenue 214%, and first-half revenue passed ₩100 trillion for the first time.

The HBM race tightens
ONGOING HEADWIND

For two years HBM was SK hynix's lane. In Q2 2026 Samsung lifted its HBM revenue share from 21% to 33% while SK hynix fell from 58% to 50% and Micron from 21% to 18% (Counterpoint, via Korea Herald). In conventional DRAM, Samsung regained first place with 39% and Micron, at 25%, came within a point of SK hynix. HBM4 is the next contest: SK hynix says its HBM4 meets customer speed requirements with industry-leading power efficiency and cost competitiveness; Counterpoint expects Samsung's share to rise as its own HBM4 ships. Meanwhile Chinese makers led by CXMT are adding DRAM capacity — the July sell-off that hit Korean chip stocks was reported under the headline "Korean stocks skid on China's chip push" (KED Global, July 28, 2026).

Two prices for one company
MARKET STRUCTURE

Since July, SK hynix trades in Seoul in won and in New York as ADSs worth a tenth of a share. On Oct 2 the ADS closed at $195.13, against $135.31 for a tenth of a Seoul share at 1,360.59 won per dollar — a 44% premium (our calculation; Asia Times reported 40.7% on the Sep 30 closes). Because new ADSs cannot be created from Seoul shares without procedures equivalent to an additional listing, the usual arbitrage does not close the gap. For a reader the practical point is simple: US analyst targets are set on the ADS price, and on Oct 2 the Seoul share — the price on this page — cost about 31% less than ten ADSs for the same stake (our calculation).
