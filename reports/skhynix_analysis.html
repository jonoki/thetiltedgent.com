01 Company Overview

SK hynix makes memory chips — the parts of a computer that hold data while it is being worked on (DRAM) and the parts that store it (NAND flash). Its signature product is HBM, the stacked memory that sits next to the processors inside AI servers. In the first quarter of 2026 it ranked first in HBM with a 56.4% market share and second in DRAM overall at 29.1%, according to IDC data cited in its July 2026 US prospectus. FY2025 revenue was ₩97.15 trillion, up 47%, with operating profit of ₩47.21 trillion — and 2026 has dwarfed that: the first nine months alone brought in ₩236.5 trillion of revenue and ₩181.5 trillion of operating profit (6-K, October 28, 2026). The latest quarter, Q3 2026, was the first above ₩100 trillion: revenue of ₩104.62 trillion and operating profit of ₩83.33 trillion beat the ₩98.34 trillion and ₩76.72 trillion analysts expected (LSEG SmartEstimates, cited by CNBC) by about 6% and 9%, a turnaround from Q2, which missed. With the results the board set about ₩100 trillion of dividends and buybacks for 2026, including a ₩20,000-a-share Q3 dividend and a further ₩20 trillion buyback (October 28, 2026).

Memory is the most cyclical corner of the chip business: prices are set by supply and demand, not by contracts, so the same factories that lose money in a glut print money in a shortage. SK hynix lived both ends inside this report's five-year window. In FY2023 it posted an operating loss of ₩7.73 trillion; in Q3 2026 it posted an operating margin of 80% on ₩104.6 trillion of quarterly revenue. The stock followed: from a ₩75,000 month-end close in December 2022 to ₩2,650,000 at the end of June 2026, then down 35% in July alone and down by a third again in the first three weeks of October. At the October 30 close of ₩1,418,000 the company is worth about $747 billion, second in Korea only to Samsung Electronics. The central question is not whether the current numbers are extraordinary — they are — but how long a market where, the company says, demand will exceed supply through 2030 can last before new capacity, Chinese entrants and Samsung's HBM catch-up bring prices back down.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

HBM process & packaging know-how Qualified at the largest AI-chip customers Scale — one of three DRAM majors Net cash ₩98.7T Multi-year supply agreements Solidigm QLC eSSD portfolio

The memory industry has consolidated to three large DRAM makers — Samsung, SK hynix and Micron held 90% of Q2 2026 DRAM revenue between them on Counterpoint's figures. A new fab costs tens of trillions of won and years to build, and HBM adds a second barrier: stacking a dozen dies and keeping yields high is a packaging skill as much as a chip-making one, and every new generation has to pass months of qualification at the customer. SK hynix's edge has been getting there first. The company lists "superior quality, stable supply capabilities based on high yield, cost competitiveness" as the basis of its HBM leadership. The weakness is that none of this changes the commodity nature of conventional memory: when supply catches up, prices fall for everyone, and the lead in HBM is measured in quarters, not decades.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The cycle has not been repealed. FY2023 brought an operating loss of ₩7.73 trillion and a net loss of ₩9.11 trillion (stockanalysis). The 2026 profits rest on price: DRAM prices rose about 30% in Q2 and in the high-20s percent in Q3, NAND prices in the mid-50s and then the mid-20s percent. Samsung and China's CXMT are adding capacity too (KED Global; Counterpoint); SK hynix itself is spending in the high ₩40 trillion range this year and says 2027 spending will rise significantly.
02
The HBM lead is shrinking. Counterpoint has SK hynix at 50% of HBM revenue in Q2 2026, down from 58% in Q1, as Samsung rose from 21% to 33%. HBM is the product behind the case that SK hynix deserves a higher valuation than other memory makers; losing share there matters more than the headline profit suggests.
03
Customer concentration in one end-market. HBM, AI-server DRAM and eSSD drove the record quarter — all tied to the capital budgets of a handful of cloud and AI companies. The company itself says demand is supported by revenue from AI services; if that spending pauses, the products with the highest prices are the first exposed.
04
Korean market plumbing. The Kospi fell 10.84% on July 28, 2026 and recorded an unprecedented second consecutive circuit breaker the next day, amid an Asia-wide chip sell-off and leverage-fuelled selling (KED Global). Korean regulators have since tightened rules on single-stock leveraged ETFs tied to Samsung and SK hynix. In October the Kospi fell again, 18.2% between the October 2 and October 30 closes (Yahoo Finance), and on October 30 the exchange briefly paused computer-driven (program) sell orders on the main board (Seoul Economic Daily). Those flows can move the stock far more than any change in the business.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the 2026 figures. First, investment and currency swings: Q2 net profit of ₩93.92 trillion exceeded operating profit of ₩60.54 trillion because of about ₩63.3 trillion of investment-asset gains tied to the Kioxia share sale and investment valuations (Q2 earnings call; Seoul Economic Daily). Q3 went the other way: net profit of ₩39.78 trillion was less than half of operating profit of ₩83.33 trillion after ₩22.9 trillion of valuation losses on investment assets and an ₩11.6 trillion foreign-exchange loss (Q3 call). Over the trailing year net profit (₩189.3 trillion) now sits below operating profit (₩200.7 trillion), so the trailing P/E of 5.4 is no longer flattered by the Kioxia gain on balance (our calculation). Second, new shares: the July 2026 Nasdaq offering issued 17,790,000 new common shares — about 2.5% of issued shares — sold as American depositary shares (ADS) at $149 each, one ADS being a tenth of a share, for about US$26.2 billion of net proceeds earmarked for general purposes including capex. Shares outstanding rose from 711,075,500 to 728,865,500. Third, the FY2025 shareholder return: a ₩1,500 special dividend and the cancellation of 15.3 million treasury shares, a ₩14.3 trillion programme in all (FY2025 results release; AGM release). Fourth, the 2026 buybacks: the ₩40 trillion programme approved on August 19, 2026 bought 23,693,272 shares at an average ₩1,688,426 between August 20 and October 21, leaving 25,319,041 treasury shares (3.47% of the total); the 23,693,272 are due to be cancelled on November 5 (6-K and 6-K/A, October 26, 2026). On October 28 the board approved a further ₩20 trillion buyback of up to 14,920,000 shares, running from October 29, 2026 to January 28, 2027: about ₩10 trillion of it to be cancelled and about ₩10 trillion for executive and employee share pay (6-K, October 28, 2026).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of ₩103,000 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of ₩1,418,000, SK hynix rose 1,277% on price — roughly 13.8 times the starting value, against +92.9% for the KOSPI over the same window. Almost all of it came late. The stock spent 2022 falling to a ₩75,000 month-end low in December, was still below ₩200,000 as recently as April 2025, and then rose nearly fifteenfold to the June 2026 month-end of ₩2,650,000. Since then it has given back 46.5% of that month-end level, most of it in July and October.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how stretched the run became: 95.3 in February 2026, the highest reading in the five-year series, and above 80 at most month-ends from June 2025 to June 2026. The lowest reading, 27.9, came at the December 2022 trough. July's 35.2% fall reset it to the high 50s, and October's fall has taken it to 48.9, just below the neutral 50. The 10-month moving average, around ₩1,563,200, is now above the price: on this measure the long uptrend has stalled.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 4.0x this year's consensus earnings for a company that just earned an 80% operating margin, holds ₩98.7 trillion of net cash, and has long-term supply agreements with 14 customers covering more than half of its 2027–2028 capacity. Management describes a structural shift in which AI is lifting demand for both HBM and ordinary memory at once, and now says demand will exceed supply through 2030. HBM4 is expected to pass HBM3E in shipments and revenue in Q4. Forty of 41 rated analysts say Buy, the average target is 122.7% above the price, and the company plans about ₩100 trillion of dividends and buybacks for 2026 — about 10% of its market value at the Oct 30 close (our calculation). If the shortage lasts as long as management says, today's price discounts a downturn that may not come on schedule.

Bear Case

Single-digit P/Es are what memory stocks look like at the top of the cycle, when profits peak. A price of about 4 times this year's forecast earnings assumes that prices which rose about 30% (DRAM) and in the mid-50s percent (NAND) in Q2, and in the high-20s and mid-20s percent again in Q3, stay there. SK hynix lost 8 points of HBM share in Q2 as Samsung caught up, fell from first to second in DRAM, and is spending in the high ₩40 trillion range on new capacity this year, with more in 2027, alongside Samsung and China's CXMT — whose reported advances coincided with a 10.1% fall in the stock on October 13. FY2023's ₩7.73 trillion operating loss is three years old. Net profit swings with currencies and investment values: Q3's ₩34.5 trillion of such losses left it at less than half of operating profit. And the price itself is set in a market of leveraged single-stock ETFs, circuit breakers and trading halts, where the stock rose 30% in a single day in July and fell a third in under three weeks in October; a beta of 2.39 cuts both ways.

Top 3 Risks

01
Memory prices turn. Q3 revenue was more than four times the year before, and prices did most of the work (DRAM prices up about 30% in Q2 and in the high-20s percent in Q3, against bit growth of a high single digit and about 10%). When new fabs at SK hynix, Samsung and CXMT come on line, the same high fixed costs that took margins from negative in FY2023 to 80% work in reverse: a fab costs about the same to run whatever its chips sell for.
02
HBM share loss. Counterpoint's Q2 2026 figures show the share falling from 58% to 50% in one quarter, with Samsung expected to gain further as its HBM4 ramps. HBM is where the premium sits; a second quarter of share loss would undercut the case that SK hynix deserves a higher price relative to its profits than a maker of ordinary memory chips.
03
AI capex pause and market structure. Demand rests on the spending of a few AI and cloud buyers; in July a chip sell-off, leveraged-ETF selling and a Kospi crash took 35% off the stock in a month despite record results, and in October another sell-off took a third off in under three weeks. The persistent ADS premium in New York adds a second price for the same company that traders cannot easily pull back into line.
Industry Commentary
An AI-driven memory shortage
CURRENT TAILWIND

SK hynix's own framing in its Q2 release: as AI moves toward agents that carry out complex tasks, the demand base for memory is broadening, and "a structural shift is occurring where demand for both AI memory and conventional memory is expanding in tandem." Counterpoint's Q2 2026 memory tracker says DRAM demand still exceeds supply and expects conventional DRAM prices to rise further. The numbers bear it out for now: SK hynix's revenue grew 257% year on year in Q2 and 328% in Q3, when quarterly revenue passed ₩100 trillion for the first time. With the Q3 results the company went further, saying it expects demand to exceed supply through 2030 because leading-edge processes are hard to master and new fabs take years to build (Q3 release).

The HBM race tightens
ONGOING HEADWIND

For two years HBM was SK hynix's lane. In Q2 2026 Samsung lifted its HBM revenue share from 21% to 33% while SK hynix fell from 58% to 50% and Micron from 21% to 18% (Counterpoint, via Korea Herald). In conventional DRAM, Samsung regained first place with 39% and Micron, at 25%, came within a point of SK hynix. HBM4 is the next contest: SK hynix says its HBM4 meets customer speed requirements with industry-leading power efficiency and cost competitiveness; Counterpoint expects Samsung's share to rise as its own HBM4 ships. Meanwhile Chinese makers led by CXMT are adding DRAM capacity — the July sell-off that hit Korean chip stocks was reported under the headline "Korean stocks skid on China's chip push" (KED Global, July 28, 2026), and on October 13 reports of CXMT's latest advances again hit Samsung and SK hynix shares (AP).

Two prices for one company
MARKET STRUCTURE

Since July, SK hynix trades in Seoul in won and in New York as ADSs worth a tenth of a share. On Oct 30 the ADS closed at $149.21, against $105.98 for a tenth of a Seoul share at 1,337.98 won per dollar — a 41% premium (our calculation; Asia Times reported 40.7% on the Sep 30 closes). Because new ADSs cannot be created from Seoul shares without procedures equivalent to an additional listing, the trade that usually closes such gaps, buying the cheaper Seoul share and turning it into ADSs, is not available. For a reader the practical point is simple: US analyst targets are set on the ADS price, and on Oct 30 the Seoul share — the price on this page — cost about 29% less than ten ADSs for the same stake (our calculation).
