01 Company Overview

SLB is the world's largest oilfield services (OFS) company — the contractor that exploration & production (E&P) operators hire to find, drill, complete, and produce oil and gas wells. It sells measurement and evaluation services, drilling systems, subsea and surface production hardware, production chemicals, and a growing software/AI stack, across roughly 100 countries. FY2025 revenue was $35.71B, down 1.6% YoY, with GAAP EPS of $2.35 (−24%) and adjusted EPS of $2.93 (−14%); TTM revenue through September 2026 is $36.89B, and revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was up 4.5% on the same period of 2025.

At a $78.35B market capitalization SLB ranks #162 in the S&P 500, and it is worth more than Baker Hughes ($55.60B) and Halliburton ($29.31B) individually (StockAnalysis, Oct 26, 2026 close). The business is deliberately barbelled: an international/offshore franchise generating $27.94B (78% of FY2025 revenue) that is levered to long-cycle national-oil-company and deepwater spending, against a smaller North American position ($7.52B) that has been repositioned away from weak US land pressure pumping and toward production chemicals and artificial lift via the July 2025 ChampionX acquisition. Two structurally different growth engines sit on top: a Digital division carrying ~28% operating margins, and a fast-scaling Data Center Solutions business inside "All Other." On August 31, 2026 SLB agreed to buy Kelvion, a heat-exchange and cooling maker that expects $2.3–2.4B of 2026 revenue, to scale that second engine.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
100-Country Footprint NOC Relationships Subsurface Data Archive Integrated Project Delivery Delfi / Lumi Switching Costs Subsea Installed Base R&D + Patent Depth

SLB's moat is logistical and relational before it is technological. Operating in roughly 100 countries with local content, customs, HSE certification and crew qualification already in place is a barrier that takes a decade and hundreds of millions of dollars to replicate — which is why national oil companies in Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Brazil, and India award multi-year integrated contracts to a shortlist of three firms rather than tendering line items. On top of that sits a subsurface data asset: a century of well logs, formation evaluation, and petrophysical interpretation that is the training substrate for Delfi and Lumi, and that a software-native entrant simply cannot buy. Once an operator standardizes its subsurface workflows on Delfi, the switching cost is a re-training and re-validation exercise across a whole exploration organization, which is why ARR grew 13% YoY through a downcycle. Finally, subsea trees and control systems create a 20-year aftermarket annuity attached to the installed base. The counterweight is that in the highest-volume, lowest-differentiation lines — North American pressure pumping, commodity drilling fluids — the moat is thin to nonexistent, which is exactly why SLB has been retreating from them.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Customer concentration in state actors and geopolitical single points of failure. Middle East & Asia was $12.22B of FY2025 revenue. A regional conflict cut Middle East revenue 13% sequentially in Q2 2026, and Middle East & Asia revenue 16% year on year — a swing SLB could neither hedge nor price around. It grew 13% in Q3, but SLB said some countries saw renewed disruption in the final weeks of the quarter, Saudi Arabia shut its East-West oil pipeline in September after drone attacks, and the Q4 outlook assumes Middle East revenue stays flat at about $1.9B. When a handful of NOCs set your activity level, budget politics and armed conflict are operating variables, not tail risks.

2. Late-cycle dependence on E&P capital budgets that are being deliberately suppressed. Global upstream capex was roughly $420B in 2025 (−2.5%), and in January Wood Mackenzie forecast a further 2–3% decline in 2026 on sub-$60/bbl oil. Its July mid-year outlook still had development spending on course for a second year of slight decline, even with Brent averaging $91 a barrel in the first half: operators kept the windfall rather than spend it. Service companies are price-takers on volume. SLB's TTM operating margin has already fallen from 17.55% in FY2024 to 13.30%, and ROIC from mid-teens to 10.87%. Management now argues the other side: on the October 23 call it said it expects oilfield-services activity worldwide to grow at a mid-single-digit rate in 2027.

3. Commoditization pressure and Chinese/regional competition. In wireline, drilling fluids and pressure pumping, regional players — including China Oilfield Services — compete credibly on price in exactly the markets SLB most wants to grow. Reservoir Performance's Q3 2026 operating margin of 16.3% against 18.5% a year earlier is a live pricing signal.

4. Integration and dilution risk from ChampionX. The deal was struck largely in stock: shares outstanding are up 6.38% YoY (StockAnalysis), a buyback yield of −6.38% and a shareholder yield of −4.15%, despite $2.41B of repurchases in FY2025 and $1.76B in the first nine months of 2026. Charges keep GAAP EPS below adjusted — Q3 2026 GAAP $0.49 versus adjusted $0.64 (a pension settlement charge plus merger and integration costs), and FY2025 GAAP $2.35 versus adjusted $2.93. The chemicals business also imports raw-material cost inflation SLB did not previously carry, and Kelvion adds a second integration of about $4.1B (cash plus assumed debt).

Notable One-Off Items. Several distortions matter for anyone comparing GAAP and adjusted figures:

ChampionX purchase accounting. The acquisition closed in July 2025 (Q3 2025), adding production chemicals and artificial lift. It contributed $1.46B of FY2025 revenue ($879M in Q4 2025 alone, of which $874M went to Production Systems and $28M to Digital) $870M in Q2 2026 and $956M in Q3 2026. Because it was substantially stock-funded, shares outstanding are up 6.38% YoY — a real per-share headwind that shows up as a −6.38% buyback yield despite $2.41B of FY2025 repurchases, $648M (12M shares) in Q2 2026 and $656M (12.9M shares) in Q3 2026. Intangible amortization and inventory step-up sit inside the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge: FY2025 GAAP EPS $2.35 vs adjusted $2.93 (a $0.58 gap, ~20% of GAAP EPS), Q2 2026 GAAP $0.52 vs adjusted $0.55, and Q3 2026 GAAP $0.49 vs adjusted $0.64.

Antitrust-driven divestitures. The FY2025 results release did not itemize material divestiture gains or losses tied to obtaining ChampionX clearance. SLB did not flag one as material.

Charges in Q3 2026. The Q3 release lists two items: a $187M pension settlement charge ($0.12 a share) and $55M of merger and integration costs ($0.03), which together bridge GAAP EPS of $0.49 to adjusted EPS of $0.64. The FY2025 and Q2 2026 releases highlighted no material impairment or restructuring charge, and no Russia-exit item was separately disclosed in any of the three; the FY2025 gap ($0.58 a share) was dominated by acquisition accounting.

M&A. SLB acquired Tachyus Corp., a Houston reservoir-modeling technology firm, during Q2 2026, and on September 1, 2026 closed the purchase of S&P Global Energy's geoscience and petroleum-engineering software — both consistent with buying digital capability rather than building it. The large one is pending: Kelvion, signed August 31, 2026, at about $3.4B in cash plus about $0.7B of assumed debt, which SLB puts at about 11 times Kelvion's estimated 2026 EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization) before cost savings. SLB expects it to add to earnings per share and free cash flow per share within the first 12 months after closing, expected in the first half of 2027.

Plain English: RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been. Above 70 the stock has run hot and may be due a pause; below 30 it has been beaten down and may be due a bounce. It is a timing gauge, not a valuation tool.

Five-year trajectory. On actual month-end closes SLB went from a $32.26 close in October 2021 to $53.08 on October 26, 2026 — a price return of +64.5%, about 10.5% a year (SPY +66.7% and QQQ +84.5% over the same dates, price only). That headline hides three different regimes. From the November 2021 close of $28.68 through August 2023 the stock more than doubled (to $58.96) as the post-COVID restart, the 2022 energy shock and a genuine international upcycle drove revenue from $22.93B (2021) to $33.14B (2023). From that peak it de-rated for nearly two years to a $33.05 monthly close in May 2025 — a 44% drawdown — as E&P capital discipline and a shrinking US land market pushed FY2025 revenue down 1.6% and GAAP EPS down 24%. Regime three is the re-rating from $38.38 (Dec 2025) to $60.10 (Aug 2026), the highest month-end close on this chart (+56.6%), on ChampionX consolidation, digital margins and Data Center Solutions, capped by the Kelvion deal on August 31. The swings since have been sharp: −18.9% in September, a month that included the Saudi pipeline shutdown, then +7.1% on the Q3 print. The June and September drops show how much of the move is now headline-driven.

Relative performance. Over the year to October 26, 2026 SLB's price rose +48.1% against +13.0% for SPY and +15.4% for QQQ (price returns, same dates) — about 35 points ahead of the S&P 500. Over the full chart window, though, SLB slightly trails SPY's price return (+64.5% vs +66.7%), and the chart includes a 44% drawdown — a reminder that this is a cyclical business regardless of what the 5-year measured Beta of 0.84 suggests.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

SLB is being repriced from a rig-count derivative into a diversified energy-infrastructure and industrial-software company. Digital carries ~28% operating margins with $1.04B of ARR growing 13%, Data Center Solutions grew 76% YoY in Q3 toward a >$2B run rate exiting 2027, and Kelvion, if it closes, adds a cooling business that expects $1.2–1.3B of data-center revenue in 2026. ChampionX adds chemicals and artificial lift revenue tied to producing barrels rather than new wells, and had its best quarter since the deal. In Q3 every region and core division grew sequentially; management guides Q4 revenue to about $10B and sees 2027 oilfield-services activity growing mid-single digits, with a Middle East recovery as upside. A forward P/E of 18.24 against consensus 2027 EPS of $3.12 (+27.6%), with a 2.22% dividend, a 0.47 debt-to-equity ratio and slightly more than $4B of 2026 capital returns, is a reasonable price for that combination.

Bear Case

Strip out the acquisition and growth is thin: ex-ChampionX, Q3 2026 revenue rose just 1.7% YoY (our calculation). Operating margin has gone from 17.55% (FY2024) to 13.30% TTM, ROIC to 10.87%, and nine-month GAAP EPS is down 16%. Well Construction and Reservoir Performance, 46% of Q3 segment revenue, are each down 3% YoY with margins below a year ago. The Middle East is still disrupted — a Saudi pipeline shutdown in September and renewed disruption late in Q3 — and the Q4 guide assumes no recovery there. Kelvion adds about $4.1B of cash and assumed debt at about 11 times EBITDA before cost savings, and 2026 shareholder returns of slightly above $4B exceed the $3.81B of TTM free cash flow. The stock trades at 25.8x trailing GAAP earnings — 57% above the HAL/BKR average — on a data-center business that was 2.4% of Q3 revenue ($230M of $9.45B). 87% of analysts rate it Buy, which is a crowded position, not a margin of safety.

Middle East concentration and geopolitical disruption. Middle East & Asia was $12.22B of FY2025 revenue; conflict cut Middle East revenue 13% sequentially in Q2 2026. It grew 13% in Q3, but some countries saw renewed disruption late in the quarter, Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline in September, and the Q4 outlook assumes no further recovery. There is no operational or financial hedge against it.
02
E&P capital discipline caps the addressable spend. Global upstream capex was ~$420B in 2025; Wood Mackenzie forecast a 2–3% decline in 2026 in January and, in July, still a slight decline even with Brent near $90. Operators are prioritizing returns over volume growth. Service pricing follows spending with a lag, so margin pressure in Reservoir Performance and Well Construction can persist even if oil rallies.
03
Narrative-driven multiple risk on Data Center Solutions. A meaningful part of the 48% twelve-month rise rests on a business that is not yet at a $1B run rate inside a $37B-revenue company, competing against established power and industrial infrastructure providers, and SLB is now paying about $4.1B (cash plus assumed debt) for Kelvion to scale it. Any slip in the Meta campus timeline or in the >$1B/>$2B run-rate milestones removes the justification for SLB's premium to HAL and BKR and exposes how thin growth is without the acquisitions (+1.7% YoY ex-ChampionX in Q3).
  Industry Commentary
International & Offshore Upcycle vs. Weak North American Land
ONGOING HEADWIND
The market has bifurcated. Offshore is genuinely improving — Brazil pushing toward 4.0 mb/d on pre-salt, Guyana toward 1+ mb/d on Stabroek expansion, and management pointing to a stronger international outlook for 2027 on deepwater momentum and new SLB OneSubsea awards. North American land has been the opposite: capital-disciplined shale operators, and Wood Mackenzie flagging North America and Europe as the regions where cuts are offsetting gains elsewhere. SLB's positioning is the right side of this trade — 78% international, and most of its North American growth (+24% YoY in Q3) still comes from ChampionX chemicals rather than drilling. The asymmetry is narrowing: ex-ChampionX global revenue rose 1.7% YoY in Q3 (our calculation) after falling 5% in Q2, as offshore and US land activity began to offset the Middle East.
E&P Capital Discipline Caps Service Demand
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the binding constraint on the whole sector. Global upstream investment was roughly $420B in 2025 (−2.5%) and Wood Mackenzie's January forecast had it down another 2–3% in 2026 on sub-$60/bbl crude. Its July mid-year outlook still had spending on course for a slight decline although Brent averaged $91 in the first half: higher oil prices have not turned into a spending surge. Operators are also getting more barrels per dollar, which is a problem for service companies, since efficiency gains are deflationary for service revenue. Oilfield services is late-cycle and capex-dependent: SLB's revenue is a derivative of a spending pool that is shrinking, and no amount of share gain fixes a contracting TAM. It shows up directly in the numbers — operating margin 17.55% (FY2024) → 13.30% (TTM), ROIC → 10.87%.
Digital & AI in Oilfield Operations — and the Bull/Bear Frame
EMERGING TAILWIND
In digital and AI for oil and gas, SLB is the clear scale player: Delfi, Lumi, Agora and Tela underpin $1.04B of ARR growing 13% YoY, plus a Data Center Solutions business that grew 76% YoY in Q3 and that, combined with Kelvion, would have had more than $2B of 2026 data-center revenue on a pro forma basis (SLB). Bull framing: if Digital plus All Other reach ~$6–7B of combined revenue at ~28% margins by 2028, roughly 20% of SLB's revenue would carry software-like economics and near-zero oil-price correlation — which would justify a permanent re-rating above HAL and BKR and change what the trough of the next cycle looks like. Bear framing: those two lines are 12.6% of FY2025 revenue today, All Other actually declined 6% in FY2025, and Well Construction and Reservoir Performance, 46% of Q3 segment revenue, are still below last year with lower margins. Buy the first story and 18.24x forward earnings is cheap; buy the second and you are paying 25.77x trailing for a business whose drilling core is still shrinking year on year.
