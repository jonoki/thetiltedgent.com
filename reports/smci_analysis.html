01 Company Overview

Supermicro builds the physical machines that artificial intelligence runs on: rack-scale servers packed with Nvidia and other accelerators, storage systems, and increasingly the liquid-cooling plumbing and "data center building blocks" (DCBBS) that turn a warehouse into an AI factory. Fiscal 2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) revenue was $39.06B, up 77.8% from $21.97B, with GAAP net income of about $2.2B and diluted EPS of $3.26. Management then guided fiscal 2027 to $65–72B after booking more than $60B of orders in a single quarter.

Those are the numbers of a company doubling in size. The market pays 12.6x trailing earnings for them — while Dell, selling into the same AI build-out, trades at 33.2x. The gap is not about demand. It is about trust. In twenty-four months Supermicro has survived a short-seller report (August 2024), a delayed annual report, the resignation of its auditor Ernst & Young (October 2024), ejection from the NASDAQ-100 after five months (December 2024), an adverse auditor opinion on its internal controls (February 2025), a still-unfilled CFO search promised in December 2024, and — on March 19, 2026 — the criminal indictment of co-founder and board member Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw for allegedly diverting about $2.5B of Nvidia-equipped servers to China. The company itself was not charged. Then, in June 2026, it sold $5.6B of new equity to fund working capital. The central question: is this a growth stock wearing a governance discount that will close as the controls get fixed — or a thin-margin assembler whose discount is the correct price for a business that keeps drawing the wrong kind of headlines?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Building-block design speed Liquid-cooling capacity (>3,000 racks/mo) US manufacturing footprint Full-stack DCBBS offering Founder-led, 12.6% insider-owned

The honest description of the moat is speed, not ownership. Supermicro does not make the GPU, the CPU, the memory or the network chips. What it sells is the ability to turn a new accelerator generation into working, cooled racks faster and in more configurations than larger rivals — plus, increasingly, the rest of the data-center shell around them. That is a real edge in a market moving this fast, and a fragile one: it is an execution advantage that has to be re-won every product cycle, against Dell and HPE, whose market caps are 13.5x and 3x Supermicro's and whose governance does not come with a footnote. Gross margin of 10.82% on a trailing basis is the scoreboard: this is a business that adds value, but not much of it per dollar of revenue.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Controls that still are not fixed. BDO issued an adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting in February 2025 and reaffirmed it for fiscal 2025. As of March 31, 2026 the material weaknesses — IT controls, segregation of duties, information completeness and related-party disclosure among them — "remain unremediated," per the company's 10-Q (CFO Dive). The fiscal 2026 10-K, accepted August 31, 2026, still refers to "previously identified material weaknesses"; whether BDO's FY2026 opinion is again adverse was not confirmed from pages fetched.
02
Export-control exposure. The March 2026 US indictment alleges a co-founder, a Taiwan sales manager and a contractor diverted about $2.5B of servers to China, including by fooling the compliance team with "dummy" servers. On August 25, 2026 Taiwanese prosecutors separately indicted nine people, including two Supermicro Taiwan employees. The company says it is not a target in Taiwan and was not named as a defendant in the US — but it sells a controlled product, and every future shipment is now read through this lens.
03
Growth funded by shareholders. With trailing operating cash flow at −$6.81B, the company sold 45.45M common shares at $27.50 and $3.75B of 7.0% mandatory convertible preferred in June 2026 — $5.6B net in total — on top of convertible notes due 2028, 2029 and 2030. The preferred converts into a variable number of common shares around June 1, 2029. Market cap is up 1.4% year over year while the share price is down 10.06%: the difference is dilution.
04
One quarter of margin is not a trend. Q4 FY26 GAAP gross margin hit 17.5% versus 9.9% in Q3. Management attributed about a quarter of the jump to lower tariff costs and reduced inventory reserves it called potentially nonrecurring, and guided Q1 FY27 gross margin back to 10.4–10.8%.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, the Q4 FY2026 gross-margin spike to 17.5%: about 75% of the sequential improvement came from mix (including contract deferrals into FY2027) and about 25% from lower tariff costs and reduced inventory reserves that management itself called potentially nonrecurring. On the preliminary update of July 21, 2026, margins were guided at 15–17% against an original 8.2–8.4%. Strip Q4 out and fiscal 2026 was a ~10% gross-margin year. Second, dilution: the June 2026 offerings (45.45M common shares at $27.50, plus 75M depositary shares of 7.0% mandatory convertible preferred at $50) raised $5.6B after expenses; EPS from here is spread over more shares, and the preferred dividend comes ahead of common holders. Third, GAAP vs non-GAAP: FY2026 GAAP EPS was $3.26 against non-GAAP $3.63 — an 11% gap (the itemised reconciliation was not retrieved).

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

On a split-adjusted basis SMCI went from $3.66 at the end of September 2021 to $41.20 on September 21, 2026 — an 11.3x move, +1,026%, with no dividends to add. That headline hides a round trip. The stock ran from about $10.50 in April 2023 to a $101.00 month-end close in March 2024, the month it joined the S&P 500, and it has not been back since: today's price is 59% below that month-end peak. The last two and a half years have been a range between the low $20s and the high $50s, with the governance headlines, not the revenue line, deciding which end of the range the stock sits at.

Since mid-2024 it has swung between roughly 30 and 68, touching 29.5 in October 2024 (the auditor-resignation month, the only sub-30 reading in the window). It sits at 57.1 now, neutral. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 12.6x trailing and about 9.5x forward earnings, 0.69x sales and a PEG of 0.44 for a company that grew revenue 77.8% last year, booked more than $60B of orders in one quarter and guides to $65–72B of revenue this year — while Dell trades at 33x for the same market. The worst governance chapters are behind it: the filings are current, no restatement was ever required, the special committee found no misconduct, the company was not charged in the export case, and the $5.6B raise removes the near-term funding question. If the controls get a clean opinion and a new CFO arrives, even a partial close of the gap to peer multiples is a large move — and 14% short interest adds fuel to any good news.

Bear Case

Supermicro earns about 10 cents of gross profit on each dollar of other companies' chips, burned $6.97B of free cash flow in the last twelve months, and pays for its growth by issuing shares and preferred stock. Its internal controls have carried a material weakness for two years, its CFO succession is 21 months overdue, and a co-founder faces trial for export violations on the company's own product. Q4's 17.5% gross margin was partly borrowed from FY2027 and partly nonrecurring, and management already guides back to ~10.6%. A low multiple on a business with thin margins, negative cash flow and a governance discount is not cheap — it is priced. Susquehanna's $15 target is the version of this where the discount widens instead.

Top 3 Risks

01
Governance and controls. Unremediated material weaknesses (as of March 31, 2026), no permanent CFO successor, an acting chief compliance officer, and a history that includes a 2018 trading suspension and the 2024 auditor resignation. Another control failure would land on a stock with no benefit of the doubt left.
02
Export-control and legal exposure. Live criminal cases in the US and Taiwan over diverted AI servers. The company is not charged in either, but it ships controlled products and its compliance program was, per the indictment, deceived with dummy servers. Any action naming the company or restricting its licences would hit the order book directly.
03
Cash conversion and dilution. Trailing operating cash flow of −$6.81B means growth consumes capital. Converting a $60B-plus order book requires funding inventory ahead of payment; if customers slow payments or orders slip, more equity or debt follows, and the mandatory convertible preferred converts into common around June 2029.
Industry Commentary
Export controls on AI hardware
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The most advanced AI accelerators cannot be sold into China without a US licence, and the FY2026 10-K flags "significant export control and sanctions risk, including evolving U.S. restrictions on advanced computing and semiconductor exports" that could limit sales to China and the Middle East. The March 2026 US indictment alleges servers were shipped through Taiwan and Southeast Asia with markings removed and routed on to China; the August 2026 Taiwan case covers servers transshipped via Indonesia and Japan and 56 more seized at Taiwan's border. For a server integrator, compliance is not a back-office cost — it is a licence to operate, and customers weighing Supermicro against Dell and HPE can price that risk into who gets the order.

Thin margins in a supplier's market
ONGOING HEADWIND

In AI servers most of the value sits in the accelerator, which the integrator buys in. That caps gross margin: Supermicro's trailing 10.82% and guided 10.4–10.8% for Q1 FY27 are the structural level, and Q4's 17.5% needed favourable mix, deferrals and lower tariffs to happen. Liang's own framing — balancing GPU-system revenue against higher-margin CPU and storage — is the whole industry's problem. The route to better economics is selling more of the data center around the GPU: liquid cooling, networking, storage and the DCBBS building blocks. That is where Supermicro's capacity build (6,000-plus racks a month, 3,000-plus liquid-cooled) is aimed.

AI build-out demand
EMERGING TAILWIND

Demand is not the constraint. Supermicro took more than $60B of orders in a single quarter; Dell's market cap rose 317% over roughly a year and HPE's 151% on the same spending wave. The bull framing: a company growing 66–84% into a market this strong, at a third of its peers' multiple, only needs to stop making the wrong headlines for the gap to narrow. The bear framing: in a boom every server maker has demand; what separates them is who converts it to cash at an acceptable margin without diluting shareholders — and on that test Supermicro's record is the weakest of the three. The tiebreaker is observable: gross margin holding near guidance, operating cash flow turning positive, and a clean controls opinion.
