01 Company Overview

Supermicro builds the physical machines that artificial intelligence runs on: rack-scale servers packed with Nvidia and other accelerators, storage systems, and increasingly the liquid-cooling plumbing and "data center building blocks" (DCBBS) that turn a warehouse into an AI factory. In fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter ended September 30, 2026) net sales were $19.9B, up 298% from a year earlier and 79% from the prior quarter, with GAAP net income of $1.59B and diluted EPS of $1.75. Management then raised its fiscal 2027 revenue guide to $72–82B from $65–72B, against $39.06B in fiscal 2026.

Those are the numbers of a company doubling in size. The market pays 6.1x trailing earnings for them, while Dell, selling into the same AI build-out, trades at 19.2x. The gap is not about demand. It is about trust and cash. Since August 2024 Supermicro has survived a short-seller report, a delayed annual report, the resignation of its auditor Ernst & Young (October 2024), ejection from the NASDAQ-100 after five months (December 2024), an adverse auditor opinion on its internal controls (February 2025), and, on March 19, 2026, the criminal indictment of co-founder and then-director Yih-Shyan "Wally" Liaw for allegedly diverting about $2.5B of Nvidia-equipped servers to China. The company itself was not charged, and an independent investigation completed in August 2026 found no evidence that current senior management knew of the alleged scheme. In June 2026 it sold $5.6B of new equity to fund working capital, and in October it finally named a new CFO. Cash is still going out: operating cash flow over the last twelve months was −$6.33B. The central question: is this a growth stock whose discount will close as the controls get fixed and cash comes in, or a thin-margin assembler whose discount is the correct price for one customer at 44% of sales and a record that keeps drawing the wrong kind of headlines?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Building-block design speed Liquid-cooling capacity (4,000+ racks/mo targeted by December) US manufacturing footprint Full-stack DCBBS offering Founder-led, 12.5% insider-owned

The honest description of the moat is speed, not ownership. Supermicro does not make the GPU, the CPU, the memory or the network chips. What it sells is the ability to turn a new accelerator generation into working, cooled racks faster and in more configurations than larger rivals — plus, increasingly, the rest of the data-center shell around them. That is a real edge in a market moving this fast, and a fragile one: it is an execution advantage that has to be re-won every product cycle, against Dell and HPE, whose market caps are 11x and 3.1x Supermicro's and whose governance does not come with a footnote. Gross margin of 11.67% on a trailing basis is the scoreboard: this is a business that adds value, but not much of it per dollar of revenue.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Controls: better, not finished. BDO issued an adverse opinion on internal control over financial reporting in February 2025 and reaffirmed it for fiscal 2025. The FY2026 10-K reports that three of the four material weaknesses (serious gaps in the checks on financial reporting) have been fixed, and says internal control over financial reporting was still not effective at June 30, 2026. The one left is in information-technology controls, and the Q1 FY2027 10-Q says it was still open at September 30, 2026, with disclosure controls judged not effective.
02
Export-control exposure. The March 2026 US indictment alleges a co-founder, a manager in the Taiwan office and a contractor diverted about $2.5B of servers to China, including by using "dummy" servers to fool inspectors. On August 24, 2026 Taiwanese prosecutors separately said they had indicted nine people, including two linked to Supermicro's Taiwan unit, whom the company calls former employees. The company says it is not a target in Taiwan and was not named as a defendant in the US. On August 20, 2026 it said an independent investigation found no evidence of direct sales of controlled products to known restricted parties, but its 10-K warns that authorities "are not bound by the conclusions of the internal investigation," and it discloses a grand jury subpoena from federal prosecutors in Manhattan and an SEC subpoena for documents on certain customers.
03
Growth funded by shareholders. With trailing operating cash flow at −$6.33B, the company sold common shares at $27.50 (45.45M priced, before the underwriters' option) and $4.31B of 7.0% mandatory convertible preferred (preferred stock that must turn into common shares; 4,312,500 preferred shares outstanding at September 30) in June 2026, $5.6B net in total, on top of convertible notes due 2028, 2029 and 2030. The preferred converts into a variable number of common shares around June 1, 2029, and its dividend cost $75.8M in Q1. Weighted-average basic shares rose 10.5% in a year (595.6M to 658.1M) while the share price fell 28.71%: each share now owns a smaller piece.
04
Margin is guided down from here. Q1 FY27 GAAP gross margin of 12.7% beat the 10.4–10.8% guide but fell from 17.5% in Q4. Long guided Q2 to 12–12.5% and the full year to 11.8–12.2%, which implies a lower second half as large AI systems take a bigger share. The sales total also included about $2.3B of catch-up from earlier periods.
05
One customer is 44% of sales. A single large data-center customer was 44.0% of Q1 FY27 net sales (10-Q), against 28% for all of fiscal 2026 (Q1 call). Two customers made up 49.0% and 16.8% of receivables at September 30. A delay or renegotiation by one buyer now moves the whole company.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported numbers. First, the Q4 and Q1 margins: Q4 FY2026 gross margin spiked to 17.5%, which management put down largely to customer and product mix plus a one-time benefit in the quarter. Q1 FY2027 then fell back to 12.7%, with about $2.3B of catch-up from earlier periods in its sales. On the call management put the two quarters together at about 14.5% on a non-GAAP basis, against about 9.5% a year earlier. Second, dilution: the June 2026 offerings (45.45M common shares at $27.50 before the underwriters' option, plus 7.0% mandatory convertible preferred sold as depositary shares at $50, each a 1/20 interest in a preferred share, 86.25M of them or $4.31B including the option) raised $5.64B after expenses. The company guides to 753M diluted GAAP shares for Q2, against 659.23M common shares outstanding, because convertibles and the preferred count in the diluted total. Third, GAAP vs non-GAAP: Q1 GAAP EPS was $1.75 against non-GAAP $1.81, and Nasdaq reports its own $1.70 for the same quarter.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

On a split-adjusted basis SMCI went from $4.14 at the end of November 2021 to $28.76 on November 6, 2026: a 6.9x move, +595%, with no dividends to add. That headline hides a round trip. The stock ran from about $10.50 in April 2023 to a $101.00 month-end close in March 2024, the month it joined the S&P 500, and it has not been back since: today's price is 71.5% below that month-end peak. Since October 2024 month-end closes have ranged from $22.77 to $58.97, with the governance headlines, not the revenue line, deciding which end of the range the stock sits at.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows two stretches. It printed 92–96 in May–July 2023 and again in February–March 2024, the most one-sided buying in the series, peaking at 95.8. Since mid-2024 it has swung between roughly 30 and 68, touching 29.5 in October 2024 (the auditor-resignation month, the only sub-30 reading in the window). It sits at 42.7 now, neutral. The price is below both its 3-month average (~$34.26) and its 10-month average (~$32.64), and on daily closes it is 23.2% below its 50-day average and 11.8% below its 200-day average: the recovery from the March 2026 low has stalled.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 6.1x trailing and about 4.6x forward earnings (fiscal 2027 consensus, non-GAAP), 0.35x sales and a PEG of 0.21 for a company whose quarterly sales nearly quadrupled in a year, which ended the quarter with a record backlog and raised its revenue guide to $72–82B, while Dell trades at 19x in the same market. The governance list is getting shorter: the filings are current, three of four material weaknesses are fixed, a new CFO is in the seat, the independent investigation found no evidence that current senior management knew of the alleged diversion, and the company has not been charged in the export case. Customer prepayments now fund much of the inventory build. If the last weakness clears, even a partial close of the gap to peers' price-to-earnings ratios is a large move, and with 12% of the shares sold short, short sellers buying back could add to any rise.

Bear Case

Supermicro earns about 12 cents of gross profit on each dollar of mostly other companies' chips, burned $6.50B of free cash flow in the last twelve months, and has paid for its growth by issuing shares and preferred stock. One customer is 44% of sales. About $2.3B of Q1's sales was catch-up from earlier periods, margin is guided lower through the year, and the midpoint of the raised guide implies a second half below the first. Its internal controls still carry a material weakness, a co-founder faces trial over exports of the company's own product, and the company has disclosed grand jury and SEC subpoenas. A low price-to-earnings ratio on a business with thin margins, negative cash flow and a governance discount is not cheap, it is priced. The $15 low target on the board is the version of this where the discount widens instead.

Top 3 Risks

01
Governance and controls. One unremediated material weakness in information-technology controls, disclosure controls judged not effective at September 30, 2026, a CFO less than a month into the job, an acting chief compliance officer, and a history that includes a 2018 trading suspension and the 2024 auditor resignation. Another control failure would land on a stock with no benefit of the doubt left.
02
Export-control and legal exposure. Live criminal cases in the US and Taiwan over diverted AI servers, plus grand jury and SEC subpoenas disclosed in the 10-K. The company is not charged in either case, and its own investigation cleared current senior management, but the indictment alleges dummy servers were used to fool inspectors, and authorities are not bound by that investigation. Any action naming the company or restricting its licences would hit the order book directly.
03
Cash, dilution and one big customer. Trailing operating cash flow of −$6.33B means growth consumes capital. Inventories of $15.05B and receivables of $9.69B are funded ahead of payment, and two customers account for 65.8% of those receivables. If either slows payment or orders slip, more equity or debt follows, and the mandatory convertible preferred converts into common around June 2029.
Industry Commentary
Export controls on AI hardware
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The most advanced AI accelerators cannot be sold into China without a US licence, and the FY2026 10-K says current controls require licences to ship such items to China and most countries in the Middle East. The March 2026 US indictment alleges servers were shipped through Taiwan and Southeast Asia, repackaged in unmarked boxes and routed on to China; the August 2026 Taiwan case covers servers transshipped via Indonesia and Japan and 56 more seized at Taiwan's border. For a server integrator, compliance is not a back-office cost — it is a licence to operate, and customers weighing Supermicro against Dell and HPE can price that risk into who gets the order.

Thin margins in a supplier's market
ONGOING HEADWIND

In AI servers most of the value sits in the accelerator, which the integrator buys in. That caps gross margin: Supermicro's trailing 11.67% and the 11.8–12.2% management guides for fiscal 2027 are closer to the structural level than Q4's 17.5%, which management said included a one-time benefit. Long said the sequential decline "is not evidence that AI platform revenue lacks margin," yet the guide still falls as AI takes a larger share. The route to better economics is selling more of the data center around the GPU: liquid cooling, networking, storage and the DCBBS building blocks. That is where Supermicro's capacity build (8,000-plus racks a month targeted by December, 4,000-plus liquid-cooled) is aimed.

AI build-out demand
EMERGING TAILWIND

Demand is not the main constraint. On the Q1 call Long said the binding limits on AI build-outs across the industry are "land, power and data center shells," and Liang pointed to shortages of key components. Dell's shares rose 120% over the last year and HPE's 91% on the same spending wave, then all three fell sharply on November 4 and 5. The bull framing: a company guiding to +84–110% revenue growth in a market this strong, at about a third of its peers' price-to-earnings ratios, only needs to stop making the wrong headlines for the gap to narrow. The bear framing: in a boom every server maker has demand; what separates them is who converts it to cash at an acceptable margin without diluting shareholders, and with one customer at 44% of sales Supermicro's position is less secure than the revenue line suggests. The tiebreaker is observable: gross margin holding near guidance, operating cash flow turning positive, and a clean controls opinion.
