01 Company Overview

Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group is the holding company of Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation (SMBC), one of Japan's three megabanks, together with SMBC Nikko Securities, Sumitomo Mitsui Card, SMBC Consumer Finance, SMBC Trust Bank and the aircraft lessor SMBC Aviation Capital. SMBC was formed in 2001 from the merger of Sakura Bank (the old Mitsui Bank line) and Sumitomo Bank; the holding company was set up on December 2, 2002 (SMFG company history and company overview; Wikipedia for the megabank grouping and the Mitsui Bank line). At September 30, 2026 the group had ¥326.2tn of total assets, ¥117.0tn of loans and ¥199.6tn of deposits, a loan-to-deposit ratio of 58.6% (SMFG Overview of 1H FY3/2027). Its home listing is Tokyo (8316); the NYSE line is an American Depositary Share.

The fiscal year to March 2026 (SMFG calls it FY3/26) was a record: Japanese-GAAP net income of ¥1,583.0bn, up 34.4%, and an ROE of 10.4%. The first half of the new year (April–September 2026, reported November 13) set another record: net income ¥1,003.2bn, +7.5%, the first half-year above ¥1tn, and SMFG raised its full-year target to ¥1.9tn from ¥1.7tn, 20% above FY3/26. The July–September quarter on its own was ¥501.8bn, about 10% below a year earlier, when the half included a ¥94bn gain on selling Kotak Mahindra Bank shares. The engine is the end of Japan's zero-rate era. The Bank of Japan has taken its policy rate from negative to 0.25% (July 2024), 0.5% (January 2025), 0.75% (December 2025), 1.0% (June 2026) and 1.25% from September 24, 2026 (BOJ statement, September 18), and SMFG's revised plan now assumes 1.25% instead of 0.75%. SMBC's domestic loan-to-deposit spread, the gap between what its loans earn and its deposits cost, was 1.34% in the half against 1.10% a year earlier, and 1.38% in July–September, so the yen-rate story shows up in lending margins, not only in deposit income.

A new three-year plan started in April 2026. It switches the headline target to ROTE: 13% by FY3/29 (11.4% in FY3/26) on net income of ¥2tn, and 15% over the longer term. At the May 18 investor meeting Group CEO Toru Nakashima said: "As we establish ourselves as a leading global player, we are committed to achieving a ROTE of 15% over the medium- to long-term." With its FY3/26 results on May 13 the company had announced a 2-for-1 share split (effective October 1, 2026) and a ¥180bn buyback (finished July 31), and the May 18 presentation added a promise to raise the dividend every year. On November 13 it lifted the dividend forecast to ¥100 per post-split share from ¥90 and added a ¥220bn buyback, ¥400bn for the year. First-half ROTE was 13.9%, already above the FY3/29 goal. The question for the stock at about 1.6x book: how much of the jump in returns is the rate cycle doing the work, and how much will survive once the BOJ stops raising rates?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

¥200tn deposit base Domestic corporate lending scale Olive digital retail (8.0M accounts) Cards & payments (Sumitomo Mitsui Card) SMBC Nikko + Jefferies alliance Aircraft leasing (SMBC Aviation Capital)

The moat is cheap yen funding that pays again, attached to the clients who borrow it. SMBC funds ¥137.3tn of domestic deposits (September 30, 2026), about two-thirds of them savings deposits that reprice slowly on SMFG's rounded yen balance sheet, and lends into a corporate market where demand is strong; that combination is why its loan-to-deposit spread is widening rather than just its deposit income. The second layer is reach into the household: Olive, cards and consumer finance give SMFG payment and lending relationships most banks would have to buy. SMFG's published Q&A from the May 18 meeting argues that on transaction volume, consumer finance balances and assets under management it is "already approaching the level of Japan's No.1 financial institution," though it still trails on retail deposits. The weakness is overseas, where the Global unit earns less on its capital than the domestic units and the Asian franchises have so far fallen short of their original profit targets.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The Global unit drags on returns. Its net business profit fell ¥47.7bn in the first half while the other three units grew, and its overhead ratio was 69.6% in Q1. SMFG's published Q&A says it will re-accelerate overseas asset growth only once the unit reaches "at least 10% ROTE"; until then it is a capital user that earns below the group target.
02
The multi-franchise strategy is behind schedule. In the presentation script: "the Multi-franchise Strategy has not yet delivered the results initially expected." YES BANK (24.9%, about ¥300bn invested) is the newest and largest bet; the FY3/29 goal of ¥140bn of net income contribution compares with ¥50bn in FY3/26 and ¥47bn in the first half of FY3/27.
03
Deposit competition and the rate path. Deposit costs rose from 0.18% to 0.34% between the first halves of FY3/26 and FY3/27. If the BOJ pauses, the +¥110bn-per-0.25-point sensitivity stops adding, while competition for deposits can keep pushing funding costs up.
04
Unrealized gains weigh on ROTE. Large unrealized gains on equity holdings inflate the equity base (ROE including OCI was 12.7% in the first half against 16.7% on shareholders' equity alone). SMFG says it is exploring ways to use those gains, but disposals "are not factored into the Plan at this stage."

Notable One-Off Items

Four things flatter or distort the comparisons. First, gains on stocks: the first half's ¥161.6bn was ¥84.7bn below a year earlier, when the half included ¥94bn from selling Kotak Mahindra Bank shares; this half had ¥133bn of gains on selling client equity stakes (¥41bn less), ¥28bn more profit from ETFs, and no repeat of last year's ¥28bn loss on selling Bank of East Asia shares. Second, FY3/26 "one-off profits" of +¥224bn after tax (higher stock gains, Global Markets, aircraft-leasing insurance receipts) were largely spent on what SMFG calls "measures for future" of ¥188bn: bond-portfolio rebalancing, ¥46bn of forward-looking provisions, the reorganisation of a US banking subsidiary, an allowance for dormant deposits, sales of low-return assets and bad-loan disposals at the OTO/SOF subsidiary. Third, FX: the weak yen added ¥74bn to first-half gross profit and ¥39bn to expenses. Fourth, a restatement: on November 13 SMFG corrected its FY3/26 accounts for an error in the fair value of monetary claims bought, cutting March 2026 net assets by ¥68.2bn; profit was not changed. Separately, the 2-for-1 split on October 1, 2026 halves every per-share yen figure from that date; SMFG's restated FY3/26 EPS is ¥205.99 per post-split share.

Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Japan's rate rise is reaching SMFG's lending margins, not just its deposits: the domestic loan-to-deposit spread widened 0.24 percentage points in a year to 1.34% in the first half (1.38% in July–September), and the BOJ went to 1.25% in September, half a point above the plan's original assumption. First-half net income passed ¥1tn for the first time and the full-year target rose to ¥1.9tn. Wholesale, Retail and Global Markets all grew, and both the dividend (¥100 per share) and buybacks (¥400bn this year) went up. At 14.8x trailing earnings, 12.8x SMFG's own FY3/27 forecast and 1.56x book, with ROTE at 13.9% against a 13% FY3/29 target, the stock does not require heroic assumptions if rates stay where they are.

Bear Case

Much of the growth is the rate cycle and the market. July–September profit was about 10% below a year earlier; gains on stock sales fell and credit costs rose ¥43bn once a large reversal a year earlier did not repeat. The overseas unit is shrinking by design and its profit fell ¥47.7bn in the half; management itself calls the Asian strategy behind plan. Deposit costs are rising (0.18% → 0.34%), the CET1 ratio slipped to 11.99% from 12.63% in three months, and the higher ¥1.9tn target leans on rates (+¥95bn of the ¥200bn rise). The ADS has more than tripled in five years; Tokyo analysts' average target implies about 33% upside, but on the US line the only rating changes since the end of July are three downgrades by quantitative ratings services.

Top 3 Risks

01
A BOJ pause or reversal. SMFG's earnings growth plan rests on rates at or above today's 1.25%. A pause stops the +¥110bn-per-0.25-point increments; deposit competition would keep eating into the spread either way.
02
Quality of earnings. Market-driven lines (Global Markets, gains on ETFs and equity stakes) were a large share of this year's growth: Global Markets alone added ¥92.4bn of the half's ¥255.2bn rise in net business profit. If markets turn, the headline ROTE falls back toward the 11.4% of FY3/26.
03
Overseas execution and credit. India (YES BANK), Indonesia, Vietnam and the Philippines need to deliver almost three times FY3/26's contribution by FY3/29. A credit cycle abroad or in Japanese consumer finance would show up in a cost line SMFG already budgets at ¥340bn.
Industry Commentary
Japan's banks are earning on deposits again
ACTIVE TAILWIND

From March 2024 to September 2026 the BOJ took its rate from negative to 1.25% in six steps. For a deposit-funded bank the arithmetic is simple: deposits that earned nothing for a decade can now be placed or lent at a positive rate, and the loan book reprices faster than savings deposits. SMFG credits higher rates with +¥95bn of the ¥200bn rise in its FY3/27 profit target. The risk on the other side is competition: Japan's domestic deposits have edged down (SMFG's chart of BOJ data shows about ¥640tn in March 2024 and ¥629tn in February 2026), so banks now have to pay to keep them.

Cross-shareholding unwinds
FINITE TAILWIND

Japanese banks long held shares in their corporate clients to cement relationships; governance pressure is ending the practice. SMFG plans to cut ¥600bn of book value over five years and had done ¥357bn by September 2026. Each sale books a gain because the stakes were bought long ago, which flatters profit while it lasts. Rising Japanese share prices have kept the market value high, so the remaining stakes still weigh on capital efficiency; SMFG's first-half overview: "Focus on reducing the market value, as the ratio has risen due to higher share prices."

Megabanks chasing global-bank returns
STRATEGIC SHIFT

SMFG's switch from ROE to ROTE is explicitly about comparison with international peers; its presentation says leading global players "typically target ROTE of 15-20%." Getting there means moving capital from low-return overseas lending to fee businesses (investment banking with Jefferies, sales & trading, asset management) and to the Asian franchises, while defending the domestic base. The plan's three-year milestone is 13% ROTE on ¥2tn of net income; the first half's 13.9% says the cycle is ahead of the plan, not that the plan is done.
