01 Company Overview

SanDisk Corporation is a pure-play NAND flash memory and storage company — NAND is the non-volatile ("keeps data when the power is off") memory inside SSDs (solid-state drives), memory cards and USB drives. It designs and, through a long-running manufacturing joint venture with Japan's Kioxia, fabricates 3D NAND wafers, then sells them as raw components, client and enterprise SSDs, and retail-branded storage. SanDisk was carved out of Western Digital on February 21, 2025 as a standalone NASDAQ-listed company, so its entire public trading record spans roughly eighteen months.

Fiscal 2026 (ended July 3, 2026) revenue was $20.25B, up 175% YoY from $7.36B in FY2025, with gross margin expanding from 30.1% to 71.5% for the year and 84.6% in the June quarter alone. That is not a volume story: management attributes roughly two-thirds of sequential Q4 growth to price and one-third to bits shipped. SanDisk holds roughly 13.9% of global NAND revenue (TrendForce, 1Q26), tied with Micron and Kioxia for third place behind Samsung (31.6%) and the SK hynix group (17.6%). At a $266.2B market capitalisation it is the ~64th most valuable listed company in the world and was the single best-performing stock in the S&P 500 in 2026 — up roughly 4,775% from its first post-spin close.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Kioxia JV Fab Scale BiCS 3D NAND Co-Development Yokkaichi + Kitakami $93.9B NBM Backlog Enterprise SSD Qualifications #1 Retail Flash Brand Zero Long-Term Debt

SanDisk's real moat is Flash Ventures, the manufacturing joint venture it has run with Kioxia (formerly Toshiba Memory) since 2000. The two companies jointly own and fund the Yokkaichi and Kitakami fabs in Japan — together the largest NAND production complex on earth — and split the output. This is a genuinely unusual cost structure: SanDisk gets roughly half the scale economics of a top-two NAND producer while carrying only about half the wafer capex, and it shares R&D on the BiCS 3D NAND roadmap with a partner rather than funding a node transition alone. The result is visible in the numbers — gross capital spending was just 6.3% of Q4 revenue and is guided to roughly 6% for FY2027, with mid-to-high-teens bit growth delivered through node transitions (BiCS8 → BiCS10) rather than new wafer starts. Layered on top of that is a genuinely new contractual moat: eight New Business Model customers have signed multi-year agreements with floor-and-ceiling pricing covering more than half of FY2027 bits and about two-thirds of FY2028 bits, at roughly 80% gross margin even at the floor. If those contracts hold, SanDisk has converted a commodity into something closer to a take-or-pay utility. If.

Key Vulnerabilities
▸
Commodity price exposure is the entire P&L. Two-thirds of Q4's sequential growth came from price, not volume. NAND is a fungible good with five credible suppliers; the FY2026 gross margin swing of +41 points and the FY2023 operating loss of $2.04B are the same business under different pricing. A 30% decline in contract ASPs — smaller than several historical NAND down-legs — would remove the large majority of current operating income, because the cost base barely moves.
▸
No vertical integration, no DRAM, no captive demand. Samsung and SK hynix sell DRAM, HBM and NAND into the same customers and can cross-subsidise a NAND price war indefinitely; Samsung also consumes its own flash in phones. SanDisk is a single-product company with roughly half of one fab complex. It has no second leg to stand on if NAND alone rolls over, and no captive internal demand to absorb bits.
▸
The JV cuts both ways. Flash Ventures means shared capacity decisions, shared capex obligations, and equity-method accounting that makes true unit economics harder to see from the outside. SanDisk committed roughly $1.165B of manufacturing payments across 2026–2029 under the JV extension through December 2034. Kioxia is simultaneously a partner and a direct competitor at 13.9% share — the two co-develop BiCS and then fight for the same enterprise SSD sockets. Any change in Kioxia's ownership or strategy is a first-order risk to SanDisk's cost base.
▸
NBM contracts concentrate customer and counterparty risk. Eight customers underwrite $93.9B of minimum revenue. That is enormous leverage to a handful of AI infrastructure buyers whose own capex plans are themselves cyclical and, in some cases, financed. SanDisk holds $16.5B of deposits and guarantees against default — a sensible precaution that also tells you the company thinks default is a live scenario. Contract floors are only as good as the counterparty behind them.

Notable One-Off Items

Several distortions matter when reading these numbers. (1) Spin-off accounting. FY2025 was SanDisk's first year as a standalone entity and carried separation costs, stranded-cost absorption and a $1.64B GAAP net loss; year-over-year growth rates are therefore measured off an artificially depressed base and overstate the underlying improvement. (2) GAAP above non-GAAP. Q4 FY26 GAAP net income of $6.90B exceeded non-GAAP net income of $6.16B, and full-year GAAP EPS of $73.76 exceeded non-GAAP EPS of $70.88 — an unusual inversion that points to a discrete tax benefit (most plausibly a valuation-allowance release against losses accumulated during the FY2023–25 downturn). The precise driver could not be verified from the summary release and should be checked against the 10-K. (3) Joint-venture accounting. Flash Ventures is accounted for outside consolidated revenue, which is why the reported asset base and ROIC look far better than the economics of a company that actually depends on those fabs; the $1.165B of committed 2026–2029 manufacturing payments sits partly off the income statement. (4) Inventory revaluation. A rising-price environment mechanically flatters gross margin as low-cost inventory is sold into high-priced contracts — and reverses just as mechanically when prices fall. (5) Buybacks. $4.52B of FY2026 repurchases reduced the share count, modestly inflating per-share growth; guided diluted shares of ~155M for Q1 FY27 are nonetheless above the current 149.0M, implying dilution from equity compensation.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

AI infrastructure has permanently changed NAND's demand curve: datacenter goes from ~30% to ~50% of the market in a single year, and high-capacity QLC SSDs are absorbing workloads that hard drives physically cannot serve. SanDisk has converted that demand into $93.9B of contracted minimum revenue across eight customers at floor pricing that still supports ~80% gross margins, covering more than half of FY2027 bits. Meanwhile the Kioxia JV lets it grow bits mid-to-high-teens through node transitions at 6% capital intensity — so incremental revenue drops through at extraordinary rates. On $11.5B of trailing free cash flow, zero debt and a $15.5B buyback authorisation, a 8.3x forward multiple is not demanding if the contracts hold. The bull case is simply that this is no longer a commodity business.

Bear Case

Every NAND up-cycle in history has ended the same way, and this one is more extreme than any of them: 84.6% gross margins in a business that earned 7.1% three years ago, with two-thirds of growth coming from price. Extraordinary returns are precisely what triggers capacity — Samsung, SK hynix, Kioxia, Micron and YMTC all have the balance sheets to add wafers, and decisions made now become bits in 2027–28. The contract "floors" have never been tested through a downturn, and are only as strong as eight AI-infrastructure counterparties whose own capex is cyclical (SanDisk holds $16.5B of collateral precisely because it knows this). At $266B the market is capitalising peak-cycle earnings at 24x trailing; a return to even mid-cycle margins of 30–40% would cut EPS by two-thirds. The July −47% month showed how fast the exit gets crowded.

Top 3 Risks
01
Cycle roll-over. NAND pricing is the single variable that determines whether SanDisk earns $200 per share or loses money. The FY2023 → FY2026 swing — from a $2.04B operating loss on 7.1% gross margin to $12.4B of operating income on 71.5% — happened with roughly the same asset base. There is no version of this business that is defensive.
02
Capacity additions across the industry. Minimal new capacity is scheduled for 2026, which is why the shortage persists. But five suppliers are all earning record returns simultaneously, and the industry's history of responding to that with wafer additions is essentially unbroken. Watch supplier capex guidance and any greenfield fab announcement — including from China's YMTC, which competes on price rather than return on capital.
03
Contract and counterparty risk in the NBM model. The entire "this time is different" argument rests on eight long-term agreements. If AI infrastructure spending slows, customers will renegotiate, take-or-pay volumes will be disputed, and the floor pricing that underwrites 80% margins will be tested for the first time. Concentration cuts both ways: eight customers can de-risk a cycle or become the cycle.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
NAND Supercycle Economics
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
NAND is a fixed-cost industry with near-zero marginal cost, which means pricing is set by the balance between bit supply and bit demand, and profitability is a step function around it. In 1Q26 the top five suppliers grew revenue +83.7% sequentially to $38.9B almost entirely on ASP, not bits. SanDisk's own arithmetic is the same: two-thirds of Q4's 51% sequential growth was price. The corollary is unforgiving — the FY2023 downturn took gross margin to 7.1% and produced a $2.14B net loss on revenue that was only 30% below FY2026's. This is a business whose earnings power is set almost entirely outside its own control.
AI Storage: HDD → SSD Substitution
EMERGING TAILWIND
The genuinely new element in this cycle is demand-side, not supply-side. AI training and inference generate data lakes that need capacity and throughput; structural shortages in high-capacity hard drives (nearline HDD lead times have stretched to multiple quarters) have pushed hyperscalers toward high-capacity QLC enterprise SSDs as a substitute. That is why datacenter's share of the NAND market moves from ~30% in CY2025 to ~50% in CY2026 and why SanDisk's datacenter revenue grew 437% to $5.15B in one fiscal year, taking the segment from 12% to 38% of the exit-quarter mix. Unlike price, this driver has a real physical basis — but it also depends on AI capex continuing, and on HDD supply staying short.
Supply Discipline — Bull vs. Bear
ONGOING HEADWIND
Bull framing: after three years of losses the industry has genuinely changed behaviour. Suppliers are adding almost no wafers in 2026, SanDisk is growing bits mid-to-high teens purely through BiCS8→BiCS10 node transitions at ~6% capital intensity, and management says bits stay on allocation beyond calendar 2027. Long-term contracts lock in that discipline on both sides. Bear framing: discipline is a function of profitability, and profitability is currently the highest in the industry's history. Five suppliers with record cash flow, a Chinese entrant indifferent to returns, and a 2027–28 demand forecast that assumes AI capex compounds — this is the exact set-up that has preceded every NAND crash. The question is not whether capacity comes; it is whether it arrives before or after the NBM contracts have paid for themselves.
