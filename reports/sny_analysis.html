01 Company Overview

Sanofi is a Paris-based maker of prescription medicines and vaccines, and a member of France's CAC 40 index. Since April 30, 2025, when its consumer-health arm Opella moved into a joint venture with the private-equity firm CD&R (Sanofi kept a 48.2% stake and received €10.4B in net cash), it reports a single Biopharma segment. FY2025 net sales were €43.63B, up 9.9% at CER. Business EPS was €7.83 (+15.0% at CER) and IFRS EPS €6.40. One drug sits at the centre of the story: Dupixent, the antibody for eczema, asthma and other allergic-type inflammation that Sanofi shares with Regeneron. It sold €5.69B in Q3 2026 alone, 39% of group sales.

The latest quarter changed the tone. Q3 2026, released early on October 30, before the Paris and New York opens, beat on every headline line. Net sales rose 14.3% at CER to €14.44B and business EPS rose 19.6% at CER to €3.55, against a consensus of €13.25B and €2.81 (Vara Research consensus hosted by Sanofi, collected October 21). On Nasdaq's earnings calendar the ADS earned $2.04 against a Nasdaq consensus of $1.63. Sanofi raised its 2026 guide again: sales are now expected to grow by a low-teens percentage at CER (from around 10%) and business EPS by a mid-teens percentage. IFRS EPS was €2.52. In July the shares fell on a beat (Paris −8.95%) because the same release cut the pipeline: amlitelimab, the eczema antibody that was meant to follow Dupixent, was dropped (a €952M impairment), and itepekimab and balinatunfib were stopped. This time they rose: the ADS closed up 2.81% on October 30 ($43.13 → $44.34) and Paris 4.71%. The CEO who made those calls is new. The board declined to renew Paul Hudson's mandate in February, and Belén Garijo, who ran Germany's Merck KGaA, took over on April 29, 2026. Since then she has announced plans to hand 20 older medicines to Cheplapharm (September 14) and expanded the Regeneron alliance with a $1B upfront payment for next-generation immunology antibodies (October 1). In the Q3 release she put the task plainly: "Our priority is to protect earnings through Dupixent’s loss of exclusivity." The ADS closed at $45.55 on November 2, 16.6% above its 52-week intraday low of $39.07 (October 13, 2026) and 13.5% below the 52-week high of $52.68 (November 13, 2025).

Dupixent blocks two signalling proteins (IL-4 and IL-13) that drive what doctors call type 2 inflammation. It is approved in nine diseases, from eczema and asthma to COPD and hives, and more than 1.5 million people take it, per the October 1 release. Q3 2026 sales of €5,687M were above €5B in a quarter for the second time. US sales were €4,376M (+40.2%), driven by strong demand and, per the release, favourable gross-to-net price adjustments (changes in the rebates and discounts the company books against list price). Europe was €588M (+16.1%) and the rest of the world €723M (+22.6%). The growth is not free of setbacks: in Q2, two phase 3 studies in lichen simplex chronicus (an itchy skin condition) missed their main goals, and Sanofi withdrew its EU filing in bullous pemphigoid (Q2 release). Kevzara, for rheumatoid arthritis, added €222M (+67.9%). Sanofi shares Dupixent's profits with Regeneron, which invented it; the profit-sharing line took €2,230M out of Q3 operating income, against €1,487M a year earlier.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Dupixent, 9 indications Regeneron antibody alliance Vaccine manufacturing scale Rare-disease franchises (Genzyme) ~€9.6B trailing free cash flow 31 years of dividend increases

Sanofi's moat is mostly one drug and one partnership. Dupixent works across a long list of allergic-type diseases, has more than 1.5 million patients and is still adding indications, and it was built with Regeneron, whose antibody technology produced it. Vaccines add a second, harder-to-copy asset: making flu and paediatric vaccines at global scale takes specialised plants and regulatory approvals. The Genzyme rare-disease enzymes serve small, specialised markets. The weak side is the one the market has been pricing for a year: what comes after Dupixent. In September 2025 the stock fell 9.1% on the day amlitelimab's first phase 3 data came out, even though the trial met its goals; Barclays analyst Emily Field, quoted by Pharmaceutical Executive, said the stock was increasingly seen as a "cliff stock". In July 2026 the new management stopped amlitelimab altogether.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One drug is 39% of sales. Dupixent was €5,687M of €14,444M in Q3 2026 (44% in Q2), and about three-quarters of it is sold in the US. RBC's initiation on September 9 named Dupixent's coming patent cliff among its reasons for a Sector Perform rating.
02
The pipeline keeps losing its lead assets. In the past twelve months: tolebrutinib missed its phase 3 in primary progressive MS and got an FDA complete response letter in the secondary progressive form (a €1,663M impairment in FY2025); amlitelimab will not be filed (€952M impairment); itepekimab (COPD) and balinatunfib (Crohn's and colitis) were discontinued; Dupixent failed in lichen simplex chronicus. RBC also cited scepticism that the pipeline can deliver more than €10B of sales.
03
The partner takes a growing cut. Regeneron profit sharing rose 50% year on year in Q3 (€2,230M vs €1,487M). The October 1 expansion adds four early-stage antibodies on 50:50 terms, for $1B upfront and up to $7B in milestones.
04
Vaccines are shrinking in some markets. Vaccine sales fell 6.6% in Q3, with Beyfortus's US sales down 39.3% on a lower government order. In Q2, paediatric vaccine sales outside the US and Europe fell 21.2%, mainly on fewer births in China. In the US, Sanofi reached an agreement with the government to lower medicine costs (FY2025 release), and the 2025 Dupixent price adjustment in China shows that pricing can move against the company.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the reported figures. First, impairments: €1,663M on tolebrutinib in FY2025 (which turned Q4 2025 into an IFRS loss of €801M) and €952M on amlitelimab in Q2 2026. Second, the Opella transaction: H1 2025 IFRS EPS of €4.74 included €2.34 from discontinued operations, so year-on-year IFRS comparisons look much worse than the business. Third, Q2 2026 items on the good side: the Sarclisa provision reversal (more than €200M), the favourable Dupixent gross-to-net adjustment and €192M of divestment gains, partly offset by more than €200M of R&D wind-down costs. The CEO said business EPS growth was "supported by disciplined cost management and one-offs." Fourth, buybacks: €5B in 2025 and €1B completed in April 2026, which cut the average share count to 1,200.0M in Q3 from 1,218.1M a year earlier. Q3 itself was clean on this front: intangible assets showed a net impairment reversal of €13M, and IFRS EPS rose 9.6% to €2.52.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business is growing fast and the share price has only started to follow. Q3 sales rose 14.3% at CER and business EPS 19.6%, both well ahead of consensus, and the 2026 guide went up again, to low-teens sales growth. Dupixent is still growing fast (+34.8% in Q3) toward management's ~€25B in 2030, with ~€10B more from launches. At $45.55 the ADS trades at about 9x forward business EPS, with a 5.3% dividend yield on a dividend that has risen for 31 straight years, and a free-cash-flow yield near 10%. The Paris-line average target is 16% above the price. A new CEO is cutting weak programmes early instead of spending on them for years, and she has already locked in the next generation of long-acting Dupixent-type antibodies with the partner that invented Dupixent. If even one of those works, the cliff is smaller than the price implies.

Bear Case

Sanofi is a one-drug company with a weak pipeline, and the numbers show it. Dupixent was 39% of Q3 sales and 44% of Q2's; tolebrutinib, amlitelimab, itepekimab and balinatunfib have all been written down or stopped in about a year. Q3's US Dupixent growth was driven in part by favourable gross-to-net price adjustments, and Lantus is enjoying temporary demand from competitors' supply problems that the release expects to moderate. The CEO herself describes the job as protecting earnings through Dupixent's loss of exclusivity. The Regeneron deal buys antibodies that are mostly not yet in human trials, for $1B upfront and up to $7B more, and RBC still rates the stock Sector Perform. The stock is down 4% in five years while the CAC 40 is up 20%: a low P/E has not been enough to lift it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Dupixent concentration. 39% of Q3 sales from one drug, about three-quarters of it in the US. Any pricing, competitive or patent event on Dupixent moves the whole company.
02
Pipeline attrition. Four lead programmes impaired or stopped since late 2025. The replacement plan now rests on the launch portfolio, early-stage Regeneron antibodies and business development and M&A, which the CEO called "critical to complement this transformation" in the Q3 release.
03
Q4 and the 2027 guide. The raised guide leaves Q4 to deliver the rest of the year, with more flu-vaccine deliveries shifted into it than in 2025, and January 28 brings the first full-year guide set by Garijo's team. Some of 2026's growth came from items management describes as one-off or temporary: the Q2 one-offs the CEO named in July and Lantus's demand from competitors' supply problems.
Industry Commentary
Europe's drugmakers ask for better prices at home
ONGOING HEADWIND

On September 22 the chairs of nine European drugmakers, Sanofi among them, published an open letter titled "Europe Is Losing the Pharma Investment Race — But the Comeback Is Within Reach." They wrote that Europe's share of global pharma R&D has fallen from 43% in 1990 to 31%, that its share of commercial clinical trials has halved in a decade to 9%, and that more than $600B of pharmaceutical investment was announced in the US and China in two years. For Sanofi, with €2,579M of Q3 sales in Europe, down 0.9%, the letter describes the market where it earns the least growth.

Immunology's next round: longer-acting antibodies
STRATEGIC SHIFT

The October 1 deal is a bet that the next generation of these drugs will be long-acting, with fewer injections. The same idea was behind amlitelimab: in 2025 UBS noted that its less frequent dosing could appeal to patients who dislike injections (Pharmaceutical Executive). Sanofi and Regeneron will co-develop long-acting antibodies against IL-13, IL-4, the IL-4 receptor and a bispecific hitting IL-4 and IL-13 together, the same pathways Dupixent blocks. The bull framing: Sanofi keeps the commercial lead in the class it built. The bear framing: the class gets crowded and the new drugs could take sales from Dupixent before they add to it.

Big Pharma sheds its mature brands
PORTFOLIO FOCUS

Sanofi has spent three years separating businesses that do not fit an innovation-led drugmaker: consumer health went into Opella with CD&R in 2025, and 20 mature medicines are planned to go to Cheplapharm in 2027 in exchange for equity. Thomas Grenier, who runs General Medicines, described it in the Cheplapharm release as "Our multi-year journey to simplify our mature portfolio". The trade-off: each carve-out lifts growth and margin on paper but also removes steady cash generators, which raises the share of profit that depends on Dupixent and the pipeline.
