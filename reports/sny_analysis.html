01 Company Overview

Sanofi is a Paris-based maker of prescription medicines and vaccines, and a member of France's CAC 40 index. Since April 30, 2025, when its consumer-health arm Opella moved into a joint venture with the private-equity firm CD&R (Sanofi kept a 48.2% stake and received €10.4B in net cash), it reports a single Biopharma segment. FY2025 net sales were €43.63B, up 9.9% at CER. Business EPS was €7.83 (+15.0% at CER) and IFRS EPS €6.40. One drug sits at the centre of the story: Dupixent, the antibody for eczema, asthma and other allergic-type inflammation that Sanofi shares with Regeneron. It sold €5.15B in Q2 2026 alone, 44% of group sales.

The latest quarter shows the gap between the numbers and the share price. Q2 2026, reported on July 30, beat on every headline line. Sales rose 17.8% at CER to €11.60B, business EPS rose 33.3% at CER to €2.09, and the 2026 guide went up to sales growth of around 10% at CER (from high single digits), with business EPS growing slightly faster. Zacks's consensus for the ADS was $1.10 of adjusted EPS and $12.73B of revenue; Sanofi delivered $1.22 and $13.48B. Paris still fell 8.95% that day and the Nasdaq ADS 4.37%. The same release cut the pipeline: amlitelimab, the eczema antibody that was meant to follow Dupixent, will not be filed for approval (a €952M impairment), and two other programmes (itepekimab and balinatunfib) were stopped. IFRS net income fell 91% to €343M. The CEO's own summary credited the EPS jump partly to one-offs. That CEO is new. The board declined to renew Paul Hudson's mandate in February, and Belén Garijo, who ran Germany's Merck KGaA, took over on April 29, 2026. Since the print she has announced plans to hand 20 older medicines to Cheplapharm (September 14) and expanded the Regeneron alliance with a $1B upfront payment for next-generation immunology antibodies (October 1). The ADS closed at $39.92, its 52-week closing low and 23.7% below the 52-week closing high of $52.34 (November 13, 2025).

Dupixent blocks two signalling proteins (IL-4 and IL-13) that drive what doctors call type 2 inflammation. It is approved in nine diseases, from eczema and asthma to COPD and hives, and more than 1.5 million people take it, per the October 1 release. Q2 2026 sales passed €5B in a quarter for the first time. US sales were €3,899M (+42.8%), helped by demand, operational improvements and what the release calls "a favourable adjustment of gross-to-net deductions" (a change in the rebates and discounts the company books against list price). Europe was €583M (+19.4%) and the rest of the world €672M (+26.9%). The growth is not free of setbacks: two phase 3 studies in lichen simplex chronicus (an itchy skin condition) missed their endpoints, and Sanofi withdrew its EU filing in bullous pemphigoid. Kevzara, for rheumatoid arthritis, added €177M (+35.1%). Sanofi shares Dupixent's profits with Regeneron, which invented it; the profit-sharing line took €1,972M out of Q2 operating income.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Dupixent, 9 indications Regeneron antibody alliance Vaccine manufacturing scale Rare-disease franchises (Genzyme) ~€9.4B trailing free cash flow 31 years of dividend increases

Sanofi's moat is mostly one drug and one partnership. Dupixent works across a long list of allergic-type diseases, has more than 1.5 million patients and is still adding indications, and it was built with Regeneron, whose antibody technology produced it. Vaccines add a second, harder-to-copy asset: making flu and paediatric vaccines at global scale takes specialised plants and regulatory approvals. The Genzyme rare-disease enzymes serve small, specialised markets. The weak side is the one the market has been pricing for a year: what comes after Dupixent. In September 2025 the stock fell 9.1% on the day amlitelimab's first phase 3 data came out, even though the trial met its goals; Barclays analyst Emily Field, quoted by Pharmaceutical Executive, said the stock was increasingly seen as a "cliff stock". In July 2026 the new management stopped amlitelimab altogether.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One drug is 44% of sales. Dupixent was €5,154M of €11,597M in Q2 2026, and three-quarters of it is sold in the US. RBC's initiation on September 9 named Dupixent's coming patent cliff among its reasons for a Sector Perform rating.
02
The pipeline keeps losing its lead assets. In the past twelve months: tolebrutinib missed its phase 3 in primary progressive MS and got an FDA complete response letter in the secondary progressive form (a €1,663M impairment in FY2025); amlitelimab will not be filed (€952M impairment); itepekimab (COPD) and balinatunfib (Crohn's and colitis) were discontinued; Dupixent failed in lichen simplex chronicus. RBC also cited scepticism that the pipeline can deliver more than €10B of sales.
03
The partner takes a growing cut. Regeneron profit sharing rose 45% year on year in Q2 (€1,972M vs €1,360M). The October 1 expansion adds four early-stage antibodies on 50:50 terms, for $1B upfront and up to $7B in milestones.
04
Vaccines are shrinking in some markets. Paediatric vaccine sales outside the US and Europe fell 21.2% in Q2, mainly on fewer births in China, and China sales overall fell 4.9%. In the US, Sanofi reached an agreement with the government to lower medicine costs (FY2025 release), and the 2025 Dupixent price adjustment in China shows that pricing can move against the company.

FX exposure: two currencies sit between Sanofi's accounts and the ADS price. Earnings are reported in euros, so a stronger dollar lowers the dollar value of each euro of EPS. And more than half of sales are made in the US (€6,344M of €11,597M in Q2) and translated back into euros: in Q2, currency took 1.8 points off reported sales growth. Sanofi estimates currency at about −1% on 2026 sales and −2% on business EPS at July 2026 rates. The ADS also trades a few hours after Paris closes, so it can drift from parity: on October 2 the ADS closed at $39.92 against a parity value of $39.74 at the ECB fix (€70.81 × 1.1225 ÷ 2).

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the reported figures. First, impairments: €1,663M on tolebrutinib in FY2025 (which turned Q4 2025 into an IFRS loss of €801M) and €952M on amlitelimab in Q2 2026. Second, the Opella transaction: H1 2025 IFRS EPS of €4.74 included €2.34 from discontinued operations, so year-on-year IFRS comparisons look much worse than the business. Third, Q2 2026 items on the good side: the Sarclisa provision reversal (more than €200M), the favourable Dupixent gross-to-net adjustment and €192M of divestment gains, partly offset by more than €200M of R&D wind-down costs. The CEO said business EPS growth was "supported by disciplined cost management and one-offs." Fourth, buybacks: €5B in 2025 and €1B completed in April 2026, which cut the average share count to 1,196.6M in Q2 from 1,217.1M a year earlier.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

What the series is: Nasdaq ADS closes in US dollars, so the line mixes the Paris share price with the euro-dollar rate. The closes are not adjusted for dividends, and Sanofi pays a large one each May: the ADS went ex-dividend on May 4, 2026 ($2.422 per ADS) and fell 5.9% that day, most of it the dividend leaving the price. Over five years the dividends are a large part of the return that this chart does not show.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The business is growing fast and the share price is not. Q2 sales rose 17.8% at CER, business EPS 33.3%, and guidance went up. Dupixent is still compounding (+37.6% in Q2) toward management's ~€25B in 2030, with ~€10B more from launches. At $39.92 the ADS trades at about 8x forward business EPS, with a 5.8% dividend yield that has risen for 31 straight years and a free-cash-flow yield near 11%. Every target in both analyst panels sits above the price; the Paris-line average is 33% higher. A new CEO is cutting weak programmes early instead of spending on them for years, and she has already locked in the next generation of long-acting Dupixent-type antibodies with the partner that invented Dupixent. If even one of those works, the cliff is smaller than the price implies.

Bear Case

Sanofi is a one-drug company with a weak pipeline, and the numbers prove it. Dupixent was 44% of Q2 sales; tolebrutinib, amlitelimab, itepekimab and balinatunfib have all been written down or stopped in about a year. The CEO said Q2 business EPS growth was "supported by disciplined cost management and one-offs", and the release names a favourable Dupixent gross-to-net adjustment and a Sarclisa provision reversal, management expects growth to slow in H2, and consensus has Q3 business EPS below last year. The Regeneron deal buys antibodies that are mostly not yet in human trials, for $1B upfront and up to $7B more, and RBC already doubts the pipeline's €10B target. The stock is down 21% in five years while the CAC 40 is up 16%: a low P/E has not been enough to stop the slide.

Top 3 Risks

01
Dupixent concentration. 44% of sales from one drug, three-quarters of it in the US. Any pricing, competitive or patent event on Dupixent moves the whole company.
02
Pipeline attrition. Four lead programmes impaired or stopped since late 2025. The replacement plan now rests on the launch portfolio, early-stage Regeneron antibodies and the "external growth opportunities" the CEO pointed to in the Q2 release.
03
Second-half slowdown. Management has guided to a normalisation of growth after a 15.7% first half, and the Q2 margin had one-offs in it. A soft Q3 on October 30 would land on a stock already at its 52-week low.
Industry Commentary
Europe's drugmakers ask for better prices at home
ONGOING HEADWIND

On September 22 the chairs of nine European drugmakers, Sanofi among them, published an open letter titled "Europe Is Losing the Pharma Investment Race — But the Comeback Is Within Reach." They wrote that Europe's share of global pharma R&D has fallen from 43% in 1990 to 31%, that its share of commercial clinical trials has halved in a decade to 9%, and that more than $600B of pharmaceutical investment was announced in the US and China in two years. For Sanofi, with only €2,141M of Q2 sales in Europe growing 1.3%, the letter describes the market where it earns the least growth.

Immunology's next round: longer-acting antibodies
STRATEGIC SHIFT

The October 1 deal is a bet that the next generation of these drugs will be long-acting, with fewer injections. The same idea was behind amlitelimab: in 2025 UBS noted that its less frequent dosing could appeal to patients who dislike injections (Pharmaceutical Executive). Sanofi and Regeneron will co-develop long-acting antibodies against IL-13, IL-4, the IL-4 receptor and a bispecific hitting IL-4 and IL-13 together, the same pathways Dupixent blocks. The bull framing: Sanofi keeps the commercial lead in the class it built. The bear framing: the class gets crowded and the new drugs could take sales from Dupixent before they add to it.

Big Pharma sheds its mature brands
PORTFOLIO FOCUS

Sanofi has spent three years separating businesses that do not fit an innovation-led drugmaker: consumer health went into Opella with CD&R in 2025, and 20 mature medicines are planned to go to Cheplapharm in 2027 in exchange for equity. Thomas Grenier, who runs General Medicines, described it in the Cheplapharm release as "Our multi-year journey to simplify our mature portfolio". The trade-off: each carve-out lifts growth and margin on paper but also removes steady cash generators, which raises the share of profit that depends on Dupixent and the pipeline.
