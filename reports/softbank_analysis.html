01 Company Overview

SoftBank Group is best understood not as a phone company but as Masayoshi Son's investment vehicle: a Tokyo-listed holding company whose value sits in a handful of stakes. The three that matter most are 86.7% of the voting rights in Arm, the British chip-design firm whose technology is used in smartphones, data-centre servers, connected devices and cars (voting share at March 31, 2026, per SBG's Financial Report 2026); roughly 13% of OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, after the third and final tranche of a $30 billion follow-on investment was executed on October 1, 2026, bringing SBG's cumulative investment to $64.6 billion; and 40.1% of SoftBank Corp., the Japanese mobile carrier, reported in the same segment as LY Corporation (Yahoo! JAPAN and LINE) and PayPay. Around them sit the two Vision Funds, an Intel stake, the newly bought chip designer Ampere, and since September 30, 2026, the digital-infrastructure investor DigitalBridge.

Because so much of the value is in shares of other companies, the profit line swings with markets. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (SBG calls it FY2025) net sales were ¥7.80 trillion, up 7.7%, while net income attributable to shareholders was ¥5.00 trillion against ¥1.15 trillion a year earlier, driven by investment gains. The number SoftBank itself leads with is NAV: a record ¥72.3 trillion ($445.2 billion) at June 30, 2026, or ¥12,686 per share — about double the ¥6,310 share price at the October 2 close. That gap is the whole debate. A shareholder buys a basket of Arm, OpenAI and SoftBank Corp. at roughly half its stated value; the market charges that discount because the basket is concentrated in two AI bets, funded partly with debt now costing up to 9.75% a year in dollars, and steered by one founder's judgment. The stock roughly quadrupled over five years, but it got there through a 38% one-month fall (November 2025) and two consecutive 40%-plus months (April and May 2026).

The latest quarter. Q1 FY2026 (April–June 2026), released at 3:30 p.m. Tokyo time on August 6, 2026, showed net income of ¥347.3 billion, down 17.7% on the year but almost three times the ¥120.23 billion analysts expected (LSEG estimates, as reported by CNBC); net sales rose 10.9% to ¥2.02 trillion. The beat came from a ¥1.33 trillion gain on Intel shares, not from OpenAI, whose valuation SBG left unchanged from March. In the first session after the release the stock fell 2.5%, from ¥5,695 on August 6 to ¥5,552 on August 7. SBG gives no profit guidance: it "does not provide forecasts of consolidated results of operations as they are difficult to project due to numerous uncertainties affecting earnings", and its one forecast, an ¥11.00 dividend for the year to March 2027, was left unchanged. At the results briefing CFO Yoshimitsu Goto said the record NAV "was mainly driven by the increase in the value of Arm", that SBG had "committed a total investment of $18.5B for the rest of this FY" (the last OpenAI tranche, ABB Robotics and DigitalBridge), and, of the market swings: "We're not shaken by short-term market moves. We see them as a natural part of a major revolution."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Control of Arm (86.7%) ~13% of OpenAI SoftBank Corp. telecom cash flows Japanese retail bond franchise Access to the largest AI deals Founder-led, fast capital allocation

SoftBank's edge is access and nerve. It committed $30 billion to one private company in February 2026 and had paid all of it by October 1, financing it with a $40 billion bridge facility (repaid by mid-September) and $11.1 billion of dollar and euro notes that paid for the final tranche, while also selling a ¥1 trillion bond mainly to Japanese individual investors at 4.75%. Control of Arm gives it royalties on chips built to Arm designs, from phones to data-centre servers, and, with Ampere and the Arm AGI CPU, a growing role in selling finished chips as well as licensing designs. The vulnerability is the mirror image: the same concentration that makes SoftBank the purest listed play on OpenAI and Arm means that two share prices — one of them private, revalued only when OpenAI raises money — drive most of the NAV.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration in two AI assets. In SBG's NAV chart for June 30, 2026, Arm is about ¥43.4T and the Vision Funds, whose largest holding is OpenAI ($89.6 billion fair value), about ¥20.8T, out of ¥72.3T. OpenAI's value rests on private funding rounds. Jefferies made this point when it downgraded the stock to Underperform in March 2026: as reported by Investing.com, it said SoftBank had supplied the majority of capital in several OpenAI rounds, which could inflate reported valuations.
02
The cost of money has risen sharply. The September 24, 2026 dollar notes pay 8.625% (2030), 9.250% (2032) and 9.750% (2034); the euro notes 7.125% and 8.000%. Q1 finance cost was ¥328.7 billion, almost double a year earlier. SBG said on August 28, 2026 that it holds no rating from Moody's, which still publishes unsolicited ratings on it, and is rated only by S&P and JCR; the September notes are rated BB+ by S&P and by Fitch, one notch below investment grade. In March 2026 S&P affirmed its BB+ rating and moved the outlook to negative because of the additional OpenAI investment (Reuters, Bloomberg).
03
Exit timing is not in SoftBank's hands. Turning the OpenAI stake into cash needs a listing or a buyer. On September 14, 2026 Nikkei Asia reported that SoftBank shares plunged after OpenAI said it would not list this year and joined calls to slow AI development; the stock closed 10.7% lower that day.
04
Key-person and governance risk. The strategy rests heavily on Son's judgment and his appetite for very large commitments. The board includes five external directors, but the decisions that move NAV — OpenAI, Ampere, ABB Robotics, DigitalBridge — are founder-led bets.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, investment gains dominate profit: Q1 FY2026 included a ¥1,332.9 billion gain on Intel shares alone, larger than the quarter's entire ¥347.3 billion net income, offset by a ¥391.6 billion derivative loss and a ¥146.1 billion foreign-exchange loss. Second, the share split: a 4-for-1 split took effect on January 1, 2026, and every per-share figure and every chart point in this report is on the post-split basis (pre-2026 prices divided by four). Third, subsidiaries are not marked to market: the value of Arm, SoftBank Corp. and PayPay moves NAV but not net income, so the P/E and the NAV discount can point in opposite directions in the same quarter.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of ¥1,541 (split-adjusted) to the October 2, 2026 settled close of ¥6,310, SoftBank Group gained 309.5% on price — about 4.1 times the starting level. Dividends of ¥5.50 per post-split share twice a year add very little. The path was anything but smooth: the stock spent 2022 and 2023 between ¥1,225 and ¥1,810.50 at month-end, climbed with the AI trade in 2024, then went nearly vertical from April 2025 (¥1,791) to October 2025 (¥6,766.25), fell 37.8% in November 2025, sank to ¥3,555 in March 2026 and more than doubled to a record ¥7,491 by May 2026. Of the 59 full monthly moves in the window, the three largest gains all came in the last twelve months: +46.8% (April 2026), +44.8% (October 2025) and +43.5% (May 2026).

The 6-period monthly RSI peaked at 95.0 in October 2025, the most overbought reading in the series, just before the steepest monthly fall. Its low was 27.5 in April 2022. It now reads 61.2: firm but not stretched. The 10-month moving average is about ¥5,377, below the current price, so the longer trend is still up, even after the June–August 2026 pullback from the May high.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You buy control of Arm, about 13% of OpenAI and 40% of a growing Japanese telecom group at roughly half of SoftBank's own June 30 NAV (¥12,686 per share against a ¥6,310 price), or 38% below the August 5 pro forma. Leverage is modest on SBG's measure — 13.0% LTV against a 25% ceiling — the bridge loans are gone, and the OpenAI commitment is fully funded as of October 1. Arm's royalties are growing (AI Computing sales +17.8% in dollars) and its move into its own chips widens what it can sell. If OpenAI eventually lists anywhere near its $730 billion pre-money valuation, the market will have a daily price for the stake, and the discount that rests on doubts about private marks can narrow. The 19-analyst StockAnalysis consensus is Buy (15 Buy or Strong Buy), with an average target 22.8% above the price.

Bear Case

The discount exists for good reasons. Most of NAV is two AI assets, one of them private and valued at funding rounds SoftBank itself helped finance. OpenAI has ruled out a 2026 listing, so the stake cannot be sold on the market soon, while SoftBank is paying up to 9.75% on new dollar debt and Q1 finance cost nearly doubled. Even the Q1 profit beat came from a ¥1.33 trillion gain on Intel shares, not from OpenAI, whose valuation was left unchanged, and SBG publishes no profit forecast to anchor expectations. NAV fell from ¥72.3 trillion to ¥58.3 trillion in five weeks at August 5 prices, mostly in the Arm block, so the margin of safety moves with one share price. AI Computing is losing money as Arm spends on research, and the telecom stake, the one steady earner, is about ¥2.4 trillion of NAV. Jefferies' ¥3,140 target — half the current price — is the market's reminder of what happens to this stock when the AI cycle turns, as it did in November 2025 (−37.8% in a month).

Top 3 Risks

01
An AI valuation reset. Arm's share price and OpenAI's private valuation drive most of NAV. A fall in either moves SoftBank's value by trillions of yen in weeks, as the drop from ¥72.3T (June 30) to ¥58.3T (August 5 pro forma) showed.
02
Funding cost and liquidity. New dollar notes cost 8.625%–9.750%, the cash position fell from ¥3.5T to ¥2.3T in Q1, and Japanese long-term rates touched 3.030% in mid-September 2026, their highest since 1996 according to Nikkei. The 25% LTV ceiling is the line to watch if asset values fall.
03
No near-term exit for OpenAI. With no listing this year, the $64.6 billion stake stays illiquid and marked by private rounds; SVF2 has already arranged a $10 billion loan against it. A down-round or a slower path to a listing would hit NAV and the market's trust in it at the same time.
Industry Commentary
Financing the AI build-out with borrowed money
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

SoftBank's AI strategy is financed as much as it is invested. In twelve months it committed $30 billion to OpenAI and funded it in three $10 billion tranches (April 1, July 1 and October 1, 2026); bought Ampere and DigitalBridge; and arranged financing for ABB's robotics business. SBG drew $30 billion on a bridge facility and repaid it in full by mid-September, sold a ¥1 trillion retail bond at 4.75% and $11.1 billion of notes at 7.125%–9.750%, and SVF2 arranged a loan secured on OpenAI shares. At the August 6 results briefing CFO Yoshimitsu Goto put the commitments for the rest of the fiscal year at $18.5 billion and said: "Our Loan to Value (LTV) remains at a very safe level." The structure works while asset values rise: LTV fell to 13.0% at June 30 as the value of holdings rose. The test comes when they stop rising, because the interest bill does not fall with them.

The holding-company discount
ONGOING HEADWIND

Listed holding companies usually trade below the value of what they own; investors charge for debt at the parent, taxes on any sale, management's freedom to redeploy the money and, here, the private marks on OpenAI. At the October 2 close SoftBank traded at about half of its June 30 NAV per share and 0.62x the August 5 pro forma.

Japan's market and SoftBank's weight in it
EMERGING TAILWIND

The Nikkei 225 more than doubled over five years and reached 68,309.46 on October 2, 2026, and SoftBank has been one of its biggest swing factors: about 42% of the index's gain in the record-setting week to May 22, 2026, according to Rakuten Securities, and about 350 yen of lift to the index on September 15, according to Nikkei. Because the Nikkei is price-weighted, large SoftBank moves pass straight into the headline index and into the funds that track it. The flip side is that the same index flows add to the stock's volatility in both directions.
