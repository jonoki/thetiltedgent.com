01 Company Overview

SoftBank Group is best understood not as a phone company but as Masayoshi Son's investment vehicle: a Tokyo-listed holding company whose value sits in a handful of stakes. The three that matter most are 86.7% of the voting rights in Arm, the British chip-design firm whose technology is used in smartphones, data-centre servers, connected devices and cars (voting share at March 31, 2026, per SBG's Financial Report 2026); roughly 13% of OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, after the third and final tranche of a $30 billion follow-on investment was executed on October 1, 2026, bringing SBG's cumulative investment to $64.6 billion; and 40.1% of SoftBank Corp., the Japanese mobile carrier, reported in the same segment as LY Corporation (Yahoo! JAPAN and LINE) and PayPay. Around them sit the two Vision Funds, an Intel stake, the newly bought chip designer Ampere, and since September 30, 2026, the digital-infrastructure investor DigitalBridge.

Because so much of the value is in shares of other companies, the profit line swings with markets. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (SBG calls it FY2025) net sales were ¥7.80 trillion, up 7.7%, while net income attributable to shareholders was ¥5.00 trillion against ¥1.15 trillion a year earlier, driven by investment gains. The number SoftBank itself leads with is NAV: ¥59.8 trillion at September 30, 2026, down from a record ¥72.3 trillion at June 30, or ¥10,499 per share — about three times the ¥3,547 share price at the November 13 close (SBG Q2 FY2026 data sheet). Re-run at November 9 share prices, SBG put NAV at about ¥44.7 trillion, roughly ¥7,840 per share. That gap is the whole debate. A shareholder buys a basket of Arm, OpenAI and SoftBank Corp. at about a third of its September 30 value; the market charges that discount because the basket is concentrated in two AI bets, funded partly with debt now costing up to 9.75% a year in dollars, and steered by one founder's judgment. The stock more than doubled over five years, but it got there through a 38% one-month fall (November 2025), two consecutive 40%-plus months (April and May 2026) and a 30% fall in October 2026.

The latest quarter. Q2 FY2026 (July–September 2026), released after the Tokyo close on November 10, 2026, showed a net loss of ¥1,115.3 billion attributable to shareholders, against a ¥2,502.2 billion profit a year earlier, when a gain on OpenAI drove the result. Reuters reported that three analysts polled by LSEG had expected anything from a ¥233 billion profit to a ¥113 billion loss. Net sales rose 15.6% to ¥2.22 trillion. For the half year to September the loss was ¥768.0 billion, against a ¥2,924.1 billion profit, SBG's first first-half loss in three years (Jiji Press). The quarter's biggest item was a ¥597.2 billion loss on power-generation equipment for US AI data centres, booked on advance payments after SBG revised its plans for buying the equipment (Jiji Press, Reuters); losses on Intel shares and on contracts backed by them added to it, and finance cost rose to ¥461.9 billion from ¥174.7 billion. OpenAI's latest funding round is not yet reflected in the results, CFO Goto said. SBG still publishes no profit forecast; its one forecast, an ¥11.00 dividend for the year to March 2027, is unchanged. The shares rose 13.9% on November 10, before the results came out; the next day they fell as much as 10.5% during the session but closed only 0.4% lower, then lost 3.0% on November 12. At the briefing CFO Yoshimitsu Goto said that in many cases the group has had to change strategies that would have seemed fine a year or two ago, and declined to detail OpenAI's latest funding round — which people familiar with it told Reuters values OpenAI at $1.25 trillion — beyond saying an announcement was highly likely in the third quarter (Reuters).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Control of Arm (86.7%) ~13% of OpenAI SoftBank Corp. telecom cash flows Japanese retail bond franchise Access to the largest AI deals Founder-led, fast capital allocation

SoftBank's edge is access and nerve. It committed $30 billion to one private company in February 2026 and had paid all of it by October 1, financing it with a $40 billion bridge facility (repaid by mid-September) and $11.1 billion of dollar and euro notes that paid for the final tranche, while also selling a ¥1 trillion bond mainly to Japanese individual investors at 4.75%. Control of Arm gives it royalties on chips built to Arm designs, from phones to data-centre servers, and, with Ampere and the Arm AGI CPU, a growing role in selling finished chips as well as licensing designs. The vulnerability is the mirror image: the same concentration that makes SoftBank the purest listed play on OpenAI and Arm means that two share prices — one of them private, revalued only when OpenAI raises money — drive most of the NAV.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration in two AI assets. At September 30, 2026, Arm was ¥38.16T and SVF2, whose largest holding is OpenAI, ¥20.07T of SBG's ¥71.14T of holdings (data sheet). OpenAI's value rests on private funding rounds. Jefferies made this point when it downgraded the stock to Underperform in March 2026: as reported by Investing.com, it said SoftBank had supplied the majority of capital in several OpenAI rounds, which could inflate reported valuations. It moved back to Hold on October 28 (StockAnalysis ratings table).
02
The cost of money has risen sharply. The September 24, 2026 dollar notes pay 8.625% (2030), 9.250% (2032) and 9.750% (2034); the euro notes 7.125% and 8.000%. Q2 finance cost was ¥461.9 billion, against ¥174.7 billion a year earlier (data sheet). On November 6 the five-year credit default swap spread on SoftBank reached as much as 466 basis points, its widest since October 2022, according to Markit data cited by Reuters. SBG said on August 28, 2026 that it holds no rating from Moody's, which still publishes unsolicited ratings on it, and is rated only by S&P and JCR; the September notes are rated BB+ by S&P and by Fitch, one notch below investment grade. In March 2026 S&P affirmed its BB+ rating and moved the outlook to negative because of the additional OpenAI investment (Reuters, Bloomberg).
03
Exit timing is not in SoftBank's hands. Turning the OpenAI stake into cash needs a listing or a buyer. On September 14, 2026 Nikkei Asia reported that SoftBank shares plunged after OpenAI said it would not list this year and joined calls to slow AI development; the stock closed 10.7% lower that day. By November 6 Reuters was reporting that OpenAI had pushed its listing back to 2027. Deals can also be blocked: on November 6 SBG said China had prohibited its purchase of ABB's robotics business in China.
04
Key-person and governance risk. The strategy rests heavily on Son's judgment and his appetite for very large commitments. The board includes five external directors, but the decisions that move NAV — OpenAI, Ampere, ABB Robotics, DigitalBridge, the US data-centre equipment behind Q2's ¥597.2 billion loss — are founder-led bets.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the reported figures. First, investment gains and losses dominate profit: the half year swung from a ¥1,859.4 billion group investment gain in Q1 (¥1,332.9 billion of it on Intel shares) to a ¥133.6 billion investment loss in Q2, with derivative losses of ¥433.3 billion over the six months (data sheet). Second, the US data-centre equipment loss: ¥597.2 billion in Q2 on advance payments for power-generation equipment after SBG revised its procurement plans (Jiji Press), against which SBG says it would be entitled to rebates of up to $2.1 billion on future purchases (Reuters). Third, the share split: a 4-for-1 split took effect on January 1, 2026, and every per-share figure and every chart point in this report is on the post-split basis (pre-2026 prices divided by four). Fourth, subsidiaries are not marked to market: the value of Arm, SoftBank Corp. and PayPay moves NAV but not net income, so the P/E and the NAV discount can point in opposite directions in the same quarter.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 13, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of ¥1,507.50 (split-adjusted) to the November 13, 2026 settled close of ¥3,547, SoftBank Group gained 135.3% on price — about 2.4 times the starting level. Dividends of ¥5.50 per post-split share twice a year add very little. The path was anything but smooth: the stock spent 2022 and 2023 between ¥1,225 and ¥1,810.50 at month-end, climbed with the AI trade in 2024, then went nearly vertical from April 2025 (¥1,791) to October 2025 (¥6,766.25), fell 37.8% in November 2025, sank to ¥3,555 in March 2026, more than doubled to a record ¥7,491 by May 2026 and has since given back more than half of it: the November 13 close is 52.7% below that record month-end and just below the March 2026 month-end. Of the 59 full monthly moves in the window, the three largest gains came between October 2025 and May 2026: +46.8% (April 2026), +44.8% (October 2025) and +43.5% (May 2026). The two largest falls are −37.8% (November 2025) and −30.3% (October 2026).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You buy control of Arm, about 13% of OpenAI and 40% of a growing Japanese telecom group at about a third of SoftBank's own September 30 NAV (¥10,499 per share against a ¥3,547 price), or 55% below the ¥44.7 trillion re-run at November 9 prices. Leverage is still modest on SBG's measure — 15.9% LTV against a 25% ceiling — the bridge loans are gone, and the OpenAI commitment is fully funded as of October 1. The telecom segment grew sales 13.9% and profit 19.8% in Q2, and Arm's move into its own chips widens what it can sell. OpenAI's next funding round, reported by Reuters at a $1.25 trillion valuation, would be marked well above the $730 billion SBG paid in at, and SBG has not yet booked any of it. The 18-analyst StockAnalysis consensus is Buy (13 Buy or Strong Buy in its November breakdown), with an average target 108.2% above the price and even the lowest target 20.4% above it.

Bear Case

The discount exists for good reasons. Most of NAV is two AI assets, one of them private and valued at funding rounds SoftBank itself helped finance. OpenAI has pushed its listing to 2027 (Reuters), so the stake cannot be sold on the market soon, while SoftBank is paying up to 9.75% on new dollar debt, Q2 finance cost was ¥461.9 billion against ¥174.7 billion a year earlier, and the cost of insuring its debt hit a four-year high in November. The quarter added a ¥597.2 billion loss on data-centre equipment paid for in advance before SBG revised its purchase plans, and SBG publishes no profit forecast to anchor expectations. NAV fell from ¥72.3 trillion at June 30 to ¥59.8 trillion at September 30 and to about ¥44.7 trillion at November 9 prices, mostly in the Arm block, so the margin of safety moves with one share price. AI Computing is losing money, and the telecom stake, the one steady earner, is ¥3.42 trillion of ¥71.14 trillion of holdings. Two falls of 30% or more in a month inside a year (November 2025 and October 2026) are the market's reminder of what happens to this stock when the AI trade turns.

Top 3 Risks

01
An AI valuation reset. Arm's share price and OpenAI's private valuation drive most of NAV. A fall in either moves SoftBank's value by trillions of yen in weeks, as the drop from ¥59.8T (September 30) to about ¥44.7T (November 9 prices) showed.
02
Funding cost and liquidity. New dollar notes cost 8.625%–9.750%, Q2 finance cost was ¥461.9B, the five-year credit default swap spread reached 466 basis points on November 6 (Reuters), and ¥4.20T of SBG's bonds and loans fall due in fiscal 2028. Reuters reported that the margin loan on SBG's Arm shares rose by $5 billion to about $25 billion. LTV rose to 15.9% in Q2; the 25% ceiling is the line to watch if asset values keep falling.
03
No near-term exit for OpenAI. With the listing pushed to 2027, the $64.6 billion stake stays illiquid and marked by private rounds; SVF2 has already borrowed $10 billion against it. A down-round or a slower path to a listing would hit NAV and the market's trust in it at the same time.
Industry Commentary
Financing the AI build-out with borrowed money
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

SoftBank's AI strategy is financed as much as it is invested. In twelve months it committed $30 billion to OpenAI and funded it in three $10 billion tranches (April 1, July 1 and October 1, 2026); bought Ampere and DigitalBridge; and arranged financing for ABB's robotics business. SBG drew $30 billion on a bridge facility and repaid it in full by mid-September, sold a ¥1 trillion retail bond at 4.75% and $11.1 billion of notes at 7.125%–9.750%, and SVF2 arranged a loan secured on OpenAI shares. Reuters reported that SoftBank raised $56.5 billion in the July–September quarter alone, its largest quarterly total. The structure works while asset values rise: LTV fell to 13.0% at June 30 as holdings rose. The test came in the next quarter, when holdings fell from ¥83.11 trillion to ¥71.14 trillion, LTV rose to 15.9% and finance cost reached ¥461.9 billion (data sheet); the interest bill does not fall with asset values. At the November 10 briefing CFO Goto described LTV as well under the 25% line and said this is not an environment in which SBG can be all offense in investing (Investing.com transcript).

The holding-company discount
ONGOING HEADWIND

Listed holding companies usually trade below the value of what they own; investors charge for debt at the parent, taxes on any sale, management's freedom to redeploy the money and, here, the private marks on OpenAI. At the November 13 close SoftBank traded at 0.34x its September 30 NAV per share and about 0.45x the re-run at November 9 prices, a wider discount than the roughly 0.5x of early October.

Japan's market and SoftBank's weight in it
EMERGING TAILWIND

The Nikkei 225 more than doubled over five years and closed at 61,142.35 on November 13, 2026, and SoftBank has been one of its biggest swing factors: about 42% of the index's gain in the record-setting week to May 22, 2026, according to Rakuten Securities, and about 350 yen of lift to the index on September 15, according to Nikkei. Because the Nikkei is price-weighted, large SoftBank moves pass straight into the headline index and into the funds that track it. The flip side is that the same index flows add to the stock's volatility in both directions.
