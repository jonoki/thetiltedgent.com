01 Company Overview

Solventum is the old 3M Health Care business, set loose. On April 1, 2024 3M distributed 80.1% of the shares to its own stockholders and kept 19.9% to sell down later. The same day, S&P added SOLV to the S&P 500 in place of VF Corp. What came out of the door was a medtech company in name and a conglomerate fragment in practice: wound care and surgical supplies, a dental business, a hospital-coding software business, and a filtration unit that sold into biopharma and industry. FY2025 sales were $8.33B, up 0.9% reported and 3.3% organic. Adjusted EPS was $6.11. GAAP EPS was $8.88, flattered by a one-off gain.

The story since the spin is a company breaking itself into smaller pieces, on purpose. First it sold the filtration unit to Thermo Fisher. The deal was announced at $4.1B in February 2025 and closed on September 2, 2025, producing a $1,549M gain on sale for FY2025 ($1,518M booked in Q3, $31M in Q4), with the proceeds going to pay down debt. It bought Acera Surgical in December 2025. Then on August 5, 2026, alongside a quarter that beat hard, it said it intends to separate the Health Information Systems software unit within 12–18 months, by spin-off, sale or merger. CEO Bryan Hanson's aim is "a true medtech company" built on MedSurg and Dental. The stock has done its part: from a first-month close of $65.01 (April 2024) to $88.84, +36.7% since listing, with no dividend. The question for the next year is how much of that improvement is structural and how much is one very flattering quarter.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Hospital consumables installed base Clinical habit & product familiarity HIS coding used by 75%+ of US hospitals Regulatory clearances Global manufacturing & distribution Long-term software contracts (HIS)

The moat is habit and plumbing. A nurse reaches for the dressing she was trained on. A hospital's coding team builds its workflow around the software it already runs. Neither is switched on price alone. That is why gross margin held near 55% (54.78% TTM) even as the company went through a spin, a divestiture and an ERP migration. The weakness is also clear: operating margin fell from 22.95% in FY2021 to 7.62% TTM on a GAAP basis. Some of that is stand-up and separation cost that should fade. Some of it is the price of rebuilding, as a standalone company, the systems 3M used to provide. Adjusted operating margin guidance of 22.2–22.7% for FY2026 says management thinks most of the gap is temporary. The GAAP line has not shown it yet.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The best quarter was partly borrowed. Q2 2026's +9.5% organic growth and $2.55 adjusted EPS (+50.9%) included about $125M of ERP pull-forward and about $0.82 of EPS from advance ordering and tariff refunds, $0.34 of which is expected to reverse in Q3. The regular session after the print says the market had noticed. Shares rose 3.4% after hours to $90.44 on August 5 (per Investing.com), then closed August 6 at $82.96 (−5.16%).
02
GAAP profitability is thin. TTM operating margin is 7.62% against a 22.2–22.7% adjusted guide. Q2 GAAP EPS was $0.53 against $2.55 adjusted. A gap that wide means the adjustments are doing heavy lifting, and each year of "one-off" separation cost makes it harder to argue they are one-off.
03
Leverage and cash conversion. Debt-to-equity is 1.10, net debt $4.7B, current ratio 1.02 and TTM free cash flow negative (−$118M). The balance sheet is improving because of an asset sale, not because the business is producing cash. That has to change for the buyback ($1B authorised, $355M repurchased in H1 2026) to be funded by operations rather than borrowing.
04
Execution load. In three years the company has spun out, stood up its own ERP, sold a business, bought one, cut SKUs (a ~100bps drag on 2026 growth) and now started a second separation. Every one of those is a chance for customer disruption or a supply-chain miss.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

This is a spin-off doing what good spin-offs do: shrinking to its best assets and fixing the balance sheet. It has paid down $2.6B of debt since separation (per Investing.com's Q2 slide summary), sold a non-core unit for $4.0B, and has a second separation under way that could crystallise value for a software business used by 75%+ of US hospitals. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $7.10–$7.20 from $6.40–$6.60. At $88.84 that is about 12.4x consensus, for a medtech consumables franchise with 55% gross margins and an adjusted margin guide of 22.2–22.7%. If the GAAP–adjusted gap closes as stand-up costs roll off, the reported numbers catch up with the story and today's multiple looks low.

Bear Case

Strip out the one-offs and this is a ~3% grower with a 7.6% GAAP operating margin, negative trailing free cash flow, a 1.02 current ratio and 1.10 debt-to-equity. The trailing P/E of 10.85 is an artefact of a $1,549M gain. The Q2 blowout was partly borrowed from Q3, and the separation will remove the steadiest-growing segment. Consensus already expects FY2027 EPS of $7.23, just +1.2%, and 3M still has a stake to sell. At 5.6% below the 52-week high, with monthly RSI near 70, the price already reflects most of the good news.

01
Q3 payback. ERP pull-forward of about $125M and a $0.34 EPS reversal are already flagged for Q3 2026. If the underlying run rate also disappoints, the market will re-read Q2 as mostly timing, and the trailing P/E jumps mechanically as the filtration gain leaves the TTM window.
02
Separation execution and dis-synergy. The HIS separation removes the segment that grew fastest organically in FY2025. Industry Commentary
The spin-off life cycle: forced sellers, then focus
STRUCTURAL PATTERN

SOLV's first three months follow a common spin-off pattern. Shareholders of a diversified industrial were handed a healthcare stock they had not chosen, and many sold it. The stock went from a $69.15 opening month to a $52.88 June 2024 close. What followed was also typical: management simplifying the portfolio (the filtration sale, the HIS separation), paying down the debt it was spun out with, and asking to be valued as a pure-play. The remaining 3M stake means the forced-seller phase is not entirely over.

Tariffs and supply chains in medical consumables
ONGOING VARIABLE

Tariffs show up in Solventum's numbers in both directions. The CFO lists them among the headwinds offsetting cost programmes, while Q2 2026 benefited from tariff refunds and the FY2026 EPS guide includes a $0.48 tariff benefit. Neither is under management's control, and neither is recurring earnings. A consumables maker with global manufacturing lives with this exposure. Investors should put a lower multiple on the tariff-driven part of the 2026 guide.

AI in medical coding — the HIS asset
EMERGING TAILWIND

Hospital revenue-cycle management is labour-heavy, and autonomous coding is one of the clearer commercial uses of AI in healthcare administration. Solventum's HIS unit sits in the middle of it, with 40 years of coding content, most US hospitals as users and a ~$10B addressable market by the company's own sizing. Hanson's argument for separating it is that it needs "a different investment level" and "a different pace of innovation" to exploit that opportunity. The bull framing: a separation lets a software multiple be put on a software asset, and interested buyers exist. The bear framing: the parent gives up its steadiest grower and its most AI-leveraged asset, and keeps the lower-growth consumables.
