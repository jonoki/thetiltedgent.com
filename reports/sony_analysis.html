01 Company Overview

Sony is no longer an electronics company that happens to own some entertainment. On its own numbers it is an entertainment and image-sensor company that still sells cameras and TVs. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (Sony calls it FY2025; StockAnalysis labels the same year "FY 2026") it booked ¥12.48 trillion of sales, up 3.7%, and operating income of ¥1,447.5 billion. It is the #10 company in Japan by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap country ranking) with its home listing on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market (code 6758), where the benchmark index is the Nikkei 225. The US-listed security is an ADS representing one ordinary share.

The premise for this report — games, music, pictures and image sensors — is right, and the operating-income mix proves it. Of the ¥1,531.1 billion earned by the five segments in FY2025, Game & Network Services (PlayStation) made 30.3%, Music 29.2%, Imaging & Sensing Solutions 23.3%, Entertainment, Technology & Services (cameras, TVs, audio) 10.4% and Pictures 6.9%. The consumer-electronics business that gave Sony its name is now roughly a tenth of profit. The second fact that shapes every number on this page is the partial spin-off of Sony Financial Group, effective October 1, 2025: shareholders on the September 30, 2025 register received one SFGI share per Sony share, Sony kept 16.40%, and the financial-services business was reclassified as a discontinued operation from Q1 FY2025, with prior periods re-presented. On the way out it cost Sony a ¥1,357.8 billion loss from discontinued operations, almost all of it an accounting transfer of unrealised bond losses at the insurer. That is why the headline EPS is negative while the operating businesses just printed a record quarter. FX exposure in one line: Sony reports in yen but earns only 10.7% of sales in Japan — 32.6% comes from the United States and 22.7% from Europe (FY2025) — and Music and Pictures are run in US dollars, so a weaker yen inflates reported sales and profit; in Q1 FY2026 the dollar averaged ¥159.3 against ¥144.6 a year earlier, and on a constant-currency basis sales actually fell about 1%.

The spin-off, and how the numbers are presented

This part of the premise checks out, with one refinement: it was a partial spin-off, and it is already done. The board approved the plan on May 14, 2025; from Q1 FY2025 the Financial Services business was classified as a discontinued operation under IFRS 5 and prior periods were re-presented to exclude it — which is why FY2024 sales show as ¥12.03T on today's statements rather than the higher figure reported at the time. The spin-off took effect October 1, 2025 (record date September 30, 2025), one SFGI share per Sony share, with a fair value of ¥955.7B (¥159.89 per share). Sony retained 16.40% of SFGI; after SFGI's June 25, 2026 AGM it concluded it no longer had significant influence and reclassified the stake to a fair-value-through-OCI investment.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

PlayStation installed base (125M MAU) First-party game IP Music catalogue & publishing rights Image-sensor process leadership Spider-Man & film/TV library Crunchyroll anime scale (21M+ subs) Net cash balance sheet

Sony's moat is ownership of rights that get paid every time they are used. A song in the catalogue earns on every stream; a PlayStation owner pays a platform fee on every third-party game sold; a Spider-Man film feeds games, TV and merchandise. Those rights compound, and the Music segment's 21.1% margin shows what they look like when little capital is needed to keep them. The sensor business is a different kind of moat — process technology and capacity that customers cannot easily replace — but it needs heavy, cyclical capital spending, which is exactly why Sony is bringing in TSMC as a partner rather than funding the next fab alone.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Memory-price inflation hits three segments at once. Sony's own materials flag it in ET&S (camera and display costs), I&SS (fewer high-end phones shipped in H2) and G&NS (PS5 bill of materials). It has secured FY2026 PS5 memory supply; it has not said what happens to pricing or margins after that.
02
Much of the growth is the yen. Q1 FY2026 sales rose 8% reported but fell about 1% in constant currency. FX added ¥81.7B to G&NS sales, ¥42.1B to Music and ¥40.6B to I&SS. A yen recovery from ~¥157–159 per dollar would reverse that mechanically.
03
Live-service and first-party execution. The G&NS forecast already carries a negative for "adjustments to the FY26 first-party title roadmap", and StockAnalysis's news feed carried a TheFly report (Sep 21, 2026) that Bungie said Marathon "did not reach as many players as hoped". A PS5 cycle late in life depends on the hit rate of first-party releases.
04
Concentrated sensor customers and physical risk. Mobile sensors depend on a small number of smartphone makers and on Kyushu fabs; the July 28, 2026 earthquake stopped the Kumamoto Technology Center, and the cost is excluded from guidance.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the figures. First, the spin-off loss: ¥1,357.8B from discontinued operations in FY2025, almost all of it the ¥1,377.8B transfer of accumulated OCI (unrealised bond losses at the insurer) into profit or loss. It makes trailing EPS, net margin and trailing P/E meaningless, and it is gone from FY2026 onward. Second, US tariff refunds, a named driver of the Q1 G&NS profit jump and the forecast raise (about ¥80B expected, ~70% booked in Q1, per the earnings-call summary on Yahoo Finance) — not repeatable. Third, buybacks and share cancellation: Sony cancelled treasury stock worth ¥577.6B in Q1 FY2026 (issued shares fell from 6.150B to 5.965B), and under the ¥500B / 230M-share programme approved May 8, 2026 it had bought 67.58M shares for ¥237.4B by August 31, 2026. Q1 FY2026 also carried a ¥15.7B drop in financial income, mainly lower unrealised gains on its Spotify shares.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Strip out the one-off spin-off loss and Sony is a ~19.7x continuing-earnings (TTG estimate), ~17x forward business whose profit now comes mostly from rights and platforms: PlayStation (125M monthly users), a music business earning 21% margins on growing streaming revenue, and an image-sensor franchise that just doubled quarterly profit and is getting TSMC to share the next fab's cost. The insurer — and its bond-market risk — is gone. Q1 FY2026 was a record at a 16.8% operating margin, guidance was raised by ¥120B, the dividend goes up 40% to ¥35, and a ¥500B buyback is still running. Net cash, a 7.3% FCF yield and a Spider-Man film breaking box-office records in the current quarter give it room to be wrong on any one of them.

Bear Case

The record quarter was flattered. Constant-currency sales fell about 1%; FX added ¥81.7B to G&NS sales alone, and US tariff refunds that will not recur helped both the G&NS profit jump and the forecast raise. Memory-price inflation is squeezing the PS5, cameras and TVs and, by management's own account, will cut high-end phone shipments — and with them sensor revenue — in H2. The PS5 is late in its cycle while next-generation spending rises, first-party results are uneven (Marathon), and the Kumamoto earthquake cost is not yet in guidance. Four US-line analysts and a Bernstein target below the price do not make a crowded bull case.

Top 3 Risks

01
The yen turns. About 89% of sales are earned outside Japan and reported in yen. The Q2–Q4 forecast assumes ~¥153 per dollar; the ECB rate on September 21, 2026 was ¥157.27. A stronger yen reduces reported profit — and the constant-currency picture is already flat to down.
02
Memory costs and smartphone demand. I&SS earned ¥357.3B in FY2025 — 23% of segment profit — and management expects mobile-sensor revenue to slip this year as memory prices hit high-end phone volumes. The same input cost is squeezing ET&S and PS5 hardware.
03
Games execution into the next console cycle. G&NS is the largest profit line (40.6% of Q1 segment profit) and FY2026 guidance already includes next-generation platform investment, restructuring and a first-party roadmap adjustment. Tariff refunds flatter FY2026 and will not repeat in FY2027.
Industry Commentary
The memory squeeze on consumer hardware
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Sony's Q1 FY2026 materials use the phrase "continued surge in memory prices" and treat it as the key challenge for the electronics segment. They also expect it to cut high-end smartphone shipments in the second half, which is Sony's sensor demand. For PlayStation, the company says it has "secured the quantity of memory necessary" for its FY2026 volume with hardware profitability "similar to FY25". That covers supply; it says nothing about the cost of the next generation, which the segment is already investing in. Sony is on both sides of the trade — it buys memory for devices and sells sensors into phones that also need memory — so the squeeze reaches it twice.

The weak yen: tailwind now, risk later
ONGOING SWING FACTOR

The dollar averaged ¥159.3 in Q1 FY2026, 10% more than a year earlier. That gap is most of the headline growth: sales +8% reported, about −1% at constant currency. Music and Pictures report in dollars and convert; games and sensors earn in dollars and euros against partly yen-based costs. Sony's full-year forecast assumes ~¥153 per dollar for Q2–Q4, so at the current ¥157 there is modest cushion. For a US holder of the ADS, a weak yen increases yen profits but lowers the dollar value of the shares — the two effects partly cancel.

From conglomerate to IP company — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

With Sony Financial Group spun off, the group is games, music, film/anime, sensors and a shrinking electronics tail. The bull framing: investors have long discounted conglomerates, and removing the insurer — with its ¥1.64T of unrealised bond losses — makes Sony simpler to value. Almost 60% of segment profit now comes from PlayStation and Music, both rights-and-platform businesses, and the TSMC JV shares the capital burden of sensors. The bear framing: a simpler Sony is still a mix of a late-cycle console, a cyclical sensor business exposed to smartphone units, and a hit-driven studio. The market capped it at $138B — below Disney and under half of Netflix — because those three businesses carry different risks, not because of the insurer. Whether the discount narrows depends on FY2026 profit holding at ¥1.72T without tariff refunds and a weak yen doing the work.
