01 Company Overview

Sony is no longer an electronics company that happens to own some entertainment. On its own numbers it is an entertainment and image-sensor company that still sells cameras and TVs. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (Sony calls it FY2025; StockAnalysis labels the same year "FY 2026") it booked ¥12.48 trillion of sales, up 3.7%, and operating income of ¥1,447.5 billion. Its home listing is on the Tokyo Stock Exchange Prime Market (code 6758), where the benchmark index is the Nikkei 225. The US-listed security is an ADS representing one ordinary share.

The core story — games, music, pictures and image sensors — is right, and the operating-income mix proves it. Of the ¥1,531.1 billion earned by the five segments in FY2025, Game & Network Services (PlayStation) made 30.3%, Music 29.2%, Imaging & Sensing Solutions 23.3%, Entertainment, Technology & Services (cameras, TVs, audio) 10.4% and Pictures 6.9%. The consumer-electronics business that gave Sony its name is now roughly a tenth of profit. The second fact that shapes every number on this page is the partial spin-off of Sony Financial Group, effective October 1, 2025: shareholders on the September 30, 2025 register received one SFGI share per Sony share, Sony kept 16.40%, and the financial-services business was reclassified as a discontinued operation from Q1 FY2025, with prior periods re-presented. On the way out it cost Sony a ¥1,357.8 billion loss from discontinued operations, almost all of it an accounting transfer of unrealised bond losses at the insurer. That is why the headline EPS is still negative on a trailing basis even though operating income rose 45% to ¥622.3 billion in the latest quarter (Q2 FY2026, July–September 2026) and Sony raised its full-year forecast again. FX exposure in one line: Sony reports in yen but earns only 10.7% of sales in Japan — 32.6% comes from the United States and 22.7% from Europe (FY2025) — and Music and Pictures are run in US dollars, so a weaker yen inflates reported sales and profit; in Q2 FY2026 the dollar averaged ¥159.3 against ¥147.4 a year earlier, and sales rose about 4% at constant currency against 10% as reported.

The spin-off, and how the numbers are presented

This part of the story checks out, with one refinement: it was a partial spin-off, and it is already done. The board approved the plan on May 14, 2025; from Q1 FY2025 the Financial Services business was classified as a discontinued operation under IFRS 5 and prior periods were re-presented to exclude it — which is why FY2024 sales show as ¥12.03T on today's statements rather than the higher figure reported at the time. The spin-off took effect October 1, 2025 (record date September 30, 2025), one SFGI share per Sony share, with a fair value of ¥955.7B (¥159.89 per share). Sony retained 16.40% of SFGI; after SFGI's June 25, 2026 AGM it concluded it no longer had significant influence and reclassified the stake as a financial investment carried at market value.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

PlayStation installed base (125M monthly users, June) First-party game IP Music catalogue & publishing rights Image-sensor process leadership Spider-Man & film/TV library Crunchyroll anime scale (21M+ subs) Low debt (0.23× equity)

Sony's moat is ownership of rights that get paid every time they are used. A song in the catalogue earns on every stream; a PlayStation owner pays a platform fee on every third-party game sold; a Spider-Man film feeds games, TV and merchandise. Those rights keep paying year after year, and the Music segment's 21.1% margin in FY2025 shows what they look like when little capital is needed to keep them. The sensor business is a different kind of moat — process technology and capacity that customers cannot easily replace — but it needs heavy, cyclical capital spending, which is exactly why Sony is bringing in TSMC as a partner rather than funding the next fab alone.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Memory-price inflation hits three segments at once. Sony's Q1 FY2026 materials flagged it in ET&S (camera and display costs), I&SS (fewer high-end phones shipped in H2) and G&NS (the cost of PS5 parts). It secured FY2026 PS5 memory supply; Reuters reported in November that the games business is still under pressure from higher memory-chip prices, in a quarter when PS5 units fell to 2.6 million from 4 million. Sony has not said what happens to pricing or margins after that.
02
Much of the growth is the yen. Q2 FY2026 sales rose 10% reported but about 4% at constant currency. Currency added ¥73.0B to G&NS sales, ¥34.3B to Music, ¥40.9B to I&SS and ¥26.6B to ET&S. A yen recovery from ~¥158–159 per dollar would reverse that mechanically.
03
Online games and Sony's own titles. The November G&NS forecast carries lower first-party software sales (games Sony publishes itself), mainly from a change in one title's release date. TheFly reported that Sony Santa Monica delayed the PS5 game God of War Laufey to May 20, 2027 (Nov 4, 2026) and that Sony ended development of the mobile game Ratchet & Clank: Ranger Rumble (Nov 6, 2026). A PS5 cycle late in life depends on how often those first-party releases hit.
04
Concentrated sensor customers and physical risk. Mobile sensors depend on a small number of smartphone makers and on Kyushu fabs; the July 28, 2026 earthquake stopped the Kumamoto Technology Center, and the November forecast includes it without giving a figure.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the figures. First, the spin-off loss: ¥1,357.8B from discontinued operations in FY2025, almost all of it the ¥1,377.8B transfer of accumulated OCI (unrealised bond losses at the insurer) into profit or loss. It makes trailing EPS, net margin and trailing P/E meaningless until it rolls out of the trailing twelve months, and FY2026 has no discontinued line. Second, US tariff refunds, a named driver of the Q1 G&NS profit jump and of ET&S's Q2 profit (about ¥80B expected, ~70% booked in Q1, per the Q1 earnings-call summary on Yahoo Finance) — not repeatable. Third, last year's Q2 charges at G&NS: a ¥31.5B Bungie impairment and an ¥18.3B development-cost correction, whose absence makes up about half of this Q2's ¥99.7B profit gain. Fourth, buybacks and share cancellation: Sony cancelled treasury stock worth ¥577.6B in Q1 FY2026 (issued shares fell from 6.150B to 5.965B), and under the ¥500B / 230M-share programme approved May 8, 2026 it had bought 86.07M shares for ¥309.0B by October 31, 2026.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 6, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Strip out the one-off spin-off loss and Sony trades at ~20.1x continuing earnings (TTG estimate) and ~18.3x consensus earnings for the year to March 2027, for a business whose profit comes mostly from rights and platforms: PlayStation (125M monthly users in June), a music business earning about 20% margins on growing streaming revenue, and an image-sensor franchise that is getting TSMC to share the next fab's cost. The insurer and its bond-market risk are gone. Q2 FY2026 operating income rose 45% to ¥622.3B at an 18.1% margin, and the full-year forecast went up by ¥150B to ¥1,870B, on top of July's ¥120B raise. The dividend goes up 40% to ¥35, the ¥500B buyback has ¥191B left to run, debt is roughly matched by cash, and a 6.5% free-cash-flow yield gives it room to be wrong on any one of them.

Bear Case

The headline beat was flattered. Sales rose about 4% at constant currency against 10% reported, and currency added ¥73.0B to G&NS sales alone. About half of G&NS's Q2 profit gain was the absence of last year's ¥31.5B Bungie charge and ¥18.3B development-cost correction, and one Spider-Man film drove Pictures' ¥78.7B gain. PS5 units fell to 2.6 million from 4 million (Reuters). Memory prices keep squeezing the PS5, cameras and TVs. Mobile image-sensor sales fell in Q2, and currency more than explains that segment's profit rise. The PS5 is late in its cycle while next-generation spending rises, first-party results are uneven (last year's Bungie write-down, a release-date change, a dropped Ratchet & Clank mobile game), and the Kumamoto earthquake's cost is in the forecast with no number attached. The balance sheet moved from ¥299.4B of net cash at June to ¥77.5B of net debt at September, counting leases, in the quarter the July music deal closed. Only four analysts rate the US-listed shares, and Bernstein's target sits below the price, so this is not a stock everyone already loves.

Top 3 Risks

01
The yen turns. About 89% of sales are earned outside Japan and reported in yen. The November forecast assumes ~¥153 per dollar for October–March; the ECB rate on November 6, 2026 was ¥158.33. A stronger yen reduces reported profit, and Q2 sales grew only about 4% at constant currency against 10% reported.
02
Memory costs and smartphone demand. I&SS earned ¥357.3B in FY2025 — 23% of segment profit — and management said in July it expected mobile-sensor revenue to slip this year as memory prices hit high-end phone volumes. The same input cost is squeezing ET&S and PS5 hardware.
03
Games execution into the next console cycle. G&NS is the largest profit line (33.6% of Q2 segment profit; 36.0% of the FY2026 forecast) and FY2026 guidance already includes next-generation platform investment, restructuring and a first-party roadmap adjustment. Tariff refunds flatter FY2026 and will not repeat in FY2027.
Industry Commentary
The memory squeeze on consumer hardware
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Sony's Q1 FY2026 materials use the phrase "continued surge in memory prices" and call it a key business challenge for FY2026. They also expect it to cut high-end smartphone shipments in the second half, which is Sony's sensor demand. For PlayStation, the company says it has "secured the quantity of memory necessary" for its FY2026 volume with hardware profitability "similar to FY25". That covers supply; it says nothing about the cost of the next generation, which the segment is already investing in. Sony is hit from both directions: it buys memory for its devices and sells sensors into phones that also need memory, so the squeeze reaches it twice. In November Reuters reported the games business still under pressure from higher memory-chip prices.

The weak yen: tailwind now, risk later
ONGOING SWING FACTOR

The dollar averaged ¥159.3 in Q2 FY2026, 8% more than a year earlier (¥147.4). That gap is more than half of the headline growth: sales +10% reported, about +4% at constant currency. Music and Pictures report in dollars and convert; games and sensors earn in dollars and euros against partly yen-based costs. Sony's November forecast assumes ~¥153 per dollar for October–March, so at the current ¥158 there is modest cushion. For a US holder of the ADS, a weak yen increases yen profits but lowers the dollar value of the shares — the two effects partly cancel.

From conglomerate to IP company — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

With Sony Financial Group spun off, the group is games, music, film/anime, sensors and a shrinking electronics tail. The bull framing: investors have long discounted conglomerates, and removing the insurer — with its ¥1.64T of unrealised bond losses — makes Sony simpler to value. Almost 60% of forecast FY2026 segment profit comes from PlayStation and Music, both rights-and-platform businesses, and the TSMC JV shares the capital burden of sensors. The bear framing: a simpler Sony is still a mix of a late-cycle console, a cyclical sensor business exposed to smartphone units, and a hit-driven studio. The market values it at $158B — below Disney and about 61% of Netflix — because those three businesses carry different risks, not because of the insurer. Whether the discount narrows depends on FY2026 profit holding at ¥1.87T without tariff refunds and a weak yen doing the work.
