01 Company Overview
SpaceX builds and flies rockets (Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy and Starship), runs the Starlink satellite-internet network, and since its February 2026 merger with xAI also runs an AI business: the Grok models, data centers in Memphis and Southaven, cloud computing sold to other companies, and since August 14 the AI coding tool Cursor. It reports three segments: Space (rockets and launch services), Connectivity (Starlink) and Super Intelligence (SI), the new name for the AI segment from this quarter. In Q3 2026 revenue was $14.58B, up 161% on a year earlier, and SI alone was $7.56B, more than Starlink for the first time. The company earned $4.40B of net income, its first profitable quarter since it listed on Nasdaq on June 12, 2026 at $135 a share in the largest IPO on record. At the Oct 22 close it was worth $1.97T.
The debate has moved from Starlink to AI computing. In the last edition the question was whether Starlink could grow into a $2T valuation. This quarter the AI segment did most of the work: its revenue rose by about $5B in three months, with Cursor contributing $608M of it. Management now expects Q1 2027 revenue of $33–37B, more than double Q3, against an analyst average of $22.2B (IANS). The cost is the build itself: SpaceX spent $19.6B on equipment and buildings (capital expenditure, or capex) in Q3, $16.4B of it on SI, and expects $35–40B in Q4 alone. At the end of September it had $89.9B of cash and marketable securities. Investors are now weighing the revenue those data centers bring in against what they cost to build, with a possible combination with Tesla on top.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Reusable Launch at Scale Over Half the World's Launches Largest Broadband Constellation Vertical Integration (Rocket→Sat→Terminal) Starship in Orbit $89.9B Cash & Securities Owned AI Data Centers
SpaceX's advantage is a loop no rival has closed: it owns the cheapest ride to orbit, so it can launch its own satellites at cost, so it can build by far the largest broadband constellation (about 9,000 satellites at the end of 2025, per Jalopnik), and Starlink's cash pays for the next, bigger rocket. Amazon Leo has 396 satellites deployed and has not started service; Rocket Lab's Neutron has slipped to 2027; Blue Origin's New Glenn has not flown since its May 28 pad explosion. Starship reaching orbit with working satellites on board is the next turn of the loop: Flight 14 added about ten times the downlink capacity of a Falcon 9 Starlink launch (company figure). The AI business adds a second argument: SpaceX owns its data centers rather than renting them, and the Q3 results show that capacity can be rented out at a high margin once it is running.

Key Vulnerabilities

The AI build eats the cash: capex was $48.1B in the first nine months and is guided to $35–40B in Q4, so $48.1B + $35–40B = $83–88B for 2026, against $89.9B of cash and securities at Sep 30 and $13.1B of operating cash flow so far this year. Free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex) was −$35.0B for the nine months. If the Q1 2027 revenue guide is met, operating cash flow should rise sharply; if it is not, SpaceX would have to borrow or sell shares to keep building at this pace.

Profit quality: of Q3's $4.40B net income, $1.03B was "other income" and $0.39B a tax benefit; operating income was $2.79B. The CFO said Q4 gross margin should look more like Q2's (55.3%) than Q3's 62.4%, because Q3 had one-time benefits and a favourable customer mix.

Governance and the Tesla question: Musk holds about 82% of the votes with about 42% of the shares. Both companies have formed special committees of independent directors to look at a merger; no terms are agreed. A deal would put a carmaker's earnings and risks inside a space-and-AI company, and the price paid for Tesla would decide whether SpaceX holders gain or lose from it.

Share supply: the float is 2.56B shares, under a fifth of the 13.58B outstanding (StockAnalysis). The Motley Fool reported about 1.3B more shares becoming eligible for sale on Oct 22 and 328M on Oct 24, and Musk's own lockup runs until June 2027 (Wikipedia).

Regulation and spectrum: Starlink depends on FCC and foreign spectrum licences and export approvals; the 800 MHz and EchoStar spectrum both need regulators' sign-off before Starlink Mobile can act as a full carrier.

  Industry Commentary
From Rocket Company to AI Landlord
THE STORY CHANGED
A year ago SpaceX was a launch company with a fast-growing internet business. In Q3 2026 more than half of its revenue came from the AI segment, which builds data centers, fills them with chips and sells the computing power, its own Grok models and Cursor's coding tools. That changes what the stock is a bet on. Rockets and Starlink are businesses with few real rivals; AI computing is a race against OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and Meta, all spending heavily on the same chips and power. SpaceX's case is that it can build faster and more cheaply because it owns the sites and runs them itself: capacity went from 0.6 GW a year ago to 1.7 GW at Sep 30, and Musk expects at least 13 GW by the end of 2027. The cost is visible in the cash-flow statement, and it was one of the reasons the Motley Fool gave for the stock's 9.1% fall on Oct 22, two days after a quarter that beat estimates for revenue and profit.
Is Starlink a Telecom? The Subscriber-and-ARPU Test
ONGOING DEBATE
The arithmetic: 13.6M subscribers at $69 a month is about $2.8B of consumer subscription revenue a quarter, or $11.3B a year at the current rate. Connectivity revenue was $5.25B in Q3, so nearly half comes from enterprise, aviation, shipping, government and hardware on top of home internet. At Q3's pace of 1.6M net adds a quarter, Starlink would end 2026 near 15M subscribers. The next step is phones: Starlink Mobile already has 22M monthly users, and the 800 MHz licences (pending FCC approval) and the EchoStar spectrum are meant to let it sell service directly as a carrier. Amazon Leo, with 396 satellites deployed and service promised for later in 2026, is a small fraction of Starlink's size. The limits are physics and regulators: how much capacity each satellite can give a crowded city, and spectrum approvals country by country.
What This Means for SPCX
BULL & BEAR IMPLICATIONS
Bull case: The launch business keeps rivals years behind, Starlink pays the bills, and AI has stopped being a promise: $7.56B of revenue in a quarter at a 66% adjusted EBITDA margin. If Q1 2027 lands in the $33–37B range, the valuation looks far less stretched than trailing figures suggest, and 30 of 38 analysts rate it a buy.

Bear case: Q4's guided capex alone equals 39–45% of the cash and securities on hand at Sep 30, most of it for data centers whose returns depend on AI computing staying scarce and well-priced. Q3's profit had help from below the operating line, gross margin is guided down, and a Tesla merger would add a second large, Musk-run business to the same share register. Until the Q1 2027 numbers arrive, holders are paying for a guide.
