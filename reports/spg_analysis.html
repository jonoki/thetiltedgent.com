01 Company Overview

Simon Property Group is the largest owner of class-A shopping malls, Premium Outlets and mixed-use retail destinations in the United States, structured as a self-administered REIT and an S&P 100 constituent. As of March 2026 the company held interests in more than 250 properties spanning over 200 million square feet across North America, Europe and Asia. Full-year 2025 revenue was $6.365 billion, up 6.72% YoY; trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 30, 2026 has accelerated to $6.941 billion (+15.02%), helped by the consolidation of the remaining Taubman Realty Group interest acquired in late October 2025.

At a $83.43 billion market capitalization Simon ranks roughly #210 among all listed companies globally by market value — an unusual position for a business the market spent most of the 2016–2020 period pricing for terminal decline. Because Simon is a REIT, the headline earnings figure to watch is FFO per share, not EPS: GAAP net income for a REIT is distorted by depreciation on buildings that are typically appreciating, and by episodic gains on property sales. Simon reports a further refined metric, Real Estate FFO, which additionally strips out its non-property "other platform investments" and unrealized marks on publicly traded securities. Q2 2026 Real Estate FFO was $3.29 per diluted share, up 7.9% YoY, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $13.20–$13.30.

⚠ Premise correction: this analysis was commissioned on the assumption that David Simon remains Chairman and CEO. He does not. Simon Property Group announced on March 23, 2026 that David Simon had died on March 22, 2026 at age 64, after an illness with cancer, ending a 31-year run as CEO that began with the 1993 IPO. His son Eli Simon, previously Chief Operating Officer and a director, was appointed CEO and President; Larry Glasscock became non-executive Chairman. CFO Brian McDade is confirmed still in seat and led the Q2 2026 call alongside Eli Simon.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
IRREPLACEABLE CLASS-A SITES 96% OCCUPANCY 200M+ SQ FT SCALE $9.3B LIQUIDITY NET DEBT/EBITDA <5.0x TENANT NEGOTIATING LEVERAGE $4B REDEVELOPMENT PIPELINE $50B+ DIVIDENDS PAID SINCE IPO

Simon's moat is supply, not software. Essentially no new enclosed regional malls have been built in the United States for over a decade, and the class-A sites Simon owns sit on land that zoning, cost and trade-area density make functionally impossible to replicate. That produces a landlord with unusual pricing power over its own tenants: initial base minimum rent on new deals rose 17% YoY year-to-date while tenant allowances per square foot fell 12% — a landlord that is simultaneously raising price and cutting the capital it contributes is not a landlord under competitive pressure. Scale compounds this: at 200+ million square feet, a national retailer negotiating with Simon is negotiating for a material fraction of its own store fleet at once, which is why Simon can absorb a bankruptcy like Saks Off 5th (~1 million square feet returned) with zero change in reported occupancy. The balance sheet is the third leg — $9.3 billion of liquidity ($1.7B cash, $7.6B available credit), net debt/EBITDA under 5.0x and fixed-charge coverage of 4.7x let Simon buy distressed assets, buy back stock (793,077 shares plus 238,000 LP units at an average $205.10 in Q2) and fund a $1.07 billion development pipeline at a 9% blended stabilised yield — a spread over its cost of capital that few landlords in any property type can currently underwrite.

Key Vulnerabilities

1 — Tenant credit concentration and the bankruptcy cycle. Simon's revenue is contractual rent from specialty retailers, a sector with structurally thin margins and a persistent bankruptcy cadence. The Saks Off 5th absorption in Q2 2026 was handled cleanly, but that is the benign case: a single large-format bankruptcy in a soft leasing environment forces re-leasing at whatever rent the market will bear, and co-tenancy clauses can trigger rent reductions at neighbouring tenants. The 96.0% occupancy figure is flat YoY — impressive, but it also means there is no occupancy cushion left to absorb a genuine wave.

2 — Interest rates and cap-rate sensitivity. Management guided to a $0.25–$0.30 per share full-year 2026 drag from higher interest expense and lower interest income, of which roughly $0.10 was realised through Q2 and $0.20 is expected across the remainder. $4.5 billion of unsecured debt matures in the second half of 2027. Beyond the P&L, REIT equity valuations move inversely with the cap rate the market applies to property NOI; a 50bps move in cap rates changes asset value far more than a year of NOI growth adds. This is the dominant non-operational driver of the share price.

3 — The non-REIT businesses. The "other platform investments" line is small in dollars but disproportionate in what it signals: Simon has taken operating equity in retailers, exposing shareholders to merchandising, inventory and fashion risk that a landlord is not structurally equipped to underwrite and that investors did not buy a REIT to own. The swing from a $0.10 per-share positive contribution in Q2 2025 to a $0.17 per-share drag in Q2 2026 — a $0.27 swing in one line — illustrates the volatility. Management's own creation of a separate "Real Estate FFO" metric is a tacit acknowledgement that this stuff obscures the property story.

4 — Leadership transition and key-man concentration. David Simon ran the company for 31 years and was, in the Chairman's words, "the finest leader in the history of the retail real estate industry." His death in March 2026 removed the single most important variable in Simon's capital-allocation record — including the contrarian bets (Taubman, the retailer stakes) that defined it. Eli Simon has delivered two clean quarters and a guidance raise since, and the board split the Chairman role to Larry Glasscock, but a five-month track record is not a track record.

Notable One-Off Items

This is the single most important thing to understand about SPG's reported numbers right now. FY2025 GAAP net income was $4.628 billion, up 95% from $2.371 billion in FY2024, on revenue that grew only 6.72%. That is not operating improvement. Operating cash flow in the same year was $4.137 billion — meaning reported net income slightly exceeded all cash generated by operations, a signature of a large non-cash gain. The disclosed cash-flow adjustments (D&A $1.528B, gain/loss on investments $86M) do not bridge the gap, and the sources reachable for this pass did not itemise the driver. Timing points to the completion of the acquisition of the remaining ~12% interest in Taubman Realty Group at the end of October 2025, which under GAAP typically triggers a fair-value remeasurement gain on the previously held stake — but this is an inference, not a verified figure. Confirm against the FY2025 10-K before relying on it.

Consequences for anyone reading screens: the trailing P/E of 15.50 is not a value signal, the 66.57% net margin is not a margin, and the 120.51% ROE is arithmetic noise. The forward P/E of 33.73 against consensus 2026 EPS of $6.61 is the market normalising for exactly this.

Recurring but volatile non-property items. Q2 2026 alone carried a $(56.4)M unrealized loss on publicly traded equities and derivatives, a $(11.95)M loss on disposal/revaluation of equity interests and $(8.75)M of impairment/loss on assets — all excluded from Real Estate FFO. Prior-year Q2 by contrast contained a Forever 21-related gain that made FFO exceed Real Estate FFO. Redevelopment spend is capitalised, not expensed: $1.07B net cost underway at a 9% blended stabilised yield, ~50% of it mixed-use, plus $600M+ of starts expected in H2 2026, $400M+ of centre-enhancement capital committed over four years, against a $4B total planned pipeline. Property sale gains are excluded from FFO by definition, which is precisely why FFO — and Real Estate FFO in particular — is the correct lens.

In plain English: RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been. Above 70 the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been sold hard and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

The five-and-a-half-year trajectory is a repricing, not a rally. On the dividend-adjusted monthly series, SPG went from $69.66 in January 2021 to $219.90 on August 20, 2026 — approximately +216%. That period begins with the stock still trading as a distressed asset: in 2020 Simon's market capitalisation fell 42.9% to $26.09B as the market priced enclosed malls as a melting ice cube. What happened since was not a growth story — revenue grew from $5.115B in FY2021 to $6.365B in FY2025, a 5.6% compound rate — it was the market slowly conceding that the terminal-decline thesis was wrong for class-A assets. Market cap has gone from $26.09B (2020 year-end) to $83.43B today. The trailing 52-week gain alone is +25.74%.

The path was not smooth. The stock round-tripped almost the entire 2021 recovery during the 2022 rate shock, falling from $125.11 in December 2021 to $73.51 in September 2022 — a 41% drawdown driven almost entirely by discount rates, not by occupancy or rent, which held. That episode is the cleanest available demonstration of how much of SPG's price is a bond-like duration exposure rather than a property exposure. The April 2025 dip to $148.29 repeated the pattern in miniature on consumer and tariff anxiety. The current level of $219.90 sits ~7.8% below the 52-week high of $238.50, having pulled back from $229.37 at the July 2026 month-end.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The class-A mall is not the asset the market spent a decade shorting. Occupancy of 96.0% is at or near record, base rent is compounding at 6.3%, retailer sales per square foot hit $838 (+13.9%), and Simon is raising rents 17% on new deals while cutting tenant allowances 12% — the pricing profile of a scarce asset, not a declining one. Real Estate FFO is guided to $13.20–$13.30 for 2026, putting the stock at roughly 16.6x P/FFO with a 4.09% yield covered at a ~68% payout, funding a $1.07B pipeline underwritten at a 9% stabilised yield with $9.3B of liquidity and net debt/EBITDA below 5.0x. Fully owning Taubman adds a self-help margin lever already worth 300bps with 200 more identified.

Bear Case

Nearly everything good is already in the price and in the multiple: at 16.6x P/FFO the stock has re-rated from distressed to full, and the Street has responded by raising targets while downgrading ratings — 13 of 21 analysts sit on Hold, Deutsche Bank and Wolfe both stepped down on valuation alone. Occupancy at 96.0% is flat YoY with no cushion left; growth from here must come from rent, which requires retailer health to hold. Interest expense is a guided $0.25–$0.30 per-share drag for 2026 with $4.5B of unsecured debt maturing in H2 2027, and a beta of 1.33 means SPG is a leveraged bet on both rates and discretionary spending. GAAP earnings are polluted by non-cash gains, the non-REIT retailer stakes swung $0.27 per share against the company in one year, and the 31-year CEO who made every one of these capital-allocation calls died five months ago.

Top 3 Risks
01
Interest rates and cap-rate expansion. The 2022 drawdown — $125.11 to $73.51, −41% — happened with occupancy and rent intact, purely on discount rates. With $29.44B of total debt, a guided $0.25–$0.30 FFO drag in 2026 and $4.5B refinancing in H2 2027, a higher-for-longer path damages both the earnings line and the multiple simultaneously.
02
Consumer discretionary rollover and retailer credit. Retailer sales of $838/sf up 13.9% is a peak-cycle figure; management has already assumed moderation in guidance. A genuine consumer downturn hits Simon twice — through percentage rent and through tenant bankruptcies that turn 96.0% occupancy into a re-leasing problem at whatever spread the market allows, with co-tenancy clauses amplifying the damage.
03
Capital allocation under new leadership. Eli Simon inherits a company whose distinguishing feature was contrarian, occasionally strange capital allocation — buying Taubman at the bottom, taking equity in the retailers that were failing on its own rent roll. Those bets worked because of a specific 31-year track record that no longer exists. Five months and two quarters do not establish that the process, rather than the person, was the moat.
  Industry Commentary
Class-A Resilience vs. the "Retail Apocalypse"
EMERGING TAILWIND
The retail-apocalypse thesis was directionally right about American retail square footage and badly wrong about which square footage. The bifurcation is now measurable. Simon's predominantly class-A portfolio runs 96.0% occupancy with base rent up 6.3% to $62.42/sf and tenant sales up 13.9% to $838/sf; The Mills runs 98.8% with average daily rent up 12.3%. New-deal rents are up 17% YoY with tenant allowances down 12% — landlords in structural decline do not get to raise price and cut concessions at the same time. Contrast the class-B/C end of the market: Macerich, the closest listed comparable, carries a TTM net loss of $170M, negative 6.18% ROE and a beta of 2.07. The lesson is not that malls survived; it is that roughly the top decile of malls became scarce infrastructure while the rest kept dying, and Simon owns the former. Deutsche Bank's framing — SPG as "one of the biggest beneficiaries of a K-shaped economy" — is the honest version: this is a bet on affluent consumers and premium tenants, not on retail broadly.
Interest Rates & REIT Valuations
ONGOING HEADWIND
REIT equity is priced off a cap rate, and cap rates track long rates. SPG's implied cap rate is roughly 4.6% (TTM EBITDA $5.13B over enterprise value $111.85B — an approximation, since EBITDA is not NOI). At that level, a 50bps upward move in the market's required cap rate would compress implied asset value by roughly a tenth — far more than a full year of 8% NOI growth adds. The P&L channel is separate and already quantified: management guided a $0.25–$0.30 per-share FFO drag for 2026 from higher interest expense and lower interest income, of which about $0.10 has landed and $0.20 remains, with $4.5B of unsecured debt maturing in H2 2027. Simon's defence is duration and optionality — $9.3B liquidity, €500M of Q2 senior notes, $1.4B of secured loan transactions, exploration of yen funding — plus free cash flow that deleverages naturally from below 5.0x net debt/EBITDA. It mitigates the earnings hit; it does not touch the multiple.
Consumer Spending, Retailer Health — Bull vs. Bear
TWO-SIDED — THE DECIDING VARIABLE
Bull framing: tenant demand is broad and deepening — 1,200+ leases on 4.8M sq ft in one quarter, new deals up 20% YoY, a prospective pipeline up 26%, comparable sales +5.7% in Q2 and +6.3% YTD, luxury and jewellery strong across price points, Gen Z "juniors" brands posting 16 straight months of positive comps, and $400–500M of incremental restaurant sales in the pipeline. Simon absorbed ~1M sq ft from the Saks Off 5th bankruptcy without moving occupancy — that is what demand depth looks like in practice. Bear framing: $838/sf tenant sales up 13.9% is a cycle-peak print, and management itself has built moderation into guidance despite the YTD run-rate. Occupancy at 96.0% is flat, meaning there is no slack left to absorb a cluster of failures rather than one. Simon's own equity stakes in retail operating companies swung from a $0.10/share FFO contribution to a $0.17/share drag in twelve months — direct evidence of how quickly retailer economics can turn. The resolution: at 16.6x P/FFO with a 4.09% yield covered at ~68% of FFO, the stock is priced for continuation, not acceleration and not deterioration. A soft-landing consumer plus one or two rate cuts is a re-rating; a discretionary-spending recession is a repeat of 2022.
