01 Company Overview

Simon Property Group is the largest owner of class-A shopping malls, Premium Outlets and mixed-use retail destinations in the United States, structured as a self-administered REIT. It left the S&P 100 (the 100 largest S&P 500 companies) on September 21, 2026, in S&P Dow Jones Indices' quarterly changes, and remains in the S&P 500. At December 31, 2025 the company owned or had an interest in 254 properties comprising 206 million square feet in North America, Asia and Europe. Full-year 2025 revenue was $6.365 billion, up 6.72% YoY; trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 30, 2026 reached $7.154 billion (+16.2%), helped by the consolidation of the remaining Taubman Realty Group interest acquired in late October 2025.

At an $88.13 billion market capitalization Simon ranks #144 in the S&P 500 by market value — an unusual position for a business the market spent most of the 2016–2020 period pricing for terminal decline. Because Simon is a REIT, the headline earnings figure to watch is FFO per share, not EPS: GAAP net income for a REIT is distorted by depreciation on buildings that are typically appreciating, and by episodic gains on property sales. Simon reports a further refined metric, Real Estate FFO, which additionally strips out its non-property "other platform investments" and unrealized marks on publicly traded securities. Q3 2026 Real Estate FFO was $3.37 per diluted share, up 4.7% YoY, and management raised full-year 2026 guidance again, to $13.30–$13.40, alongside a higher quarterly dividend of $2.30. GAAP net income fell to $1.54 per share from $1.86, after $0.27 per share of non-cash mark-to-market losses (paper losses from re-pricing investments at their market value). On October 30 a Simon subsidiary agreed to buy University Village, a nearly 600,000-square-foot open-air centre in Seattle, for an undisclosed price.

⚠ Leadership change: David Simon is no longer Chairman and CEO. Simon Property Group announced on March 23, 2026 that David Simon had died on March 22, 2026 at age 64, after an illness with cancer, ending a 31-year run as CEO that began in 1995, two years after the 1993 IPO. His son Eli Simon, previously Chief Operating Officer and a director, was appointed CEO and President; Larry Glasscock became non-executive Chairman. CFO Brian McDade remains in the role and presented the Q3 2026 results on the November 5 call alongside Eli Simon.

Simon holds equity stakes in retail operating companies and other investments — an unusual posture for a landlord, and awkward inside a REIT, which is designed to collect rent rather than run merchandising. Simon quantifies the effect precisely: the Q3 2026 reconciliation from FFO of $1.172B to Real Estate FFO of $1.277B adds back $99.6M of unrealized losses on publicly traded equity and derivative instruments and $6.5M of loss on disposal or revaluation of equity interests, and takes out $1.0M of income from other platform investments. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
IRREPLACEABLE CLASS-A SITES 96.5% OCCUPANCY 200M+ SQ FT SCALE $9.0B LIQUIDITY NET DEBT/EBITDA 5.0x TENANT NEGOTIATING LEVERAGE $1.2B DEVELOPMENT UNDERWAY $2.30 QUARTERLY DIVIDEND

Simon's moat is supply, not software. Very few new enclosed regional malls have been built in the United States for over a decade, and the class-A sites Simon owns sit on land that zoning, cost and trade-area density make functionally impossible to replicate. That produces a landlord with unusual pricing power over its own tenants: on the Q3 release's new-lease figures, average starting rent rose 16.8% YoY to $76.03 per square foot while tenant allowances fell 10.0% — a landlord that is simultaneously raising price and cutting the capital it contributes is not a landlord under competitive pressure. Scale compounds this: at 200+ million square feet, a national retailer negotiating with Simon is negotiating for a material fraction of its own store fleet at once, which is why Simon could absorb a bankruptcy like Saks Off 5th (~1 million square feet returned in Q2) with zero change in reported occupancy. The balance sheet is the third leg — about $9.0 billion of liquidity ($1.3B cash, $7.7B available credit) at September 30, net debt of five times EBITDA and fixed-charge coverage of 4.6x let Simon buy assets (it agreed on October 30 to acquire University Village in Seattle), buy back stock (1,137,182 shares at an average $207.98, or $236.5 million, in Q3) and fund a development pipeline of $1.2 billion (Simon's share of net cost) at a 9% blended yield — well above its borrowing cost (its new Q3 notes carry a 5.45% average coupon), a gap few landlords in any property type can currently match.

Key Vulnerabilities

1 — Tenant credit concentration and the bankruptcy cycle. Simon's revenue is contractual rent from specialty retailers, a sector with thin margins and a persistent bankruptcy cadence. The Saks Off 5th absorption in Q2 2026 was handled cleanly, but that is the benign case: a single large-format bankruptcy in a soft leasing environment forces re-leasing at whatever rent the market will bear, and co-tenancy clauses (lease terms that cut a tenant's rent when an anchor neighbour leaves) can trigger rent reductions at neighbouring tenants. Occupancy of 96.5% is up only 0.1 point YoY and ties the highest since 2016 — impressive, but it also means there is little empty space left to fill and little cushion to absorb a genuine wave.

2 — Interest rates and cap-rate sensitivity. Higher net interest expense cut Q3 FFO by $0.05 per share year over year, and Eli Simon said on the Q3 call that the headwind next year would probably be in a similar range of about $0.30 per share. CFO Brian McDade put 2027 unsecured maturities at about $2 billion, which Simon expects to refinance in the unsecured bond market. Beyond the P&L, REIT share prices move inversely with the cap rate — the yield buyers demand on a property's net operating income; a half-percentage-point rise in cap rates changes asset value far more than a year of NOI growth adds. This is the dominant non-operating driver of the share price.

3 — The non-REIT businesses. The "other platform investments" and listed-securities lines are small in dollars but disproportionate in what they signal: Simon has taken equity in retailers and other operating businesses, exposing shareholders to merchandising, inventory and market-price risk that a landlord is not built to manage and that investors did not buy a REIT to own. The swing from a $0.03 per-share positive contribution in Q3 2025 to a $0.28 per-share drag in Q3 2026 — mostly paper losses on the Klépierre exchangeable bonds and a newly listed equity stake — illustrates the volatility. Management's own creation of a separate "Real Estate FFO" metric is a tacit acknowledgement that these items obscure the property story.

4 — Leadership transition and key-man concentration. David Simon ran the company as CEO for 31 years. His death in March 2026 removed the single most important variable in Simon's capital-allocation record — including the contrarian bets (Taubman, the retailer stakes) that defined it. Eli Simon has since reported three quarters, raising Real Estate FFO guidance at the Q2 and Q3 prints, and the board split the Chairman role to Larry Glasscock, but under eight months is not a track record.

Notable One-Off Items

This is the single most important thing to understand about SPG's reported numbers right now. FY2025 GAAP net income was $4.628 billion, up 95% from $2.371 billion in FY2024, on revenue that grew only 6.72%. That is not operating improvement. Operating cash flow in the same year was $4.137 billion — meaning reported net income slightly exceeded all cash generated by operations, a signature of a large non-cash gain. The disclosed cash-flow adjustments (D&A $1.528B, gain/loss on investments $86M) do not bridge the gap. The driver is a $2.89 billion non-cash gain in Q4 2025, primarily from the acquisition of the remaining ~12% interest in Taubman Realty Group at the end of October 2025 (Q4 2025 release, Feb 2, 2026): taking control triggered a fair-value remeasurement of the previously held stake. That quarter is still inside the trailing twelve months, so trailing EPS ($13.86) and margins stay inflated until the Q4 2026 report replaces it.

Consequences for anyone reading screens: the trailing P/E of 16.81 is not a value signal, the 63.04% net margin is not a margin, and the 125.20% ROE is arithmetic noise. The forward P/E of 34.37 on the FY2027 consensus EPS of $6.78 (StockAnalysis, adjusted basis) shows what earnings look like without the gain.

Recurring but volatile non-property items. Q3 2026 carried $99.6M ($0.27 per share) of non-cash after-tax losses from marking to market the Klépierre exchangeable bonds and an equity security that became publicly traded in the quarter, plus a $6.5M loss on disposal or revaluation of equity interests — all excluded from Real Estate FFO. A year earlier the same lines netted to a small gain, which is why FFO exceeded Real Estate FFO in Q3 2025. Development spend is capitalised, not expensed: Simon's share of net cost on projects underway is $1.2B at a 9% blended yield, and on the November 5 call Eli Simon said Simon would break ground the following week on Sagefield, a new ground-up project south of Nashville that it expects to open by the end of 2028. Property sale gains are excluded from FFO by definition, which is precisely why FFO — and Real Estate FFO in particular — is the correct lens.

In plain English: RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been. Above 70 the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been sold hard and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

The five-year trajectory is a repricing, not a rally. On actual month-end closes, SPG went from $152.84 at the end of November 2021 to $233.01 on November 9, 2026 — a price return of about +52.5%, or about 8.9% a year, with dividends excluded. The window opens near the top of the post-COVID reopening rally, so it carries the whole 2022 rate shock. What happened over the period was not a growth story — revenue grew from $5.115B in FY2021 to $6.365B in FY2025, a 5.6% compound rate — it was the market slowly conceding that the terminal-decline thesis was wrong for class-A assets. The trailing 52-week gain alone is +26.91%.

The path was not smooth. The stock fell from $159.77 in December 2021 to $89.75 in September 2022 — a 44% drawdown on month-end closes while the Fed raised rates through 2022. The business did not weaken: occupancy was 94.5% at September 30, 2022, up from 92.8% a year earlier, and base rent rose to $54.80 a square foot from $53.91 (Q3 2022 release). That episode is the clearest demonstration of how much SPG's price behaves like a bond when rates move. The April 2025 dip to $157.38, 15% below February's close, was a smaller drop. After a $229.37 July 2026 month-end, the stock slid 12.5% to $200.76 at the end of September, then rebounded 16.1% to $233.01 by November 9 — the highest point on this chart and 2.3% below the 52-week intraday high of $238.50.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The class-A mall is not the asset the market spent a decade shorting. Occupancy of 96.5% ties the highest since 2016, base rent is up 6.1%, retailer sales per square foot hit $852 (+14.8%), and Simon is raising starting rents on new leases 16.8% while cutting tenant allowances 10% — the pricing profile of a scarce asset, not a declining one. Real Estate FFO is guided to $13.30–$13.40 for 2026 after another raise, putting the stock at about 17.5x price/FFO with a 3.95% yield covered at a ~69% payout, funding a $1.2B pipeline at a 9% blended yield with about $9.0B of liquidity. Fully owning Taubman adds a margin lever: on the Q2 call management said it had lifted TRG margins by 300 basis points (3 percentage points) this year, with more room to go, and University Village adds a hard-to-replicate Seattle centre.

Bear Case

Much of the good news is already in the price: at about 17.5x price/FFO the stock has gone from distressed to fully valued, the $233.01 close sits just above the $232.29 average target, and 15 of 21 analysts rate it Hold. Occupancy at 96.5% leaves little space to fill; growth from here must come from rent, which requires retailer health to hold. Eli Simon expects interest costs to be a headwind of about $0.30 per share again in 2027, with about $2B of unsecured debt to refinance, and a beta of 1.31 means SPG swings with both rates and discretionary spending. GAAP earnings are clouded by non-cash items — Q3 carried $0.27 per share of mark-to-market losses — and the CEO of 31 years who made the big capital-allocation calls died less than eight months ago.

Top 3 Risks
01
Interest rates and cap-rate expansion. The 2022 drawdown — $159.77 to $89.75, −44% — came as interest rates rose, while occupancy and rent went up. With $29.21B of total debt, a net-interest headwind Eli Simon put at about $0.30 per share for 2027 and about $2B of unsecured debt to refinance next year, a higher-for-longer path hurts both earnings and the valuation the market puts on them.
02
Consumer discretionary rollover and retailer credit. Retailer sales of $852/sf, up 14.8%, are a peak-cycle figure. A genuine consumer downturn hits Simon twice — through percentage rent (rent tied to a tenant's sales) and through tenant bankruptcies that turn 96.5% occupancy into a re-leasing problem at whatever rent the market allows, with co-tenancy clauses amplifying the damage.
03
Capital allocation under new leadership. Eli Simon inherits a company whose distinguishing feature was contrarian, occasionally strange capital allocation — buying Taubman at the bottom, taking equity in the retailers that were failing on its own rent roll. Those bets worked because of a specific 31-year track record that no longer exists. Under eight months and three quarterly reports do not establish that the process, rather than the person, was the moat; University Village is an early test.
  Industry Commentary
Class-A Resilience vs. the "Retail Apocalypse"
EMERGING TAILWIND
The retail-apocalypse thesis was directionally right about American retail square footage and badly wrong about which square footage. The split is now measurable. Simon's predominantly class-A portfolio runs 96.5% occupancy with base rent up 6.1% to $62.75/sf and tenant sales up 14.8% to $852/sf; The Mills runs 98.9% with base rent up 11.0%. Starting rents on new leases are up 16.8% YoY with tenant allowances down 10% — landlords in long-term decline do not get to raise price and cut concessions at the same time. Contrast the weaker end of the market: Macerich, the closest listed comparable, carries a TTM net loss of $99.75M, a negative 3.65% ROE and a beta of 2.12. The lesson is not that malls survived; it is that roughly the top tier of malls became scarce infrastructure while the rest kept dying, and Simon owns the former. Put plainly, this is a bet on affluent consumers and premium tenants, not on retail broadly.
Interest Rates & REIT Valuations
ONGOING HEADWIND
REIT equity is priced off a cap rate (the yield buyers demand on a property's net operating income), and cap rates track long-term interest rates. SPG's implied cap rate is roughly 4.5% (TTM EBITDA $5.29B over enterprise value $116.77B — an approximation, since EBITDA is not NOI). At that level, a half-percentage-point rise in the market's required cap rate would cut implied asset value by roughly a tenth — far more than a full year of ~7% NOI growth adds. The P&L channel is separate: higher net interest expense cut Q3 FFO by $0.05 per share year over year, and Eli Simon expects a headwind of about $0.30 per share again in 2027, with about $2B of unsecured debt maturing. Simon's defence is time and flexibility — about $9.0B of liquidity, $800M of Q3 senior notes at a 5.45% average coupon, about $970M of Q3 secured loans and a pre-funded 2026 maturity — plus free cash flow. It softens the earnings hit; it does not touch the valuation.
Consumer Spending, Retailer Health — Bull vs. Bear
TWO-SIDED — THE DECIDING VARIABLE
Bull framing: tenant demand is broad — nearly 1,200 leases on more than 4.3M sq ft in Q3, comparable sales +4.9% in the quarter and +5% over twelve months, traffic growth accelerating with October running in line, and Eli Simon saying "We really haven't seen any slowdown." Simon absorbed ~1M sq ft from the Saks Off 5th bankruptcy in Q2 without moving occupancy — that is what deep demand looks like in practice. Bear framing: $852/sf tenant sales up 14.8% is a cycle-peak print, and occupancy at 96.5% leaves little slack to absorb a cluster of failures rather than one. Simon's own investment stakes swung from a $0.03/share FFO contribution to a $0.28/share drag in twelve months — direct evidence of how quickly the value of those holdings can turn. The resolution: at 17.5x price/FFO with a 3.95% yield covered at ~69% of FFO, the stock is priced for continuation, not acceleration and not deterioration. A soft-landing consumer plus one or two rate cuts would lift the valuation; a discretionary-spending recession is a repeat of 2022.
