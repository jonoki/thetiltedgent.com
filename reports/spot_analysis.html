01 Company Overview

Spotify is the world's largest audio-streaming subscription service: 777 million monthly active users and 300 million Premium subscribers at June 30, 2026, selling music, podcasts, audiobooks and a growing video catalogue. At a $106.2B market value it ranks #216 globally and #2 in Sweden by market capitalisation (companiesmarketcap country ranking, behind Investor AB). The legal parent is a Luxembourg société anonyme; the company is run from Stockholm; the only listing is the NYSE line, held as ordinary shares rather than ADRs. Because there is no Stockholm listing it does not sit in Sweden's home index, the OMX Stockholm 30. FY2025 revenue was €17,186M, up 9.65%, with operating income of €2,198M (12.8% margin). Most recent reported period: Q2 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026), reported August 4, 2026.

The premise supplied for this report is "finally profitable". The data say that is two years out of date. Spotify's operating loss was €659M in FY2022 (StockAnalysis) and €446M in FY2023 (20-F basis). It turned to about €1,365M of operating profit in FY2024 and €2,198M in FY2025, and every quarter since Q3 2024 has been profitable at the operating line. The live question in 2026 is not whether Spotify makes money but how far the margin can run against a cost base that is mostly royalties it does not control. Two numbers carry the whole story. Gross margin went from 26% in FY2023 to 32% in FY2025 to a record 33.4% in Q2 2026 — meaning roughly two-thirds of every euro still goes to cost of revenue, but that share is shrinking. And operating expenses fell, from €3,843M in FY2023 to €3,298M in FY2025, while revenue rose €3.9B. Operating profit is the gap between those two lines, and both moved the right way at once. One more caution: the net income line (TTM €3,339M) runs €686M above operating income because of a deferred-tax benefit and financial-instrument revaluations — the operating line is the clean one.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

777M MAU scale 300M paying subscribers Personalisation & listening data Free-to-Premium funnel Marketplace programs vs royalties Family/Duo plan retention Net cash balance sheet

The catalogue is not the moat — every serious competitor licenses the same songs from the same four rights groups. The moat is scale on the demand side: the largest audience lets Spotify sell promotion back to the labels (marketplace programs), recommendation data improves with usage, and multi-device, multi-member plans raise switching costs. The 20-F notes that subscribers on several devices "have higher engagement and lower churn." Price increases that stick without a subscriber exodus are the proof — subscribers grew 9% in the year ARPU rose 7%. The weakness is that the moat protects the audience, not the margin: the labels keep the power to reprice the licence.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Supplier concentration. Four licensors supply ~72% of label-delivered streams. The 20-F warns that these rights holders "may attempt to take advantage of their market power (including by leveraging their publishing affiliate) to seek onerous financial or other terms." Every point of gross margin Spotify has won is a point the labels can bargain back at renewal.
02
Competitors who do not need music to make money. Apple, Amazon and Alphabet run music streaming as part of larger ecosystems and can price it as a loss leader. Spotify's margin comes only from audio (and now video).
03
Governance. With 69.3% of the vote held by two founders, outside shareholders cannot force a strategy change, a sale or a board change. That has worked so far — the founders drove the 2023–25 cost discipline — but it is a structural discount for any holder who needs a voice.
04
Share-price-linked costs. Spotify accrues social charges on share-based pay at the current share price, so the operating line moves with the stock: Q2 2026 social charges were €1M, €9M below forecast "due to share price movements." A rally raises costs; a sell-off flatters them.

Notable One-Off Items

Spotify's net income is noisy, and most of the noise sits below the operating line. (1) Tax: FY2025 income tax expense was only €12M, versus €203M in FY2024, "primarily related to the incremental recognition of deferred tax assets", worth about €159M (20-F). (2) Finance items: the results include fair-value moves on the Exchangeable Notes and on the long-term stake in Tencent Music Entertainment, plus FX on monetary balances. FY2025 finance income was €292M and finance costs €266M. Q2 2026 was a net +€65M. Quarterly swings are large: Q2 2025 had €406M of operating income and still reported a €86M net loss, a €492M gap below the line. Q4 2025 had €701M of operating income and €1,174M of net income. (3) Social charges are marked to the share price every quarter (€1M in Q2 2026; the Q3 guide assumes €9M at a $459.13 share price). The TTM EPS behind the P/E above includes Q4 2025. On operating income alone, TTM is €2,653M.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of $225.34 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $516.77, SPOT gained 129.3% in price (no dividends). The endpoints hide a round trip on each side. First came a 65% collapse to $78.95 by December 2022, when the market priced an unprofitable, royalty-bound business at a steep discount. Then came a nearly tenfold climb to $767.34 at June 2025 month-end as the operating line turned positive and stayed there. Since then the stock has given back 32.7% while the fundamentals kept improving. The 2025–26 de-rating is a multiple story, not an earnings story.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows the regime change. It sat above 90 for most of 2024, peaking at 98.8 in November 2024 — as one-sided as a monthly series gets. It fell to 28.9 in April 2026, the first sub-30 reading since the 2022 bear market, and now reads 49.0, neutral. The 10-month moving average is about $504.46, just under the current price, and the 3-month average is $520.11, just above it. The stock is sitting on its trend line, not breaking out of it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Spotify has shown over three years that it can widen gross margin against a concentrated supplier base: 26% in FY2023, a record 33.4% in Q2 2026. Premium is at 34.9%, and Ad-Supported recovered from 4% to 19.1%. Operating costs have fallen while revenue compounds, so operating income went from −€446M to €2,198M in two years and is guided at €670M for Q3 alone. Revenue growth reaccelerated to +14% in Q2 as price increases stuck, with subscribers still growing 9%. The balance sheet holds about $7.45B of net cash, and free cash flow is running at €3.3B a year. The stock is 30% below its 52-week high, and 33 of 40 analysts rate it Buy.

Bear Case

At 28x trailing and about 37x forward earnings, the stock already prices in the margin expansion — while the trailing EPS is flattered by a deferred-tax benefit and finance gains, and consensus FY2026 EPS (€12.28) is below TTM (€15.92). Q2's record gross margin is guided down to 32.9% for Q3, operating income fell from Q1 to Q2, and AI, marketing and video payouts are new cost lines. Four licensors control about 72% of label streams and can reprice at renewal. Ad-Supported, the funnel for 477M free users, is not growing. Two founders hold 69.3% of the vote, so outside holders get the economics without the control.

Top 3 Risks

01
Royalty repricing. Gross margin is the entire thesis, and its cost base is set by licences with Universal, Sony, Warner and Merlin (~72% of label-delivered streams). Price increases that lift ARPU also enlarge the revenue pool the labels negotiate over.
02
Reinvestment eats the operating leverage. Q2 operating income (€655M) came in below Q1 (€715M). The Q3 guide steps gross margin down to 32.9%, and per Yahoo Finance's Q2 call summary about €200M of incremental marketing and AI costs are expected for full-year 2026, weighted to Q2 and Q3. Video-podcast and audiobook costs now sit inside Premium's cost of revenue. Management is choosing to reinvest part of the margin gain, and that choice can be repeated.
03
Earnings quality and governance. Net income swings by hundreds of millions of euros a quarter on tax, finance items and share-price-linked social charges. That makes the P/E a poor anchor. And with 69.3% of the vote held by Ek and Lorentzon through beneficiary certificates, minority holders have no lever if capital allocation disappoints.
Industry Commentary
Streaming grows up: from subscriber land-grab to price and margin
EMERGING TAILWIND

Spotify's own numbers mark the turn. In FY2025 Premium revenue grew 11% on subscriber growth "partially offset by a decrease in Premium ARPU". By Q2 2026 ARPU was growing 7% and revenue 14%. The platform has moved from buying growth with low prices to charging for it. The same shift produced the margin recovery: Ad-Supported gross margin, 4% in FY2023, recovered mainly through "a reduction in podcast costs" (20-F). What the industry has not yet shown is where the equilibrium sits between platform margin and label economics. The labels are the other side of every point Spotify gains.

Video, audiobooks and AI: new content, new cost lines
ONGOING HEADWIND

Spotify is widening the product from music to video podcasts (500,000+ shows), audiobooks (with an Audiobooks+ add-on launched in 2025) and creator payouts through the Spotify Partner Program. Each raises engagement and supports pricing. Each also adds a cost-of-revenue line — the company's own Q2 explanation for Premium margin lists "audiobooks costs and video podcast costs" alongside music. The Q3 guide shows the trade-off: the gross margin guide steps down and, per Yahoo Finance, AI and marketing spend steps up. The investment case now rests on management keeping content breadth accretive.

Supplier power and the valuation gap
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

On Sep 21, 2026 the market valued Spotify at $106.2B, about 2.4 times Universal Music Group ($30.33B) and Warner Music Group ($14.48B) combined. Yet those two, with Sony and Merlin, control the content behind about 72% of label-delivered streams. The bull framing: scale has flipped the bargaining power — the labels need Spotify's 300M payers and its marketplace tools more than Spotify needs any single label, and three years of margin gains are the evidence. The bear framing: licences are renegotiated, not permanent, and the 20-F itself warns the rights holders may use their market power "to seek onerous financial or other terms." The next licence cycle, not the next quarter, sets the ceiling on Spotify's margin.
