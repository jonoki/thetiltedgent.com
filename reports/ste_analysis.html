01 Company Overview

Every surgical instrument that touches a patient has to be clean, and every catheter, implant and syringe that leaves a factory has to be sterile. STERIS sits in the plumbing of both. It sells hospitals the sterilizers, washers, endoscope reprocessing gear, consumables and service contracts that turn a used tray back into a safe one; it runs contract sterilization plants for medical-device makers through its Applied Sterilization Technologies (AST) segment; and it supplies pharmaceutical manufacturers with the equipment and chemistry for sterile production. Fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) revenue from continuing operations was $5.94B, up 8.7%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $7.93 and adjusted EPS of $10.17.

The company is a product of deals as much as of engineering. Founded in Ohio in 1985, it bought the UK's Synergy Health in a $1.9B transaction announced in October 2014 — an inversion that moved its legal home to the UK — then re-domiciled to Ireland in November 2018. It acquired Key Surgical (~$850M, November 2020) and Cantel Medical (completed June 2021), which is why fiscal 2022 revenue jumped 35.9%. In April 2024 it agreed to sell its Dental segment to an affiliate of Peak Rock Capital for $787.5M (the deal's closing was not confirmed in the sources fetched), leaving three segments. The story now is a boring one told well: high-single-digit growth, most of it recurring. The market has nonetheless marked the stock down from an all-time high of $269.44 on January 16, 2026 to $206.97 — a 23% slide in eight months — while management kept its fiscal 2027 guidance intact. The debate is whether tariffs, a $600M plant build and a higher capex bill are a temporary margin tax or a sign that the premium multiple was the thing that was wrong.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base → consumables & service Regulated, validated processes Contract sterilization network (AST) Procedure-volume exposure, not elective capex 15 years of dividend growth Modest leverage (D/E 0.28)

The moat is validation. A device maker's sterilization process is written into its regulatory filings; switching sterilizer or sterilization method means re-validating, which costs time and carries risk nobody in a quality department wants to own. A hospital that standardises on one vendor's washers, sterilizers and chemistry has the same inertia on a smaller scale. That shows in the numbers: Healthcare service and consumables grew 10% and 9% in the latest quarter while capital equipment grew 1%, and AST earns a 48% segment margin. The vulnerability is that the moat protects volume, not input costs — tariffs, labour and depreciation all ran through the margin line over the past year.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Tariffs sit on the cost line. Q3 fiscal 2026 gross margin fell 70bps to 43.9% as "increased tariffs and inflation" outran price and productivity, and the fiscal 2026 tariff estimate rose to ~$55M pre-tax from ~$45M. Refunds helped in Q1 fiscal 2027, but a policy-driven cost can come back as quickly as it left.
02
Ethylene oxide litigation. STERIS's Isomedix subsidiary agreed in March 2025 to pay up to $48.15M to resolve substantially all pending personal-injury claims in Cook County, Illinois, tied to a Waukegan plant it operated from 2005 to 2008 — without admitting liability. The industry-wide litigation continues; new venues or new plants are an open-ended tail.
03
Capex step-up. The $600M North Carolina Formulated Chemistry Center of Excellence is the company's largest single-site investment, with operations 2–3 years out and a target of ROIC above 10% within 3–5 years. Until then it lifts capex, adds $55–70M of restructuring charges and lowers free cash flow.
04
Endoscopy concentration. About a third of Healthcare is linked to endoscopy activity, and the CEO acknowledged elevated volumes partly reflect a push toward earlier colon-cancer screening, saying sustainability remained uncertain.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported figures. First, the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is wide: fiscal 2026 GAAP EPS of $7.93 against adjusted $10.17, and Q1 fiscal 2027 $2.04 against $2.59. Valuation looks very different depending on which line you use (25.3x GAAP TTM, 18.5x on the adjusted guide). Second, portfolio changes: the Cantel acquisition (completed June 2021) drove the 35.9% revenue jump in fiscal 2022, and the Dental segment, agreed for sale in April 2024, is reported as discontinued operations, so current figures are "continuing operations". Third, restructuring: $55–70M of pre-tax charges (~$40–50M cash) for consolidating chemistry manufacturing into North Carolina and closing facilities in St. Louis, Missouri and Plymouth, Minnesota. Free cash flow for fiscal 2026 is $972.4M on StockAnalysis and $982.9M in the company's release; the difference was not reconciled.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A business with high-single-digit growth, 44% gross margins that have not moved more than a point in five years, 48% margins in contract sterilization and a customer base that has to keep buying consumables and service is trading at 18.5x the midpoint of its own fiscal 2027 guide. Every analyst price target, including the lowest at $232, is above the current price. Leverage is modest (debt/EBITDA 1.19), dividends have grown for 15 straight years, $900M of buyback authorisation remains, and procedure volumes have not slowed. If tariffs stay a manageable cost and the North Carolina plant delivers its targeted return, today's price looks like the market overreacting to a noisy year.

Bear Case

On GAAP numbers this is still a 25.3x P/E for a company growing revenue 7–8%, with a 4.6% free-cash-flow yield that is about to shrink: fiscal 2027 FCF is guided to ~$800M against $925.6M TTM as capex jumps to ~$450M. Tariffs are a policy variable outside management's control, AST organic growth of 5% is running below its 7–8% guide, and segment margins fell in both AST and Life Sciences last quarter. Ethylene oxide litigation is a tail that has already cost up to $48.15M in one county. Five years of flat share price suggest the market was paying for growth in 2021 that it no longer believes is worth a premium.

Top 3 Risks

01
Tariff and input-cost pressure on Healthcare margins. The fiscal 2026 tariff estimate rose to ~$55M pre-tax and cost 70bps of gross margin in Q3. Q1 fiscal 2027 benefited from $4M of refunds; that is not a recurring line.
02
Ethylene oxide liability. AST's most profitable franchise uses a sterilant under industry-wide litigation. STERIS settled its Illinois cases for up to $48.15M without admitting liability; any new wave of claims would hit the segment that carries the group's margins.
03
Capex and execution on the North Carolina build. $600M, 2–3 years to operation, restructuring charges of $55–70M and plant closures in two states. Delays or overruns would extend the period of depressed free cash flow.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs as a margin tax on medical equipment
ONGOING HEADWIND

STERIS's 2026 is a clean case study in what tariffs do to a company with pricing power but not unlimited pricing power. Revenue kept growing at high single digits; gross margin fell 70bps in Q3 fiscal 2026 as "increased tariffs and inflation" outran price and productivity, and the fiscal 2026 tariff estimate rose from ~$45M to ~$55M. CEO Dan Carestio framed the response as "cost discipline" delivering EPS growth "despite increased tariff headwinds." In Q1 fiscal 2027 gross tariff costs were $14M against $12M a year earlier, with $4M of refunds received. The market has treated this as a structural hit to the multiple rather than a temporary one.

Ethylene oxide: essential and litigated
TAIL RISK

Ethylene oxide sterilizes roughly 20 billion medical devices a year and accounts for about half of all sterile device sterilization globally, per Medical Design & Outsourcing. It is also the subject of hundreds of personal-injury claims across the industry alleging harm from long-term, low-level emissions. STERIS's exposure so far has centred on a Waukegan, Illinois plant its Isomedix unit ran from 2005 to 2008: after a January 2025 mistrial, it agreed in March 2025 to pay up to $48.15M to resolve substantially all pending Cook County claims, excluded from adjusted earnings. The method is hard to replace for many heat- and radiation-sensitive devices, which is part of AST's moat; the litigation is the price of that moat.

Procedure volumes — the demand side is holding
SUPPORTIVE

The bull and bear cases agree on demand. Management said on the Q1 fiscal 2027 call that it "had not seen a slowdown in hospital procedure activity," with strong growth in ambulatory surgery centres and endoscopy, and Healthcare service and consumables grew 10% and 9%. The bull framing: a company whose demand is steady and whose cost problem is partly policy-driven is being priced as if its growth were in doubt. The bear framing: part of the endoscopy strength reflects a screening push the CEO himself called of uncertain sustainability, and a stock that de-rated through a year of steady demand is telling you the multiple, not the demand, was the problem. Gross margin and AST organic growth over the next two quarters are where the two framings get tested.
