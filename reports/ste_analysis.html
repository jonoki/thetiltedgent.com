01 Company Overview

Every surgical instrument that touches a patient has to be clean, and every catheter, implant and syringe that leaves a factory has to be sterile. STERIS sits in the plumbing of both. It sells hospitals the sterilizers, washers, endoscope reprocessing gear, consumables and service contracts that turn a used tray back into a safe one; it runs contract sterilization plants for medical-device makers through its Applied Sterilization Technologies (AST) segment; and it supplies pharmaceutical manufacturers with the equipment and chemistry for sterile production. Fiscal 2026 (year ended March 31, 2026) revenue from continuing operations was $5.94B, up 8.7%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $7.93 and adjusted EPS of $10.17. In the latest quarter, the second of fiscal 2027 (ended September 30, 2026), revenue rose 9% to $1.59B and adjusted EPS was $2.94.

The company is a product of deals as much as of engineering. Founded in Ohio in 1985, it bought the UK's Synergy Health in a $1.9B transaction announced in October 2014, which moved its legal home to the UK (a tax-driven structure known as an inversion), then re-domiciled to Ireland in March 2019. It acquired Key Surgical (~$850M, November 2020) and Cantel Medical (completed June 2021), which is why fiscal 2022 revenue jumped 35.9%. In April 2024 it agreed to sell its Dental segment to an affiliate of Peak Rock Capital for $787.5M, leaving three segments. On October 11, 2026 it agreed to buy Xodus Medical, a Pennsylvania maker of single-use operating-room products, for $2.1B in cash plus up to $150M more if calendar 2028 targets are met, paid for mainly with new debt. The stock has swung through all of this: from a high of $269.44 on January 16, 2026 to a low of $195.14 on May 7, and back to $252.30 at this report's date, after a second quarter that beat forecasts and lifted the full-year earnings guide. The debate now is how much of that beat lasts: tariff refunds supplied part of the margin gain, contract-sterilization growth was guided down, and a debt-funded deal and a $600M plant are landing at the same time.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base → consumables & service Regulated, validated processes Contract sterilization network (AST) Demand follows procedure counts, not optional equipment spending 21 years of dividend growth Modest debt today (debt/equity 0.28)

The moat is validation. A device maker's sterilization process is written into its regulatory filings; switching sterilizer or sterilization method means re-validating, which costs time and carries risk nobody in a quality department wants to own. A hospital that standardises on one vendor's washers, sterilizers and chemistry has the same inertia on a smaller scale. That shows in the numbers: Healthcare service and consumables each grew 10% in the latest quarter while capital equipment grew 4%, and AST earns a 44.7% segment margin. The vulnerability is that the moat protects volume, not input costs — tariffs, labour and depreciation all ran through the margin line over the past year.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Tariffs sit on the cost line. Q3 fiscal 2026 adjusted gross margin fell 0.7 percentage points to 43.9% as "increased tariffs and inflation" outran price and productivity. Q2 fiscal 2027's 1.0-point rise in adjusted gross margin, to 45.3%, was driven primarily by about $20M of tariff refunds, and the CFO expects no material further refunds this fiscal year, so that help does not repeat.
02
Ethylene oxide litigation. STERIS's Isomedix subsidiary agreed in March 2025 to pay up to $48.15M to resolve substantially all pending personal-injury claims in Cook County, Illinois, tied to a Waukegan plant it operated from 2005 to 2008 — without admitting liability. The 10-Q for September 30, 2026 says the remaining amounts were paid in the quarter and substantially all of those claims have been dismissed with prejudice, meaning they cannot be refiled. The industry-wide litigation continues, and new venues or new plants could bring more claims.
03
Capex step-up. The $600M North Carolina Formulated Chemistry Center of Excellence is the company's largest single-site investment, with operations 2–3 years out, restructuring completion expected in fiscal 2030 and a target of return on invested capital above 10% within 3–5 years. Until then it lifts capex, adds $55–70M of restructuring charges and lowers free cash flow.
04
Endoscopy concentration. About a third of Healthcare is linked to endoscopy activity, and on the Q1 fiscal 2027 call the CEO acknowledged elevated volumes partly reflect a push toward earlier colon-cancer screening, saying sustainability remained uncertain.
05
A debt-funded deal. Xodus costs $2.1B in cash. JPMorgan and Goldman Sachs committed up to $2.0B of bridge financing (short-term loans meant to be replaced by permanent borrowing), and a $500M term loan that can be drawn once the deal closes was signed on November 4. Borrowings were $1.89B at September 30, about 1.1 times EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation and amortization) by the CFO's count, so the deal would roughly double them before any paydown, and it brings integration work while the chemistry consolidation is under way.

Notable One-Off Items

Four things distort the reported figures. First, the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is wide: fiscal 2026 GAAP EPS of $7.93 against adjusted $10.17, and Q2 fiscal 2027 $2.19 against $2.94. Valuation looks very different depending on which line you use (30.0x GAAP TTM, 22.0x on the adjusted guide). Second, tariff refunds: about $20M received in Q2 and about $24M in the first half, worth about $0.18 of adjusted EPS year to date. The CFO said adjusted EPS grew 13% excluding refunds, against 19% as reported. Third, restructuring: $55–70M of pre-tax charges (~$40–50M cash) for consolidating chemistry manufacturing into North Carolina and closing facilities in St. Louis, Missouri and Plymouth, Minnesota, plus a separate plan of about $45M (about $25M booked in Q2) for facility, product and organizational changes that the release expects to save about $9M a year. Fourth, portfolio changes: the Cantel acquisition (completed June 2021) drove the 35.9% revenue jump in fiscal 2022, and the Dental segment, agreed for sale in April 2024, is reported as discontinued operations, so current figures are "continuing operations". Free cash flow on this page uses the company's definition ($982.9M for fiscal 2026); StockAnalysis, which shows $972.4M for that year, puts the trailing figure at $937.2M.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A business with high-single-digit growth, 44% gross margins that stayed within about a point across fiscal 2022–2026, roughly 45% margins in contract sterilization and a customer base that has to keep buying consumables and service just beat forecasts and raised its full-year adjusted EPS guide to $11.35–$11.55. Life Sciences grew 18% at a margin the CEO called a record, Xodus would add single-use operating-room products that are bought again with each procedure, and debt today is modest (debt/EBITDA 1.16). Dividends have grown for 21 straight years and $800M of buyback authorisation remains. If tariffs stay manageable, AST growth recovers and the North Carolina plant delivers its targeted return, the recovery since May has further to run.

Bear Case

At 30.0x trailing GAAP earnings and 22.0x the fiscal 2027 guide, the stock already reflects the beat, and part of the beat was tariff refunds that management does not expect to continue. AST organic growth of 5% led management to cut that segment's guide to 6–7%, Healthcare's margin fell 1.1 points, and free cash flow is guided to ~$800M against $947.7M TTM as capex jumps to ~$450M. Top 3 Risks

01
A debt-funded deal on top of a plant build. Xodus costs $2.1B in cash, funded mainly with debt, while the $600M North Carolina site takes capex to ~$450M and brings restructuring charges and plant closures in two states. Delays, overruns or a weak integration would extend the period of depressed free cash flow.
02
Ethylene oxide liability. AST's most profitable franchise uses a sterilant under industry-wide litigation. STERIS settled its Illinois cases for up to $48.15M without admitting liability; any new wave of claims would hit the segment that carries the group's margins.
03
Tariff costs and the end of refunds. Gross tariff costs ran at about $15M in Q2 fiscal 2027. About $20M of refunds that quarter lifted margins, and management does not expect material further refunds this fiscal year, while AST's organic growth guide has come down to 6–7%.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs as a margin tax on medical equipment
ONGOING HEADWIND

STERIS's 2026 is a clean case study in what tariffs do to a company with pricing power but not unlimited pricing power. Revenue kept growing at high single digits; gross margin fell 0.7 percentage points in Q3 fiscal 2026 as "increased tariffs and inflation" outran price and productivity, and the fiscal 2026 tariff estimate rose from ~$45M to ~$55M. CEO Dan Carestio framed the response as "cost discipline" delivering EPS growth "despite increased tariff headwinds." In Q2 fiscal 2027 gross tariff costs were about $15M, more than offset by about $20M of refunds, which the CFO does not expect to recur in any material amount this fiscal year. Refunds are a one-time gain, so the cleaner read on margins comes once they drop out of the numbers.

Ethylene oxide: essential and litigated
LEGAL RISK

Ethylene oxide is used on about 20 billion medical devices a year, roughly half of all sterile medical devices, per Medical Design & Outsourcing. It is also the subject of hundreds of personal-injury claims across the industry alleging harm from long-term, low-level emissions. STERIS's exposure so far has centred on a Waukegan, Illinois plant its Isomedix unit ran from 2005 to 2008: after a January 2025 mistrial, it agreed in March 2025 to pay up to $48.15M to resolve substantially all pending Cook County claims, excluded from adjusted earnings; the 10-Q for September 30, 2026 says the remaining amounts have been paid. The method is hard to replace for many heat- and radiation-sensitive devices, which is part of AST's moat; the litigation is the price of that moat.

Procedure volumes — the demand side is holding
SUPPORTIVE

The bull and bear cases agree on demand. On the November 5 call the CEO said he expected procedure growth at the lower end of the range, around 3%, and that endoscopy growth has remained strong; Healthcare service and consumables each grew 10% in the quarter. The bull framing: a company whose demand is steady and whose cost problem is partly policy-driven deserves a higher price than the market paid for most of 2026. The bear framing: on the Q1 call the CEO himself called the sustainability of the endoscopy screening push uncertain, and a stock that fell for most of 2026 while demand held is saying the starting price, not demand, was the problem. Gross margin without the refunds and AST organic growth over the next two quarters are where the two framings get tested.
