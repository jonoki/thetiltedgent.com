01 Company Overview

Steel Dynamics is the third-largest US-listed steel producer by market capitalisation and one of the largest EAF (electric arc furnace — a mill that melts recycled scrap with electricity rather than smelting iron ore in a blast furnace) steelmakers in North America. It runs four reporting segments: steel operations, metals recycling, steel fabrication and, since the start-up of its recycled aluminium flat-rolled mill, aluminium operations. FY2025 revenue was $18.18B, up 3.63% on FY2024's $17.54B, but FY2025 net income fell 22.9% to $1.19B — the trough year of the current cycle. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 is $22.22B, up 26.0% (from $17.63B a year earlier), and TTM net income is $1.96B, up 73.9% (from $1.13B). The company ranks #289 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation at $35.54B.

The central debate on this page is simple to state and hard to answer: where in the cycle are we, and what is the right multiple to pay for it? Steel Dynamics earned EPS of $20.92 in FY2022 and $7.99 in FY2025 — a 61.8% peak-to-trough collapse in three years, on a revenue decline of only 18.3%. That is the arithmetic of a spread business: a mill buys scrap, converts it, and sells steel, and almost all of the swing in profit comes from the gap between the two prices, not from volume. Q3 2026 (EPS $5.26) extended the run — record shipments again, a wider spread again, though the EPS came in just under the company's own $5.34–$5.38 guide — and the stock has responded, up 61.2% over 52 weeks (October 21, 2025 close to October 21, 2026 close). The bull case annualises the last two quarters. The bear case notes that the same annualisation in mid-2022 would have implied an EPS run-rate the company then missed for three consecutive years. Sections 04 and 07 take that apart with the actual numbers rather than the narrative.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
EAF COST POSITION VERTICAL SCRAP SUPPLY DOWNSTREAM FABRICATION NON-UNION LABOUR MODEL LOW LEVERAGE (D/E 0.45) CARBON INTENSITY EDGE GREENFIELD EXECUTION RECORD

The durable advantage is structural cost, and it has three parts that compound. First, the EAF route: melting scrap with electricity has a far lower fixed-cost base than integrated blast-furnace steelmaking, which means the marginal ton can be switched off in a downturn without the fixed-cost bleed that has repeatedly crushed integrated producers — the reason a 61.8% EPS drawdown from 2022 to 2025 still left the company earning $7.99 a share and a 6.52% net margin in its worst year, versus a current steel-industry aggregate profit margin of 3.18%. Second, vertical integration into scrap means the largest input cost is partly internalised, dampening the spread volatility that defines the business. Third, downstream fabrication converts commodity tons into differentiated, longer-cycle products at about 1.7 times the steel segment's per-ton operating profit in Q3 2026 ($476 against $273), and its backlog is up 50% year-over-year. Add a balance sheet carrying debt-to-equity of just 0.45 with a current ratio of 3.14, and this is a cyclical that can fund a multi-billion-dollar greenfield aluminium mill through the trough of its own cycle. That is the moat. It is a cost-and-balance-sheet moat, not a pricing-power moat — and the distinction matters enormously for what multiple it deserves.

Vulnerability 01
Price-taker in a commodity
Steel Dynamics sets no prices. Hot-rolled coil is a globally arbitraged commodity and realised price moved $102/ton in Q3 and $105/ton in Q2. The company's 15.90% (trailing twelve months) gross margin is a residual of two markets it does not control, which is why FY2022's 27.48% gross margin was not a capability but a moment.
Vulnerability 02
Trade-policy dependence
The Q3 release credits the strong domestic market partly to historically low import levels, and the company's outlook assumes a supportive trade environment. Section 232 steel and aluminium tariffs are a policy variable, not a moat: they can be narrowed, exempted or litigated. A material softening would shrink the premium US steel sells for over imports, and that comes straight out of the metal spread.
Vulnerability 03
Aluminium ramp execution
The aluminium segment still lost $26M in Q3, after a hot-strip-mill coolant problem held volumes below plan for several weeks, and it carries the $16M impairment and $10–20M of extra relocation capex from Q2. The CFO said on the call that positive aluminium operating income in the second half is unlikely, with profit expected from Q1 2027. Greenfield mills slip: in April 2022 the company cut its new Sinton, Texas steel mill's first-year shipment goal from 2 million tons to just over 1.5 million (Steel Market Update).
Vulnerability 04
End-market concentration in construction
Fabrication's 50% backlog growth is tied to construction demand the release lists as commercial, manufacturing, warehouse, data-center and healthcare projects. Non-residential construction is interest-rate sensitive and lumpy; a pause in hyperscaler capex would hit the highest-margin-per-ton segment first and with a lag long enough to surprise.

RSI compares the size of recent monthly gains to recent monthly losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has been rising faster than it usually does and is described as "overbought"; below 30 the reverse, "oversold". It measures momentum, not value — it says nothing about what the company is worth, only how stretched the recent move has been.

Over the five years from the November 2021 close of $59.80 to $247.97 on October 21, 2026, Steel Dynamics returned +314.7% on price alone — roughly a 4.1× move, before dividends. That number is real and it is also a trap, because the path bears almost no resemblance to a compounding line. From March 2024 through September 2025 — nineteen month-end closes — the stock never closed outside $114.07–$148.23, while earnings fell from $9.84 (FY2024) to $7.99 (FY2025). Most of the five-year gain came in two stretches: January 2022 ($55.52) to February 2023 ($126.11), the post-pandemic steel-price spike, and September 2025 ($139.43) to now. This is what owning a spread business looks like — long flat stretches punctuated by violent repricings when the spread moves.

Momentum is elevated but cooler than in the spring. The 6-period monthly RSI reads about 65 (it was 56 at the September month-end), having printed 88.7 in May 2026 at the $260.15 month-end close — the most stretched reading in the five-year window; the pullback to $229.46 in June was the correction. The $247.97 close sits above the 3-month moving average ($234.07) and the 10-month average ($222.44), the trend line that has defined the up-move since late 2025: every month-end close since August 2025 has been above it. A monthly close below it would be the first technical evidence that the cycle has turned; that has not happened yet.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The metal spread keeps widening — realised price up $102/ton in Q3 while scrap was $8/ton cheaper — while shipments hit a record 3.8 million tons, and the highest-margin downstream segment carries a backlog 50% above last year's that runs into Q2 2027. The aluminium mill, losing $26M a quarter (about $104M annualised), reached EBITDA breakeven in Q3, and the CFO expects the segment to turn positive at the operating line from Q1 2027, turning a loss centre into an earnings contributor. At $247.97 the stock trades on 14.5× consensus 2026 EPS of $17.11, with a 0.45 debt-to-equity ratio, $2.4B of liquidity and $276M of buyback in Q3. Net margin of 8.82% against 3.18% for the industry says the EAF cost advantage is structural, not cyclical.

Bear Case

This is a price-taker whose EPS fell 61.8% — from $20.92 to $7.99 — in three years on an 18.3% revenue decline, and the market is being asked to capitalise the best quarters of the recovery. The 18.3× trailing multiple is not cheap on mid-cycle earnings: the 2021–25 average EPS of $13.79 implies 18.0×, and trough EPS ($7.99) implies about 31×. Management's constructive outlook rests on a supportive trade environment and historically low imports — policy variables that can reverse. The aluminium ramp has already produced a $16M impairment, $10–20M of cost inflation and a Q3 yield problem, and greenfield mills slip; Q3 EPS of $5.26 also came in 1.9% below the midpoint of the company's own $5.34–$5.38 guide. Buying a cyclical on a low P/E at peak margins is the oldest mistake in the sector.

01
Metal spread compression. The entire earnings recovery is one variable: the gap between realised steel price and scrap cost, which widened roughly $110/ton sequentially in Q3. Applied to 3.8 million tons, each $50/ton of spread compression is worth roughly $190M of quarterly operating income — about $1.32 a share a quarter before tax, or $5.28 a year (our calculation: 3.784M tons × $50 ÷ 143.33M shares).
02
Aluminium ramp slippage. A segment losing $26M a quarter, already carrying a $16M impairment and a Q3 yield problem. The CFO called positive operating income in the second half of 2026 unlikely (per the call transcript) and the company points to Q1 2027. Every quarter of delay is roughly $26M of foregone operating income plus the credibility cost.
03
Trade policy and construction demand together. The bull case needs Section 232 protection to hold and non-residential construction — particularly data centres and reshored manufacturing — to keep absorbing tons. Those two supports are more correlated than they look: both depend on the same policy and capital-spending environment, so they are likely to weaken together rather than offset each other.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The spread is the business
Q3 2026: realised price $1,400/ton, ferrous scrap $404/ton, implied simple spread roughly $996/ton against roughly $886 in Q2. That $110 sequential widening came with a 43% jump in steel segment operating income on 3.8 million tons against 3.7 million. Run the arithmetic backwards and the same sensitivity destroys earnings: FY2022 gross margin of 27.48% became FY2025's 13.16%, and EPS went from $20.92 to $7.99. No amount of operational excellence changes the fact that two prices the company does not set determine most of its profit.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Policy-dependent pricing
In the Q3 release management credits the strong domestic market to steady demand, low customer inventories and historically low import levels, and says it remains optimistic about the domestic steel and aluminum industries for 2027 and beyond, citing infrastructure, manufacturing, energy and data-center investment. Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminium underpin the domestic price premium that the metal spread depends on, so a change in them would reach earnings directly.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Reshoring and data-centre steel
Steel fabrication backlog 50% above a year ago and extending into Q2 2027 is the cleanest forward indicator in the release. Joists and decking go into warehouses, factories, data-center shells and healthcare buildings, and fabrication earned roughly $476 of operating income per ton in Q3 against $273 for steel operations. This is real, it is visible, and it is the part of the demand picture least tied to the spot steel price.
BULL VS BEAR — THE FRAME
What are you actually paying for?
At $247.97 you pay 18.3× trailing and 14.5× consensus 2026 — and 18.0× the 2021–25 average EPS of $13.79. The bull is buying a mid-cycle multiple on a business whose structural cost position is demonstrably better than the industry's, with aluminium due to turn profitable at the operating line from 2027. The bear is buying 18× normalised earnings in a price-taking commodity with a 1.49 beta, five months after the May month-end peak, on policy-supported prices. Both are looking at the same numbers. The difference is entirely the denominator each one believes in — and that, not the tape, is the decision this report is asking you to make.
