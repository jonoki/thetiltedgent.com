01 Company Overview

Steel Dynamics is the third-largest US-listed steel producer by market capitalisation and one of the largest EAF (electric arc furnace — a mill that melts recycled scrap with electricity rather than smelting iron ore in a blast furnace) steelmakers in North America. It runs four reporting segments: steel operations, metals recycling, steel fabrication and, since the start-up of its recycled aluminium flat-rolled mill, aluminium operations. FY2025 revenue was $18.18B, up 3.63% on FY2024's $17.54B, but FY2025 net income fell 22.9% to $1.19B — the trough year of the current cycle. Trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 2026 is $20.54B, up 19.8%, and TTM net income is $1.61B, up 54.4%. The company ranks roughly #735 globally by market capitalisation at $33.98B.

The central debate on this page is simple to state and hard to answer: where in the cycle are we, and what is the right multiple to pay for it? Steel Dynamics earned EPS of $20.92 in FY2022 and $7.99 in FY2025 — a 61.8% peak-to-trough collapse in three years, on a revenue decline of only 18.3%. That is the arithmetic of a spread business: a mill buys scrap, converts it, and sells steel, and almost all of the operating leverage sits in the gap between the two prices, not in volume. Q2 2026 delivered record shipments and a sharply wider spread, and the stock has responded — up 80.5% over 52 weeks. The bull case annualises Q2. The bear case notes that the same annualisation in mid-2022 would have implied an EPS run-rate the company then missed for three consecutive years. Sections 04 and 07 take that apart with the actual numbers rather than the narrative.

This is where the entire investment case lives, and it is a spread business: Q2 2026 average realised selling price was $1,298 per ton, up $105 sequentially, while average ferrous scrap cost was $412 per ton, up $16 sequentially. 03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
EAF COST POSITION VERTICAL SCRAP SUPPLY DOWNSTREAM FABRICATION NON-UNION LABOUR MODEL LOW LEVERAGE (D/E 0.47) CARBON INTENSITY EDGE GREENFIELD EXECUTION RECORD

The durable advantage is structural cost, and it has three parts that compound. First, the EAF route: melting scrap with electricity has a far lower fixed-cost base than integrated blast-furnace steelmaking, which means the marginal ton can be switched off in a downturn without the fixed-cost bleed that has repeatedly crushed integrated producers — the reason a 61.8% EPS drawdown from 2022 to 2025 still left the company earning $7.99 a share and a 6.52% net margin in its worst year, versus an industry aggregate profit margin of 2.95%. Second, vertical integration into scrap means the largest input cost is partly internalised, dampening the spread volatility that defines the business. Third, downstream fabrication converts commodity tons into differentiated, longer-cycle products at roughly ten times the per-ton operating profit, and its backlog is up 45% year-over-year. Add a balance sheet carrying debt-to-equity of just 0.47 with a current ratio of 3.18, and this is a cyclical that can fund a multi-billion-dollar greenfield aluminium mill through the trough of its own cycle. That is the moat. It is a cost-and-balance-sheet moat, not a pricing-power moat — and the distinction matters enormously for what multiple it deserves.

Vulnerability 01
Price-taker in a commodity
Steel Dynamics sets no prices. Hot-rolled coil is a globally arbitraged commodity and realised price moved $105/ton in a single quarter. The company's 14.65% gross margin is a residual of two markets it does not control, which is why FY2022's 27.48% gross margin was not a capability but a moment.
Vulnerability 02
Trade-policy dependence
Management's own outlook cites "domestic trade actions" as a pillar of its constructive view. Section 232 steel and aluminium tariffs are a policy variable, not a moat: they can be narrowed, exempted or litigated. A material softening would compress domestic premia directly into the metal spread.
Vulnerability 03
Aluminium ramp execution
The aluminium segment is still losing $33M a quarter and has already taken a $16M impairment plus $10–20M of incremental capex on a facility relocation. Full second-cast-house utilisation is guided to H1 2027. Greenfield mills historically slip; Sinton itself took longer than planned to reach run-rate.
Vulnerability 04
End-market concentration in construction
Fabrication's 45% backlog growth is largely a data-centre and reshoring story. Non-residential construction is interest-rate sensitive and lumpy; a pause in hyperscaler capex would hit the highest-margin-per-ton segment first and with a lag long enough to surprise.

RSI compares the size of recent monthly gains to recent monthly losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 the stock has been rising faster than it usually does and is described as "overbought"; below 30 the reverse, "oversold". It measures momentum, not value — it says nothing about what the company is worth, only how stretched the recent move has been.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $66.08 to $237.08 on September 10, 2026, Steel Dynamics returned +258.8% on price alone — roughly a 3.6× move, before dividends. That number is real and it is also a trap, because the path bears almost no resemblance to a compounding line. The stock traded sideways-to-down for the whole of 2024 and the first three quarters of 2025, spending fifteen months oscillating between roughly $114 and $148 while earnings fell from $9.84 to $7.99 a share. Essentially the entire five-year return was generated in two discrete bursts: the 2022–23 post-pandemic steel-price spike, and the eleven months from October 2025 to the present. Between those bursts, nothing. This is what owning a spread business looks like — long flat stretches punctuated by violent repricings when the spread moves.

Momentum is currently elevated but not extreme. The 6-period monthly RSI reads about 63, having printed 88.7 in May 2026 at the $260.15 monthly high — that May reading was as stretched as the indicator has been in five years, and the subsequent June pullback to $229.46 was the correction. Price sits above the 3-month moving average ($240 is marginally above, so the two are effectively converged) and comfortably above the 10-month at roughly $216, which is the trend line that has defined the up-move since late 2025. A monthly close below that 10-month average would be the first technical evidence that the cycle has turned; it has not happened yet.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The metal spread is widening decisively — realised price up $105/ton against $16/ton of scrap inflation — while shipments hit a record 3.7 million tons, and the highest-margin downstream segment carries a backlog up 45% year-over-year. The aluminium mill stops being a roughly $130M annualised drag when the second cast house reaches full utilisation in H1 2027, converting a loss centre into an earnings contributor without further capital. At $237.08 the stock trades on 14.1× consensus 2026 EPS and 12.5× 2027, with a 0.47 debt-to-equity ratio, $2.0B of liquidity and $200M a quarter of buyback. Net margin of 7.83% against a 2.95% industry aggregate says the EAF cost advantage is structural, not cyclical.

Bear Case

This is a price-taker whose EPS fell 61.8% — from $20.92 to $7.99 — in three years on an 18.3% revenue decline, and the market is being asked to capitalise the best quarter of the recovery. The 21.5× trailing multiple is not cheap on mid-cycle earnings: the 2021–25 average EPS of $13.79 implies 17.2×, and true trough EPS implies close to 30×. Management's own constructive outlook rests explicitly on "domestic trade actions" — a policy variable that can reverse. The aluminium ramp has already produced a $16M impairment and $10–20M of cost inflation, and greenfield mills slip. Buying a cyclical at a low P/E on peak margins is the oldest mistake in the sector.

01
Metal spread compression. The entire earnings recovery is one variable: the gap between realised steel price and scrap cost, which widened roughly $89/ton sequentially in Q2. Applied to 3.7 million tons, each $50/ton of spread compression is worth roughly $185M of quarterly operating income — around $1.00 of annualised EPS. Steel pricing has historically reversed faster than it rose.
02
Aluminium ramp slippage. A segment losing $33M a quarter with a guided H1 2027 inflection, already carrying a $16M impairment and $10–20M of incremental capex. Every quarter of delay is roughly $33M of foregone operating income plus the credibility cost — and analyst 2027 estimates of $18.95 EPS almost certainly assume the ramp lands on schedule.
03
Trade policy and construction demand together. The bull case needs Section 232 protection to hold and non-residential construction — particularly data centres and reshored manufacturing — to keep absorbing tons. Those two supports are more correlated than they look: both depend on the same policy and capital-spending environment, so they are likely to weaken together rather than offset each other.
Industry Commentary
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The spread is the business
Q2 2026: realised price $1,298/ton, ferrous scrap $412/ton, implied simple spread roughly $886/ton against roughly $797 in Q1. That $89 sequential widening drove a 30% jump in steel segment operating income on essentially the same tonnage. Run the arithmetic backwards and the same sensitivity destroys earnings: FY2022 gross margin of 27.48% became FY2025's 13.16%, and EPS went from $20.92 to $7.99. No amount of operational excellence changes the fact that two prices the company does not set determine most of its profit.
ONGOING HEADWIND
Policy-dependent pricing
Management's outlook for "strong domestic steel and aluminium consumption through the remainder of 2026 and into 2027" is attributed to three things: domestic trade actions, manufacturing reshoring and infrastructure funding. Two of those three are government policy. Section 232 tariffs on steel and aluminium underpin the domestic price premium that the metal spread depends on — and the specific rates, exemptions and pending proceedings were not verified from sources fetched this session, so nothing further is asserted here. What can be said is that the company itself names trade policy as load-bearing.
EMERGING TAILWIND
Reshoring and data-centre steel
Steel fabrication backlog up nearly 45% year-over-year is the cleanest forward indicator in the release. Joists and decking go into warehouses, factories and data-centre shells, and that backlog converts over the following two to four quarters at roughly $528 of operating income per ton — far above the commodity steel segment. This is real, it is visible, and it is the part of the demand picture least dependent on the spot steel price.
BULL VS BEAR — THE FRAME
What are you actually paying for?
At $237.08 you pay 21.5× trailing, 14.1× consensus 2026, 12.5× consensus 2027 — and 17.2× the 2021–25 average EPS of $13.79. The bull is buying a mid-cycle multiple on a business whose structural cost position is demonstrably better than the industry's, with a free option on aluminium from H1 2027. The bear is buying 17× normalised earnings in a price-taking commodity with a 1.52 beta, four months after a momentum peak, on policy-supported prices. Both are looking at the same numbers. The difference is entirely the denominator each one believes in — and that, not the tape, is the decision this report is asking you to make.
