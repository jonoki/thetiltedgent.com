01 Company Overview

Constellation Brands is, in practice, an American importer and marketer of Mexican beer with a shrinking wine and spirits business attached. It sells Corona, Modelo, Pacifico and Victoria in the US, and wine and spirits under labels such as Robert Mondavi Winery, Kim Crawford, Casa Noble and High West. In the quarter to August 31, 2026, beer was $2,473.6M of $2,633.0M in net sales — 93.9% — and it produced virtually all of the profit, at a 39.0% beer operating margin against 3.8% in wine & spirits.

The story of the last five years is a winning hand played into a changing table. Under former CEO Bill Newlands (CEO from 2019), Modelo became the number-one selling beer in the US and the company sold its mainstream wine brands — Woodbridge, Meiomi, Robert Mondavi Private Selection, Cook's, SIMI and J. Rogét — to The Wine Group, closing June 2, 2025, to keep only wines "predominantly priced $15 and above". Then the beer engine stalled. Fiscal 2026 (year to February 28, 2026) beer depletions fell 2.1%, beer operating margin slipped to 38.0%, and group revenue fell 10.48% to $9,139M (most of that the divestiture). The company itself has said high-end beer purchase rates fell harder among Hispanic consumers than in the general market — an outsized problem for a portfolio built on Mexican brands. The stock went from a $272.80 month-end close in July 2023 to $122.64 on October 9, 2026, down 55.0%, and sits 10.9% above its 52-week intraday low of $110.60, set on October 5, 2026.

The central question is simple to state: is this a cyclical and demographic squeeze on a still-dominant beer franchise — 39% operating margins, share gains, $1.8B of free cash flow — or the start of a structural decline in its two biggest brands, with Modelo Especial and Corona Extra depletions still negative while the growth comes from smaller labels? At 11.0x trailing earnings, the market is still pricing the second answer.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

US rights to Corona & Modelo Owned Mexican brewing supply Brand portfolio breadth (Pacifico, Victoria) Distributor scale 39% beer operating margin $1.8B annual free cash flow

The moat is brand plus supply. Imported Mexican lager is a category where Constellation owns the leading labels and the breweries that make them, and it has kept taking dollar share even while volumes fall. That shows in the margin: 39% on beer is a luxury-goods number for a mass-market drink. The weakness is that a moat protects share, not the size of the market. If the core customer drinks less, a dominant share of a smaller market is still a smaller business.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Concentration in one consumer. The company has disclosed that high-end beer purchase rates fell harder among Hispanic consumers than in the general market, and Food Dive and Yahoo Finance link that to cost-of-living pressure and immigration enforcement. That is a policy and demographic risk the company cannot manage away with marketing.
02
The two biggest brands are shrinking. Modelo Especial (−2%) and Corona Extra (−5%) are still declining in Q2 FY27 depletions. Pacifico and Victoria growing 15–19% on a smaller base cannot offset that indefinitely.
03
Tariffs on the cans. Management has flagged softer demand and margin pressure from aluminum tariffs on the cans it imports from Mexico, as Investing.com reported. A cross-border supply chain is a moat in normal times and a target in a tariff war.
04
A thin liquidity cushion. Cash of $98.5M against total debt of $10.32B, a current ratio of 1.00 and a quick ratio of 0.26. Debt/EBITDA of about 3.0x ($10.32B ÷ $3.43B) is manageable while beer earns 39%; it becomes tighter if EBITDA falls.

Notable One-Off Items

Two things distort the headline numbers. First, the wine divestiture: reported revenue fell 10.48% in FY2026 and wine & spirits fell 47% in Q1 FY2027, but organic wine & spirits growth in Q1 was +8%. Use organic and beer-only figures to judge the trend. Second, the gap between GAAP and comparable EPS swings widely: FY2026 GAAP $9.61 against comparable $11.82, and GAAP losses in FY2022, FY2023 and FY2025. In Q1 FY2027 it ran the other way — reported $3.79 against comparable $3.43 — and in Q2 reported EPS was $3.32 against comparable $3.74. On a comparable basis, FY2027 guidance ($11.20–$11.90) sits at or below FY2026's $11.82, so management is guiding to flat-to-down earnings.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 11.0x trailing and 10.4x forward earnings, an 8.9% free-cash-flow yield and a 3.36% dividend for a beer business that still earns a 39% operating margin and still gains dollar share. The problem brands are shrinking by low single digits, not collapsing, and Pacifico and Victoria are growing double digits. Buybacks retire about 3.3% of the stock a year. A new CEO is aiming the company at costs (a programme targeting more than $200M of net annualized savings by fiscal 2028, per the company's April 2025 release) rather than capacity, so capex should come down. The Street's average target is $151.74, 23.7% above the price, and management said September trends were improving. If the Hispanic consumer steadies, depletions only need to reach zero for a 10x multiple to look like the wrong price.

Bear Case

The two brands that built the company — Modelo Especial and Corona Extra — are both declining, and the core customer is under policy pressure the company cannot control. Management is guiding comparable EPS flat to down ($11.20–$11.90 against $11.82), called August lackluster (September improved, it said on October 7), and the dividend has stopped growing (+0.98%). Aluminum tariffs are squeezing a cross-border supply chain. The balance sheet carries $10.32B of debt against $98.5M of cash. A famously patient shareholder, Berkshire, sold out. Low multiples on shrinking earnings are not cheap, and the Street's targets have been chasing the price down.

Top 3 Risks

01
Core-brand volume decline. Modelo Especial −2% and Corona Extra −5% depletions in Q2 FY2027, with total beer depletions −0.6%. If the first-half shipment build reverses, the second half is at risk.
02
Consumer concentration and policy. Hispanic consumers are the company's disclosed pressure point, and the pressure comes from cost of living and immigration enforcement — neither of which is in management's control.
03
Tariffs and leverage together. Aluminum tariffs compress margin on imported cans while the balance sheet carries about 3.0x debt/EBITDA and a 1.00 current ratio. Each is manageable alone; a sustained margin hit makes the leverage heavier.
Industry Commentary
The imported-beer squeeze
ONGOING HEADWIND

Constellation's own disclosures describe a consumer who is value-conscious and under inflation pressure, with lower-income and Hispanic drinkers pulling back most. Yahoo Finance also points to fuel costs linked to the Iran conflict. The company keeps gaining share (the #1 dollar share gainer in Circana's US tracked channels in Q1, per the company), which shows the brands are winning inside a category that is shrinking for them. Share gains like these are what a dominant franchise looks like in a downturn. They are also what a franchise looks like when it is cutting deals to hold its place. The Q2 report (June–August) showed depletions down 0.6% with the company the #1 dollar share gainer in beverage alcohol, per management.

Portfolio reshaping — the wine exit
EMERGING TAILWIND

Selling Woodbridge, Meiomi and the other mainstream wine brands to The Wine Group removed about half of wine & spirits revenue and left a smaller premium business that is growing organically (+8% in Q1). It has not yet made money (−$1.1M operating result in Q1). The bull framing: management time and capital now go almost entirely to beer, and the remaining wine is priced where the category is still healthy. The bear framing: the diversification is gone. Constellation is now a single-category, single-origin, largely single-demographic business, and it is valued like one.
