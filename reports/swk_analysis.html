01 Company Overview

Stanley Black & Decker is the toolbox brand portfolio of the American job site — DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, STANLEY, BLACK+DECKER and Cub Cadet — selling power tools, hand tools, storage, outdoor equipment and engineered fasteners to tradespeople, builders and DIYers. The company dates to 1843, when Frederick Trent Stanley founded Stanley's Bolt Manufactory in New Britain, Connecticut; today's company is the product of the March 12, 2010 merger of Stanley Works with Black & Decker. FY2025 (fiscal year ended January 3, 2026) revenue was $15.13B, down 1.53%, with GAAP EPS of $2.65. At the September 21, 2026 close of $88.68 the equity is worth about $13.39B (our calculation: $88.68 × 151.02M shares).

This is a story about a company shrinking itself back to its core. Five years ago SWK closed September 2021 at $175.31 and December 2021 at $188.62; by April 2025 it closed at $60.02, a 68% fall in month-end terms. In between came a revenue decline in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025, a GAAP loss in FY2023, and in April 2025 a tariff shock on a business that, per The Motley Fool, sources $0.9B to $1.0B from China. The response has been subtraction: the electronic security business was sold to Securitas, the oil & gas unit went in 2022, the aerospace fastener business (CAM) closed in April 2026, and on September 4, 2026 the company agreed to sell Excel Industries, maker of Hustler mowers. What is left is roughly 90% Tools & Outdoor. The stock has recovered to $88.68 — up 47.8% from the April 2025 month-end low — on margin repair, debt paydown and one-off tariff refunds. The debate is whether that recovery is a new earnings base or a flattering quarter.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

DEWALT pro brand CRAFTSMAN / STANLEY heritage Battery-platform lock-in Big-box retail shelf space Global manufacturing footprint 149-year dividend record

The moat is the brand on the job site. A contractor who owns a bag of batteries for one cordless platform buys the next tool on the same platform — switching means buying the batteries again. DEWALT is the pro brand; CRAFTSMAN, STANLEY and BLACK+DECKER cover the garage. What the moat does not protect is cost: per The Motley Fool, SWK imports $0.9B to $1.0B from China and $1.2B to $1.3B from Mexico (only a third of the latter tariff-exempt under USMCA rules), and when tariffs moved in April 2025 the stock fell 21.9% in a month as management cut its 2025 adjusted EPS midpoint to $4.50 from $5.25, per The Motley Fool. Brand protects the shelf space; it does not protect the margin.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Trade policy sets the gross margin. In 2025 tariffs cost margin; in Q2 2026 tariff refunds added ~250bps back. Either way, a policy decision outside management's control is moving the most important line in the income statement. As summarised by Moby Intelligence, management left any second-half refunds out of guidance because of uncertainty.
02
Revenue has not grown. FY2023 −6.88%, FY2024 −2.63%, FY2025 −1.53%; TTM +0.57%. Margin repair can lift earnings for a while, but a tools company whose revenue does not grow is relying on cost cuts and buybacks for per-share progress.
03
Housing and DIY cyclicality. Tools & Outdoor is 90% of sales and tied to repair, remodel and construction activity. Q2 showed the split: power tools strong, outdoor down on weather and lighter retailer restocking.
04
The dividend is expensive to protect. The payout ratio is about 82% of TTM GAAP earnings (stockanalysis.com), and those earnings include a one-off CAM gain. The FY2026 free cash flow guide of $600–$800M compares with ~$508M a year of dividends ($3.36 × 151.02M shares, our calculation) — covered, but without much room.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The clean-up is mostly done. CAM is sold, $1.7B of debt is gone, Excel is next, and what remains is the DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN and STANLEY franchise. Gross margin has gone from 24.92% in FY2023 to 32.21% TTM; management is targeting 34–35% for the second half (as summarised by Moby Intelligence). At $88.68 you pay 15.8x consensus FY2026 EPS and collect a 3.79% yield from a company with 58 straight years of dividend increases. If the adjusted EPS guide of $5.20–$5.80 holds and FY2027 consensus of $6.38 is reached, the stock is not expensive, and every analyst target sits above the price.

Bear Case

Revenue fell three years running and TTM growth is 0.57%. The best quarter in years leaned on tariff refunds (~250bps of margin) and a one-off $273.7M gain; strip those out and the trailing P/E of 21.7 is doing less work than it seems. The payout ratio is about 82%, FY2026 free cash flow guidance is $600–$800M against ~$508M of annual dividends, and a new $1B U.S. investment program competes for the same cash. ROIC of 6.5% is low for a business at this stage of a turnaround, and 11 of 15 analysts still say Hold.

Top 3 Risks

01
Tariff and trade policy. April 2025 showed the downside: −21.9% in a month and a $0.75 EPS hit. The 2026 refunds show the upside, and they are not in guidance beyond Q2. Either way, trade policy sets the margin more than management does.
02
Flat revenue. Without organic growth, per-share progress depends on margin repair and buybacks, both of which have limits. Q2's +3% organic is a start; outdoor was down 7% in the same quarter (as summarised by Moby Intelligence).
03
Dividend strain. An 82% payout ratio and modest free cash flow guidance leave little room if housing and DIY demand weakens. The 58-year growth streak is part of why many shareholders own SWK — so the dividend is hard to cut, and hard to cut is a risk too.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs and the global tool supply chain
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

When U.S. tariffs rose in April 2025, SWK's exposure was concrete: per The Motley Fool, $0.9B to $1.0B of sourcing from China and $1.2B to $1.3B from Mexico, only a third of which was tariff-exempt under USMCA rules. The company raised prices and cut guidance; as the same article put it, "It will take time to shift sourcing away from those countries and increase USMCA-compliant imports from Mexico." In 2026 the flow reversed: tariff refunds added roughly $0.17 per share in Q2. The August 2026 $1B U.S. investment program is the long answer: more domestic manufacturing means less exposure to whatever trade policy does next.

Pro vs DIY demand and the housing cycle
ONGOING HEADWIND

Tool demand follows repair, remodel and construction activity. Q2 2026 showed power tools strong in U.S. retail and commercial & industrial channels, while outdoor products fell on weather and lighter retailer restocking (as summarised by Moby Intelligence). The skilled-trades shortage the company cites in its $1B release is both a headwind (fewer pros) and a reason pros pay up for productivity tools.

Simplification — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull framing: selling CAM and Excel turns SWK into a focused tools company with a repaired balance sheet, a gross margin back above 32% and a CEO who ran the tools business before he ran the company. The bear framing: each sale shrinks revenue, and a company that has sold its security, oil & gas, aerospace and professional-mower businesses has fewer places left to find growth. The way to judge it is organic growth in Tools & Outdoor and a gross margin that holds without refunds. Q3 2026 is the first clean read on both.
