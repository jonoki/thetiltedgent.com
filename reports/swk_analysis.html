01 Company Overview

Stanley Black & Decker is the toolbox brand portfolio of the American job site — DEWALT, CRAFTSMAN, STANLEY, BLACK+DECKER and Cub Cadet — selling power tools, hand tools, storage, outdoor equipment and engineered fasteners to tradespeople, builders and DIYers. The company dates to 1843, when Frederick Trent Stanley founded Stanley's Bolt Manufactory in New Britain, Connecticut; today's company is the product of the March 12, 2010 merger of Stanley Works with Black & Decker. FY2025 (fiscal year ended January 3, 2026) revenue was $15.13B, down 1.53%, with GAAP EPS of $2.65. At the November 6, 2026 close of $114.85 the equity is worth about $17.08B (our calculation: $114.85 × 148.70M shares outstanding on October 29, 2026, from the Q3 10-Q cover).

This is a story about a company shrinking itself back to its core. SWK closed November 2021 at $174.76 and December 2021 at $188.62; by April 2025 it closed at $60.02, a 68% fall from that December peak in month-end terms. In between came a revenue decline in each of FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025, a GAAP loss in FY2023, and in April 2025 a tariff shock on a business that, per The Motley Fool, sources $0.9B to $1.0B from China. The response has been subtraction: the electronic security business was sold to Securitas, the oil & gas unit went in 2022, the aerospace fastener business (CAM) closed in April 2026, and on September 4, 2026 the company agreed to sell Excel Industries, maker of Hustler mowers, a sale management expects to close around year-end. What is left is roughly 89% Tools & Outdoor.

Third-quarter results on November 4, 2026 sent the stock up 13.1% that day ($104.62 to $118.33, close to close). Adjusted earnings per share were $2.31 against a Nasdaq consensus of $1.54, and about $0.67 of that was refunds of tariffs charged under the IEEPA emergency-powers law, after the costs and taxes tied to them. Sales fell 1% as reported and rose 4% organically (leaving out businesses sold, a product-line change and currency). At $114.85 the stock is up 91.4% from the April 2025 month-end low. The debate has moved: margins held up even without the refunds, but CEO Chris Nelson says the company has now received "the vast majority of what we have applied for", and the CFO expects meaningful cost inflation to deal with in 2027.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

DEWALT pro brand CRAFTSMAN / STANLEY heritage Battery-platform lock-in Big-box retail shelf space Global manufacturing footprint 150-year dividend record

The moat is the brand on the job site. A contractor who owns a bag of batteries for one cordless platform buys the next tool on the same platform — switching means buying the batteries again. DEWALT is the pro brand; CRAFTSMAN, STANLEY and BLACK+DECKER cover the garage. What the moat does not protect is cost: per The Motley Fool, SWK imports $0.9B to $1.0B from China and $1.2B to $1.3B from Mexico (only a third of the latter tariff-exempt under USMCA rules), and when tariffs moved in April 2025 the stock fell 21.9% in a month as management cut its 2025 adjusted EPS midpoint to $4.50 from $5.25, per The Motley Fool. Q3 2026 gave the brand case some support: DEWALT, STANLEY and CRAFTSMAN each grew organically, per Nelson. Brand protects the shelf space; it does not protect the margin.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Trade policy sets the gross margin. In 2025 tariffs cost margin; in Q3 2026 net tariff refunds added about 4.3 percentage points back. Either way, a policy decision outside management's control is moving the most important line in the income statement. Management has now received most of the refunds it applied for, left any Q4 refunds out of guidance, and assumes tariffs return to their former level very late in the year.
02
Revenue has barely grown. FY2023 −6.88%, FY2024 −2.63%, FY2025 −1.53%; trailing twelve months +0.38% (our calculation from the company's releases). Q3's 4% organic growth came with prices down 3 points in Tools & Outdoor, and management expects "roughly a flat to down market" this year. Margin repair can lift earnings for a while, but a tools company whose revenue does not grow is relying on cost cuts and buybacks for per-share progress.
03
Housing and DIY cyclicality. Tools & Outdoor is 89% of sales and tied to repair, remodel and construction activity. Q3 showed the split: power tools up 10% organically, outdoor down 12%, while weak automotive demand and rising material costs mean management now expects a flat full-year margin in Engineered Fastening.
04
Inflation in 2027. CFO Hallinan: "Whether or not tariff levels rise to the former IEEPA levels, we will have meaningful inflation to address in 2027." Steel and electrical components are the pressure points. The plan is productivity gains and a price increase likely around the turn of the year, which has to stick in a market management expects to be flat to down.
05
Several claims on the same cash. The payout ratio is about 60% of trailing GAAP earnings (stockanalysis.com), and those earnings include a one-off CAM gain and tariff refunds. The FY2026 free cash flow guide of $1.0–$1.2B comfortably covers about $500M a year of dividends ($3.36 × 148.70M shares, our calculation), but that guide includes refunds that do not repeat, and a $1B buyback authorization and a $1B U.S. investment program draw on the same money.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items distort the 2026 numbers. First, the $273.7M gain on businesses sold, largely the CAM sale and booked in Q2 2026, lifts GAAP earnings for the trailing twelve months and makes the trailing P/E look lower than the business alone would justify. Second, IEEPA tariff refunds: the Q3 10-Q shows pre-tax gains of $180.7M in the quarter and $298.7M over nine months, booked in cost of sales, partly offset by $48.2M and $131.6M of related incentive pay and growth spending. After those costs and taxes the refunds added about $0.67 to Q3 EPS, and the full-year guide includes about $0.60 because roughly $0.25 of related costs and taxes land in Q4. Q3 also carried $89.3M of pre-tax costs the company leaves out of adjusted earnings, mainly restructuring, a $19.7M non-cash asset impairment and the costs of changing its factory network. In writing Excel's assets down to their expected sale value, the company counted $53.7M of goodwill moved to assets held for sale.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The clean-up is nearly done. CAM is sold, debt is down, Excel is due to go around year-end, and Nelson says the company is at "the end of the major transformational cycle". Gross margin has gone from 24.92% in FY2023 to 33.75% over the last twelve months, and Q3's adjusted margin was about 35.1% even without tariff refunds; management expects 34% to 35% in Q4 and targets above 35%, towards 37%, in the long run. Organic sales grew 4% in Q3, led by power tools and by contractors' channels. Free cash flow was $836M in nine months, and there is a new $1B buyback authorization. At $114.85 you pay 19.3 times consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS and collect a 2.93% yield from a company with 59 straight years of dividend increases; if FY2027 consensus of $6.53 is reached, the stock is at 17.6 times.

Bear Case

Revenue fell three years running and trailing growth is 0.38%. The best quarter in years leaned on about $0.67 of net tariff refunds, and management says it has already collected most of them. Take the $0.60 of net refunds out of the guidance midpoint of $6.10 and the stock is at about 20.9 times $5.50 of FY2026 adjusted EPS (our calculation), and the trailing P/E of 20.6 also rests on a one-off $273.7M gain. Steel and electrical costs are rising into 2027, and the price increase meant to cover them has to land in a market management expects to be flat to down. ROIC of 6.24% is low for a business at this stage of a turnaround, 11 of 15 analysts still say Hold, and the stock closed above their average target.

Top 3 Risks

01
Tariff and trade policy. April 2025 showed the downside: −21.9% in a month and a $0.75 EPS hit. The 2026 refunds show the upside, but they are mostly collected and none are assumed in Q4 guidance, while management expects tariffs to return to their former level. Either way, trade policy sets the margin more than management does.

Flat revenue and rising costs. Full-year revenue is guided about flat. Without organic growth, per-share progress depends on margin repair and buybacks, both of which have limits. Q3's +4% organic is a start, but outdoor was down 12% and prices down 3 points in Tools & Outdoor, and a price increase is planned into 2027 inflation.
03
Refund-flattered cash. A 60% payout ratio and the $1.0–$1.2B free cash flow guide look comfortable for 2026, but both include money that will not recur. The 59-year growth streak is part of why many shareholders own SWK — so the dividend is hard to cut, and hard to cut is a risk too if housing and DIY demand weakens.
Industry Commentary
Tariffs and the global tool supply chain
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

When U.S. tariffs rose in April 2025, SWK's exposure was concrete: per The Motley Fool, $0.9B to $1.0B of sourcing from China and $1.2B to $1.3B from Mexico, only a third of which was tariff-exempt under USMCA rules. The company raised prices and cut guidance; as the same article put it, "It will take time to shift sourcing away from those countries and increase USMCA-compliant imports from Mexico." In 2026 the flow reversed: net tariff refunds added roughly $0.17 per share in Q2 and about $0.67 in Q3. Management says lower tariffs since late February are still largely offsetting cost inflation, and assumes tariffs go back to their former level very late in the year. The August 2026 $1B U.S. investment program is the long answer: more domestic manufacturing means less exposure to whatever trade policy does next.

Pro vs DIY demand and the housing cycle
ONGOING HEADWIND

Tool demand follows repair, remodel and construction activity. Q3 2026 showed professional demand holding up better than the consumer's: the US commercial and industrial channel rose by a mid-teens percentage and US retail by a mid-single-digit percentage, while outdoor products fell 12%. Management expects the overall tools market to be flat to down this year and is counting on winning share. The skilled-trades shortage the company cites in its $1B release is both a headwind (fewer pros) and a reason pros pay up for productivity tools.

Simplification — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

The bull framing: selling CAM and Excel turns SWK into a focused tools company with a repaired balance sheet, a gross margin back near 34% over the last twelve months and a CEO who ran the tools business before he ran the company. The bear framing: each sale shrinks revenue, and a company that has sold its security, oil & gas and aerospace businesses, and agreed to sell its professional-mower business, has fewer places left to find growth. The way to judge it is organic growth in Tools & Outdoor and a gross margin that holds without refunds. Q3 2026 was a good first read on both: 4% organic growth in the segment and an adjusted gross margin of about 35.1% without refunds. Nelson says there is nothing further on the docket beyond smaller pruning.
