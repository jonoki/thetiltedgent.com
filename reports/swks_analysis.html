01 Company Overview

Identity first. SWKS is Skyworks Solutions, the Irvine, California maker of RF, analog and mixed-signal chips, formed in 2002 when Alpha Industries merged with Conexant's wireless division. It has been an S&P 500 member since March 12, 2015 and still is; it left the NASDAQ-100 on December 19, 2022. The ticker has not changed. What has changed is the company behind it: on October 5, 2026 Skyworks completed its purchase of Qorvo, its closest US rival, paying $32.50 in cash plus 0.960 Skyworks shares for each Qorvo share. Skyworks kept its name and ticker; its existing holders own about 63% of the combined company and Qorvo's former holders about 37%, on a fully diluted basis. China's SAMR cleared the deal without conditions between September 21 and October 4 (MLex, Oct 15, 2026).

Here is the hand. Skyworks spent four years folding: revenue fell from $5.49B in FY2022 to $3.96B in FY2026, EPS on the official (GAAP) accounting from $7.81 to $1.14, and gross margin from 47.5% to 40.8%, as its largest customer began buying from two suppliers instead of one (dual-sourcing) and the value of its chips in each fall-2025 iPhone was guided down 20–25%. The share price followed, from a $155.14 month-end in December 2021 to $53.55 in March 2026. Management's answer was to buy the other big stack at the table, and that is now done. The first results since are the last that Skyworks will report alone: the quarter to October 2 came in a penny above consensus on adjusted earnings (which leave out stock pay, deal costs and similar items), and the stock fell 2.90% the next session. It closed at $82.19, up 53.5% from the March low and down 7.4% from the last edition of this report. The dividend went on July 28, redirecting the cash to buybacks, debt reduction and acquisitions. The stock is now a bet on one thing: whether $500 million or more of promised annual cost savings turn two phone-chip businesses into one that earns its price.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

RF module integration know-how Incumbent flagship-phone sockets Owned manufacturing footprint Long-lived auto & industrial design wins R&D scale ($785.5M in FY2025) Scale from Qorvo (closed Oct 2026)

RF front-ends are hard to make well: dozens of bands, filters that must separate them within fractions of a percent, amplifiers that have to be efficient enough not to cook a battery, all packed into a module the size of a fingernail. That is a moat against new entrants — which is why the field has been down to a handful of serious players for years. But it is a moat without pricing power against the customers that matter. A handful of phone makers buy most of the output, dual-source whenever they can, and redesign every year. The margin history is the evidence: gross margin was 49.18% in FY2021 and 40.78% in FY2026. The Qorvo deal is, in poker terms, an attempt to stop being one of two players the dealer can play off against each other. On the November 3 call management said it does not expect its largest customer's award process to change after the merger, and described the market as highly competitive.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One customer sets the weather. The largest customer was about 62% of Skyworks' September-quarter revenue, Apple has been above 10% of revenue in each of the three fiscal years through FY2025, and three receivable balances made up 82% of gross receivables at the FY2025 year-end. When that customer dual-sourced, Skyworks guided a 20–25% content drop in one phone cycle. Adding Qorvo does not remove this exposure; Qorvo sells into the same handsets.
02
Earnings have been shrinking for five years. GAAP diluted EPS: $8.97 (FY2021), $7.81, $6.13, $3.69, $3.08 (FY2025), $1.14 (FY2026). Part of the recent drop is deal and one-off cost (Q4 FY2026 alone carried $35.7M of acquisition-related expense, $19.1M of restructuring and $66.4M of settlements and impairments), but the trend began long before the deal, and adjusted EPS also fell, from $5.93 to $5.05 in FY2026.
03
The balance sheet has changed shape. At October 2, 2026, three days before the close, Skyworks had about $2.6B of cash and investments against $2.47B of debt, the debt including roughly $2B raised for the deal. The cash portion of about $2.87B (our calculation) then went out, and the Qorvo notes came in, taking debt to about $4.02B (our calculation, from the October 7 8-K). A company that paid $320.1M in dividends in fiscal 2026 now pays none and carries acquisition debt. Moody's raised it to Baa3, the lowest investment-grade rating, and Fitch affirmed its BBB+ rating and moved its outlook to stable from negative (Q4 release).
04
Costs are rising while the phone market softens. Adjusted gross margin fell to 44.4% in Q4 from 46.5% a year earlier, and both the CEO and CFO called input costs a continuing headwind. The December-quarter guide has smartphone revenue down by a low-teens percentage on seasonality and Android weakness, just as the company has to start delivering its cost savings.

Notable One-Off Items

Q4 FY2026 adjustments were share-based compensation $49.5M, amortisation of intangibles $34.2M, acquisition-related expenses $35.7M, settlements, gains, losses and impairments $66.4M and restructuring $19.1M — which is how $0.15 of GAAP EPS becomes $1.28 adjusted. Accounting for the Qorvo purchase will add yearly amortisation charges (the cost of the patents and customer relationships it bought, spread over time) from here, so GAAP and adjusted earnings are likely to stay far apart for several years. Note also the fiscal calendar: Skyworks uses a 52/53-week year (FY2025 ended October 3, 2025; FY2026 ended October 2, 2026). Buybacks came late: $114.5M in FY2026, of which $107.0M in Q4 at an average of $66.27 a share, under a $2B program that replaced the one due to expire in February 2027. The CFO's line on the call: "We have a bias towards share buybacks."

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The merger fixes the thing that has been wrong for years: two RF suppliers, each too small on its own, competing for the same few customers. The combined September quarter was about $1.98B of revenue, and management has promised $500M or more of annual cost savings, with about $150M of operating-expense savings in fiscal 2027 alone. Data center grew about 90% year over year by the CEO's account, and Diversified Growth Markets are guided to more than 40% of sales. At 14.4x FY2027 consensus EPS the stock is not expensive for a business with a large cost plan under its own control, the dividend money now goes toward a $2B buyback, and Moody's has lifted it to investment grade. Management's targets, 50–55% adjusted gross margin and 30–35% adjusted operating margin within 24–36 months of the close, compare with 44.4% and 21.7% for Skyworks alone last quarter.

Bear Case

Skyworks' own revenue fell from $5.49B (FY2022) to $3.96B (FY2026), gross margin from 49% (FY2021) to 41%, GAAP EPS from $8.97 (FY2021) to $1.14, and the merger does not change who the customers are — one customer was about 62% of Skyworks' last stand-alone quarter. The company swapped a net-cash balance sheet and a dividend for about $4.02B of debt (our calculation). The December guide has smartphone revenue falling by a low-teens percentage, input costs are still rising, and the year's savings are weighted to the second half. At 72x trailing GAAP earnings, the price rests on adjusted numbers and on savings that have not yet arrived.

Top 3 Risks

01
The savings have to arrive. The deal is done, so the risk has moved from whether it closes to whether it pays. Management needs $500M or more of annual cost savings within 24–36 months, and computer systems are not due to be merged until the first half of calendar 2027. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $5.71 is above the $5.00 that the December guide of $1.25 a quarter would annualise to, so the estimates already assume progress.
02
Customer concentration. The largest customer was about 62% of Skyworks' September-quarter revenue, and three receivables were 82% of the total at FY2025 year-end. A 20–25% content loss in a single phone cycle has already happened once; adding Qorvo, which sells into the same devices, raises the stakes of the next socket decision.
03
Debt and margin pressure. About $4.02B of debt after the close (our calculation), no dividend, and GAAP EPS already 87% below its FY2021 level ($1.14 vs $8.97). If the cost plan slips while input costs rise and mobile content keeps eroding, the combined company carries that debt into a shrinking market.
Industry Commentary
Consolidation in RF front-end — the two-player problem
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

For a decade Skyworks and Qorvo were the two US independent chip suppliers most exposed to the same flagship-phone programmes, and they could be played off against each other. The 2025 dual-sourcing decision that cost Skyworks 20–25% of its content is what that looks like in practice. The merger, completed in October 2026, combines the two, aims to take out $500M or more of cost, and sells from a larger share of the front-end. Management says it does not expect the largest customer's award process to change. Whether customers respond by moving more content in-house or to other suppliers is the question the next two phone cycles will answer.

Broad Markets — the diversification bet
EMERGING TAILWIND

The growth in this business is outside the phone. Skyworks' Broad Markets grew 9% YoY in Q4 FY2026, led by data center, Wi-Fi and automotive, and management is aiming timing and isolation products at AI data-center build-outs. In the combined company these markets are guided to more than 40% of sales, led by data center and defense. It is still the smaller half, and it competes with much larger analog franchises, but it is the part of the combined company with a growth rate and a customer list that no single buyer controls.

What the price already says
MARKET SIGNAL

Three numbers set against each other tell the story. Forward P/E: 14.4x FY2027 consensus EPS — cheap if the combined earnings arrive. Average analyst target: $78.44, 4.6% below the price — analysts, 20 of 25 at Hold, are waiting to see them. GAAP EPS: $1.14 against $5.05 adjusted — the gap between the official number and the one the market is valuing. The bull reads a cost plan under management's control; the bear reads a stock priced on adjusted earnings with one customer at 62% of revenue. Both agree that the next number that matters is the first combined quarter, due with the December-quarter results.
