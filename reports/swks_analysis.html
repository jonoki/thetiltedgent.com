01 Company Overview

Identity first. SWKS is Skyworks Solutions, the Irvine, California maker of RF, analog and mixed-signal chips, formed in 2002 when Alpha Industries merged with Conexant's wireless division. It has been an S&P 500 constituent since March 12, 2015 and still is; it left the NASDAQ-100 on December 19, 2022. The ticker has not been recycled and has not changed. What is changing is the company behind it: on October 28, 2025 Skyworks agreed to buy Qorvo, its closest US rival, for $32.50 in cash plus 0.960 Skyworks shares per Qorvo share. Skyworks is the acquirer and the surviving listed stock; Skyworks holders are expected to own about 63% of the combined company and Qorvo holders about 37%. As of the September 21, 2026 close the deal had not closed: US antitrust waiting periods have expired and the debt financing is done, but China's SAMR review — in its third and final phase — is still open. The name and ticker of the combined company were not stated in any release fetched for this report.

Here is the hand. Skyworks has spent four years folding: revenue fell from $5.49B in FY2022 to $4.09B in FY2025, GAAP EPS from $7.81 to $3.08, and gross margin from 47.5% to 41.2%, as its largest customer moved to dual-sourcing and its content in the fall-2025 iPhone was guided down 20–25%. The share price followed, from a $167.13 month-end in October 2021 to $53.55 in March 2026. Management's answer is not to play the small pot better but to buy the other big stack at the table. The market's verdict is loud: the stock closed at $88.74, up 65.7% from the March low and above every published analyst target. And on July 28 the company ended its dividend — $2.84 a share over the trailing year — redirecting the cash to buybacks, deleveraging and M&A. The stock is now a bet on one thing: whether $500 million of promised synergies turn two shrinking RF businesses into one that earns its multiple.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

RF module integration know-how Incumbent flagship-phone sockets Owned manufacturing footprint Long-lived auto & industrial design wins R&D scale ($785.5M in FY2025) Scale via Qorvo (pending)

RF front-ends are hard to make well: dozens of bands, filters that must separate them within fractions of a percent, amplifiers that have to be efficient enough not to cook a battery, all packed into a module the size of a fingernail. That is a moat against new entrants — which is why the field has been down to a handful of serious players for years. But it is a moat without pricing power against the customers that matter. A handful of phone OEMs buy most of the output, dual-source whenever they can, and redesign every year. The margin history is the evidence: gross margin was 49.18% in FY2021 and 40.73% over the trailing twelve months. The Qorvo deal is, in poker terms, an attempt to stop being one of two players the dealer can play off against each other.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One customer sets the weather. Apple has been above 10% of revenue in each of the last three fiscal years, and three receivable balances made up 82% of gross receivables at the FY2025 year-end. When that customer dual-sourced, Skyworks guided a 20–25% content drop in one phone cycle. A combined Skyworks–Qorvo does not remove this exposure; it enlarges it, because Qorvo sells into the same handsets.
02
Earnings have been shrinking for four years. GAAP diluted EPS: $8.97 (FY2021), $7.81, $6.13, $3.69, $3.08 (FY2025), $1.93 trailing. Operating margin went from 31.74% to 10.38% over the same span. Part of the recent drop is merger and restructuring cost (Q3 FY2026 included $25.7M of acquisition-related expense and $22.0M of restructuring), but the trend began long before the deal.
03
The balance sheet is about to change shape. At July 3, 2026 Skyworks had $813.8M of cash and marketable securities against $496.9M of debt — net cash. It said it expected to raise about $2B of acquisition debt, and the dividend has been dropped to redirect cash to buybacks and deleveraging. A company that paid $320.1M in dividends in nine months is now a levered acquirer.
04
China holds the last card. SAMR's review is in phase three. A Capitol Forum headline said SAMR was "harboring concerns" and likely to launch a phase-three review (headline only; the article body was paywalled and not read). Both companies sell heavily into Chinese-assembled phones; a remedy or a delay is not priced by a 0.41% arbitrage spread.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The merger fixes the thing that has been wrong for four years: two sub-scale RF suppliers competing for the same few customers. Put together, the company has ~$7.7B of pro forma revenue, ~$2.1B of adjusted EBITDA before $500M of cost synergies, a ~$5.5B mobile business with more bargaining weight, and a ~$2.5B non-mobile business growing in Wi-Fi, automotive and data center. Broad Markets grew 8% last quarter, the three growth engines 15%. At 17.9x FY2027 consensus EPS the stock is not expensive for a business about to get a large, controllable cost lever, and the dividend money now goes to a $2B buyback. The 0.41% arbitrage spread says the market sees little risk the deal fails.

Bear Case

Two shrinking businesses do not make a growing one. Skyworks' revenue fell from $5.49B to $4.01B, gross margin from 49% to 41%, GAAP EPS from $8.97 to $1.93, and the merger does not change who the customers are — it concentrates more revenue in the same handsets. The company is swapping a net-cash balance sheet and a dividend for ~$2B of new debt, and the stock has run 65.7% off its March low to sit above every one of 23 analyst targets. China's regulator still holds the final card; a delay or remedy would hit a stock priced for certainty. At 46x trailing GAAP earnings, the price already assumes the synergies arrive.

Top 3 Risks

01
Deal risk at a full price. SAMR phase-three review is outstanding. The stock closed above the highest analyst target and the arbitrage spread is 0.41%, so the price leaves little room for a delay, a remedy or a block. The outside date runs to April 27, 2027 (extendable to October 27, 2027).
02
Customer concentration, doubled. Apple has been above 10% of revenue for three years and three receivables were 82% of the total at FY2025 year-end. A 20–25% content loss in a single phone cycle has already happened once; adding Qorvo, which sells into the same devices, raises the stakes of the next socket decision.
03
Integration and leverage. ~$2B of acquisition debt, $2.87B of cash consideration (our calculation), no dividend, $500M of synergies to find over 24–36 months, and GAAP EPS already about 78% below its FY2021 level ($1.93 vs $8.97). If the cost plan slips while mobile content keeps eroding, the combined company carries debt into a shrinking market.
Industry Commentary
Consolidation in RF front-end — the two-player problem
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

For a decade Skyworks and Qorvo were the two US merchant suppliers most exposed to the same flagship-phone programmes, and they could be played off against each other. The 2025 dual-sourcing decision that cost Skyworks 20–25% of its content is what that looks like in practice. The merger is a direct response: combine the two, take out $500M of cost, and negotiate from a larger share of the front-end. Whether customers accept that — or move more content in-house or to other suppliers — is the question the next two phone cycles will answer. The deal's approval path runs through China precisely because so many of these phones are assembled there.

Broad Markets — the diversification bet
EMERGING TAILWIND

The growth in this business is outside the phone. Skyworks' Wi-Fi, automotive and data-center lines grew 15% YoY in Q3 FY2026, and management is aiming timing and isolation products at AI data-center build-outs. Post-merger the non-mobile half would be about $2.5–2.6B of revenue. It is still the smaller half, and it competes with much larger analog franchises, but it is the part of the combined company with a growth rate and a customer list that no single buyer controls.

What the price already says
MARKET SIGNAL

Three numbers set against each other tell the story. Arbitrage spread: 0.41% — the market thinks the deal closes, and soon. Average analyst target: $68.35, 23% below the price — the sell side has not re-underwritten the combined company. Short interest: 22.97% of shares — plausibly arbitrage hedges rather than bearish bets (our interpretation). The bull reads these as a sell side that is behind the tape; the bear reads a stock priced for a clean close and full synergies with no margin for a slip. Both agree that after close, SWKS stops being a stand-alone RF supplier and becomes a leveraged integration story — and that the next number that matters is the first combined quarter, not the last stand-alone one.
