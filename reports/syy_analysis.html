01 Company Overview

Sysco is the largest foodservice distributor in the world: it buys food and non-food product in bulk, warehouses it, and delivers it in cases on its own trucks to roughly 700,000 customer locations — independent restaurants, chains, hospitals, schools, hotels, prisons and caterers. Fiscal 2026, which ended June 27, 2026, produced sales of $84.55B, up 3.9% from $81.37B in FY2025, with GAAP diluted EPS of $3.66 and adjusted diluted EPS of $4.61. At a $39.4B market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #650 globally. Note the calendar convention used throughout this page: Sysco's fiscal year runs July to June, so "FY2026" means the twelve months to June 27, 2026 — not calendar 2026.

The central debate is not whether Sysco is a good business — it is what kind of business it is. Distribution is structurally a thin-margin, volume-driven model: a 2.08% net margin is not evidence of weakness, it is the definition of the industry. The right unit of analysis is the case: how many cases move, what gross profit each case earns, and what it costs to pick and deliver it. On that basis FY2026 was a grind — total U.S. Foodservice case volume grew +1.4% for the year and +2.5% in Q4, with local (independent-restaurant) cases at +1.7% for the year and +2.6% in Q4. The company has now bolted three things onto that grind: a $500M multi-year AI efficiency program, an agreement to acquire Restaurant Depot, and FY2027 guidance of 9–11% adjusted EPS growth. Whether a low-single-digit case-growth business can deliver double-digit earnings growth without permanently capitalising the difference is the question this page works through.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
ROUTE DENSITY PURCHASING SCALE SYSCO BRAND PENETRATION 17,000-STRONG SALESFORCE COLD-CHAIN FIXED ASSETS BROADEST SKU BREADTH SWITCHING FRICTION

The moat is route density and purchasing scale, and both compound from the same thing: being the biggest buyer and the biggest deliverer in a given geography. On the buy side, $84.6B of purchasing volume means Sysco pays less per case than a regional distributor for identical product — and its private label, Sysco Brand, converts that buying power into a higher mark-up because the customer cannot price-shop a Sysco-only SKU. Management explicitly credited FY2026 gross-profit-per-case improvement to "strategic sourcing and Sysco Brand penetration momentum." On the deliver side, the economics are a stop-density problem: a truck that makes fourteen drops on a route earns far more gross profit per mile than one making eight, so the incumbent with the most customers per square mile has the lowest cost to serve and can quote a price a challenger cannot match and still fund a salesperson. That is why the fight is local, why the marginal customer is worth more to Sysco than to anyone else, and why the low consolidated net margin is not a signal of a weak business. Do not read a 2.08% net margin as fragility. Distribution is a spread business run at high asset turns: Sysco converted that thin margin into a 17.12% ROIC and $1.94B of free cash flow. The correct comparison for a distributor is gross profit per case against cost to serve per case, not net margin against a software company.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Volume is the whole model, and volume is macro. Cases move when people eat out. FY2026 total U.S. case growth of +1.4% is barely above flat, and it is the aggregate of restaurant traffic, which Sysco does not control. In a business where fixed warehouse and fleet costs are already committed, a swing from +1.4% to −1% case growth does not trim earnings, it decimates the operating leverage — the same mechanism that made SYGMA's Q4 operating income grow 11.1% on 3.1% sales works identically in reverse.
▸
Local-case share is contested by a stronger US Foods. Independent-restaurant cases are where the gross profit per case lives, and they are exactly what every competitor is also chasing. US Foods grew EPS +39.5% over the trailing year and its market cap rose +27.1% while Sysco's stock went sideways — the market is currently paying more for the challenger's execution than the incumbent's scale. Performance Food Group grew revenue +7.4%, nearly double Sysco's rate.
▸
Balance-sheet leverage and the Restaurant Depot deal. Debt-to-equity is 5.65 and enterprise value exceeds market cap by $13.3B of net debt. FY2026 operating income rose while net income fell 3.9% — the gap is below the operating line. Layering a sizeable cash acquisition onto that structure, with an interim CFO in the chair, is the leverage question the FY2027 guidance quietly depends on.
▸
Product-cost inflation cuts both ways. Distributors earn a percentage mark-up, so modest inflation is a tailwind to gross profit dollars per case. Sharp inflation is not: customers trade down, cut menu items and reduce order frequency, and case volume falls faster than price rises. Deflation is worse still — it shrinks the dollar mark-up on the same physical case. FY2026 gross margin improved only 10 basis points to 18.5%, and Q4 gross margin actually fell 17 bps to 18.7%, which is the pricing environment showing through.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the case, because everything else is downstream of it. Sysco moved roughly 1.4% more cases through U.S. Foodservice in FY2026 than in FY2025, with local cases up 1.7%. On that volume it earned $11.2B of segment gross profit, up 3.3% — so gross profit dollars grew about twice as fast as cases, which is the arithmetic proof that gross profit per case rose. Management attributes that to strategic sourcing and Sysco Brand penetration, and the direction is right. Then the chain breaks: U.S. Foodservice operating income grew 0.1%. Gross profit per case improved and none of it reached the operating line, because the cost of picking, loading and delivering a case rose at almost exactly the same rate. That gap — between gross profit per case and cost to serve per case — is the entire investment case, and the $500M AI efficiency program is management's answer to it.

The local-versus-national mix, and why it is the margin. A local independent restaurant orders small quantities, frequently, across a wide SKU range; it takes the Sysco salesperson's recommendation, buys Sysco Brand product, and cannot meaningfully price-shop. A national chain negotiates centrally, takes enormous drops on fixed schedules, and contracts a thin mark-up. The two customers generate completely different gross profit per case on the same truck. In Q4 FY2026 local cases grew 2.6% and national cases also grew 2.6% — the mix did not shift. That is a meaningfully different outcome from the same headline volume with local outgrowing national, and it is the detail most summaries of the quarter drop. FY2027's ~2.5% local case guide is a growth target, not a mix-shift target; if national keeps pace again, the gross-margin benefit of the volume recovery is smaller than the volume number suggests.

On the thin margin — say it plainly. Sysco's 2.08% net profit margin and 18.5% gross margin are not weaknesses. Foodservice distribution is a spread business: buy a case, add a mark-up, deliver it, repeat 84 billion dollars' worth a year. The correct measures are capital productivity and cash conversion, and on both Sysco looks like a good business: 17.12% ROIC and $1.94B of free cash flow — 110% of GAAP net income. A business that converts more than all of its accounting profit into cash and earns 17% on invested capital is not fragile. The fragility is elsewhere: it is operating leverage. With fixed warehouses, fixed fleet and a largely fixed salesforce, a 1% swing in case volume swings operating income by multiples of that. SYGMA's Q4 — 3.1% sales growth, 11.1% operating income growth — is the mechanism working in your favour. It runs exactly as hard the other way.

Valuation, and which earnings base. On GAAP FY2026 EPS of $3.66, SYY trades at 22.5×. On the adjusted base of $4.61, it trades at 17.9×. On the FY2027 consensus of $5.09, 16.2×. The $0.95 GAAP-to-adjusted gap is 26% of GAAP earnings, so which number you use changes the answer materially. Against peers the cheap ranking holds on any base — US Foods trades at 31.7× trailing, Performance Food Group at 41.2× — but the PEG of 2.72 explains why: a 22.5× multiple on a business guiding to ~2.5% case growth is not obviously cheap, it is appropriately priced for slow. The 9–11% adjusted EPS growth guide bridges that gap only if the AI program and the Restaurant Depot synergies deliver; note that roughly two points of the 6–7% FY2027 sales guide is a 53rd week. Meanwhile the shareholder is paid 2.67% to wait, with the dividend covered 1.9× by free cash flow. The stock is priced as a bond proxy with an option on a cost program. If the program lands, the multiple re-rates and the EPS grows; if it does not, you own a 2.7% yield on a business growing cases at 2%, and the multiple has nowhere good to go.

Bull Case

Volume is inflecting — Q4 total cases +2.5% and local +2.6% were the best of FY2026, with local up 130 bps sequentially on a two-year stack — and gross profit per case is already rising on strategic sourcing and Sysco Brand. Layer on ~$100M of in-year FY2027 AI savings inside a $500M multi-year program, $250M of expected Restaurant Depot procurement synergies, and a cash-and-carry format to be expanded into 125+ new geographies, and the 9–11% adjusted EPS guide has three independent sources. You are paid 2.67% with 60% payout coverage and 110% cash conversion to hold a 0.63-beta business earning 17.1% ROIC at 16.2× forward earnings — cheaper than both listed peers on every multiple.

Bear Case

FY2026 was the year gross profit dollars grew 3.3% and U.S. Foodservice operating income grew 0.1% — cost to serve absorbed the entire benefit. Net income fell 3.9% on $13.27B of net debt and 5.65× debt-to-equity, GAAP EPS fell 1.9%, and Q4 gross margin actually declined 17 bps. The 9–11% EPS guide depends on a cost program run by an interim CFO and an acquisition that has not closed; roughly two points of the sales guide is a 53rd week. US Foods grew EPS 39.5% and its market cap 27.1% over the same twelve months in which Sysco went sideways. At a 2.72 PEG and a five-year price CAGR of 1.4%, the market has been right about this stock for five years.

Top 3 Risks

01
Case volume rolls over. Everything in the model is a function of cases moved, and cases are a function of restaurant traffic, which Sysco does not control. FY2026 total U.S. case growth of +1.4% is already thin; a consumer pullback that turns it negative hits a cost base of fixed warehouses, fixed fleet and fixed salesforce. The operating leverage that makes SYGMA's 3.1% sales growth into 11.1% income growth works identically in reverse, and there is no hedge for it.
02
The cost program under-delivers while leverage stays high. The 9–11% adjusted EPS guide is arithmetically dependent on cost savings, not volume — ~2.5% case growth does not produce 10% EPS growth on its own. That program is being executed with an interim CFO, against $13.27B of net debt and a pending acquisition. If the AI savings arrive late or the Restaurant Depot integration consumes the synergy, the buyback is the flex item and the guide misses from the cost line, not the sales line.
03
Share loss in local cases to a better-executing US Foods. Local independent cases carry the gross profit per case that funds everything. US Foods grew EPS 39.5% and Performance Food Group grew revenue 7.4% over the trailing year while Sysco grew sales 3.9% and GAAP EPS −1.9%. Scale is Sysco's moat, but scale does not win the individual street account — salesforce quality, delivery reliability and local density do, and the incumbent has the most to lose in that fight.
  Industry Commentary
Food-Away-From-Home Demand Is the Sector's Only Real Driver
ONGOING HEADWIND
Every foodservice distributor's revenue line is restaurant traffic multiplied by a mark-up. FY2026's +1.4% total U.S. case growth and +1.7% local case growth tell you what the underlying demand environment actually is — positive, but barely. Through the cycle, consumers under pressure trade from full-service restaurants to fast food to grocery, and each step down that ladder moves cases to a different distributor or out of the channel entirely. Sysco's Q4 acceleration to +2.5% total and +2.6% local is genuinely encouraging; one quarter is not a trend, and the FY2027 guide of only ~2.5% local growth says management does not treat it as one either.
Cost to Serve Is Rising as Fast as Gross Profit Per Case
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the defining industry problem of the current cycle and FY2026 is the cleanest possible illustration of it. U.S. Foodservice gross profit rose 3.3% to $11.2B on 1.4% case growth — gross profit per case genuinely improved. Segment operating income rose 0.1%. Warehouse labour, driver wages, fleet and fuel consumed the entire gain. Consolidated FY2026 operating income was up just 0.2% and net income fell 3.9%. Every distributor in the industry is running the same play in response — automation, route optimisation, and now AI — because the alternative, raising the mark-up, invites the customer to call a competitor. Sysco's $500M multi-year program with ~$100M of in-year FY2027 savings is the scale leader's version of a sector-wide necessity.
Consolidation and Format Expansion — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND
A mature, low-growth, fragmented industry consolidates. Sysco's pending Restaurant Depot acquisition — expected to close by Q3 FY2027, with ~$250M of targeted procurement synergies and a plan to open the cash-and-carry format in 125+ new geographies — is the clearest strategic move it has made in years, because it reaches the smallest independent operators who never take a delivery truck at all. Bull framing: buying volume is the cheapest way to fix a cost-to-serve problem, since every incremental case spreads the same fixed network; combined with the AI program, that is how ~2.5% case growth becomes 9–11% EPS growth. Bear framing: the deal has not closed, the price was not disclosed on the sources fetched, debt-to-equity is already 5.65 with $13.27B of net debt, and an interim CFO is signing the cheques. A synergy target is a forecast; the $13.27B is a fact.
