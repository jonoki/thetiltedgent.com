01 Company Overview

Sysco is the largest foodservice distributor in the world: it buys food and non-food product in bulk, warehouses it, and delivers it in cases on its own trucks to about 670,000 customer locations — independent restaurants, chains, hospitals, schools, hotels, prisons and caterers. Fiscal 2026, which ended June 27, 2026, produced sales of $84.55B, up 3.9% from $81.37B in FY2025, with GAAP diluted EPS of $3.66 and adjusted diluted EPS of $4.61. The first quarter of fiscal 2027 (13 weeks to September 26, 2026), reported November 3, brought sales of $21.74B, up 2.8%, GAAP diluted EPS of $1.09, up 10.1%, and adjusted EPS of $1.19, up 3.5%, which puts GAAP EPS for the last twelve months at $3.76. The market capitalisation is $37.9B (492.3M shares outstanding on October 16, 2026, per the 10-Q, at the November 4 close; our calculation). Note the calendar convention used throughout this page: Sysco's fiscal year runs July to June, so "FY2026" means the twelve months to June 27, 2026 — not calendar 2026.

The central debate is not whether Sysco is a good business — it is what kind of business it is. Distribution is structurally a thin-margin, volume-driven model: a 2.1% net margin is not evidence of weakness, it is the definition of the industry. The right unit of analysis is the case: how many cases move, what gross profit each case earns, and what it costs to pick and deliver it. On that basis FY2026 was a grind — total U.S. Foodservice case volume grew +1.4% for the year, local (independent-restaurant) cases +1.7% — and the first quarter of FY2027 kept the better pace that began in Q4: total cases +2.6%, local +2.7%. Three things now sit on top of that grind: a $500M multi-year AI efficiency program (about $100M of savings expected in FY2027), the pending ~$29.1B acquisition of Jetro Restaurant Depot, whose financing was completed in October, and FY2027 guidance of 9–11% adjusted EPS growth, reaffirmed on November 3 even as sales growth was guided to the low end of 6–7% because food prices are rising more slowly. The stock fell 9.3% on the day of the print. Whether a low-single-digit case-growth business can deliver double-digit earnings growth while borrowing heavily for a large acquisition is the question this page works through.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
ROUTE DENSITY PURCHASING SCALE SYSCO BRAND PENETRATION LOCAL SALESFORCE COLD-CHAIN FIXED ASSETS WIDEST PRODUCT RANGE SWITCHING FRICTION

The moat is route density and purchasing scale, and both compound from the same thing: being the biggest buyer and the biggest deliverer in a given geography. On the buy side, $84.6B of annual sales means Sysco buys more of each product than a regional distributor and pays less per case for it — and its private label, Sysco Brand, converts that buying power into a higher mark-up because the customer cannot price-shop a product only Sysco sells. The FY2026 results release credits the gross-profit gains partly to strategic sourcing efficiencies and a better mix from higher Sysco Brand penetration. On the deliver side, the economics are a stop-density problem: a truck that makes fourteen drops on a route earns far more gross profit per mile than one making eight, so the incumbent with the most customers per square mile has the lowest cost to serve and can quote a price a challenger cannot match and still fund a salesperson. That is why the fight is local, why one more customer on an existing route is worth more to Sysco than to anyone else, and why the low consolidated net margin is not a signal of a weak business. Do not read a 2.1% net margin as fragility. Distribution is a spread business that earns its return by moving a lot of product through the same assets: Sysco converted that thin margin into a 16.75% ROIC and $2.19B of free cash flow over the last twelve months. The correct comparison for a distributor is gross profit per case against cost to serve per case, not net margin against a software company.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Volume is the whole model, and volume is macro. Cases move when people eat out. FY2026 total U.S. case growth of +1.4% is barely above flat, and it is the aggregate of restaurant traffic, which Sysco does not control. Q1 FY2027's +2.6% is better, but in a business where warehouse and fleet costs are already committed, a swing to −1% case growth would not trim earnings, it would cut deep into them, because the costs stay while the cases go. SYGMA showed both directions in successive quarters: operating income +11.1% on +3.1% sales in Q4 FY2026, then −16.0% on +1.1% sales in Q1 FY2027.
▸
Local-case share is contested by a stronger US Foods. Independent-restaurant cases are where the gross profit per case lives, and they are exactly what every competitor is also chasing. US Foods grew EPS +39.5% over its trailing twelve months (to June 2026) and its share price rose 49.2% in the 52 weeks to November 4, 2026, against +5.3% for Sysco (stockanalysis for US Foods; Yahoo closes for Sysco) — the market is paying more for the challenger's execution than the incumbent's scale. Performance Food Group grew trailing revenue +6.1%, against Sysco's +2.8% in Q1 FY2027.
▸
Balance-sheet leverage and the Restaurant Depot deal. At September 26, 2026 Sysco had $13.82B of debt and $3.17B of cash (net debt $10.6B, about 2.4× adjusted EBITDA — earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation — per the release), and debt-to-equity of 3.79 on stockanalysis's measure, which counts leases. Those figures predate most of the deal borrowing: on October 6 Sysco completed a $25.65B financing package for the ~$29.1B Restaurant Depot purchase — $1B of new shares, $10.75B of senior notes, C$1.5B of Canadian notes, $3.9B and €1B of junior subordinated notes (bonds that rank behind the senior debt), plus term loans and a revolving credit line (Paul, Weiss client note, Oct 6, 2026). Q1 interest expense was already $261M against $172M a year earlier, $90M of it bridge-loan costs (a bridge loan is a short-term standby loan arranged until the long-term debt is raised). On the call management restated its commitment to paying debt down and keeping a mid-BBB credit rating.
▸
Product-cost inflation cuts both ways. Distributors earn a percentage mark-up, so modest inflation is a tailwind to gross profit dollars per case. Sharp inflation is not: customers trade down, cut menu items and reduce order frequency, and case volume falls faster than price rises. Deflation is worse still — it shrinks the dollar mark-up on the same physical case. Q1 FY2027 showed the first half of that: food-cost inflation came in lower than Sysco expected, which management said accounts for sales falling short of its own plan, and full-year sales growth is now guided to the low end of 6–7%. Gross margin still rose 0.18 percentage points to 18.6%.

(4) Restaurant Depot is not in the base. In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the case, because everything else is downstream of it. Sysco moved roughly 1.4% more cases through U.S. Foodservice in FY2026 than in FY2025, with local cases up 1.7%. On that volume it earned $11.2B of segment gross profit, up 3.3% — so gross profit dollars grew about twice as fast as cases, which is the arithmetic proof that gross profit per case rose. Management attributes that to strategic sourcing and Sysco Brand penetration, and the direction is right. Then the chain breaks: U.S. Foodservice operating income grew 0.1%. Gross profit per case improved and none of it reached the operating line, because the cost of picking, loading and delivering a case rose at almost exactly the same rate. Q1 FY2027 narrowed the gap a little: U.S. Foodservice gross profit rose 3.2% and operating income 1.6% (adjusted +2.7%) on 2.6% case growth. That gap — between gross profit per case and cost to serve per case — is the entire investment case, and the $500M AI efficiency program is management's answer to it.

The local-versus-national mix, and why it is the margin. A local independent restaurant orders small quantities, frequently, across a wide range of products; it takes the Sysco salesperson's recommendation, buys Sysco Brand product, and cannot meaningfully price-shop. A national chain negotiates centrally, takes enormous drops on fixed schedules, and contracts a thin mark-up. The two customers generate completely different gross profit per case on the same truck. In Q4 FY2026 local cases grew 2.6% against 2.5% in total — the mix barely shifted. In Q1 FY2027 local cases grew 2.7% against 2.6% in total, and on the call Hourican put growth in contract (chain and national-account) cases at about 2.2%, so local edged ahead: a small shift in the right direction. FY2027's ~2.5% local case guide is a growth target, not a mix-shift target; if national keeps pace, the gross-margin benefit of the volume recovery is smaller than the volume number suggests.

On the thin margin — say it plainly. Sysco's 2.1% net profit margin and 18.5% gross margin are not weaknesses. Foodservice distribution is a spread business: buy a case, add a mark-up, deliver it, repeat 84 billion dollars' worth a year. The correct measures are capital productivity and cash conversion, and on both Sysco looks like a good business: 16.75% ROIC and $2.19B of trailing free cash flow — 121% of GAAP net income. A business that converts more than all of its accounting profit into cash and earns 17% on invested capital is not fragile. The fragility is elsewhere: it is fixed costs. With fixed warehouses, fixed fleet and a largely fixed salesforce, a 1% swing in case volume swings operating income by several times that. SYGMA showed it both ways in two quarters: 3.1% sales growth and 11.1% operating income growth in Q4 FY2026, then 1.1% and −16.0% in Q1 FY2027.

Valuation, and which earnings base. On trailing GAAP EPS of $3.76, SYY trades at 20.5×. On trailing adjusted EPS of $4.65, 16.5×. On the FY2027 consensus of $5.06, 15.2×. The $0.89 GAAP-to-adjusted gap is 24% of GAAP earnings, so which number you use changes the answer materially. Against peers the cheap ranking holds on any base — US Foods trades at 33.8× trailing, Performance Food Group at 43.7× — but the PEG of 1.74 says why: a 20× price on a business guiding to ~2.5% case growth is not obviously cheap, it is priced for slow. The 9–11% adjusted EPS growth guide, reaffirmed on November 3, bridges that gap only if the AI program and the Restaurant Depot synergies deliver; note that roughly two points of the 6–7% FY2027 sales guide, now at its low end, is a 53rd week. Meanwhile the shareholder is paid 2.86% to wait, with the dividend covered about twice by free cash flow. The stock is priced like a bond substitute — a steady payer people own for the income — with an option on a cost program and a large acquisition. If both land, earnings grow and investors may pay more for each dollar of them; if not, you own a 2.9% yield on a business growing cases at 2–3% and carrying far more debt.

Bull Case

Volume has held its better pace — Q1 FY2027 total cases +2.6% and local +2.7%, after +2.5% and +2.6% in Q4 — and gross profit per case is rising on strategic sourcing and Sysco Brand. Layer on ~$100M of FY2027 AI savings inside a $500M multi-year program, about $250M a year of targeted Restaurant Depot cost synergies, plans for 125+ new Restaurant Depot locations, and deal financing already in hand, and the 9–11% adjusted EPS guide, reaffirmed on November 3, has three independent sources. You are paid 2.86% with a 58.5% payout and 121% cash conversion to hold a 0.64-beta business earning 16.75% ROIC at 15.2× forward earnings — cheaper than both listed peers on trailing and forward P/E.

Bear Case

Q1 FY2027 sales fell short of management's own plan as food inflation cooled, the sales guide slipped to the low end of 6–7%, and the stock fell 9.3% on the day. Reported operating income fell 10.4%, SYGMA's fell 16.0%, and net earnings rose 10.5% only with the help of a one-off $234M hedging gain. Ahead lies the debt for a ~$29.1B acquisition that has not closed and is still in FTC review: $10.75B of senior notes and $3.9B plus €1B of junior subordinated notes issued in October, on top of $13.82B of debt at quarter-end. US Foods' shares rose 49.2% in the twelve months in which Sysco's rose 5.3%. At a 1.74 PEG and a five-year price return of about 1.9% a year, the market has been right about this stock for five years.

Top 3 Risks

01
Case volume rolls over. Everything in the model is a function of cases moved, and cases are a function of restaurant traffic, which Sysco does not control. FY2026 total U.S. case growth of +1.4% is already thin; a consumer pullback that turns it negative hits a cost base of fixed warehouses, fixed fleet and fixed salesforce. SYGMA's Q1 FY2027 — operating income −16.0% on +1.1% sales — shows how fast fixed costs bite when volume stalls, and there is no hedge for it.
02
The cost program under-delivers while leverage stays high. The 9–11% adjusted EPS guide is arithmetically dependent on cost savings, not volume — ~2.5% case growth does not produce 10% EPS growth on its own. That program is being executed against $10.6B of net debt at quarter-end, the new deal notes on top, and a pending acquisition. If the AI savings arrive late or the Restaurant Depot integration consumes the synergy, the guide misses from the cost line, not the sales line — and with no buybacks in Q1, there is little room left to cushion it.
03
Share loss in local cases to a better-executing US Foods. Local independent cases carry the gross profit per case that funds everything. US Foods grew trailing EPS 39.5% and Performance Food Group trailing revenue 6.1%, while Sysco grew Q1 FY2027 sales 2.8%. Scale is Sysco's moat, but scale does not win the individual street account — salesforce quality, delivery reliability and local density do, and the incumbent has the most to lose in that fight.
  Industry Commentary
Food-Away-From-Home Demand Is the Sector's Only Real Driver
ONGOING HEADWIND
Every foodservice distributor's revenue line is restaurant traffic multiplied by a mark-up. FY2026's +1.4% total U.S. case growth and +1.7% local case growth tell you what the underlying demand environment actually is — positive, but barely. Through the cycle, consumers under pressure trade from full-service restaurants to fast food to grocery, and each step down that ladder moves cases to a different distributor or out of the channel entirely. Sysco's acceleration to +2.5% total and +2.6% local in Q4, then +2.6% and +2.7% in Q1 FY2027, is genuinely encouraging; two quarters are a start, and the FY2027 guide of ~2.5% local growth says management is not counting on more.
Cost to Serve Is Rising as Fast as Gross Profit Per Case
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the defining industry problem of the current cycle and FY2026 is the cleanest possible illustration of it. U.S. Foodservice gross profit rose 3.3% to $11.2B on 1.4% case growth — gross profit per case genuinely improved. Segment operating income rose 0.1%. Warehouse labour, driver wages, fleet and fuel consumed the entire gain. Consolidated FY2026 operating income was up just 0.2% and net income fell 3.9%; in Q1 FY2027 U.S. Foodservice gross profit rose 3.2% and operating income 1.6%. Every distributor in the industry is running the same play in response — automation, route optimisation, and now AI — because the alternative, raising the mark-up, invites the customer to call a competitor. Sysco's $500M multi-year program with ~$100M of in-year FY2027 savings is the scale leader's version of a sector-wide necessity.
Consolidation and Format Expansion — Bull vs Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND
A mature, low-growth, fragmented industry consolidates. Sysco's pending ~$29.1B Restaurant Depot acquisition — expected to close by Q3 FY2027, with ~$250M a year of targeted cost synergies, mostly from buying, and a plan for 125+ new Restaurant Depot locations — is the clearest strategic move it has made in years, because it reaches the smallest independent operators who never take a delivery truck at all. Bull framing: buying volume is the cheapest way to fix a cost-to-serve problem, since every incremental case spreads the same fixed network; combined with the AI program, that is how ~2.5% case growth becomes 9–11% EPS growth. Bear framing: the deal has not closed and is still in FTC review; Sysco arranged $25.65B of financing in September and October, most of it debt, and net debt was already $10.6B before the October notes. A synergy target is a forecast; the debt is a fact.
