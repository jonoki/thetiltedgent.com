01 Company Overview

Takeda is a Tokyo-based prescription-drug company that sells mainly outside Japan. In the quarter to June 2026 the United States brought in ¥568.9B of ¥1,219.9B in revenue, and Japan ¥103.9B. Its shape comes from the 2019 purchase of Shire, which brought rare-disease medicines and a large plasma business into a Japanese company. Its biggest product is Entyvio, a gut-selective antibody for ulcerative colitis and Crohn's disease. Entyvio sold ¥958.0B in FY2025 (the year to March 31, 2026), about a fifth of group revenue. FY2025 revenue was ¥4,505.7B, down 1.7%, as the ADHD drug Vyvanse kept losing sales to generics (−42% to ¥203.2B). On the company's core basis, operating profit was ¥1,172.5B (a 26.0% margin) and core EPS ¥517. The reported result was a net loss of ¥152.4B, after a ¥402.5B provision for a US jury verdict in the AMITIZA antitrust case.

This report turns on one question: can three new medicines take over from the mature portfolio before Entyvio meets biosimilars? In about four weeks of August 2026 Takeda got US approval for two first-in-class drugs. Orzeyful (oveporexton) is the first medicine aimed at the cause of narcolepsy type 1, the loss of the brain chemical orexin, and was approved on August 5. Mimrylo (rusfertide), for the blood cancer polycythemia vera, was approved on August 28. A third drug, zasocitinib, a once-daily pill for psoriasis, had its US filing accepted for Priority Review on September 14, with a decision due in the first quarter of 2027. Management's FY2026 guide, the year these launches start, is still a step down at CER: core revenue down low single digits, core operating profit down 5–8% and core EPS down by a mid-teens percentage. The weak yen flatters the reported numbers. In Q1 FY2026 revenue was +10.2% in yen but −0.5% at constant currency.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Entyvio IBD franchise Plasma collection network First-in-class orexin science US commercial scale Home market in Japan ¥771.7B TTM free cash flow

A drug company's moat lasts only as long as its patents. Each patent is a lease with an expiry date, and the lasting asset is the ability to replace expiring leases with new ones. Takeda has two moats that last longer than one patent. The first is plasma. Turning donated plasma into immunoglobulin needs collection centres and fractionation licences. Indonesia's licence to Takeda in July 2026 is an example, so supply, not only patents, limits competition. The second is orexin biology. The Japanese approval release says the drug class originated in Takeda's own labs, and it has led to a first-in-class approval in three countries plus a follow-on candidate. The weak point is concentration: one product, Entyvio, is about a fifth of sales, and its biosimilar challengers have already filed.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Entyvio biosimilars. Takeda's own results deck says vedolizumab biosimilar applications are filed in the US and Europe. Copies "seeking to launch prior to 2032" must get past its patents, and the company says it will defend them. Entyvio was ¥958.0B of FY2025 revenue, so any early entry would cut straight into group sales.
02
The mature portfolio is shrinking faster than new launches can replace it. Vyvanse (−17% CER), Advate (−18%) and Takhzyro (−2%, US competition) are all falling. New launches are 4% of revenue, and management guides FY2026 core revenue down low single digits.
03
AMITIZA antitrust verdict. On May 18, 2026 a US jury awarded plaintiffs $884.9M in single damages. The wholesaler and retailer portions ($821.7M combined) are trebled automatically once judgment is entered. Takeda booked a ¥402.5B provision and says it will appeal. The district court judgment is expected in the second half of calendar 2026. In May the company said the adjusted free cash flow forecast was the one FY2026 figure that could move, depending on the amount and timing of any payment.
04
Leverage and the yen. Adjusted net debt is 2.7x EBITDA against a 2x target. About three-quarters of the ¥4,940.0B of bonds and loans is in dollar and euro notes (¥3,823.2B at June 30, 2026, bond table in the Q1 report), so the yen weakness that lifts revenue also lifts the yen value of the debt. The ¥204 dividend forecast for FY2026 costs about ¥322B a year (our estimate: ¥204 × 1.58B shares), against a FY2026 adjusted free cash flow forecast of ¥650–750B.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items separate the reported figures from the core ones. First, the AMITIZA provision. After the May 18, 2026 verdict Takeda added ¥402.5B of legal provisions to FY2025 (with ¥58.4B of related tax benefit), which turned the year into a ¥152.4B net loss. The company re-filed its FY2025 results on June 5. Second, restructuring: ¥70.8B in FY2025 for the efficiency programme, and ¥170.0B forecast for FY2026 for the transformation announced on March 25, 2026, which targets more than ¥200B of gross annual savings by FY2028. Third, amortisation and impairment of acquired product rights. This was ¥633.5B in FY2025, and the company's forecast lowers it for FY2026 now that Vyvanse's amortisation has ended. Fourth, tax: Q1 FY2026 tax expense rose 87.7% on a reassessment of deferred tax assets, which is why net profit fell while operating profit rose. Management's FY2026 reported forecast is net profit of ¥166.0B (EPS ¥104.26), a return to profit.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The low point is FY2026, and the replacement drugs are already approved. Orzeyful (first-in-class, approved in three countries), Mimrylo (first-in-class) and zasocitinib (Priority Review, superior head-to-head against deucravacitinib) give Takeda three new brands that management says could become blockbusters. They reach the market after Vyvanse has already shrunk to ¥203.2B a year (−42% in FY2025). The transformation programme funds the launches without sinking the margin: Q1 core margin was 29.4%. At ¥5,643 the stock trades on 10.9x TTM core EPS, yields 3.6% on a dividend set to rise from ¥200 to ¥204, and the ¥771.7B of TTM free cash flow is an 8.7% FCF yield. Every broker action in Kabutan's ratings tables after the approvals raised its target, Nomura's twice. The Capital Markets Day on December 11 is the obvious moment for a re-rating.

Bear Case

New launches are 4% of revenue, and the business that pays the bills is ageing. Entyvio is about a fifth of sales and has biosimilar filings against it. Takhzyro is losing US share. Plasma is a low-growth supply business. FY2026 core EPS is guided down by a mid-teens percentage, before any AMITIZA payment. Under US antitrust law, most of the $884.9M single-damages award is trebled once judgment is entered, against leverage already at 2.7x with a 2x target. The headline growth is mostly the yen: Q1 was −0.5% at constant currency. A dollar holder also carries the risk of the yen turning, which would reverse that effect. Launch drugs for rare and specialist conditions (120,000 narcolepsy type 1 patients in the US) can take years to build sales, and the R&D chief who built the pipeline retires in June 2027.

Top 3 Risks

01
Entyvio patent defence. Biosimilar applications are filed in the US and Europe. An early entry would cut ¥958B of annual sales at the same moment the launches are still small.
02
Launch execution. Orzeyful must clear DEA scheduling before US sales start, the rusfertide (Mimrylo) launch competes for haematologists' attention, and zasocitinib still needs FDA approval in Q1 2027. FY2026 core profit guidance already assumes heavy launch spending.
03
AMITIZA judgment and leverage. The judgment is expected in the second half of 2026. A trebled award paid before the appeal is decided would slow the move from 2.7x toward the 2x leverage target. In May the company named the adjusted free cash flow forecast as the figure that could move.
Industry Commentary
Biosimilars come for the big antibodies
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Once biosimilar makers file against an antibody drug, the defence moves from the difficulty of copying it to the patent courts. Takeda says it planned for the vedolizumab filings and is confident in its patents, and its deck says any copy launching before 2032 must get past them. Alongside the legal defence it is pushing Entyvio further toward under-the-skin dosing, through the Entyvio pen and the Halozyme ENHANZE licence.

Japanese drugmakers and the weak yen
DOUBLE-EDGED

Takeda earns most of its revenue abroad and reports in yen, so a weak yen lifts reported revenue and profit. In Q1 FY2026 it turned a 0.5% constant-currency decline into a 10.2% increase. The same currency move cuts both ways for investors. Tokyo shareholders saw the shares rise 76% in five years. Dollar-based ADS holders saw 27%. The FY2026 plan assumes ¥156 to the dollar, close to the October 2 rate of ¥157.93, so currency is not currently a big source of upside or downside against the guide.

First-in-class drugs from Japanese labs
EMERGING TAILWIND

Orzeyful is the first approved drug to treat narcolepsy type 1 by replacing the missing orexin signal rather than managing symptoms. On the Japanese approval Julie Kim called it "a successful representation of Japan-originated science". The bull framing: a new drug class with a follow-on (TAK-360 for narcolepsy type 2) can become a franchise, not just a product. The bear framing: the first approved indication is a rare disease. The label lists insomnia and urinary frequency as common side effects. A controlled-substance classification is still pending. How to judge it: US prescription uptake in the first two quarters after DEA scheduling.
