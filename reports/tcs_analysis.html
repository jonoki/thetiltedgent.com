01 Company Overview

Tata Consultancy Services is India's largest IT services company by revenue and market value. Tata Sons founded it in 1968 as Tata Computer Systems, and it listed in August 2004; Tata Sons still owns 71.74% of it. In the fiscal year to March 31, 2026 it booked ₹2,67,021 crore of revenue ($30.02B): up 4.6% in rupees, down 0.5% in dollars and down 2.4% in constant currency. Its operating margin was 25.0% before one-off charges, and it paid out ₹110 a share in dividends on ₹136.01 of reported earnings per share. North America supplies 48.3% of revenue, the UK 17.2% and continental Europe 15.4% (Q1 FY27); India itself is 6.2%. It had 593,798 people on the payroll at June 30.

The business has stopped shrinking; the share price has not stopped falling. At ₹2,075.00 the stock is 45.0% below its September 2021 close and 35.3% down since the end of 2025, while the NIFTY 50 is up 27.3% over the same five years. Two things are being priced. The first is AI: clients ask for the savings that AI tools make possible, and CEO K Krithivasan put a number on it at the July 9 results call: "One quantification I can give you is in most places, the productivity gain passed on is around 10% to 15% range." The second is the parent: since August the leadership of Tata Sons has been contested between its board and Tata Trusts, its majority owner. The question for an owner: is 15.1x trailing earnings, with a 5.3% dividend yield and a 24% operating margin, cheap for a business that just signed $9.5B of contracts in a quarter, or fair for one whose revenue barely grows in constant currency?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale: 593,798 people, 55 countries Industry-leading margins Run-the-business switching costs No borrowings, $5.3B invested funds Tata brand and group ties AI partnerships (Anthropic, Mistral, Google Cloud)

TCS's advantage is cost, embeddedness and discipline at scale. A bank that has run its back office on TCS people and software for decades would have to rebuild that knowledge to leave. The result shows in the numbers: a 25.0% operating margin in FY26 before one-offs, about seven and a half points above the TTM median of Infosys, HCLTech and Wipro, a 48.7% return on equity, no borrowings, and ₹48,016 crore of free cash flow in FY26, most of it paid out (₹39,437 crore of dividends). The CFO restated the policy in July: "Our capital allocation policy remains unchanged, and we remain committed to returning substantial free cash flows to our shareholders while judiciously investing to support our long-term growth aspirations." The vulnerability is that embeddedness protects the relationship, not the price per hour.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
AI hands savings back to clients. The CEO's rule of thumb is a 10%–15% productivity gain passed on when AI is used on an engagement, recovered, he says, through extra work from the same clients. Revenue is people × hours × rate; AI attacks the hours. Constant-currency revenue fell 2.4% in FY26 and grew 0.4% quarter on quarter in Q1.
02
The parent is in a fight. Tata Sons owns 71.74% of TCS. Its board reappointed N Chandrasekaran on September 17 over the vote of Tata Trusts' chairman, the Trusts called the resolution illegal, and the Reserve Bank of India's listing requirement for Tata Sons is unresolved. The direction of TCS's controlling shareholder is not settled.
03
Buying growth is new for TCS. Coastal Cloud and ListEngage, both acquired in FY26, then MHP (€320M, pending) and the Best Buy centre: Systematix called MHP TCS's third acquisition in twelve months, after a long period of limited M&A, and expects it to be mildly margin-dilutive at first; MHP's revenue fell 11% in 2025.
04
Legal and conduct costs. The US Supreme Court denied TCS's petition in the Computer Sciences Corporation trade-secrets case on June 15, 2026. TCS had provided $150 million in FY26 and took a further $70 million exceptional charge (₹668 crore) plus $7 million of interest in Q1 FY27.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the comparisons. First, FY26 exceptional charges of ₹4,526 crore: ₹1,388 crore of restructuring, ₹2,128 crore for India's new Labour Codes and ₹1,010 crore for the CSC legal claim, which is why reported FY26 EPS (₹136.01) sits well below the ₹145.99 before one-offs. Second, the CSC case closed in June: after the Supreme Court denied review, Q1 FY27 carried a further ₹668 crore exceptional charge, so reported Q1 profit of ₹13,349 crore compares with ₹13,849 crore before it. Third, the rupee: it weakened from about ₹74 per dollar in 2021 to ₹95.93, so rupee growth (+13.9% in Q1) runs far ahead of constant-currency growth (+3.2%). For a dollar-based owner, the dollar line (+2.7%) is the one that pays.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 close of ₹3,775.55 to the October 1, 2026 close of ₹2,075.00, TCS lost 45.0% of its price. Adding back the ₹472 a share of dividends paid over the period (Yahoo's dividend record, without reinvestment) brings the loss to about 32.5%. Over the same window the NIFTY 50 rose 27.3% and the NIFTY IT index fell 19.2%, so TCS lagged both its home market and its own sector. For a dollar-based holder it was worse: at ₹74.32 and ₹95.93 per dollar, the share went from $50.80 to $21.63, a 57.4% fall.

The 6-period monthly RSI fell to 17.3 at the June 2026 close, its lowest reading in the series, after sitting below 30 at every month-end from February to June. July's rally lifted it to 37.8; it stands at 30.7 now, back at the oversold line. The 10-month moving average, at ₹2,377.50, sits 14.6% above the price and has been lower at every point since February 2025: the long trend is still down.

Relative performance

Over five years (September 30, 2021 → October 1, 2026, price only): TCS −45.0%, NIFTY 50 +27.3%, NIFTY IT −19.2%. Over one year: TCS −28.8%, NIFTY 50 −9.7%, NIFTY IT −16.5%. The Indian market has itself been weak this year, and the whole IT sector with it, but TCS has fallen further than its sector index. On valuation it is not an outlier: at 15.1x trailing earnings it sits between Infosys (13.5x) and HCLTech (18.8x), with a higher operating margin and a higher trailing dividend yield than any of its three listed peers. The market is pricing the labour-based IT services group as one trade, and TCS's flat constant-currency growth gives it little room to argue for a premium.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 15.1x reported and 13.9x pre-exceptional trailing earnings, and 13.4x forward, for a business more profitable than its large Indian peers: a 24%–25% operating margin, a 48.7% return on equity, no borrowings, and a 5.3% trailing dividend yield backed by a 6.4% free-cash-flow yield. The order book is not shrinking — $40.7B in FY26 and $9.5B in Q1, with an $800 million net-new SKF deal — and TCS is still hiring. The CEO expects demand to improve in Q2, the CFO targets a 25%-plus exit margin, and MHP plus the €1.25B Porsche contract give manufacturing a visible growth anchor. The share has already fallen 54% from its 2024 peak and sits near its RSI floor; an in-line Q2 on October 8 could be enough.

Bear Case

Constant-currency revenue fell 2.4% in FY26 and grew 0.4% sequentially in Q1; the rupee's slide flatters the reported numbers. The CEO himself puts AI's give-back at 10%–15% per engagement, and CLSA expects AI work to outrun that deflation only toward FY2030. Citi sees FY27 growth below consensus and a ₹1,875 target, two brokers downgraded in August, and the consensus targets on screens predate those moves. The controlling shareholder is in open dispute over its chairman and a possible listing, and TCS is buying growth through acquisitions at a time when its organic engine has stalled. A low multiple on flat growth is how a mature business looks on the way down.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI deflation outruns AI revenue. AI revenue is $2.6B a year and lumpy; the productivity passed back to clients applies to a much larger book of work. If clients bank the savings faster than TCS wins new AI work, revenue per client falls even while headcount rises.
02
Parent-company governance. A contested chairmanship at Tata Sons, an RBI listing requirement and a Trusts' restructuring proposal all sit above a company that is 71.74% owned by Tata Sons. The two biggest Tata Sons headlines, on August 12 and September 17, were each followed by a 3.9% fall in TCS.
03
Demand that does not come back. Management has said demand will improve "sometime in Q2". If consumer and auto clients keep deferring projects, Q2 on October 8 would be a third quarter of near-flat constant-currency growth, and the 25%-plus margin target would have to be reached through cost rather than growth.
Industry Commentary
The productivity give-back meets the effort-priced model
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The CEO's description of renewals on the July call is the clearest account of the problem from inside the industry: "as and when the projects come up for renewal, we find opportunities along with our clients to unearth productivity benefits and pass them on to our customers." TCS's answer is to sell the AI work itself — the Anthropic partnership that puts Claude in front of 50,000 TCS employees, Mistral and Google Cloud — and to price more of it on outcomes. The scoreboard is the gap between the two: $2.6B of annualised AI revenue growing 13.6% a quarter against a ₹2.76 lakh crore TTM revenue base that grew 0.4% quarter on quarter in constant currency in Q1. Citi's late-September sector note expected a fourth straight subdued growth year for Indian IT.

Buying captive centres and consultancies
STRATEGY SHIFT

CLSA lists clients' own captive "global capability centres" (GCCs) among the structural changes weighing on Indian IT. TCS's response this year is to take such centres over: a dedicated Global Value & Innovation Centres unit launched in Q1, and on October 1 the agreement to take over Best Buy's India centre, paid for with a nominal ₹1 lakh but bringing about 450 people and a multi-year contract. At the other end it is buying consulting reach: MHP in Germany and Coastal Cloud. After what Systematix called a long period of limited M&A, that is a change in how TCS buys growth.

Cash returns and data centres
SUPPORT

TCS paid ₹39,437 crore of dividends in FY26 against ₹48,016 crore of free cash flow, and declared ₹110 a share for the year including a ₹46 special. It is also placing a bet outside services: HyperVault, its data-centre subsidiary, in which TPG took 49% in March, secured 264 acres in Hyderabad in September for a campus of up to 1 GW, with HyperVault and its partners expected to invest up to ₹70,000 crore. Moody's raised TCS's local-currency rating to A2 in May, four notches above India's sovereign rating. The balance sheet can carry both the dividend and the new bets; the question is whether either can restart growth.
