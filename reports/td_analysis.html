01 Company Overview

TD is Canada's second-largest listed company by market capitalisation — #2 in the companiesmarketcap Canada ranking, behind Royal Bank of Canada — and roughly #94 in the world, at $204.22B. It runs four businesses: Canadian personal and commercial banking, a US retail bank of 1,048 stores from Maine to Florida, wealth management and insurance, and TD Securities (wholesale). Its home market index is the S&P/TSX Composite; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500 because the line analysed is the NYSE listing in US dollars.

This report is about what a regulator can do to a franchise. On October 10, 2024 TD announced a "Global Resolution" of US investigations into its BSA/AML program: consent orders with the OCC, the Federal Reserve and FinCEN, and plea agreements with the US Department of Justice entered by certain US subsidiaries — about US$3 billion in penalties and a US$434 billion cap on the total assets of its US bank. A bank that grew by acquisition in the US for two decades was told it could not get bigger there. The stock's NYSE line fell 12.6% in October 2024 and closed December 2024 at $53.24, its lowest month-end of the five-year window.

What happened next is the case study. TD sold its entire stake in Charles Schwab in February 2025 (about US$14.6B gross, ~C$20B after tax), used C$8B to authorise the repurchase of up to 100 million shares, shrank and restructured the US balance sheet to create room under the cap, and re-pointed growth at Canada and the wholesale bank. Eighteen months later, reported diluted EPS for Q3 FY2026 (three months to July 31, 2026) was C$2.74 vs C$1.89, reported ROE was 15.8% vs 11.3%, and the NYSE line closed September 21, 2026 at $124.04 — up 133% from the December 2024 low. The market has stopped pricing a penalty box. The open question is whether it has started pricing a US business that is still, legally, not allowed to grow its balance sheet past US$434B — and that on TD's own disclosure sat at about US$375.7B on July 31, 2026.

FX exposure: TD reports in Canadian dollars; the NYSE line trades in US dollars, so a US holder carries CAD/USD risk on the whole earnings stream. The US Banking segment earns in USD (24.7% of Q3 FY2026 revenue); the rest is mostly Canadian-dollar business. All financial statement figures below are in CAD unless labelled US$; price, market cap and per-share valuation figures are in USD.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Canadian big-bank oligopoly Low-cost deposit base CET1 14.3% Aa1 / A+ / AA+ / AA deposit ratings Schwab IDA sweep deposits to 2034 1,037 Canadian + 1,048 US branches Scale in insurance & direct investing

The Canadian franchise is the moat: a 32.3% segment ROE at a 38.6% efficiency ratio is the return profile of a concentrated market with sticky deposits. The US bank has a different moat — a dense store network in the Northeast corridor and the Schwab insured-deposit agreement, under which TD holds Schwab client sweep balances with a US$60B floor through an initial expiry of July 1, 2034. What the 2024 resolution showed is that a moat does not protect against the regulator. A bank's licence to grow is not a property right; it is a permission, and in the US it has been partially withdrawn.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The cap has no scheduled end. Remediation steps must be validated by TD's internal audit (planned for calendar 2026–2027), reviewed by the appointed monitor and ultimately approved by US regulators and the DOJ. TD's own release warns that further remediation actions after calendar 2027 could extend the timeline, and the lookback scope could expand. A cap with an open end date is a growth constraint the market cannot discount to a date.
02
US returns are still sub-par. 10.2% segment ROE against a 13% medium-term target and a 16.0% group adjusted ROE. The US bank absorbs capital at below the group's return, and cannot use balance-sheet growth to close the gap.
03
Canadian credit. Canadian P&C impaired-loan provisions rose to C$446M from C$376M YoY. Group PCLs were 0.37% of average loans in Q3 and 0.41% YTD — benign, but the growth plan leans on the one market where the loss line is rising.
04
The rally has outrun the analysts. At $124.04 the NYSE line trades above the StockAnalysis mean target of $121.61 and at 18.7x trailing reported EPS — TD's own release puts the TSX-line P/E at 18.0x reported, versus 8.6x a year earlier. The re-rating, not the earnings, did most of the work.

Notable One-Off Items

TD's reported numbers across FY2025–FY2026 are dominated by the regulator's aftermath. FY2025 carried a C$8,975M pre-tax gain on the Schwab sale (Corporate segment), C$2,318M of pre-tax US balance-sheet-restructuring losses (nine months to July 2025) and C$496M of restructuring charges — which is why FY2025 reported EPS was C$11.56 and TTM EPS now shows a 20% "decline" that is in fact a clean base replacing a gain. FY2026 year-to-date items are small: C$200M of restructuring charges, a C$197M charge for a change in the partnership share of the US strategic cards portfolio (Q2), a C$288M tax benefit tied to the prior-year Schwab gain (Q2), and recurring amortisation and FHN-hedge items of a few cents per share. Q3 FY2026 reported EPS of C$2.74 vs adjusted C$2.77 — the gap has nearly closed, which is itself the signal that the restructuring phase is ending.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a strong stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $66.15 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $124.04, the NYSE line gained 87.5% on price, before dividends. Almost all of it came in the last 21 months. For three years the stock went nowhere — from a February 2022 month-end high of $80.69 it drifted to $53.24 in December 2024, a 34% drawdown that ended two months after the plea. Since then it has risen 133%, closed 2025 at $94.20, and added another 31.7% in 2026 to date. Part of the USD-line move is currency: the Bank of Canada rate on the close date was 1.4021 CAD per USD; the TSX line closed Q3 FY2026 at C$168.04 versus C$100.92 a year earlier (+66.5%, TD release).

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 88.7 — deep in overbought territory and has been above 85 since April 2026; the series high was 93.5 in December 2025. The oversold readings came long before the plea: the five-year low was 29.9 in May 2023, and the RSI at the December 2024 price low was only 35.2 — the market ground lower rather than capitulating. The 10-month moving average sits at roughly $108.59, well below the price. Momentum is extended; on a monthly chart that describes the trend, it does not time its end.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The regulator took away TD's US growth, and TD turned it into a capital return. Selling Schwab released 247 bps of CET1; the buyback has cut the share count 4.0% in a year; the US bank's ROE has risen from 7.1% to 10.2% while shrinking; and group ROE is back to 15.8% reported with CET1 still at 14.3%. The Canadian bank earns 32% on equity, Wealth 49%, and Wholesale just posted a record. If the US bank reaches its 13% ROE target on roughly the same equity, that is about 27% more US earnings (13 ÷ 10.2 − 1, estimate) without needing a single dollar of balance-sheet room. And ~US$58B of headroom under the cap is enough for organic loan growth and a 100-branch build-out.

Bear Case

The price already assumes the story ends well. At 18.7x trailing EPS, 2.5x book and above the average analyst target, the stock is priced like a clean bank, but the US$434B cap has no end date, the remediation bill just went up (US$500M → ~US$550M for FY2026), the SAR lookback runs into 2027, and TD itself warns the timeline could extend and the lookback scope could widen. The buyback that flattered per-share numbers is finite. Canadian impaired-loan provisions are rising. And the NYSE holder carries the Canadian dollar on top: a regulated-growth bank bought after a 133% run is a bet that nothing else goes wrong.

Top 3 Risks

01
Remediation slips, the cap stays. Relief requires audit validation, monitor review and regulator/DOJ approval; TD discloses that additional actions after calendar 2027 could extend the timeline. Every year the cap persists is a year the US bank competes against peers who can grow.
02
Valuation leaves no margin of safety. The stock trades 2.0% above the mean analyst target and at a monthly RSI of 88.7. A stock re-rated from 8.6x to 18.0x trailing earnings on the TSX line in a year needs execution to continue, not merely to hold.
03
Canadian credit and the CAD. Canadian P&C is the earnings engine and the segment where impaired provisions are rising (C$446M vs C$376M YoY). For a USD holder, every Canadian-dollar profit is translated at 0.71322 today; a weaker loonie cuts the USD value of the same earnings.
Industry Commentary
Regulation as a growth limit, not a fine
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The US$3B in penalties was a one-time hit that TD's capital absorbed. The asset cap is the part that changes the franchise: it converts a US bank that grew through acquisitions into one that can only grow organically inside a fixed box, until regulators decide otherwise. TD's response shows the playbook for a capped bank: shrink low-return assets to create headroom (US average loans −4% YoY, while core loans grew 3%), raise margins on what remains (US NIM 3.47% vs 3.19%), cut structural cost, and send the capital somewhere the cap does not reach — buybacks, Canada and the wholesale bank. The regulator did not only fine TD; it chose where TD would allocate capital for years.

What the Schwab sale and buybacks were worth
QUANTIFIED

Schwab was an equity-method stake: TD booked a share of Schwab's profit (US$196M in US Banking for the first nine months of FY2025, before the sale) without controlling it. Selling it turned a passive earnings stream into ~C$20B of capital: 247 bps of CET1, of which ~131 bps (247 − 116) was earmarked for the C$8B, 100M-share repurchase. The buyback's effect is measurable: 1,707.2M shares a year ago, 1,638.4M now, so each remaining share owns about 4.2% more of the bank (1,707.2 ÷ 1,638.4 − 1). The P&L cost of exiting was equally visible: FY2025 reported earnings carry both the C$8.98B pre-tax gain and C$2.32B of pre-tax balance-sheet-restructuring losses. The net effect on the ratios that matter: adjusted ROE up to 16.0% from 13.2% a year earlier, with CET1 at 14.3%.

Canadian banks re-rated — TD most of all
EMERGING TAILWIND

TD's release shows its TSX-line P/E moving from 8.6x to 18.0x reported (12.8x to 17.2x adjusted) in twelve months, and one-year TSR of 71.9%. Part of that is the sector — the Canadian bank group trades at a premium to its own history — and part is TD-specific: a bank priced for a penalty box is now priced for recovery. The bull framing: the discount was the regulator's; remove the uncertainty and TD deserves a peer multiple, which it now has. The bear framing: the uncertainty has not been removed, only narrowed. The cap is still there; the lookback is still running; the remediation budget is still rising. The market has paid in advance for an outcome that regulators, not TD, will decide.
