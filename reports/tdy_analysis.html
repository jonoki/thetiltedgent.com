01 Company Overview

Teledyne makes the eyes and ears for places people cannot easily go — infrared cameras that see heat through smoke and darkness, image sensors that ride on satellites, instruments that sit on the ocean floor, and electronics inside military and commercial aircraft. It is not one product but a portfolio of niche franchises, assembled largely by acquisition and run for cash. FY2025 revenue was $6.12B, up 7.9%, with GAAP EPS of $18.88 and $1.07B of free cash flow. Trailing twelve months through Q2 2026: $6.37B revenue, $974.8M net income, $20.67 EPS.

The defining move of the last five years was FLIR Systems — the thermal-imaging company Teledyne agreed to buy on January 4, 2021 in a cash-and-stock deal valued at approximately $8.0B ($28.00 cash plus 0.0718 Teledyne shares per FLIR share). It shows up plainly in the numbers: FY2021 revenue grew 49.5%, and Digital Imaging — where FLIR sits — is now more than half the company. The five-year price chart below starts after that deal was struck, so it measures what Teledyne did with FLIR, not the purchase itself. The playbook is now running again: on August 10, 2026 Teledyne agreed to buy Varex Imaging, a maker of X-ray tubes and detectors, for $18.90 a share in cash — about $1.1B including net debt — expected to close in early 2027. The question for the stock is simple to state and hard to answer: at 29x trailing GAAP earnings, how much future acquisition success is already in the price?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Thermal-imaging scale (FLIR) Space-qualified sensors Defense program incumbency ~$5.0B funded backlog Niche instrument franchises Acquisition & integration discipline FCF margin 17.9%

Teledyne's moat is a collection of small ones. Each business tends to be a leading supplier in a market too specialised to attract a giant — a detector for a satellite, a sensor for an underwater vehicle — where the customer qualifies a part once and keeps buying it for the life of the program. That is why gross margin has sat in a tight 42–43% band every year from FY2021 to FY2025 through inflation, a defense budget cycle and tariffs. The second moat is the machine itself: a company that converts ~18% of revenue into free cash flow and redeploys it into more niches. The vulnerability is that the machine needs targets at sensible prices — and every acquisition adds integration risk and intangible amortisation.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Defense-budget dependence. The current growth engine — infrared for unmanned and counter-unmanned systems, defense electronics (+$20.8M in Q2 alone) — runs on government spending. Programs can be delayed, re-scoped or cut, and a continuing resolution in Washington slows orders regardless of demand.
02
Acquisition risk, again. Varex is a ~$1.1B deal into medical and industrial X-ray — a market where Teledyne has been present since the 2011 DALSA acquisition, per Mehrabian. Varex's own revenue and margins were not in the announcement fetched, so whether it dilutes or lifts Teledyne's margin profile is not verified here.
03
Instrumentation margin stall. Sales +5.5%, operating income −0.2% in Q2 2026. One quarter of mix is noise; three would be a trend.
04
Leadership transition. The CEO seat changed hands in April 2025, and the Executive Chairman remains the public face of results and deals. How the capital-allocation culture carries through a generational handover is the long-run question for any acquirer built around one allocator.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Record orders, record sales, record operating profit, ~$5.0B of funded backlog, and guidance raised twice this year. Digital Imaging is growing double digits with operating income up 42% in Q2, driven by infrared for space, unmanned and counter-drone systems — demand that is not going away. Gross margin has held at 42–43% for five years, free cash flow is ~18% of revenue, and net debt fell $436M in six months, leaving room to buy Varex without straining the balance sheet. At ~24x forward adjusted earnings and 25% below the average Street target, a compounder with this record is priced at a discount to its own February high.

Bear Case

At 29x trailing GAAP earnings, a PEG near 3 and a ~4.1% FCF yield, the price already assumes the acquisition machine keeps working. The stock fell after the record Q2 and again after the Varex announcement — the market may be saying that good news is priced in. The growth engine leans on defense budgets, Instrumentation grew sales without growing profit, part of Q2's imaging margin came from tariff refunds, and Varex takes Teledyne deeper into medical X-ray at a price whose margin impact is not yet visible. An 11% slide from the February peak month-end with the stock below its 10-month average says the trend is not on the bulls' side right now.

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation. 29.2x trailing GAAP EPS and 18.7x EV/EBITDA leave little room for a miss. If growth slows toward the 5.6% consensus for 2027 revenue, the multiple can compress even as earnings rise.
02
Acquisition execution. Varex (~$1.1B) needs regulatory and shareholder approval and then integration. Growth by acquisition works until the price paid is too high or the target does not fit — and the results show up years later.
03
Defense and government spending. The fastest-growing parts of the business — infrared for unmanned and counter-unmanned systems, defense electronics — depend on government budgets and program timing that Teledyne does not control.
Industry Commentary
Seeing in the dark is now a defense priority
TAILWIND

Teledyne's own description of where organic growth came from in Q2 2026 — infrared detectors and systems for space, airborne and marine unmanned systems, and counter-unmanned applications — maps directly onto how conflicts are now fought: many small drones, and a need to detect and track them. That is the reason Digital Imaging's operating income rose 42.3% in a quarter when sales rose 12.7%. The open question is duration: defense demand cycles, and the current one is unusually intense.

The serial-acquirer premium
DOUBLE-EDGED

Markets pay up for companies that turn free cash flow into acquisitions and acquisitions into more free cash flow. Teledyne has earned that premium: gross margin steady at 42–43%, operating margin up from 16.1% in FY2021 to 19.6% TTM, and FLIR absorbed without breaking the balance sheet. But the premium is a bet on the next deal as much as the last one. Varex is the next test — and the stock's reaction since August 10 suggests the market is waiting to see the numbers before paying for it.
