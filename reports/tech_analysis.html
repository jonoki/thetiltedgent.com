01 Company Overview

Bio-Techne makes the small, unglamorous ingredients modern biology runs on: reagents, antibodies, assay kits and instruments for research, diagnostics and bioprocessing. In fiscal 2026 (year ended June 30, 2026) it sold $1.215B, down 0.38% on the year, with organic revenue flat. GAAP EPS recovered to $1.16 from $0.46; adjusted EPS was $1.93 against $1.92. Gross margin was 66.24% — the economics of a business that sells milligrams of protein at laboratory prices.

The story, though, is no longer the business. On June 25, 2026 Merck KGaA, Darmstadt, Germany agreed to buy Bio-Techne for $73 per share in cash, an enterprise value of about $11.3B and, per the announcement, a 36% premium to the one-month volume-weighted average price. The stock closed May 2026 at $51.68 and June at $70.65. Since then it has done what deal stocks do: stopped moving. Every close from August 3 to September 21 sat between $71.90 and $72.48. At $72.47 the market is paying 99.3 cents on the dollar for a cheque that arrives, per the companies, in "late 2026 or early 2027". The only questions left are whether and when — the spread is the price of those two questions, and the 5-year chart below shows why the board said yes: the stock that closed October 2021 at $130.91 (split-adjusted) had spent most of the next four years well below $85.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Validated-reagent lock-in 66% gross margin 41% Protein Sciences segment margin GMP-grade materials for cell & gene therapy Brand in research labs Low leverage (D/E 0.14)

The moat is the published methods section. When a paper, an assay or a drug-manufacturing process is validated on a specific reagent, changing it means re-validating the work — which is why a vial of protein carries a two-thirds gross margin and why this business earns 41 cents of segment operating profit on every dollar. The same moat has a ceiling: it protects price, not volume. When research budgets and biotech funding slow, labs run fewer experiments and the reagent line flattens, which is exactly what fiscal 2026 looked like (organic revenue flat). A buyer with a global distribution network — the rationale Merck gave — is a way to push more volume through a moat that already defends its price.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Deal break risk is now the dominant risk. With the stock pinned near $73, the upside is $0.53 and the downside is whatever the business is worth alone. The stock closed at $51.68 the month before the deal. That asymmetry — roughly 0.7% up against a potentially large move down — is the whole trade.
02
Growth stalled before the bid. Revenue grew 18.75% in FY2022, then 2.81%, 1.97%, 5.23% and −0.38% (FY2023–FY2026, StockAnalysis). A standalone Bio-Techne would have to re-accelerate to justify anything like the deal price.
03
Litigation and approvals. A shareholder suit filed September 9, 2026 in Minnesota's Fourth Judicial District sought to enjoin the vote; Bio-Techne said it believes the allegations are without merit and issued supplemental disclosures (as reported by Investing.com, September 14, 2026). Regulatory approvals are still required. Suits of this type are common in cash mergers; the regulatory path is the variable to watch.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — and for a stock pinned to a cash offer, momentum has stopped meaning much.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $121.14 (split-adjusted) to the September 21, 2026 close of $72.47, TECH lost 40.2% of its price. About $1.60 per share of dividends over the period ($0.32 a year, our approximation on the post-split basis) barely changes that. The shape: a peak at $130.91 in October 2021, a 2022 collapse as the pandemic-era research boom unwound, three years chopping between the mid-$50s and low-$80s, a low of $48.40 in May 2025, and then a single month — June 2026, +36.7%, the largest monthly gain in the series — in which the takeover reset the price to just under the offer. Even the $73 deal price is 44% below the October 2021 month-end.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 68.2 — the highest in the series, just under the 70 line — but it is an artefact: three months of flat prices after a one-month jump. Its lowest reading was 19.0 in October 2023, when the stock fell 19.7% in a month. The 10-month moving average sits at about $62.87, far below the price, for the same reason. Read the technicals here as a record of the bid, not a signal.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

A signed $73 cash offer from an investment-grade buyer, approved by Bio-Techne's board and Merck KGaA's relevant corporate bodies, funded from cash and new debt, with the vote on September 23, 2026. At $72.47 you are paid $0.53 (0.73%) to wait for regulatory approval and closing, plus any dividends paid before close (not confirmed in sources). Merck KGaA is buying a business with a 66% gross margin, a 41%-margin protein franchise and a diagnostics segment whose margin nearly doubled to 11.2% — assets a strategic buyer wants. The analyst community has converged on Hold at $73 — a sign the market expects the deal to close.

Bear Case

The payoff is asymmetric. The upside is capped at $0.53; the downside if the deal fails is a return toward standalone value — and the last standalone month-end was $51.68, with revenue flat, organic growth zero and a 62x trailing GAAP multiple. A 0.73% spread (≈1.4–2.7% annualised, depending on close date) is thin compensation for regulatory timing risk, a live disclosure lawsuit, and the tail event of a break. For anyone not running a merger-arbitrage book, the question is not whether Bio-Techne is a good business — it is whether 73 cents on a $72 position is worth tying the capital up for three to six months.

Industry Commentary
Merger arbitrage: the spread is a probability
HOW TO READ THIS STOCK

Once a cash deal is signed, the stock becomes a bet with two outcomes: close and collect $73, or break and fall back toward standalone value. Price it like a poker hand. If the break price were ~$52 (the last pre-deal month-end — a rough stand-in, since the business and market have moved since), the current price of $72.47 implies the market assigns a failure probability of roughly (73.00 − 72.47) ÷ (73.00 − 52) ≈ 2.5%, before any time value of money. That is our illustrative calculation, not a sourced estimate — and it shows why the spread is so tight: the market thinks this one is close to a lock.

Life-science tools: consolidation over growth
STRUCTURAL THEME

Bio-Techne's own numbers tell the sector story: revenue growth of 18.75% in FY2022, then 2.81%, 1.97%, 5.23% and −0.38%. When a whole industry's organic growth slows, the scale players buy the niche franchises — high-margin, well-regarded, too small to grow fast alone. Merck KGaA's stated rationale was to strengthen its position in "multi-omics, spatial biology, cell and gene therapy, precision diagnostics and advanced research tools", with ~EUR 140M of cost synergies by year 3. The buyer is paying for the moat and planning to supply the growth through distribution.

What $73 values the business at
VALUATION CHECK

At $73, Merck pays 62.9x FY2026 GAAP EPS and 37.8x adjusted EPS (our calculation); InsideArbitrage put the deal at 30.28x EBITDA. These are full multiples for a business growing zero organically — the premium is paid for margin quality and what Merck expects to do with it, not for the recent growth record. For TECH holders the valuation debate is over: the board accepted, and the market price has followed the offer.
