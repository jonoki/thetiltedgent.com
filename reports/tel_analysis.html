01 Company Overview

TE Connectivity makes the unglamorous parts that let electricity and data move between two things that must come apart again: connectors, terminals, relays, antennas, heat-shrink, fibre-optic assemblies and sensors. It sells them into cars, trucks, aircraft, factory automation, medical devices, the electrical grid and — increasingly — the inside of an AI data centre. Fiscal 2026 revenue (year ended September 25, 2026) was $19.885B, up 15.2% from $17.262B, or 12% excluding currency and acquisitions (organic), per the company's October 28, 2026 results release. At $61.25B of market value the company ranks #200 in the S&P 500.

The organising idea of this report is content per unit. TE does not primarily sell more things than it did five years ago — global light-vehicle production has been flat to slightly down, and TE's own automotive volumes track it. What it sells is a rising dollar value of connector and sensor content inside each unit shipped. Management quantifies exactly one version of this: automotive revenue growth four to six percentage points above global vehicle production, a spread it guided to for fiscal 2026 and described in July as a longer-term run rate. That is the entire Transportation thesis in one number. The second question is whether the newer, faster version of the same idea — content per server rack, where, on the July 2026 call, management said it was running ahead of the $3B AI cloud revenue target it had set at its Investor Day — is a cycle or a level shift.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Design-in lock at the platform Qualification cost per part Tiny cost, catastrophic failure mode Local-for-local manufacturing Content growth > unit growth Record $22.2B of FY26 orders

The moat is not technological brilliance; it is switching friction at the point of design. A connector is specified into a vehicle platform or a server generation years before volume production, validated for vibration, temperature cycling, corrosion and current density, and then shipped unchanged for the life of the programme. The part costs a few dollars. Requalifying it costs far more than it saves, and a failure — an intermittent contact in a 800-volt battery loom, or a melted power connector in a rack — is a warranty or downtime event out of all proportion to the component price. That asymmetry is why incumbency persists, why price pressure is real but bounded, and why 36.2% gross margins and a 22.0% adjusted operating margin survive in a business that outsiders describe as "commodity plastic and stamped metal." The same logic explains why TE can bolt on adjacent capability by acquisition — the $1.4B Astrodyne TDI deal announced on July 22, 2026, adding over $250M of annual sales in power filters and custom power supplies, which TE then expected to close by the end of calendar 2026, and the $1.6B the company says it has committed to Industrial bolt-ons in optics and power filtering — rather than having to invent it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Half the company is tied to a market that is not growing in units. Transportation was 50.4% of fiscal 2026 segment sales and grew 4.5% organically (7.3% reported). On its July 2026 call management said it expects global vehicle production to be similar in 2027 to 2026. The Transportation thesis therefore rests on a single spread — automotive growth of 4 to 6 points above production, as guided in July. Automotive grew 3.0% organically for fiscal 2026. If the spread compresses because EV mix stalls or Chinese domestic suppliers take share in their home market, the largest segment slows and there is no volume tailwind to hide behind.
02
The AI order book is concentrated and cyclical. DDN sales up 44.2% for the year are spectacular while hyperscaler (the largest cloud companies) capital spending is spectacular. Connectors sit near the end of that chain, which means TE gets the upside late and would get the downside late too — but with a backlog built at peak-cycle order rates.
03
Currency and geography move reported profit; they are not a footnote. Fiscal 2026 sales grew 15% reported but 12% organic; the three-point gap is currency plus acquired sales, which reverse or fade. A large majority of sales are outside the United States, manufacturing is deliberately local-for-local, and the euro, renminbi and Japanese yen all move the reported line. In the September quarter reported growth (12%) was below organic (13%), with an $87M reconciling item for tariff refunds the company expects to return to customers.
04
The tax line has been volatile. FY2025 carried a GAAP effective tax rate of 42.5% against an adjusted 22.7%, which is why GAAP EPS was $6.16 while adjusted EPS was $8.76. In the March 2025 quarter GAAP net income collapsed to $13M ($0.04 per share) on $802M of operating income. Fiscal 2026 swung back: GAAP EPS of $11.07 (+80%) against adjusted $11.52 (+24%), on income tax of $695M on $3,950M of pre-tax income (17.6%, our calculation from the release's statements). Anyone building a model off GAAP earnings across the redomicile period will get nonsense; anyone building it off adjusted earnings is trusting management's definition of "one-off."

Notable one-off items. Three distortions matter. First, tax. FY2025 GAAP earnings of $1,842M ($6.16 per share) were struck after a 42.5% GAAP effective tax rate versus 22.7% adjusted — the difference between GAAP EPS of $6.16 and record adjusted EPS of $8.76. The March 2025 quarter is the extreme case: $802M of operating income produced $13M of net income and $0.04 of EPS. Second, the mirror image in FY2024: net income of $3,193M exceeded operating income, which is only possible with a large net tax benefit ($397M, a negative 14.2% effective rate, per the fiscal 2025 10-K) — the year before TE completed its move from Switzerland to Ireland, on September 30, 2024, days into fiscal 2025. Third, the segment restatement: Communications Solutions was absorbed into Industrial Solutions, inflating Industrial's FY2025 reported growth (+23.7%) six points above organic (+17.6%). Taken together, TEL's GAAP earnings line is close to uninformative across FY2024–FY2025 and the free cash flow line is the one to anchor on.

On the Ireland redomicile and the tax rate specifically. TE moved its place of incorporation from Switzerland to Ireland, completing the move on September 30, 2024, and now reports as TE Connectivity plc with registered offices in Galway. What the disclosures show is a settled adjusted effective tax rate in the low twenties — 22.7% for FY2025, 24.6% in the June 2025 quarter, 23.1% in the June 2026 quarter and 21.1% in the September 2026 quarter (GAAP 16.3%, helped by a $59M benefit from a lower valuation allowance) — and CFO Heath Mitts said on the July 2026 call: "we expect our cash tax rate to remain well below our adjusted effective tax rate." What can be said from the accounts is that the year before the move (FY2024) produced a very large one-time tax benefit and the move year (FY2025) a very large one-time charge, and that the rate now looks stable and unremarkable.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen a lot relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means the opposite ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value — a great business can sit oversold for months.

The five-year trajectory, in one line: $153.93 at the November 2021 close to $213.03 on October 30, 2026 — a price gain of 38.4%, or roughly 6.8% a year compounded over 59 months, before dividends. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a real AI beneficiary at 15.6× fiscal 2027 earnings estimates while the acknowledged AI beneficiary trades at about 21.9× its 2027 estimates. The evidence is disclosed, not inferred: management saying on the July call that it is running ahead of its $3B AI cloud revenue target, digital data networks sales up 44.2% in fiscal 2026, and orders of $6.1B in the September quarter, up 29%. Industrial's adjusted operating margin has already overtaken Transportation's (22.4% vs 21.0% in the quarter), so the mix shift now adds to group margin. Meanwhile $3.41B of free cash flow funds a $1.4B acquisition, a covered 1.46% dividend and $1.9B of fiscal 2026 buybacks, and the auto half needs only flat production plus its disclosed 4–6 points of content growth to keep contributing.

Bear Case

The discount to Amphenol exists for a reason: it has $33.05B of trailing revenue against TE's $19.885B and a market value more than three times as large. Of TE's 15% reported growth, three points are currency and acquired sales. Over half the company is still bolted to a vehicle market management expects to be flat again in 2027, and the entire Transportation case rests on one self-reported spread that TE does not support with any disclosed dollar-content-per-vehicle figure. The AI order book was built at peak hyperscaler capital spending and connectors sit late in that chain. And the stock has told you what it thinks: a beat-and-guide-up quarter in October was met with a 1.82% fall on the day, leaving TEL 13.8% below its price a year ago.

Top 3 Risks

01
Content-outperformance compression. If the 4–6 point automotive spread narrows — slower EV mix, Chinese domestic suppliers winning content in China, or platform price-downs — about half of revenue (Transportation, 50.4% of fiscal 2026 segment sales) stops growing while production is flat, with no volume offset available.
02
An AI capital-spending digestion phase. DDN sales up 44.2% for the year are an extraordinary rate that a backlog is now built on. Component suppliers see order cancellations and push-outs late in a downturn, after the shares' price-to-earnings multiple has already fallen.
03
Currency and tax reversal. Three points of fiscal 2026 reported growth (15% reported against 12% organic) came from currency and acquired sales and fade or reverse with the dollar. Separately, the adjusted effective tax rate has run 21.1–24.6% across recent periods with enormous GAAP volatility around the Ireland redomicile; a settled rate at the higher end of that band costs real EPS.
Industry Commentary
Global light-vehicle production is not the growth engine — and management has stopped pretending it is
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The volume backdrop is flat to slightly down and expected to stay there. On the July 2026 call management said production is "staying exactly where we thought" — slightly down for the year — and that it expects similar global vehicle production levels in 2027. Against that, TE's automotive revenue grew 5.9% reported and 3.0% organic in fiscal 2026 and 7.0% reported and 5.9% organic in the September quarter. The gap to production is content. That is the mechanism the picks-and-shovels framing describes, and it is genuinely working — but note the honest limit of the disclosure: TE quantifies the spread, not the dollars. Investors are asked to take 4–6 points on trust, refreshed each quarter.

The AI data centre has become the fastest-growing part of a 1941-vintage connector company
EMERGING TAILWIND

Two years ago Industrial Solutions was the slower half of TE. In fiscal 2026 it grew 25.8% (20.8% organic) to $9.903B, within $166M of Transportation's $10.069B, and earned a higher adjusted operating margin (22.6% vs 21.2%); in the September quarter it was the larger segment ($2.799B vs $2.600B). Digital data networks grew 44.2% for the year; energy grew 40.4% (18.1% organic). On the July call management said it was running ahead of its $3B AI cloud revenue target. The nuance worth holding onto: about a third of that AI revenue is power connectivity, not signal — which ties TE's AI exposure to rack power density and grid capacity as much as to interconnect bandwidth, and is precisely what the Industrial bolt-ons are buying more of: TE says it has committed $1.6B to them, in optics and power filtering, and the $1.4B Astrodyne TDI purchase is a power-filtering business.

The gap to Amphenol — bull and bear framed
ONGOING HEADWIND

Everything above is true simultaneously, and the market has resolved it by paying TE 15.6× forward earnings against Amphenol's about 21.9× (both on 2027 consensus estimates). The bull reading: that gap is a mispricing born of a stale mental model. TE is now a 50/50 company with the faster half growing above 20% and carrying the better margin; a mix that keeps shifting mechanically closes the multiple gap, and a buyback shrinks the share count while it happens. Consensus agrees, with an average target of $258.37 (+21.3%) and no sell ratings in the stockanalysis.com count. The bear reading: the gap is the market pricing exactly what it can see — half the revenue in a flat end market, three points of the reported growth from currency and acquisitions, and an October quarter that beat guidance and was answered with a 1.82% fall. The resolution is empirical and dated: the late-January fiscal Q1 print, against a guide of sales up 15% and adjusted EPS up 21%, where AI sales either step up again or turn out to have been a spike.
