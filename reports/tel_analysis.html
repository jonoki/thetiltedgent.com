01 Company Overview

TE Connectivity makes the unglamorous parts that let electricity and data move between two things that must come apart again: connectors, terminals, relays, antennas, heat-shrink, fibre-optic assemblies and sensors. It sells them into cars, trucks, aircraft, factory automation, medical devices, the electrical grid and — increasingly — the inside of an AI data centre. Fiscal 2025 revenue (year ended late September) was $17.26B, up 8.9%; the TTM figure through the June 2026 quarter is $19.32B, up 16.5%. At $60.41B of market value the company ranks roughly #419 globally.

The organising idea of this report is content per unit. TE does not primarily sell more things than it did five years ago — global light-vehicle production has been flat to slightly down, and TE's own automotive volumes track it. What it sells is a rising dollar value of connector and sensor content inside each unit shipped. Management quantifies exactly one version of this: automotive revenue growth four to six percentage points above global vehicle production, a spread it has now guided to for fiscal 2026 and describes as a longer-term run rate. That is the entire Transportation thesis in one number. The second question is whether the newer, faster version of the same idea — content per server rack, where the AI build-out has taken TE's data-centre business from a rounding error to a $3B annualised revenue run rate a full year ahead of plan — is a cycle or a level shift.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Design-in lock at the platform Qualification cost per part Tiny cost, catastrophic failure mode ~$500K median part revenue, 500K+ SKUs Local-for-local manufacturing Content growth > unit growth Record $5.7B order book

The moat is not technological brilliance; it is switching friction at the point of design. A connector is specified into a vehicle platform or a server generation years before volume production, validated for vibration, temperature cycling, corrosion and current density, and then shipped unchanged for the life of the programme. The part costs a few dollars. Requalifying it costs far more than it saves, and a failure — an intermittent contact in a 800-volt battery loom, or a melted power connector in a rack — is a warranty or downtime event out of all proportion to the component price. That asymmetry is why incumbency persists, why price pressure is real but bounded, and why 36.1% gross margins and a 21.9% adjusted operating margin survive in a business that outsiders describe as "commodity plastic and stamped metal." The same logic explains why TE can bolt on adjacent capability by acquisition — the $1.4B Astrodyne TDI deal announced with the June quarter, adding over $250M of annual sales in power filters and custom power supplies, expected to close by the end of calendar 2026 — rather than having to invent it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Half the company is tied to a market that is not growing. Transportation was 54.4% of FY2025 revenue and shrank 1.0%. Management expects global vehicle production to be similar in 2027 to 2026. The entire Transportation thesis therefore rests on a single disclosed spread — 4 to 6 points of content outperformance. If that spread compresses to 2 points because EV mix stalls or Chinese domestic suppliers take share in their home market, the largest segment goes ex-growth and there is no volume tailwind to hide behind.
02
The AI order book is concentrated and reflexive. DDN orders up 70%+ year to date and half of Industrial's order growth is spectacular while hyperscaler capital expenditure is spectacular. Connectors sit near the end of that chain, which means TE gets the upside late and would get the downside late too — but with a backlog built at peak-cycle order rates. Amphenol's 54% revenue growth in the same market is also evidence that TE is not the preferred share gainer here.
03
Currency and geography are a live P&L variable, not a footnote. The gap between reported and organic growth was roughly two points in the June 2026 quarter (14% vs 12%) — a foreign-exchange tailwind, and one that reverses. A large majority of sales are outside the United States, manufacturing is deliberately local-for-local, and the euro, renminbi and Japanese yen all move the reported line. Investors comparing TE's reported growth to a US-centric peer are partly comparing dollar weakness.
04
The tax line has been volatile and is not fully settled. FY2025 carried a GAAP effective tax rate of 42.5% against an adjusted 22.7%, which is why GAAP EPS was $6.16 while adjusted EPS was a record $8.76. In the March 2025 quarter GAAP net income collapsed to $13M ($0.04 per share) on $802M of operating income. Anyone building a model off GAAP earnings across the redomicile period will get nonsense; anyone building it off adjusted earnings is trusting management's definition of "one-off."

Notable one-off items. Three distortions matter. First, tax. FY2025 GAAP earnings of $1,842M ($6.16 per share) were struck after a 42.5% GAAP effective tax rate versus 22.7% adjusted — the difference between GAAP EPS of $6.16 and record adjusted EPS of $8.76. The March 2025 quarter is the extreme case: $802M of operating income produced $13M of net income and $0.04 of EPS. Second, the mirror image in FY2024: net income of $3,193M exceeded operating income of $2,936M, which is only possible with a large net tax benefit — the fiscal year in which TE completed its redomicile from Switzerland to Ireland. Third, the segment restatement: Communications Solutions was absorbed into Industrial Solutions, inflating Industrial's FY2025 reported growth (+23.7%) six points above organic (+17.6%). Taken together, TEL's GAAP earnings line is close to uninformative across FY2024–FY2025 and the free cash flow line is the one to anchor on.

On the Ireland redomicile and the tax rate specifically. TE moved its place of incorporation from Switzerland to Ireland, completing the move in 2024, and now reports as TE Connectivity plc with registered offices in Galway. What the fetched disclosures show is a settled adjusted effective tax rate in the low twenties — 22.7% for FY2025, 24.6% in the June 2025 quarter, 23.0% in the June 2026 quarter, guided to 22–23% for the September 2026 quarter — with management noting that the cash tax rate "remains well below our adjusted effective tax rate." The pre-move Swiss rate was not sourced from any page fetched for this report, so no before-and-after delta is asserted here. What can be said from the accounts is that the move year produced a very large one-time tax benefit (FY2024) and the year after produced a very large one-time charge (FY2025), and that the run-rate now looks stable and unremarkable.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a real AI beneficiary at 16.7× forward earnings while the acknowledged AI beneficiary trades at 28.2×. The evidence is disclosed, not inferred: a $3B AI revenue run rate reached a full year early, digital-data-network orders up more than 70% year to date, record orders of $5.7B at a 1.1 book-to-bill and an all-time-high backlog. Industrial's adjusted operating margin has already overtaken Transportation's (22.8% vs 21.0%), so the mix shift now adds to group margin. Meanwhile $3.32B of free cash flow funds a $1.4B acquisition, a covered 1.50% dividend and a buyback pace running above $2.6B annualised, and the auto half needs only flat production plus its disclosed 4–6 points of content growth to keep contributing.

Bear Case

The discount to Amphenol exists for a reason: 54.2% revenue growth versus 16.5%, of which about two points of TE's is currency. Over half the company is still bolted to a vehicle market management expects to be flat again in 2027, and the entire Transportation case rests on one self-reported spread that TE does not support with any disclosed dollar-content-per-vehicle figure. The AI order book was built at peak hyperscaler capital spending and connectors sit late in that chain. And the stock has told you what it thinks: a beat-and-raise quarter in July was met with three target cuts and a lower share price, leaving TEL up 1.30% over a year in which its closest comparable rose 51%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Content-outperformance compression. If the 4–6 point automotive spread narrows — slower EV mix, Chinese domestic suppliers winning content in China, or platform price-downs — 54% of revenue goes ex-growth against flat production, with no volume offset available.
02
An AI capital-spending digestion phase. DDN orders up 70%+ and energy revenue up 33% organically are extraordinary rates that a backlog is now built on. Component suppliers see order cancellations and push-outs late in a downturn, after the multiple has already compressed.
03
Currency and tax reversal. Roughly two points of reported growth was foreign exchange in the June quarter and it reverses with the dollar. Separately, the adjusted effective tax rate has run 22.7–24.6% across recent periods with enormous GAAP volatility around the Ireland redomicile; a settled rate at the higher end of that band costs real EPS.
Industry Commentary
Global light-vehicle production is not the growth engine — and management has stopped pretending it is
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The volume backdrop is flat to slightly down and expected to stay there. On the July 2026 call management said production is "staying exactly where we thought" — slightly down for the year — and that it expects similar global vehicle production levels in 2027. Against that, TE's automotive revenue grew 5% reported and 3% organic in the June quarter, and its China automotive business grew 6% against a 2% decline in Chinese production. The entire gap is content. That is the mechanism the picks-and-shovels framing describes, and it is genuinely working — but note the honest limit of the disclosure: TE quantifies the spread, not the dollars. There is no company-published figure for connector content in a battery-electric vehicle versus a combustion one on any source fetched for this report. Investors are asked to take 4–6 points on trust, refreshed each quarter.

The AI data centre has become the fastest-growing part of a 1941-vintage connector company
EMERGING TAILWIND

Two years ago Industrial Solutions was the slower half of TE. In the June 2026 quarter it grew 21.9%, matched Transportation's revenue dollar for dollar at $2.580B, and earned a higher adjusted operating margin (22.8% vs 21.0%). Digital data networks grew 34% with orders up over 70% year to date, contributing about half of the segment's order growth; the energy business grew 33% organically on grid hardening and data-centre electrical infrastructure and is guided to mid-teens growth through fiscal 2027. The $3B AI cloud revenue run rate arrived a year ahead of the fiscal-2027 target. The nuance worth holding onto: about a third of that AI revenue is power connectivity, not signal — which ties TE's AI exposure to rack power density and grid capacity as much as to interconnect bandwidth, and is precisely what the $1.4B Astrodyne TDI acquisition is buying more of.

The re-rating that did not happen — bull and bear framed
ONGOING HEADWIND

Everything above is true simultaneously, and the market has resolved it by paying TE 16.7× forward earnings against Amphenol's 28.2×. The bull reading: that gap is a mispricing born of a stale mental model. TE is now a 50/50 company with the faster half compounding above 20% and carrying the better margin; a mix that keeps shifting mechanically closes the multiple gap, and a $2.6B-annualised buyback shrinks the share count while it happens. The resolution is empirical and dated: the late-October fiscal Q4 print, where the AI run rate either steps up again or reveals itself as a spike, and where the fiscal 2027 vehicle-production and content-spread assumptions get put in writing.
