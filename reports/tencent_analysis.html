01 Company Overview

Tencent is the company behind Weixin (WeChat outside China), the messaging app that doubles as China's everyday operating system — chat, payments, shopping, government services and games in one place — with 1,439 million combined monthly active users at June 30, 2026. Around that network sit a games business (Honour of Kings, Peacekeeper Elite and Delta Force at home; Supercell's titles, PUBG MOBILE and the VALORANT franchise abroad), an advertising business, the Weixin Pay fintech arm and Tencent Cloud. FY2025 revenue was RMB751.8B, up 13.9% from RMB660.3B, with IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB224.8B and diluted EPS of RMB24.153. The shares trade in Hong Kong dollars while the accounts are kept in renminbi, so this page converts where the two meet (RMB1 = HK$1.17022, Yahoo Finance CNYHKD=X, Oct 2 close).

The business is still compounding: Q2 2026 revenue rose 11% to RMB204.8B, domestic games +17%, advertising +22%. Against the LSEG consensus reported by CNBC, revenue of RMB204.78B beat the RMB202.17B expected by 1.3%, while IFRS net profit attributable of RMB56.0B came in 9.4% below the RMB61.82B expected, a figure that includes a RMB10.0B loss from Tencent's share of associates and joint ventures (see Notable One-Off Items). The stock is not. From a HK$663.00 close at the end of September 2025 it has fallen to HK$421.20, down 36.5%, and it closed the banner date 2.5% above its 52-week low. The reason sits in the cash-flow statement. Tencent spent RMB52.8B on capital expenditure in Q2 2026, up 176%, as it stepped up its purchases of computing power for AI, and posted negative free cash flow of RMB13.8B for the quarter; its own measure of operating profit excluding its new AI products was RMB86.1B against RMB75.6B including them, meaning those products cost about RMB10.5B of operating profit in three months. The central question for the stock is whether that spending buys a second growth engine (models, AI agents inside Weixin, cloud demand) or a cost line that the games and ads businesses must carry for years.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Weixin network (1.44B MAU) Payments inside the chat app Game franchises & studios Closed-loop ad targeting Mini Programs ecosystem RMB875B investment portfolio Cash generation (FY25 FCF RMB182.6B)

The moat is the network itself. A Chinese user who leaves Weixin leaves their contacts, their payment history, their work groups and the Mini Programs they use to book a doctor or pay a utility bill. That lock-in lets Tencent sell ads with a lower ad load than rivals, as its FY2025 release puts it, and puts new products — Video Accounts, Mini Shops, now an AI agent — in front of more than a billion users from launch. Games add a second, separate moat: franchises like Honour of Kings run for a decade and throw off cash that funds everything else. The vulnerability is that neither moat protects Tencent from the cost of the AI race, or from decisions made in Beijing and Washington.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
AI spending has turned free cash flow negative. Q2 2026 free cash flow was −RMB13.8B: RMB52.7B of operating cash flow against RMB59.3B of capex payments, RMB5.0B of media content and RMB2.2B of leases. Excluding prepayments for compute, management says it would have been RMB37.6B. FY2025 free cash flow was RMB182.6B. The question is how many quarters look like Q2.
02
The new AI products lose money. Non-IFRS operating profit was RMB75.6B (+9%) in Q2; excluding the new AI products (the Hy models, Yuanbao, CodeBuddy, WorkBuddy and the Xiaowei agent) it was RMB86.1B (+19%). The gap, about RMB10.5B in one quarter, is what the AI push costs at the operating line today, before any revenue it may bring.
03
US–China politics. In January 2025 the US Department of Defense added Tencent to its list of "Chinese military companies"; Tencent called the listing a mistake and said it relates only to US defence procurement. The stock fell 7.3% on January 7, 2025 (HK$409.40 → HK$379.60). By Tencent's account it touches only defence procurement, yet the one-day fall shows how quickly Washington can reprice the shares.
04
The older franchises are flat. Social-network revenue grew 0.8% in Q2, fee-based subscriptions fell 2%, QQ users fell 2%, and international games shrank 0.8% as some Supercell titles faded. Group growth increasingly depends on domestic hits and on ads, and any game released or monetised in mainland China needs a game-publishing licence (ISBN) from the state regulator.

Notable One-Off Items

Tencent's IFRS profit moves with its investment portfolio. In Q2 2026 its share of associates and joint ventures was a loss of RMB9,993M against a RMB4,473M profit a year earlier, while net gains from investments were RMB11,184M against RMB2,638M — which is why IFRS net profit attributable rose only 0.7% while non-IFRS rose 9%. In July 2026 the group sold part of its stake in a listed associate that runs "a leading content community and social platform," which it still accounts for as an associate. On per-share figures, buybacks matter: the weighted diluted share count fell to 9,153M in Q2 2026 from 9,247M a year earlier, and Tencent kept buying in late September at roughly 230,000 shares a day (HKEX next-day disclosure returns). Shareholder payouts in the first half of 2026 totalled about HK$72.3B: HK$24.4B of buybacks plus the HK$47.9B final dividend.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the September 2021 close of HK$425.34 (adjusted) to the October 2, 2026 close of HK$421.20, Tencent's price went almost nowhere: −1.0%, against −2.5% for the Hang Seng Index over the same window, before dividends on either. The last twelve months alone cost 37.7%, three times the Hang Seng's 12.1% fall.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows the swing. It hit 13.4 in October 2022, the most oversold reading in the series, and 89.9 in September 2025, the most overbought. Since February 2026 it has stayed between 24 and 44, and it stands at 31.0, on the edge of oversold. The price sits below its 10-month moving average of roughly HK$471: the trend that began in late 2025 has not turned.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 14.1x trailing IFRS earnings and 12.3x non-IFRS for a business growing revenue 11% with a 58% gross margin, China's dominant messaging network (1.44 billion monthly users), a games portfolio that keeps producing hits (Delta Force, Roco Kingdom: World), an ad business growing 22% on AI targeting, and an investment portfolio worth roughly 27% of the market cap. Excluding the new AI products, operating profit grew 19% in Q2, and revenue beat the LSEG consensus. Management says the compute could earn a decent return at once if rented out, and is choosing to use much of it for its own models and applications instead. If the compute Tencent is buying turns Weixin into the place a billion people use an AI agent — Xiaowei is already in testing — the capex buys Tencent's AI a distribution few rivals can match, and the consensus target of HK$663.96 is 57.6% above the price.

Bear Case

Free cash flow went negative in Q2, capex is up 176%, net cash fell from RMB146.9B to RMB58.2B in a quarter, and the new AI products cost about RMB10.5B of operating profit in three months with no disclosed revenue to show for them. The two big prints of 2026 both drew next-day sell-offs; Q2 IFRS profit came in 9.4% below the LSEG consensus, and Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, UBS and Benchmark all cut their targets afterwards. Management has said more cash may go to capex and less to buybacks. Outside domestic games and ads the franchises are flat. The 1-year price fall of 37.7% is three times the Hang Seng's, and the risks the market discounts — Chinese regulators, US lists, a capex cycle with no end date — are not ones the company controls.

Top 3 Risks

01
An open-ended AI capex cycle. First-half 2026 capex of RMB84.7B already exceeds FY2025's RMB79.2B. If the next prints repeat Q2, free cash flow stays negative and the buyback and dividend come out of the balance sheet rather than earnings. Tencent gives no capex or profit guidance. On the August 12 call President Martin Lau described the compute for its AI-native business as largely a lump sum to be invested this year and next, not a step-up to repeat every year, and James Mitchell said that if compute earns superior returns, Tencent will steer more cash to capex than in the past and potentially less to buybacks. After the print UBS raised its 2026 capex forecast to RMB250B from RMB170B.
02
Regulation at home. Each game needs a state publishing licence before it can be released or monetised in mainland China, and fintech and platform rules are set in Beijing. A tightening cycle would hit the two businesses — domestic games and ads — that currently fund the AI push.
03
Geopolitics and the listing venue. The January 2025 Pentagon listing cost 7.3% in a day. Further US measures, or a weaker yuan against the Hong Kong dollar (reported RMB earnings translate into a HKD share price), move the stock regardless of results.
Industry Commentary
China's AI build-out: who pays for the compute
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Tencent is paying for AI chips, data centres and models at once, and its numbers show the trade-off clearly: core operating profit up 19% excluding new AI products, total non-IFRS operating profit up 9% including them; capex up 176%; a quarter of negative free cash flow that management attributes to "large AI-related prepayments" for compute. Chairman Ma Huateng's argument, in the Q2 release, is that stepping up compute "will enable us to convert usage of our applications and models into revenue going forward." On the results call, Chief Strategy Officer James Mitchell said Tencent could achieve "a decent return in an immediate timeframe" by renting its new compute out to third parties, but is instead putting a very substantial share of it into its own models and AI applications, which he expects to convert into "superior economic returns over the longer term," for example by selling tokens through WorkBuddy. Management also said cloud revenue grew at a low-twenties percentage rate in Q2. The evidence so far is on the advertising line, where better AI targeting is lifting prices. The evidence still missing is direct revenue from the models and agents themselves.

Games: domestic strength, international slowdown
MIXED

China's games market rewards incumbents with franchises that run for years, and Tencent's evergreen titles — games with more than 5 million average daily users on mobile (2 million on PC) and over RMB4B of annual gross receipts, by its own definition — keep hitting records: Delta Force and VALORANT PC reached lifetime-high daily users in Q2 2026. Abroad, growth stalled: international games fell 0.8% in Q2 (up 4% in constant currency) after rising 33% in FY2025, as some Supercell games declined. Miniclip's Arrows – Puzzle Escape, the most-downloaded mobile game globally in Q2 on Sensor Tower data, shows Tencent's studios can still find hits; Tencent says the game is generating robust in-app advertising revenue.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At HK$421.20 the market values Tencent at about HK$3.83T, of which roughly HK$1.02T is the listed and unlisted investment portfolio at the values Tencent reports. The bull framing: strip the portfolio out (about HK$112.63 a share) and the remaining HK$308.57 is about 9.0x TTM non-IFRS EPS of HK$34.29 (our calculation) for 11% revenue growth, with a buyback running daily in late September and 40 of 44 analysts rating it a buy. The bear framing: the portfolio is not cash, its listed values fell from RMB547.1B to RMB487.2B in one quarter, and earnings multiples mean little while free cash flow is negative. The deciding numbers are Q3 capex and free cash flow on November 18.
