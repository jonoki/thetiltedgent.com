01 Company Overview

Tencent is the company behind Weixin (WeChat outside China), the messaging app that doubles as China's everyday operating system — chat, payments, shopping, government services and games in one place — with 1,449 million combined monthly active users at September 30, 2026. Around that network sit a games business (Honour of Kings, Peacekeeper Elite and Delta Force at home; Supercell's titles, PUBG MOBILE and the VALORANT franchise abroad), an advertising business, the Weixin Pay fintech arm and Tencent Cloud. FY2025 revenue was RMB751.8B, up 13.9% from RMB660.3B, with IFRS net profit attributable to shareholders of RMB224.8B and diluted EPS of RMB24.153. The shares trade in Hong Kong dollars while the accounts are kept in renminbi, so this page converts where the two meet (RMB1 = HK$1.17632, Yahoo Finance CNYHKD=X, Nov 20 close).

The core business is still growing, but more slowly, and the AI bill keeps rising. Q3 2026 revenue rose 8% to RMB207.3B, about 1.6% below the roughly RMB210.8B analysts expected (Investing.com), as international games fell 9% against a strong year-earlier quarter; domestic games grew 11%, advertising 19% and cloud revenue 35%. Tencent's own measure of operating profit (non-IFRS, which strips out items such as share-based pay and investment gains) fell 6% to RMB68.1B; excluding its new AI products it rose 8% to RMB83.3B, so those products cost about RMB15.2B of operating profit in the quarter, up from about RMB10.5B in Q2. Capital expenditure was RMB81.4B, more than six times the RMB13.0B of a year earlier, as Tencent kept buying computing power for AI; free cash flow was −RMB69.1B, and the RMB58.2B of net cash Tencent held at June 30 became net debt of RMB13.7B at September 30. The stock has paid for it. It fell 6.5% the session after the November 18 results and closed the banner date at HK$344.00, its lowest close since April 2024, down 48.1% from the HK$663.00 close at the end of September 2025 and 1.3% above the 52-week intraday low it touched the same day. The central question for the stock is whether that spending buys a second growth engine (models, AI agents inside Weixin, cloud demand) or a cost line that the games and ads businesses must carry for years; management said on the results call that cash capex is at its peak and may hover around that level through 2027.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Weixin network (1.45B MAU) Payments inside the chat app Game franchises & studios Closed-loop ad targeting Mini Programs ecosystem RMB903B investment portfolio Cash generation (FY25 FCF RMB182.6B)

The moat is the network itself. A Chinese user who leaves Weixin leaves their contacts, their payment history, their work groups and the Mini Programs they use to book a doctor or pay a utility bill. That lock-in lets Tencent sell ads with a lower ad load than rivals, as its FY2025 release puts it, and puts new products — Video Accounts, Mini Shops, now an AI agent — in front of more than a billion users from launch. Games add a second, separate moat: franchises like Honour of Kings run for a decade and throw off cash that funds everything else. The vulnerability is that neither moat protects Tencent from the cost of the AI race, or from decisions made in Beijing and Washington.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
AI spending has turned free cash flow negative. Q3 2026 free cash flow was −RMB69.1B: RMB2.6B of operating cash flow against RMB65.1B of capex payments, RMB4.6B of media content and RMB2.0B of leases. Operating cash flow was low because Tencent paid large sums up front for computing power; excluding those prepayments, it says free cash flow would have been RMB16.9B. Q2 was −RMB13.8B and FY2025 +RMB182.6B. Management says the prepayments will decline; the question is how fast.
02
The new AI products lose more money each quarter. Non-IFRS operating profit was RMB68.1B (−6%) in Q3; excluding the new AI products (the Hy models, Yuanbao, CodeBuddy, WorkBuddy and the Xiaowei agent) it was RMB83.3B (+8%). The gap, about RMB15.2B in one quarter against about RMB10.5B in Q2, is what the AI push costs at the operating line today, net of the revenue those products bring.
03
US–China politics. In January 2025 the US Department of Defense added Tencent to its list of "Chinese military companies"; Tencent called the listing a mistake and said it relates only to US defence procurement. The stock fell 7.3% on January 7, 2025 (HK$409.40 → HK$379.60). By Tencent's account it touches only defence procurement, yet the one-day fall shows how quickly Washington can reprice the shares.
04
The older franchises are flat or shrinking. Social-network revenue fell 1% in Q3, fee-based subscriptions fell 5% to 253 million, and international games shrank 9% (4% in constant currency) against a year-earlier quarter lifted by some Supercell titles. Group growth increasingly depends on domestic hits and on ads, and any game released or monetised in mainland China needs a game-publishing licence (ISBN) from the state regulator.

Notable One-Off Items

Tencent's IFRS profit moves with its investment portfolio. In Q3 2026 its share of associates and joint ventures was a profit of RMB4,482M against RMB7,854M a year earlier, while net gains from investments were RMB10,057M against RMB2,820M — which is why IFRS net profit attributable rose 0.8% while non-IFRS fell 6%. General and administrative expenses, the line that carries research and development, rose 29% to RMB44.1B. On per-share figures, buybacks matter: Tencent bought back about 15.9 million shares for HK$7.0B in Q3, and HKEX next-day disclosure returns ran until October 15. Shareholder payouts in the first nine months of 2026 totalled about HK$79.3B: HK$31.4B of buybacks plus the HK$47.9B final dividend.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 20, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over the five years from the November 2021 close of HK$423.50 (adjusted) to the November 20, 2026 close of HK$344.00, Tencent's price fell 18.8%, against −4.5% for the Hang Seng Index over the same window, before dividends on either. The endpoints hide a round trip: a fall to HK$195.27 in October 2022 (adjusted), a 240% climb to HK$663.00 in September 2025, and a 48.1% fall since. The last twelve months alone cost 44.6%, more than three times the Hang Seng's 13.3% fall.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows the swing. It hit 12.4 in October 2022, the most oversold reading in the series, and 89.9 in September 2025, the most overbought. Since February 2026 it has stayed between 19 and 44, and it stands at 19.9 on the banner date (a part month), deep in oversold territory and the lowest reading since October 2022. The price sits well below its 10-month moving average of roughly HK$441: the trend that began in late 2025 has not turned.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 11.4x trailing IFRS earnings and 10.1x non-IFRS for a business growing revenue 8% with a 56% gross margin, China's dominant messaging network (1.45 billion monthly users), a games portfolio whose long-running hits keep setting records (Delta Force and VALORANT MOBILE at their highest daily users ever), an ad business growing 19% on AI targeting, cloud revenue up 35%, and an investment portfolio worth roughly 34% of the market cap. Excluding the new AI products, operating profit grew 8% in Q3. Management says cash capex has peaked and that compute prepayments will decline, and CLSA expects free cash flow to turn positive again in Q4. If the compute Tencent is buying turns Weixin into the place a billion people use an AI agent — Xiaowei's rollout is widening — the capex buys Tencent's AI a distribution few rivals can match, and the consensus target of HK$608.71 is 77.0% above the price.

Bear Case

Free cash flow was −RMB69.1B in Q3, capex rose more than sixfold, net cash of RMB58.2B at June 30 turned into net debt of RMB13.7B by September 30, and the new AI products cost about RMB15.2B of operating profit in three months, up from about RMB10.5B in Q2. All three prints of 2026 drew next-day sell-offs (−6.8%, −4.5%, −6.5%); Q3 revenue came in about 1.6% below expectations, and Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, UBS, J.P. Morgan, Citi and CICC were among the brokers that cut their targets afterwards. Management says cash capex may stay around its peak through 2027, and Goldman sees 2027 as the possible low point for earnings per share. Social networks and international games are shrinking. The 1-year price fall of 44.6% is more than three times the Hang Seng's, and the risks the market discounts — Chinese regulators, US lists, a capex cycle with no end date — are not ones the company controls.

Top 3 Risks

01
An open-ended AI capex cycle. Capex for the first nine months of 2026, RMB166.1B, is more than twice FY2025's RMB79.2B, and net cash has turned into net debt. Tencent gives no capex or profit guidance in figures. On the August 12 call President Martin Lau described the compute for its AI-native business as largely a lump sum to be invested this year and next; on the November 18 call management said cash capex is at peak levels and may move up and down around that level through the rest of 2026 and 2027, with 2028 expected to be much better. Morgan Stanley expects about RMB65B of cash capex a quarter until the end of 2027. If that holds, free cash flow depends on the compute prepayments falling away, and the buyback and dividend lean on borrowing.
02
Regulation at home. Each game needs a state publishing licence before it can be released or monetised in mainland China, and fintech and platform rules are set in Beijing. A tightening cycle would hit the two businesses — domestic games and ads — that currently fund the AI push.
03
Geopolitics and the listing venue. The January 2025 Pentagon listing cost 7.3% in a day. Further US measures, or a weaker yuan against the Hong Kong dollar (reported RMB earnings translate into a HKD share price), move the stock regardless of results.
Industry Commentary
China's AI build-out: who pays for the compute
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Tencent is paying for AI chips, data centres and models at once, and its numbers show the trade-off clearly: operating profit up 8% excluding new AI products, total non-IFRS operating profit down 6% including them; capex more than six times its level a year earlier; a second straight quarter of negative free cash flow, which the Q3 release ties to "significant prepayments for compute procurement." Chairman Ma Huateng's Q3 statement points to where the compute is going: "Our cloud services revenue growth rate increased, driven by WorkBuddy and as we allocated more compute to Model as a Service and GPU rental." In August Chief Strategy Officer James Mitchell said Tencent could earn a quick return by renting its compute out but was putting most of it into its own models and AI applications for better long-term returns; Q3 shows more of it going to outside customers, with cloud revenue up 35%, GPU rental revenue up more than 30% on Q2 and Model-as-a-Service revenue more than tripling, per Martin Lau on the call. The evidence so far is on the advertising and cloud lines. Tencent gives growth rates for its AI products, not revenue amounts, so whether the models and agents pay for themselves is still hidden.

Games: domestic strength, international slowdown
MIXED

China's games market rewards incumbents with franchises that run for years, and Tencent's evergreen titles — games with more than 5 million average daily users on mobile (2 million on PC) and over RMB4B of annual gross receipts, by its own definition — keep hitting records: Delta Force and VALORANT MOBILE reached lifetime-high daily users in Q3 2026, and domestic games grew 11%. Abroad, growth has reversed: international games fell 9% in Q3 (4% in constant currency) after rising 33% in FY2025, against a year-earlier quarter lifted by some Supercell games and the launch of Dying Light: The Beast. On the call James Mitchell said the slowdown came before the run-up to GTA VI, a rival blockbuster. In Q2, Miniclip's Arrows – Puzzle Escape was the most-downloaded mobile game globally on Sensor Tower data, a sign Tencent's studios can still find hits.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At HK$344.00 the market values Tencent at about HK$3.13T, of which roughly HK$1.06T is the listed and unlisted investment portfolio at the values Tencent reports. The bull framing: strip the portfolio out (about HK$116.77 a share) and the remaining HK$227.23 is about 6.7x TTM non-IFRS EPS of HK$34.09 (our calculation) for 8% revenue growth, with 39 of 42 analysts rating it a buy. The bear framing: the portfolio is not cash, even though its listed values rose from RMB487.2B to RMB505.4B in Q3; earnings multiples mean little while free cash flow is negative and net cash has become net debt; and Goldman expects earnings per share to bottom only in 2027. The deciding numbers are Q4 capex, free cash flow and the dividend, due with the FY2026 results.
