01 Company Overview

Teradyne designs and sells automated test equipment (ATE — the machines that electrically verify every chip, board and device before it ships) and, through Universal Robots and Mobile Industrial Robots, collaborative and autonomous mobile robots. It is one half of the global ATE duopoly with Japan's Advantest. FY2025 revenue was $3,190M, up 13.1% from $2,820M, but that number is already ancient history: trailing-twelve-month revenue through June 28, 2026 is $4,464M, up 57.9%, and the two most recent quarters printed $1,282M and a record $1,329M — the latter up 103.9% year over year. At $55.82B the company ranks roughly #458 globally by market capitalisation, having risen about 191% in a year.

The debate on this page is a single question with two halves. First: has the AI build-out — testing custom accelerators for hyperscalers, HBM (High Bandwidth Memory — the stacked DRAM bolted onto every AI accelerator) and networking silicon — made Semiconductor Test structurally larger, or is 2026 a cyclical spike that will be given back the way 2022–2023 gave back 2021? Second: is Robotics, a business that shrank to roughly 8% of revenue and absorbed two rounds of layoffs in 2025, worth keeping? The stock's own behaviour — a 5-year monthly-close range of $74.21 to $483.84 and a −26% drawdown from the June 2026 peak in ten weeks — says the market has not decided either.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

ATE DUOPOLY (~90%+ COMBINED) QUALIFICATION LOCK-IN INSTALLED BASE & SOFTWARE APPLE / MOBILE INCUMBENCY MEMORY TEST RECORD RUN NET-CASH BALANCE SHEET DUAL-VENDOR TAILWIND

The moat is qualification, not patents. A chipmaker does not swap testers the way it swaps a supplier of screws: every new device must be "correlated" against the tester — months of engineering to prove the machine's pass/fail decisions match — and every test programme is written against one vendor's instrument set and software. Once a device family is on UltraFLEXplus, the switching cost is a re-qualification of the whole family, which is why the SoC tester market has been a stable two-firm oligopoly for two decades and why share moves in slow, sticky increments. Teradyne's specific pocket of that moat is the mobile and Apple-silicon base: the third-party analysis fetched describes Teradyne as dominating "Apple silicon and the broader mobile/auto base" — which, notably, contradicts the task brief's premise that Apple test share was lost to Advantest years ago; no source fetched supports that premise. The weakness of the moat is symmetrical: Advantest built exactly the same lock-in at Nvidia and AMD during 2023–2025, and gained ten points of SoC share in a single year doing it. The most interesting new development is that the largest compute customers are now pursuing dual-vendor qualification — deliberately paying the switching cost to avoid depending on one tester supplier — which is the only realistic mechanism by which Teradyne gets into GPU and hyperscaler-ASIC sockets at scale.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Advantest owns the AI socket. Advantest's SoC tester share rose from ~56% to ~66% in CY2025 (third-party estimate) and its revenue is $7.58B TTM versus Teradyne's $4.46B — it is roughly 1.7× Teradyne's size and qualified at Nvidia, AMD and the merchant-ASIC supply chain. Teradyne's compute growth of ~600% is real but is coming off a small base, and its own CEO guides to flat SoC share. If the AI test TAM ever stops growing, the challenger loses first.
▸
The cycle has not been repealed. FY2022 revenue fell 14.8% and FY2023 fell another 15.2% after the 2021 peak; EPS halved from $5.53 to $2.73. Test equipment demand is a derivative of chip unit volume and complexity, and both have historically been violently cyclical. Management itself guides 2026 as first-half weighted (50–52% of the year) and flags "softness in mobile and compute order timing" in the second half — the Q3 revenue guide of $1.2–1.3B is at best flat sequentially.
▸
Customer concentration. Teradyne has historically had one customer above 10% of revenue (Apple, via its foundry and OSAT partners), and the new hyperscaler wins add further concentration to a handful of programmes. A single delayed tape-out or a cancelled ASIC generation moves quarterly revenue by a nine-figure amount. Specific 2025 concentration percentages were not verified from sources fetched.
▸
Robotics is sub-scale in a market that is commoditising. Universal Robots pioneered cobots and now competes with FANUC, ABB, Techman and a wave of Chinese entrants at lower price points. Five quarters of growth off a restructured base is encouraging, but a $400M-a-year business with undisclosed (historically near-zero) margins sits inside a company whose test business now earns north of a billion dollars a year; the strategic case for keeping it is optionality in factory automation, not current returns.

Notable One-Off Items

Nothing in the reported numbers is a large accounting distortion, but three things shape how the trailing figures should be read.

The TTM straddles two different companies. Trailing revenue of $4,464M is the sum of Q3 2025 ($769M), Q4 2025 ($1,083M), Q1 2026 ($1,282M) and Q2 2026 ($1,329M). The last two quarters annualise to roughly $5.2B; the first two to roughly $3.7B. Every trailing ratio on the table above (P/E, margins, ROIC) is therefore a blend — the run-rate versions are better, and the cyclical-average versions are worse.

Robotics restructuring, FY2025. Approximately 400 employees were impacted across two rounds. The associated charges are excluded from non-GAAP EPS (FY2025 GAAP $3.47 vs non-GAAP $3.96; Q2 2026 GAAP $2.38 vs non-GAAP $2.47). The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is modest — about 4% in the latest quarter — so unlike many technology names, the adjusted numbers here are not hiding a large stock-compensation problem.

MultiLane joint venture (Jan 29, 2026) and Technoprobe stake (~10%). Neither is material to reported earnings yet, but both introduce equity-method or minority-interest lines that will complicate future GAAP comparisons. Terms of the JV were not verified from sources fetched.

Buyback: $702.1M repurchased in FY2025 when the stock averaged well under $150; only $68.7M in Q2 2026 at prices above $350. The company slowed buying as the stock tripled, which is the correct behaviour and also means the buyback will not support the share price at these levels.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Structural or cyclical — the numbers that matter. The case that Semiconductor Test is structurally larger rests on three verified facts: Compute is now 70% of SoC revenue and grew ~600%; memory test has posted three straight quarters above $200M on HBM; and AI-driven revenue is above 60% of the company. The case that it is cyclical rests on three others: management guides Q3 flat-to-down sequentially and 2026 first-half weighted; Teradyne's own CEO says SoC share will be "pretty flat" against an Advantest that gained ten points last year; and the last time revenue peaked (2021), it fell 28% over the following two years. The honest synthesis is that the floor has moved — a company doing $1.2B quarters in a "digestion" period is not the $650M-a-quarter company of mid-2025 — while the ceiling is set by Advantest's incumbency at Nvidia and AMD, not by Teradyne's execution.

Robotics — keep or sell. At $100M a quarter and roughly 8% of revenue, Robotics no longer moves the stock; the question is whether it moves the multiple. Bulls argue that the restructured business, growing 33% with electronics manufacturing and semis as its largest end-market, is now a factory-automation call option that the market prices at nothing. Bears argue that a sub-scale cobot business with undisclosed margins consumes management attention and capital that belongs in the test fight against Advantest. The data point that would settle it — segment operating profit — is not disclosed in the sources fetched. Until it is, Robotics is a rounding error in the valuation and a live governance question.

Valuation. At $357.03 the stock trades at 49× trailing GAAP EPS, 39× 2026 consensus EPS of $9.09, and 31× 2027 consensus of $11.65. Enterprise value is $55.07B, or 69× trailing free cash flow and 36.7× trailing EBITDA. On run-rate earnings (H1 2026 GAAP EPS of $4.91, annualised to ~$9.80) the multiple is about 36×. That is not a cheap semi-cap stock at a cyclical peak, but it is not an absurd one for a business compounding earnings at 129% this year if — and this is the whole bet — 2027 grows another 28% as consensus expects rather than rolling over. The 0.97 PEG is the number to distrust: it divides a fair multiple by a growth rate that cannot recur.

Bull Case

AI has permanently raised the complexity and test-time of every high-end chip, and Teradyne is finally getting into the compute socket: a second hyperscaler correlated, a merchant GPU order shipped, dual-vendor qualification emerging at the largest customers. Memory test is a record run on HBM. Gross margin near 60%, operating margin near 34%, ROIC 38%, and a net-cash balance sheet mean nearly every incremental revenue dollar drops to shareholders. If 2027 consensus of $11.65 EPS is right, the stock is at 31× a year out with the Advantest share fight still ahead of it.

Bear Case

Advantest holds ~66% of SoC testers and is qualified at Nvidia, AMD and the merchant-ASIC chain; Teradyne's CEO guides to flat share. Q3 is guided down sequentially, management calls 2026 first-half weighted and flags compute order-timing softness — the shape of a cycle top, not a structural step. The stock has already fallen 26% from a parabolic June peak, sits below its 50-day average, and trades at 69× free cash flow with a 1.78 beta. The last two times revenue peaked (2021, mid-2024) the stock halved or round-tripped within a year.

Consensus 2027 growth of 21.8% has no room for that.

If Teradyne's hyperscaler and GPU wins stall at "second source" status while Advantest takes the next ASIC generations, Teradyne's compute revenue is a one-time catch-up rather than a growth curve.

  Industry Commentary
The Duopoly Has Tilted Toward Advantest
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The ATE market grew roughly 50% to ~$9.0B in CY2025, and Advantest captured a disproportionate share of the growth: SoC tester share up ten points to ~66%, revenue of $7.5B (+47%), operating margin from 29% to 44%. Its lock is at exactly the customers that matter — Nvidia, AMD, and the merchant-ASIC supply chain. Teradyne's compute revenue growing ~600% off a small base and its "second hyperscaler correlated" milestone are real progress, but the CEO's own framing — flat SoC share — tells you the fight is for the next generation of sockets, not this one. For the sector, the constraint is that test capacity at the accelerator socket is effectively single-sourced, which is why customers are now paying for dual qualification.
Mobile Is Still the Base, and It Is Soft
ONGOING HEADWIND
Beneath the AI headlines, Teradyne's historical franchise is mobile SoC test — the Apple-silicon and smartphone base the third-party analysis fetched still describes as Teradyne's stronghold. That base drove the 2022–2023 revenue decline of 28% when smartphone units fell, and management flagged "softness in mobile" for 2H 2026. The auto and industrial test markets are described as at prior peak levels with China localisation pressure. The point: roughly 30% of SoC revenue is still non-compute, and that 30% is in a flat-to-down market. AI growth has to outrun it.
HBM and Optical Are Making Test a Bigger Slice of the Chip
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two secular shifts favour test intensity regardless of who wins the SoC socket. HBM stacks must be tested at multiple insertions — die, stack, and package — and a single bad die can scrap a multi-thousand-dollar accelerator, so memory makers over-test; Teradyne's memory business has printed three consecutive $200M+ quarters on it. Separately, AI racks carry orders of magnitude more optical transceivers and high-speed boards than conventional servers, which is why Teradyne stood up the MultiLane JV, Omnyx and Photon 100 inside Product Test. Bull framing: test intensity per dollar of silicon is rising structurally, so even a flat-share Teradyne grows with the market. Bear framing: Advantest is also the memory-test leader, and Product Test at $107M a quarter is too small to offset a compute downturn; the tailwind is real but it does not change who wins the biggest sockets.
