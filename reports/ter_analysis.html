01 Company Overview

Teradyne designs and sells automated test equipment (ATE — the machines that electrically verify every chip, board and device before it ships) and, through Universal Robots and Mobile Industrial Robots, collaborative and autonomous mobile robots. It is one half of the global ATE duopoly with Japan's Advantest. FY2025 revenue was $3,190M, up 13.1% from $2,820M, but that number is already history: trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 27, 2026 is $5,000M, up 74.8%, and the last three quarters printed $1,282M, a record $1,329M and $1,306M — the latest up 69.8% year over year. Management puts full-year 2026 revenue at about $5.2B. At the October 29 close the company is worth $46.31B, and the stock is up about 70% in a year.

The debate on this page is a single question with two halves. First: has the AI build-out — testing custom accelerators for hyperscalers, HBM (High Bandwidth Memory — the stacked DRAM bolted onto every AI accelerator) and networking silicon — made Semiconductor Test structurally larger, or is 2026 a cyclical spike that will be given back the way 2022–2023 gave back 2021? Q3 gave both sides something: revenue held at the $1.3B level for a third quarter and memory test set a record, but SoC test fell 20% from Q2 and the Q4 guide calls for a lower gross margin. Second: is Robotics, a business of roughly 7% of revenue that absorbed two rounds of layoffs in 2025 and turned profitable in Q3 2026, worth keeping? The stock's own behaviour — a 5-year monthly-close range of $74.21 to $483.84, and a fall of 39% from the June 2026 month-end close to $296.23 on October 29 — says the market has not decided either.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

ATE DUOPOLY (~90%+ COMBINED) QUALIFICATION LOCK-IN INSTALLED BASE & SOFTWARE APPLE / MOBILE INCUMBENCY MEMORY TEST RECORD RUN NET-CASH BALANCE SHEET DUAL-VENDOR TAILWIND

The moat is qualification, not patents. A chipmaker does not swap testers the way it swaps a supplier of screws: every new device must be "correlated" against the tester — months of engineering to prove the machine's pass/fail decisions match — and every test programme is written against one vendor's instrument set and software. Once a device family is on UltraFLEXplus, the switching cost is a re-qualification of the whole family, which is why the SoC tester market has been a stable two-firm oligopoly for two decades and why share moves in slow, sticky increments. Teradyne's specific pocket of that moat is the mobile and Apple-silicon base: a third-party analysis describes Teradyne as dominating "Apple silicon and the broader mobile/auto base". The weakness of the moat is symmetrical: Advantest built exactly the same lock-in at Nvidia and AMD during 2023–2025, and gained ten points of SoC share in a single year doing it. The most interesting new development is that the largest compute customers are now pursuing dual-vendor qualification — deliberately paying the switching cost to avoid depending on one tester supplier — which is the only realistic mechanism by which Teradyne gets into GPU and hyperscaler-ASIC sockets at scale.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Advantest owns the AI socket. Advantest's SoC tester share rose from ~56% to ~66% in CY2025 (third-party estimate) and its trailing revenue to September 2026 is ¥1.45T, about $9.2B, versus Teradyne's $5.00B — roughly 1.8× Teradyne's size — and it is qualified at Nvidia, AMD and the merchant-ASIC supply chain. Teradyne's compute business slowed from near-600% growth in Q2 to nearly 50% in Q3, and management describes its own share gains in compute as modest. If the market for AI chip testing ever stops growing, the challenger loses first.
▸
The cycle has not been repealed. FY2022 revenue fell 14.8% and FY2023 fell another 15.2% after the 2021 peak; EPS halved from $5.53 to $2.73. Test equipment demand follows chip unit volumes and complexity, and both have historically swung hard. Revenue has now sat at about $1.3B for three quarters ($1,282M, $1,329M, $1,306M), the Q4 guide of $1.25–1.35B keeps it there, and management expects a pause in memory buying after the record Q3 and guides Q4 gross margin down to 57.5–58.5%. A plateau is not a downturn; the next two quarters will show which this is.
▸
Customer concentration. Teradyne's 2025 Form 10-K names no customers but reports one buyer at 19% of revenue and two customers who require the contract chipmakers building their chips to use Teradyne testers at 12% and 10%; the new hyperscaler wins add further concentration to a handful of programmes. A single delayed tape-out or a cancelled ASIC generation moves quarterly revenue by a nine-figure amount.
▸
Robotics is sub-scale in a market that is commoditising. Universal Robots pioneered cobots and now competes with FANUC, ABB, Techman and a wave of Chinese entrants at lower price points. Growth off a restructured base and a profitable Q3 2026 are encouraging, but a business running at about $400M a year sits inside a company whose test business now earns north of a billion dollars a year; the case for keeping it is the chance of a larger factory-automation business later (management points to 2028), not current returns.

Notable One-Off Items

Nothing in the reported numbers is a large accounting distortion, but three things shape how the trailing figures should be read.

The TTM is now all AI-era quarters. Trailing revenue of $5,000M is the sum of Q4 2025 ($1,083M), Q1 2026 ($1,282M), Q2 2026 ($1,329M) and Q3 2026 ($1,306M). The last three sit within $47M of each other, so trailing ratios are close to the current run-rate — which also means there is no weak base quarter left to make next year's growth look easy.

Adjustments are small. Non-GAAP EPS excludes acquired-intangible amortization, amortization of the Technoprobe investment ($7.6M in Q3), ERP-system costs and restructuring (Q3 GAAP $2.17 vs non-GAAP $2.29, a 5.5% gap). Restructuring in Q3 was $2.0M, against $6.6M a year earlier when the Robotics layoffs were running. Stock-based compensation was $21.7M in the quarter. The adjusted numbers here are not hiding a large stock-compensation problem.

Minority interests have arrived. Q3 net income included $5.3M attributable to noncontrolling interests (outside partners' share of businesses Teradyne consolidates), and the September 27 balance sheet carries $49.1M of redeemable noncontrolling interests; neither line existed a year earlier. Together with the MultiLane joint venture (Jan 29, 2026) and the Technoprobe stake, they make GAAP comparisons a little noisier.

Buyback: $702.1M repurchased in FY2025 when the stock averaged well under $150; $173.5M in the first nine months of 2026, $99.3M of it in Q3. The company slowed buying as the stock tripled, so the buyback is a smaller support for the share price at these levels.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 month-end close of $138.24 to the October 29, 2026 close of $296.23, TER returned +114% before dividends — roughly 16% a year compounded — against +68% for the S&P 500 (SPY) and +85% for the Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) over the same dates, also price only. Almost none of it was earned steadily. The stock fell 54% from its December 2021 close ($163.53) to September 2022 ($75.15) as the post-pandemic chip glut hit test demand; it then went essentially nowhere for two and a half years, and printed a lower low of $74.21 in April 2025 during the tariff shock. From that April 2025 close to the June 2026 close of $483.84 the stock rose 552% in fourteen months — the whole of the AI repricing — before falling 39% in the four months since, to $296.23 on October 29. Directionally, TER lagged both the Nasdaq-100 and the S&P 500 badly from late 2021 through spring 2025 and has beaten both by a very wide margin since, so the five-year result is ahead of both benchmarks on the strength of the last six quarters alone.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Structural or cyclical — the numbers that matter. The case that Semiconductor Test is structurally larger rests on three facts: revenue has held near $1.3B for three straight quarters and management guides the same for Q4; memory test hit a record $301M on HBM, with management calling HBM the main driver for 2027; and AI-driven revenue is still above 60% of the company, with two more hyperscalers and a merchant GPU customer lined up for 2027. The case that it is cyclical rests on three others: SoC test fell 20% from Q2 to Q3 as compute growth slowed from near 600% to about 50%; management expects memory to pause after the record quarter and guides Q4 gross margin down; and the last time revenue peaked (2021), it fell 28% over the following two years. The honest synthesis is that the floor has moved — a company doing $1.3B quarters while it waits for the next round of customer ramps is not the $650M-a-quarter company of mid-2025 — while the ceiling is set by Advantest's incumbency at Nvidia and AMD, not by Teradyne's execution.

Robotics — keep or sell. At about $100M a quarter and roughly 7% of revenue, Robotics no longer moves the stock; the question is whether it moves the valuation. Bulls point to Q3: +28% growth, a profitable quarter and about a fifth of the first nine months' segment revenue tied to AI-related demand, which makes it a cheap option on factory automation. Bears argue that a sub-scale cobot business consumes management attention and capital that belongs in the test fight against Advantest, and management itself expects the payoff more in 2028 than in 2027. Robotics is a rounding error in the valuation and a live governance question.

Valuation. At $296.23 the stock trades at 34× trailing GAAP EPS, 31× 2026 consensus EPS of $9.44, and 25× 2027 consensus of $11.90 (both adjusted EPS, stockanalysis.com). Enterprise value is $45.83B, or about 45× trailing free cash flow and 26× trailing EBITDA (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortization; stockanalysis.com). On run-rate earnings (GAAP EPS of $7.08 for the first three quarters of 2026, annualised to ~$9.44) the multiple is about 31×. Between editions the price fell 17% while trailing EPS rose from $7.29 to $8.71, so the trailing multiple dropped from 49× to 34× without the business getting worse. That is not a cheap chip-equipment stock near a cyclical peak, but it is not an absurd one if — and this is the whole bet — 2027 grows another 26% as consensus expects rather than rolling over. The 0.74 PEG is the number to distrust: it divides a fair multiple by a growth rate that cannot recur.

Bull Case

AI has permanently raised the complexity and test-time of every high-end chip, and Teradyne is getting into the compute socket: a second hyperscaler qualified, a third in qualification, a merchant GPU customer adding revenue in 2027. Memory test set a record on HBM, and Product Test grew 56% with more orders than shipments. Gross margin near 59%, a non-GAAP operating margin above 31%, ROIC above 40% and no borrowings mean most extra revenue reaches shareholders. If 2027 consensus of $11.90 EPS is right, the stock is at 25× a year out — cheaper than at the last edition — with the Advantest share fight still ahead of it.

Bear Case

Advantest holds ~66% of SoC testers, is about 1.8× Teradyne's size and is qualified at Nvidia, AMD and the merchant-ASIC chain; Teradyne calls its own compute share gains modest. Revenue has flattened at $1.3B a quarter, SoC test fell 20% in Q3, memory is expected to pause and Q4 gross margin is guided lower — the shape of a cycle top, not a structural step. The stock has fallen 39% from its June month-end close, sits below both its 50- and 200-day averages, and carries a 1.75 beta. In the last cycle the stock fell 54% from its December 2021 month-end close to September 2022, and the mid-2024 rally gave back 28% within four months.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI spending pauses. Test demand lags accelerator unit growth. If hyperscaler capital spending plateaus in 2027, tester orders — already expected to pause in memory after the record Q3 — fall faster than chip revenue does, because customers stop adding capacity before they stop shipping chips. Consensus 2027 revenue growth of 23.8% has no room for that.
02
Losing the share fight. Advantest's gain from 56% to 66% SoC share in one year shows how fast the duopoly can tilt when a new device class arrives. If Teradyne's hyperscaler and GPU wins stall at "second source" status while Advantest takes the next ASIC generations, Teradyne's compute revenue is a one-time catch-up rather than a growth curve.
03
Paying a high multiple at the top of a cycle. Every valuation ratio on this page is computed on peak or near-peak margins (27.5% net, 42% ROIC). Chip-equipment stocks have historically bottomed when the price-to-earnings ratio looked high on depressed earnings and topped when it looked low on peak earnings; a 34× trailing P/E on what may be peak earnings is the wrong side of that pattern, even after the fall since the last edition.
  Industry Commentary
The Duopoly Has Tilted Toward Advantest
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The ATE market grew roughly 50% to ~$9.0B in CY2025, and Advantest captured a disproportionate share of the growth: SoC tester share up ten points to ~66%, revenue of $7.5B (+47%), operating margin from 29% to 44% (Wing VC, May 2026). It kept pulling ahead in 2026: trailing revenue to September of ¥1.45T, up 48.1% (stockanalysis.com). Its lock is at exactly the customers that matter — Nvidia, AMD, and the merchant-ASIC supply chain. Teradyne's second hyperscaler passing qualification is real progress, but compute growth slowed to about 50% in Q3 and management calls its share gains modest — the fight is for the next generation of sockets, mostly in 2027, not this one. For the sector, the constraint is that test capacity at the accelerator socket is effectively single-sourced, which is why customers are now paying for dual qualification.
Mobile Is Still the Base, and It Is Soft
ONGOING HEADWIND
Beneath the AI headlines, Teradyne's historical franchise is mobile SoC test — the Apple-silicon and smartphone base a third-party analysis still describes as Teradyne's stronghold. That base drove the 2022–2023 revenue decline of 28% when smartphone units fell, and on the Q2 call in July management expected mobile softness in the second half of 2026. On the Q3 call the CFO said mobile was up 60% year over year from a low base but still below historical levels, and the CEO said it was underperforming the company's own plan; auto and industrial test was up nearly 90%. AI growth has to carry a mobile business that is still below its old levels.
HBM and Optical Are Making Test a Bigger Slice of the Chip
EMERGING TAILWIND
Two secular shifts favour test intensity regardless of who wins the SoC socket. HBM stacks must be tested at multiple insertions — die, stack, and package — and a single bad die can scrap a multi-thousand-dollar accelerator, so memory makers over-test; Teradyne's memory test revenue reached a record $301M in Q3 2026, up from $212M in Q2. Separately, AI racks carry orders of magnitude more optical transceivers and high-speed boards than conventional servers, which is why Teradyne stood up the MultiLane JV, Omnyx and Photon 100 inside Product Test. Bull framing: test intensity per dollar of silicon is rising structurally, so even a flat-share Teradyne grows with the market. Bear framing: management expects memory buying to slow after the record Q3, and Product Test at $138M a quarter is too small to offset a compute downturn; the tailwind is real but it does not change who wins the biggest sockets.
