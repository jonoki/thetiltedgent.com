01 Company Overview

Target Corporation is a general-merchandise retailer that sells food, household essentials, beauty, apparel, home and hardlines through large-format stores and a digital business fulfilled almost entirely out of those same stores. Fiscal 2025 (the year ended January 31, 2026) revenue was $104.78B, down 1.68% — the third revenue decline in four years — with GAAP EPS of $8.13 and adjusted EPS of $7.57. It ranks #335 globally by market capitalisation at $74.70B — roughly one-eleventh of Walmart and one-fifth of Costco.

The premise of this page has to be corrected before it starts. Target has spent four years as the canonical share-loser in US retail, and the obvious frame is "a low-teens multiple on depressed earnings — value or value trap?" That frame is already stale. The stock closed Friday at $164.44, up 78.06% over 52 weeks, within 3.7% of its 52-week high of $170.75 and 97% above its 52-week low of $83.44. It trades at 17.05× trailing earnings, not a low-teens multiple. The repricing has largely happened. The question that actually matters now is narrower and harder: how much of the recovery in reported earnings is real operating improvement, and how much is a one-time tariff refund the market appears to have capitalised as if it recurs? Section 04 and the normalised-earnings block in Section 08 take that apart numerically.

02 Core Businesses

Target reports as a single operating segment and does not publish revenue by merchandise category, by channel, or for its advertising and marketplace businesses. What it does disclose — and what carries the whole story — is the decomposition of comparable sales into traffic and average ticket. Start there, because traffic is the variable that broke and the variable that has now inflected.

Target's stores are both the demand generator and the warehouse — the overwhelming majority of digital orders are picked, packed or handed over from a store. That is why store traffic is not a legacy metric here: it is the input to everything. The turn is unambiguous in the sequence −3.9% (Q4 FY25) → +4.7% (Q1 FY26) → +2.7% (Q2 FY26). Digital comps of +8.7% in Q2 and +8.9% in Q1 run at roughly three times the store rate, and the engine inside them is same-day: delivery powered by Target Circle 360 (the paid membership that bundles unlimited same-day delivery through the Shipt network with free two-day shipping) grew over 25% in Q2 and over 27% in Q1. Category Revenue

This is the mix question that explains four years of underperformance versus Walmart and Costco, and it is simple. Food, household essentials and beauty are bought on a weekly cadence; home décor, apparel and hardlines are bought when a household feels comfortable. Walmart and Costco carry a far heavier weighting to the first group; Target carries a far heavier weighting to the second. In a cautious consumer environment the frequency retailers hold traffic and the discretionary retailer loses it — which is precisely what the disclosure shows. In Q3 fiscal 2025, Food & Beverage and Hardlines were positive while discretionary categories stayed soft and merchandise sales fell 1.9%. In Q4 fiscal 2025, Food & Beverage, Beauty and Toys grew while the total store comp was −3.9%. Target's fix has been to attack the frequency side directly: price investment concentrated in food, and a beauty assortment overhaul deep enough to be the single largest driver of Q2 traffic alongside food. Target does not disclose category-level revenue, so no shares or dollar figures are estimated here.

The structural asymmetry: Target must win frequency trips it does not naturally own, while defending discretionary trips that only return when consumer confidence does. Walmart and Costco have to do only the second half of that.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
STORE-AS-HUB FULFILMENT OWNED-BRAND PORTFOLIO TARGET CIRCLE 360 MEMBERSHIP SHIPT SAME-DAY NETWORK ROUNDEL RETAIL MEDIA DESIGN & BRAND PARTNERSHIPS SUBURBAN REAL-ESTATE FOOTPRINT

The core advantage is the store, used twice. Target's real estate sits close to the American suburban consumer, which makes it both a destination and a distribution node — the reason a digital comp of +8.7% costs Target far less to serve than a parcel-shipped equivalent, and the reason same-day delivery could grow more than 25–35% in every one of the last four quarters regardless of whether total comps were positive or negative. Layered on top, owned brands give Target margin and differentiation that a pure reseller cannot copy, and Roundel monetises the resulting traffic a second time at near-pure margin. What Target does not have is Walmart's scale in food or Costco's membership-driven price authority — which is why the moat is real but narrower than the two companies it is measured against.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Discretionary mix in a cautious consumer. Home, apparel and hardlines are the categories that fund Target's above-average gross margin and the categories that vanish first when households trade down. The company's own quarterly commentary repeatedly pairs "Food & Beverage positive" with "discretionary soft." Until discretionary demand normalises, Target is structurally disadvantaged against Walmart and Costco in exactly the environment it is currently in.
02
Tariff exposure on owned-brand sourcing. Owned brands are the margin engine and they are imported. The Q2 result contained a 3.7 percentage point gross-margin benefit from tariff refunds and a $1.65 per share EPS benefit; full-year guidance embeds roughly 90 basis points of operating-margin tariff benefit. A refund is the reversal of a cost previously paid — which means the underlying exposure is large enough to move EPS by 40% in a single quarter, in either direction, depending on trade policy Target does not control.
03
Traffic is bought, not yet earned. The Q2 traffic gain came alongside price cuts on roughly 10,000 items and 27% higher capex. Traffic +3.6% with ticket +0.2% is a good problem compared with the alternative, but flat ticket in a mildly inflationary environment means real basket size is shrinking. If price investment is what is buying the visits, the comp is rented rather than owned.
04
Balance-sheet flexibility is thinner than the ROE suggests. Debt-to-equity of 1.08, a current ratio of 0.99 and a quick ratio of 0.26 mean Target funds a $13.2B inventory position with payables and short-term obligations. Free cash flow fell to $2.84B in fiscal 2025 from $4.48B the year before while capex rose — and buybacks were absent in Q1 fiscal 2026. The 26.4% ROE is partly leverage, not purely profitability.

Notable One-Off Items — read this before using any earnings number on this page.

(1) The Q2 tariff refund. Q2 fiscal 2026 GAAP EPS of $4.11 included $1.65 of tariff-refund benefit, and the reported gross-margin rate of 33.7% included 3.7 percentage points from the same source. Strip it and the quarter earned roughly $2.46 — still up about 20% on the prior year's $2.05, but not the 100% the headline showed. Trailing twelve-month EPS of $9.64 becomes roughly $7.99 on the same adjustment, and the trailing P/E moves from 17.05× to about 20.6×. Full-year guidance of $9.90–$10.90 embeds a further ~90bps of operating-margin tariff benefit; management separately quantified the underlying, ex-tariff raise at $0.75 at the midpoint.

(2) Business-transformation charges. Q3 fiscal 2025 carried $120M after-tax of business transformation costs, taking GAAP EPS of $1.51 to adjusted EPS of $1.78. These relate to the Enterprise Acceleration restructuring announced October 24, 2025: 1,800 roles eliminated — 1,000 corporate layoffs plus 800 open positions withdrawn — around 8% of the global headquarters workforce, with leader-level roles cut at three times the rate of individual contributors. Stores and supply chain were untouched. Target explicitly framed the cuts as removing decision layers rather than saving cost.

(3) Comparability of the fiscal calendar. Fiscal 2023 was a 53-week year (ended February 3, 2024). And note the naming trap in third-party data: the year ended January 31, 2026 is Target's fiscal 2025 but appears as "FY2026" on aggregator sites. Every fiscal label on this page uses Target's own convention with the calendar end-date stated.

(4) The boycott and traffic controversy. No source fetched for this report documented a quantified financial effect from the consumer boycott activity reported in 2025. Per this site's standard, it is therefore not covered rather than characterised. What can be said from disclosure is that traffic was negative (−2.2% in Q3 fiscal 2025) and is now positive (+3.6% in Q2 fiscal 2026); Target attributed the recovery to merchandising and price, and did not attribute the decline to any single cause in the releases fetched.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The chain of causation, in order. Target lost sales because it lost traffic, and it lost traffic because its category mix is heavier in discretionary goods than Walmart's or Costco's at exactly the moment households stopped buying discretionary goods. Q3 fiscal 2025 is the cleanest snapshot of the disease: comps −2.7%, transactions −2.2%, ticket −0.5%, with Food & Beverage positive and discretionary soft. The cure has been to buy back the frequency trip — price cuts on roughly 10,000 items weighted to food, 3,000 new beauty items across 60 brands, a 75% reset of home décor accessories — and it worked: Q1 fiscal 2026 comps +5.6% on traffic +4.4%, Q2 comps +3.8% on traffic +3.6%. Traffic swung roughly 5.8 percentage points in nine months. But note the shape of the win: ticket is +0.2%. Target is getting the visits back and not yet the basket.

The margin story, disentangled. Three things are moving the gross-margin line at once and they must be separated. (a) Shrink has genuinely improved — Target cited lower inventory shrink as a margin driver in both Q3 and Q4 of fiscal 2025, and shrink is a real, durable recovery of dollars previously lost. (b) Mix is improving because non-merchandise revenue — Roundel advertising, Target Circle 360 memberships, the Target Plus marketplace — is growing at 20–25% against total sales growth of 5%, and those dollars carry almost no cost of goods. (c) Tariff refunds contributed 3.7 percentage points to the Q2 gross-margin rate, taking it to 33.7% from an underlying rate near 30%. (a) and (b) are the thesis; (c) is noise that looks exactly like the thesis. The Q1 comparison is the more honest one: gross margin 29.0%, up 80bps, with no tariff distortion, driven by supply-chain productivity, higher advertising revenue and lower markdowns. 80 basis points is the real run rate. Inventory, meanwhile, is behaving: $13.2B at Q2 against $12.3B at the start of the year, up roughly 7% while sales grew 5.3% — a mild build, nothing like the 2022 blowout when inventory forced the markdowns that halved earnings.

Cheap or broken? Run the normalisation. The framing this report was commissioned under — a low-teens multiple on depressed earnings — no longer describes the security. Here is the arithmetic, with every assumption labelled.

Step 1 — what you actually pay for trailing earnings. Headline TTM EPS is $9.64, giving 17.05×. Remove the $1.65 Q2 tariff refund and underlying TTM EPS is about $7.99, giving 20.6×. That is the first correction, and it moves the stock from "reasonable" to "full."

To get there Target must add roughly 190 basis points of operating margin on top of the ex-tariff run rate — which, at the 80 basis points a year that Q1 demonstrated cleanly, is a two-and-a-half-year project executed without a discretionary-demand recession, without a tariff step-up on owned-brand sourcing, and without the price investment that bought the traffic having to be repeated. That is not impossible. It is also not a discount. On what the company earns today, ex-tariff, the stock is at 18.6× — a full multiple for a retailer growing 5% with flat basket size and a 4.08% net margin. The value opportunity was at $89.70 in September 2025, when the same franchise, the same ROIC of 13% and the same 55-year dividend record were available at roughly half the price. Buying it now is not a value trade; it is a momentum-and-execution trade with a value pedigree.

Two cross-checks support that reading rather than contradicting it. The average analyst price target of $161.91 sits 1.54% below the traded price — 38 analysts collectively see no upside. And forward P/E of 17.71 exceeds trailing P/E of 17.05, which means the consensus forward earnings base is lower than the trailing one: the street has already stripped the tariff refund out of next year and expects fiscal 2027 to earn less than the last twelve months did.

Bull Case

The operating turn is real and it is the right kind: traffic, not price-driven ticket. Comps went from −2.7% to +5.6% to +3.8% in three quarters, driven by transactions in every merchandise department. Digital compounds at 8.7% and same-day delivery at 25%+ on a store-fulfilled cost base, while Roundel, Circle 360 and Target Plus grow 20–25% at near-pure margin and lift gross margin 80bps a quarter independent of tariffs. Shrink is recovering, inventory is clean at $13.2B, ROIC is 13.19%, and the dividend has grown for 55 straight years. A new CEO with 20 years inside the business has already taken 1,800 roles out. If operating margin walks from ~5.1% toward the 7% Target ran pre-2022, the stock earns $12.40 and trades on 13.3× — and the discretionary categories that have been dead for four years are pure optionality on top.

Bear Case

The stock is up 78% in twelve months, sits 1.5% above the average analyst target, and is priced at 18.6× ex-tariff normalised earnings — not the low-teens the value case requires. The Q2 headline that drove the move was 40% tariff refund: $1.65 of $4.11. Ticket is +0.2%, so real basket size is shrinking and the traffic was bought with price cuts on 10,000 items and 27% higher capex. Store comps already decelerated from +4.7% to +2.7%. Free cash flow fell 36.7% in fiscal 2025 to $2.84B, the current ratio is 0.99, the quick ratio 0.26, buybacks were absent in Q1, and the dividend raise was 1.78% — a streak being preserved, not a signal being sent. Four of five analysts raised targets and only three rated it positively. And the structural problem is untouched: Target still sells more discretionary goods than Walmart or Costco into a consumer that still is not buying them.

Top 3 Risks

01
The tariff benefit does not repeat and the multiple has to absorb it. Fiscal 2026 guidance embeds ~90bps of operating-margin tariff benefit and Q2 alone carried $1.65 of EPS. When it laps, reported EPS must fall unless underlying margin fills the gap at roughly twice the 80bps annual rate demonstrated so far. The forward P/E already sitting above the trailing P/E shows the street knows this; the price does not obviously reflect it.
02
Traffic proves rented rather than earned. Price cuts on ~10,000 items and a 27% capex increase bought +3.6% transactions with +0.2% ticket. If the price investment has to be repeated to hold the traffic, the gross-margin expansion that justifies the re-rating never arrives — and the store comp has already stepped down from +4.7% to +2.7%.
03
Discretionary demand stays where it has been for four years. Every quarter of disclosure fetched for this report pairs positive food and beauty with soft discretionary. Target's mix means it cannot fully re-rate on frequency categories alone — those are the ones Walmart and Costco own. A holiday quarter that repeats fiscal 2025's −3.9% store comp in the discretionary-heavy period would break the recovery narrative at its weakest joint.
  Industry Commentary
Frequency Beats Discretionary in a Cautious Consumer
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
This is the whole reason Target underperformed Walmart and Costco for four years, and it is not a management failure — it is a mix property. Food, essentials and beauty generate weekly trips that survive any consumer mood; home, apparel and hardlines generate occasional trips that do not. Target's disclosure says the same thing every quarter: Food & Beverage and Hardlines positive with discretionary soft (Q3 FY2025), Food, Beauty and Toys growing while the total store comp ran −3.9% (Q4 FY2025). Walmart ($852.62B market cap) and Costco ($406.11B) sit at 11.4× and 5.4× Target's $74.70B partly because the market pays a premium for demand that does not have a mood. Target's response — price cuts weighted to food, 3,000 new beauty items — is an explicit attempt to buy frequency it does not structurally own.
Tariffs Have Become an Earnings Variable, Not a Cost Line
ONGOING HEADWIND
For a retailer whose margin advantage comes from imported owned brands, trade policy is now a bigger swing factor than merchandising. The evidence is in Target's own numbers: a refund event moved the Q2 gross-margin rate by 3.7 percentage points and earnings by $1.65 per share, and full-year operating margin guidance carries roughly 90 basis points of the same. Consider what the symmetry implies — an exposure capable of adding that much can subtract it. The sector-wide consequence is that reported retail earnings have become harder to read, and any multiple applied to a headline retail EPS in this environment is applying a multiple to a policy outcome. This report normalises it out for exactly that reason.
Retail Media and Marketplace Are Where Retail Margin Now Comes From
EMERGING TAILWIND
Across the sector, the incremental profit dollar increasingly comes from selling advertising and marketplace access against traffic the retailer already has, not from selling goods. Target is running that play with Roundel, Target Plus and Circle 360, and non-merchandise revenue growing 20–25% against 5% total sales growth is why gross margin can expand without a single price increase. Bull framing: if that mix shift compounds, Target earns a structurally higher margin than its 2015–2019 self, the 7% operating-margin scenario becomes reachable, and $12.40 of EPS puts the stock on 13.3× — genuine value. Bear framing: Target discloses no Roundel revenue, no Target Plus GMV and no other-revenue dollar total. Investors are asked to accept the highest-margin part of the story entirely on growth rates. Until it is broken out, the checkable number is the one in the metrics table — a 4.08% net margin and 18.6× ex-tariff normalised earnings.
