01 Company Overview
The TJX Companies is the world's largest off-price apparel and home-fashions retailer, selling branded and designer merchandise at a discount to full-price channels through 5,285 stores across the United States, Canada, Europe and Australia under the TJ Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods, Homesense, Sierra, Winners and TK Maxx banners. Fiscal 2026 (ended January 31, 2026) revenue reached $60.37 billion, up 7.1% YoY, on consolidated comparable-store sales growth of 5% and diluted EPS of $4.87. On a trailing-twelve-month basis through the quarter ended August 1, 2026, revenue is $62.36 billion (+7.7%) with net income of $6.07 billion. At the September 21 close the company is worth about $144 billion — some $22 billion less than on August 18, the day before it reported a quarter that beat on every consolidated line.
The business model is deliberately un-glamorous: a large, decentralised organisation of buyers purchases opportunistically — close-outs, cancelled orders, packaway, mid-season overruns — rather than committing months ahead to a planned assortment. That merchandise is flowed to stores in small, rapidly rotating lots, creating the "treasure hunt" that drives repeat traffic without the advertising and markdown machinery full-price retail requires. The structural result shows up in the returns line: ROE of 62.17% and ROIC of 30.13% on a gross margin of only 31.47%. TJX does not earn its returns on price; it earns them on turns.
What the Q2 FY27 print changed. The August 19 report was a portfolio split in two. HomeGoods (+7%), TJX Canada (+6%) and TJX International (+7%) all ran well ahead of the street's comp estimates; Marmaxx — TJ Maxx, Marshalls and Sierra, more than 60% of sales — comped just +1% against a 2.7% estimate and a +6% first quarter. Management called the Marmaxx shortfall a merchandise-mix problem, in Herrman's words on the call, “self inflicted and within our control”, and said the third quarter had started with improvement there. The market decided the core division matters more than the three that beat: the stock closed down 4.2% on the day and kept falling into mid-September. The earnings line was also flattered by a one-off — $331 million of IEEPA tariff refunds, net $219 million pretax after related bonus accruals, worth $0.14 of Q2 EPS.

Vendor Relationship Depth
The moat is a buying network, not a brand. Off-price economics rest on one asymmetry: TJX can absorb, in size and without notice, inventory that a vendor or a full-price retailer needs to move. Doing that requires three things simultaneously — a very large buying organisation with standing relationships across thousands of vendors, a store network big enough to place an odd-lot purchase without wrecking any single store's assortment, and a logistics system that can flow small quantities of many SKUs rather than large quantities of few. TJX has all three at a scale no one else does: its $62.36 billion of trailing revenue is 62.9% of the combined revenue of the three scaled U.S. off-price players (TJX, Ross at $24.51B, Burlington at $12.21B). A vendor that wants to clear a problem in one phone call has essentially one address.
Two consequences follow. First, the model is counter-cyclical on the supply side: when full-price retailers over-order, when a season turns warm, when a department-store chain shrinks or liquidates, TJX's input costs improve. Herrman's Q1 FY27 framing — "excellent buying opportunities," availability "outstanding" — is the model describing its own tailwind. Second, the model is counter-cyclical on the demand side too: a consumer trading down from full price arrives at TJX rather than leaving the category. Very few retailers are levered positively to both the supply and demand consequences of a weak retail environment. That combination is why the market paid a P/E of 29.3 at the August 18 edition for a business with a ~31% gross margin — and why it now pays 24.3: the multiple is paid for durability, and one soft quarter at the largest division was enough to mark it down five turns. Advertising intensity stays low because the merchandise is the marketing; SG&A ran 19.5% of sales in Q1 FY27, essentially flat year-on-year even as comps rose 6% — but 20.3% in Q2, up 0.8 points — 0.6 of that from tariff-refund-related bonus accruals, the remaining 0.2 from what the company described as incremental store wage and payroll costs. At a 4% comp the operating leverage runs the other way.
Key Vulnerabilities

1. Inventory availability is not contractual. The entire model depends on other people's mistakes. If the supply chain tightens structurally — vendors producing closer to demand, better demand-planning software, fewer over-orders, fewer distressed retailers to buy from — the buying advantage compresses. TJX has no long-term supply agreements to fall back on; merchandise margin is re-earned every season. Inventory per store was up 6–7% in Q1 FY27 and 2–3% in Q2, which is a positive read on availability today but says nothing about next year. Q2 added a second, internal version of the same risk: Marmaxx comped +1% while the other three divisions comped +6–7% from the same buying network — management calls it a mix-execution miss, and Jefferies reported Ross outpacing Marshalls and TJ Maxx.

2. Tariff and sourcing-cost exposure is real and two-sided. TJX imports a large share of its merchandise, and tariffs raise landed cost on goods it buys directly. Management's FY27 guidance at Q1 explicitly assumed "a higher cost of fuel will be in place for the remainder of the year," and on the Q2 call, as summarised by Investing.com, it pointed to second-half margin pressure from fuel and freight as first-half tariff benefits anniversary. The Q2 print showed the other side of the ledger too: $331 million of refunds of previously paid IEEPA tariffs. That is a one-off — it lifted reported gross margin by 2.0 points and pretax margin by 1.4 points in the quarter — and reading the reported margins without stripping it out overstates the run-rate.

3. Valuation still assumes the comp comes back. The August 18 edition warned that the multiple left no room for a comp miss; Q2 tested that with a miss at one division, and the stock lost 13.2% while consolidated earnings beat. At 24.3x trailing and 24.2x forward earnings with a PEG of 2.44, TJX is cheaper but still at a premium to the apparel-retail group (est.). Raised FY27 guidance is $5.31–$5.36 GAAP and $5.15–$5.20 excluding tariff refunds — the adjusted range sits below the $5.22 consensus cited by The Motley Fool at the time; Q3 guidance calls for comps of just 2–3%. If Marmaxx does not recover, there is room for the multiple to fall further.

4. Digital and ultra-low-price competition. The treasure-hunt format does not translate cleanly online, and TJX's e-commerce presence is small relative to its store base. Meanwhile Shein and Temu attack the same value-seeking customer at a lower price point with a direct-from-factory cost structure, and Amazon continues to expand branded apparel. None of these replicates the branded-close-out proposition, but they compete for the same discretionary dollar and can cap pricing.

Notable One-Off Items

IEEPA tariff refunds (Q2 FY2027). TJX received $331 million of refunds for a portion of the IEEPA tariffs it had previously paid and accrued $112 million of incremental year-end incentive compensation and discretionary bonuses against it — a net $219 million pretax benefit, $0.14 of EPS. It lifted Q2 gross margin by 2.0 points and pretax margin by 1.4 points; GAAP Q2 EPS of $1.36 compares with adjusted $1.22. It sits inside the $5.40 TTM EPS and the $5.31–$5.36 GAAP guide, so the 24.3x trailing P/E is a little flattering.

Credit-card interchange litigation settlement (FY2026). TJX recorded a $470 million gain from a credit-card interchange litigation settlement, partially offset by $249 million of related expenses, for a net $221 million pretax benefit. This is the single largest distortion in the trailing numbers: GAAP FY26 EPS of $4.87 compares with adjusted EPS of $4.73, and GAAP pretax margin of 12.1% compares with an adjusted 11.7%. Any year-on-year comparison that uses GAAP FY26 as the base is flattered by roughly 14 cents of non-recurring earnings. (Which FY26 quarter booked it, and therefore whether it is still inside the TTM window, was not re-verified at this edition.)

Currency translation and hedging. With 22.7% of revenue outside the U.S. (TJX Canada plus TJX International), reported growth carries a translation component that management routinely strips out via constant-currency comps. Q1 FY27 gross margin expansion of 180 basis points was attributed partly to favourable hedges — a genuine benefit, but a mark-to-market one that does not repeat automatically. Inventory per store was up 7% reported versus 6% constant currency, quantifying the FX drag/benefit at roughly one point in that period.

Fuel and freight cost assumption. FY27 guidance explicitly embeds an assumption of higher fuel costs for the remainder of the year, a negative revision to the margin outlook versus prior expectations. This is a genuine cost headwind rather than an accounting artefact, and it is one of the few items management has pre-flagged against its own raised guidance. Beyond these items, the previous edition found no material write-downs, litigation charges, acquisitions or unusual tax items in the trailing period.

Off-price inventory availability and the department-store cycle
The single most important non-earnings variable. Management characterised buying opportunities as "excellent" and availability of quality branded merchandise as "outstanding" in Q1, with inventory per store up 6% in constant currency; at Q2 it was up 3% in constant currency. Continued contraction and liquidation in full-price and department-store retail directly feeds TJX's merchandise supply and merchandise margin. A reversal — vendors tightening production discipline — would be the earliest warning signal in the model.

2H FY27
Tariffs, landed costs and the fuel assumption
TJX embedded higher fuel costs into its FY27 guidance, a pre-flagged margin headwind, and Q2 brought $331 million of IEEPA tariff refunds — a one-off, not a trend. Tariff policy is the genuinely two-sided variable: higher import duties raise TJX's own landed cost on directly-imported goods, but they compress full-price retailers harder and historically generate exactly the kind of order cancellations and excess inventory the off-price channel monetises. Net effect depends on the pass-through split between vendors, TJX and the shopper.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

TJX is the rare retailer that benefits from both sides of a difficult retail environment — consumers trading down arrive as customers, and struggling full-price retailers arrive as suppliers. That shows up in a 62.17% ROE, a 30.13% ROIC and 7.7% revenue growth on a business already doing $62.4 billion. Q2 beat on consolidated comps, margin and EPS; three of four divisions comped +6–7%; guidance went up twice this year; and management raised the long-term store target to 7,500 with openings accelerating to 4% a year. The one miss is at Marmaxx, which management calls self-inflicted and says was improving as Q3 began. At $130.96 the stock trades at 24.3x trailing earnings, five turns cheaper than a month ago, 23% off its high, with even the lowest street target ($136) above the price and a $169.80 average implying +29.7%.

Bear Case

Marmaxx is 60% of sales and, per Jefferies, nearly 70% of EBIT, and it just comped +1% after +6% — its weakest showing since fiscal 2018 outside the pandemic — while Jefferies has Ross outpacing Marshalls and TJ Maxx. A merchandise-mix miss at the division with the deepest buying bench is exactly the kind of problem that is supposed not to happen at TJX. The headline beat leaned on $0.14 of one-off tariff refunds; the adjusted full-year guide of $5.15–$5.20 sits below the $5.22 consensus cited at the time, Q3 comps are guided to just 2–3%, and Q2 SG&A rose 0.8 points (0.2 excluding refund-related bonus accruals). At 24.3x trailing and a 2.44 PEG the stock is cheaper, not cheap.

Top 3 Risks
01
Marmaxx does not recover. This is the risk the August 18 edition called multiple compression on a decelerating comp — and it has partly happened: the multiple fell from 29.3x to 24.3x on one division's +1% comp. It is still a premium to an estimated ~22x apparel-retail average. Q3 guidance of +2–3% consolidated comps leaves little room; a second quarter of low-single-digit Marmaxx comps would hit estimates as well as the multiple, and would test management's "self-inflicted" diagnosis against Jefferies' concern about competition from Ross.
02
Merchandise availability normalising. Off-price margin is bought, not manufactured. If vendors and full-price retailers tighten production discipline, or if the wave of department-store liquidations exhausts itself, the "outstanding availability" management describes today reverses into a merchandise-margin headwind with a one-to-two-season lag — and there is no contract or backlog to cushion it.
03
Tariff and input-cost pass-through failing. Higher import duties and the company's own assumed higher fuel costs raise landed cost immediately. TJX's value proposition is a price gap versus full-price retail; if it passes costs through it narrows that gap, and if it absorbs them the ~31% underlying gross margin — already thin by design — compresses. Q2's $331 million tariff refund is the reverse of this risk and will not repeat on schedule; management has flagged that first-half tariff benefits anniversary in the second half. The theoretical offset (more distressed inventory from tariff-squeezed competitors) is real but arrives later and is impossible to size in advance.
  Industry Commentary
Consumer Trade-Down as an Off-Price Tailwind
EMERGING TAILWIND
The demand-side driver of the entire cycle. TJX delivered 5% consolidated comps in FY26, 6% in Q1 FY27 and 4% in Q2. Ross Stores grew TTM revenue 14.0% and Burlington 10.9% against TJX's 7.7%, so the channel is still growing — but Q2 is the first sign that some of the growth is a share shift among the three rather than pure channel expansion, at least at Marmaxx: Jefferies has Ross outpacing Marshalls and TJ Maxx. The read-through: a value-seeking shopper is not a weak shopper for off-price, but the trade-down tailwind is not TJX's alone to keep.
Tariffs, Sourcing Costs and Freight
ONGOING HEADWIND
The genuine two-sided variable, and the one most often argued badly. Cost side: TJX imports heavily, so duties raise landed cost on directly-sourced goods, and FY27 guidance explicitly assumes higher fuel costs for the balance of the year — a pre-flagged margin negative against otherwise-raised guidance. Benefit side: tariffs compress full-price retailers with longer lead times and firmer pre-commitments, producing cancelled orders and excess stock that flows to the off-price channel at attractive prices. Q1 FY27 gross margin of 31.3% was up 180bps on better merchandise margin and favourable hedges; Q2 gross margin was 33.4%, or 31.4% (+0.7 points) after stripping out $331 million of IEEPA tariff refunds. The benefit side is currently winning — but hedges expire, refunds are one-offs, and duties do not.
Department-Store Decline as Inventory Supply — Bull vs Bear
STRUCTURAL — DOUBLE-EDGED
Bull framing: every closed department store removes competing capacity and releases branded inventory into the off-price channel. TJX added 129 net stores in FY26 and 71 more in the first half of FY27 to reach 5,285, and now targets 7,500, taking real estate while the full-price channel shrinks. Herrman's "excellent buying opportunities" is this dynamic in management language, and TJX's $62.36B of revenue — 62.9% of the scaled U.S. off-price pool — means it gets first call on the biggest lots.

Bear framing: a shrinking pool of distressed sellers is a depleting resource. Once the legacy department stores are gone, the remaining vendor base is more disciplined, more direct-to-consumer, and better at demand planning — precisely the conditions that shrink the supply of mistakes TJX monetises. The bull case and the bear case are the same trend at different points on its curve; the debate is only about where on the curve we currently sit, and even after the de-rating a 24.3x multiple assumes it is early.
