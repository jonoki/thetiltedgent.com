01 Company Overview

Tokyo Electron (TEL) builds the machines that make chips. It does not design or sell semiconductors; it sells the wafer fab equipment that chipmakers such as memory and logic producers install in their factories, and then services that installed base for years. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (TEL calls it FY2026) it booked ¥2,443.5B of sales, up 0.5%, operating income of ¥624.9B (−10.4%) and net income of ¥574.5B. Then the AI build-out arrived in its order book: the April–June 2026 quarter set records for sales (¥732.4B, +33.3% year on year) and gross profit, with operating income up 46.1% to ¥211.4B and net income up 39.5% to ¥164.3B. Against the consensus compiled by Investing.com, earnings per share of ¥72.27 (per current share) beat the ¥71.43 forecast by 1.2%, while sales came in 3.7% short of the ¥760.35B expected. On July 30 management raised its first-half forecast from ¥1,570B to ¥1,620B of sales and from ¥431B to ¥458B of operating income, and lifted the interim dividend from ¥361 to ¥384 per pre-split share; Kawai said "we are receiving pull-in request for delivery and additional orders from our customers." Almost all of it is sold abroad — 90.2% of FY2026 sales were outside Japan, with China the largest single region at 34.1%.

The stock has priced the turn in loudly. It closed December 2025 at ¥6,864 and June 2026 at ¥15,430 (split-adjusted), then gave back 28% in July in a sector-wide chip selloff, and settled at ¥12,100 on October 2, 2026 — the day after a 5-for-1 split took effect. At that price the market values TEL at about ¥27.4 trillion ($173B), roughly 44.5 times trailing earnings and 35 times what analysts expect for the year to March 2027. The question for the next two quarters is simple to state: does the order book deliver the ¥3 trillion of sales and the roughly ¥1 trillion of operating profit that management now says are within reach, and can TEL lift its margins while it does — or was the June peak the top of the cycle?

Etch tools carve the circuit pattern into the layers on a wafer. TEL's strength is dielectric etch (cutting through insulating layers), where it claims more than 50% share, and the deep, narrow holes of memory: DRAM capacitors, HBM interconnects and the "HARC" (high aspect ratio) holes of 3D NAND. In April TEL guided FY2027 etch sales up more than 25%; on July 30 Kawai said "A full-year sales of etch systems of 1 trillion yen are coming within our reach." Two further hooks: five-year cumulative sales of DRAM interconnect etch to FY2030 are now approaching ¥1 trillion, against the ¥500B or more first guided in October 2025, and a cryogenic etch tool for 400-layer NAND is due in customers' mass production from calendar 2027. The new Miyagi production centre, due in summer 2027, is meant eventually to triple etch capacity.

New-Equip Mix

A coater/developer spreads light-sensitive resist on the wafer before it goes into a lithography scanner and develops the pattern afterwards; the two machines are docked together. Every EUV, immersion or KrF scanner a chipmaker installs needs one, and TEL supplies nine in ten of them. That makes this the closest thing to a toll booth in TEL's portfolio. Management expects sales to rise more than 50% in FY2027, helped by the new LITHIUS Pro DICE model, which it says has won adoption in several advanced logic and DRAM processes. In the call Q&A summary the company attributes the growth to demand across the full spectrum of lithography, from EUV to more general processes.

Sales Mix

Everything sold to a tool after it ships: parts and services (¥135.7B in Q1 FY2027) plus used equipment and upgrades (¥52.3B). TEL ships 4,000 to 6,000 machines a year, so the installed base it services keeps growing, and this revenue tracks how hard customers run their fabs rather than how much they spend on new ones. That makes it the shock absorber of the model. Q1 FY2027 was ¥188.0B, up from ¥141.2B a year earlier; management says leading-edge customers are running their fabs at very high utilisation (Q&A summary) but guides conservatively because used-tool deals are short-lead and hard to forecast.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

91% of coater/developers >50% of dielectric etch (company) Process-of-record lock-in Installed-base service (¥626B) R&D ¥277.8B a year Japanese supply chain No borrowings, 71.7% equity ratio

The moat is the process of record. A chipmaker qualifies a tool for one step of a production flow over months of trial wafers; once it is in, every copy of that fab and every capacity expansion tends to buy the same tool for the same step, and the installed machine then generates service revenue for a decade. TEL's 91% share in coater/developers is the extreme case — the tool sits bolted to the scanner — and the ¥626B of Field Solutions sales is the annuity that follows. The weakness is that the moat protects position within a step, not the volume of steps bought: when customers cut capital budgets, TEL's sales fall with them, as FY2024's 17.1% revenue decline showed.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China and export controls. China was 41.7% of sales in FY2025 and is still guided at the low 30s% for FY2027. Kawai named export controls triggered by the US-China trade conflict among the changes the 2022 medium-term plan did not anticipate. The August 1, 2025 session showed the sensitivity: the day after TEL cut its full-year operating-profit forecast by 21.6%, with Chinese spending on mature-node equipment down in the June quarter (per Reuters), the stock closed down 18.0%.
02
The margin target is slipping. The plan to March 2027 calls for ¥3T of sales, a 35% operating margin and 30% ROE. Management says sales and ROE are on track but calls the margin target "challenging"; FY2026 delivered 25.6% and Q1 FY2027 28.9%, and the raised first-half guide implies about 27.8% for Q2 (our calculation: ¥246.6B of operating income on ¥887.6B of sales), which the company puts down to product mix and higher fixed costs such as labour (Q&A summary). Its fix — pricing that passes on inflation and ramp-up costs — is expected to contribute only from the second half, visibly in Q4 (Q&A summary).
03
Share has been drifting. Asked why TEL's market share had declined over recent years, the company answered that the decline was driven primarily by exchange-rate moves (Q&A summary). TEL prices exports mainly in yen; a weak yen makes its dollar-denominated share look smaller. Winning share back depends on the pricing push above.
04
A cyclical business at a growth multiple. Revenue fell 17.1% in FY2024 and the stock fell 49.2% from its March 2024 month-end to March 2025. At 44.5 times trailing earnings, the price leaves little room for a pause in AI capital spending.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the reported figures. First, the gain on strategic shareholdings: FY2026 income before taxes was ¥748.2B against ordinary income of ¥630.3B, mainly because TEL sold investment securities at a ¥115.5B gain, booked in Q4. That is why FY2026 net income rose 5.6% while operating income fell 10.4%, and why the CFO said Q1 FY2027 net income fell 23.3% from Q4. The trailing EPS still includes it. Second, the split: every per-share figure before October 1, 2026 must be divided by five, including the ¥384 interim dividend, which is paid on pre-split shares. Third, currency: TEL says its export sales are mainly priced in yen, so a weaker yen does little to reported profit, but it does shrink TEL's sales when competitors and market researchers count in dollars.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

TEL owns 91% of a tool every lithography line needs, more than half of dielectric etch, and a fast-growing position in the probers and bonders that assemble AI chips. Q1 FY2027 delivered record sales of ¥732.4B (+33.3%) and operating income up 46.1%, management raised its first-half operating-profit forecast by 6.3% on the back of customers asking for earlier deliveries and extra orders, sees calendar 2027 equipment spending of at least $190B, and it expects a stronger second half than first. If FY2027 lands near consensus — ¥3.42T of sales and ¥1.02T of operating profit — the stock trades at about 35 times this year's earnings with no debt, a 50% payout policy, ¥150B of buybacks just completed, and Field Solutions revenue that keeps growing as the installed base does. Pricing that recovers inflation from H2, plus a 50% gross-margin goal for early FY2028, would add margin to volume.

Bear Case

This is a cyclical company priced at 44.5 times trailing earnings and twice the Nikkei's forward multiple, and its last two cycles both ended in drawdowns of more than 45%. The trailing earnings include a one-off ¥115.5B investment gain. Q1 sales fell 3.7% short of consensus even as earnings per share edged past it, and Jefferies has cut its target twice since the print, to ¥13,000. The 35% margin target is, in management's own word, challenging; market share has drifted; and China — still about a third of sales — sits under US export controls. July showed how quickly a crowded AI trade can reverse: −28.1% in a month in which TEL raised its first-half guidance. If AI capital spending pauses even briefly, the stock is pricing the peak.

Top 3 Risks

01
The AI capital-spending cycle turns. TEL's own guidance stops at September 2026; everything after is consensus. A pause by a few large memory and foundry customers would hit new-equipment sales, which are 72.7% of revenue, within quarters.
02
China and export controls. China was 34.1% of FY2026 sales and 30.4% of Q1 FY2027. Tighter US or Japanese rules on shipments or servicing would remove sales that are hard to replace quickly; the July 2025 forecast cut came from a slowdown in exactly this market.
03
Margins fail to follow volume. Q&A guidance points to lower gross and operating margins in Q2 than Q1 because of product mix and labour costs. If pricing does not land in H2, the market may stop paying a premium multiple for growth that does not reach the margin line.
Industry Commentary
AI turns the equipment cycle into a capacity race
TAILWIND

TEL's July outlook put calendar-2026 WFE at $150B+ and 2027 at $190B+, with AI data-centre applications exceeding half the market. In its Q&A summary TEL breaks 2026 down as DRAM in the low $40 billions, NAND in the mid-teens, logic in the mid-$80 billions and wafer-level packaging around $10B, with DRAM up 30%+, NAND 40%+ and logic 15%+ from 2025. The constraint has moved from demand to supply: asked whether it could serve a market some investors put at $250–300B, TEL said it cannot claim that capacity today and is building smart fabs, starting in Miyagi, to get there. When the bottleneck is how many tools can be built, pricing power shifts towards the toolmaker — which is exactly what TEL's H2 pricing push is testing.

China: from swing buyer to managed exposure
ONGOING HEADWIND

China reached 44.4% of TEL's sales in FY2024 and 41.7% in FY2025. When Chinese spending on mature-generation tools fell in the June 2025 quarter, TEL cut its FY2026 profit forecast in July 2025 and the stock fell 18.0% in a day. The share is now falling for a better reason — leading-edge customers in Taiwan and Korea are growing faster — and guidance for FY2027 is the low 30s%. That is still a large exposure to a market where US-China export controls, which Kawai names among the forces reshaping the business, can change the rules at short notice.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
DEBATE

At ¥12,100 the market pays 44.5 times trailing and about 35 times consensus FY2027 earnings, against 17.37 times forecast earnings for the Nikkei 225. The bull framing: trailing earnings understate a business whose quarterly sales just rose a third, whose order book management expects to accelerate in H2, and whose ¥1 trillion operating-profit target would put the forward multiple well below today's trailing one; scarcity of tool capacity supports price. The bear framing: both earlier downturns in this chart took the stock down 46–49% from month-end peak to trough, the multiple is already pricing the good outcome, and the 28.1% July drop showed how little it takes to unwind it. The sell side is split the same way, though all four of these houses still rate it Buy: since the print Goldman Sachs and UBS have held targets of ¥18,000 or more, while Jefferies has cut to ¥13,000 and Morgan Stanley sits at ¥13,600, 7–12% above the price. The October 30 full-year forecast is where the two framings meet the numbers.
