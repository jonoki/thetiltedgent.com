01 Company Overview

Tokyo Electron (TEL) builds the machines that make chips. It does not design or sell semiconductors; it sells the wafer fab equipment that chipmakers such as memory and logic producers install in their factories, and then services that installed base for years. In the fiscal year to March 2026 (TEL calls it FY2026) it booked ¥2,443.5B of sales, up 0.5%, operating income of ¥624.9B (−10.4%) and net income of ¥574.5B. The AI build-out has since filled its order book. The July–September 2026 quarter (Q2 FY2027) set a record for quarterly sales at ¥935.0B, +48.4% year on year, with operating income up 86.4% to ¥295.3B and an operating margin of 31.6%. Against the consensus compiled by Investing.com, earnings per share of ¥93.86 beat the ¥86.59 forecast by 8.4% and sales beat the ¥893.21B expected by 4.7%; the first half beat TEL's own July forecast on every line. With the results on October 30 came TEL's first full-year forecast: ¥3.7 trillion of sales (+51.4%), ¥1,169B of operating income (+87.1%, above ¥1 trillion for the first time) and ¥865B of net income, against S&P Global consensus before the print of ¥3.42T, ¥1.02T and ¥784.69B (via StockAnalysis, read October 5). The board also forecast a record ¥191 dividend for the year and set up a new buyback of up to ¥150B. CEO Toshiki Kawai said "currently our customers keep asking to pull forward delivery timing and purchase additional systems." Almost all of it is sold abroad — 90.2% of FY2026 sales were outside Japan, with China the largest single region at 34.1% (36.4% in Q2).

The stock has not followed the numbers. It closed December 2025 at ¥6,864 and June 2026 at ¥15,430 (split-adjusted), gave back 28% in July in a sector-wide chip selloff, and lost another 19.5% in October, closing the month at ¥9,514 on the day of the results, which came out after the market closed. It rose 5.2% in the first session after them (November 2) and fell 3.4% in the second (November 4, after a Tokyo holiday), to ¥9,663. At that price the market values TEL at about ¥21.9 trillion ($138B), 31.0 times trailing earnings and about 25 times the company's own forecast for the year to March 2027. The question for the next two quarters: management says customers keep pulling deliveries forward and that it has the capacity for more than ¥2 trillion of sales in the second half alone. Does that order book hold long enough to deliver it, and can margins climb towards the 35% target while it does — or does the 37% fall from the June month-end mean the market is already pricing the top of the cycle?

New-Equip Mix

Etch tools carve the circuit pattern into the layers on a wafer. TEL's strength is dielectric etch (cutting through insulating layers), where it claims more than 50% share, and the deep, narrow holes of memory: DRAM capacitors, HBM interconnects and the "HARC" (high aspect ratio) holes of 3D NAND. In April TEL guided FY2027 etch sales up more than 25%; on July 30 Kawai said "A full-year sales of etch systems of 1 trillion yen are coming within our reach." Two further hooks from July: five-year cumulative sales of DRAM interconnect etch to FY2030 were then approaching ¥1 trillion, against the ¥500B or more first guided in October 2025, and a cryogenic etch tool for 400-layer NAND is due in customers' mass production from calendar 2027. The new Miyagi production centre, due in summer 2027, is meant eventually to triple etch capacity. In the first half of FY2027 TEL won its first process-of-record position in DRAM conductor etch (the capacitor mask step) and reported steady orders for cryogenic NAND etch in volume-production lines (October 30 presentation).

New-Equip Mix

A coater/developer spreads light-sensitive resist on the wafer before it goes into a lithography scanner and develops the pattern afterwards; the two machines are docked together. Every EUV, immersion or KrF scanner a chipmaker installs needs one, and TEL supplies nine in ten of them. That makes this the closest thing to a toll booth in TEL's portfolio. Management expects sales to rise more than 50% in FY2027, helped by the new LITHIUS Pro DICE model, which it says has won adoption in several advanced logic and DRAM processes. In the call Q&A summary the company attributes the growth to demand across the full spectrum of lithography, from EUV to more general processes.

Deposition tools lay down the thin films that become transistors and wiring; cleaning tools strip particles and residues between steps. TEL holds 27% of deposition overall, with 38% of CVD, 31% of oxidation/diffusion furnaces and 15% of ALD, and 20% of cleaning (calendar 2025). AI chips are stacks: a GPU next to towers of HBM memory, bonded and tested before packaging. TEL sells the wafer probers that test each die (38% share, and what it calls an overwhelming position in high-end logic) and wafer bonders (27% share, with a high share in HBM bonding). Sales Mix

Everything sold to a tool after it ships: parts and services (¥144.3B in Q2 FY2027, up 6.4% on Q1 and the sixth straight quarterly rise) plus used equipment and upgrades (¥46.6B). Every machine TEL ships joins the installed base it services, and this revenue tracks how hard customers run their fabs rather than how much they spend on new ones. That makes it the shock absorber of the model. Q2 FY2027 was ¥191.0B, up from ¥160.3B a year earlier; the CFO said customers' need to keep their fabs running at high utilisation remained strong. In July management said it guides used-tool deals conservatively because they are short-lead and hard to forecast (Q&A summary).

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

91% of coater/developers >50% of dielectric etch (company) Process-of-record lock-in Installed-base service (¥626B) R&D ¥277.8B a year Japanese supply chain No borrowings, 68.2% equity ratio

The moat is the process of record. A chipmaker qualifies a tool for one step of a production flow over months of trial wafers; once it is in, every copy of that fab and every capacity expansion tends to buy the same tool for the same step, and the installed machine then generates service revenue for a decade. TEL's 91% share in coater/developers is the extreme case — the tool sits bolted to the scanner — and the ¥626B of Field Solutions sales is the annuity that follows. The weakness is that the moat protects position within a step, not the volume of steps bought: when customers cut capital budgets, TEL's sales fall with them, as FY2024's 17.1% revenue decline showed.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China and export controls. China was 41.7% of sales in FY2025 and 36.4% in Q2 FY2027, and is still guided at the low 30s% for FY2027. Kawai named export controls triggered by the US-China trade conflict among the changes the 2022 medium-term plan did not anticipate. The August 1, 2025 session showed the sensitivity: the day after TEL cut its full-year operating-profit forecast by 21.6%, with Chinese spending on mature-node equipment down in the June quarter (per Reuters), the stock closed down 18.0%.
02
The margin target is slipping. The plan to March 2027 calls for ¥3T of sales, a 35% operating margin and 30% ROE. The sales goal is now forecast to be beaten, at ¥3.7T; the margin is not. Q2 FY2027 reached 31.6% and the full-year forecast is 31.6%, with 32.6% needed in H2 (TEL's October 30 estimates). In July management called the margin target challenging and said price changes that pass on inflation and ramp-up costs would contribute only from the second half, visibly in Q4 (Q&A summary).
03
Share has been drifting. Asked why TEL's market share had declined over recent years, the company answered that the decline was driven primarily by exchange-rate moves (Q&A summary). TEL prices exports mainly in yen; a weak yen makes its dollar-denominated share look smaller. Winning share back depends on the pricing push above.
04
A cyclical business priced like a steady grower. Revenue fell 17.1% in FY2024 and the stock fell 49.2% from its March 2024 month-end to March 2025. At 31.0 times trailing earnings and about 25 times TEL's own FY2027 forecast, the price still assumes AI capital spending does not pause; the stock fell 19.5% in October, before the results, and is 37.4% below its June 2026 month-end.

Notable One-Off Items

Four things distort the reported figures. First, the gain on strategic shareholdings: FY2026 income before taxes was ¥748.2B against ordinary income of ¥630.3B, mainly because TEL sold investment securities at a ¥115.5B gain, booked in Q4. That is why FY2026 net income rose 5.6% while operating income fell 10.4%. The trailing EPS still includes it. Second, the split: every per-share figure before October 1, 2026 must be divided by five. TEL's own releases now restate EPS and past dividends for it, but the ¥415 interim dividend is paid on pre-split shares (¥83 per current share). Third, the Kumamoto earthquake of July 2026: the first half carries a ¥6.2B extraordinary loss for restoring buildings and production and development facilities and for scrapping inventory. Fourth, currency: TEL says its export sales are mainly priced in yen, so a weaker yen does little to reported profit, but it does shrink TEL's sales when competitors and market researchers count in dollars.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value.

From the November 2021 month-end close of ¥3,989.33 (split-adjusted) to the November 4, 2026 close of ¥9,663, the share price rose 142.2% — about 2.4 times. Dividends come on top and are not in the chart. It was not a smooth climb: the stock lost 46.1% from December 2021 to its September 2022 low of ¥2,380, more than tripled into the first AI peak of March 2024, lost 49.2% again between March 2024 and March 2025, nearly quadrupled from that low to the June 2026 month-end of ¥15,430, and has since given back 37.4%. Over the last 52 weeks it is up 38.9% (from the ¥6,958 close of November 4, 2025).

Momentum has turned down. The 6-period monthly RSI hit 87.7 in June 2026, below the 93.1 peak of March 2024, and reads 48.6 now — neutral. The price sits 7.5% below its 10-month moving average of about ¥10,442: the July drop broke the spike, and October's fall took the price under the longer-term trend line. The window's two sub-30 readings, 26.6 in September 2022 and 29.1 in March 2025, marked the bottoms of the last two downturns.

Relative performance

Against its home benchmark, the Nikkei 225, TEL is still ahead over the five years, but by much less than at the last edition: the Nikkei went from 27,821.76 at the November 2021 month-end to 62,188.63 on November 4, 2026, a 123.5% price gain, against TEL's 142.2% (both in yen, price only, Yahoo Finance). Since October 2 the Nikkei has fallen 9.0% and TEL 20.1%. The cost was volatility: on our calculation TEL's monthly beta to the Nikkei is 1.91. Among peers, TEL's $138.5B market value compares with Applied Materials at $303.1B and Lam Research at $288.2B, and with Japanese rivals SCREEN Holdings at $10.7B and Kokusai Electric at $9.4B, which joined the Nikkei 225 on October 1 (our calculations: November 4 Tokyo closes and November 3 US closes × StockAnalysis share counts, yen converted at ¥157.91).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

TEL owns 91% of a tool every lithography line needs, more than half of dielectric etch, and a fast-growing position in the probers and bonders that assemble AI chips. Q2 FY2027 delivered record quarterly sales of ¥935.0B (+48.4%) and a 31.6% operating margin. The first full-year forecast — ¥3.7T of sales and ¥1,169B of operating profit — came in 8% and 15% above consensus before the print; management says customers keep asking for earlier deliveries and extra tools, and that it has the capacity for more than ¥2T of sales in the second half. TEL now sees calendar-2027 equipment spending above $210B. At ¥9,663 the stock trades at about 25 times TEL's own FY2027 earnings forecast, with no debt, a record ¥191 dividend, a new ¥150B buyback and Field Solutions revenue that keeps growing as the installed base does. Pricing that recovers inflation from H2, plus a 50% gross-margin goal for early FY2028, would add margin to volume.

Bear Case

This is a cyclical company priced at 31 times trailing earnings, and its last two cycles both ended in drawdowns of more than 45%. The trailing earnings include a one-off ¥115.5B investment gain. The forecast is back-loaded: the second half must bring ¥2,032.5B of sales and ¥662.2B of operating profit, 22% and 31% more than the record first half. The 35% margin target is out of reach this year (forecast 31.6%); market share has drifted; and China — 36.4% of Q2 sales — sits under US export controls. UBS lowered its target after the results while keeping a Buy, and the stock fell 19.5% in October, before a record quarter: when many investors hold the same AI stocks, prices can reverse quickly. If AI capital spending pauses even briefly, the forecast is at risk.

Top 3 Risks

01
The AI capital-spending cycle turns. TEL's forecast runs to March 2027 and leans on a second half bigger than any half-year it has reported; beyond that everything is consensus. A pause by a few large memory and foundry customers would hit new-equipment sales, which were 72.7% of FY2026 revenue, within quarters.
02
China and export controls. China was 34.1% of FY2026 sales and 36.4% of Q2 FY2027. Tighter US or Japanese rules on shipments or servicing would remove sales that are hard to replace quickly; the July 2025 forecast cut came from a slowdown in exactly this market.
03
Margins fail to follow volume. The FY2027 forecast's 31.6% operating margin is still short of the plan's 35%, and the H2 guide needs 32.6%, above any half-year TEL has reported. If pricing does not land in H2, investors may stop paying a high price relative to earnings for growth that does not reach the margin line.
Industry Commentary
AI turns the equipment cycle into a capacity race
TAILWIND

TEL's October outlook puts calendar-2026 WFE at about $160B and 2027 at more than $210B, raised from $150B+ and $190B+ in July, with growth of around 30% in each year. Kawai's case: AI agents run many tasks on their own, the number of tokens processed rises sharply, and that needs not only faster GPUs but much more memory — HBM, DRAM and NAND storage — so memory is becoming central to AI performance. In its July Q&A summary TEL broke 2026 down as DRAM in the low $40 billions, NAND in the mid-teens, logic in the mid-$80 billions and wafer-level packaging around $10B. The constraint has moved from demand to supply: asked in July whether it could serve a market some investors put at $250–300B, TEL said it cannot claim that capacity today and is building smart fabs, starting in Miyagi; in October Kawai said it has secured capacity for more than ¥2 trillion of second-half sales. When the bottleneck is how many tools can be built, pricing power shifts towards the toolmaker — which is exactly what TEL's H2 pricing push is testing.

China: from swing buyer to managed exposure
ONGOING HEADWIND

China reached 44.4% of TEL's sales in FY2024 and 41.7% in FY2025. When Chinese spending on mature-generation tools fell in the June 2025 quarter, TEL cut its FY2026 profit forecast in July 2025 and the stock fell 18.0% in a day. Leading-edge customers in Taiwan and Korea have grown fast since, but Q2 FY2027 showed China is not fading: its share rose 6 points to 36.4%, which the CFO put down to active investment by major customers, while TEL still guides the full year to the low 30s%. That is a large exposure to a market where US-China export controls, which Kawai names among the forces reshaping the business, can change the rules at short notice.

The valuation question — bull and bear framing
DEBATE

At ¥9,663 the market pays 31.0 times trailing earnings, about 26 times consensus FY2027 earnings and 25 times TEL's own forecast. The bull framing: operating profit is forecast above ¥1 trillion for the first time, consensus net income has risen since the print while the stock has fallen 37% from its June month-end, and scarcity of tool capacity supports price. The bear framing: both earlier downturns in this chart took the stock down 46–49% from month-end peak to trough, the second-half ramp still has to land, and the July and October falls showed how quickly investors can stop paying a high price for each yen of earnings. The sell side has stayed positive: on November 2 Goldman Sachs raised its target to ¥19,000 and UBS cut to ¥16,500, both still Buys, while the lowest target in the S&P Global consensus, ¥9,600, sits just below the price. The Q3 report, expected in February, is the first test of the second-half forecast.
