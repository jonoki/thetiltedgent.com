01 Company Overview

Targa Resources is a Permian-weighted North American midstream company built around two segments — Gathering and Processing (G&P) and Logistics and Transportation (L&T). FY2025 revenue was $17.03B, up 3.95% on FY2024's $16.38B, but revenue is the least informative number in this business; adjusted EBITDA is what management guides and what the market pays for, and Q3 2026 came in at $1,504.3M, up 18% year over year and 6% below the record $1,603M of Q2. Management now expects 2026 at at least $6.0B, up from the top end of a $5.7–5.9B range in August. At $56.77B the company ranks #210 in the S&P 500.

The central debate on this page is not whether Targa is growing — it plainly is, with Permian inlet volumes up 14% YoY and fractionation volumes up 11% in Q3 2026. It is what kind of company you are buying. Targa spends ~$5.0B of growth capital in 2026 against at least $6.0B of adjusted EBITDA guidance — roughly 83 cents of every EBITDA dollar going back into steel — while raising the dividend 25% this year, planning a further 20% step for 2027, buying back stock, and carrying $19.8B of debt at about 3.3× net debt to EBITDA (our calculation). Trailing free cash flow of $933.8M does not cover the $1.07B annual dividend. That gap is deliberate and it is the whole argument: either the build-out converts into fee-based earnings that make today's 23.4× multiple look cheap, or Targa is a levered bet on the Permian rig count wearing an infrastructure costume.

02 Core Businesses

The value chain, plainly. A well in West Texas produces a wet gas stream — methane mixed with heavier hydrocarbons. Targa's gathering pipelines collect it at the wellhead and carry it to a processing plant, which chills the stream until the heavy molecules drop out as a mixed liquid called NGLs, leaving dry residue gas to be sold. The mixed NGLs then ride Targa's NGL pipelines roughly 400 miles southeast to Mont Belvieu, Texas — the world's NGL clearing house — where fractionation trains boil the mixture apart into ethane, propane, normal butane, isobutane and natural gasoline. Propane and butane are then loaded onto ships at Targa's Galena Park terminal as LPG exports. Targa owns links at every step, which is the point: a molecule can pay Targa four separate times on its way from a Midland wellhead to a tanker.

Why this is not Kinder Morgan. The distinction the market frequently blurs is that G&P is not a toll road. A long-haul interstate gas pipeline like Kinder Morgan's or Williams' earns a demand charge on contracted capacity whether or not molecules flow. Targa's G&P segment earns on volumes actually gathered, and those volumes track the Permian rig count with a lag of months, not years. Commodity prices feed straight through. Targa's realised natural gas price in G&P was negative $2.48/MMBtu in Q2 2026, because pipeline capacity out of the Permian was short and prices at the Waha hub in West Texas stayed negative for an extended period (Q2 2026 release), so producers were effectively paying to get gas out of the basin, then swung to positive $1.61 in Q3 2026 (against $1.20 a year earlier). Over nine months it averaged negative $0.04, against positive $1.45 in the first nine months of 2025. A pure fee-based pipeline does not have a line item that swings like that. In Q3 the swing helped: gas (+34%), NGLs at $0.50/gal (+28%) and condensate at $78.79/Bbl (+16%) all rose against Q3 2025, and G&P adjusted operating margin grew 22%, faster than the 10% growth in L&T.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Wellhead-to-water integration Permian acreage dedications Mont Belvieu fractionation Galena Park export dock Delaware Basin density Permitting & right-of-way C-corp index eligibility

The core advantage is integration density, not scale. Targa is not the biggest midstream company — Williams and Kinder Morgan are both larger by market cap, and ONEOK moves more revenue. What Targa has is an unbroken chain across a single basin: gathering systems whose acreage dedications feed processing plants Targa owns, feeding NGL pipelines Targa owns, feeding fractionators Targa owns, feeding an export dock Targa owns. Each incremental molecule therefore earns four margins instead of one, and — critically — the cost of adding the fourth margin once the first three exist is small. In Q3 2026 NGL pipeline volumes grew 15% and fractionation 11%, against 14% for Permian inlet volumes, so the downstream links are growing in step with the gathering base. Replicating this requires not capital but time — acreage dedications signed years ago, Mont Belvieu land, dock capacity, and pipeline rights-of-way that cannot be bought at any price on a two-year horizon.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Rig-count sensitivity that Kinder Morgan and Williams do not carry. G&P margin is a function of volumes gathered, and volumes are a function of drilling. Roughly 55% of segment margin sat in G&P in Q3 2026. If Permian activity rolls over, Targa's inlet volumes decline within quarters — there is no demand charge underpinning them. Kinder Morgan's and Williams' interstate gas systems are contracted on capacity reservations that pay regardless of throughput. Targa's beta of 0.74 versus Kinder Morgan's 0.57 and Williams' 0.63 is the market's rough acknowledgement of this, and it understates the operational difference because beta is measured against the whole market, not against oil.
02
Gas prices swing, and have been negative. The realised natural gas price in G&P was −$2.48/MMBtu in Q2 2026 and +$1.61 in Q3; over nine months it averaged −$0.04. Pipeline capacity out of the Permian has been short enough that gas had negative value as it left the plant. Strong NGL and condensate prices carried both quarters. A quarter where NGL prices fall and gas goes negative again is the untested combination.
03
The capital programme outruns cash generation. Growth capex of ~$5.0B plus ~$250M maintenance against at least $6.0B of 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance leaves little for the $1.07B dividend and the buyback except balance sheet. Trailing free cash flow is $933.8M (stockanalysis.com); the dividend alone is $1.07B, and management intends to recommend $1.50 a quarter ($6.00 a year) for 2027. Debt is $19.8B against $143M of cash and about $2.9B of total liquidity. The plan works if the plants and trains deliver EBITDA on schedule. It does not work if they slip and rates stay where they are.
04
Downstream growth slowed. L&T adjusted operating margin grew 10% year over year in Q3 2026 against 22% for G&P, and total adjusted EBITDA was 6% below Q2. The case for steadier earnings rests on the downstream segment, so another quarter of slower L&T growth would weaken it.

Leverage. The metric Targa actually manages to is not debt-to-equity. Total consolidated debt at September 30, 2026 was $19.8B (company release, including finance leases) against $143M of cash, giving net debt of about $19.7B. Total liquidity was about $2.9B at September 30, 2026: $2.6B on the revolving credit facility, $149M on the receivables facility and $143M of cash.

Notable one-off items. Five-year trajectory. From the December 2021 monthly close of $52.24 to the November 6, 2026 close of $264.59, TRGP returned +406.5% on price alone — a compound annual rate of about 39.7% over 4.85 years, before dividends, which have themselves compounded to $5.00 a share annualised. In FY2021 Targa's gross margin was 19.0% and common shareholders took a small loss: −$16.1M, or −$0.07 per share, after $87.3M of preferred dividends took net income attributable to Targa of $71.2M below zero. TTM gross margin is 41.82% and EPS is $11.30. The market did not just pay for a commodity cycle; it paid a much higher price for each dollar of earnings from a changed business.

Relative performance. From the November 10, 2025 close to the November 6, 2026 close, price changes before dividends (Yahoo Finance daily closes) were: TRGP +56.1%, ONEOK +25.5%, Kinder Morgan +10.7%, Williams +5.7%. Targa beat the best of its peers, ONEOK, by about 31 percentage points (56.1 − 25.5 = 30.6). That is the asymmetry the G&P weighting creates: when volumes and NGL prices rise, Targa's earnings rise faster than a toll-road pipeline's. It has cut the other way too: from the October 30 to the November 6, 2026 close Targa fell 16.0%, against 13.8% for Williams, 12.2% for ONEOK and 11.5% for Kinder Morgan.

08 Summary & Key Risks

The stack, in the order the molecules move. Start at the wellhead: 7,536.6 MMcf/d of Permian gas into Targa's plants in Q3 2026, up 14%, split Midland 3,472.8 (+7%) and Delaware 4,063.8 (+20%). Process it and the NGLs come out; ship them to Mont Belvieu and you get 1,171.7 MBbl/d of NGL pipeline transportation, up 15%. Fractionate them and you get 1,261.5 MBbl/d, up 11%. Load the propane and butane onto ships and you get 447.9 MBbl/d of LPG exports, up 10%. Every link grew 10–15% in the quarter, so the integration thesis is intact: Targa is not just moving more gas, it is moving more of each molecule down its own chain.

Where the earnings actually are — and the crossover that reversed. Q3 2026 adjusted operating margin was $1,069.9M in G&P and $888.1M in L&T. A year earlier it was $873.7M and $808.8M, so G&P grew 22% and L&T 10%. G&P is the larger segment again, supplying 54.6% of segment margin. That matters for anyone worried about rig-count sensitivity, because G&P is the volume-driven half. Total adjusted EBITDA of $1,504.3M was 18% above Q3 2025 but 6% below Q2.

The commodity exposure is real and it cut both ways. Targa's realised natural gas price in G&P was negative $2.48 per MMBtu in Q2 2026 and positive $1.61 in Q3 against $1.20 a year earlier; over nine months it averaged negative $0.04. In Q3 NGL realisations rose 28% to $0.50/gal and condensate rose 16% to $78.79/Bbl. A pure fee-based pipeline has no line item that can print a negative price, and none that can move like that. This is the difference Targa's peers do not carry, and it is why beta ranks TRGP (0.74) above WMB (0.63) and KMI (0.57). The point is not that commodity exposure is bad, but that it is there and moves the results.

The capital equation, done explicitly. 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance: at least $6,000M. Growth capex: ~$5,000M. Maintenance capex: ~$250M. Dividend: $5.00 × 214.55M shares ≈ $1,073M. Buyback: $52M in Q3, or about $208M annualised. Add the outflows: $5,000 + $250 + $1,073 + $208 = $6,531M against $6,000M of EBITDA — and EBITDA is before interest and tax, so the true gap is considerably wider once $19.8B of debt is serviced. Trailing free cash flow is $933.8M, which does not cover the dividend alone. The shortfall is funded with debt, and net debt is already about 3.3× guided EBITDA. This is not a criticism — it is a deliberate strategy for a company whose projects earn well above its cost of capital, evidenced by ROIC of 13.79%. But it is a strategy with little slack. Every dollar of the plan assumes the plants come in on time and the volumes show up.

Valuation, and which number to use. On trailing earnings of $11.30, TRGP trades at 23.4× — above Kinder Morgan (18.6×) and ONEOK (13.2×), below Williams (25.2×). On 2026 consensus EPS of $11.83 it is 22.4×; on 2027's $12.34, 21.4× (stockanalysis.com estimates). The midstream-native measure is more useful: enterprise value (market value plus net debt) of about $76.5B against 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance of at least $6.0B gives at most 12.7× (our calculation: 214.55M shares × $264.59 plus $19.7B of net debt), against about 13.1× on stockanalysis.com's trailing EBITDA of $5.82B, restated at the November 6 close. The stock is priced for growth: 2027 consensus revenue of $22.91B is 21.9% above 2026's $18.8B, and it is priced as though the Delaware plants, Trains 12 to 14, Speedway and the Q3 2027 LPG export expansion all land. It offers a 1.89% dividend yield while doing so — less than half Kinder Morgan's 3.97% and well under ONEOK's 5.02%. You are not being paid to wait. You are being asked to underwrite a construction schedule.

Adjusted EBITDA rose 18% year over year, 2026 guidance was raised to at least $6.0B, and management intends to recommend a 20% dividend increase for 2027. Bear Case

Free cash flow of $933.8M does not cover a $1.07B dividend, let alone $5.0B of growth capex and about $208M of annualised buybacks — the difference is debt, and net debt is already about 3.3× guided EBITDA. Q3 adjusted EBITDA fell 6% from Q2, L&T growth slowed to 10%, and the realised gas price swung from −$2.48 to +$1.61 in a quarter, which shows how live the commodity exposure is. The stock fell 16.0% in the five sessions to November 6 after peaking at $314.99, yet still trades at 23.4× trailing earnings, with a 1.89% yield less than half the peer median of 3.97%. Every dollar of the plan depends on a long list of Delaware plants, fractionation trains, a pipeline and an export dock all delivering on schedule.

  Industry Commentary
Permian Pipeline Limits Still Move the Gas Price
KEY CONSTRAINT
The most concrete data point in this report is the swing in Targa's realised natural gas price: negative $2.48 per MMBtu in Q2 2026, positive $1.61 in Q3 2026 and negative $0.04 over nine months. When there is not enough pipeline capacity out of the basin, producers in West Texas are effectively paying to dispose of associated gas. Targa's own response is visible in its project list — the Buffalo Run, Bull Run II and Forza residue gas pipelines under construction, and the Bull Run Extension now operating, exist to move residue gas to where it has value. Until pipeline capacity out of the basin catches up, every G&P operator in the Permian carries a byproduct whose price can turn negative, and the companies best insulated are those, like Targa, whose economics lean on the liquids rather than the methane.
NGL Export Demand Is the Sector's Real Growth Driver
EMERGING TAILWIND
US NGL production keeps growing because it is a byproduct of oil drilling, and domestic petrochemical demand cannot absorb it all — so the extra barrels go overseas. Targa's LPG exports rose 10% to 447.9 MBbl/d in Q3 2026 and the Galena Park expansion targets roughly 19 million barrels per month by Q3 2027. This is the part of the business least connected to US drilling economics and most connected to Asian and European demand, even though L&T as a whole grew more slowly than G&P in Q3 (10% against 22%).
Consolidation and the Capex Arms Race in the Permian
ONGOING HEADWIND
ONEOK agreed on August 30, 2026 to buy Brazos Midstream's Permian Midland assets for $4.425 billion, backed by a $9 billion nonvoting minority equity investment from Apollo funds; the deal is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026. It is a direct move onto Targa's home turf, funded by an outside balance sheet. Targa's answer is to build rather than buy: $5.0B of organic growth capex in 2026. Bull framing: building at a 13.79% ROIC beats paying an acquisition premium, and Targa's existing downstream chain means new Delaware plants immediately feed pipes, fractionators and a dock it already owns — the incremental returns on integrated capacity are structurally higher than a standalone buyer's. Bear framing: well-funded rivals adding capacity into a basin whose gas price has gone negative is how gathering fees get competed down, and Targa is committing $5.0B in 2026, funded partly with debt, into exactly that environment while paying out a dividend its free cash flow does not cover.
