01 Company Overview

Thomson Reuters sells the reference material professionals are not allowed to get wrong: Westlaw and Practical Law for lawyers, ONESOURCE and Checkpoint for corporate tax departments, UltraTax and GoSystem for accountants, plus the Reuters news agency and a shrinking print business. It reports in US dollars from Toronto, lists on both Nasdaq and the TSX, and is ~68% owned by Woodbridge, the Thomson family holding company. FY2025 revenue was $7.48B, up 3.0% reported; the trailing-twelve-month figure through June 2026 is $7.83B, up 7.0%, and Q2 2026 grew 9% reported / 8% organic — the fastest organic print in years. It ranks roughly #569 globally by market cap at $45.8B.

The central question is uncomfortable because both halves are true at once. Operationally, this is the best the business has looked in a decade: the "Big 3" segments grew 10% organically in Q2 2026, 32% of annualized contract value is now tied to generative-AI products, guidance was raised, and free cash flow is running $2.1B. Yet the stock has fallen 47.5% from its June 2025 monthly-close high of $201.13 and sits 41.5% lower than a year ago. The market is asking whether a company that sells curated legal and tax content can keep charging premium prices when general-purpose language models, and legal-AI startups built on them, can read the same case law. This report is about whether Westlaw and CoCounsel are the toll booth on the AI highway or the thing the highway bypasses.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Proprietary editorial content (KeyCite, headnotes) Fiduciary-grade accuracy / liability positioning Workflow embedding in law & tax firms ~80%+ recurring revenue, multi-year contracts Bundling: Westlaw + Practical Law + CoCounsel Reuters trust brand & licensable text $9B capital capacity for M&A

The core advantage is not the raw text of statutes and cases — that is public. It is a century of editorial metadata layered on top of it: which cases have been overruled, which headnote applies to which fact pattern, which tax position the IRS has accepted. A general model can read the law; it cannot easily tell you it is citing a case that was reversed last year. That is why management's chosen phrase is "Fiduciary-Grade AI" — in a profession where a wrong citation is a sanctionable event, the buyer pays for the provenance, not the prose. The company is now training its own frontier model ("Thomson," announced August 24, 2026, with a stated $40M training investment) on that proprietary corpus, and has moved 32% of annualized contract value onto GenAI-enabled products, up from 30% a quarter earlier. The competitive logic is that CoCounsel is priced as a premium tier, so every seat that migrates lifts revenue per user — the "AI-priced upsell" case in the brief.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
General LLMs and legal-AI startups compress the research premium. Management acknowledged on the Q2 call that law firms are "testing multiple tools" in a "fluid market." Startups such as Harvey (private; not verified from sources fetched here) and frontier-model vendors can offer drafting and first-pass research at a fraction of Westlaw's price. If the market decides that "good enough plus a human check" is acceptable, Thomson Reuters' pricing power erodes even as its usage grows.
▸
Model cost inflation versus subscription pricing. Agentic workflows burn tokens. The CFO said current premium pricing "comfortably" covers LLM cost growth, but no margin disclosure separates AI gross margin from the legacy database business. If inference costs do not fall as fast as usage rises, the 48% Legal margin is the line to watch.
▸
Concentrated float and a controlling shareholder. Woodbridge owns ~68% and was a net seller in March 2026. With only ~125.6M shares in public hands and short interest at 10.1% of float, the stock moves violently on modest flows — the 5.4% drop on September 4 is a reminder — and minority holders have no say on capital allocation.
▸
Cyber and data integrity. The company disclosed a breach of its C-Track court-management platform affecting courts in the US and Canada in early September 2026. For a business whose entire value proposition is "trust us with the record," security incidents are a direct hit to the brand, not a footnote.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Beneficiary or target — what the numbers actually say. Start with the disclosure that matters most: 32% of annualized contract value now comes from GenAI-enabled products, up two points in one quarter, and the Big 3 accelerated to 10% organic growth with Legal ex-government at 11%. If AI were cannibalizing the business, you would expect that share to rise while total growth stalled — customers swapping a Westlaw seat for a cheaper CoCounsel seat. Instead both rose together, which is what an upsell looks like. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 30bp to 38.1% despite absorbing inference costs, and the CFO said premium-tier pricing "comfortably" covers model cost growth. On the evidence available in the P&L, 2026 Thomson Reuters is behaving like a beneficiary.

What the numbers do not say. The company will not quantify Westlaw Advantage penetration, CoCounsel attach rates, net revenue retention, or AI gross margin. "One million users" of CoCounsel Legal is a usage figure, not a revenue figure, and management concedes law firms are "testing multiple tools." The beneficiary case therefore rests on aggregate growth plus management's word — exactly the kind of evidence that reads well until the quarter it doesn't. Wolters Kluwer's ADR is down 48% and RELX's market cap ~26% over the same year, so the market has decided the whole category is at risk, and one good quarter from TR has not changed that view: the stock is still 47% below its June 2025 high.

Valuation. At $105.68 the stock trades at 28.0× trailing GAAP EPS ($3.78), 22.3× forward, 22.8× trailing free cash flow ($2.23B) and roughly 22× EV/EBITDA. Against 8% organic growth and 11–15% adjusted EPS growth that is not cheap, but it is a very different proposition from the ~45× (est.) paid at the June 2025 peak. The peer comparison is the awkward part: RELX at 20.6× and Wolters Kluwer at 12.1× trailing are cheaper with similar or better margins, so the market is still paying TR a premium — for faster growth, a cleaner AI narrative and a $9B M&A war chest. A 4.5% shareholder yield (2.4% dividend + 2.1% buyback) and a 33-year dividend-growth record provide a floor of sorts; a 10% short interest on a 125.6M-share float provides fuel in both directions.

Bull Case

Proprietary, editorially-verified content is the scarce input for professional AI, and TR is monetizing it: 32% of ACV is GenAI, the Big 3 are growing 10% organically with margins expanding, guidance was raised, and the company is now training its own model on data no competitor can license. Global Print exits the P&L, headline growth re-bases toward 9–10%, $9B of capital capacity funds tuck-in M&A, and the dividend compounds at 10% a year. If the stock merely re-rates back to RELX's premium-to-market multiple on 2027 EPS of $5.08, it is back at the Street's high target of $146.

Bear Case

Frontier models improve every quarter and legal-AI startups sell drafting and research at a fraction of Westlaw's price; TR's own admission that firms are "testing multiple tools" is the sound of pricing power eroding. The company declines to disclose penetration, retention or AI margins, so the 10% growth could be one-time bundle conversion rather than durable uplift. Inference costs scale with usage while subscriptions do not. At 28× GAAP earnings — a premium to RELX and more than double Wolters Kluwer — with a controlling shareholder that was a net seller in March 2026 and a fresh security breach in a trust-based business, the downside to peer multiples is 25–40% even if the operations are fine.

Top 3 Risks

01
AI commoditization of legal research. The whole premium rests on the claim that provenance and accuracy are worth a large multiple of raw model cost. If general models with retrieval over public case law reach "good enough" for most tasks, CoCounsel becomes a feature, not a product, and Legal's 48% margin is the first casualty.
02
Multiple compression toward peers. TR trades at 28× GAAP versus 20.6× for RELX and 12.1× for Wolters Kluwer. No operating miss is required for the stock to fall further — the sector merely has to keep de-rating, and TR's premium narrows. The 41.5% one-year decline happened while earnings grew.
03
Governance and float. Woodbridge controls ~68% of votes and economics, sold shares in March 2026, and public holders have no influence over the "$9B capital capacity" being deployed "aggressively" into M&A. A thin float with 10% short interest means outsized moves on any disappointment — the September 4 breach reaction is the template.

The Great Professional-Information De-Rating
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
The three listed leaders in curated professional content have lost between a quarter and a half of their value in twelve months: TRI −41.5%, Wolters Kluwer ADR −47.6%, RELX market cap ~−26%. Nothing in their income statements justifies it — all three grew earnings — so the move is a change in the market's prior about terminal value: the fear that a large language model with web retrieval eventually replaces the subscription. Until one of these companies publishes retention and AI-margin data that refutes it, the sector trades on narrative, and TR's 8% organic growth is a data point in a debate it cannot win with growth alone.
Token Costs Meet Seat Pricing
ONGOING HEADWIND
The legacy database business had near-zero marginal cost per query. Agentic AI does not: every multi-step research task consumes inference that someone pays for. TR's response is premium-tier pricing, its own $40M "Thomson" model to reduce dependence on third-party APIs, and the assertion that pricing "comfortably" covers cost growth. The whole industry faces the same squeeze — Q3 2026's guided ~36% adjusted EBITDA margin (versus 38–40% in other quarters) is partly seasonal, but the trend in Legal's 48% margin over the next four quarters is the cleanest available test of whether AI economics are accretive or dilutive at the segment level.
Content Owners Become Model Owners
EMERGING TAILWIND
The August 2026 "Thomson" model announcement marks a strategic shift: rather than renting intelligence from frontier labs and wrapping it around proprietary content, TR is training on its own corpus of editorially-tagged law, tax rulings and Reuters text that no competitor can license. If it works, the moat migrates from "we own the content" to "we own the only model that has read it properly." Bull framing: a vertically-integrated, fiduciary-grade model sold at premium prices into a profession that cannot afford hallucinations — the Big 3's 10% growth is the early proof. Bear framing: $40M is a rounding error against frontier-lab training budgets; a 25,200-person publisher competing on model quality with companies spending tens of billions is a mismatch, and the KKR Print deal and $9B capital capacity look like a company preparing to buy growth it fears it cannot generate organically.
