01 Company Overview

Trimble began in November 1978 as Trimble Navigation, a start-up founded by Charles Trimble and two Hewlett-Packard engineers that bought a navigation technology Hewlett-Packard had stopped developing — for $50,000. It rode GPS into surveying, construction machine control and farm guidance, and then spent the last decade trying to become something the market pays more for: a recurring-revenue software company for the people who design, build and move physical things. The tape measure became a data platform. Today it reports three segments — AECO (architects, engineers, construction and owners: SketchUp, Tekla, Viewpoint), Field Systems (the GNSS receivers, total stations, scanners and machine-control kits) and Transportation & Logistics (Transporeon and the freight software stack). Trailing-twelve-month revenue is $3.78B, up 5.8%; ARR reached a record $2.51B at the end of Q2 2026, up 14% (12% organic).

The portfolio was rebuilt in plain sight. Trimble contributed its agriculture business to a joint venture with AGCO (PTx Trimble, closed April 1, 2024 — Trimble kept 15%), booking a $1.69B gain on divestitures that pushed FY2024 GAAP net income to $1.50B, or $6.09 a share, against $2.85 non-GAAP. It then sold the Mobility telematics business to Platform Science for an equity stake, closing February 8, 2025 — which is why FY2025 revenue fell 2.6% to $3.59B while the business that remained grew. Then came 2026. The operating numbers kept beating — Q1 revenue up 12% organic, Q2 up 10% organic, guidance raised in August — while the stock fell from a $78.35 December close to $51.18 at the end of June. In Q2 the gap between the two stories became an accounting entry: a $562.0M goodwill impairment on Transportation & Logistics, which the company attributed to its own falling market capitalisation and "lower market multiples for software businesses." That charge is why trailing EPS is −$0.44, and why a trailing P/E does not exist for this stock right now. On the same day it reported the charge, management put T&L itself up for a strategic review. The question the price is asking: is Trimble a hardware cyclical wearing a software multiple, or a software compounder being priced like a hardware cyclical?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base of survey & machine-control hardware Caterpillar JV (since 2002) Construction ERP switching cost (Viewpoint) SketchUp user base Connected field-to-office data Freight network effects (Transporeon) 71.8% gross margin

The moat is the job site's workflow. A surveyor's receiver, the grade-control kit on the dozer, the model the engineer built in Tekla and the contractor's accounting in Viewpoint all speak to each other — and the bet behind "Connect and Scale" is that each connection makes the next piece harder to replace. The evidence it is working sits in the gross margin, which climbed from 58.0% in FY2021 to 71.8% on a trailing basis as low-margin hardware lines left and software grew. The vulnerability is that connection is not the same as lock-in: construction software is contested by well-funded pure plays, and the hardware half still sells into a cyclical capex budget.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Software multiples are being repriced — and Trimble said so itself. The company's own explanation of the $562.0M impairment was a "sustained decline in market capitalization and stock price reflecting heightened macroeconomic uncertainty and lower market multiples for software businesses." When the issuer writes the de-rating into its accounts, the market is not imagining it.
02
Half the revenue is still hardware. Field Systems is 45.5% of Q2 revenue but only about 16% of ARR. Its Q2 strength rested on data-centre, utility and energy-infrastructure demand; if that build-out slows, the biggest segment has no subscription cushion under it.
03
AI could compress the seat. Much of AECO software is priced per user. If AI tools let a smaller design team do the same work, seat counts fall. Management's answer is hybrid licence-plus-consumption pricing, but on its own account the near-term priority is adoption, not monetisation.
04
A current ratio below 1. Current ratio 0.95, quick ratio 0.58, net debt $1.24B (1.58x EBITDA). Not alarming for a business with $773M of trailing free cash flow, but there is less slack than the margin profile suggests.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items make Trimble's GAAP history nearly unreadable without adjustment. FY2024: a $1,687.9M gain on divestitures, primarily the agriculture contribution to the AGCO joint venture, lifted GAAP EPS to $6.09 against $2.85 non-GAAP. FY2025: the Mobility sale (closed February 8, 2025) removed revenue, so reported revenue fell 2.6% while the continuing business grew. Q2 2026: the $562.0M non-cash goodwill impairment on Transportation & Logistics turned a quarter with $200.3M of non-GAAP net income into a $471.7M GAAP loss, and pushed the guided full-year GAAP tax rate to 145%. None of these touch cash. The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap outside those items is also large — Q2 GAAP operating margin 13.6% versus 26.8% adjusted — so the reader should decide how much of the adjustments they are willing to accept.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 15.7x this year's consensus earnings and 17.4x trailing free cash flow for a business whose recurring revenue grows at 12% organic, whose gross margin has climbed nearly 14 points since FY2021, and which beat expectations in both 2026 quarters and raised guidance in August. The $562.0M impairment is a non-cash reflection of the share price, not a hole in the business. A T&L sale at a decent price would turn the segment the market worries about into cash, leave a simpler AECO-plus-field company, and fund more of the buyback that has already cut the share count 3.87% in a year. Every one of the 13 polled analysts' targets sits above the price.

Bear Case

Trimble told you itself why the stock is down: lower market multiples for software. If per-seat design software faces AI compression, the multiple can fall further without earnings missing. Nearly half the revenue is hardware sold into construction and infrastructure capex, the current ratio is below 1, and net debt is $1.24B. The GAAP record is a string of one-offs — a $1.69B gain one year, a $562.0M write-down two years later — which makes the "real" earnings power a matter of whose adjustments you accept. And a strategic review with "no predetermined outcome" can end in no sale at all.

Top 3 Risks

01
Software de-rating. The multiple, not the earnings, drove the 2026 decline, and the company's own impairment note names falling software multiples. A further sector-wide de-rating would hit TRMB regardless of execution.
02
Hardware cycle in Field Systems. 45.5% of Q2 revenue and the best segment margin, but only about 16% of ARR. Growth currently leans on data-centre, utility and energy-infrastructure demand, which is cyclical and concentrated.
03
Execution on the T&L review and the white-label transition. A failed or low-priced sale, or a longer-than-flagged ARR drag from replacing the white-label product, would both land in the metrics the bull case depends on.
Industry Commentary
The software multiple reset
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Trimble's 2026 is a case study in the market re-pricing software as a category. Its own operating results improved every quarter — organic revenue growth of 12% and then 10%, record ARR, EBITDA margin up — yet the stock lost a third of its value between December and June, and the accounting rules then forced the company to write $562.0M off T&L goodwill because its market value no longer supported the book. The market's working thesis, as the impairment note puts it, is simply "lower market multiples for software businesses." For Trimble the question is sharper than for a pure software name: roughly half the revenue is hardware, so part of what it was paid for was the promise of becoming more of a software company. If software itself earns a lower multiple, that promise is worth less.

Freight: four years down, "initial green shoots"
ONGOING HEADWIND

The T&L segment has lived through what Painter called "four years of a freight recession." In August he said Trimble was "seeing initial green shoots," pointing to rising spot rates and tender rejections (FreightWaves, Aug 12, 2026). T&L ARR still grew 7% and margins widened 240 basis points through the downturn. The timing of the strategic review matters: selling a freight-software business near the bottom of the freight cycle hands the upside to the buyer; waiting risks the inbound interest going cold.

Building the physical economy: data centres and infrastructure
EMERGING TAILWIND

Management told analysts that Field Systems demand was strong from data-centre, utility and energy-infrastructure projects — the physical side of the AI build-out. That is the counterweight to the AI risk in design software: the same capital cycle that could compress per-seat pricing is pouring concrete and laying cable, and every one of those sites needs positioning, surveying and machine control. The bull framing: Trimble is paid on both sides of AI — in the tools and in the build. The bear framing: the hardware half is levered to one capex cycle, and the software half is exposed to the technology driving that cycle. Field Systems growth and AECO seat counts over the next four quarters will show which framing holds.
