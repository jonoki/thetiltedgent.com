01 Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest contract chipmaker — a pure-play foundry — and, at a $2.31T ADR-implied value, the #1 company on the Taiwan Stock Exchange by market cap and the #6 company in the world (companiesmarketcap). The premise for this report was "near-monopoly at the leading edge". The company's own numbers support the leading-edge half: in Q2 2026 (Apr–Jun 2026), advanced technologies (7nm and below) were 77% of wafer revenue — 3nm 30%, 5nm 33%, 7nm 11%, and the new 2nm already 3% in its first revenue quarter. HPC was 66% of net revenue, up from 60% a year earlier. The monopoly half — a market-share number for the leading-edge foundry market — was not found in any source fetched, so this page does not state one.

The scale is hard to overstate. FY2025 revenue was NT$3,809.05B, up 31.61%, with EPS of NT$65.47 (+46.56%). The trailing twelve months through June 2026 are larger still: NT$4,440.49B of revenue (+30.56%) and NT$2,216.81B of net income (+53.43%) — a 49.9% net margin on a manufacturing business. Q2 2026 alone printed a 67.7% gross margin and a 60.3% operating margin, and management now expects full-year 2026 revenue growth of "slightly above 40%" in US-dollar terms. TSMC also publishes revenue monthly: August 2026 came in at NT$514.81B, +53.3% YoY, and January–August at NT$3,386.87B, +39.3%.

The central debate is the one in the brief: Taiwan geopolitical risk versus the growth rate. The data complicate the usual "discount" framing. On the NYSE line TSM trades at 33.1x trailing earnings — above the S&P 500's 26.1x — and US holders pay a premium over the Taiwan-listed shares for the ADR wrapper (see Section 04). Where a discount does show up is against the growth: 33x for 53% trailing EPS growth, and roughly 19.9x FY2027 consensus. The question is not whether the market ignores the Strait — it is how much of the growth it refuses to capitalise because of it. The home-market benchmark is the TAIEX, where TSMC is by far the largest constituent; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500.

FX exposure: TSMC reports in New Taiwan dollars, while the ADR trades in US dollars and 78% of Q2 2026 revenue came from customers based in North America. Q2 revenue grew 36.0% in NT$ but 33.7% in US$ — the currency flattered the reported growth by about two points. Q3 guidance assumes US$1 = NT$32.0.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

77% of wafers at ≤7nm 2nm in volume; A14 risk 2027 Advanced packaging capacity Pure-play: never competes with clients Design-ecosystem lock-in US$60–64B/yr capex Net cash ~$77B

The moat is time plus capital. C.C. Wei put the first half plainly: developing a new node such as A14, building the capacity and ramping it "now takes five to seven years. There are no shortcuts." The second half is the budget — US$60–64B of capex this year, funded out of a business with a 64% trailing gross margin and ~US$77B of net cash (StockAnalysis). A competitor needs both the process know-how and the willingness to spend at that rate for years before earning a return. The pure-play model adds a trust layer: TSMC does not sell chips of its own, so a customer's design is never handed to a rival. Asked about competitors, Wei was blunt — one in Korea "make[s] a huge amount of money. And I'm jealous about it"; the other in the US has "very strong US Government support" — and then returned to "no shortcut".

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Geography. The advanced nodes that produce 77% of wafer revenue are concentrated in Taiwan. A blockade, conflict or severe earthquake is a tail risk no financial metric captures — the April 3, 2024 earthquake still shows up as insurance-claim income in Q2 2026 operating income. The overseas build-out (Arizona, Japan, Germany) diversifies this, at the margin cost below.
02
Overseas fabs dilute margins — and management says by how much. The CFO's guidance: gross-margin dilution from the overseas ramp of 2–3 percentage points in the early stages, widening to 3–4 points in the latter stages, over "the next several years". Arizona is now a US$265B commitment after July's extra US$100B. On TTM revenue of NT$4,440B, each point of gross margin is roughly NT$44B of gross profit (our arithmetic).
03
Customer and platform concentration. 78% of revenue comes from North America-based customers and 66% from one platform (HPC). Asked on the call whether large customers getting larger is a risk, Wei said "that's not our concern." It is still the risk: AI capex that pauses would hit HPC and the packaging bottleneck in the same quarter.
04
Capital intensity. TTM free cash flow was NT$1,143.6B against NT$2,216.8B of net income — about half of earnings converts to free cash because capex absorbs the rest. The market's reaction in July 2026, when capex was raised, shows investors now price the cost of funding the next phase, not just the growth.

The ADR premium — the "discount" runs the other way

The same company carries two market caps: $2.308T on companiesmarketcap (ADR price × shares) and $2.03T on StockAnalysis (Taiwan-listed price). The gap implies US holders are paying roughly 14% more per underlying share for the NYSE line (our estimate from two rounded figures). Reported in April 2026, UBS's Eric Song put the premium at about 17%, a one-year low, down from nearly 26% in December, against a historical average of around 22%. The premium can compress fast, as the swing from ~26% to ~17% in four months showed. For a US buyer that is a second risk layered on the geopolitical one — the premium itself can shrink even if TSMC's business does not.

Notable One-Off Items

One item matters. In Q2 2026, disposal and mark-to-market gains of NT$63B on Vanguard International Semiconductor shares (TSMC sold 8.1% of Vanguard on May 15, 2026) contributed NT$2.24 to Q2 EPS of NT$27.25. Strip it out and underlying Q2 EPS is NT$25.01, TTM EPS NT$83.25 ($13.09 per ADR), and the trailing P/E ~34.0x instead of 33.1x. Q2 operating income also includes NT$5.27B of other operating income, mainly insurance claims from the April 3, 2024 earthquake. No other material non-recurring items were identified in the sources fetched.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $111.65 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $445.14, the ADR is up 298.7% on price — close to a 4x in five years, before dividends. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

77% of wafer revenue comes from nodes at 7nm and below, 2nm is already 3% in its first quarter, and A14 is on schedule for 2028. HPC is 66% of revenue and grew 20% in one quarter; August revenue grew 53.3% YoY; management guides 2026 growth slightly above 40% in US dollars. All this at a 60% operating margin, ~$77B of net cash and 40% ROE. On FY2027 consensus the ADR is ~19.9x earnings. If AI spending holds through 2027, the growth covers the multiple, and the overseas build-out gradually reduces the Taiwan concentration the market is worried about.

Bear Case

You pay 33x trailing earnings — a premium to the S&P 500 — plus a ~14% ADR premium over the same company's Taiwan shares, for a business whose most valuable plants sit in one geopolitical flashpoint. Growth is concentrated: 78% of revenue from North American customers and two-thirds from one AI-driven platform. Capex jumped to US$60–64B and Arizona to US$265B, and management itself guides overseas fabs to cost 2–3 points of gross margin now and 3–4 later, on top of 3–4 points from the 2nm ramp. July 2026 showed what happens when the market starts pricing the cost of growth: −15.4% in a month after a beat.

Top 3 Risks

01
Taiwan geopolitical risk. The leading-edge capacity behind 77% of wafer revenue is in Taiwan. Nothing in the financial statements prices a blockade or conflict; the trailing multiple and the ADR premium show the market is not applying a large explicit discount today, which also means there is little cushion if that changes.
02
Margin dilution from the build-out. Management's own numbers: overseas fabs −2 to −3 points of gross margin early, −3 to −4 later; 2nm ramp −3 to −4 in H2 2026. Q3 is already guided 1.7 points lower at the midpoint. Each gross-margin point is ~NT$44B a year at the TTM revenue run-rate (our arithmetic).
03
An AI capex cycle turn. HPC is two-thirds of revenue and the packaging bottleneck is the growth constraint. A pause in hyperscaler spending would hit volume, packaging and the capex plan at the same time — and the stock is priced for the consensus Strong Buy, not for that.
Industry Commentary
The price of funding growth
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

July 2026 is the clearest evidence of how the market now reads TSMC. The Q2 release was a beat — US$40.20B at the top of guidance, 67.7% gross margin, a higher full-year outlook — yet the ADR fell about 6% on the day and 15.4% for the month. The new information was cost: the 2026 capital budget rose to US$60–64B from US$52–56B, and C.C. Wei announced another US$100B for Arizona, taking the US commitment to US$265B. Management has no three-year capex figure: "as long as there are business opportunities, we will not hesitate to invest." The market has stopped asking whether demand exists and started asking what the return on each additional dollar of capex will be.

Geopolitics, priced two ways
ONGOING HEADWIND

The brief's premise was a "Taiwan geopolitical discount". The data show something narrower. On an absolute basis there is no discount on the US line: 33.1x trailing versus 26.1x for the S&P 500, and a ~14% ADR premium over the Taiwan shares (17% in April per UBS, versus a ~22% historical average). The discount is relative to growth: 53% trailing EPS growth and ~19.9x FY2027 consensus is cheaper than most companies growing this fast. The Arizona programme is the company's answer, and management has priced it openly: 2–3 points of gross margin now, 3–4 later. Investors are paying, in margin, for geographic diversification.

Agentic AI and the return of the CPU
EMERGING TAILWIND

The newest demand thread on the July call was not GPUs. Wei said agentic AI "is leading to a resurgence in the role of CPUs in AI data centers", and because x86, Arm and RISC-V designs are "almost all TSMC's customers", the company wins whichever architecture prevails. He also welcomed competing packaging technologies — packaging is so tight "it limits my customers' growth", and alternatives free up demand for TSMC's front-end wafers, "which is a majority part of TSMC's business." A foundry that wins on every architecture and can let others relieve its bottleneck sells to the whole industry; it is not a bet on one AI winner.
