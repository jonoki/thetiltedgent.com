01 Company Overview

TSMC is the world's largest contract chipmaker — a pure-play foundry — and, at a $2.14T ADR-implied value (ADR price × shares, our calculation), the #1 company on the Taiwan Stock Exchange by market cap. The case for "near-monopoly at the leading edge" starts with the company's own numbers: in Q3 2026 (Jul–Sep 2026), advanced technologies (7nm and below) were 79% of wafer revenue — 3nm 33%, 5nm 28%, 7nm 10%, and 2nm already 8%, up from 3% in its first revenue quarter. HPC was 68% of net revenue, up from 66% in Q2 2026.

The scale is hard to overstate. FY2025 revenue was NT$3,809.05B, up 31.6%, with diluted EPS of NT$66.25 (+46.4%). The trailing twelve months through September 2026 are larger still: NT$4,944.82B of revenue (+36.2%) and NT$2,547.84B of net income (+60.6%) — a 51.5% net margin on a manufacturing business. Q3 2026 alone printed a 66.4% gross margin and a 58.7% operating margin, and management now expects full-year 2026 revenue growth of about 44% in US-dollar terms, up from the "slightly above 40%" it gave in July. Q3 revenue of US$46.72B finished above the top of the US$44.6–45.8B guidance range, and Q4 is guided to US$52.3–54.3B.

The central debate: Taiwan geopolitical risk versus the growth rate. The data complicate the usual "discount" framing. On the NYSE line TSM trades at 27.1x trailing earnings — above the S&P 500's 25.9x — and US holders pay a premium of about 10% over the Taiwan-listed shares for the ADR wrapper (see Section 04). Where a discount does show up is against the growth: 27x for 61% trailing EPS growth, and roughly 18.0x FY2027 consensus. The question is not whether the market ignores the Strait — it is how much of the growth it declines to pay for because of it. The ADR itself closed 15.1% below its October 5 record close of $485.80 by October 16 (closing prices), nine sessions that included a clean beat and a higher outlook. The home-market benchmark is the TAIEX, where TSMC is by far the largest constituent; this report benchmarks against the S&P 500.

FX exposure: TSMC reports in New Taiwan dollars, while the ADR trades in US dollars and 79% of Q3 2026 revenue came from customers based in North America. Q3 revenue grew 50.9% in NT$ but 41.2% in US$ — the currency flattered the reported NT$ growth by about nine and a half points. Q4 guidance assumes US$1 = NT$31.8.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

79% of wafers at ≤7nm 2nm in volume; A14 risk 2027 Advanced packaging capacity Pure-play: never competes with clients Design-ecosystem lock-in US$60–64B/yr capex Net cash ~$100B

The moat is time plus capital. C.C. Wei put the first half plainly on the July 16 call: developing a new node such as A14, building the capacity and ramping it "now takes five to seven years. There are no shortcuts." The second half is the budget — US$60–64B of capex this year, funded out of a business with a 65.9% trailing gross margin and ~US$100B of net cash (StockAnalysis). A competitor needs both the process know-how and the willingness to spend at that rate for years before earning a return. The pure-play model adds a trust layer: TSMC does not sell chips of its own, so a customer's design is never handed to a rival. On the same July call, asked about competitors, Wei was blunt — one in Korea "make[s] a huge amount of money. And I'm jealous about it"; the other in the US has "very strong US Government support" — and then returned to "no shortcut".

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Geography. The advanced nodes that produce 79% of wafer revenue are concentrated in Taiwan. A blockade, conflict or severe earthquake is a tail risk no financial metric captures — the April 3, 2024 earthquake still shows up as insurance-claim income in Q2 2026 operating income. The overseas build-out (Arizona, Japan, Germany) diversifies this, at the margin cost below.
02
Overseas fabs dilute margins — and management says by how much. The CFO said on October 15 that overseas-fab dilution of about 2 points of gross margin is expected to widen to 3–4 points over the next few years, on top of the 2nm ramp, which drove Q3's 1.3-point sequential decline. Arizona is a US$265B commitment after July's extra US$100B, with its second fab's volume production now slated for Q3 2027. On TTM revenue of NT$4,945B, each point of gross margin is roughly NT$49B of gross profit (our arithmetic).
03
Customer and platform concentration. 79% of Q3 revenue comes from North America-based customers and 68% from one platform (HPC). Asked on the July call whether large customers getting larger is a risk, Wei said "that's not our concern." It is still the risk: AI capex that pauses would hit HPC and the packaging bottleneck in the same quarter.
04
Capital intensity. TTM free cash flow was NT$1,480.7B against NT$2,547.8B of net income — about 58% of earnings converts to free cash because capex absorbs the rest. The market's reaction in July 2026, when capex was raised, and the slide after the October print, with the budget unchanged but 2026–2028 spending guided higher, show investors now price the cost of funding the next phase, not just the growth.

The ADR premium — the "discount" runs the other way

The same company trades in two places. At the October 16 close the NYSE ADR at $412.61 was worth NT$2,668 per ordinary share (× NT$32.33 per US$ ÷ 5 shares per ADR), against a Taiwan close of NT$2,420 that day — US holders paid roughly 10% more per underlying share for the NYSE line (our calculation; the Taiwan market closes hours before New York, and the FX rate is the New York-session close). By the same method the premium was 14.2% on September 21 (ADR $445.14, NT$31.81, Taiwan close NT$2,480). Reported in April 2026, UBS's Eric Song put the premium at about 17%, a one-year low, down from nearly 26% in December, against a historical average of around 22%. The premium can compress fast, as the swing from ~26% to ~17% in four months showed, and it narrowed again in the month to October 16. For a US buyer that is a second risk layered on the geopolitical one — the premium itself can shrink even if TSMC's business does not.

Notable One-Off Items

One item still matters in the trailing figures. In Q2 2026, disposal and mark-to-market gains of NT$63B on Vanguard International Semiconductor shares (TSMC sold 8.1% of Vanguard on May 15, 2026) contributed NT$2.24 to Q2 EPS of NT$27.25, and so sits inside the TTM EPS of NT$98.26. Strip it out and TTM EPS is NT$96.02 ($14.85 per ADR), and the trailing P/E ~27.8x instead of 27.1x. Q3 2026 EPS of NT$29.43 is the first quarter in the TTM window without a gain of that size to flatter it.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 16, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $111.65 to the October 16, 2026 settled close of $412.61, the ADR is up 269.6% on price — close to a 3.7x in five years, before dividends. The path was not smooth: a 2022 drawdown to $61.55 in October 2022 (−49.8% from the January 2022 month-end of $122.63), a slow 2023 recovery, and then the AI leg from 2024 onward, which took the stock through $200 in January 2025 and to a $477.57 month-end high in June 2026. The intraday high is $487.47 (October 5, 2026; closing high $485.80).

The 6-period monthly RSI shows how persistent the momentum has been: it stayed above 70 for every month-end of 2024 and peaked at 92.4 in June 2024. July 2026's 15.4% drop cut it from 86.4 to 60.6; it recovered to 69.6 at the September month-end, and with October's 9.6% decline so far (to the October 16 close, a partial month) it reads 56.3 — neutral, between the 30 and 70 lines. The 10-month moving average is about $402, some 2.5% below the price, so the long-term trend is intact; the price sits 3.6% below the 3-month average (~$428), which turned up after July.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

79% of wafer revenue comes from nodes at 7nm and below, 2nm is already 8% in its second quarter, and A14 risk production is on track for 2027 with volume in 2028 (management, October 15). HPC is 68% of revenue and grew 22% in one quarter; Q3 revenue grew 50.9% YoY; management guides 2026 growth of about 44% in US dollars. All this at a 58.7% operating margin, ~$100B of net cash and 42% ROE. On FY2027 consensus the ADR is ~18.0x earnings. If AI spending holds through 2027, the growth covers the multiple, and the overseas build-out gradually reduces the Taiwan concentration the market is worried about.

Bear Case

You pay 27x trailing earnings — a premium to the S&P 500's 25.9x — plus a ~10% ADR premium over the same company's Taiwan shares, for a business whose most valuable plants sit in one geopolitical flashpoint. Growth is concentrated: 79% of revenue from North American customers and over two-thirds from one AI-driven platform. The 2026 capex budget is US$60–64B with 2026–2028 spending guided higher, Arizona is US$265B, and management itself guides overseas fabs to cost about 2 points of gross margin now and 3–4 later, on top of the 2nm ramp. The stock has sold off on good news twice: −15.4% in July 2026 after a beat, and −15.1% from the October 5 closing high to October 16, a window that includes a Q3 beat and a higher outlook.

Top 3 Risks

01
Taiwan geopolitical risk. The leading-edge capacity behind 79% of wafer revenue is in Taiwan. Nothing in the financial statements prices a blockade or conflict; the trailing multiple and the ADR premium show the market is not applying a large explicit discount today, which also means there is little cushion if that changes.
02
Margin dilution from the build-out. Management's own numbers: overseas fabs about −2 points of gross margin now, widening to −3 to −4 over the next few years; the 2nm ramp took 1.3 points off Q3 (66.4%) and Q4 is guided to 65–67%, a 66.0% midpoint. Each gross-margin point is ~NT$49B a year at the TTM revenue run-rate (our arithmetic).
03
An AI capex cycle turn. HPC is over two-thirds of revenue and the packaging bottleneck is the growth constraint. A pause in hyperscaler spending would hit volume, packaging and the capex plan at the same time — and the stock is priced for the consensus Strong Buy, not for that.
Industry Commentary
The price of funding growth
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

July 2026 is the clearest evidence of how the market now reads TSMC. The Q2 release was a beat — US$40.20B at the top of guidance, 67.7% gross margin, a higher full-year outlook — yet the ADR fell 2.3% on the day, 2.8% the next, and 15.4% for the month. The new information was cost: the 2026 capital budget rose to US$60–64B from US$52–56B, and C.C. Wei announced another US$100B for Arizona, taking the US commitment to US$265B. October repeated the pattern in milder form: Q3 revenue beat the top of guidance, the 2026 growth outlook went to about 44%, the capex range was left alone, and management said 2026–2028 capex will be significantly higher than the past three years. The ADR closed -1.80% on the print day and -2.28% the day after. CFO Wendell Huang said on the call that "if we do our job right, the increase in revenue will outpace the increase in CapEx". The market has stopped asking whether demand exists and started asking what the return on each additional dollar of capex will be.

Geopolitics, priced two ways
ONGOING HEADWIND

The usual framing is a "Taiwan geopolitical discount". The data show something narrower. On an absolute basis there is little discount on the US line: 27.1x trailing versus 25.9x for the S&P 500, and a ~10% ADR premium over the Taiwan shares at October 16 (our calculation; 17% in April per UBS, versus a ~22% historical average). The discount is relative to growth: 61% trailing EPS growth and ~18.0x FY2027 consensus is cheaper than most companies growing this fast. The Arizona programme is the company's answer, and management has priced it openly: about 2 points of gross margin now, 3–4 later. Investors are paying, in margin, for geographic diversification.

Agentic AI and the return of the CPU
EMERGING TAILWIND

The newest demand thread on the July call was not GPUs. Wei said agentic AI "is leading to a resurgence in the role of CPUs in AI data centers", and because x86, Arm and RISC-V designs are "almost all TSMC's customers", the company wins whichever architecture prevails. He also welcomed competing packaging technologies — packaging is so tight "it limits my customers' growth", and alternatives free up demand for TSMC's front-end wafers, "which is a majority part of TSMC's business." A foundry that wins on every architecture and can let others relieve its bottleneck sells to the whole industry; it is not a bet on one AI winner. On October 15 Wei repeated the point: "all the AI players are working with TSMC", and agentic AI CPUs and networking chips are adding to silicon demand.
