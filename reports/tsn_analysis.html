01 Company Overview

Tyson Foods is four businesses under one roof, and in 2026 they pointed in different directions. It buys live cattle and hogs and turns them into beef and pork; it raises and processes its own chickens; and it makes the packaged stuff — Tyson, Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm, Ball Park, Wright, Aidells, State Fair. Fiscal 2026 sales were $56.42B, but only $453M of that reached net income — a 0.80% net margin. That is the whole shape of the company: a very large top line, a very thin cushion, and the size of the cushion set by the price of an animal Tyson does not control.

In 2026 that animal was the steer. With the U.S. cattle herd small, packers bid up for every head they buy while shoppers push back on beef prices; Tyson's beef business lost $654M on an adjusted basis in fiscal 2026, up from a $275M loss the year before, and the company cut its outlook twice. Chicken ($1.86B) and prepared foods ($1.33B) carried the year. The fourth-quarter results on November 16, 2026 changed the timing question rather than the story: the first fiscal-2027 outlook sees beef anywhere from a $300M loss to a $100M profit, helped by closed plants, while chicken is guided down to $1.3B–$1.6B. The total lands at $1.7B–$2.15B of adjusted operating income, against $1.98B in fiscal 2026. The stock rose 8.72% that day. COO Wes Morris put the company's view on the call: "The challenges in our Beef segment are a cattle cycle issue, not a broad deterioration in the business."

A beef packer's profit is the spread between what it pays for a live steer and what it sells the cuts for. When the national herd is small, that spread gets squeezed from the cost side, and there is no quick fix: a heifer held back to rebuild the herd is one fewer animal going to slaughter today, and a calf born this year takes the better part of two years to reach market weight. Q4 lost $171M on an adjusted basis (a year earlier: $52M), taking fiscal 2026 to a $654M loss — inside the $625M–$775M range Tyson set on September 3, nearer its better end. Tyson has shrunk the footprint to match the herd: the 10-K says it closed two beef plants and one case-ready plant (where meat is cut and packaged for supermarket shelves) shared with pork during fiscal 2026. The closures announced in August were the Joslin, Illinois beef plant and the Eagle Mountain, Utah case-ready plant, with the Pasco, Washington beef plant for sale. For fiscal 2027 the company sees U.S. beef production down about 2% and Tyson's beef volumes lower because of the restructuring and tight cattle supply, yet guides the segment to between a $300M loss and a $100M profit — a smaller business expected to lose less. On the call Morris said Tyson will fully reopen the second shift at Amarillo, Texas.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale across four proteins Brand portfolio (Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm) Vertically integrated chicken Plant network & logistics Retail & foodservice relationships Family control = long horizon

The moat is diversification plus brands, and fiscal 2026 was its stress test. A pure beef packer in this cycle would have lost money with nothing to offset it; Tyson's chicken and prepared foods kept total segment operating income positive at $2.95B for the year even as beef lost $654M. The brands matter more than they look: prepared foods earns a double-digit margin on under a fifth of the sales, and it is the one segment guided to hold its ground in fiscal 2027. What the moat does not do is protect the income statement from the cattle cycle. Gross margin was 13.87% in fiscal 2021 and 5.73% in fiscal 2026. Scale in a commodity business lowers your cost per pound; it does not set the price of the steer.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Beef is a price-taker at both ends. Tyson buys cattle at the market and sells beef at the market. The outlook was cut twice in fiscal 2026, and the fiscal-2027 beef range spans $400M, from a $300M loss to a $100M profit — the company itself cannot say within $400M what beef will earn, because the herd does not rebuild on a quarterly timetable.
02
The dividend is bigger than GAAP earnings. $2.08 a share at the new rate against $1.28 of fiscal-2026 GAAP EPS — a 162.5% payout ratio. On cash it was covered in fiscal 2026: $704M of dividends paid against $1.66B of free cash flow. At the new rates ($2.08 a Class A share, $1.872 a Class B share) the bill is about $702M a year (our calculation from the share counts on the 10-K cover), against fiscal-2027 free cash flow guided to $0.8B–$1.3B: covered 1.1x to 1.9x.
03
Chicken's good run is fading. Q4 chicken profit fell to $389M from $562M, and fiscal 2027 is guided to $1.3B–$1.6B against $1.86B. Chicken was the story of fiscal 2024–2026; the new outlook takes $260M–$560M out of it, eating much of beef's expected $354M–$754M improvement (our arithmetic on the company's ranges).
04
Governance: one vote does not carry far. The Tyson Limited Partnership controls 71.48% of the vote through ten-vote Class B shares. Minority holders cannot force a strategy change, a sale, or a break-up; they depend on the family and a CEO who started on October 4 and comes from consumer brands rather than meat.

Notable One-Off Items

The gap between GAAP and adjusted numbers is wide and it matters for the P/E. Q4 FY2026 GAAP EPS was −$0.22 against adjusted $0.61; GAAP operating income was $1M against adjusted $361M. The quarter carried $254M of restructuring and related charges (the plant closures) and $124M of legal contingency accruals, $110M of it booked as a reduction of sales. For the full year, GAAP operating income was $1.10B against adjusted $1.98B, with $429M of restructuring charges and an $83M charge for the CEO transition. The extra (53rd) week added $1.05B to fourth-quarter and full-year sales. That is why the trailing P/E (49.7) and forward P/E (17.8) tell such different stories: one uses reported earnings, the other adjusted estimates.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $78.96 to the November 17, 2026 settled close of $63.65, TSN lost 19.4% of its price; SPY, an S&P 500 index fund, rose 60.8% over the same dates (price only). Dividends of $9.30 a share went ex in the window (19 quarterly payments, Yahoo Finance's dividend record), which brings the loss to −7.6% with dividends added back but not reinvested (our calculation). The shape is a cycle, not a line: a 2022 peak when margins were fat, a 2023 collapse into a loss-making year, a two-year grind back to the mid-$60s, a 2026 slide to a September month-end of $50.53 — the lowest since November 2023 — as the cattle shortage turned beef into the whole story, and then a 26.0% rebound by November 17, most of it in October and on the day of the fourth-quarter results.

The 6-period monthly RSI is 62.9 — neutral, up from 24.6 at the September month-end, which was the second-lowest reading in the five-year series after 23.5 in May 2023. The 10-month moving average is about $59.57 and the 3-month about $57.07, both now below the price: the short-term trend has turned up. The two oversold episodes ended differently. After May 2023 the stock rose for two months, then fell to $46.35 by October 2023; after September 2026 it rose 12.9% in October and another 11.6% by November 17.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The bottom of the cattle cycle is in the numbers, and the company is now guiding up from it. Beef lost $654M in fiscal 2026; the fiscal-2027 range runs from a $300M loss to a $100M profit, and every dollar of that improvement lands in a total that was $1.98B this year. Prepared foods — the branded business — grew profit to $1.33B and is guided to hold or grow. The balance sheet got stronger in a bad year: debt down $1.08B to $7.75B, $1.66B of free cash flow, net leverage 2.1x, $4.1B of liquidity, and a dividend raised again. At $63.65 you get a 3.27% yield, a forward P/E of 17.8 on consensus fiscal-2027 EPS of $3.57, and analysts expecting $4.75 in fiscal 2028. Five of the six post-print calls raised their targets, to as high as $75.

Bear Case

The stock has already priced the turn: up 26% from the September month-end, while the outlook's midpoint ($1.925B) is below fiscal 2026's $1.98B. The beef fix is offset by chicken, guided down by $260M–$560M as supply grows across the industry; sales are guided to shrink 3.5%–5.5%. Fiscal-2027 free cash flow of $0.8B–$1.3B covers the roughly $702M dividend bill only 1.1x at the low end, while capex rises. GAAP EPS was $1.28, ROE 2.63% and ROIC 3.11% — below what shareholders and lenders could reasonably expect for the risk — and the dividend is 162.5% of GAAP earnings. A new CEO from consumer brands is running a meat business, and the family's 71.48% of the vote means outside holders cannot push for anything. After the rally, the average analyst target is only 5.4% above the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
The cattle cycle runs longer than the outlook assumes. The fiscal-2027 beef range is $400M wide, and Tyson cut its full-year outlook twice during fiscal 2026. U.S. beef production is expected to fall again in fiscal 2027, and herd rebuilding is measured in years.
02
Chicken keeps falling. Q4 chicken profit was down 31% from a year earlier and fiscal 2027 is guided 14%–30% below fiscal 2026. Chicken is a commodity with fast supply response; if the industry keeps adding birds, the low end of the $1.3B–$1.6B range is in play.
03
Dividend coverage and transition risk together. At $2.08 a share the dividend exceeds GAAP EPS, and guided free cash flow covers it 1.1x–1.9x (our calculation) while capex rises to $1.1B–$1.4B. A weak beef year under a new CEO is the combination that would put it in play.
Industry Commentary
The cattle shortage — one of the most severe in U.S. history
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Tyson's own language on September 3 was "significant margin compression amid volatile cattle prices and one of the most severe cattle shortages in U.S. history." The mechanics are simple and slow. Fewer cattle means packers compete for each animal; cattle cost rises faster than retail beef can follow without losing shoppers. Rebuilding the herd requires holding heifers back from slaughter, which tightens supply further before it loosens it. The industry's answer so far has been fewer plants: Tyson closed two beef plants and a case-ready plant in fiscal 2026, and for fiscal 2027 it expects U.S. beef production down about 2%. Morris on the Q4 call: "The challenges in our Beef segment are a cattle cycle issue, not a broad deterioration in the business."

Consumers trading between proteins — and too much chicken
MIXED

Expensive beef pushes demand toward chicken and pork, which is part of why Tyson's chicken carried fiscal 2024–2026. The flip side arrived in the fourth quarter: chicken profit fell from $562M to $389M, which Talk Business & Politics tied to weaker foodservice sales and industry oversupply, and pork's average price fell 13.6% on ample hog supply. Tyson expects U.S. chicken production up about 1% in fiscal 2027. Branded prepared foods is the part that held up, growing profit about 5% in the year. The multi-protein model hedges the cycle; it does not escape it.

Where the upside comes from
EMERGING TAILWIND

The swing factor is still beef, and the arithmetic is lopsided. Fiscal-2027 guidance has beef between a $300M loss and a $100M profit while the other segments are guided to earn $2.75B–$3.38B combined (chicken, prepared foods, pork and international; our addition of the company's ranges), less about $1.0B of corporate costs and amortisation. If beef returned to its pre-shortage profits, the gap to today's guide would be the upside; when that happens is the question no one has answered. Consensus has fiscal-2027 adjusted EPS of $3.57 and fiscal-2028 of $4.75 — the second number is the bet that the turn comes.
