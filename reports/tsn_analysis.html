01 Company Overview

Tyson Foods is four businesses under one roof, and in 2026 they are pointed in different directions. It buys live cattle and hogs and turns them into beef and pork; it raises and processes its own chickens; and it makes the packaged stuff — Tyson, Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm, Ball Park, Wright, Aidells, State Fair. Trailing-twelve-month sales were $55.69B, but only $574M of that reached net income — a 1.03% net margin. That is the whole shape of the company: a very large top line, a very thin cushion, and the size of the cushion set by the price of an animal Tyson does not control.

Right now that animal is the steer. Management describes "one of the most severe cattle shortages in U.S. history". When there are few cattle, the packer bids up for every head it buys while the consumer pushes back on price — in fiscal Q3 2026 beef volumes fell 15.9% while pricing rose 12.1%, and the segment still lost $138M. On September 3, 2026 Tyson cut its full-year outlook for the second time, widening the expected beef loss to $625M–$775M, and the stock fell 7.26% that day. Meanwhile chicken made $488M in the same quarter, its seventh straight quarter of growth. The bet on TSN is a bet on timing: how long the cattle cycle keeps beef under water, and whether chicken and prepared foods can carry the dividend until it turns. A new CEO takes the chair on October 4, 2026.

A beef packer's profit is the spread between what it pays for a live steer and what it sells the cuts for. When the national herd is small, that spread gets squeezed from the cost side, and there is no quick fix: a heifer held back to rebuild the herd is one fewer animal going to slaughter today, and a calf born this year takes the better part of two years to reach market weight. That is why Q3 looked the way it did — volume −15.9%, price +12.1%, sales −3.8%, and a $138M adjusted operating loss. In August Tyson guided a fiscal-2026 beef loss of $500M–$650M; on September 3 it widened that to $625M–$775M, citing volatile cattle prices and the hit from lower cattle prices on the value of live-cattle inventory. Tyson is also shrinking the footprint: case-ready plants in Joslin, Illinois and Eagle Mountain, Utah are closing and the Pasco, Washington plant is planned for sale.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale across four proteins Brand portfolio (Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm) Vertically integrated chicken Plant network & logistics Retail & foodservice relationships Family control = long horizon

The moat is diversification plus brands, and 2026 is its stress test. A pure beef packer in this cycle would be losing money with nothing to offset it; Tyson's chicken and prepared foods kept total segment operating income positive at $779M in Q3 even as beef lost $138M. The brands matter more than they look: prepared foods earns a double-digit margin on a fraction of the sales, and its retail volumes held up better than the category. What the moat does not do is protect the income statement from the cattle cycle. Gross margin was 13.87% in fiscal 2021 and 6.55% on a trailing basis now. Scale in a commodity business lowers your cost per pound; it does not set the price of the steer.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Beef is a price-taker at both ends. Tyson buys cattle at the market and sells beef at the market. The Q3 volume drop of 15.9% alongside a 12.1% price rise and a loss says higher prices are not keeping up with what cattle cost. The outlook was cut twice in 2026, and the herd does not rebuild on a quarterly timetable.
02
The dividend is bigger than GAAP earnings. $2.04 a share against $1.62 of trailing EPS — a 126% payout ratio on GAAP earnings. On cash it is covered: $2.04 × 350.9M shares ≈ $716M against $1.16B of TTM free cash flow (our calculation, ~62%). Thin, but covered — as long as chicken holds.
03
Chicken's good run is the cushion — and it was trimmed too. The September 3 update lowered chicken's range to $1.85B–$1.95B on softer foodservice demand. Chicken has been the story of fiscal 2024–2026; any reversal lands on a company with a 1% net margin and a beef segment already under water.
04
Governance: one vote does not carry far. The Tyson Limited Partnership controls 71.48% of the vote through ten-vote Class B shares. Minority holders cannot force a strategy change, a sale, or a break-up; they depend on the family and a CEO who starts on October 4 and comes from consumer brands rather than meat.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $78.94 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $52.02, TSN lost 34.1% of its price. Dividends of roughly $9.8 a share over the window (an estimate: fiscal 2022–2025 dividends of $1.84, $1.92, $1.96 and $2.00 plus the $2.04 trailing figure) bring the loss to roughly −22% before reinvestment. The shape is a cycle, not a line: a 2022 peak when margins were fat, a 2023 collapse into a loss-making year, a two-year grind back to the mid-$60s, and a 2026 slide as the cattle shortage turned beef from a drag into the whole story.

The 6-period monthly RSI is 27.0 — below 30, formally oversold, and the second-lowest reading in the five-year series after 22.4 in May 2023. The 10-month moving average is about $60.05 and the 3-month about $55.04, both above the price: the trend is down on every timescale shown. Oversold is a statement about speed, not value. The last time TSN was this oversold, in May 2023, it did not bottom until October 2023 at $46.35, five months later.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying near the bottom of a cattle cycle, not a broken company. Beef is guided to lose $625M–$775M this year; any recovery toward break-even is operating income that goes straight to the bottom line of a company guiding $1.85B–$2.05B in total. Meanwhile chicken ($1.85B–$1.95B) and prepared foods ($1.30B–$1.35B) — the two businesses growing through the cycle — already earn more than the whole company's guide. At $52.02 you get a 3.92% yield covered by free cash flow, 2.1x net leverage, $4.0B of liquidity, consensus EPS rising 11.38% into FY2027, an average analyst target of $64.42, a fresh J.P. Morgan upgrade, and a monthly RSI of 27. Cycles turn; the Mexican cattle border is a first sign of supply returning.

Bear Case

The outlook has been cut twice in one year and nobody controls when cattle come back. Trailing EPS is $1.62, ROE 3.26%, ROIC 4.21% — returns below the cost of capital. The dividend is 126% of GAAP earnings. Chicken, the cushion, was trimmed too, on softer foodservice demand. A new CEO from consumer brands starts in a meat crisis, and the family's 71.48% of the vote means outside holders cannot push for anything. Analyst targets still sit above the price because they have not fully absorbed September 3. The last oversold reading, in May 2023, was followed by five more months of decline.

Top 3 Risks

01
The cattle cycle runs longer than the balance sheet's patience. Beef losses were widened on September 3 to $625M–$775M for fiscal 2026. Herd rebuilding is measured in years, and the Mexican-import help is about a year away by management's own estimate.
02
Chicken normalises. Chicken has carried the company for seven straight quarters of growth. It is also a commodity with fast supply response; the September 3 trim on foodservice softness is a reminder that the good run is not guaranteed.
03
Dividend coverage and transition risk together. At $2.04 a share the dividend exceeds GAAP EPS; FCF of $1.16B covers it about 1.6x on our math. A weaker FY2027 plus a new CEO's first reset is the combination that would put it in play.
Industry Commentary
The cattle shortage — one of the most severe in U.S. history
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Tyson's own language on September 3 was "significant margin compression amid volatile cattle prices and one of the most severe cattle shortages in U.S. history." The mechanics are simple and slow. Fewer cattle means packers compete for each animal; cattle cost rises faster than retail beef can follow without losing shoppers (Tyson's Q3 beef volume −15.9% against price +12.1%). Rebuilding the herd requires holding heifers back from slaughter, which tightens supply further before it loosens it. The one near-term supply source management named is the reopened Mexican border, historically about 5% of U.S. harvest, with benefits close to a year out. Specific national herd statistics were not in the sources fetched.

Consumers trading between proteins
MIXED

Expensive beef pushes demand toward chicken and pork, which is part of why Tyson's chicken has grown seven quarters in a row. But the September 3 update also cited a challenging foodservice environment and softer consumer demand in chicken, and excess pork supply pressuring pork margins. Branded prepared foods held up better than the category — Tyson's retail branded volume −0.4% against −1.9% for food and beverage — but absorbed about $30M of higher beef-trim cost in Q3. The multi-protein model hedges the cycle; it does not escape it.

Where the upside comes from
EMERGING TAILWIND

The swing factor is beef, and the arithmetic is lopsided. Fiscal 2026 guidance has beef losing $625M–$775M while the other segments are guided to earn $3.50B–$3.75B combined (chicken, prepared foods, pork and international; our addition of the company's ranges). If beef simply returned to break-even, segment income would rise by the size of today's loss. When that happens is the question no one has answered — Stephens' Pooran Sharma put it plainly: "the timing and magnitude of a beef recovery remain the key swing factors for earnings." Consensus is betting on FY2027 EPS of $4.13; the stock at $52.02 says the market wants to see it first.
