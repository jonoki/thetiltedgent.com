01 Company Overview

Trane Technologies makes and services HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning — the equipment that moves heat into or out of a building) systems for commercial, industrial and residential buildings, plus transport refrigeration under the Thermo King brand. Full-year 2025 revenue was $21.32 billion, up 7.5%, with adjusted EPS of $13.06 (up 16%). Over the trailing twelve months through September 2026, revenue is $23.23 billion, up 10.3%, and the third quarter alone grew 18%. Trane is a member of the S&P 500; its rank by market value is in the header.

The reason a company that makes air conditioners belongs in a series about AI infrastructure is physical rather than digital. A data centre is a building whose purpose is to turn electricity into heat and then get that heat out again, and at scale that is done with chillers (large machines that cool water, which is then circulated to remove heat from a building) and the pumps, piping and controls around them. That is Trane's applied-systems business. But data centres are a fast-growing part of Trane's commercial HVAC demand, not the whole of it. The steadier part is the installed base (equipment Trane has already sold that is running in buildings) and the service attach rate (the share of that equipment for which Trane also sells maintenance, parts and controls). Management said on the Q3 call that services are about one-third of company revenue and have grown at a low-teens annual rate since 2020.

Annual Growth Since 2020
Management said on the Q3 2026 call that services are about one-third of company revenue and have grown at a low-teens compound annual rate since 2020; Americas services grew about 10% in the quarter. The mechanism is simple and slow-moving: a commercial chiller is a decades-long asset. Once installed, it needs planned maintenance, refrigerant, spare parts, controls upgrades and eventually a replacement, and the manufacturer, whose technicians know the machine and whose controls sit on it, is the natural provider. Every unit sold therefore seeds recurring revenue that runs for the life of the machine. Two consequences follow. First, margin: service revenue needs less capital and faces less price competition than equipment, so a rising services share tends to lift the group's operating margin (adjusted operating margin was 18.5% in FY2025). Second, valuation: recurring, contracted revenue is usually worth a higher price-to-earnings multiple than lumpy equipment revenue, which helps explain why investors pay more for Trane than for a pure equipment maker.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Installed Base Revenue Service Attach Rate Direct Sales & Service Network Applied Engineering Capacity Controls & Building Automation Lock-In Low-GWP Refrigerant Position $12.5B Backlog Visibility

The moat is not the box; it is what happens after the box is installed. A large applied chiller is specified by a consulting engineer years before it ships, commissioned on site, integrated into a building automation system, and then maintained for two to four decades. Switching supplier mid-life means re-engineering the central plant, re-training the facilities team and, usually, replacing controls, so the original maker tends to keep the service, parts and upgrade work. That is why services can be about a third of revenue growing at a low-teens annual rate: it builds on the cumulative installed base, not on this year's shipments. Trane reinforces this with a largely direct commercial sales and service organisation rather than a pure distribution model. The result shows in returns: ROE of 36.9% and ROIC of 28.4% on a business with only $4.22B of total debt. Meanwhile backlog of $12.5 billion, up 73%, with more than $8 billion scheduled for 2027 and beyond, gives forward revenue visibility that most industrials lack — book-to-bill was 107% in Q3 2026 and above 100% in every segment.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. Non-residential construction is still a cycle. Backlog of $12.5B is about 54% of trailing revenue and gives real visibility, but it is an order book, not a contract in perpetuity. Orders are what the market watches, and they slowed: enterprise bookings grew 21% in Q3 after 39% in Q2, and Oppenheimer wrote that the sequential slowdown contributed to the share price drop. Management said on the call that it was not concerned about the slowdown, attributing it to the lumpiness of large data-centre projects.

2. Margins are not widening yet. Q3 2026 adjusted operating margin was 20.2%, down 40 basis points (0.4 percentage points) from 20.6%; GAAP operating margin (under U.S. accounting rules) was 19.9% against 20.3%. Gross margin, our calculation of revenue less cost of goods sold from the release's income statement, was 35.3% against 36.9% a year earlier. Management said price was below expectations in Q3 on timing and mix, and the release cites supply-chain and inflation headwinds. A lower-tier component supplier caught by U.S. forced-labour import rules caused shortages and rework in the quarter, which management said was fully behind it by the call.

3. Refrigerant rules keep moving. The refrigerant transition creates replacement demand because rules set the dates, and the dates have shifted. EPA's reconsideration of its Technology Transitions rules was finalised and published on 26 May 2026: supermarket and remote-condensing systems get an interim 1,400 GWP limit (global warming potential, how strongly a gas warms the atmosphere) until 1 Jan 2032, when the 150 or 300 limits apply; semiconductor-industry chillers have until 1 Jan 2030; and the rule removed the 1 Jan 2026 installation deadline for certain older residential and light-commercial systems. EPA also proposed exempting transport refrigeration units from the 2024 leak-repair requirements. Every deferral pushes replacement demand later, although Holland & Knight notes EPA did not change the rule that phases down HFC supply.

4. Geographic concentration. With 83.6% of Q3 2026 revenue in the Americas, Trane depends heavily on one construction cycle and one regulatory regime. EMEA's 17.2% margin is below the group's 20.2% and lost about $18M of operating income year on year, 80% or more of it to the Middle East conflict, per the call. Asia Pacific is only 5.3% of revenue, and China is about a third of it.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Trane is a business with steady, recurring revenue disguised as an air-conditioning manufacturer. Services are about a third of revenue and have grown at a low-teens rate since 2020, so each year more of the business is repair, parts and upgrades attached to a decades-long installed base. ROIC is 28.4%. On top of that sits unusual forward visibility: a $12.5B backlog, up 73%, with more than $8B scheduled for 2027 and beyond, book-to-bill of 107%, and revenue guidance just raised to about 13.5% growth. Data centres, a residential recovery and a transport-refrigeration upcycle give several independent sources of demand — and at 28.95 times trailing earnings, down from 33.69 two months ago, the stock is cheaper than it was before a quarter that beat estimates.

Bear Case

You are paying 26.9 times this year's expected earnings for a business whose margins are not widening: Q3 adjusted operating margin fell 40 basis points and gross margin fell from 36.9% to 35.3% despite 18% revenue growth, because price came in below plan, costs rose and acquired revenue such as Stellar carries lower margins. Orders slowed from +39% to +21%, fourth-quarter guidance was below Wall Street expectations according to Oppenheimer, and the stock has told you how it feels: −16.8% from its April close and −8.6% in two sessions after a beat. EMEA's margin fell 250 basis points and Asia Pacific's 390. Refrigerant deadlines have moved later, and 84% of revenue rides one American construction cycle.

  Industry Commentary
Data centres are buildings with a heat problem
EMERGING TAILWIND
Every watt a server consumes becomes a watt of heat that must leave the building, and at gigawatt scale that is a chilled-water and thermal-management engineering problem, not an IT one. Trane's evidence of participation, per its Q3 2026 call: global applied bookings up more than 40%, about 1,700 mega-projects tracked (over 90% data centres), more than $8 billion of backlog scheduled for 2027 and beyond, and Stellar Energy at ~$500M of revenue in 2026, targeting about $1B in a couple of years. But scale it honestly: $500M against roughly $24.2B of guided 2026 revenue is about 2% of the company. Data centres are the fastest-growing part of the mix, not the mix. Anyone buying TT purely as an AI-infrastructure bet is buying a diversified building-systems company.
The refrigerant transition — a replacement schedule that keeps moving
DATES MOVED LATER
Refrigerant GWP (global warming potential — how much a gas warms the atmosphere relative to CO₂ over a set period) is regulated in the US under the AIM Act, which phases down hydrofluorocarbon (HFC) supply; Holland & Knight notes EPA made no change to the rule that implements that phasedown. The Technology Transitions rules set GWP limits by equipment type and date, which forces equipment replacement on a calendar and favours whoever moved earliest on low-GWP designs. But the calendar has moved, and the previous edition of this report treated it as still pending: EPA's reconsideration was finalised and published on 26 May 2026 (91 Fed. Reg. 31284). Supermarket and remote-condensing systems get an interim 1,400 GWP limit until 1 Jan 2032, when 150 or 300 applies; cold-storage warehouses have an interim 700 GWP limit to 2032; semiconductor-industry chillers get until 1 Jan 2030; and the 1 Jan 2026 installation deadline was removed for certain older residential and light-commercial systems. A separate proposal would exempt transport refrigeration units from the 2024 leak-repair requirements. Every move later defers the replacement demand these rules create.
The bull-bear frame: steady revenue priced like a cyclical, or a cyclical priced like steady revenue?
THE CENTRAL QUESTION
Everything reduces to which half of Trane you think dominates. The bull frame: a third of revenue is services growing at a low-teens rate off an installed base that only gets bigger, ROIC of 28.4%, an Irish domicile holding the tax rate at 18.0% for nine months, $12.5B of backlog with more than $8B in 2027+, and several independent demand sources (data centres, a residential recovery, a 2027 freight upcycle). On that view 28.95 times trailing earnings is not expensive. The bear frame: 84% of revenue is Americas construction, margins are not widening, orders slowed to +21%, refrigerant deadlines are moving later, and the market has voted: TT closed October 2026 1.5% below its November 2024 close, with 9 of 26 analysts at Hold. The next resolution point is the Q4 report and formal 2027 guidance in late January: if orders re-accelerate and the operating margin starts widening, the steady-revenue frame wins; if orders keep cooling while margins slip, the stock trades like a company whose profits rise and fall with the construction cycle.
