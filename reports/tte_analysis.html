01 Company Overview

TotalEnergies is France's oil major and, by market capitalisation, the third-largest company in France (behind L'Oréal and LVMH) and #97 in the world, per companiesmarketcap. It is a constituent of the CAC 40 in Paris; the US line is not an ADR any more — since December 8, 2025 the company's ordinary shares trade directly on the NYSE under the same ticker, one-for-one with the old ADRs. It runs five segments: E&P, Integrated LNG, Integrated Power, Refining & Chemicals, and Marketing & Services. FY2025 revenue was $182.34B, down 6.78%, with GAAP net income of $13.13B and EPS of $5.78. Then 2026 happened: on a TTM basis through June 2026, revenue is $196.38B, net income $17.84B and EPS $8.02, up 46.2%.

The reason is in the company's own words: "a high-price environment related to the Middle East conflict." Brent averaged $103.8/b in Q2 2026 against $67.9 a year earlier, the company's European refining margin marker averaged $12.4/b in 1H26 versus $4.3/b in 1H25, and the stock rose from a $65.42 year-end close to $90.06 — while TotalEnergies itself lost an average 210 kboe/d of Middle East production because of difficulties getting cargoes through the Strait of Hormuz. That is the first thing to understand about this report: the windfall is real but it is not the thesis. The thesis is the gap underneath it. On the same Monday close, TTE trades at 8.76x forward earnings versus 13.21x for ExxonMobil and 13.09x for Chevron, and at 5.91x EV/EBITDA versus 10.06x and 8.53x. Shell, the other European major, sits at 8.99x forward — so the discount is European, not TotalEnergies-specific. The market's stated reason for the European haircut is capital allocated to low-carbon power at utility-like returns. TotalEnergies discloses exactly that number, which lets us test it: the Integrated Power segment earned a 7.6% return on average capital employed over the twelve months to June 2026, against a company target of 12% by 2030 and a group average of 13.9%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

$5/b upstream operating cost Global LNG portfolio (incl. new ECA LNG, Mexico) Integrated trading book Brazil / US Gulf / Suriname deepwater Flexible gas generation (EPH) Investment-grade balance sheet (13.1% gearing) Dual Paris / NYSE listing

The moat in oil is cost position and portfolio breadth, not technology. At $5/b upstream opex TotalEnergies is profitable deep into any plausible down-cycle, and the integrated model did what it is meant to do in 2026: when Hormuz disrupted its Middle East barrels, refining margins and trading captured the price on the other side of the same event. The Power strategy is a bet that the same logic — owning generation, flexible gas plants, trading and retail customers together — can earn oil-like returns in electricity. So far it earns 7.6%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Power returns may never reach 12%. TTM ROACE of 7.6% on $30.87B of capital employed; the 2030 target needs roughly $3.7B of ANOI on today's capital alone (our arithmetic: 0.12 × $30.87B), versus $2.21B earned. Q2 ANOI of $533M was below Q2 2025's $574M even with EPH assets contributing for two months. The segment is growing its capital faster than its profit.
02
Hormuz exposure cuts both ways. 5–10% of company production is affected by the Middle East conflict per management, and liftings are "conditional on the ability to export through the Strait of Hormuz." A resolution lowers the oil price and restores volume; an escalation raises price but strands more barrels.
03
The earnings base is a windfall. R&C ANOI of $1.8B in Q2 is 4.6x the year-earlier quarter; consensus (StockAnalysis) already has FY2027 EPS falling 9.4% from FY2026's $11.16. A trailing P/E of 11.2x on peak-ish earnings is less cheap than it looks.
04
Home-market politics. A voluntary pump-price cap in France, a staff fuel bonus and competition complaints from French retailers show how a national champion's margins become public property in a price spike. European windfall-tax risk is a recurring feature of 2022-style environments (no 2026 measure verified in sources fetched).

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the numbers. First, GAAP vs adjusted: Q2 2026 GAAP net income was $5.44B against adjusted net income of $6.03B — but in Q1 2026 GAAP ($5.81B) was above adjusted ($5.39B); inventory valuation effects swing with the oil price and make single-quarter GAAP EPS noisy. Second, share count: diluted weighted shares rose from 2,164M in Q1 to 2,216M in Q2 (+2.8% QoQ on StockAnalysis's count) because ~95.4M new shares were issued to EPH at closing on April 29, 2026 — while the year-over-year count is still down 4.09% from buybacks. Third, portfolio moves: Integrated Power booked $749M of net divestments in Q2 (including the sale of all European distributed-solar assets), which lowers reported net investment and flatters the capex-to-guidance comparison. Beyond these, no other material non-recurring items were identified in the sources fetched.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF SEP 21, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end close of $47.93 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $90.06, TTE gained 87.9% on price — before a dividend that has run at roughly $3.00–$4.03 a share a year. The shape is two oil shocks separated by a long plateau: the 2022 energy-shock re-rating took the stock from the mid-$40s to the low $60s, it then drifted in a $55–$73 band for three years, bottomed at $54.50 in December 2024, and in 2026 went from $65.42 (December 2025) to a five-year high month-end of $92.71 in April 2026 — +41.7% in four months — as the Middle East conflict pushed Brent above $100.

The 6-period monthly RSI hit 89.2 in April 2026, the most overbought reading in the five-year series, and has cooled to 67.2 — still elevated, just under the 70 line. The lowest reading of the window was 21.9 in December 2024. The 10-month moving average is about $83.32, below the current price: unlike most of the last three years, the long-term trend is up. The June 2026 drop (−10.9% to $77.76) and July recovery (+13.0%) show how tightly the stock now trades on the conflict headlines; by the start of Q3 the company described oil as "above $80/b" in "very volatile markets," well off the $103.8/b Q2 average.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 8.76x forward earnings and 5.91x EV/EBITDA for a company whose group ROACE (13.9%) and ROE (15.9% adjusted) match or beat ExxonMobil and Chevron, which trade at ~13x forward and 8.5–10x EV/EBITDA. It yields 4.47% in cash dividends plus a ~4% buyback yield, carries 13.1% gearing, runs upstream at $5/b and just proved the integrated model under stress: Hormuz cost it ~210 kboe/d and it still printed its best cash flow since 2022. The Power build-out is ~19% of capital, not the whole company, and it is now self-funding: 2027 free-cash-flow breakeven, EPH's flexible gas plants adding cash immediately, and asset rotation (distributed solar sold) recycling capital. If Power's ROACE merely moves from 7.6% toward 10%, the main argument for the European discount weakens — and closing even half the gap to US multiples is worth ~25% on the forward P/E arithmetic (8.76 → ~11).

Bear Case

The earnings are a windfall and the multiple knows it. Brent at $103.8/b, refining margins at "historically high levels" and R&C profit 4.6x the prior year are not a run-rate — consensus already cuts FY2027 EPS 9.4%. Strip the cycle and what remains is a company diverting $3–4B a year into a Power segment that earns 7.6% on $30.9B, with Q2 ANOI down YoY despite the EPH acquisition, and a 12% 2030 target that needs ~$3.7B of ANOI on today's capital. The European discount is not a mispricing; it is the market charging for that allocation, plus French political exposure (pump-price caps) and a ~39% tax rate. Shell trades at the same multiple with the same arguments. And MarketBeat's 19-analyst consensus target, $88.50, is below today's price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Oil and refining normalisation. The TTM earnings base ($8.02 EPS, +46%) was built on a conflict-driven price spike. Management's sensitivities are calibrated to $60–70/b Brent; a de-escalation in the Gulf compresses liquids, gas and refining margins simultaneously, even as it restores Middle East volumes.
02
Integrated Power misses its return target. 7.6% TTM ROACE vs 12% by 2030; capital employed up $3.84B in a year while ANOI fell. If the Q3 and Q4 prints with a full EPH contribution do not lift ANOI, the market will keep applying the European discount — and it will be right to.
03
Hormuz and Middle East operations. 5–10% of production affected, liftings conditional on the strait, SATORP refinery at 70% until end-Q3. Escalation raises price but strands more barrels; the company's own guidance calls the situation "very volatile."
Industry Commentary
The 2026 Middle East supply shock
CRITICAL VARIABLE

TotalEnergies' Q2 release is the cleanest first-hand description available of the environment: Brent $103.8/b (Q1: $81.1), European refining margins at "historically high levels" as Russian refining capacity, Middle East supply to Asian refiners and global inventories were all constrained at once, and Qatari LNG — "close to 20% of world market" — affected by the conflict. European gas forwards were quoted at $16–20/Mbtu for Q3 with storage low before winter. For an integrated major this is the best possible tape for R&C and trading, and a mixed one for upstream, because barrels that cannot pass Hormuz earn nothing at any price. The investment question is not whether 2026 is a good year — it is — but how much of it the market will capitalise. So far, at 8.76x forward, the answer is: very little.

The European valuation discount
PREMISE CONFIRMED

The premise that European majors trade at a discount to US majors is confirmed by same-day data: forward P/E TTE 8.76 / Shell 8.99 / BP 8.20 versus ExxonMobil 13.21 / Chevron 13.09; EV/EBITDA 5.91 / 5.40 / 4.32 versus 10.06 / 8.53. The discount is roughly a third on forward earnings and does not depend on TTE-specific facts. The usual explanations — a smaller US investor base for Paris/London primaries, higher effective tax rates (TotalEnergies' ~39%), energy-transition capital at lower returns, and European political risk on fuel prices — are all visible in this company's own disclosures. TotalEnergies' December 2025 move from ADRs to directly listed NYSE shares is a deliberate attempt to widen the US holder base; its effect on the multiple is not yet measurable.

Does the power strategy earn its capex? — bull and bear framing
THE TESTABLE QUESTION

TotalEnergies publishes the scorecard, which is more than most transition stories offer. Today: 7.6% ROACE (TTM to June 2026) on $30.87B; group 13.9%; E&P 14.5%; LNG 8.5%. Targets: 12% ROACE by 2030; free-cash-flow positive in 2027; 100–120 TWh/y by 2030 (Q2 2026 ran at 14.8 TWh, ~59 TWh annualised). The bull framing: power returns are back-end loaded — projects earn nothing while under construction, the EPH gas fleet adds merchant cash in a tight European market, and asset rotation (selling down stakes in built projects) crystallises value above book, so a 7.6% blended return understates the mature-asset return. The bear framing: the segment has been "on its way" to 12% since the targets were set, Q2 ANOI fell YoY despite a large acquisition, and at a group cost of capital that the market implicitly prices far above the stated returns, every dollar here is valued below a dollar. The way to adjudicate is not a DCF; it is two numbers the company reports every quarter — Integrated Power ANOI and capital employed — and the 2027 FCF milestone.
