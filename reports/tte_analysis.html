01 Company Overview

TotalEnergies is France's oil major and a member of the CAC 40 in Paris, the index of 40 large French companies. The US line is not an ADR any more: since December 8, 2025 the company's ordinary shares trade directly on the NYSE under the same ticker, one-for-one with the old ADRs. It runs five segments: E&P, Integrated LNG, Integrated Power, Refining & Chemicals, and Marketing & Services. FY2025 revenue was $182.34B, down 6.78%, with GAAP net income of $13.13B and EPS of $5.78. Then 2026 happened: on a TTM basis through September 2026, revenue is $214.40B, net income $22.42B and diluted EPS $10.03 (the fourth quarter of 2025 plus the first nine months of 2026, from the company's results releases).

The reason is in the company's own words. Chairman and CEO Patrick Pouyanné framed the third quarter as "a high-price environment characterized by historic refining margin levels and a bull gas market." Brent averaged $97.0/b in Q3 2026 against $69.1 a year earlier, the European refining margin marker averaged $33.2/b against $8.4 a year earlier and $13.5 in Q2, and the stock rose from a $65.42 year-end close to $95.33 — while TotalEnergies itself lost about 5% of its year-on-year production to the Middle East conflict. That is the first thing to understand about this report: the windfall is real but it is not the thesis. The thesis is the gap underneath it. On the October 30 close, TTE trades at 7.77x forward earnings versus 13.09x for ExxonMobil and 12.55x for Chevron, and at 4.97x EV/EBITDA versus 9.96x and 8.40x (peer figures are StockAnalysis data from the November 2 close scaled to the October 30 close, our calculation). Shell, the other European major, sits at 9.26x forward — so the discount is European, not TotalEnergies-specific. Is the discount about capital put into low-carbon power at utility-like returns? TotalEnergies discloses the return on that capital, which lets us test it: the Integrated Power segment earned a 7.9% return on average capital employed over the twelve months to September 2026, against a company target of 12% by 2030 and a group average of 17.4%. In the same quarter it booked about $1.9B of impairments, mainly on offshore wind inside that segment, and lifted the buyback to $2.5B for each of the next two quarters as gearing fell to 9.1%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Sub-$5/boe upstream operating cost Global LNG portfolio (incl. new ECA LNG, Mexico) Integrated trading book Brazil / US Gulf / Suriname deepwater Flexible gas generation (EPH) Investment-grade balance sheet (9.1% gearing) Dual Paris / NYSE listing

The moat in oil is cost position and portfolio breadth, not technology. At $4.7 a barrel of oil equivalent in Q3 (anchored below $5, per the release), its upstream operating cost is a small fraction of today's oil price, and the integrated model did what it is meant to do in 2026: when Hormuz disrupted its Middle East barrels, refining margins and trading captured the price on the other side of the same event. The Power strategy is a bet that the same logic — owning generation, flexible gas plants, trading and retail customers together — can earn oil-like returns in electricity. So far it earns 7.9%.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Power returns may never reach 12%. TTM ROACE of 7.9%, with $29.65B of capital employed at September 30; the 2030 target needs roughly $3.6B of ANOI on today's capital alone (our arithmetic: 0.12 × $29.65B), versus $2.24B earned. Q3 ANOI of $602M was up on Q2's $533M and Q3 2025's $571M, but the quarter also carried impairments mainly on offshore wind. The segment has grown its capital faster than its profit: capital employed is up $2.69B in a year while Q3 ANOI rose 5.4% year on year.
02
Hormuz exposure cuts both ways. The Middle East conflict cost about 5% of year-on-year production in Q3 and now represents less than 5% of production per the Q3 release (the Q2 release put it at 5–10%). A resolution lowers the oil price and restores volume; an escalation raises price but strands more barrels.
03
The earnings base is a windfall. R&C ANOI of $3.6B in Q3 is 5.2x the year-earlier quarter; consensus (S&P Global via StockAnalysis, read November 2) has FY2027 EPS of $11.41, 10.5% below FY2026's $12.75. A trailing P/E of 9.5x on near-peak earnings is less cheap than it looks.
04
Home-market politics. A voluntary pump-price cap in France (described in the Q2 release) and measures for employees' energy purchasing power show how a national champion's margins become public property in a price spike. European windfall-tax risk is a recurring feature of 2022-style environments.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the numbers. First, GAAP vs adjusted: Q3 2026 IFRS net income was $8.26B against adjusted net income of $9.10B — adjusting items netted to about −$0.8B, made up of roughly +$1.5B of inventory and fair-value effects and about −$1.9B of impairments (mainly offshore wind in Integrated Power and the Hanwha TotalEnergies Petrochemical stake). Single-quarter GAAP EPS swings with the oil price. Second, share count: diluted weighted shares were 2,234M in Q3 against 2,216M in Q2 and 2,200M a year earlier (company release), reflecting the ~95.4M shares issued to EPH at closing on April 29, 2026, partly offset by buybacks (17.5M shares in Q3). Third, portfolio moves: Q3 divestments of $1.75B, mainly the agreement with Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP) on some African energy infrastructure assets (reported at $1.8B), against $240M of acquisitions, mainly the Bab Gas Cap concession.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $50.11 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $95.33, TTE gained 90.2% on price (price only; the dividend, $4.00 a share over the last twelve months per StockAnalysis, is not included). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You pay 7.77x forward earnings and 4.97x EV/EBITDA for a company whose GAAP ROE (17.83%) and ROIC (13.95%) beat ExxonMobil's (15.53%, 12.96%) and Chevron's (12.23%, 10.78%) on the same StockAnalysis basis, while those two trade at ~13x forward and 8.4–10x EV/EBITDA. It yields 4.20% in cash dividends, has authorised buybacks of $2.5B for each of the next two quarters (about 4.8% of the $206.66B market cap a year if repeated four times — our arithmetic: $10B ÷ $206.66B), carries 9.1% gearing, runs upstream at under $5/boe and just proved the integrated model under stress: the conflict cost it about 5% of year-on-year production and it still printed record downstream profit ($4.1B ANOI) and $12.3B of cash flow. The Power build-out is ~19% of capital, not the whole company, and is meant to become self-funding in 2027, helped by EPH's flexible gas plants and by asset rotation (a 50% stake in 1.2 GW sold to KKR). If Power's ROACE moves from 7.9% toward 10%, the main argument for the European discount weakens — and closing even half the gap to the US pair's forward price-to-earnings ratio is worth about a third (our arithmetic: 7.77 → ~10.3).

Bear Case

The earnings are a windfall, and the low price-to-earnings ratio already reflects it. Brent at $97.0/b, a European refining margin of $33.2/b and R&C profit 5.2x the prior year are not a level to count on every year — consensus already cuts FY2027 EPS 10.5%. Strip the cycle and what remains is a Power segment that earns 7.9% on $29.6B, that took impairments mainly on offshore wind in Q3, and a 12% 2030 target that needs ~$3.6B of ANOI on today's capital. The European discount is not a mispricing; it is the market charging for that allocation, plus French political exposure (pump-price caps) and an average tax rate of 37–39% (Q3 and Q2 2026). Shell and BP trade at 9.26x and 8.22x forward, so TTE's 7.77x is not unusual among European majors. And MarketBeat's 18-analyst average target, $92.71, is below today's price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Oil and refining normalisation. The TTM earnings base ($10.03 EPS) was built on a conflict-driven price spike, and Q3 refining margins were $33.2/b against $8.4 a year earlier. Management's sensitivities are calibrated to $60–70/b Brent; a de-escalation in the Gulf compresses liquids, gas and refining margins simultaneously, even as it restores Middle East volumes.
02
Integrated Power misses its return target. 7.9% TTM ROACE vs 12% by 2030; capital employed up $2.69B in a year while Q3 ANOI rose 5.4%, and Q3 carried impairments mainly on offshore wind. If the Q4 print does not lift ANOI, the market will keep applying the European discount — and it will be right to.
03
Hormuz and Middle East operations. The conflict cost about 5% of year-on-year production in Q3 and is now under 5% of production; the company's Q4 outlook still carries oil above $100/b. Escalation raises price but strands more barrels.
Industry Commentary
The 2026 Middle East supply shock
CRITICAL VARIABLE

TotalEnergies' Q3 release is the cleanest first-hand description available of the environment: Brent $97.0/b (Q2: $103.8), the European refining margin marker $33.2/b (Q2: $13.5), TTF gas $21.5/Mbtu, JKM $22.8/Mbtu and Henry Hub $2.9/Mbtu. At the start of Q4 the company says oil is above $100/b, product markets are highly volatile, global inventories are at their lowest levels and European gas prices remain on an upward trend; Qatari LNG is "close to 20% of world market" per the Q2 release. For an integrated major this is the best possible market for R&C and trading, and a mixed one for upstream, because barrels that cannot pass Hormuz earn nothing at any price. The investment question is not whether 2026 is a good year — it is — but how much of it the market will capitalise. So far, at 7.77x forward, the answer is: very little.

The European valuation discount
CONFIRMED

European majors trade at a discount to US majors on same-day data: forward P/E TTE 7.77 / Shell 9.26 / BP 8.22 versus ExxonMobil 13.09 / Chevron 12.55; EV/EBITDA 4.97 / 5.21 / 3.73 versus 9.96 / 8.40 (StockAnalysis, scaled to the October 30 close). The discount is roughly 40% on forward earnings against the US pair and does not depend on TTE-specific facts. Possible explanations — fewer US investors in shares whose main listing is in Paris or London, higher tax rates (TotalEnergies' average was 37.0% in Q3), energy-transition capital at lower returns, and European political risk on fuel prices — are all visible in this company's own disclosures. Since December 2025 the same TotalEnergies share has traded in Paris and New York, replacing the ADRs; any effect on the valuation is not yet measurable.

Does the power strategy earn its capex? — bull and bear framing
THE TESTABLE QUESTION

TotalEnergies publishes the scorecard, which is more than most transition stories offer. Today: 7.9% ROACE (twelve months to September 2026), with $29.65B of capital employed; group 17.4%; E&P 15.9%; LNG 10.4%. Targets: 12% ROACE by 2030; free-cash-flow positive in 2027; 100–120 TWh/y by 2030 (Q3 2026 ran at 17.9 TWh, ~72 TWh annualised — our arithmetic: 17.9 × 4). The bull framing: power returns come late — projects earn nothing while under construction, the EPH gas fleet earns cash selling power at market prices in a tight European market, and asset rotation (selling down stakes in built projects, as with the 50% stake sold to KKR) can turn projects into cash at prices above their value on the books, so a 7.9% blended return understates the mature-asset return. The bear framing: the segment has been on its way to 12% since the targets were set, Q3 carried impairments mainly on offshore wind, and if investors demand a higher return than the segment earns, each dollar invested there is worth less than a dollar to them. The way to adjudicate is not a DCF; it is two numbers the company reports every quarter — Integrated Power ANOI and capital employed — and the 2027 FCF milestone.
