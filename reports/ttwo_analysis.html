01 Company Overview

Take-Two is the holding company for three publishing labels: Rockstar Games (Grand Theft Auto, Red Dead Redemption), 2K (NBA 2K, WWE 2K, Borderlands, Mafia, Civilization, BioShock) and Zynga (Toon Blast, Match Factory!, Empires & Puzzles, Words With Friends, Zynga Poker — the mobile business bought in 2022). In fiscal 2026 (the year ended March 31, 2026) it reported GAAP net revenue of $6.66B, up 18.2%, and a GAAP net loss of $298M (−$1.62 per share). At $40.14B of market value it is one of the two or three largest independent Western game publishers still listed — and, since Electronic Arts went private on August 4, 2026, the only one of its size left on a US exchange.

The debate here is narrow and enormous at the same time. The stock is a claim on one product: Grand Theft Auto VI, confirmed for November 19, 2026 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, priced at $79.99 standard / $99.99 Ultimate. Everything else — a mobile business generating half the bookings, a sports franchise that sells 12M+ units a year, a slate of 29 announced titles through fiscal 2029 — is the floor. Fiscal 2027 net bookings guidance of $8.0–8.2B (~20% growth) tells you how much of GTA VI management is willing to underwrite for the roughly 19 weeks it will be on sale before the fiscal year closes. This page tries to separate the floor from the option, and to be honest that the option has not yet sold a single unit.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

GTA — 230M+ units, no substitute GTA Online: 13-year live-service annuity NBA 2K annual cycle (12M+ units) Rockstar development culture Pricing power: $80/$100 SKUs Mobile DTC storefront margin 99% digital distribution

The core advantage is irreplaceable intellectual property attached to a studio that has never shipped a mainline failure. Grand Theft Auto is not a genre with competitors; it is the genre. That lets Take-Two do things no other publisher can: skip the PC platform at launch, set an $80 standard price when the industry norm is $70, run a 13-year-old online mode that still grows, and describe pre-orders as "unprecedented" without a single analyst arguing. Layered on top is the most durable annual sports franchise not owned by EA, and a mobile portfolio whose 84% recurrent-spending share makes the base-year cash flows far steadier than the GAAP losses suggest.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Single-title dependence. Roughly 37% of FY27 guided bookings are Rockstar, and the year-over-year increase in guidance (≈$1.35B) is essentially GTA VI. A slip from November 19 into FY28 — the game has already moved twice, from "fall 2025" to May 26, 2026 to the current date — would take the fiscal year's growth to roughly zero. The Q2 guide (bookings $1.62–1.67B, down from $1.96B a year ago) shows what the business looks like without a Rockstar release.
▸
Mobile is shrinking and was overpaid for. Mobile bookings fell 7% in Q1 FY27 and management guides mobile RCS down for the year. The $6.8B of write-downs across FY24–FY25 have already been taken, but Zynga's still-amortising intangibles (~$0.9B a year of D&A) keep GAAP earnings depressed, and user-acquisition costs are rising across the industry.
▸
Leak and platform risk. Gameplay leaks on August 31 knocked 6.2% off the stock in a day; Rockstar called the episode "painful for the team." A console-only launch also concentrates the outcome on PS5/Xbox installed bases and on Sony/Microsoft's 30% platform take.
▸
Consolidated, better-capitalised competitors. Microsoft owns Activision Blizzard and King; Sony is a platform owner and first-party publisher; Tencent owns Supercell and holds a controlling stake in Ubisoft's core-franchise vehicle; EA is now backed by Saudi Arabia's PIF and Silver Lake with a private balance sheet. Take-Two is the smallest of the majors and the only one still answering to quarterly GAAP.

Notable One-Off Items. Three distortions matter. (1) Goodwill and intangible impairments of $3,038M in FY2024 and $3,806M in FY2025 — the quarter ended March 31, 2025 alone posted a $3,726M net loss (−$21.08/share) — are why FY24 and FY25 EPS read −$22.01 and −$25.58. (2) Acquired-intangible amortisation of roughly $860–900M a year continues to run through cost of revenue and operating expense, which is the main reason trailing GAAP earnings are negative while EBITDA margin is +11.5%. (3) A $43.4M impairment in Q1 FY27 for a cancelled unannounced third-party title. On the other side of the ledger, FY27 GAAP guidance of $104–143M net income is after continued amortisation — the first positive GAAP year since FY2022 if achieved.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 close of $181.00 to today's $214.69, TTWO has returned +18.6% in just under five years — roughly 3.5% a year, no dividend. That headline hides a complete round trip. The stock fell 42.5% to a $104.13 close in December 2022 as rates rose and the Zynga deal closed into a mobile downturn; it then rose 148% to a $258.36 peak in September 2025 as the GTA VI timeline firmed up; and it has since given back 16.9%, sitting 19% below its 52-week high of $265.94 with the launch eleven weeks away. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the right number. Take-Two guides and should be judged on net bookings, not GAAP revenue. GAAP revenue for a game with online components is recognised over an estimated service period (which is why FY27 GAAP revenue guidance of $7.9–8.1B sits below bookings of $8.0–8.2B — cash from GTA VI sales in November will be deferred into FY28). Bookings are what customers paid. On that basis the picture is: Q1 FY27 bookings $1,386M, −3%; Q2 guided $1,620–1,670M, ~−16% against a quarter that had Borderlands 4 and Mafia a year ago; and a full year of $8.0–8.2B, +20%. Arithmetic on the guidance: the first half is roughly $3.0–3.05B, so the second half must deliver roughly $5.0–5.15B — against a second half of around $3.5B in the prior year (derived from the ~20% growth statement; FY26 bookings were not directly fetched and this is an estimate). The increment, ~$1.5B, is the company's implicit GTA VI underwriting for its first 19 weeks. If GTA V's launch is any guide, that is conservative — and management said as much by declining to raise.

Recurrent spending is the floor. 84% of Q1 bookings were recurrent — GTA Online, NBA 2K virtual currency, mobile in-app purchases. That number falls to a guided 64% for FY27 only because a $1.5B+ up-front product lands in the denominator. The steadiness of that base is why FY26 produced $624M of operating cash flow and $434M of FCF in a year with no major Rockstar release, and why the company can guide to >$1.0B of operating cash flow for FY27. Against a $41.3B enterprise value that is roughly 41× guided FY27 operating cash flow, or about 39× EBITDA on the company's own $993–1,053M FY27 EBITDA projection — expensive for the year in which the biggest launch in entertainment history only contributes for four months, and defensible only if FY28 is the true peak year.

Mobile — the part everyone would rather not discuss. Zynga is 53% of bookings and falling (−7% in Q1). The company has written off $6.8B of the deal's carrying value across FY24 and FY25, and amortisation of what is left still costs ~$0.9B a year in reported earnings. The honest read: Take-Two paid $12.7B for a business that provides diversification and a steady cash stream but has not grown, and whose direct-to-consumer storefront is the only margin lever that does not depend on Apple and Google's mercy. For the equity, mobile matters mostly as the reason the trailing P/E is n/m — and as a reminder of what this management team did with the last windfall.

Valuation — which earnings base? (i) Trailing GAAP: n/m, EPS −$1.73. (ii) FY27 company GAAP guidance of $0.55–0.75 per share: ~290–390× this year's GAAP earnings — a number nobody uses but which is the fully-loaded truth for the launch year. (iii) FY27 consensus EPS of $6.87, which excludes acquired-intangible amortisation and stock compensation ($378M TTM, ~2 points of share dilution a year): 26.2× forward, PEG 0.96. (iv) Looking through to FY28, the first full year of GTA VI plus a PC release if it comes — no consensus numbers were fetched for FY28 and none are invented here, but that year, not FY27, is what the 25 Strong Buys are actually pricing. The gap between (ii) and (iii) is the amortisation of a deal that has already been written down; the gap between (iii) and (iv) is the release date holding.

Bull Case

GTA VI ships November 19 into pre-orders management calls unprecedented, with buyers skewing to the $99.99 Ultimate edition. GTA V sold 230M+ units and still grows online spending 13 years on; a successor at $80 with a modern online mode resets the bookings base for a decade. FY27's $8.0–8.2B guide is deliberately sandbagged, FY28 becomes the record year, NBA 2K compounds at high single digits, direct-to-consumer lifts mobile margins, and the balance sheet ($2.9B debt, $1.8B cash, FCF turning positive) de-levers on its own. At 26× a forward number that is itself conservative, with a Street-high target of $368, the stock is priced for a good launch — not a great one.

Bear Case

The stock is down 10% over twelve months in which nothing bad happened to the thesis — the market has stopped pre-paying for GTA VI and will make Take-Two prove it. The game has moved twice already; a third slip pushes ~$1.5B of bookings and essentially all of FY27's growth into FY28 and makes the 26× forward multiple meaningless. Ex-Rockstar, the business is shrinking: Q2 bookings are guided down 16%, mobile is down 7%, and Borderlands 4, Mafia and Civilization VII generated no disclosed unit figures worth a headline. Trailing GAAP earnings are negative, the share count grows 4.5% a year, insiders sold $140M+ of stock in six months with zero purchases, and 28 of 29 analysts are already on the same side of the boat.

Top 3 Risks

01
Release-date slip. The single variable. Two prior delays (fall 2025 → May 26, 2026 → Nov 19, 2026) each cost the stock roughly a quarter of its value in the following months. A move into FY28 would zero out FY27 growth and invite a re-basing of every forward estimate on this page.
02
Launch quality and the leak overhang. Leaked gameplay already cost 6.2% in a day. A technically rough launch, review backlash on the $80 price point, or a weaker-than-expected Ultimate mix would hit the FY28 online-spending ramp that the bull case actually depends on.

The $80 Game: Pricing Power Meets a Stretched Consumer
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Take-Two is the first major publisher to anchor a flagship at $79.99 standard and $99.99 Ultimate. Zelnick's argument — that game prices have not kept pace with inflation and GTA VI is "an incredible bargain as experiences go" — is true and also exactly what a company with pricing power says before testing it. The industry has been unable to move off $70 since 2020; if GTA VI clears $80 without backlash, every publisher follows and the sector's revenue-per-unit resets upward. If it becomes the focus of consumer anger, Take-Two eats the reputational cost alone. Watch the Ultimate-edition mix, which management says is currently running ahead.
Consolidation Has Left One Public Major Standing
ONGOING HEADWIND
Microsoft closed Activision Blizzard; Tencent controls Supercell and Ubisoft's core franchises; and on August 4, 2026 Electronic Arts was taken private by Saudi Arabia's PIF, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners for $55B ($210/share, delisted from Nasdaq). Every scaled Western competitor now has either a platform owner's balance sheet or a sovereign wealth fund behind it and no quarterly GAAP scrutiny. For Take-Two that is a headwind on talent costs, on marketing intensity and on any future M&A — and, in the other direction, it makes TTWO the only liquid, index-eligible way to own AAA games in the US, which supports the multiple even when the numbers don't.
Live Services and Direct-to-Consumer Are Where the Margin Lives
EMERGING TAILWIND
Recurrent spending was 84% of Take-Two's most recent quarter, and GTA Online still grows a decade after launch. The next lever is bypassing the 30% platform toll: Zynga's web storefront is already called out as "a significant driver of revenue and margin enhancement," and regulatory pressure on Apple and Google app-store terms is moving in publishers' favour. Bull framing: GTA VI's online mode plus a growing DTC share turns a hit-driven publisher into a subscription-like cash machine, and the 26× forward multiple compresses fast into FY28. Bear framing: the industry has spent a decade promising this transition and Take-Two's own mobile business — the part that is most "recurrent" — is shrinking; recurring revenue is only a moat if it also grows.
