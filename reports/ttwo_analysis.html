01 Company Overview

Take-Two is the holding company for three publishing labels: Rockstar Games (Grand Theft Auto, Red Dead Redemption), 2K (NBA 2K, WWE 2K, Borderlands, Mafia, Civilization, BioShock) and Zynga (Toon Blast, Match Factory!, Empires & Puzzles, Words With Friends, Zynga Poker — the mobile business bought in 2022). In fiscal 2026 (the year ended March 31, 2026) it reported GAAP net revenue of $6.66B, up 18.2%, and a GAAP net loss of $298M (−$1.62 per share). At $42.65B of market value it is, since Electronic Arts went private on August 4, 2026, the last large independent publisher of console games listed in the US (Activision Blizzard belongs to Microsoft).

The debate here is narrow and enormous at the same time. The stock is a claim on one product: Grand Theft Auto VI, confirmed for November 19, 2026 on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, priced at $79.99 standard / $99.99 Ultimate. Everything else — a mobile business generating close to half the bookings, a sports franchise that sells 12M+ units a year, a slate of 29 announced titles through fiscal 2029 — is the floor. Fiscal 2027 net bookings guidance of $8.0–8.2B (~20% growth), reiterated on November 9, tells you how much GTA VI revenue management is willing to count on for the roughly 19 weeks it will be on sale before the fiscal year closes. This page tries to separate the floor from the option, and to be honest that the option has not yet sold a single unit.

The last print before launch, on November 9, showed what the floor looks like without a Rockstar release: second-quarter net bookings fell 18% to $1.60B, just under the company's own $1.62–1.67B range, as NBA 2K27 sold below plan and mobile slipped. Management held the full-year range anyway and said GTA VI pre-orders are "tracking far in excess of our initial expectations." The stock rose 8.3% the next day.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

GTA V — nearly 235M units, no substitute GTA Online: 13-year live-service annuity NBA 2K annual cycle Rockstar development culture Pricing power: $80/$100 SKUs Mobile DTC storefront margin 98% digital distribution

The core advantage is irreplaceable intellectual property attached to a studio that has never shipped a mainline failure. Grand Theft Auto is not a genre with competitors; it is the genre. That lets Take-Two do things no other publisher can: skip the PC platform at launch, set an $80 standard price when the industry norm is $70, run a 13-year-old online mode that still grows, and describe pre-orders as "unprecedented" without a single analyst arguing. Layered on top is the most durable annual sports franchise not owned by EA, and a business in which recurrent spending was 81% of September-quarter bookings, which makes the base-year cash flows far steadier than the GAAP losses suggest.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Single-title dependence. Roughly 37% of FY27 guided bookings are Rockstar, and the increase in guided bookings over FY26's $6.72B (≈$1.38B at the midpoint) is essentially GTA VI. A slip from November 19 into FY28 — the game has already moved twice, from "fall 2025" to May 26, 2026 to the current date — would take the fiscal year's growth to roughly zero. The September quarter (bookings $1.60B, down 18% from $1.96B a year ago and just under the company's own range) showed what the business looks like without a Rockstar release.
▸
Mobile is shrinking and was overpaid for. Mobile bookings fell 13.5% in the September quarter as Color Block Jam came off its peak. The $6.8B of write-downs across FY24–FY25 have already been taken, but acquired intangibles that are still being written off over time (amortisation guided at $623M for FY27, company-wide) keep GAAP earnings depressed.
▸
Leak and platform risk. GTA VI gameplay footage leaked through late August; Rockstar said on August 26 the leaks had been "heartbreaking for our team," and on August 31, amid more leak reports and bond-market worries, the stock closed 6.7% lower. A console-only launch also concentrates the outcome on PS5/Xbox installed bases and on Sony/Microsoft's 30% platform take.
▸
Consolidated, better-capitalised competitors. Microsoft owns Activision Blizzard and King; Sony is a platform owner and first-party publisher; Tencent owns Supercell and 26% of Vantage Studios, the Ubisoft subsidiary behind Assassin's Creed, Far Cry and Rainbow Six; EA is now backed by Saudi Arabia's PIF and Silver Lake with a private balance sheet. Take-Two is the smallest of these and the only listed one whose results rest on games alone.

Notable One-Off Items. Three distortions matter. (1) Goodwill and intangible impairments of $3,038M in FY2024 and $3,806M in FY2025 — the quarter ended March 31, 2025 alone posted a $3,726M net loss (−$21.08/share) — are why FY24 and FY25 EPS read −$22.01 and −$25.58. These are non-cash and non-recurring, but they are the accounting admission that the $12.7B Zynga price was too high. (2) Acquired-intangible amortisation — $623M in the FY27 guide, $145.1M in the September quarter — continues to run through cost of revenue and operating expense, which is the main reason trailing GAAP earnings are negative while EBITDA margin is +11.3%. (3) A $43.4M impairment in Q1 FY27 for a cancelled unannounced third-party title. On the other side of the ledger, FY27 GAAP guidance of $109–142M net income is after continued amortisation — the first positive GAAP year since FY2022 if achieved.

Five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 close of $165.88 to the November 13, 2026 close of $227.82, TTWO has returned +37.3% in just under five years — roughly 6.6% a year, no dividend — against +65.6% for SPY and +72.6% for QQQ over the same dates (price only). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Start with the right number. Take-Two guides and should be judged on net bookings, not GAAP revenue. GAAP revenue for a game with online components is recognised over an estimated service period (which is why FY27 GAAP revenue guidance of $7.98–8.18B sits below bookings of $8.0–8.2B — cash from GTA VI sales in November will be deferred into FY28). Bookings are what customers paid. On that basis the picture is: first-half FY27 bookings $2,987M, −12% (Q2 $1,601M, −18%, just under the company's own range, against a quarter that had Borderlands 4 and Mafia a year ago); Q3 guided to $3.35–3.5B against $1.76B a year ago; and a full year of $8.0–8.2B, +20%, reiterated on November 9. Arithmetic on the guidance (our calculation): the second half must deliver $5.01–5.21B, 68–75% more than the first half. That jump is what the company is implicitly counting on from GTA VI's first 19 weeks. If GTA V's launch is any guide, that is conservative — and management again declined to raise.

Recurrent spending is the floor. 81% of Q2 bookings were recurrent — GTA Online, NBA 2K virtual currency, mobile in-app purchases. Management expects that share to be about 65% for FY27 only because a large up-front product lands in the total. The steadiness of that base is why FY26 produced $624M of operating cash flow and $434M of FCF in a year with no major Rockstar release, and why the company still guides to >$1.0B of operating cash flow for FY27 after using $173.5M in the first half. Against a $43.9B enterprise value (market value plus $2.91B of debt less $1.67B of cash, stockanalysis.com) that is at most about 44× guided FY27 operating cash flow, or about 41–43× EBITDA on the company's own $1,024–1,082M FY27 EBITDA projection — expensive for a year in which GTA VI contributes for only about four months, and defensible only if FY28 is the true peak year.

Mobile — the part everyone would rather not discuss. Mobile was 45% of September-quarter bookings and falling (−13.5%). The company has written off $6.8B of the deal's carrying value across FY24 and FY25, and amortisation of acquired intangibles still costs about $620M a year in reported earnings ($623M in the FY27 guide, company-wide). The honest read: Take-Two paid $12.7B for a business that provides diversification and a steady cash stream but has not grown, and whose direct-to-consumer storefront is the only margin lever that does not depend on Apple and Google's mercy. For the equity, mobile matters mostly as the reason the trailing P/E is n/m — and as a reminder of what this management team did with the last windfall.

Valuation — which earnings base? (i) Trailing GAAP: n/m, EPS −$1.29. (ii) FY27 company GAAP guidance of $0.57–0.75 per share: ~300–400× this year's GAAP earnings — a number nobody uses but which is the fully-loaded truth for the launch year. (iii) FY27 consensus EPS of $6.88, on analysts' adjusted (non-GAAP) basis: 33.1× forward. (iv) Looking through to FY28 — consensus EPS $10.17, or 22.4× — the first full year of GTA VI plus a PC release if it comes: that year, not FY27, is what the 25 Strong Buys are actually pricing. The gap between (ii) and (iii) is the costs analysts adjust out, including $623M of guided amortisation of acquired intangibles; the gap between (iii) and (iv) is the release date holding.

Bull Case

GTA VI ships November 19 into pre-orders management says are "tracking far in excess of our initial expectations," and an outlook that assumes "robust sales" of the $99.99 Ultimate Edition. GTA V has sold nearly 235M units and the series still grows recurrent spending 13 years on; a successor at $80 with a modern online mode resets the bookings base for a decade. FY27's $8.0–8.2B guide is deliberately set low, FY28 becomes the record year, NBA 2K's recurrent spending picks up in the second half as management expects, direct-to-consumer lifts mobile margins, and the balance sheet ($2.9B debt, $1.7B cash and short-term investments, a net cash position targeted by March) pays down debt on its own. At 33× this year's consensus and 22× next year's, with a Street-high target of $330, the stock is priced for a good launch — not a great one.

Bear Case

The stock is down 4% over twelve months in which nothing bad happened to the thesis — the market has stopped pre-paying for GTA VI and will make Take-Two prove it. The game has moved twice already; a third slip pushes essentially all of FY27's guided growth (about $1.4B of bookings over FY26's $6.72B at the midpoint, our calculation) into FY28 and makes the 33× forward multiple meaningless. Ex-Rockstar, the business is shrinking: Q2 bookings fell 18% and missed the company's own range, mobile fell 13.5%, and NBA 2K27 — the most dependable annual title — came in below forecast. Trailing GAAP earnings are negative, the share count rose 1.4% in a year, and 28 of 29 analysts are already on the same side of the boat.

Top 3 Risks

01
Release-date slip. The single variable. Of the two prior delays (fall 2025 → May 26, 2026 → Nov 19, 2026), the first cost about 3% in the month it was announced and was made back by June 2025; the second was followed by a 23% slide from the October 2025 close to March 2026. With launch six days away, a move into FY28 would zero out FY27 growth and force every forward estimate on this page to be redone.
02
Launch quality and the leak overhang. The stock closed 6.7% lower on August 31 amid leak reports. A technically rough launch, review backlash on the $80 price point, or a weaker-than-expected Ultimate mix would hit the FY28 online-spending ramp that the bull case actually depends on.
03
Mobile keeps shrinking and what's left of Zynga gets written down again. Close to half the bookings base is in a category where bookings fell 13.5% in the September quarter. Further impairments would be non-cash, but they would confirm the market's view of capital allocation just as management gets its largest-ever cash windfall to redeploy.
  Industry Commentary
The $80 Game: Pricing Power Meets a Stretched Consumer
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
GTA VI is priced at $79.99 standard and $99.99 Ultimate. Nintendo charged $79.99 for Mario Kart World in 2025, but GTA VI is the biggest test yet of an $80 standard price on PlayStation and Xbox, where $70 has been the norm since 2020. Zelnick's argument, made in interviews, is that a game's price should be weighed against the entertainment it delivers, which by his account makes GTA VI a bargain; it is also what a company with pricing power says before testing it. If GTA VI clears $80 without backlash, other publishers can follow and the industry's revenue per copy moves up. If it becomes the focus of consumer anger, Take-Two eats the reputational cost alone. Watch the Ultimate-edition mix: on November 9 Goldstein said the outlook assumes "robust sales of Grand Theft Auto VI: Ultimate Edition."
Consolidation Has Left One Public Major Standing
ONGOING HEADWIND
Microsoft closed Activision Blizzard; Tencent owns Supercell and 26% of Ubisoft's biggest franchises; and on August 4, 2026 Electronic Arts was taken private by Saudi Arabia's PIF, Silver Lake and Affinity Partners for $55B ($210/share, delisted from Nasdaq). Every large Western rival now has either a platform owner's balance sheet or a sovereign wealth fund behind it, and none has to answer to shareholders for its games results alone. For Take-Two that is a headwind on talent costs, on marketing intensity and on any future M&A — and, in the other direction, it makes TTWO the only large US-listed company that does nothing but make and publish games, which supports its valuation even when the numbers don't.
Live Services and Direct-to-Consumer Are Where the Margin Lives
EMERGING TAILWIND
Recurrent spending was 81% of Take-Two's September quarter, and the GTA series still grew it by double digits more than a decade after GTA V's launch. The next lever is bypassing the 30% platform toll: Zelnick says Zynga's direct-to-consumer business "is significant, and it is growing," and regulatory pressure on Apple and Google app-store terms is moving in publishers' favour. Bull framing: GTA VI's online mode plus a growing DTC share turns a hit-driven publisher into a subscription-like cash machine, and the 33× forward multiple falls to 22× on FY28 consensus. Bear framing: the industry has spent a decade promising this transition and Take-Two's own mobile business — the part that is most "recurrent" — is shrinking; recurring revenue is only a moat if it also grows.
