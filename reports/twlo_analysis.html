01 Company Overview

Twilio is the plumbing behind the texts, phone calls, emails and log-in codes that companies send their customers. A developer plugs Twilio's APIs into an app, and Twilio carries the message across phone networks and inboxes worldwide; on top of that sits Segment, a customer-data platform that tells the business who it is talking to. It counted more than 402,000 active customer accounts at the end of 2025. FY2025 revenue was $5.07B, up 13.7%, with GAAP EPS of $0.21: a profit after losses in each of 2021–2024. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to June 2026 is $5.57B. On October 6, 2026 Twilio moved up from the S&P MidCap 400 into the S&P 500, replacing Warner Bros. Discovery, which Paramount Skydance is acquiring.

The five-year chart is a round trip with a hole in the middle. The stock closed October 2021 at $291.36, fell 83% to a $48.96 month-end in December 2022, while revenue growth slowed from 61% in 2021 to 35% in 2022 and 9% in 2023 and annual losses ran near $1B, then spent nearly two years between $51 and $76. Since late 2024 it has climbed back to $275.77, 5.6 times the low and still 5.4% below where the window starts. What changed is the shape of the business: growth that had slowed to single digits is accelerating again (organic revenue up 16% in Q1 2026 and 17% in Q2), and a company that lost money for years now generates over $1B of free cash flow a year. The new driver is voice: AI agents that answer and make phone calls need phone lines, and management says most of the voice-AI start-ups are choosing Twilio to provide them. The central question is whether that AI-driven usage is a lasting second engine of growth, or whether a price of 46.5 times this year's expected earnings has already paid for it, with reported growth flattered by roughly $250M of carrier fees that Twilio collects and passes on.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

402,000+ customer accounts Developer-first adoption Carrier connections at scale Usage expansion (DBNE 116%) Voice-AI infrastructure share Customer data (Segment) Net cash $1.59B

The moat is being the default. A developer who needs to send a text or place a call from code usually starts with Twilio, builds it into the product, and stays as volumes grow, which is what a 116% net expansion rate measures. Behind the API sit the connections to carriers worldwide, the number registrations and the compliance work that a start-up does not want to rebuild. The weakness is that the core service, moving a message from A to B, is hard to tell apart from a rival's, and the carriers set a large share of the cost. Twilio's answer is to sell the layers above the pipe: identity checks, customer data and the memory and orchestration tools an AI agent needs. Software add-on revenue growing 25%+ in Q2 (CFO) says that is working; the company does not say how large it is.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reported growth includes fees Twilio passes through. Q2's 22% reported growth contained $71.1M of revenue from US carrier fee increases; full-year guidance assumes about $250M. That money goes out to the carriers again, so it adds revenue without adding much profit, and it stops flattering the growth rate once the fee increases are a year old. The organic figure (17% in Q2, 11–12% guided for Q3) is the one to watch.
02
The price assumes the acceleration lasts. At $275.77 the stock trades at 46.5 times consensus FY2026 earnings and about 7.3 times trailing sales, and it has moved more than 20% in a single session after each of the last two reports (+23.8% on May 1 and +24.9% on August 7, 2026). The Q3 guide implies organic growth slowing from 17% to 11–12%. Twilio has beaten its own guide by a wide margin twice this year; a quarter where it only meets it would test the multiple.
03
GAAP profit is thin under the non-GAAP margin. Q2's GAAP operating margin was 5.6% against 19% non-GAAP. Stock-based compensation ran at $141.9M in the quarter, and the headline GAAP net income of $1.07B is almost entirely a $991.7M tax benefit, most of it the release of a valuation allowance (an accounting reserve against old losses, written back once the company expects to keep earning profits).
04
Competition on every side. The 10-K lists other communications-platform companies, regional network operators, customer-relationship and customer-experience vendors, and stand-alone customer-data-platform vendors as competitors. The fastest-growing customers, voice-AI start-ups, are also the easiest to lose: they can route calls through another provider if price or quality slips.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items distort the 2026 figures. First, the tax benefit: Q2 carried an income-tax benefit of $991.7M, and the 10-Q shows a $944.1M release of the valuation allowance in the first half. The company now expects to earn enough taxable profit to use its past losses, so it put them back on the balance sheet as an asset. That turned $75.5M of pre-tax income into $1.07B of net income and GAAP EPS of $6.68, and it is why the trailing P/E (38.1) sits below the forward P/E (46.5). Second, carrier fees: $71.1M of Q2 revenue came from increases in US carrier fees that Twilio passes through; the organic growth rate (17%) excludes them, the reported rate (22%) does not. Smaller items: a $32.8M impairment loss on prepaid assets in Q2's operating expenses, and $66.0M of buybacks (0.5M shares) in the quarter.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

By management's account Twilio is growing faster than at any time in more than three years, and doing it profitably: organic growth of 16% then 17%, a net expansion rate climbing from 108% to 116%, a 19% non-GAAP margin and over $1B of free cash flow guided for 2026, with $1.59B of net cash. Voice AI gives it a new source of volume: every AI agent that talks to customers needs a phone line, and management says most of the voice-AI start-ups are building on Twilio. Software add-ons growing 25%+ show the company can sell more than the pipe. It has beaten its own guide by six to seven points of organic growth in Q2, and S&P 500 membership puts it in front of a wider set of large-cap buyers. If 2027 looks like 2026, 46 times this year's earnings becomes much less demanding.

Bear Case

The stock is up 142% in a year and trades above the average analyst target. The headline numbers flatter: the trailing P/E of 38 rests on a $944M tax-accounting gain, the 22% reported growth includes carrier fees passed straight through, and the GAAP operating margin is 5.6% once stock pay is counted. Guidance already has organic growth slowing to 11–12% in Q3. The voice-AI customers driving the acceleration are young companies that can switch providers, and the core service — moving a message or a call — is a commodity whose costs the carriers set. Index-inclusion buying has come and gone: the stock fell 8.4% in the three sessions after it. In January 2025, the only time monthly RSI ran higher than September's 89.3, the stock fell 33% over the next two months.

Top 3 Risks

01
Deceleration from a high multiple. Q3 guidance implies organic growth of 11–12% after 17% in Q2. At 46.5 times FY2026 consensus earnings, a quarter that only meets guidance could cost more than the 20%+ gains the last two beats delivered. The October 29 report is the first test as an S&P 500 member.
02
Voice-AI concentration and churn. The newest and fastest-growing demand comes from AI-agent and voice-AI companies. That growth is usage-billed, so it can fall as fast as it rose if those customers consolidate, fail, or move calls to a rival or a carrier directly.
03
Carrier fees and margin. Twilio pays network operators for every message and call. Fee increases lift reported revenue without lifting profit, and fees it cannot pass on would come straight out of a 48% gross margin, already low for a software company.
Industry Commentary
AI agents need phone lines
EMERGING TAILWIND

For most software companies, AI raises the question of whether customers will need fewer seats. Twilio's pricing is mostly per message, per minute and per check (the 10-K: Messaging, Voice and identity revenue is recognised mainly on a usage basis), so an AI agent that places or answers calls is another source of billable traffic rather than a replaced user. That is the bull reading of Q2's voice growth above 20%. The products Twilio launched at SIGNAL, its customer conference, are built for it: the CEO said the conversations layer — Conversation Memory, Conversation Orchestrator, Conversation Intelligence, Conversation Relay and Agent Connect — provides "the building blocks customers need to deliver context-rich conversations over a consumer's lifetime." The open question is pricing power: the same AI firms are sophisticated buyers who can route traffic across several providers.

Carrier fees: revenue that is not quite growth
ONGOING DISTORTION

US carriers charge registration and per-message fees on business texting (A2P, application-to-person traffic). When they raise those fees, platforms like Twilio collect the increase from customers and hand it on, which lifts reported revenue. Twilio's organic revenue definition excludes the increases until a year after they start, and its 2026 guidance assumes about $250M of them. The practical effect: in 2026 the reported growth rate (18–18.5% guided) runs about five points above the organic rate (13–13.5%), and that gap narrows as the fee increases pass their first anniversary.
