01 Company Overview

Twilio is the plumbing behind the texts, phone calls, emails and log-in codes that companies send their customers. A developer plugs Twilio's APIs into an app, and Twilio carries the message across phone networks and inboxes worldwide; on top of that sits Segment, a customer-data platform that tells the business who it is talking to. It counted more than 402,000 active customer accounts at the end of 2025. FY2025 revenue was $5.07B, up 13.7%, with GAAP EPS of $0.21: a profit after losses in each of 2021–2024. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to September 2026 is $5.83B. On October 6, 2026 Twilio moved up from the S&P MidCap 400 into the S&P 500, replacing Warner Bros. Discovery.

The five-year chart is a round trip with a hole in the middle. The stock closed November 2021 at $286.15, fell 83% to a $48.96 month-end in December 2022, while revenue growth slowed from 61% in 2021 to 35% in 2022 and 9% in 2023 and annual losses ran near $1B, then spent nearly two years between $51 and $76. From late 2024 it climbed to a $292.46 month-end in September 2026, then fell 29.9% in October; at $207.60 it is 4.2 times the low and 27.5% below where the window starts. What changed is the shape of the business: growth that had slowed to single digits accelerated again (organic revenue up 16%, 17% and 16% in the first three quarters of 2026), and a company that lost money for years now generates over $1B of free cash flow a year. The new driver is voice: AI agents that answer and make phone calls need phone lines, and management says most of the voice-AI start-ups are choosing Twilio to provide them. The October 29 report changed the question. Twilio beat its own forecast again, but guided organic growth to 11–12% for the fourth quarter and at least 10% for 2027, and the stock fell 15.2% the next day. At 33.8 times this year's expected earnings, the test is whether AI-driven usage keeps growth above those guides, with reported growth still lifted by carrier fees that Twilio collects and passes on.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

402,000+ customer accounts Developer-first adoption Carrier connections at scale Usage expansion (DBNE 114%) Voice-AI infrastructure share Customer data (Segment) Net cash $1.83B

The moat is being the default. A developer who needs to send a text or place a call from code usually starts with Twilio, builds it into the product, and stays as volumes grow, which is what a 114% net expansion rate measures. Behind the API sit the connections to carriers worldwide, the number registrations and the compliance work that a start-up does not want to rebuild. The weakness is that the core service, moving a message from A to B, is hard to tell apart from a rival's, and the carriers set a large share of the cost. Twilio's answer is to sell the layers above the pipe: identity checks, customer data and the memory and orchestration tools an AI agent needs. Software add-on revenue growing 25%+ in both Q2 and Q3 (CFO) says that is working; the company does not say how large it is.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Reported growth includes fees Twilio passes through. Q3's 20% reported growth contained $53.1M of revenue from US carrier fee increases; full-year guidance assumed about $250M (Q2 call). That money goes out to the carriers again, so it adds revenue without adding much profit, and it stops lifting the growth rate once the fee increases are a year old. The organic figure (16% in Q3, 11–12% guided for Q4) is the one to watch.
02
The price still assumes growth outruns the guide. At $207.60 the stock trades at 33.8 times consensus FY2026 earnings and about 5.2 times trailing sales, and it has moved 15% or more in a single session after each of the last three reports (+23.8% on May 1, +24.9% on August 7 and −15.2% on October 30, 2026). The Q4 guide implies organic growth slowing from 16% to 11–12%, and the Q3 beat over Twilio's own guide (16% against 11–12%) was smaller than Q2's (17% against 10–11%). A quarter where it only meets the guide would test the price again.
03
GAAP profit is thin under the non-GAAP margin. Q3's GAAP operating margin was 9.5% against 21% non-GAAP. Stock-based compensation ran at $153.9M in the quarter, and nine-month GAAP net income of $1.32B includes a $937.1M tax benefit from the release of a valuation allowance (an accounting reserve against old losses, written back once the company expects to keep earning profits), booked mostly in Q2.
04
Competition on every side. The 10-K lists other communications-platform companies, regional network operators, customer-relationship and customer-experience vendors, and stand-alone customer-data-platform vendors as competitors. The fastest-growing customers, voice-AI start-ups, are also the easiest to lose: they can route calls through another provider if price or quality slips.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items distort the 2026 figures. First, the tax benefit: the Q3 10-Q's cash flow statement shows a $937.1M tax benefit from releasing the valuation allowance in the first nine months, most of it in Q2, when the income-tax benefit was $991.7M. The company now expects to earn enough taxable profit to use its past losses, so it put them back on the balance sheet as an asset. That turned $316.9M of nine-month pre-tax income into $1.32B of net income and nine-month diluted EPS of $8.25, and it is why the trailing P/E (26.1) sits below the forward P/E (33.8). Second, carrier fees: $53.1M of Q3 revenue came from increases in US carrier fees that Twilio passes through; the organic growth rate (16%) excludes them, the reported rate (20%) does not. Smaller items: a $22.0M income-tax benefit in Q3, and $127.2M of buybacks (about 0.5 million shares) in the quarter.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $286.15 to the November 2, 2026 settled close of $207.60, TWLO is down 27.5% on price; Twilio pays no dividend, so that is also the total return. SPY, an S&P 500 index fund, rose 65.1% on price over the same window (Yahoo Finance closes, $455.56 to $752.10). The endpoints hide the path. The stock lost 83% by December 2022, closed every month from January 2023 to September 2024 between $51.26 and $75.87, and then more than doubled in four months, from $65.22 in September 2024 to $146.58 in January 2025. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Twilio keeps beating its own forecasts and doing it profitably: organic growth of 16%, 17% and 16% in the first three quarters of 2026, full-year organic guidance raised to 15%, a 21% non-GAAP operating margin in Q3 and $1.19–1.20B of free cash flow guided for 2026, with $1.83B of net cash. Voice AI gives it a new source of volume: every AI agent that talks to customers needs a phone line, voice revenue grew 20%+ in Q3 and messaging 24% (CFO), and the CEO says voice-AI penetration is still extremely low. The CFO's 2027 floor of 10% organic growth is above the 7–8% framework of the 2025 Investor Day. After a 29% fall from the September close the stock trades at 33.8 times FY2026 consensus earnings and 28% below the average analyst target.

Bear Case

The slowdown is now in the guide: Q4 organic growth of 11–12% after 16% in Q3, and 2027 organic growth of "at least 10%" (CFO). The Q3 beat over Twilio's own forecast was smaller than Q2's, and net expansion slipped from 116% to 114%. The headline numbers flatter: the trailing P/E of 26 rests on a $937M tax-accounting gain, the 20% reported growth includes $53M of carrier fees passed straight through, and the GAAP operating margin is 9.5% once stock pay is counted. The voice-AI customers driving the acceleration are young companies that can switch providers, and the core service — moving a message or a call — is a commodity whose costs the carriers set. Index-inclusion buying has come and gone: the stock is 31% below its October 5 close.

Top 3 Risks

01
Slowing growth at a still-high price. Q4 guidance implies organic growth of 11–12% after 16% in Q3, and the CFO's 2027 floor is 10%. At 33.8 times FY2026 consensus earnings the stock fell 15.2% on a quarter that beat its own guide; the Q4 report (expected in February, date not yet announced) is the next test.
02
Voice-AI concentration and churn. The newest and fastest-growing demand comes from AI-agent and voice-AI companies. That growth is usage-billed, so it can fall as fast as it rose if those customers consolidate, fail, or move calls to a rival or a carrier directly.
03
Carrier fees and margin. Twilio pays network operators for every message and call. Fee increases lift reported revenue without lifting profit, and fees it cannot pass on would come straight out of a 49% gross margin, already low for a software company.
Industry Commentary
AI agents need phone lines
EMERGING TAILWIND

For most software companies, AI raises the question of whether customers will need fewer seats. Twilio's pricing is mostly per message, per minute and per check (the 10-K: Messaging, Voice and identity revenue is recognised mainly on a usage basis), so an AI agent that places or answers calls is another source of billable traffic rather than a replaced user. That is the bull reading of voice growth of 20%+ in both Q2 and Q3 (CFO). The products Twilio launched at SIGNAL, its customer conference, are built for it: on the Q2 call (Aug 6, 2026) the CEO said the conversations layer — Conversation Memory, Conversation Orchestrator, Conversation Intelligence, Conversation Relay and Agent Connect — provides "the building blocks customers need to deliver context-rich conversations over a consumer's lifetime." In the Q3 release he added: "Consumer AI has accelerated AI development, and its adoption demands fast, reliable infrastructure built for AI speed." The open question is pricing power: the same AI firms are sophisticated buyers who can route traffic across several providers.

Carrier fees: revenue that is not quite growth
ONGOING DISTORTION

US carriers charge registration and per-message fees on business texting (A2P, application-to-person traffic). When they raise those fees, platforms like Twilio collect the increase from customers and hand it on, which lifts reported revenue. Twilio's organic revenue definition excludes the increases until a year after they start; its 2026 guidance assumed about $250M of them (Q2 call), and Q3 carried $53.1M. The practical effect: in 2026 the reported growth rate (20% guided) runs about five points above the organic rate (15%), and that gap narrows as the fee increases pass their first anniversary: the Q4 guide puts it at 17–17.5% reported against 11–12% organic.

A volatile software stock
MARKET RISK

Twilio's own record shows how large the moves in a software stock can be: single-day gains of 19.5%, 23.8% and 24.9% after the Q3 2025, Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 reports, a 15.24% fall after the Q3 2026 report, a 10.62% fall on October 16 with no company news filed, and a 6.81% fall on its first day in the S&P 500. Index funds now hold it, but at a 1.36 beta the stock is likely to keep amplifying both the sector's mood and its own results.
