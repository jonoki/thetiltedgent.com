01 Company Overview

UBS is a Swiss bank built around managing rich people's money. Its Global Wealth Management division looks after $4.94 trillion of client assets and earned half of the revenue of UBS's four core divisions in the first half of 2026. Around it sit a Swiss retail and business bank (Personal & Corporate Banking), an asset manager and an investment bank. Group invested assets reached a record $7.3 trillion at June 30, 2026. In the first half of 2026 (1H26) revenue rose 13.3% to $27.9B, pre-tax profit rose 72% to $7.4B, net profit was $5.84B (+43%) and diluted EPS was $1.81, against $1.23 a year earlier. The second quarter alone (2Q26) brought net profit of $2,800M, up 16.9% from a year earlier and 17% above the $2.39B average in a company-provided poll of analysts (Reuters, July 29, 2026). UBS reports in US dollars, and each NYSE share is an ordinary UBS Group share, so per-share figures need no conversion.

The last three years have been about one deal. In March 2023, in a rescue brokered by the Swiss government, UBS took over its long-time rival Credit Suisse; UBS completed the acquisition on June 12, 2023. The integration is now in its final stretch. Every former Credit Suisse client account had moved onto UBS systems by March 2026, more than 90% of the old Credit Suisse IT applications in scope were out of use by June 30, and $12.6B of gross cost savings had been booked against a target of about $13.5B by the end of 2026. CEO Sergio Ermotti put it this way in the second-quarter results release of July 29, 2026: "the acquisition of Credit Suisse was not a gift that we received, but rather a prize that we would all have to fight to win." The fight has moved to capital. The Swiss government wants the parent bank, UBS AG, to back its foreign subsidiaries fully with top-quality equity. UBS estimated that would mean about $20B of extra capital at UBS AG, or about $22B including rule changes already decided. On September 23, 2026 the upper house of parliament backed a 90% version. UBS said that would mean about $16B of extra CET1 at UBS AG and, if confirmed, around $33B of incremental CET1 in all since the takeover, at an annual cost of around $2.5B. A week later a top-10 shareholder, Artisan Partners, urged the board to move the bank out of Switzerland; UBS replied that its goal is to keep operating as a global bank from Switzerland. The shares fell 12.5% between the September 11 and September 23 closes, and September (−13.6%) was the worst month since June 2022. Over five years the NYSE shares still rose from $18.15 (October 2021 month-end) to $47.71, +162.9%.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

$4.9T wealth platform Swiss banking franchise Global reach: Americas, Asia, Europe Fee-based, capital-light revenue Wealth–investment bank cross-selling Credit Suisse cost savings

UBS's edge is trust at scale. Fees on client money grow with the markets and with new inflows, and they need little of the bank's own capital. With its former Swiss rival absorbed, UBS runs a wealth business across the Americas, Switzerland, Asia-Pacific and Europe, and the integration also took out costs on a scale no smaller rival can match: $12.6B of gross savings so far. The weak spot of this moat is its home country. After the government had to broker the Credit Suisse rescue, Switzerland now hosts a single global bank with a $1.7 trillion balance sheet (June 30, 2026), and how much capital that bank must hold at home is what the capital debate is about.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Swiss capital rules. The Federal Council's bill, sent to parliament in April 2026, would make UBS AG fully deduct its investments in foreign subsidiaries from its own CET1 capital, phased in from 65% to 100% over seven years. UBS estimated the full version at about $20B of extra CET1 at UBS AG, or about $22B with the ordinance changes (calculated on its December 31, 2025 balance sheet). The upper house backed 90% on September 23, 2026; UBS put that version at about $16B of extra CET1, plus about $2B from ordinance measures announced earlier in the year. The bill now goes to the lower house.
02
Buybacks depend on politics. UBS has tied the pace and size of buybacks beyond the reserved $3B to "further visibility on the deliberations by the Swiss Parliament on the capitalization of foreign subsidiaries". A tougher final law would mean capital going into the parent bank instead of back to shareholders.
03
Credit Suisse legacy litigation. UBS inherited Credit Suisse's lawsuits. They include challenges by former holders of Credit Suisse AT1 bonds to FINMA's decree of March 19, 2023, which wrote off CHF 16B of those bonds; the Swiss Federal Administrative Court published a partial decision in October 2025.
04
Market dependence. Wealth fees follow asset prices, and the investment bank's record second quarter was led by Equities, with revenue up 53%. The 2Q26 outlook warned that geopolitics and volatile energy prices make the inflation and rate outlook highly uncertain, which "could contribute to … more measured investor sentiment".

Notable One-Off Items

UBS splits its results into reported and underlying. Underlying leaves out integration costs, the accounting effects of the Credit Suisse purchase price allocation (PPA) and a few one-offs. 1H26: revenue included $824M of PPA effects and other integration items, and expenses included $1,395M of integration-related expenses and PPA effects. The gap took underlying pre-tax profit to $7,877M against $7,434M reported. Revenue also included a $163M gain related to the Swisscard transactions, $128M of which is excluded from underlying. 4Q25: net profit was only $1,199M (diluted EPS $0.37) on a 29.1% effective tax rate, which holds down the TTM EPS used in the P/E. 1H25: a negative effective tax rate in 2Q25 (−9.5%) and net releases of $298M for litigation flattered that half, so year-on-year profit growth in 1H26 (+43%) is cleaner at the pre-tax level (+72% reported, +49.5% underlying). Integration costs should fade after 2026. UBS expects to have spent about $15B in total by the end of the year.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The hard part of the Credit Suisse deal is done: clients migrated, 93% of the cost savings booked, the wind-down unit nearly empty. Second-quarter net profit beat the company-polled consensus by 17%. Returns are already above the 2026 target (16.7% underlying RoCET1 against ~15%), and the 2028 aim is about 18% reported. Integration costs that weighed on reported profit fade after 2026. The wealth franchise is still growing: $7.3 trillion of group invested assets, $72.9B of net new assets in GWM in six months, and wealth NII guided up about 10% for 2026. At 1.77x tangible book and 16.2x trailing (13.1x on the consensus estimate), with a progressive dividend and $3B of buybacks reserved, every analyst target on the S&P Global panel is above the price. If parliament ends softer than 100%, capital is freed for shareholders.

Bear Case

Switzerland is moving toward making UBS hold far more capital at its parent bank. In its September 23 statement UBS put the 90% version at about $16B of extra CET1 at UBS AG, on top of about $2B from ordinance steps, for around $33B of incremental CET1 since the takeover at an annual cost of around $2.5B; on UBS's own estimate the full version means about $22B at UBS AG including the ordinance changes. Every dollar of that is a dollar not bought back, and returns on equity fall if the equity base has to grow. The final law may not come until 2027, which leaves buybacks uncertain. The investment bank's record second quarter was led by equities trading, which follows market activity, and wealth fees fall when markets do. The shares had already run 163% in five years before September's 13.6% drop, and UBS has said publicly that the vote failed to address the causes of Credit Suisse's collapse. The fight with its home country is not over.

Top 3 Risks

01
The final Swiss capital law. The full deduction of foreign subsidiaries means about $20B of extra CET1 at UBS AG on UBS's estimate; the upper house's 90% version about $16B, on top of about $2B from ordinance steps, on UBS's own September 23 statement. The lower house decides next, likely in 2027. Both the chairman and a top-10 shareholder have now raised moving the bank's base out of Switzerland, which UBS says is not its goal.
02
Capital returns slow down. The $3B buyback runs to mid-2027 at the latest, and anything more depends on parliament. Investors who bought for buybacks could be disappointed.
03
Markets turn. 1H26 profits rode strong equity trading and rising asset values. A market downturn would hit wealth fees, transaction income and the investment bank together, just as integration savings stop adding new gains.
Industry Commentary
Too big for its home country
KEY DEBATE

The Credit Suisse collapse left Switzerland with one global bank, with a $1.7 trillion balance sheet at June 30, 2026. The government's answer is to make the parent bank, UBS AG, fully deduct its investments in foreign subsidiaries from its own capital, so that losses abroad are covered by capital held at home. UBS said the 90% upper-house version was not a compromise and did not address the root causes of Credit Suisse's collapse. The debate has since reached the question of where UBS is based: chairman Colm Kelleher warned on September 17 that the bank would have to think about its Swiss base if it could not compete (Reuters), and on September 30 Artisan Partners, a top-10 shareholder, urged the board to leave. UBS replied that its goal is to keep operating as a global bank from Switzerland. For shareholders the question is not whether UBS can afford the capital, since its CET1 ratio is 14.4%, but how many years of buybacks it absorbs.

Global wealth keeps compounding
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Wealth management is the part of banking investors pay most for: fee income, sticky clients and little capital. UBS's GWM took in $72.9B of net new assets in 1H26, and the CEO said profit before tax doubled in Asia-Pacific and grew 85% in the Americas in the second quarter. The 2028 ambitions (more than $5.5 trillion of invested assets and more than $200B a year of net new assets) assume this keeps going.

Rates: dollars help, lower-rate currencies squeeze
MIXED

UBS earns interest income in many currencies. The CFO said the raised 2026 GWM interest-income outlook reflects strong loan growth, "higher US dollar rates than previously assumed" and a better deposit mix, more than offsetting margin compression in lower-rate currencies. In Switzerland the CFO pointed to higher loan volumes as the driver of P&C net interest income. A fall in US rates would remove one of 2026's tailwinds.
