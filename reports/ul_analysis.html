01 Company Overview

Unilever sells soap, shampoo, deodorant, detergent and mayonnaise to most of the planet — Dove, Vaseline, Sunsilk, Rexona, Persil/Omo, Knorr, Hellmann's. Since demerging its ice-cream arm in December 2025 it reports four Business Groups: Beauty & Wellbeing, Personal Care, Home Care and Foods. It publishes a full P&L only twice a year, with quarterly trading statements in between. The latest reported period is H1 2026 (January–June 2026, released July 28, 2026): turnover €25.6B (+0.5%), underlying sales growth 4.8%, underlying operating margin 20.3%. The most recent full year, FY2025, produced turnover of €50.50B (−3.8%) on StockAnalysis's presentation. At $133.67B it ranks #167 globally and #8 in the United Kingdom by companiesmarketcap's country ranking (sixth among FTSE 100 names on that list). Its home index is the FTSE 100; the NYSE ADS is the line priced on this page.

The premise to test: is this a reset under new management? The people check out — the H1 2026 release names Fernando Fernandez as CEO and Srinivas Phatak as CFO, and StockAnalysis's profile (updated Sep 19, 2026) agrees. The reset is real in the portfolio: The Magnum Ice Cream Company was demerged on December 6, 2025; on March 31, 2026 Unilever agreed to combine most of Foods with McCormick; a string of small brands has been sold. And it is showing up where it matters for a staples company — in volume. H1 2026 USG of 4.8% was 4.2% volume (UVG) and only 0.6% price (UPG); Q2 alone was 5.8% USG with 5.5% volume, which Unilever calls its best volume quarter in more than a decade. The part the premise leaves out: the stock has not rewarded it. The ADS is −9.7% over 52 weeks, it fell 22.8% in March 2026 as the McCormick deal emerged, and consensus is split — Buy from 5 analysts on StockAnalysis, Hold from 12 on MarketBeat.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Power Brands: 78% of turnover Emerging-market distribution (EM USG 7.0%) India scale (USG 8%) Advertising & shelf-space scale 20.3% underlying operating margin

A staples moat is the ability to sell more units without cutting price. H1 2026 shows exactly that: 4.2% volume growth on 0.6% price, with the Power Brands at 5.4% volume. In inflationary years the industry grows by raising prices; the test of a moat is whether volume holds once pricing power is spent. On H1 2026 evidence Unilever's does — but it is carried by emerging markets (7.0% USG) and India (8%, 10% in Q2), while developed markets (1.5%) and especially Europe (−0.9%) lag.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Price has stopped helping. UPG was 0.6% in H1 and 0.2% in Q2. Volume-led growth is healthier, but with price near zero, cost inflation has to be absorbed by productivity — and the €800M productivity programme launched in 2024 is already complete.
02
Developed markets are soft. Europe −0.9% USG and North America 2.7% in H1 2026. The growth is in markets whose currencies have tended to weaken against the euro — one reason 4.8% USG became 0.5% reported turnover growth.
03
Leverage. Net debt €26.0B, 2.3x net debt/EBITDA at June 30, 2026; StockAnalysis shows debt/equity of 1.75 and a current ratio of 0.74. Manageable for a staples business, but it caps buybacks until the Foods transaction closes.
04
Two separations at once. TMICC is gone, Foods is going, and more than half a dozen small disposals closed or are pending in 2026. Each one strands overhead and costs management attention — and the chairman of the European Works Council has said employee representatives were not involved at an early stage (FoodNavigator).

Notable One-Off Items

The trailing numbers are noisy. First, the demerger. StockAnalysis shows FY2025 net income of €9.47B against €5.74B in FY2024 and EPS of €4.32 against €2.58 — a 67% jump that underlying trading (margin 20.0%) does not explain; the likely cause is accounting for The Magnum Ice Cream Company's separation within discontinued operations (our inference; the items were not itemised in the sources fetched). Second, disposals. H1 2026 discontinued operations include a €299M gain on selling the India and Portugal ice-cream businesses to TMICC, and the half saw exits from Graze, the Indonesian tea business, Côte d'Ivoire, Central American Home Care and the Kwality Wall's stake. Third, the reported/underlying gap: H1 2026 underlying EPS €1.61 (+2.4%) vs diluted EPS €1.52 (−4.9%). For valuation, underlying EPS (consensus €3.22 for FY2026) is the cleaner base — which is why the forward P/E (16.8x) is well above the trailing (12.8x).

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The reset is working where it counts. USG 4.8% in H1 2026 with 4.2% from volume; Q2 volume 5.5%, the best in more than a decade; Power Brands (78% of turnover) +6.0%. Margins are holding (20.3%) without price. Once Foods is gone, Unilever is a Beauty, Personal Care and Home Care company whose groups grew 4.8–7.6% in H1 — and it trades at 16.8x forward earnings against 21.0x for P&G, with a 3.5% dividend yield, a €6B buyback plan, a 19.9% stake in TMICC and, after completion, a stake in the combined McCormick business as extra value. If the volume run-rate holds, the multiple gap should narrow.

Bear Case

Five years of flat price say the market has heard reset stories before. The deal that defines this one — handing the highest-margin group (Foods, 23.3%) to McCormick for mostly shares — cost the stock 22.8% in a month and prompted a high-profile long-term holder to sell out. Price growth is almost gone (0.6%), Europe is shrinking (−0.9%), leverage is 2.3x, and the trailing P/E of 12.8x is flattered by one-offs; on underlying consensus it is 16.8x. Two separations at once is a lot of execution to run alongside a volume push.

Top 3 Risks

01
Volume fades back to guidance. Management guides to "around 3%" volume for FY2026 after 4.2% in H1. If Q3 (Oct 28) shows volume slowing toward that level with price still near zero, USG drifts to the bottom of the 4–6% range and the reset narrative loses its evidence.
02
McCormick deal risk. CMA scrutiny, worker-guarantee disputes and shareholder approvals all sit between now and a mid-2027 close. A delay leaves Unilever carrying a separated-in-spirit Foods business; a renegotiation changes the value holders receive.
03
Currency. The growth engine is emerging markets, the accounts are in euros and the ADS is in dollars. Weak EM currencies have already turned 4.8% USG into 0.5% reported growth; a weaker euro would hit the USD value of the dividend directly.
Industry Commentary
Volume is the new price
CENTRAL QUESTION

For two years after the 2022 inflation shock, household-products growth came mainly from price. Unilever's H1 2026 mix — 4.2% volume, 0.6% price — is the opposite, and its Q2 (5.5% volume, 0.2% price) is the cleanest volume print the company has reported in more than a decade. Our reading (analysis, not sourced fact): the market is not yet paying for it because investors cannot tell how much is durable brand strength and how much is emerging-market recovery that could reverse with currencies or commodity costs. Two or three more quarters of volume at or above guidance would settle it.

The end of the consumer conglomerate
ONGOING THEME

"Death of the consumer conglomerate? Unilever bets less is more" ran as a headline in late August 2026. Unilever has now separated ice cream, is separating Foods, and is selling small brands (Graze, Zwitsal, SariWangi). The bet is that a narrower Beauty, Personal Care and Home Care company earns a higher multiple than the sum it used to trade as. The counter-argument, made publicly by Terry Smith on his exit, is that break-ups driven by activists are a substitute for running the business better.
