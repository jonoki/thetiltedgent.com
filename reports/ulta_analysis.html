01 Company Overview

Ulta Beauty calls itself the largest specialty beauty retailer in the United States, and the pitch fits on a store sign: All Things Beauty. All in One Place. A drugstore mascara and a department-store serum sit in the same aisle, a salon chair waits at the back, and a loyalty program ties it all together. At the end of the second quarter of fiscal 2026 (August 1, 2026) the company ran 1,622 stores — 1,534 in the US and 88 international, almost all of the latter Space NK shops in the UK and Ireland. Fiscal 2025 (the year ended January 31, 2026) brought net sales of $12.39B, up 9.7%, comparable sales up 5.4%, and diluted EPS of $25.64, up only 1.2%. The company was known as ULTA Salon, Cosmetics & Fragrance until January 2017, and it has been in the S&P 500 since April 18, 2016.

This report is about the gap between those two growth rates. Sales grew almost 10% last year; earnings per share barely moved, because the money went into advertising, corporate overhead, incentive pay and the Space NK deal. In fiscal 2025's fourth quarter, SG&A (selling, general and administrative costs) jumped 23.0% and operating margin fell to 12.2% from 14.8%. When management paired that with a fiscal 2026 guide of 6–7% sales growth, the stock went from a $684.79 close at the end of February 2026 to $522.71 at the end of March. Since then the business has done what it promised. Q2 fiscal 2026 sales rose 8.9%, EPS rose 13.3%, and guidance went up twice. The stock sits at $557.86, about 18.5% below that February close. So the question is whether this year's spending buys growth or just keeps a crowded market from taking share.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

≈47M loyalty members Mass-to-prestige under one roof In-store salon services 1,534 US stores Vendor co-marketing scale Stores as e-com fulfilment nodes ROIC 25.42%

The moat is the customer file. Plenty of retailers sell lipstick. Few can tell a brand which 47 million shoppers bought its product, what else they bought, and when they will be back. That data is why brands launch at Ulta and fund promotions there, and it is why a store full of other companies' products earns a 25.42% return on invested capital. The weakness is that none of it is exclusive. Sephora has its own loyalty program and prestige brands. Amazon and Walmart sell the same mass brands at lower prices. Even after the Target partnership ends, Target is building its own beauty offer in the same stores where Ulta used to operate.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth is being bought with SG&A. In fiscal 2025, SG&A grew 17.4% against 9.7% sales growth, and in Q4 it jumped 23.0%, which cut the quarter's operating margin to 12.2% from 14.8%. The company cited strategic enterprise investments, advertising and incentive pay. Q2 FY2026 showed some reversal, with SG&A at 26.4% of sales against 26.6% a year earlier. The full-year result still depends on that spending turning into sustained comps.
02
The second half gets harder. The raised guidance still implies a slowdown: roughly 2–3% comps and 4–5% sales growth in H2 against 3.8% comps in Q2. The stock fell 4.18% on August 28, 2026, the day after an EPS beat and a guidance raise. The market was pricing the slowdown, not the beat.
03
Channel and mix dilute gross margin. Q2 gross margin slipped to 39.1% from 39.2% "primarily due to the impact of the Space NK business mix," and the FY2025 release named "adverse channel mix" (more e-commerce) as a drag. Every extra point of online growth and every Space NK pound of sales is worth a little less gross profit than a US store sale.
04
The Target channel is gone. The shop-in-shop partnership that launched in 2021 ended in August 2026, when Ulta and Target mutually chose not to renew (joint release, Aug 14, 2025). Target is replacing it with its own beauty offer. Revenue from those shops was never broken out, so the direct financial hit cannot be measured from the sources fetched. What is certain is that a distribution partner is now a competitor.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, Space NK: the July 2025 acquisition (terms undisclosed; $386.8M "acquisitions, net of cash acquired" in the H1 FY2025 cash-flow statement) inflates year-over-year sales growth until it laps in Q3 FY2026. It also dilutes gross margin. Second, buybacks: Ulta repurchased 1.4M shares for $791.1M in H1 FY2026, has $1.0B left under its $3.0B authorisation, and says it expects to use all of it by the end of FY2026. That is about 4.2% of the $23.85B market cap within roughly six months. EPS growth will run well ahead of net income growth. Third, short-term debt rose to $339.6M at August 1, 2026 from $62.3M at fiscal year-end. The company is borrowing short-term to fund part of the buyback, which reverses the net-cash balance sheet it had a few years ago. There are no adjusted figures to reconcile: Ulta reports GAAP EPS.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The investment year is paying off. Q2 FY2026 brought 3.8% comps on top of a 6.7% comparison, operating margin that expanded year on year (12.5% vs 12.4%), EPS up 13.3%, and a second guidance raise to $28.70–$29.00. The business earns a 25.42% ROIC, has about 47M loyalty members, and posts high-teens e-commerce comps with stores handling most online fulfilment. Management will retire another ~4% of the share count by January. At 19–20x earnings with EPS growth in the low teens, the stock is priced at about its own 200-day average, not at a premium. The Street's 13% average upside assumes nothing more than delivering the guidance.

Bear Case

The best quarter of the year is behind it. The guidance implies second-half comps of 2–3% and sales growth of 4–5% once Space NK laps. The Target channel closed in August and is being replaced by a Target-run competitor. Sephora, Amazon and Walmart are fighting for the same customer. TTM net income grew 0.7%, and the 5.2% EPS growth came from the buyback, which is now partly funded by short-term debt that rose to $339.6M. The stock has fallen more than 20% in a single month three times in five years, including once on a mid-quarter warning about the category (April 2024) and once on a margin-squeezed annual report (March 2026). A PEG of 1.86 leaves little room for another.

Top 3 Risks

01
Second-half comp slowdown. The raised guidance implies 2–3% comps in H2 against 3.8% in Q2. The stock has already shown what it does when growth slows without margin to show for it: −25.7% in May 2023, −22.6% in April 2024, and −23.7% in March 2026.
02
Margin squeeze from mix and spend. Space NK and e-commerce both dilute gross margin. The SG&A build-up that cut FY2025 operating margin to 12.4% from 13.9% has only just stopped getting worse. A promotional holiday season would hit both at once.
03
Competitive channel shift. Target's beauty business moves from partner to rival after August 2026, Sephora competes directly for prestige customers, and the mass platforms compete on price. Ulta's defence is the loyalty file and the salon chair. Both are real, but neither prevents a shopper from buying the same bottle somewhere else.
Industry Commentary
The end of the beauty boom, and the cost of defending share
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

For years beauty was the category that kept growing when shoppers cut back on clothing. That drew in competitors: Target opened Ulta shops, Kohl's opened Sephora shops, and Macy's expanded Bluemercury (CNBC, April 2024). The category then slowed, and in fiscal 2025 Ulta chose to spend to defend its position. That meant more advertising, strategic enterprise investment, and the Space NK acquisition. It worked on the sales line: comps +5.4% and net sales +9.7%. EPS grew just 1.2%. The fiscal 2026 question is whether that spending has now become a fixed base on which sales growth can generate operating leverage. Q2 was the clearest evidence so far that it can: SG&A 26.4% of sales vs 26.6%, and operating margin 12.5% vs 12.4%.

Channel reshuffle: Target out, international in
ONGOING TRANSITION

The distribution map is changing on both sides. Domestically, the Ulta Beauty at Target partnership, running since 2021, ended in August 2026 by mutual agreement, and Target is keeping the beauty space for its own offer. Internationally, Ulta now owns Space NK (83 UK and Ireland stores at acquisition, run as a standalone subsidiary), has a joint venture in Mexico and a franchise in the Middle East. Management highlighted World Cup activations in Mexico and a Middle East store-opening plan on the Q2 call (Investing.com). International is the new source of growth, but for now it is small. It is 88 of 1,622 stores, and its main visible effect so far has been a lower gross margin.

The valuation debate — bull and bear framing
BALANCED

At $557.86, ULTA trades at 20.32x trailing and about 19x forward earnings, 13.53x EV/EBITDA, and a 4.82% free-cash-flow yield, all returned through buybacks. The bull framing: a business earning 25% on invested capital, growing EPS in the low teens and shrinking its share count 4% a year deserves at least a market multiple. The March collapse punished an investment year that is now paying off. The bear framing: the EPS growth is financial rather than operational. Net income is flat, the buyback is partly debt-funded, and the second half gets harder just as a partner turns into a competitor. The next two reports settle it: Q3 FY2026 comps against the 2–3% implied range, and whether holiday-quarter operating margin beats last year's 12.2%.
