Section 01

Company Overview

UnitedHealth Group is two businesses bolted together: UnitedHealthcare, the largest health insurer in the United States, covering 48.3 million people; and Optum, a health-services conglomerate that owns one of the country's three big pharmacy benefit managers, its largest employed-physician network, and the plumbing that processes roughly one in three American medical claims. FY2025 revenue was $447.6B, up 11.8% year over year, with TTM revenue of about $450B through September 2026. That made it No. 3 on the 2026 Fortune 500 list of US companies, behind Amazon and Walmart.

The story of the last two years, though, is not growth — it is the collapse and repair of profitability. Net income fell from $22.4B in FY2023 to $12.1B in FY2025 as medical costs ran far ahead of premium pricing, Medicare Advantage risk-model changes bit, and the company absorbed the Change Healthcare cyberattack, a DOJ investigation, and a CEO change. FY2025 net margin bottomed at 2.69%. 2026 has been the repair: three straight quarters above Nasdaq consensus, the latest on October 13, when Q3 adjusted EPS of $4.69 beat a $4.12 consensus and the medical care ratio came in at 88.4% against 89.9% a year earlier. Management raised FY2026 guidance to at least $20.50 of adjusted EPS (at least $19.50 GAAP), and CFO Wayne DeVeydt called the bottom of the company's 13–16% long-term growth target "a more reasonable starting point" for 2027 than current analyst forecasts.

Section 03

Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of Moat

Scale & Purchasing Power Vertical Integration Claims Data Asset Regulatory Barriers Provider Network Density Employer Switching Costs Actuarial & Risk-Coding Capability Insurance Float

The moat is that UnitedHealth sits on both sides of most of its own transactions. A UnitedHealthcare member can see an Optum Health physician, fill a prescription through an Optum Rx mail pharmacy, and have the claim adjudicated on Change Healthcare rails — with the margin captured at every step and the underlying data feeding back into pricing and risk-coding models nobody else can replicate. Scale compounds this: at ~$450B of revenue, UNH negotiates hospital and drug rates that a regional plan simply cannot match, and a one-point improvement in medical cost ratio is worth roughly $3B of pre-tax earnings. Entry is effectively closed — state-by-state insurance licensing, capital reserve requirements, CMS contracting and multi-year Medicare Advantage Star Ratings history make greenfield competition impossible, and employer benefit contracts are sticky on three-year cycles. The company also enjoys classic insurance float: premiums arrive before claims are paid, funding $28.5B of cash and short-term investments at September 30 and $5.0B of buybacks in the first nine months of 2026.

The uncomfortable corollary: that same integration is precisely what regulators, plaintiffs' lawyers and both political parties have identified as the problem. The moat and the legal exposure are the same asset.

Key Vulnerabilities

Department of Justice criminal and civil investigations

UNH has confirmed it is under both criminal and civil DOJ investigation over Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment practices — specifically whether diagnosis codes were added to inflate CMS payments. This is not a settled-and-move-on matter: it goes to the mechanics of how the most profitable segment earns revenue. A negative resolution could bring a large monetary penalty, a corporate integrity agreement, and — most damaging — a forced change to coding practice that permanently lowers MA revenue per member. Timing is unknowable and headlines are the single largest source of single-day drawdown risk in this name.

Vertical integration under political attack

PBM reform bills targeting spread pricing and rebate retention have bipartisan support; separate proposals would force insurers to divest owned pharmacies. Optum Rx's own May 2026 move to transparent fee-based pricing is an acknowledgement that the old economics are not defensible. Meanwhile the FTC and state AGs continue to scrutinise insurer ownership of physician practices. Any structural remedy would hit the earnings engine, not just the growth rate.

Medical cost trend and the limits of repricing

The 2024–25 earnings collapse happened because utilisation — outpatient procedures, behavioural health, specialty drugs, GLP-1s — ran materially above pricing assumptions for six consecutive quarters. The Q3 2026 ratio improved, but $610M of it came from reserves set aside earlier in the year that proved too large, and management said on the call that commercial cost trend is still running above the 11% it had priced for. Insurers price annually; if trend surprises again mid-year, there is no lever to pull until the next bid cycle.

Membership attrition and reputational damage

UNH is shrinking on purpose, but shrinking is dangerous in a business with fixed administrative costs and Star Ratings tied to scale. Medicare Advantage is down 840,000 lives since year-end 2025; ACA exchange membership is falling with the expiry of enhanced subsidies. The 2027 Star Ratings, published October 8, went the wrong way: Reuters reported UnitedHealth's ratings fell even as about 71% of members in Medicare Advantage drug plans landed in four-star-or-better plans (CMS), and those ratings set the bonus payments for 2028. Layer on the post-2024 public backlash over claim denials and prior authorisation — which drove the company's own May 2026 pledge to cut its prior-authorisation volume by 30% — and the brand is a liability in employer and Medicaid procurement in a way it was not five years ago.

Concentration in government-funded revenue

Roughly half of UnitedHealthcare's premium revenue ultimately comes from federal and state governments via Medicare Advantage, Medicaid and exchange subsidies. That is a single counterparty with the unilateral ability to change rates, audit methodology (RADV) and eligibility rules. Annual CMS rate notices are, functionally, the most important earnings input the company does not control.

Notable One-Off Items

• GAAP vs adjusted gap of $1.00/share in FY2026 guidance (at least $19.50 GAAP vs at least $20.50 adjusted) is about $1.55 of amortisation of acquisition-related intangibles, less about $0.25 of divestiture gains and $0.30 of tax effects, per the company's reconciliation — the amortisation is a recurring consequence of two decades of dealmaking, not a true one-off.

• Latin America exit — the disposal of the South American operations (principally Banmédica) produced multi-billion-dollar losses on sale recognised across 2024–2025, depressing GAAP earnings without affecting ongoing cash generation.

• Value-based care contract exits in Optum Health are suppressing reported revenue growth while improving margin — a deliberate, multi-quarter distortion rather than a discrete charge.

The five-year trajectory

From $460.47 at the end of October 2021 to $406.67 on October 15, 2026, UNH has lost 11.7% on price over five years. Over the same window SPY, the S&P 500 fund, rose 68.1% on price, and QQQ, the Nasdaq-100 fund, 88.6%. An investor who bought the largest healthcare company in the US at the start of the period and held is still behind on price, after a 59% fall between the November 2024 and July 2025 month-end closes along the way. That is the whole lesson of this chart.

The path was not a slow grind. It was a three-year bull market, an eight-month collapse, and then a second, smaller collapse most people have already forgotten. From late 2021 through November 2024 the stock compounded from ~$460 to a month-end record of $610.20 (an intraday high of $630.73 on November 11), on Medicare Advantage growth, Optum acquisitions and margins near 8.8% at the operating line. Then, between the November 2024 close and the July 2025 close of $249.56, it lost 59% — a sequence of a murdered division CEO and a public backlash, a 22% single-day guidance cut, a CEO resignation, suspended guidance and a reported DOJ criminal probe, each landing within weeks of the last. The recovery off the August 2025 low ran to a $345.30 close by September — and then failed: the stock slid through the winter to a $270.59 close in March 2026, setting the current 52-week low of $255.97 along the way, before an extraordinary +37% April (to $370.48) around the Q1 print and the 2027 Medicare Advantage rate cycle. June 2026's $415.63 was the highest month-end close of the recovery; the stock slid to $367.08 at the end of September, then rallied after the Q3 print to $406.67, 2.2% below that June close.

The moving averages tell a clean version of this. The 10-month line (the "200-day" proxy) capped every rally from December 2024 into early 2026 and was reclaimed decisively only in April 2026; at ~$359 it still sits well below the current price, so the longer trend has not broken. The 3-month line (~$388) was lost at the August close and regained with the October rally. Monthly RSI(6) bottomed near 20 in July 2025 and near 29 in March 2026 — two genuine capitulation readings in nine months — peaked around 67 in June, fell to 49 at the end of September and is back near 60: firm but not overbought. On daily data the 14-day RSI is 68, just under the 70 line, and the price is 5.0% above its 50-day average ($387.35) and 13.4% above its 200-day average ($358.75).

Relative performance

Investors cut the price they will pay for every managed-care insurer's earnings on medical cost trend and political risk, with UNH the largest and most visible casualty. Against the S&P 500 the gap is stark: from the October 2021 month-end to October 15, 2026, SPY rose 68.1% on price and UNH fell 11.7%, a gap of almost 80 points, most of it created in the eight months from November 2024 to July 2025 and the rest in the winter of 2025–26. The twelve-month picture is ordinary: UNH is up 12.6% on price from its October 15, 2025 close of $361.15, behind SPY's 16.0% and QQQ's 20.9% over the same dates. Both statements are true, which is exactly why position sizing and entry point matter more in this name than in almost any other large US stock.

Section 08

Summary & Key Risks

BULL CASE

UnitedHealth is the lowest-cost, most vertically integrated operator in a structurally growing $1.6T market, and it is emerging from a two-year margin reset with pricing discipline restored: the MCR improved 150 bp year over year in Q3, UnitedHealthcare's operating margin rose to 3.3% from 2.1%, and FY26 guidance has now been raised twice, to at least $20.50 of adjusted EPS. At 18x forward earnings for a business that generated $25.1B of operating cash flow in nine months, pays a 2.3% dividend with 16 years of growth, and has bought back $5.0B of stock this year, the market is paying a modest premium for a franchise whose normalised ROE is closer to 20–25% than today's 16%.

If 2027 delivers the at-least-13% EPS growth management now points to, off a $20.50 base, the stock is worth the $477–$490 mean and median targets the sell side has on it — a 17–21% gap to the Oct 15 close — with more upside if investors start valuing each business separately, since Optum Insight alone earns a 23% margin.

BEAR CASE

The recovery is being bought at 18x forward earnings on a company whose revenue fell 0.4% in Q3, whose largest profit pool has shed 840,000 members this year and just took weaker 2027 Star Ratings, and whose most profitable practice — Medicare Advantage risk coding — is the explicit subject of an open DOJ criminal investigation with no timetable and no quantifiable outcome. Margin recovery achieved by exiting customers is finite; you cannot shrink to a growth multiple forever.

The July 16 tape is the warning: a 29% earnings beat against Nasdaq consensus with raised guidance produced a 1.2% close after opening up 7.4%, and the shares then fell 13% to the September close with no guidance change and no DOJ headline. October's first session repeated it — a beat and a raise, and a close down 0.2% — before the stock recovered. Part of the Q3 beat was a $610M reserve release, which a later quarter need not repeat. Meanwhile PBM reform, prior-authorisation concessions, RADV audits and now a Texas investigation each chip at economics that were never politically durable, and management says commercial cost trend is still running above what it priced for — the improvement is UNH's pricing, not the trend.

Top 3 Risks

  1. DOJ resolution on Medicare Advantage risk adjustmentAn adverse criminal or civil outcome could combine a large penalty with mandated coding changes that permanently reduce revenue per Medicare Advantage member — the highest-margin revenue in the company. Because timing and magnitude are both unknowable, the market cannot price it, which is precisely why headline risk here dominates day-to-day volatility.
  2. Medical cost trend reaccelerating before the next bid cycleQ3's 88.4% MCR was helped by $610M of favourable reserve development, and management says commercial cost trend is running above the 11% it had assumed. Insurers can only reprice annually; a repeat of 2024–25, when utilisation ran above assumptions for six straight quarters, would break the recovery narrative with no lever available until the 2028 bids.
  3. Structural regulation of vertical integrationPBM reform, forced pharmacy divestiture proposals, prior-authorisation mandates and FTC scrutiny of insurer-owned physician practices all target the same thing: UNH's ability to capture margin at every step of its own claims. Optum Rx's voluntary move to transparent fee-based pricing signals management believes the old economics are indefensible — which means some of them are already being given away.

Industry Commentary

Medical cost trend is still the only number that matters

Critical Constraint

Every managed-care thesis since 2023 reduces to one question: is utilisation normalising or permanently higher? Post-pandemic deferred care, an aging Medicare Advantage cohort, behavioural health expansion, outpatient surgical volume and GLP-1 spend all pushed industry medical cost ratios up 200–400 bp between 2023 and 2025. UNH's MCR went from 82.0% in FY2022 and 83.2% in FY2023 to 89.4% in Q2 2025 and 89.9% in Q3 2025, before recovering to 86.7% in Q2 2026 and 88.4% in Q3 2026 — and the Q3 figure was helped by $610M of favourable reserve development. On the Q3 call UnitedHealthcare CEO Tim Noel said commercial cost trend is running above the 11% the company had expected, citing No Surprises Act arbitration costs, pricier care from clinicians and high-cost specialty drugs. The improvement is UNH's own repricing, not sector-wide relief. On a $450B revenue base, 100 bp of MCR is roughly $3B pre-tax. Nothing else in the model has that leverage.

Managed care has become a political asset class

Ongoing Headwind

Prior authorisation, claim denial rates, PBM spread pricing, insurer-owned physician networks and Medicare Advantage upcoding are now bipartisan targets, and the December 2024 killing of Brian Thompson converted a policy debate into a public one. The industry's response has been pre-emptive concession: UnitedHealthcare has pledged to remove 30% of current prior-approval volume and nearly two-thirds of paediatric requirements by the end of 2026; Optum Rx replaced traditional PBM pricing with a transparent fee model; and in its Q3 release the company said it will rebate its 2026 profits on individual ACA plans to about 1 million members. Each move reduces legislative tail risk and reduces earnings. The pressure is now coming from states too: Texas opened an investigation into UnitedHealth's care denials on October 7 — a reminder that half of this industry's revenue is set by legislators, not customers.

The margin-over-membership pivot — and what it means for 2027

Emerging Tailwind

For fifteen years the entire sector optimised for membership growth, on the theory that scale eventually converts to margin. The 2024–25 reset ended that. UNH has shed 1.46M medical members since the end of 2025 (49.76M to 48.30M), 840,000 of them in Medicare Advantage, by exiting counties, redesigning benefits and walking away from value-based care contracts — and UnitedHealthcare's Q3 operating margin rose to 3.3% from 2.1% while doing so. The bull framing: this is a one-time purge that leaves a smaller, structurally more profitable book, and 2027 becomes the first clean year since 2023; management now points to at least 13% adjusted EPS growth off the at-least-$20.50 2026 figure, which would justify the current 18x forward multiple. The bear framing: shrinking is not a strategy, fixed administrative costs and Star Ratings both scale with membership, the 2027 ratings already went the wrong way, competitors are taking the lives UNH gives up, and a company whose revenue is flat should not trade at a ~30% premium to its peer group. The fuller 2027 outlook promised before year-end will settle which framing the market adopts.
