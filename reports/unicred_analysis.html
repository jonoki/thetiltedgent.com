01 Company Overview

UniCredit is a Milan-based commercial bank with five operating divisions — Italy, Germany (HypoVereinsbank, "HVB"), Central & Eastern Europe, Austria (Bank Austria) and a shrinking Russian unit — that together hold about €443B of customer loans and €504B of deposits (June 30, 2026, excluding repos). It was formed in 1998 from the merger of Credito Italiano and Unicredito, added HVB in 2005 and Capitalia in 2007, and is a constituent of the FTSE MIB and the Euro Stoxx 50. FY2025 net profit was €10.6B (+14%) on revenue of €24.5B, with EPS of €6.89 (+20%) and a dividend of €3.15 per share (+31%).

The latest reported period is the second quarter of 2026, published July 23, 2026. Net profit was €2.9B, about 2.5% above the €2.835B average of 16 brokers that UniCredit's own investor-relations team collected on July 7; first-half profit was a record €6.1B (€6.37B excluding a €261M trading one-off, mainly hedging and funding costs on its larger Commerzbank position) at a RoTE of 23.7%. Management raised its 2026 target to "well above" €11B, or about €11.5B excluding integration costs, from "equal to or above" €11B. The shares still fell 4.8% that day (€83.21 → €79.22). The reason is the other half of the story: UniCredit is taking control of Germany's Commerzbank. Its exchange offer (0.485 UniCredit shares per Commerzbank share) closed in July with UniCredit at 47.59% of Commerzbank's capital and 49.65% of its voting rights, subject to regulatory approval — and paying for it means cancelling the €4.75B FY2025 share buyback and running capital about 2 points lower once Commerzbank is consolidated.

CEO Andrea Orcel, in the results release: "Unlimited is delivering a step change: we are accelerating profitable revenue growth, with clear evidence of quality market share gains and commercial momentum, while continuing to reset the efficiency frontier." ("UniCredit Unlimited" is the 2026–28 plan launched with the FY2025 results.) Over five years the stock has gone from €11.50 (September 2021 month-end) to €78.85 — up 585.7% on price alone, against +96.6% for the FTSE MIB — plus €8.88 a share of dividends paid along the way.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

€504B deposit base Top-tier cost efficiency Corporate & Mittelstand franchise Multi-country CEE network Capital generation Single-A credit ratings

UniCredit's edge is operating discipline on top of a deposit franchise. Deposits fund loans at an 87.8% loan-to-deposit ratio; deposit costs moved only about 30% of the way with market rates over the period (its "pass-through"); and a €218B structural hedge of core deposits at an average 1.59% yield, about five years' average maturity, smooths net interest income as rates move. On costs it has cut while growing: operating costs fell 0.9% in 1H26 and the cost/income ratio of 34% compares with 36.5% a year earlier. In 2Q26 alone it generated about 85bps of CET1 organically (€2.5B), against 84bps of accrued distributions — enough to fund an 80% payout. Moody's raised it to A3 in November 2025, its third single-A rating, and S&P moved its outlook to positive in February 2026.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Commerzbank execution and politics. An internal ECB assessment reported by Reuters (August 12, 2026) found no grounds to object but expects a difficult, drawn-out integration; Germany's BaFin, per the same report, flagged weaknesses in UniCredit's checks and balances. Commerzbank's management and the German government (holding just under 13%) resisted the bid; by late September Berlin had set conditions instead — a continued listing, a Frankfurt head office and continued lending to the Mittelstand — and Commerzbank's CEO said the two banks were discussing options (Reuters, via Retail Banker International, September 25).
02
Rate sensitivity. 1H26 net interest income fell 0.8% to €7.2B on lower average Euribor. UniCredit's own sensitivity: ±50bps in rates ≈ €0.35B of annual NII, and each percentage point of deposit pass-through ≈ €90M.
03
Home economy and Russia. UniCredit's economists forecast Italian GDP growth of 0.5% in 2026 and euro-area inflation near 3% in the second half after an energy shock tied to the Middle East. The Russian bank is shrinking but remains a sanctions and write-off exposure.
04
Hedging costs and complexity. The strategic portfolio pays dividends but needs hedging: a €245M one-off trading loss in 2Q26 on the larger Commerzbank position, plus €214M of structural hedge cost in the quarter. Earnings now depend partly on stakes in other banks and an insurer.

Notable One-Off Items

2Q26: a −€245M trading one-off (−€261M in 1H26), "mainly due to the hedging & funding costs connected to increased position in Commerzbank - and related protection". Excluding it, 2Q26 net profit would have been €3.1B and revenue growth 10.7% rather than 6.6%. Integration costs were €0.1B. 2Q25 carried a circa €650M positive one-off below the line. UniCredit's adjusted measure strips out that one-off and the trading loss, excludes Russia and spreads provisions evenly — on that basis 1H26 profit is up 24%, against +0.1% reported. Badwill: €231M of 1H26 profit came from accounting gains on equity-consolidating Commerzbank and Alpha Bank and is excluded from distributions. Ahead: about 200bps of CET1 on fully consolidating Commerzbank, before purchase-price-allocation effects, and €2.2B of planned pre-tax investment to deliver an expected €1.2B of pre-tax value before any merger.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the September 2021 month-end of €11.50 to the October 2, 2026 close of €78.85, UniCredit rose 585.7% on price — almost a sevenfold gain — while the FTSE MIB roughly doubled (+96.6%) and Intesa Sanpaolo gained 157.9%. Dividends of €8.88 a share were paid over the same period (Yahoo dividend records), equal to 77% of the 2021 starting price. The path had three phases: a 2022 slump to a five-year low of €8.90 (April 2022); a long climb from late 2022 to early 2025 as rates rose and the payout grew; and a 2025–26 run to a five-year high month-end of €84.14 (August 2026), interrupted by a −16.0% March 2026.

The 6-period monthly RSI peaked at 94.5 in March 2024, its most overbought reading in the series, and sat between 72 and 79 from June to September 2026; the October 1–2 drop pulled it to 60.7, back in neutral territory. The price is below its 3-month average (≈€81.96) and above its 10-month average (≈€75.23): the uptrend is intact on this measure, momentum has cooled.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

UniCredit earns a 23.7% return on tangible equity at a 34% cost/income ratio, just raised 2026 guidance to about €11.5B, and pays out 80% of profit. At €78.85 it trades on 11.2x trailing and 10.3x consensus 2026 earnings, 1.90x tangible book and a 4.0% trailing yield. Commerzbank comes with 49.65% of the votes lined up pending approval — the tendered 17.6% to be paid in UniCredit shares at 0.485 per Commerzbank share once approved — with €1.2B of pre-tax value creation targeted before any merger and an ECB reported to be leaning towards approval. The broker average target is €93.63, 18.7% above the price.

Bear Case

The best part of the re-rating has happened: up almost sevenfold in five years. Commerzbank swaps a €4.75B buyback for a contested integration that the ECB, per Reuters, expects to be difficult and drawn-out, against a Commerzbank management that resisted and a German government that has set conditions, and takes CET1 from about 15% to the 13% area. Net interest income slipped 0.8% in 1H26 on lower Euribor, Italy is barely growing, a Russian bank remains on the books, and a growing share of profit depends on hedged stakes in other financial companies. The market's verdict on a beat-and-raise quarter was −4.8%.

Top 3 Risks

01
Commerzbank goes slower or costs more. Approvals, German political conditions and a reluctant Commerzbank management could delay control past early 2027 or force concessions; the €2.2B of planned pre-tax investment and ~200bps capital hit come regardless of how fast value shows up.
02
Capital returns shrink. Running at the 13% area after consolidation (versus a 12.5–13% management range) leaves less room for buybacks while Commerzbank is absorbed; the FY25 buyback has already been redirected into the deal.
03
Rates and the Italian economy. ±50bps on rates moves annual NII by about €0.35B; with Italian growth forecast at 0.5% and an energy shock feeding inflation, credit costs could rise from the current 17bps toward or above the 20bp top of the guide.
Industry Commentary
European bank consolidation, cross-border at last
KEY DEBATE

UniCredit–Commerzbank would be a large cross-border banking combination inside the eurozone: a combined group that, per UniCredit's deck, would serve over 34 million clients (plus 3.7 million at Alpha Bank) across "15+1" federated banks. Deutsche Bank's Razzoli wrote on September 30 that consolidation is lifting Italian bank valuations. The open question is execution — whether a bank built on cost cutting can do the same in Germany, where finews reports UniCredit has indicated it could cut around 7,000 full-time jobs in Germany alone and that about €20B of corporate loans outside Germany and Poland could go, under a government that still owns just under 13% of Commerzbank.

After the rate cuts, deposit hedges carry the margin
ONGOING HEADWIND

As Euribor fell, the question for every deposit-rich eurozone bank has been how much interest income survives. UniCredit's answer is a €218B replicating portfolio and structural hedge (about 1.59% average yield, ~5-year maturity) plus disciplined deposit pricing. Its 1H26 NII fell only 0.8% despite lower Euribor, and its July outlook expected one final 25bp ECB hike in September 2026 — a scenario that would help margins rather than hurt them.

Fees, insurance and AI-led efficiency
EMERGING TAILWIND

With interest income flat, European banks are leaning on fee businesses — asset management, insurance, advisory — and on technology to cut costs. UniCredit's fees and insurance rose 10.9% to about 38% of net revenue in 1H26, investment fees +16.6% in 2Q26, while operating costs fell 0.9%. The results deck lists targeted AI in know-your-customer checks, corporate lending and transaction monitoring, and on the call management said AI is deployed only against clear bottom-up business cases (Investing.com transcript); the measurable outcome so far is a cost base that shrinks while loans and deposits grow 8%.
