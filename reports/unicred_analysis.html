01 Company Overview

UniCredit is a Milan-based commercial bank with five operating divisions — Italy, Germany (HypoVereinsbank, "HVB"), Central & Eastern Europe, Austria (Bank Austria) and a shrinking Russian unit — that together hold about €447B of customer loans and €509B of deposits (September 30, 2026, excluding repos). It was formed in 1998 from the merger of Credito Italiano and Unicredito, added HVB in 2005 and Capitalia in 2007, and is a constituent of the FTSE MIB and the Euro Stoxx 50. FY2025 net profit was €10.6B (+14%) on revenue of €24.5B, with EPS of €6.89 (+20%) and a dividend of €3.15 per share (+31%).

The latest reported period is the third quarter of 2026, published before the Milan open on November 9, 2026. Net profit was a record €3.26B, up 24% on a year earlier and about 17% above the €2.78B average of the brokers that UniCredit's own investor-relations team collected on October 16; revenue was €6.90B against €6.48B expected, and the RoTE reached 25.5%. Management raised its 2026 target to "well above" €11.5B before extraordinary charges, from about €11.5B excluding integration costs in July, and more than doubled the interim dividend to €4.5B (€3.0034 a share). The shares rose 6.3% that day (€76.80 → €81.62) and another 3.2% over the next two sessions, to €84.24. The other half of the story is Germany: UniCredit is taking control of Commerzbank. Its exchange offer (0.485 UniCredit shares per Commerzbank share) closed in July with UniCredit at 47.59% of Commerzbank's capital and 49.65% of its voting rights, subject to approvals. The ECB approved the change of control on October 15; UniCredit now expects the remaining approvals by late November, then settlement and control, and new governance at Commerzbank in the first quarter of 2027. Paying for it means cancelling the €4.75B FY2025 share buyback, and capital will run lower once Commerzbank is consolidated.

CEO Andrea Orcel, in the 3Q26 results release: "UniCredit has delivered its best quarter ever, and the best in the sector, beating across all KPIs …" And: "We are building a bank that can deliver through cycles." ("UniCredit Unlimited" is the 2026–28 plan launched with the FY2025 results.) Over five years the stock has gone from €10.69 (November 2021 month-end) to €84.24 — up 688.0% on price alone, against +101.2% for the FTSE MIB — plus €8.88 a share of dividends paid along the way.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

€509B deposit base Top-tier cost efficiency Corporate & Mittelstand franchise Multi-country CEE network Capital generation Single-A credit ratings

UniCredit's edge is operating discipline on top of a deposit franchise. Deposits fund loans at an 87.9% loan-to-deposit ratio; deposit costs moved only about 32% of the way with market rates in 3Q26 (its "pass-through", excluding Russia); and a €224B structural hedge of core deposits, at an average yield of about 1.67% and about five years' average maturity at the end of September 2026 (3Q26 results presentation), smooths net interest income as rates move. On costs it has cut while growing: operating costs fell 1.0% in 9M26 while loans grew 9%, and the cost/income ratio of 33.8% compares with 36.7% a year earlier. In 3Q26 alone it generated about 92bps of CET1 from profits (€2.8B), against 84bps set aside for shareholder payouts — enough to fund an 80% payout. Moody's rates it A3 (its stand-alone rating under review for an upgrade), S&P A- with a positive outlook and Fitch A- (3Q26 release).

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Commerzbank execution and politics. The ECB approved UniCredit taking control on October 15, 2026, but an internal ECB assessment reported by Reuters (August 12) expected a difficult, drawn-out integration, and Germany's BaFin, per the same report, flagged weaknesses in UniCredit's checks and balances. Commerzbank's management and the German government (holding just under 13%) resisted the bid; in September Berlin set conditions instead — a continued listing, a Frankfurt head office and continued lending to the Mittelstand. UniCredit's plan for Commerzbank includes cutting around 7,000 jobs (dpa-AFX, November 9).
02
Rate sensitivity. Net interest income is the largest revenue line: €11.1B in 9M26 (+2.1%), with 3Q26 up 8.2% on higher average Euribor and loan growth. UniCredit's 3Q26 sensitivity: ±0.5 percentage point on rates ≈ €0.35B of annual NII, and each percentage point of deposit pass-through ≈ €100M. Rising rates help the margin but raise borrowers' costs; a turn back down would undo the gain.
03
Home economy and Russia. UniCredit's economists forecast Italian GDP growth of 0.8% in 2026 and 0.6% in 2027, with Italian inflation already above 5% and euro-area inflation expected to climb above 4% after an energy shock tied to the Middle East. The Russian bank is shrinking but remains a sanctions and write-off exposure.
04
Hedging costs and complexity. The strategic portfolio pays dividends (€1.59B in 9M26) but needs hedging: a €245M one-off trading loss in 2Q26 on the larger Commerzbank position, before trading income recovered to €293M in 3Q26. Earnings now depend partly on stakes in other banks and an insurer.

Notable One-Off Items

3Q26: integration costs of €154M (€239M in 9M26); a €39M profit on investments, mainly badwill (an accounting gain) from Commerzbank's own share buyback; and a positive tax one-off in Germany that helped bring the stated tax rate down to 21.9%. 2Q26: a −€245M trading one-off (−€261M in 1H26), "mainly due to the hedging and funding cost connected to the increased position in Commerzbank and related protection". 2Q25: a €653M positive one-off from revaluing stakes in the life-insurance joint ventures; excluding it, 9M26 net profit is up 16% rather than 7%. Badwill: €308M of 9M26 profit, mostly from equity-consolidating Commerzbank and Alpha Bank, is excluded from distributions. Ahead: CET1 of 13% or above on fully consolidating Commerzbank, before purchase-price-allocation effects, and (per the July deck) €2.2B of planned pre-tax investment to deliver an expected €1.2B of pre-tax value before any merger.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 11, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end of €10.69 to the November 11, 2026 close of €84.24, UniCredit rose 688.0% on price — almost an eightfold gain — while the FTSE MIB roughly doubled (+101.2%) and Intesa Sanpaolo gained 197.9%. Dividends of €8.88 a share were paid over the same period (Yahoo dividend records), equal to 83% of the November 2021 starting price. The path had three phases: a 2022 slump to a five-year low of €8.90 (April 2022); a long climb from late 2022 to early 2025 as rates rose and the payout grew; and a 2025–26 run to a five-year high month-end of €84.14 (August 2026), interrupted by a −16.0% March 2026 and a −11.9% October 2026. The November 11 close is just above that month-end high.

The 6-period monthly RSI peaked at 94.4 in March 2024, its most overbought reading in the series, and sat between 72 and 79 from June to September 2026; October's fall pulled it to 48.2, and the November 11 close lifted it back to 64.9, below the 70 "overbought" line. The price is above both its 3-month average (≈€80.06) and its 10-month average (≈€75.73): on this measure the uptrend is intact and momentum has picked up again.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

UniCredit earned a 25.5% return on tangible equity in 3Q26 at a 32.8% cost/income ratio, beat its own analysts' consensus profit by 17%, raised 2026 guidance to well above €11.5B and more than doubled the interim dividend to €4.5B. At €84.24 it trades on 11.2x trailing and 11.1x consensus 2026 earnings, 1.94x tangible book and a 3.7% trailing yield, and CET1 rose to 15.0% after setting aside 80% of profit for shareholders. The ECB has approved control of Commerzbank; UniCredit expects the last approvals by late November and projects FY28 EPS well above €10 with Commerzbank included. The pre-results broker average target was €98.83, 17.3% above the price.

Bear Case

Up almost eightfold in five years, the easy gains are behind it. Commerzbank swaps a €4.75B buyback for a contested integration that the ECB, per Reuters, expected to be difficult and drawn-out, against a Commerzbank management that resisted and a German government that has set conditions, and it takes CET1 from about 15% to 13% or a little above. Loan-loss provisions nearly doubled in 9M26 (€587M against €305M), Italian inflation is above 5% by UniCredit's own count, a Russian bank remains on the books, and a growing share of profit depends on hedged stakes in other financial companies. October showed how fast Italian bank shares can fall: −11.9% in one month.

Top 3 Risks

01
Commerzbank goes slower or costs more. Approvals, German political conditions and a reluctant Commerzbank management could delay control past the first quarter of 2027 or force concessions; the €2.2B of planned pre-tax investment and ~200bps capital hit come regardless of how fast value shows up.
02
Capital returns shrink. Running at 13% or a little above after consolidation (versus a 12.5–13% management range) leaves less room for buybacks while Commerzbank is absorbed; the FY25 buyback has already been redirected into the deal.
03
Rates and the Italian economy. ±0.5 percentage point on rates moves annual NII by about €0.35B (3Q26 results presentation); with Italian growth forecast at 0.8%, Italian inflation above 5% and UniCredit's economists expecting another ECB rate rise in December, credit costs could rise from the current 17bps toward or above the 20bp top of the guide.
Industry Commentary
European bank consolidation, cross-border at last
KEY DEBATE

UniCredit–Commerzbank, cleared by the ECB on October 15, would be a large cross-border banking combination inside the eurozone: a combined group that, per UniCredit's deck, would serve over 34 million clients (plus 3.7 million at Alpha Bank) across "15+1" federated banks. Deutsche Bank's Razzoli wrote on September 30 that consolidation is lifting Italian bank valuations. The open question is execution — whether a bank built on cost cutting can do the same in Germany, where UniCredit's "Commerzbank Unlocked" plan includes cutting about 7,000 jobs and thinning Commerzbank's foreign network (dpa-AFX, November 9), under a government that still owns just under 13% of Commerzbank.

Rates turn up, and deposit pricing decides who keeps it
NEAR-TERM TAILWIND

After the ECB's cuts, Euribor has turned up again: UniCredit's 3Q26 net interest income rose 4.7% on the quarter, mainly on higher average Euribor and loan and deposit growth, while its deposit pass-through (the share of a rate move handed on to depositors) closed the quarter at 32.2%, up about one percentage point. Its economists expect at least one more 25bp ECB rate rise in December, with euro-area inflation above 4% — better for margins, harder on borrowers. A €224B structural hedge of core deposits (about 1.67% average yield and five-year maturity at the end of September 2026) smooths the path in either direction.

Fees, insurance and AI-led efficiency
EMERGING TAILWIND

Beyond interest income, European banks are leaning on fee businesses — asset management, insurance, advisory — and on technology to cut costs. UniCredit's fees and insurance rose 10.9% to €7.4B in 9M26, investment fees +14.8% in 3Q26, while operating costs fell 1.0%, helped, the release says, by AI-enabled process redesign and more automation. The July results deck listed targeted AI in know-your-customer checks, corporate lending and transaction monitoring, and on the July call management said AI is deployed only against clear bottom-up business cases (Investing.com transcript); the measurable outcome so far is a cost base that shrinks while loans grow 9% and deposits 7%.
