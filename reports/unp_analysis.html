01 Company Overview

Union Pacific runs a railroad of about 32,900 route miles across 23 states in the western two-thirds of the country, hauling agricultural goods, chemicals, coal, metals, forest products, automobiles and containerized freight. Fiscal 2025 operating revenue was $24.51B, up 1.1% YoY, with net income of $7.14B and diluted EPS of $11.98 (+8%). Results have picked up since: third-quarter 2026 revenue was $7.20B, up 15%, with adjusted EPS of $3.53 (+15%), and the TTM figures now stand at $26.36B of revenue and $12.82 of EPS. It ranks #69 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization and is the largest of the 12 railroad stocks on stockanalysis.com's industry list by market value.

The company is in the middle of the most consequential corporate event in modern American railroading: its proposed ~$85 billion acquisition of Norfolk Southern, which Union Pacific calls America's first transcontinental railroad. The deal, announced July 29, 2025, now sits in a formal Surface Transportation Board (STB) review. Under the schedule the Board adopted on August 18, 2026, final briefs are due May 28, 2027 and the Board decides within 90 days after it closes the record; on the Q3 call CEO Jim Vena said he expects the first earnings as a merged company about a year from now. The deal dominates the investment case, the risk profile and the capital allocation policy: buybacks have been effectively halted: $26M repurchased in the first nine months of 2026, against $2,679M for 2025, all of it in the first nine months. The deal’s consideration includes $88.82 in cash per Norfolk Southern share.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
IRREPLACEABLE RIGHT-OF-WAY WESTERN DUOPOLY vs BNSF ~32,900 ROUTE MILES / 23 STATES CAPTIVE SHIPPER PRICING REGULATORY ENTRY BARRIER ROIC 16.54%

Union Pacific's moat is the physical network itself. The roughly 32,900 route-mile western system was assembled from 19th-century land grants and a century of consolidation and could not be rebuilt at any price — the land assembly, environmental permitting and grade engineering alone are prohibitive, and the Surface Transportation Board's control rules make new Class I entry effectively impossible. West of the Mississippi the practical competitive set is Union Pacific and BNSF; large stretches of chemical, coal and grain origination are served by a single railroad, which is why "captive shipper" pricing is a live regulatory issue rather than a theoretical one. The economics that fall out of this structure are conspicuous: a gross margin of 55.77%, an operating margin of 40.75%, a net profit margin of 28.90%, ROIC of 16.54% and ROE of 38.75%, all on a business whose principal asset is dirt, steel and dispatching software. Pricing power is the durable part: freight revenue excluding fuel surcharge grew 3% on 1% carload growth in 2025 and 6% on 6% carload growth in Q3 2026, and management's stated 2026 objective is pricing dollars in excess of inflation dollars.

KEY VULNERABILITIES

1. The merger depends on a regulator, not a vote. The Norfolk Southern deal is decided by the STB. The Board accepted the revised application on May 28, 2026 but held it in abeyance (paused) pending supplemental information, then on August 18, 2026 lifted the abeyance and adopted a schedule: comments, protests and requests for conditions due November 18, 2026; responses February 16, 2027; final briefs May 28, 2027; then a decision within 90 days after the Board closes the record. BNSF, CSX and shipper groups filed motions asking the Board to deny the application outright, and the Board deferred those motions to a later decision. Every month of delay pushes out the $2.75B annual synergy figure the companies claim and extends the period during which Union Pacific's own capital allocation is constrained.

2. Volume and fuel are not in management's control. Carloads grew 1% in FY2025 and 6% in Q3 2026, with Bulk flat and the growth coming from Premium (+9%) and Industrial (+6%). Freight revenue rose 15% in Q3 but only 6% excluding fuel surcharge, so roughly nine of the fifteen points came from a diesel-linked pass-through. Union Pacific's fuel bill was $1.040B in the quarter against $616M a year earlier; on the call the CFO said the price per gallon rose from $2.56 to $4.21, adding 140 basis points to the operating ratio, and that management expects about $4.85 a gallon in the fourth quarter.

3. Trucking and tariff exposure in Premium. Intermodal is the one book where a determined competitor can take share quickly. Cheap diesel, loose truckload capacity, or a drop in West Coast import volumes all hit Premium directly; the group shrank 2% in FY2025 before growing 21% in Q3 2026. Premium is also where tariff policy transmits fastest, because international intermodal is a derivative of containerised imports through Los Angeles/Long Beach, Oakland, Seattle and Tacoma.

4. Balance sheet and buyback dependence. At September 30, 2026 Union Pacific carried $30.32B of total debt against $2.21B of cash (Q3 release), a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.41 and a current ratio of 1.16 (stockanalysis.com). Much of that leverage exists because the company retired shares aggressively for a decade — $2,679M of buybacks in the first nine months of 2025 alone. That has stopped: only $26M of repurchases in the first nine months of 2026, while the share count contribution to EPS growth is now near zero as long as the merger is pending.

NOTABLE ONE-OFF ITEMS

Reported GAAP results carry several material non-recurring distortions that materially change the trend line:

Industrial-park land sales (FY2025): +$234M pre-tax in Q4, +$0.32 to full-year EPS. The largest adjustment of the year, booked in other income rather than revenue. With the deferred tax item below, it is why reported FY2025 EPS of $11.98 (+8%) runs ahead of adjusted EPS of $11.66 (+5%). Because the gain sits below operating income, it does not touch the operating ratio: reported full-year OR was 59.8% (10 bps better than 2024) and adjusted OR 59.3% (60 bps better), the gap coming from merger costs and a crew-staffing agreement charge.

Merger-related costs: $30M in Q4 2025 (−$0.05 EPS), $35M in Q2 2026 (−$0.05) and $22M in Q3 2026 (−$0.04). Advisory, legal and regulatory expense tied to the Norfolk Southern transaction; the nine-month 2026 total is $93M ($0.16 per share). Q3 2026 reported EPS was $3.49 versus adjusted $3.53; reported OR 59.1% versus adjusted 58.8%. These costs will continue to accrue through the STB review and should be treated as a recurring cost, not a true one-off, until a decision lands.

Deferred tax adjustment (FY2025): +$115M to reported net income ($0.19 a share). A one-time tax item with no bearing on operations; adjusted results exclude it.

Buyback discontinuity. Not an accounting item, but the most important non-operating change in the numbers: repurchases fell from $2,679M in the first nine months of 2025 (also the full-year 2025 total) to $26M in the first nine months of 2026, while the Norfolk Southern consideration includes $88.82 in cash per share. Any model that extrapolates historical EPS growth without removing the buyback contribution will overstate 2026–2027 earnings.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

An irreplaceable ~32,900-mile western network throwing off 28.90% net margins, 16.54% ROIC and $6.69B of trailing free cash flow, trading at 23.2x trailing earnings against 25.7x for the S&P 500 column. Operations are demonstrably improving: Q3 2026 delivered a 58.8% adjusted operating ratio (59.1% reported) while carloads grew 6%, with terminal dwell at 19.9 hours and workforce productivity up 5%; freight revenue rose 15% and adjusted EPS 15%. On the call, CEO Jim Vena said the core operating ratio, excluding fuel price and merger costs, improved 110 basis points from a year earlier. On top of that sits optionality: STB approval of the Norfolk Southern deal would create the first US transcontinental single-line railroad — 50,000+ route miles, 43 states, ~100 ports on the companies' figures — with $2.75B of claimed annual synergies. If the deal fails for lack of regulatory approval, Union Pacific owes a $2.5B termination fee in certain circumstances, but it would no longer need cash for the deal’s $88.82-per-share cash component.

Bear Case

Strip the fuel surcharge out and Q3 2026 freight revenue grew 6%, not the 15% reported, with about nine of those points a diesel-linked pass-through; fuel cost $1.040B in the quarter against $616M a year earlier and added 140 basis points to the operating ratio. Bulk carloads were flat, and the buyback that supplied a large part of historical EPS growth has been switched off ($26M in the first nine months of 2026 versus $2,679M a year ago) with $30.32B of debt behind it. The 2026 capital plan was raised to $3.5B. The STB review runs into 2027 — final briefs are due May 28, 2027 and the Board then has 90 days after it closes the record — against motions from BNSF and CSX to deny the application, and the CFO who has run the finance side since 2020 retires in early 2027 with no successor named. A 2.50 PEG and a price 6.0% below the 52-week high mean much of the good outcome is already in the price; the bad outcome is not.

TOP 3 RISKS
01
STB rejection or value-destroying conditions. This is the dominant risk and it is binary and non-diversifiable. The Board accepted the revised application only with a requirement for supplemental information, held it in abeyance, and then adopted a schedule that runs through final briefs on May 28, 2027, with a decision within 90 days after the record closes. Conditions such as forced trackage rights (letting other railroads run trains over the combined network), divestitures or open-gateway requirements could preserve the deal while destroying much of the $2.75B synergy claim. Failing to win approval is costly too: Union Pacific owes Norfolk Southern a $2.5B termination fee in certain circumstances where regulatory approval is not obtained.
02
Volume, fuel and industrial-economy exposure. Carloads grew 1% in FY2025 and 6% in Q3 2026, with Bulk flat. Premium — 28.7% of FY2025 revenue — is tied to containerised imports through West Coast ports, making it the fastest channel for tariff and trade-flow shocks. Management expects diesel around $4.85 a gallon in Q4; a fuel-price decline would remove the surcharge revenue that flattered 2026 and improve trucking's competitive position against intermodal.
03
Financial flexibility and management change during the review. $30.32B of total debt against $2.21B of cash, a 1.41 debt-to-equity ratio and a suspended buyback while $2.48B went out in dividends in the first nine months of 2026. The cash component of the NS consideration is $88.82 per NS share, a multi-billion-dollar obligation that must be funded on top of a $3.5B capital plan for 2026. If credit conditions tighten before a decision, the company has limited slack. CFO Jennifer Hamann retires in early 2027 and the company has not named a successor, which puts a new finance chief in place mid-review.
  Industry Commentary
Rail Consolidation Meets a Regulator Applying Tougher Merger Rules
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The UP–NS transaction is the first merger to be judged under the Surface Transportation Board's tougher 2001 review rules. The process so far: the Board accepted the revised application on May 28, 2026 but placed it in abeyance (paused it) pending supplemental information, which the companies filed in July. On August 18, 2026 it lifted the abeyance and adopted a schedule: notices of intent to participate by September 4, comments, protests and requests for conditions by November 18, 2026, responses by February 16, 2027, a public hearing after March 29, 2027 on a date to be set, and final briefs by May 28, 2027. The Board sets the date the record closes separately and decides within 90 days after that. BNSF, CSX and shipper groups had filed motions asking the Board to deny the application outright, and the Board deferred those motions to a later decision. On the Q3 call management said it had asked the STB, for the second time, to allow ex parte communications (direct contact with the Board outside the formal filings) about the application.

Freight Recession Versus Recovery — and How Much of the Recovery Is Just Diesel
RECOVERY, PART DIESEL

Union Pacific's own numbers show how the recovery has built. FY2025: revenue +1.1%, carloads +1%, freight revenue ex-fuel +3%. Q3 2026: freight revenue +15% and carloads 2,293K (+6%), but ex-fuel-surcharge freight revenue grew 6%, meaning roughly nine of the fifteen percentage points came from a diesel-indexed pass-through that broadly offsets the company's own fuel bill ($1.040B in the quarter against $616M a year earlier). Underneath, the mix is uneven: Premium carloads +9% and revenue +21%, Industrial carloads +6%, Bulk carloads flat. Management affirmed high-single-digit reported EPS growth for 2026 and said it expects diesel to average about $4.85 a gallon in the fourth quarter. The network is improving: freight car velocity of 228 daily miles per car (+1%), terminal dwell of 19.9 hours (−2%) and workforce productivity of 1,219 car miles per employee (+5%). Service improvement is what converts pricing power into operating-ratio gains — and at $26.4B of trailing revenue, each 100 basis points of OR is worth roughly $264M of operating income.

Tariffs, West Coast Ports and the Intermodal Swing Factor — Bull Versus Bear
EMERGING TAILWIND

Premium is where trade policy meets the income statement. International intermodal originates as containers landing at Los Angeles/Long Beach, Oakland, Seattle and Tacoma and moving inland on UP and BNSF; UNP fell 8.7% in April 2025 ($215.66) as tariffs escalated, and Premium revenue fell 2% for FY2025. The 2026 reversal has been sharp — Premium revenue +21% in both Q2 and Q3, with carloads +4% then +9% — but the driver is disputable between restocking, tariff front-running and genuine truck-to-rail conversion. Management said on the Q3 call that it expects double-digit growth in international intermodal in the fourth quarter as it compares against last year's tariff volatility.

Bull framing: a merged UP–NS network promises 24–48 hours of transit-time savings from single-line service across ~100 ports and 43 states — precisely the service improvement that could win freight from highways. The companies’ July 2025 merger presentation splits the $2.75B synergy target into $1.75B of revenue synergies and $1.0B of cost synergies, and names the Ohio Valley and the Mississippi River “watershed” as underserved areas (company claims).

Bear framing: those synergy figures are company claims that have not been tested in the STB record, they assume a merger the Board will not rule on until after final briefs on May 28, 2027, and Premium's $1,694 FY2025 average revenue per car is 44% of Industrial's $3,840 — meaning intermodal growth dilutes yield even when it works. If diesel falls, both the surcharge revenue and the intermodal cost advantage over trucking shrink at the same time.
