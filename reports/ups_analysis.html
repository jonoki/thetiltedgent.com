01 Company Overview

United Parcel Service is the largest package delivery company in the world by revenue, moving parcels, freight and healthcare shipments through an integrated air-and-ground network across more than 200 countries and territories. Full-year 2025 revenue was $88.7 billion, down 2.65% from $91.07 billion in 2024 and down roughly 12% from the 2022 pandemic peak of $100.3 billion — the third consecutive year of flat-to-negative top line. UPS ranks approximately #281 globally and #188 among US-listed companies by market capitalisation, and captured roughly 34% of US parcel industry revenue ($69.8B in 2024) on only ~21% of volume, a spread that is the entire point of the current strategy.

The company is in the middle of the largest network restructuring in its history. Since the start of 2025 it has removed roughly 78,000 positions, closed about 150 buildings, taken out ~50 million operational hours and eliminated approximately $4.5 billion of expense — all while deliberately shedding around 2 million Amazon packages per day. Management declared that "glide-down" complete on the 2Q 2026 call (July 28, 2026) and raised full-year guidance to ~$91.2B revenue, ~$8.65B adjusted operating profit and ~$7.22 adjusted EPS.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of moat:
INTEGRATED AIR + GROUND NETWORK US RESIDENTIAL DENSITY ~460,000 EMPLOYEES ~$3B/YR CAPEX & AUTOMATION HEALTHCARE COLD CHAIN — 17M SQ FT B2B CONTRACT BOOK & SLAs CUSTOMS / BROKERAGE SCALE

UPS's advantage is a single integrated network that carries residential, commercial, freight and healthcare volume over the same assets — the reason it can convert ~21% of US parcel volume into ~34% of US parcel revenue. Density is the mechanism: each additional stop on an existing route is close to pure contribution margin, which is why the company can afford to give away 2 million low-yield Amazon packages a day and still expand U.S. Domestic adjusted margin by 100 basis points. The moat is reinforced by capital intensity (~$3.0B of capex per year), by regulatory and customs infrastructure that a new entrant cannot replicate quickly, and by the healthcare franchise, where cGMP/GDP-certified space, validated cold-chain lanes and pharma qualification cycles create genuine switching costs. That last piece is where UPS is deliberately reallocating capital — Andlauer at ≈$1.6B was the largest expression of it.

Key vulnerabilities:

1. Customer concentration inverting into competition. Amazon was over 13% of UPS volume at the pandemic peak and is now roughly 9%. But Amazon Logistics delivered 6.7 billion US parcels in 2025 (+9.8%), overtaking USPS to become the largest US parcel carrier — and it is increasingly selling third-party shipping. The Big Three (USPS, UPS, FedEx) now handle about 61% of ~23.9 billion annual US parcels, down from ~85% pre-pandemic. UPS chose to exit low-margin Amazon work; Amazon did not stop growing.

2. A unionised cost base its largest competitors do not carry. The Teamsters national agreement runs through July 31, 2028 and delivers $7.50/hour of increases to seniority employees over 2023–2028, plus cost-of-living escalators — Teamsters who completed progression received a $1.22/hour raise on August 1, 2026, including a $0.22 COLA tied to 4.4% May-to-May inflation. Part-time starting pay is $21/hour rising to $23/hour after August 1, 2027, and UPS committed to at least 22,500 permanent full-time openings and 28,000 replacement vehicles. Amazon Logistics' delivery-service-partner model and USPS carry structurally different labour economics.

3. Dividend coverage. The declared annual dividend of $6.56 exceeds TTM GAAP EPS of $5.38, a payout ratio of 121.95%, and roughly equals TTM free cash flow per share of $6.41. Guided 2026 dividends of ~$5.4B against ~$5.5B of guided free cash flow leaves essentially no cushion after ~$3.0B of capex. This is the core of the value-versus-value-trap argument.

4. Regulatory and trade-policy exposure. The elimination of the de minimis duty exemption re-routed a large slice of Asia-origin e-commerce; tariffs continue to reshape lane mix. UPS is currently positioning this as a September tailwind, but the same mechanism can reverse. UPS reportedly also carries a US Postal Service air-cargo agreement won from FedEx — this specific contract could not be verified against a live source in this build and should be confirmed before it is relied on.

Notable One-Off Items — the gap between GAAP and adjusted results is unusually wide right now, and reported earnings should not be taken at face value.

2Q 2026 transformation charge. UPS booked $1,172 million pre-tax / $891 million after-tax ($1.05 per diluted share) under "Network Reconfiguration & Efficiency Reimagined," principally employee separation costs from the completed Driver Choice Program (a voluntary driver buyout capped at 7,500 participants after union negotiation). This drove U.S. Domestic GAAP operating profit to $16M against $1,188M adjusted, and consolidated GAAP EPS to $0.71 against $1.76 adjusted.

Full-year 2026 charge guidance. Management expects $1.3–$1.5 billion of transformation costs for 2026, of which roughly $1.1 billion relates to the Driver Choice Program. Cumulative program cost through June 2026 was $1.8 billion ($1.2 billion incurred in 2026).

FY2025 items. A $182 million pre-tax ($137 million after-tax, $0.16/share) impairment on retirement of the MD-11 aircraft fleet; total transformation costs of $593 million ($0.53/share after tax — Transformation 2.0 $37M, Fit to Serve $47M, Network Reconfiguration $509M); and a +$109 million ($0.13/share) benefit from reversal of an income-tax valuation allowance.

M&A and divestitures. The Andlauer Healthcare Group acquisition closed November 3, 2025 for approximately US$1.6 billion, adding inorganic revenue to Supply Chain Solutions and healthcare from 4Q 2025 onward — 2026 segment growth rates are therefore not fully organic. UPS's earlier divestiture of the Coyote Logistics truckload brokerage is frequently cited as a driver of the Supply Chain Solutions revenue decline (4Q 2025 revenue −12.7%); the transaction value and closing date were not verified against a live source in this build. No goodwill impairment or pension mark-to-market charge was identified in the 2025 or 2026 releases reviewed here — pension remeasurement has historically been a recurring 4Q distortion for UPS, so the upcoming 4Q 2026 report warrants attention.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

The hard part is done. UPS removed ~2 million low-margin Amazon packages a day, ~78,000 positions, ~150 buildings, ~50 million hours and ~$4.5 billion of cost — and came out with U.S. Domestic adjusted margin up 100bp to 8.0% on revenue per piece +9.3% against cost per piece +4.9%. Guidance was raised to ~$91.2B / ~$8.65B / ~$7.22, International is compounding at 12.5% with an 18.9% yield gain and a September de minimis tailwind ahead, and healthcare is a $12B-run-rate franchise reinforced by the $1.6B Andlauer deal. At a 13.22 forward P/E — 5.3 turns below FedEx and less than half the index — you are paid a 6.38% yield to wait for 2027 consensus EPS of $8.08.

Bear Case

The dividend costs more than the company earns. $6.56 per share against $5.38 TTM GAAP EPS is a 121.95% payout; $5.4B of dividends against ~$5.5B of guided free cash flow leaves nothing after $3.0B of capex, on a balance sheet already at 1.90 debt-to-equity. Meanwhile net margin has fallen from 13.25% (2021) to 5.08%, revenue is still negative on a TTM basis, volume is shrinking, and the customer UPS fired — Amazon Logistics — just became the largest US parcel carrier at 6.7 billion parcels and is taking third-party freight. The Big Three's share of US parcels has fallen from ~85% to 61%. Teamsters escalators run to 2028 regardless. A yield near 6.4% on a 122% payout is not a value signal; it is the market pricing a cut.

Top 3 Risks
01
Dividend rebasing. With a 121.95% payout ratio, FCF per share of $6.41 against a $6.56 dividend, and an income-dominated shareholder base (60.26% institutional, heavily indexed and yield-mandated), a cut would trigger forced selling well beyond the arithmetic of the reduced payout. The board's next declaration is the single highest-variance event in the story.
02
The yield gains prove to be a mix artefact. Revenue per piece rose 9.3% in 2Q 2026 largely because two million of the cheapest packages left the network. Once the Amazon base laps, the comparison resets and the underlying question — can UPS raise price on the volume it kept, against a 24%-share Amazon Logistics and a growing pool of alternative carriers up 13% — has not yet been answered. Volume is still declining ~3.3%.
03
Operating leverage cuts both ways in a stripped-down network. After ~150 building closures and 50 million hours removed, UPS has far less capacity slack. Management's Q4 plan assumes a 24% sequential volume lift with rational pricing; a soft peak, a tariff shock, or an unexpected volume surge into a thinned network would all damage margin, and $1.3–$1.5B of 2026 transformation charges are still flowing through GAAP results while savings arrive on a lag ($1.2B realised in H1 versus ~$3B targeted for the year).
  Industry Commentary
Parcel volume and pricing after the e-commerce normalisation
ONGOING HEADWIND
The US parcel market grew to 22.4 billion shipments and $203.2 billion of revenue in 2024 — volume up just 3.4% and revenue up 2.7%, with revenue per parcel falling to $9.09 from $9.10. Pitney Bowes projects ~5% annual volume growth to 30.5 billion shipments by 2030 (+36%), which is healthy but nothing like the pandemic step-function. The structural problem is share: the Big Three now handle roughly 61% of ~23.9 billion annual US parcels, down from ~85% pre-pandemic, while alternative and regional carriers grew 13% in 2025 to 2.6 billion parcels. A market growing at 5% with price flat and share leaking to lower-cost entrants is a market where incumbents must earn margin from cost, not from volume — which is exactly the bet UPS has made.
Tariffs and cross-border e-commerce flows
EMERGING TAILWIND
The elimination of the de minimis exemption removed the duty-free treatment that made ultra-low-value Asia-to-US parcels economic, gutting a high-volume, low-yield lane. UPS is now on the other side of that shock: China-to-US lanes returned to growth in May 2026, Asia-to-Asia volume grew 13.6%, International revenue rose 12.5% to $5.04B with revenue per piece up 18.9%, and CFO Brian Dykes has guided that the September wrap-around will be "a significant year-over-year tailwind." The catch is that this is policy-derived, not demand-derived — March 2026's −15.2% month showed how fast trade headlines re-price the stock. International is 21% of revenue but earned a 15.8% adjusted margin in FY2025, roughly double U.S. Domestic, so the lane mix matters far more to profit than to revenue.
Labour cost structure versus non-union competitors
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
UPS employs roughly 460,000 people under a Teamsters national agreement running to July 31, 2028: $7.50/hour of increases across 2023–2028, a $1.22/hour raise on August 1, 2026 including a $0.22 COLA, part-time entry at $21/hour rising to $23/hour after August 2027, plus commitments to 22,500 permanent full-time openings and 28,000 replacement vehicles. Amazon Logistics' delivery-service-partner network and the alternative carriers taking share operate on materially different labour economics. Even the buyout mechanism was constrained — the Driver Choice Program had to be capped at 7,500 participants after union negotiation, and still cost ~$1.1B of the $1.3–$1.5B of 2026 transformation charges.

Bull vs bear framing: the bull argues UPS has already absorbed the worst of it — $4.5B of cost removed, 78,000 positions gone, U.S. Domestic adjusted margin up 100bp to 8.0% despite the escalators, and a 13.22 forward P/E that assumes none of it sticks. The bear argues that a fixed, contractually rising cost base against declining volume is precisely the definition of negative operating leverage, that the 121.95% payout ratio removes the balance-sheet cushion needed to absorb it, and that every dollar of savings from here is harder than the last because the easy 150 buildings are already closed.
