01 Company Overview

Vale is a Brazilian miner that describes itself as a leader in high-quality iron ore — the raw material for steel — with a significant position in copper and nickel. It runs two businesses: Iron Ore Solutions (fines, pellets and the railways and ports that move them, centred on the Carajás mines in northern Brazil and the Minas Gerais complexes) and Vale Base Metals (copper and nickel in Brazil and Canada, 90% owned since Manara Minerals took 10% in April 2024). Its common shares trade on B3's Novo Mercado as VALE3; each New York ADS represents one common share. Over the twelve months to September 30, 2026 Vale booked $41.42B of revenue and $16.30B of proforma EBITDA, both in US dollars, its reporting currency.

The headline earnings number tells a misleading story. Reported net income attributable to shareholders over those twelve months was only $0.68B ($0.16 a share), because the fourth quarter of 2025 carried a $3.5B impairment on the Canadian nickel assets after a cut in long-term nickel price assumptions and a $2.8B write-off of deferred tax assets. Strip those and the other one-offs out and Vale's own proforma net income was $6.18B. That gap — about 71x reported earnings versus about 7.8x proforma earnings — is the first thing to understand about the stock. The second is the dams. The Fundão dam at Samarco, the Vale–BHP joint venture, failed at Mariana on November 5, 2015, and Vale's own B1 dam at Brumadinho collapsed on January 25, 2019. Both still draw cash: $553M in the third quarter of 2026 alone. The third is the commodity mix. In 3Q26 iron ore made 73% of revenue, but its EBITDA fell 34% on a 10% lower realised fines price and higher freight and fuel costs, while copper's realised price rose 56% year over year and base metals EBITDA rose 61%, to 31% of the group's proforma EBITDA. The share of profit that does not depend on Chinese steel demand is rising. Most of the stock's fall since the last edition came before the results: the ADS lost 15.1% in October as iron ore miners fell (Rio Tinto's ADR −13.0%) and Brazilian markets sold off after the October 25 presidential runoff. With the results Vale kept its 2026 guidance ranges, pointing iron ore to the lower end and copper and nickel to the upper half.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Carajás ore body (S11D) Owned railway + port Pellet & premium-product capacity By-product-funded copper Scale: 88.4 Mt a quarter Low leverage (0.8x net debt/EBITDA)

Mining moats are geological and logistical. Vale's Northern System combines three mining complexes (Serra Norte, Serra Sul and Serra Leste), the Carajás Railroad and the Ponta da Madeira maritime terminal. Ore, rail and port are all owned, so the whole chain from pit to ship is under one operator, and nobody can build a second Carajás. The Ligga deal shows the other side of owning the railway: third-party ore from the region will ship on Vale's Carajás Railroad, and Vale will buy all of Ligga's sinter feed. The copper business has a second, quieter moat: by-product credits kept its all-in cost below zero in both 2Q26 and 3Q26. In a down-cycle, the lowest-cost producer keeps earning after its rivals stop. The limit of the moat is that Vale sets none of its prices. Iron ore, copper and nickel all trade on global benchmarks, and the real, which drives most of Vale's costs, floats.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
One commodity still pays most of the bills. Iron Ore Solutions was 73% of 3Q26 revenue and $2,633M of the $3,508M proforma EBITDA. Every dollar a tonne on the realised fines price moves annual revenue by roughly the volume sold: about $0.3B on 2026 production guidance of 335–345 Mt (our arithmetic: volume × $1).
02
The dams are a liability that keeps drawing cash. Payments for Brumadinho and the decharacterization (decommissioning) of upstream dams reached $15.6B from 2019 to 3Q26, with $3.7B of provisions left at September 30, 2026. The Mariana definitive agreement of October 25, 2024 totals about R$170B (US$30.4B) across Samarco, Vale and BHP. Vale's provision for its share was R$14.5B (US$2.6B) at the end of 2025, including a UK lawsuit for which it added $449M in 4Q25. Each new estimate update can bring another charge.
03
A strong real raises costs. Revenue is in dollars; much of the cost base is in reais. The real's strength took $91M from 3Q26 iron ore EBITDA year over year, and in July it forced the C1 cost guidance up from $20.0–21.5/t to $22.5–23.5/t. The currency turned after the October 25 election: USD/BRL was 5.4762 at the end of 2025, 5.2226 on October 2 and 5.4351 on November 2, 2026 (Yahoo Finance), so the real's gain for the year shrank from 4.9% to 0.8%. A weaker real lowers Vale's costs in dollars.
04
Reported earnings are volatile. Impairments, tax-asset write-offs and derivative revaluations moved attributable net income from +$2.69B in 3Q25 to −$3.84B in 4Q25. In 2Q26 derivatives marked to market took $798M off proforma net income year over year. Investors who screen on trailing P/E will see an expensive stock (about 71x); investors who use proforma earnings will see a cheap one (about 7.8x). In 3Q26 there were no non-recurring items, so the two measures matched.

Notable One-Off Items

Capital returns: the US$1.701B 1H26 payout, 52% of 1H26 attributable net income, was paid in September 2026. Vale bought back 13.75 million shares for $214M in the first nine months of 2026, and a new programme of up to 100 million shares over 18 months started in August 2026.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $12.37 to the November 2, 2026 close of $11.34, the ADS lost 8.3% on price in five years. Vale's dividends make the difference. On Yahoo's dividend-adjusted series the same window returned about +39%, because Vale paid out a large share of its cash through the 2022–2023 commodity upswing. The path between the endpoints swung widely. The stock peaked at $19.99 in March 2022, slid to a five-year low of $8.87 in December 2024, then climbed to $17.18 by February 2026 before losing a third of that level to reach today's price.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

On proforma earnings you pay about 7.8x, and about 4.0x proforma EBITDA including every dam provision in the debt. The business generated about $5.4B of recurring free cash flow in the last twelve months, about 11% of its market value, and leverage is 0.8x net debt to EBITDA. S11D's expansions are coming on: Serra Sul +20 started in July and Compact Crushing is being commissioned. Copper is the hidden asset: realised prices were up 56% in 3Q26, by-products keep all-in costs below zero, Bacaba is due in 3Q27 ahead of the original plan, the Salobo flotation expansion was pulled forward a year at lower capex, and management wants to double output by 2035. Base metals EBITDA rose 61% in 3Q26 to 31% of the group total. At today's price the two ordinary distributions of the past year come to about 7%, plus buybacks.

Bear Case

Three-quarters of revenue still rides on one commodity whose price Vale does not set, and in 3Q26 it went the wrong way: the realised fines price fell 10% while all-in costs rose 21% year over year, and iron ore EBITDA dropped 34%. Cost guidance went up mid-year. Brumadinho and dam decharacterization payments have reached $15.6B since 2019, and the Mariana provision keeps getting updated: $449M more in 4Q25 for the UK claim, with cash payments planned for years. Reported earnings swing on impairments and derivative marks; the nickel business has been written down $3.5B and earned only $98M of EBITDA in 3Q26. Brazilian assets sold off after the October 25 election on worries about public spending. The stock lost 8.3% on price over five years while the Ibovespa rose 65%, and 12 of 25 analysts sit at Hold, with the average target down to $15.44 from $16.70 a month earlier.

Top 3 Risks

01
Iron ore price. Iron Ore Solutions produced $2,633M of the $3,508M 3Q26 proforma EBITDA. Vale's realised fines price of $85.1/t sits about $25/t above its $60.4/t all-in cost, down from about $33/t in 2Q26, so a fall in the benchmark goes almost straight to EBITDA: lower prices alone took $673M from iron ore EBITDA against 3Q25. The September and October 2026 sell-offs across Vale, BHP and Rio Tinto show how closely the three trade together.
02
Dam liabilities and litigation. At September 30, 2026 Vale carried $1.6B of Brumadinho agreement provisions and $2.2B of Samarco provisions inside expanded net debt, plus $2.1B for dam decharacterization outside it. The Samarco provision now includes the UK lawsuit. Every revision to these estimates goes through the income statement and any acceleration in payments through cash flow. A separate dispute opened in August: Brazil's National Mining Agency has pressed mining royalty (CFEM) charges that Vale calls unfounded; the filing gave no amount.
03
Brazilian real and input costs. The real's strength took $91M from 3Q26 iron ore EBITDA year over year, and freight cost another $275M as bunker fuel rose. The Brent oil hedge and freight agreements returned a $97M gain in 3Q26, about $1.4 a tonne, outside reported costs. The real weakened after the October 25 election, which lowers Vale's costs in dollars; if it strengthens again, iron ore margins shrink even at steady prices.
Industry Commentary
Iron ore: softer prices, rising costs
SQUEEZED

The 61% Fe reference price Vale reports averaged $96.7/t in 3Q26, against $99.4/t a year earlier, but Vale's realised fines price fell 10% to $85.1/t as the quality premium on its fines narrowed to $1.6/t from $3.5/t; the all-in premium was $4.4/t, as in 2Q26. Fines sales held at 75.5 Mt. The squeeze is on both sides: against 3Q25, price took $673M off iron ore EBITDA, costs $331M, freight $275M and the stronger real $91M, and iron ore EBITDA fell 34% to $2,633M. Production missed too: 88.4 Mt against the 90.1 Mt Visible Alpha consensus Reuters cited, and Vale now expects the lower end of its 335–345 Mt guidance.

Copper and by-products: the profit shift
EMERGING TAILWIND

Vale Base Metals' EBITDA was $1,105M in 3Q26, up 61%, and it generated $484M of free cash flow on its own. Copper EBITDA rose to $1,021M from $614M a year earlier on a realised price of $15,314/t, up 56%, plus by-product credits from gold and other metals that kept copper's all-in cost at −$175/t. Nickel went the other way: its all-in cost rose 18% to $14,552/t and its EBITDA was $98M. On the call CEO Gustavo Pimenta said "The approval of the Salobo CPF project brings us closer to producing around 500,000 tonnes of copper in 2030." One caveat sits in the footnotes: at September 30 Vale had 79,016 t of copper provisionally priced at $14,445/t, so a fall in the copper price would reverse part of the gain through price adjustments.

The dam legacy: Brumadinho and Mariana
LONG-TAIL LIABILITY

Brumadinho: the B1 dam at the Córrego do Feijão mine collapsed on January 25, 2019; Vale's published list of identified deaths carries 272 names. The Integral Reparation Agreement signed on February 4, 2021 with the Minas Gerais government and public bodies was valued at R$37.69B; about 84% of its commitments were complete by 3Q26, its payment ("To Pay") obligations were completed in October 2026, and Vale expects to finish the programme in 2031. Mariana: the Fundão dam at Samarco, the Vale–BHP joint venture, failed on November 5, 2015. The October 25, 2024 definitive agreement totals about R$170B (US$30.4B) of past and future obligations. Samarco is the primary debtor, with each shareholder obliged to fund up to 50% of whatever Samarco cannot pay. R$83.8B had been disbursed by September 30, 2026, and the Renova Foundation that ran the earlier programme was dissolved in October 2025. By August 20, 2026, 45 of the 49 eligible municipalities had joined the agreement, each waiving its lawsuits over the collapse. For investors these are now scheduled cash outflows rather than open-ended unknowns. The UK lawsuit is the part that still moves.
