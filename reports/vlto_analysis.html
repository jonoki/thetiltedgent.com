01 Company Overview

Veralto is the part of Danaher that tests water and puts the date code on your yogurt. It was Danaher's Environmental & Applied Solutions segment until October 2, 2023, when Danaher shareholders received one Veralto share for every three Danaher shares and VLTO began regular-way trading on the NYSE — joining the S&P 500 the same morning, in place of DXC Technology. It sells water analytics, water treatment, marking and coding, and packaging and colour solutions through two segments: Water Quality and Product Quality & Innovation (PQI). Its operating brands include Hach, Trojan Technologies, ChemTreat, Sea-Bird Scientific, Videojet, Linx, Esko and X-Rite/Pantone. FY2025 sales were $5.50B, up 5.97%, with GAAP EPS of $3.76 and adjusted EPS of $3.90.

The pitch is that nothing here is optional. CEO Jennifer Honeycutt has put it plainly: roughly 80% of sales tie to water, food and essential goods. A municipal utility does not stop testing drinking water in a recession; a food plant cannot ship a product without a legible lot code. Much of the revenue is consumables and service — reagents, inks, chemicals, software — sold into an installed base of instruments and printers. That is the Danaher playbook, and it shows in the numbers: a 60.2% gross margin, a 22.8% operating margin, and free cash flow that runs at or above net income. The problem is that the market already knows all of this. At $95.18 the stock trades on 24.0x trailing earnings, is 15.3% below its August 2024 month-end high of $112.43, and has spent 2026 asking the only question a quality compounder ever gets asked: is mid-single-digit growth worth a premium multiple? In 2026 the answer went from "no" (a $82.23 May close, the lowest month-end since January 2024) back to "maybe" as core growth re-accelerated to 4.2% in Q2 and guidance went up twice.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base + consumables Regulated test methods Pantone colour standard Essential end markets (~80% of sales) Danaher operating heritage Low capex (~1% of sales)

The moat is the razor-and-blade model. A utility that has standardised on Hach analysers buys Hach reagents for as long as the instruments run; a bottler with Videojet printers on the line buys Videojet ink. A brand that specifies its red as a Pantone number is locked into Pantone's language. None of this is exciting, and that is the point: switching costs are operational, not contractual. The FY2025 numbers — 60% gross margin, capex of $56M on $5.7B of TTM sales, free cash flow above net income — are what that looks like on paper. The vulnerability is the flip side: a moat built on steady replacement demand grows at the speed of the installed base. Core growth has run between 1.6% and 4.7% over the periods fetched. To grow faster, Veralto has to buy growth, and in 2026 it is doing exactly that.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth is slow and management knows it. FY2025 core growth was 4.7%; Q4 2025 slowed to 1.6% and Q1 2026 to 1.9%. When the first 2026 guide came out in February ("low to mid single digits" core growth, $4.10–$4.20 adjusted EPS), the stock fell 5.76% after hours despite an earnings beat. The re-rating case needs the promised 5–6% second half to actually show up.
02
M&A is now the growth engine. Management calls M&A the "first call on capital" and has put roughly $1B to work in 2026 (In-Situ $435M, GlobalVision ~15x EBITDA, Alfaa UV). Bolt-ons at mid-teens EBITDA multiples add growth but dilute returns, and In-Situ was flagged at ~25bps of margin dilution. The Danaher playbook works when integration works; there is no public track record yet for Veralto doing it at this pace.
03
PQI rides consumer-goods capex. Printers and packaging software are bought when brands launch products and add lines. PQI's 2.0% Q2 core growth and its margin slipping from 25.7% to 25.2% in FY2025 are the signs of a customer base that is spending carefully.
04
The balance sheet is less clean than it was. Debt of $3.59B against cash of $2.12B leaves net debt of $1.47B, and debt-to-equity reads 1.16 because the spin left the company with a small equity base (book value $12.68/share). Leverage at 2.44x debt/EBITDA is manageable, but buybacks plus ~$1B of acquisitions in one year use up the cushion.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a Danaher-trained consumables business with 60% gross margins, 23.5% ROIC and free cash flow above net income at 24x trailing and ~21.6x this year's consensus earnings. Core growth re-accelerated from 1.6% to 4.2% in two quarters, guidance has been raised twice in 2026, and management is guiding 5–6% core growth for the second half. Buybacks are shrinking the share count, and acquisitions add a couple of points of growth on top. Consensus has EPS up 12.85% this year and 8.4% next. If the second half delivers, the stock re-rates toward the $114.79 average target. Even the lowest target on the Street ($100) is above today's price.

Bear Case

A 24x multiple for a business whose core growth was under 2% for two straight quarters is not cheap; it is a quality premium the business has to keep earning. The Q2 beat included ~$0.05 of tariff refunds that will not repeat, and the stock gave back the whole post-earnings pop within three days. PQI grew 2.0% and its margin fell in FY2025. Growth is increasingly bought — roughly $1B of 2026 acquisitions at mid-teens EBITDA multiples — and net debt is now $1.47B. If the 5–6% second half turns out to be 3–4%, the market has already shown it will take the stock back to the low $80s.

Top 3 Risks

01
The second-half acceleration does not arrive. Management has put a number on it: 5–6% core growth in H2 2026, 4.0–5.0% in Q3. A miss against a stated number is punished harder than a vague guide.
02
Acquisition integration and return dilution. Several deals at once — In-Situ, GlobalVision, Alfaa UV — at ~15x EBITDA against a 23.5% ROIC business. The Danaher operating heritage is the reason to trust it; a short public track record is the reason to check.
03
Multiple compression on a slow grower. At 24x trailing earnings the stock depends on the market keeping its quality premium. The move from $107.90 to $80.03 inside the last 52 weeks happened with no earnings miss.
Industry Commentary
Water is essential; water stocks are still stocks
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

Monitoring and treating water is regulated, non-discretionary spending, which is why Veralto's Water Quality segment grew core sales 5.7% in Q2 2026 while consumer-exposed PQI grew 2.0%. Data-centre cooling is a new, fast-growing but still small pocket for ChemTreat. None of that stopped the group from de-rating: Xylem's market cap is down 27.0% over the year and Veralto's 12.4%. Defensive revenue does not make a stock defensive when you pay a premium multiple for it.

Tariffs cut both ways
ONE-OFF NOISE

Veralto has been pricing to offset cost inflation — management described its pricing as "very surgical" and expected price at the high end of a 100–200bps range in 2026. In Q2 2026 the flow reversed: about $16M of IEEPA tariff refunds added roughly 110bps to margin. Management excluded further refunds from guidance, with potential upside of ~$0.02/share if more arrive (call summary). Read the Q2 margin with that refund stripped out.

The Danaher question
WHAT THE MULTIPLE PRICES

Every Danaher spin-off gets priced on the same assumption: that the operating system travels with it and that the new company can keep buying businesses and making them better. Veralto's 2026 is the first real test — three acquisitions, a $750M buyback and a margin target, all at once. The bull reading: this is the playbook working, and a 4.5% FCF yield on a business compounding EPS at low double digits is fair value, not a stretch. The bear reading: buying growth at 15x EBITDA to top up 2–4% organic growth is exactly how quality premiums erode slowly. The next two quarters of core growth, not the acquisition headlines, will settle it.
