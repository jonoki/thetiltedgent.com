01 Company Overview

Veralto is the part of Danaher that tests water and puts the date code on your yogurt. It was Danaher's Environmental & Applied Solutions segment until October 2, 2023, when Danaher shareholders received one Veralto share for every three Danaher shares and VLTO began regular-way trading on the NYSE — joining the S&P 500 the same morning, in place of DXC Technology. It sells water analytics, water treatment, marking and coding, and packaging and colour solutions through two segments: Water Quality and Product Quality & Innovation (PQI). Its operating brands include Hach, Trojan Technologies, ChemTreat, Sea-Bird Scientific, Videojet, Linx, Esko and X-Rite/Pantone. FY2025 sales were $5.50B, up 5.97%, with GAAP EPS of $3.76 and adjusted EPS of $3.90.

The pitch is that nothing here is optional. CEO Jennifer Honeycutt's 2025 letter to shareholders puts it plainly: about 80% of revenue is tied to essential end markets. A municipal utility does not stop testing drinking water in a recession; a food plant cannot ship a product without a legible lot code. Much of the revenue is consumables and service — reagents, inks, chemicals, software — sold into an installed base of instruments and printers. That is the Danaher playbook, and it shows in the numbers: a 60.2% gross margin, a 21.8% operating margin, and free cash flow above net income ($1,093M against $1,002M over the last twelve months). For the first half of 2026 the market doubted the growth: core sales growth had run under 2% for two quarters, and the stock closed May at $82.23, its lowest month-end since January 2024. Then growth picked up, to 4.2% in Q2 and 5.1% in Q3, and guidance went up three times. At $116.32 the stock trades on 28.7x trailing earnings and sits 3.5% above its August 2024 month-end high of $112.43. The question has changed from whether growth comes back to whether a business growing core sales around 5% is worth nearly 29 times its earnings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base + consumables Regulated test methods Pantone colour standard Essential end markets (~80% of sales) Danaher operating heritage Low capex (~1% of sales)

The moat is the razor-and-blade model. A utility that has standardised on Hach analysers buys Hach reagents for as long as the instruments run; a bottler with Videojet printers on the line buys Videojet ink. A brand that specifies its red as a Pantone number is locked into Pantone's language. None of this is exciting, and that is the point: switching costs are operational, not contractual. The numbers — a 60% gross margin, nine-month capex of $43M on $4.4B of sales, free cash flow above net income — are what that looks like on paper. The vulnerability is the flip side: a moat built on steady replacement demand grows at the speed of the installed base. Core growth has run between 1.6% and 5.1% over the periods shown. To grow faster, Veralto has to buy growth, and in 2026 it spent $1.1B on four acquisitions.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth is modest, and the price now assumes it lasts. FY2025 core growth was 4.7%; Q4 2025 slowed to 1.6% and Q1 2026 to 1.9% before recovering to 4.2% and 5.1%. When the first 2026 guide came out in February (core growth in the "low-to-mid-single digits", $4.10–$4.20 adjusted EPS), the stock slid despite an earnings beat: $97.43 at the end of February, $88.42 at the end of March. Q4 has to deliver the guided 5.5–6.5% for the full-year 4.2–4.5% to hold.
02
M&A is now the growth engine. Management puts acquisitions first among uses of cash and has spent $1.1B on four of them in 2026 (In-Situ $435M, GlobalVision at about 15x EBITDA, Alfaa UV, Cleanwater1 $465M at about 17x). Small acquisitions at mid-teens EBITDA multiples add growth but dilute returns: the CFO called Cleanwater1 "a little dilutive" to margin. The Danaher playbook works when integration works; there is no public track record yet for Veralto doing it at this pace.
03
PQI rides consumer-goods capex. Printers and packaging software are bought when brands launch products and add lines. PQI's core growth (2.0% in Q2, 3.2% in Q3) and its margin slipping from 25.7% to 25.2% in FY2025 are the signs of a customer base that is spending carefully.
04
The balance sheet is less clean than it was. Borrowings of $2.92B against cash of $1.79B left net debt of $1.14B at October 2, before the $465M Cleanwater1 purchase, which was paid with commercial paper and cash on hand (10-Q). Debt-to-equity is 0.90 because the spin left the company with a small equity base (book value about $13.29 a share). Debt at about 2.2 times trailing EBITDA is manageable, but $550M of buybacks plus $1.1B of acquisitions in one year use up the cushion.

Notable One-Off Items

Four things distort the 2026 figures. First, tax items: Q3 operating profit carries a $39M write-down of an indemnification asset after a tax audit settlement covering the years before the separation from Danaher; a matching cut in tax liabilities offsets it, so it lowers the operating margin but not net earnings. One-time tax items took Q3 income tax down to $20M from $67M a year earlier. Second, restructuring: the cost programme announced in April 2026 booked $22M in Q3 and $51M in nine months, of $85–105M expected by the end of 2028; 2026 savings are now guided at $10–15M. Third, acquisitions: they added 2.3 points to Q3 reported growth (7.5% reported against 5.1% core), and Cleanwater1 joins in Q4. Fourth, tariffs: refunds of tariffs paid under an emergency-powers law added about $0.05 a share to Q2 results (Q2 release), and on the Q3 call the CFO said the company "got a little bit of benefit from tariffs," which will not be there next year. Q3 GAAP EPS was $1.03 against adjusted $1.18. Diluted shares fell to 245.3M from 250.6M a year earlier, so buybacks are adding to per-share growth.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a Danaher-trained consumables business with 60% gross margins, a 23% return on invested capital and free cash flow above net income, at 28.7x trailing GAAP earnings and about 23.9x FY2027 consensus adjusted EPS. Core growth has risen three quarters in a row, from 1.6% in Q4 2025 to 5.1% in Q3 2026, guidance has been raised three times in 2026, and Q4 is guided to 5.5–6.5%. Buybacks cut the diluted share count by about 2% in a year, and acquisitions add about two points of growth on top. Consensus has adjusted EPS up 15.6% this year and 7.9% next (StockAnalysis). Every firm that moved its target after the print raised it; the average is $124.63.

Bear Case

At 28.7x trailing earnings for a business growing core sales around 5%, the price now assumes the acceleration lasts; after a 41.5% run from the May low, the stock sits only 7.1% below the average target. Q4's adjusted margin is guided roughly flat at about 24.5%, the CFO said Cleanwater1 may be "a little dilutive" to margins, and he said this year's tariff benefit will not be there next year. Growth is increasingly bought — $1.1B of 2026 acquisitions at 15–17x EBITDA — and $465M of that closed after the quarter, on commercial paper and cash. Four of the seven firms that moved after the print still rate it Hold, with targets 3–8% above the price. Earlier this year the market took the stock from $99.78 (December 2025) to $82.23 (May 2026) with no earnings miss.

Top 3 Risks

01
The acceleration stalls. Management has put numbers on it: 5.5–6.5% core growth in Q4 2026 and 4.2–4.5% for the year. After a 22% rise since September, a miss against a stated number would be punished harder than a vague guide.
02
Acquisition integration and return dilution. Four deals in one year — In-Situ, GlobalVision, Alfaa UV, Cleanwater1 — at roughly 15–17x EBITDA, bought by a business earning a 23% return on invested capital. The Danaher operating heritage is the reason to trust it; a short public track record is the reason to check.
03
A high price for a slow grower. At 28.7x trailing earnings the stock depends on investors continuing to pay up for quality. Within the last 52 weeks it has traded as low as $80.03 and as high as $117.05, with no earnings miss in between.
Industry Commentary
Water is essential; water stocks are still stocks
LONG-TERM TAILWIND

Monitoring and treating water is regulated spending that utilities and plants cannot skip, which is why Veralto's Water Quality segment grew core sales 6.4% in Q3 2026 while consumer-exposed PQI grew 3.2%. Data-centre cooling is a new pocket for ChemTreat, but management calls its data-centre sales small. The stocks have split: over the 52 weeks to November 3, Xylem's share price fell 36.2% while Ecolab's rose 18.8% (StockAnalysis) and Veralto's 17.6%. Defensive revenue does not make a stock defensive when you pay a premium price for it.

Tariffs cut both ways
ONE-OFF NOISE

In Q2 2026 Veralto got money back: refunds of tariffs it had paid under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, an emergency-powers law, added about $0.05 a share to that quarter's results (Q2 release). On the Q3 call the CFO said the company "got a little bit of benefit from tariffs" and that it will not be there next year. Read the 2026 margins with that in mind.

The Danaher question
WHAT THE MULTIPLE PRICES

Every Danaher spin-off gets priced on the same assumption: that the operating system travels with it and that the new company can keep buying businesses and making them better. Veralto's 2026 is the first real test — four acquisitions, a $750M buyback and a margin target, all at once. The bull reading: this is the playbook working: core growth rose from 1.6% to 5.1% while the deals were being done, and adjusted EPS is guided up 15–16% for the year. The bear reading: at a 3.9% free-cash-flow yield the price already assumes it works, and buying growth at 15–17x EBITDA to top up mid-single-digit growth from the existing businesses is how a quality premium slowly wears away. Q4's 5.5–6.5% guide and February's first look at 2027 will settle it.
