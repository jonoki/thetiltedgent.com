01 Company Overview

Vulcan sells rocks. Specifically, it produces and supplies construction aggregates in the United States — crushed stone, sand and gravel — plus the asphalt and ready-mixed concrete that consume them. In the twelve months to September 30, 2026 it shipped 227.6 million tons of aggregates on $8.01B of revenue, earning $1.10B of net income and $2.33B of adjusted EBITDA. FY2025 revenue was $7.94B, up 7.06%, with diluted EPS from continuing operations of $8.15, up 17.9% from $6.91.

The whole business rests on one physical fact: stone is heavy and cheap, so it cannot travel far before the truck costs more than the rock. A quarry is a local monopoly with a radius, and new quarries take years of permitting that neighbours fight. That is how Vulcan keeps raising price while volume stalls — freight-adjusted price (the price of the stone itself, before delivery charges) reached $22.84 a ton in Q3 2026, up 4% as reported and 5% mix-adjusted (after allowing for the mix of products and places sold) — while shipments fell 3%. The 2026 story is a tug-of-war between that pricing power and energy costs: diesel pushed cash cost per ton up 7% in Q3 (3% without it), single-family housing is held back by affordability and interest rates, and on October 29, 2026 Vulcan lowered its full-year adjusted EBITDA outlook to about $2.4B, the bottom of its earlier $2.4–2.6B range. The market took the quarter well — the shares rose 5.78% that day — because price kept rising and management said it expects another year of earnings growth in 2027. At $281.25 the stock is 15.0% above its $244.63 September month-end, set after Wells Fargo cut it to Underweight, and 9.3% below the $310.00 February month-end. The question is the same as before: can price keep outrunning cost while volume does not help?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Local quarry monopolies (freight radius) Permitting scarcity of new quarries Decades of reserves in place Pricing power through volume cycles Rail & yard logistics network Public-infrastructure demand base

The moat is geology plus geography. Because stone sells for about $23 a ton freight-adjusted and trucking eats that fast, a customer's real choice is between the two or three quarries within reach, and a new one must win zoning, environmental permits and a fight with neighbours. That is why aggregates pricing rose 4% in FY2025 on the reported line (6% mix-adjusted) while volume grew 3%, and rose again in Q3 2026 (+4% reported) while shipments fell 3%. The same logic shows up in the returns: Vulcan reports ROIC of 15.9% on its own definition for the trailing twelve months (16.5% a year earlier). The vulnerability is that the moat protects price, not volume or cost. When housing stalls, infrastructure money slows and diesel spikes at the same time, the price lever has to do all the work.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Price has to outrun cost, every year. In Q3 2026 price rose $0.82/ton (to $22.84 from $22.01) while cash cost rose $0.72 (to $10.89 from $10.17). The spread still widened, but by 11 cents. The release says energy inflation continued into the fourth quarter, and the full-year outlook is now at the bottom of the old range.
02
Volume has no cushion. Shipments fell 3% in Q3 and management expects only very slight growth for 2026. Housing is held back by affordability and interest rates, management said on the Q3 call, and Wells Fargo's September 9 downgrade cited slowing construction, uncertainty about federal infrastructure funding, tight state budgets and little lift from data centers; the bank kept its Underweight rating after the print.
03
Weather is a real variable. Management partly blamed Q3's lower shipments on favourable weather a year earlier, and heavy May–June rainfall in Texas and the Southeast held back Q2. Quarterly numbers in this business are partly a weather report.
04
The multiple leaves little room. At 33.3x trailing earnings and about 18x EV/EBITDA, the stock is priced for years of steady growth, while earnings per share were flat over the last year. When growth slows, the multiple usually falls faster than the earnings do.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the comparisons. First, portfolio changes: the Q4 2025 sale of the Houston asphalt/construction business and the Q2 2026 sale of California ready-mixed concrete cut reported Asphalt and Concrete revenue (Concrete revenue fell 61% in Q3) and brought in $572.1M of sale proceeds through September. Against that, Vulcan spent $290.6M on acquisitions in the first nine months, including two aggregates bolt-ons in Q3 that strengthened Tennessee and added southern Ohio. Reported revenue growth understates the aggregates business and overstates the decline downstream. Second, energy: management put the diesel and liquid-asphalt headwind at about $40M in Q3, which is why adjusted EBITDA fell to $707M from $735M even as its margin edged up to 32.3%. Third, adjusted versus GAAP: Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS from continuing operations was $2.81 against adjusted $2.83. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying a US aggregates franchise 9% below its February month-end whose prices keep rising through an energy shock. In Q3 aggregates price rose 4–5% while shipments fell 3%, and cash gross profit per ton still rose, to $11.95. Leverage is 2.0x total debt to adjusted EBITDA after about $750M of buybacks this year; the company is buying quarries and selling downstream businesses that earned 9–17% gross margins in Q3 against 33% in stone; and management expects mid- to high-single-digit price gains and another year of earnings growth in 2027. Consensus FY2027 EPS of $10.14 would put the stock at 27.7x — an estimate, not a result. If diesel eases, the cost line that hurt 2026 starts to help.

Bear Case

The bear case says the price lever is doing all the work. Cash cost per ton rose 7% in Q3 against a 4% reported price gain; shipments fell 3%; housing is stuck on affordability and rates; and the full-year EBITDA outlook was cut to the bottom of its range, about $2.4B. EPS over the last twelve months is flat ($8.44 against $8.47). At 33.3x trailing earnings and about 18x EV/EBITDA, after a 15% October bounce, the stock already prices in the 2027 growth management is promising. Wells Fargo still rates it Underweight with a $253 target; an energy spike or a delayed highway bill would hit the estimates and the multiple at once.

Top 3 Risks

01
Unit-margin stall. Energy-driven cost inflation (+7% per ton in Q3, +3% excluding diesel) took most of the price gain. The release says energy inflation continued into the fourth quarter, and the CFO expects about $120M of energy headwinds for 2026. If 2027 price gains land at the low end of management's range, unit margins stall again.

Industry Commentary
Energy inflation vs. aggregates pricing power
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Quarrying is diesel-heavy — haul trucks, loaders and crushers — so fuel is the cost line that moves fastest. In Q3 2026 management put the energy headwind (diesel and liquid asphalt) at about $40M, and adjusted EBITDA came in at $707M against $735M a year earlier. The industry's answer is always price: Vulcan has already announced January 1 increases for 2027 and keeps a mid-year option, per the Q3 call. The contest is simple arithmetic: +$0.82 per ton of price against +$0.72 of cost in Q3. When fuel eases the spread widens quickly; while it stays high, the spread barely moves.

Construction demand: public steady, private split
ONGOING HEADWIND

On the Q3 call management described public demand as steady: contract awards in its markets are up 2% over twelve months against 1% nationally, about 35% of the 2021 infrastructure law's money is still unspent, and it expects a new highway bill after the midterm elections. Data centers and large factories are the private-side bright spots; housing is held back by affordability and interest rates. Wells Fargo took the other side in September and kept Underweight after the print. Share prices across the group lagged the market over the past year: at October 30 closes CRH was down 22.5%, Martin Marietta 11.8%, Knife River 4.1% and Vulcan 3.0%, against an 11.2% gain for SPY.

Selling the side businesses — becoming a pure quarry company
EMERGING TAILWIND

Vulcan is steadily becoming more of a quarry company: out of Houston asphalt in Q4 2025, out of California ready-mix and U.S. Virgin Islands assets in 2026, into quarries and yards in Colorado, Texas, Tennessee and southern Ohio. The bull framing: concrete earned an 8.7% gross margin in Q3 and asphalt 16.8%, against 32.6% in stone, so group margins and returns rise as reported revenue shrinks, and a purer aggregates franchise deserves a premium multiple. The bear framing: purification does not change the cycle. A pure quarry company is more exposed, not less, to what happens to tons shipped when public budgets tighten. The way to judge it is cash gross profit per ton and shipments over the next four quarters, both reported every quarter.
