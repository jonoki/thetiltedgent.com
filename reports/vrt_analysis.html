01 Company Overview

Vertiv designs, manufactures and services the power and thermal-management equipment that keeps data centres alive: UPS (uninterruptible power supply — battery-backed systems that carry the load when utility power fails), busway (overhead pre-fabricated power distribution rails that feed racks without individual conduit runs), switchgear, power distribution units, precision air conditioning, and — increasingly — liquid cooling (piping coolant directly to the chip or the rack rather than blowing cold air at it). It is one of the most direct listed bets on AI data-centre spending that is not a chip company: it sells the physical plant that every accelerator rack must sit inside.

FY2025 net sales were $10,229.9M, up 27.7% year over year (26.3% organic), and the trailing-twelve-month figure through Q3 2026 is $13.12B, up 35.3%. Q3 2026 alone brought in $4,313M, up 61%. Management now guides FY2026 to $14.65B at the midpoint — organic growth of about 38% — and says it expects growth well above the market's again in 2027. At a $94.69B market capitalisation Vertiv ranks #117 in the S&P 500. The heritage is older than the AI story: the business began in 1946 as Ralph Liebert's precision-cooling shop in Columbus, Ohio, became Emerson Network Power, was bought by Platinum Equity in 2016, and reached the NYSE on 10 February 2020 through a SPAC merger (a listed shell company that buys a private one) with GS Acquisition Holdings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED BASE 5,000 FIELD ENGINEERS LIEBERT BRAND (1946) POWER + THERMAL UNDER ONE ROOF CAPACITY AHEAD OF DEMAND NVIDIA REFERENCE DESIGNS QUALIFICATION LOCK-IN

Vertiv's moat is not technological exclusivity — competitors can build a UPS — it is time. A hyperscaler committing to a campus needs equipment qualified, manufactured, delivered and commissioned on a schedule measured in quarters, and needs someone to service it for twenty years afterwards. Vertiv is one of a very small number of vendors that can supply the power train and the thermal train together, at multi-megawatt scale, with a global field-service organisation of more than 5,000 engineers attached. That combination is why the company can commit ~4% of sales to capacity expansion and expect it to be absorbed. The second, newer moat is co-design: on the Q3 2026 call Albertazzi highlighted Vertiv's work with NVIDIA, and being inside a chipmaker's reference design early means being specified by default when the next rack generation lands. The October 2026 purchase of UtilityInnovation Group adds a third leg: microgrid controls and on-site power for data centres that cannot wait for a grid connection.

What that moat does not do is protect price. This is an assembled-metal business with copper, steel, semiconductors and freight in the cost line; gross margin was 38.12% for the twelve months to September 2026, versus 36.32% in FY2025 and 30.47% in FY2021. That is a genuine and impressive repair, but it is manufacturing-margin repair — mix, price/cost discipline and volume — not a software gross margin. Q3 2026 gross margin was 38.1% against 37.8% a year earlier: the jump in operating margin came from spreading fixed costs over far more sales, not from pricing.

Customer concentration. Vertiv does not publish a named-customer concentration table in its earnings materials, but the shape of the order book can be read from the disclosure that does exist: Americas grew 41.9% in FY2025, Q4 2025 organic orders rose approximately 252% in a single quarter, and Q3 2026 organic orders more than doubled. Order growth of that size does not come from thousands of small buyers; it comes from a handful of very large campus commitments. On the Q3 call management said its customer mix — hyperscalers, enterprises, newer cloud providers — broadly mirrors the market's, but gave no shares. The practical exposure is that the cancellation, deferral or re-phasing of two or three programmes could move a year.

Disclosure quality has deteriorated, by choice. Through FY2025 Vertiv published backlog and book-to-bill every quarter: $8.5B and ~1.2x at Q2 2025, $9.5B and ~1.4x at Q3 2025, $15.0B and ~2.9x at Q4 2025. None of the three 2026 releases contains a backlog figure or a book-to-bill ratio. In Q3 the company offered one orders number, in words — organic orders up more than 100% year over year — and Albertazzi drew the line on the call: “We do not disclose orders. And we will continue not to.” Executive Chairman David Cote added that the company does not plan to share orders data going forward. Removing the single most forward-looking disclosure at the moment investors most want it is a choice, and it deserves to be treated as information in its own right.

Larger, better-capitalised competition. Eaton carries $30.03B of trailing revenue and a $152.9B market capitalisation against Vertiv's $13.12B and $94.69B; Schneider Electric is slightly larger still, at about $155.0B of market value on 29 October 2026 (562.3M shares from its June 2026 half-year report × the €247.60 Paris close × $1.113 per euro, the ECB reference rate; our calculation). Both are pushing hard into data-centre power and thermal, both have deeper balance sheets, and both can afford to cut prices to win market share while customers rush to secure equipment. Vertiv's advantage is focus and speed; it is not scale.

Tariffs and input costs. Physical goods crossing borders are directly exposed to trade policy in a way that a chip designer's intellectual property is not. Vertiv manufactures across a global footprint; changes in tariff regimes hit landed cost immediately and can only be recovered through price with a lag, which shows up as a margin timing problem even when it is eventually passed through. On the Q3 call management said it still expects price increases to outrun cost increases in 2026, tariffs included.

In plain English: RSI compares the size of recent up-months to recent down-months. Above 70 the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been sold hard and may be washed out. It says nothing about whether the business is good.

The five-year trajectory. From $25.68 at the end of October 2021 to $245.92 on 29 October 2026, VRT returned about +858% in price terms — roughly 9.6x — against +67.5% for SPY over the same dates. Almost none of it came in a straight line. The path has three distinct regimes. From late 2021 to mid-2022 the stock fell 68%, from $25.68 in October 2021 to a low month-end close of $8.22 in June 2022, as cost inflation on fixed-price contracts destroyed the margin structure. From mid-2023 the AI order cycle arrived and the stock climbed from $19.30 in May 2023 to $127.60 by November 2024 — a 6.6x move in eighteen months. And from March 2025's $72.20 trough it ran to $334.82 by June 2026, a further 4.6x, before the July 2026 revenue miss knocked it back to $241.57 in a single month. Since then it has gone sideways: $258.72 at the end of August, $241.31 at the end of September, a low close of $194.32 on 23 October and $245.92 after the Q3 print.

Read the 2022 chapter carefully, because it is the closest thing to a controlled experiment on this business. The demand was fine; the pricing and cost discipline were not. Gross margin was 28.39% in FY2022 and net income was $76.6M on $5.69B of sales. Over the twelve months to September 2026 gross margin is 38.12% and net income is $2.10B on $13.12B. The company has been rebuilt — but a company that once lost two-thirds of its value without a demand shock is a company where execution risk is not theoretical.

Where the price sits now. $245.92 is 66.4% above the 52-week low of $147.82 (17 December 2025) and 35.3% below the 52-week high of $379.93 (14 May 2026), both intraday. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Vertiv is the physical bottleneck of AI compute and it is not a chip company, so it does not carry chip-cycle obsolescence risk — a UPS installed in 2026 is still a UPS in 2041, and Vertiv services it. The Q3 2026 quarter answered the near-term worry: sales up 61%, every region growing, EMEA included, organic orders up more than 100%, and full-year guidance raised by $650M of sales. Margin repair is real (gross margin 30.47% → 38.12%; GAAP operating margin 5.20% → 20.14% since FY2021), the balance sheet holds about $0.8B of net cash, ROIC is 42.67% (StockAnalysis), and at 25.2x the FY2027 consensus EPS of $9.74 you are not paying a bubble price for a company guiding 38% organic growth this year. Liquid cooling and, now, on-site power through UtilityInnovation Group raise what Vertiv sells into each rack and each campus.

Bear Case

The company stopped telling you the number the thesis depends on, and now says it will not tell you again. Backlog, orders and book-to-bill were published every quarter through FY2025; in 2026 they have been replaced by a growth rate in words. That happened in a year in which the stock fell 17.3% in a day on a quarter that beat on earnings, then slid 23% in three weeks before a quarter that beat on everything. The multiple assumes the order book converts: 45.97x trailing GAAP earnings, 17.2x book value and a beta of 2.05 on an industrial manufacturer with copper and tariff exposure and a gross margin stuck near 38%. A handful of hyperscalers drive the orders; the circular-funding worry aimed at NVIDIA — data-centre spending justified by demand that the spending itself is meant to create — applies one layer down here, with the added twist that Vertiv books it as orders before anyone knows whether the racks get filled. And the UIG deal adds up to $1.15B of further cash payments on top of the $1.45B already paid.

01
Backlog durability and the risk of a sudden gap in orders. A 2.9x book-to-bill at Q4 2025 meant orders came in at nearly three times the rate of shipments, and Q3 2026 orders doubled again — that cannot persist, and the question is whether the ratio settles at 1.1x or 0.7x. Large data-centre commitments can be deferred or re-phased with limited penalty long before they are formally cancelled. If hyperscaler capital expenditure pauses, Vertiv's revenue does not fall the next quarter — it falls when the backlog runs out (on the Q3 call management said customers still order 12 to 18 months ahead), and by then the capacity built at ~4% of sales is standing idle. Without a published backlog this risk cannot be measured from the outside, which is not the same as it being absent.
02
Customer concentration inside an undisclosed order book. Vertiv publishes no named-customer concentration data. Order growth of 252% in one quarter and more than 100% in another is the signature of a few very large programmes, not broad demand. Concentration risk is bearable when you can see it; here you can see neither the customers nor, since Q1 2026, the dollar size of the order book.
03
Margin ceiling meeting a scaled competitive response. This is a manufacturer: gross margin is 38.12% and barely moved in Q3 (38.1% against 37.8%), so recent profit gains came from volume, not price. Eaton ($30.03B revenue, $152.9B market cap) and Schneider Electric are both larger and better capitalised and are both attacking data-centre power and thermal directly. While customers rush to secure equipment, bigger rivals can cut prices to win market share. Add tariff exposure on physically shipped goods and margin becomes the variable that absorbs the pressure — which, at 45.97x trailing earnings, is not a variable this stock has much room to disappoint on.
04
Acquisition pace. Six acquisitions since August 2025 — Great Lakes, Waylay, PurgeRite, ThermoKey, King Environmental Services and UtilityInnovation Group, the last the largest at about $1.45B up front plus up to $1.15B of earn-out. Each fills a real gap, but integration, earn-out charges in the GAAP numbers and acquired growth flattering the headline rate all rise with the count.
  Industry Commentary
Liquid cooling and on-site power: more content per rack and per campus
EMERGING TAILWIND
Rack density — the electrical power drawn by a single equipment rack, and therefore the heat it must shed — is the variable that governs Vertiv's revenue per square foot of data centre. A conventional enterprise rack draws single-digit kilowatts and can be cooled with moving air. An AI training rack draws an order of magnitude more, and past a certain point air physically cannot carry the heat away fast enough. The industry's answer is liquid cooling — running coolant through cold plates in direct contact with the chip, or through rear-door heat exchangers on the rack, with pumps, manifolds, coolant distribution units and heat-rejection equipment behind it. That is a far larger and more complex bill of materials than a computer-room air conditioner, and it is Vertiv's core competence rather than a side line.

Vertiv has bought toward this deliberately: CoolTera (coolant distribution), PurgeRite in December 2025 (fluid management, flushing and filling of coolant loops, and the associated services), ThermoKey in June 2026 (heat rejection and heat exchange) and King Environmental Services in October 2026 (the same fluid services in EMEA). UtilityInnovation Group, also completed in October 2026, extends the same logic to power: when the grid connection is the bottleneck, microgrid controls and on-site generation decide how soon a campus can switch on. In the Q3 release Albertazzi said Vertiv is “industrializing converged infrastructure at a scale that didn't exist before.”

The honest limitation: Vertiv does not disclose liquid-cooling revenue, thermal revenue, or content per rack. The transition is real and the strategic logic is sound, but anyone quoting a specific liquid-cooling revenue figure for Vertiv is not getting it from Vertiv's filings. It cannot be quantified from disclosure, and it is not quantified here.
The disappearing backlog disclosure
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
For a capital-equipment business, revenue is a report on the past. Orders, book-to-bill and backlog are the report on the future, and Vertiv used to publish all three. The series ran: Q2 2025 backlog $8.5B on a ~1.2x book-to-bill with organic orders +15% year over year; Q3 2025 backlog $9.5B on ~1.4x with organic orders +60%; Q4 2025 backlog $15.0B on ~2.9x with organic orders +252% and trailing-twelve-month organic orders +81%.

Then it stopped. The Q1 2026 release (net sales $2,650M, +23% organic), the Q2 2026 release (net sales $3,274M, +18% organic) and the Q3 2026 release (net sales $4,313M, +55% organic) contain no backlog figure and no book-to-bill ratio; "backlog" appears only inside the standard risk language. Q3 brought one concession — organic orders “more than 100%” up year over year, stated in words — and a firm line: Albertazzi said on the call that Vertiv does not disclose orders and will continue not to, and Executive Chairman David Cote said there are no plans to share orders data going forward. Asked about backlog, Albertazzi said Vertiv counts only firm purchase orders, not capacity agreements, and that orders are still placed 12 to 18 months out.

There are benign readings. Order lumpiness at a 2.9x book-to-bill makes any later quarter look like a slowdown, and a company may reasonably decline to hand the market a metric that will print awkward comparisons off an extreme base — and the Q3 numbers show the demand was real. There are less benign readings, and they are the obvious ones. What is not in dispute is the effect: the most valuable number in the disclosure package has been withdrawn for good, and no outside party can now check order momentum except through revenue, which lags it by a year or more.
Circular demand, one layer below the chips — how the bull and bear cases resolve
ONGOING HEADWIND
The circular-funding critique aimed at NVIDIA runs like this: AI capital expenditure is being funded on the expectation of demand that the capital expenditure itself is supposed to create, and each participant's spending validates the others' forecasts. Vertiv sits one layer down that same stack, and the structure has a specific consequence here. Because Vertiv books orders well ahead of shipment, its reported results lag any change in that expectation by a year or more. That cuts both ways. It means Vertiv would keep reporting excellent numbers well into a hyperscaler pause — which is comforting if you own it and dangerous if you use those numbers as evidence the pause is not happening.

The bull resolution: the demand is real, power and cooling remain the binding physical constraint on deployment regardless of which model or chip wins, EMEA keeps growing as it did in Q3, liquid cooling and on-site power raise content faster than unit growth slows, and the shares grow into a 34.39x forward multiple as FY2026 adjusted EPS lands near $7.15 and the FY2027 consensus of $9.74 comes into view. The analysts sit here: 30 of them, Moderate Buy on MarketBeat (Strong Buy on StockAnalysis), with an average target near $338.

The bear resolution: the 2025–26 order surge was customers ordering early — securing scarce long-lead power and thermal equipment ahead of a build cycle rather than adding to it — the backlog converts to revenue but is not replaced, book-to-bill drops below 1.0 sometime in 2027, and a 45.97x trailing / 17.2x book / 2.05-beta stock falls hard, as it did on 29 July 2026 on a quarter that beat on earnings. The two cases are not distinguishable from published financials in 2026. They are distinguishable from the order book — which is why the disclosure decision is the most important development of the year, and why every figure in this report is worth less than the number Vertiv is no longer printing.
