01 Company Overview

Vertiv designs, manufactures and services the power and thermal-management equipment that keeps data centres alive: UPS (uninterruptible power supply — battery-backed systems that carry the load when utility power fails), busway (overhead pre-fabricated power distribution rails that feed racks without individual conduit runs), switchgear, power distribution units, precision air conditioning, and — increasingly — liquid cooling (piping coolant directly to the chip or the rack rather than blowing cold air at it). It is the clearest listed pure-play on AI capital expenditure that is not a chip company: it sells the physical plant that every accelerator rack must sit inside.

FY2025 net sales were $10,229.9M, up 27.7% year over year (26.3% organic), and the trailing-twelve-month figure through Q2 2026 is $11.48B, up 26.2%. Management guides FY2026 to roughly $14.0B at the midpoint — organic growth of about 31%. At a $99.60B market capitalisation Vertiv ranks as the world's 246th most valuable company (companiesmarketcap, August 2026). The heritage is older than the AI story: the business began in 1946 as Ralph Liebert's precision-cooling shop in Columbus, Ohio, became Emerson Network Power, was bought by Platinum Equity in 2016, and reached the NYSE on 10 February 2020 through a SPAC merger with GS Acquisition Holdings.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INSTALLED BASE 5,000 FIELD ENGINEERS LIEBERT BRAND (1946) POWER + THERMAL UNDER ONE ROOF CAPACITY AHEAD OF DEMAND NVIDIA REFERENCE DESIGNS QUALIFICATION LOCK-IN

Vertiv's moat is not technological exclusivity — competitors can build a UPS — it is time. A hyperscaler committing to a campus needs equipment qualified, manufactured, delivered and commissioned on a schedule measured in quarters, and needs someone to service it for twenty years afterwards. Vertiv is one of a very small number of vendors that can supply the power train and the thermal train together, at multi-megawatt scale, with a global field-service organisation of roughly 5,000 engineers attached. That combination is why the company can commit ~4% of sales to capacity expansion and expect it to be absorbed. The second, newer moat is co-design: the Q2 2026 materials reference deployments alongside NVIDIA technology, and being inside a reference architecture early means being specified by default when the next rack generation lands.

What that moat does not do is protect price. This is an assembled-metal business with copper, steel, semiconductors and freight in the cost line; gross margin was 38.04% on a trailing basis versus 36.32% in FY2025 and 30.47% in FY2021. That is a genuine and impressive repair, but it is manufacturing-margin repair — mix, price/cost discipline and volume leverage — not a software gross margin, and the arithmetic gets harder as the base rises.

Customer concentration. Vertiv does not publish a named-customer concentration table in its earnings materials, and none was verified for this snapshot. But the shape of the order book is inferable from the disclosure that does exist: Americas grew 41.9% in FY2025 and Q4 2025 organic orders rose approximately 252% in a single quarter. Order growth of that magnitude does not come from thousands of small buyers; it comes from a handful of very large campus commitments. The practical exposure is that the cancellation, deferral or re-phasing of two or three programmes could move a year.

Disclosure quality has deteriorated. Through FY2025 Vertiv published backlog and book-to-bill every quarter: $8.5B and ~1.2x at Q2 2025, $9.5B and ~1.4x at Q3 2025, $15.0B and ~2.9x at Q4 2025. Neither the Q1 2026 nor the Q2 2026 earnings release contains a backlog figure, an orders figure or a book-to-bill ratio — the word "backlog" survives only inside the boilerplate risk factors. Management has substituted qualitative language ("strong pipeline momentum points to another year of expected robust orders growth"). Removing the single most forward-looking disclosure at the exact moment investors most want it is a choice, and it deserves to be treated as information in its own right.

Larger, better-capitalised competition. Eaton carries $30.03B of trailing revenue and a $156.1B market capitalisation against Vertiv's $11.48B and $99.60B; Schneider Electric is larger still. Both are pushing hard into data-centre power and thermal, both have deeper balance sheets, and both can afford to buy share in a land-grab phase. Vertiv's advantage is focus and speed; it is not scale.

Tariffs and input costs. Physical goods crossing borders are directly exposed to trade policy in a way that a fabless chip designer's IP is not. Vertiv manufactures in roughly 30 facilities across a global footprint including Mexicali; changes in tariff regimes hit landed cost immediately and can only be recovered through price with a lag, which shows up as a margin timing problem even when it is eventually passed through.

The five-year trajectory. From $24.09 at the end of September 2021 to $258.72 on 31 August 2026, VRT returned approximately +974% — roughly 10.7x — but almost none of it in a straight line. The path has three distinct regimes. From late 2021 through late 2022 the stock collapsed 66%, from $25.68 in October 2021 to a low month-end close of $8.22 in June 2022, as cost inflation on fixed-price contracts destroyed the margin structure. From mid-2023 the AI order cycle arrived and the stock compounded from $19.30 in May 2023 to $127.60 by November 2024 — a 6.6x move in eighteen months. And from March 2025's $72.20 trough it ran to $334.82 by June 2026, a further 4.6x, before the July 2026 revenue miss knocked it back to $241.57 in a single month.

Read the 2022 chapter carefully, because it is the closest thing to a controlled experiment on this business. The demand was fine; the pricing and cost discipline were not. Gross margin was 28.39% in FY2022 and net income was $76.6M on $5.69B of sales. Today gross margin is 38.04% and net income is $1.73B on $11.48B. The company has been rebuilt — but a company that once lost two-thirds of its value without a demand shock is a company where execution risk is not theoretical.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Vertiv is the physical bottleneck of AI compute and it is not a chip company, so it does not carry chip-cycle obsolescence risk — a UPS installed in 2026 is still a UPS in 2041, and Vertiv services it. The last published order data was extraordinary: $15.0B of backlog at Q4 2025, up 109%, on a 2.9x book-to-bill, against FY2025 revenue of $10.2B — roughly a year and a half of shipments already contracted. Margin repair is real and continuing (gross margin 30.47% → 38.04%; operating margin 5.47% → 19.96% since FY2021), the balance sheet is close to net-cash, ROIC is 37.58%, and at a 0.87 PEG with a 33.11x forward multiple you are not paying a bubble price for 26%+ growth. Liquid cooling is a content-per-rack step change that is only beginning.

Bear Case

The company stopped telling you the number the thesis depends on. Backlog, orders and book-to-bill were published every quarter through FY2025 and appear in neither the Q1 2026 nor the Q2 2026 release. That happened in the same year revenue missed consensus and the stock fell 17% intraday on a quarter that beat on earnings. Meanwhile the multiple assumes the order book converts: 58.53x trailing earnings, 20.8x book, and a beta of 2.08 on an industrial manufacturer with copper and tariff exposure and a 38% gross margin ceiling. A handful of hyperscalers drive the orders; the same reflexivity critique aimed at NVIDIA — capex funded by expectations of demand that the capex itself is meant to create — applies one layer down here, with the added twist that Vertiv books it as backlog before anyone knows whether the racks get filled.

01
Backlog durability and the air-pocket risk. A 2.9x book-to-bill means orders came in at nearly three times the rate of shipments — mathematically that cannot persist, and the question is whether it normalises to 1.1x or to 0.7x. Large data-centre commitments can be deferred or re-phased with limited penalty long before they are formally cancelled. If hyperscaler capital expenditure pauses, Vertiv's revenue does not fall the next quarter — it falls in six to eight quarters, when the backlog runs out, and by then the capacity that was built at ~4% of sales is standing idle. The removal of backlog disclosure makes this risk unmeasurable from the outside, which is not the same as it being absent.
02
Customer concentration inside an undisclosed order book. Vertiv publishes no named-customer concentration data. Order growth of 252% in a single quarter is arithmetically the signature of a few very large programmes, not broad demand. Concentration risk is bearable when you can see it; here you can see neither the customers nor, since Q1 2026, the aggregate.
03
Margin ceiling meeting a scaled competitive response. This is a manufacturer: gross margin is 38.04% and the drivers of further expansion — mix, price/cost, volume leverage — are largely spent. Eaton ($30.03B revenue, $156.1B market cap) and Schneider Electric are both larger and better capitalised and are both attacking data-centre power and thermal directly. In a land-grab phase, scale players can buy share on price. Add tariff exposure on physically shipped goods and margin becomes the variable that absorbs the pressure — which, at 58.53x trailing earnings, is not a variable this stock has much room to disappoint on.
  Industry Commentary
Liquid cooling: the content-per-rack step change
EMERGING TAILWIND
Rack density — the electrical power drawn by a single equipment rack, and therefore the heat it must shed — is the variable that governs Vertiv's revenue per square foot of data centre. A conventional enterprise rack draws single-digit kilowatts and can be cooled with moving air. An AI training rack draws an order of magnitude more, and past a certain point air physically cannot carry the heat away fast enough: the fans get louder, then they stop working. The industry's answer is liquid cooling — running coolant through cold plates in direct contact with the chip, or through rear-door heat exchangers on the rack, with pumps, manifolds, coolant distribution units and heat-rejection equipment behind it. That is a far larger and more complex bill of materials than a computer-room air conditioner, and it is Vertiv's core competence rather than an adjacency.

Vertiv has bought toward this deliberately: CoolTera in December 2023 (coolant distribution), PurgeRite in December 2025 (fluid management, flushing and filling of coolant loops, and the associated services), ThermoKey in March 2026 (heat rejection and heat exchange). The Q2 2026 materials reference deployments alongside NVIDIA technology and describe continued investment in "future power architectures, advanced thermal systems, services and converged infrastructure."

The honest limitation: Vertiv does not disclose liquid-cooling revenue, thermal revenue, or content per rack. The transition is real and the strategic logic is sound, but anyone quoting a specific liquid-cooling revenue figure for Vertiv is not getting it from Vertiv's filings. It cannot be quantified from disclosure, and it is not quantified here.
The disappearing backlog disclosure
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
For a capital-equipment business, revenue is a report on the past. Orders, book-to-bill and backlog are the report on the future, and Vertiv used to publish all three. The series ran: Q2 2025 backlog $8.5B on a ~1.2x book-to-bill with organic orders +15% year over year; Q3 2025 backlog $9.5B on ~1.4x with organic orders +60%; Q4 2025 backlog $15.0B on ~2.9x with organic orders +252% and trailing-twelve-month organic orders +81%.

Then it stopped. The Q1 2026 release (net sales $2,650M, +23% organic) and the Q2 2026 release (net sales $3,274M, +18% organic) contain no backlog figure, no orders figure and no book-to-bill ratio; "backlog" appears only inside the standard forward-looking-statements risk language. In their place is qualitative pipeline commentary — "strong pipeline momentum points to another year of expected robust orders growth."

There are benign readings. Order lumpiness at a 2.9x book-to-bill makes any subsequent quarter look like a deceleration, and a company may reasonably decline to hand the market a metric that will now print negative comparisons off an extreme base. There are less benign readings, and they are the obvious ones. What is not in dispute is the effect: the most valuable number in the disclosure package was withdrawn precisely when the stock's valuation became most dependent on it, and no external party can now verify order momentum until Vertiv chooses to disclose it again. Treat 21 October 2026 as the test.
Reflexivity, one layer below the chips — how the bull and bear cases resolve
ONGOING HEADWIND
The reflexivity critique aimed at NVIDIA runs like this: AI capital expenditure is being funded on the expectation of demand that the capital expenditure itself is supposed to create, and each participant's spending validates the others' forecasts. Vertiv sits one layer down that same stack, and the structure has a specific consequence here. Because Vertiv books orders into backlog years ahead of shipment, its reported results lag any change in that expectation by six to eight quarters. That cuts both ways. It means Vertiv would keep reporting excellent numbers well into a hyperscaler pause — which is comforting if you own it and dangerous if you use those numbers as evidence the pause is not happening.

The bull resolution: the demand is real, power and cooling remain the binding physical constraint on deployment regardless of which model or chip wins, EMEA inflects to mid-teens growth as guided, liquid cooling raises content per rack faster than unit growth slows, and the shares grow into a 33.11x forward multiple as FY2026 EPS lands near $6.70 and FY2027 consensus of $9.10 comes into view. Consensus sits here: 28 analysts, Strong Buy, $338.15 average target.

The bear resolution: the Q4 2025 order surge was a pull-forward — customers securing scarce long-lead power and thermal equipment ahead of a build cycle rather than adding to it — the backlog converts to revenue but is not replaced, book-to-bill drops below 1.0 sometime in 2027, and a 58.53x trailing / 20.8x book / 2.08-beta stock de-rates violently, exactly as it did in one day in July 2026 on a quarter that beat on earnings. The two cases are not distinguishable from published financials in 2026. They are distinguishable from the order book — which is why the disclosure change is the most important development of the year, and why every figure in this report is worth less than the number Vertiv is no longer printing.
