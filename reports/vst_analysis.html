01 Company Overview

Vistra Corp. is the largest US independent power producer (IPP — a generator that sells electricity into competitive wholesale markets rather than earning a regulated rate of return) paired with the country's largest competitive retail electricity business. The company owns roughly 44,000 MW (~44 GW) of generation across natural gas, nuclear, coal, solar and battery storage — enough, per the company, to power about 22 million homes — and sells power and gas to approximately 5 million residential, commercial and industrial retail customers under the TXU Energy, Dynegy, Ambit, Energy Harbor and Homefield Energy brands, "from California to Maine." Vistra generated $17.74B of FY2025 revenue, up +2.98% YoY, on $5,912M of Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA; trailing-twelve-month revenue is $19.21B (+3.8%). It ranks #249 among US companies and #537 globally by market capitalization.

The defining feature of the business is integration: Vistra owns generation and simultaneously owns the retail load that consumes it. That structure is not a diversification story — it is the hedge. It is examined in detail in Section 02. Layered on top since 2024 is the AI-power narrative: Vistra operates the second-largest competitive nuclear fleet in the United States, and has signed 20-year PPAs with Amazon Web Services (Comanche Peak, ERCOT) and Meta (Perry, Davis-Besse and Beaver Valley, PJM). The stock re-rated violently on that theme in 2024–2025 and has since given back roughly a third of its value.

This is the part of Vistra that most investors under-weight, and it is the core of the business model. Retail sells fixed-price electricity contracts to ~5 million customers; generation produces physical megawatt-hours. Standing alone, a retailer is short power (it owes fixed-price supply and must buy at market) and a generator is long power. Owned together, the two positions offset: the retail book is effectively a captive, high-margin, credit-worthy offtake for the fleet, and the fleet is a physical hedge for the retail book. That is why Retail delivered $1,622M in FY2025 — up 10.9% in a year when Texas generation EBITDA fell 9.7%. Retail margins expand when wholesale prices fall (supply cost drops faster than customer prices reset) and compress when they spike — precisely the inverse of generation. TXU Energy was the top-rated large retail energy provider in the Texas Public Utility Commission rankings in the FY2025 release. Q2 2026 Retail EBITDA of $773M was up only +2.2% YoY, a reminder that the hedge cuts both ways: in a quarter when generation EBITDA jumped ~68%, retail was flat.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INTEGRATED RETAIL + GEN HEDGE #2 COMPETITIVE NUCLEAR FLEET ~44 GW DISPATCHABLE SCALE INTERCONNECTED BROWNFIELD SITES TXU BRAND EQUITY 20-YR HYPERSCALER PPAs MULTI-MARKET DIVERSIFICATION

Vistra's moat is not technological — it is positional and structural. Positionally, the company owns ~44 GW of already-interconnected, already-permitted generation at a moment when new interconnection in ERCOT and PJM takes years and the ERCOT queue holds >400 GW of proposed projects against the 12–15 GW management believes will actually be built. Existing steel in the ground with a grid connection is the scarce asset; a hyperscaler cannot conjure a substitute. The nuclear fleet is the sharpest version of that: Perry, Davis-Besse, Beaver Valley and Comanche Peak produce firm, carbon-free, 24/7 baseload that no combination of solar and batteries currently replicates, which is exactly why Meta contracted 2,609 MW for 20 years and AWS contracted ~1,200 MW for 20 years with a 20-year extension option. Structurally, the retail-plus-generation hedge is a genuine and durable earnings-stabilizer that pure-play merchant generators lack: FY2025 is the cleanest proof, with Retail +$159M offsetting Texas −$198M. Layered on top, management hedges aggressively — roughly 100% of expected 2026 generation volumes are hedged, ~94% of 2027 and ~72% of 2028 — which converts a commodity book into something closer to a contracted cash-flow stream over the guidance horizon. Finally, brand and switching inertia in Texas residential retail is real: TXU Energy topped the Texas PUC's large-provider rankings.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The moat does not set the price. Vistra is a price-taker in wholesale energy. Hedging defers the exposure, it does not eliminate it: the ~72% hedged position for 2028 means roughly 28% of that year's volume reprices at whatever the forward curve becomes. Management explicitly flagged on the Q2 2026 call that ERCOT forward curves have "softened significantly," pushing 2027 toward the low end of the $7.4–$7.8B EBITDA range. A structural moat around volume is worth much less if price mean-reverts.

2. Regulatory and market-design risk. Vistra's earnings depend on rules it does not write. PJM's proposed IRAS framework and further capacity-market redesign could materially change capacity revenue. In Texas, a state audit of data-center interconnection requests may delay queue positions. Co-location of data centers behind the meter at nuclear plants — the highest-value version of the AI-power trade — remains subject to FERC and regional-market approval processes that have already produced adverse rulings for peers in this industry.

3. Balance-sheet leverage and acquisition stacking. Total debt is $20.51B against $435M of cash, a net debt position of −$20.07B and debt-to-equity of 3.73x. Vistra is simultaneously funding the ~$4B Cogentrix purchase, the November 2025 Lotus acquisition (2,600 MW), Permian gas construction, nuclear uprates and a $1.2B remaining buyback. That is a lot of capital committed against a softening ERCOT curve. Current ratio of 0.97 and quick ratio of 0.26 leave little balance-sheet slack, though available liquidity was ~$6,295M at June 30, 2026.

4. Customer concentration is being deliberately created. The bull case rests on a handful of counterparties — Meta and AWS — representing multi-decade commitments. That converts diversified merchant exposure into large single-name credit and performance exposure. It also means that PPA momentum, not just PPA existence, drives the multiple: if the next 12 months produce no new signed deals, the stock loses its principal narrative driver regardless of what EBITDA does.

Notable One-Off Items

Vistra's GAAP earnings are materially distorted by non-cash items, and reading them at face value is a mistake. The largest is mark-to-market on hedges: FY2025 GAAP net income of $944M (vs $2,812M in FY2024) was reduced by an unrealized loss of $808M on hedges expected to settle in future years, and Q2 2026 net income of $305M absorbed a further $472M unrealized hedge loss. These are accounting marks on positions that will settle physically against generation Vistra actually owns — economically, the loss on the derivative is offset by higher realized value on the underlying megawatt-hours. It is precisely why management guides on Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA, which rose to $5,912M in FY2025 from $5,643M in FY2024 even as GAAP net income fell 66%. Second, acquisition accounting: the March 2024 Energy Harbor close means FY2025 contained two extra months of those assets versus FY2024, inflating the East comparison; the November 2025 Lotus acquisition (2,600 MW) and the pending ~$4B Cogentrix purchase will each create further non-comparable periods. Third, insurance and closure items: FY2025 results included a Martin Lake property-damage insurance recovery and business-interruption proceeds tied to the Moss Landing battery incident, with the 100-MW Moss Landing facility transferred into Asset Closure. Fourth, asset-closure drag narrowed from −$104M (FY2024) to −$74M (FY2025) as coal retirement obligations wound down. Finally, on Winter Storm Uri: no Uri-specific charge or recovery appears in the FY2025 or Q2 2026 disclosures retrieved for this analysis — the 2021 event no longer appears to be a live line item, and no figure is asserted here.

In plain English: RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been. Above 70 means the stock has been bought hard and may be stretched; below 30 means it has been sold hard and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From a September 2021 monthly close of $15.72 to the August 19, 2026 close of $142.70, VST returned +807.8% on price alone — a roughly 9.1x move, and one of the most extreme re-ratings in the S&P 500 over that span. The path splits cleanly into three regimes. 2021 through mid-2023 was dormant: the stock oscillated between roughly $15 and $25 for two years as a levered, coal-exposed Texas merchant generator trading on gas curves and weather. Late 2023 through 2024 was the re-rating: from a December 2023 close of $37.82, the stock ended December 2024 at $136.74 — +261.6% in a single calendar year, making it one of the best-performing S&P 500 constituents of 2024. The catalysts were the March 2024 Energy Harbor close, which turned Vistra into the second-largest competitive nuclear owner in the country, and the market's simultaneous discovery that AI data centers require firm, carbon-free baseload power that essentially only nuclear supplies. May 2024 alone was +29% month-over-month. Mid-2025 to now is the give-back: after a July 2025 monthly closing high of $207.46, the stock has fallen −31.2%, and it sits −35.1% below the 52-week high of $219.82 while trading below both its 50-day ($153.94) and 200-day ($161.11) moving averages. Critically, this drawdown happened while EBITDA was growing — Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA rose 31% YoY. That is the signature of multiple compression, not deteriorating fundamentals, and it is the single most important fact in this analysis.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Vistra is now growing EBITDA faster than at any point in its history — Q2 2026 Adjusted EBITDA +31% YoY, FY2026 guided to $6.8–$7.6B against $5.9B delivered in 2025 — while the multiple has compressed to 13.9x forward earnings and ~10.2x EV/EBITDA. Two 20-year hyperscaler PPAs (Meta 2,609 MW; AWS ~1,200 MW) plus ~$700M of excluded Meta opportunity and the entire ~$4B/5,500 MW Cogentrix contribution sit outside the $7.4–$7.8B 2027 range, so consensus 2027 is structurally understated. Management is retiring stock at $143 against a ~$38 historical average repurchase price with $1.2B of authorization left, and the integrated retail hedge plus ~94% hedged 2027 generation makes near-term cash flow unusually visible for a merchant generator. Nineteen analysts average a $219.72 target.

Bear Case

The stock is down 35% from its high while fundamentals improved, which means the market is repricing the multiple, and multiple compression has no natural floor. ERCOT forward curves have "softened significantly" by management's own admission, pushing 2027 toward the low end of guidance — power price normalization is the base case, not a tail risk, and Texas generation EBITDA already fell 9.7% in FY2025. PPA momentum is the entire narrative and it is stalling: the January 2026 Cogentrix and Meta announcements were the most significant news in five years and the stock fell on them; Helix is explicitly an "early-stage pipeline," not signed contracts. Leverage is heavy (3.73x D/E, $20.07B net debt, 0.97 current ratio) into a $4B acquisition, GAAP earnings are hostage to hedge marks ($808M unrealized loss in FY2025, $472M in Q2 2026), and a beta of 1.43 with 18 Buys and zero Holds describes crowded positioning, not a margin of safety.

  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
AI Data-Center Power Demand
EMERGING TAILWIND

After twenty years of essentially flat US electricity demand, data centers have restarted load growth. Vistra's own long-term ERCOT forecast is 115–120 GW of load by 2030. The demand is real and the contracts are real: Meta signed for 2,609 MW across three PJM nuclear plants for 20 years (2,176 MW existing plus 433 MW of uprates, phased through 2034), and AWS signed for ~1,200 MW from Comanche Peak for 20 years with a 20-year extension option, delivering from late 2027 to full capacity by 2032. But the distinction between signed and speculated matters enormously here. Signed: Meta, AWS. Not yet signed: everything under the Helix Digital Infrastructure banner with KKR, KIA and NVIDIA, which management described as an early-stage pipeline against a $1.0B initial Vistra commitment plus $500M contingent on milestones. Investors marking VST on Helix are marking optionality, not contracted cash flow. The counterweight is supply: the ERCOT interconnection queue holds >400 GW of proposed projects against the 12–15 GW management expects to be built — an eightfold-plus overstatement that a state audit is now attempting to clean up.

Power Price & Capacity Market Dynamics — ERCOT vs PJM
ONGOING HEADWIND

The two markets are diverging, and Vistra sits in both. ERCOT is energy-only: there is no capacity payment, so generators earn on scarcity hours. FY2025 Texas EBITDA fell to $1,834M from $2,032M as scarcity faded, and management stated on the Q2 2026 call that forward curves have "softened significantly," pushing 2027 toward the low end of $7.4–$7.8B. Battery storage is a structural culprit — even while underperforming technical expectations, batteries add supply precisely during the critical hours that used to produce ERCOT's fat margins. PJM is the opposite: it pays a separate capacity price, and East EBITDA rose to $2,282M in FY2025 and +53.6% YoY in Q2 2026 on stronger capacity and energy pricing. That divergence is why East has overtaken Texas as Vistra's largest segment (38% vs 31% of positive-segment EBITDA), and why the Cogentrix acquisition — mostly PJM and ISO-NE gas — is strategically coherent. The risk on the PJM side is entirely regulatory: the proposed IRAS framework and further capacity-market redesign could reset the auction economics that are currently carrying the company.

Nuclear Revaluation — Bull vs Bear Implications
CRITICAL SWING FACTOR

The single question that determines whether VST is cheap at 13.9x forward earnings or expensive at 24.3x trailing is whether existing US nuclear has been permanently revalued. The bull framing: a 20-year, investment-grade, inflation-linked contract on carbon-free baseload is a fundamentally different asset from a merchant megawatt-hour, and should carry a regulated-utility or infrastructure multiple rather than an IPP multiple. Vistra has 4,048 MW of PJM nuclear (Perry 1,268, Davis-Besse 908, Beaver Valley 1,872) plus Comanche Peak in ERCOT, is pursuing subsequent license renewals for another 20 years on four PJM units, is adding 433 MW of uprates under the Meta agreements, and enjoys a nuclear production tax credit that floors the downside. On that framing, 2027 EBITDA of $7.4–$7.8B excluding both Cogentrix and Meta means consensus is anchored to a stale number. The bear framing: the revaluation already happened — that is what 2024's +261.6% year was — and what the market is now doing is taking it back. The stock is 35% off its high, the entire IPP complex is de-rating together, sell-side targets are being cut while ratings are not, and the most consequential announcements in the company's history (Cogentrix, Meta, Helix/NVIDIA) produced negative price reactions. A thesis whose good news no longer moves the stock is a thesis the market has already priced. The resolution is empirical, not rhetorical: watch whether Meta deliveries starting in late 2026 and the Cogentrix close produce upward guidance revisions to 2027, and whether Helix converts a single named site into a signed offtake. If both happen, the bear case is a valuation air-pocket. If neither does, 13.9x forward on a levered commodity producer is not obviously cheap.
