01 Company Overview

Vistra Corp. is a US independent power producer (IPP — a generator that sells electricity into competitive wholesale markets rather than earning a regulated rate of return) that also runs a large competitive retail electricity business; it describes itself as an integrated retail electricity and power generation company. Its fleet of natural gas, nuclear, coal, solar and battery plants totals about 49,000 MW (~49 GW) — enough, per the company, to power about 24 million homes — after the October 2026 Cogentrix purchase added about 5,500 MW of gas plants to the ~44,000 MW it reported for the end of 2025, and it sells power and gas to approximately 5 million residential, commercial and industrial retail customers under the TXU Energy, Dynegy, Ambit, Energy Harbor and Homefield Energy brands, "from California to Maine." Vistra generated $17.74B of FY2025 revenue, up +2.98% YoY, on $5,912M of Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA; trailing-twelve-month revenue through September 2026 is $20.59B (+19.8%). It ranks #245 in the S&P 500 by market capitalization.

The defining feature of the business is integration: Vistra owns generation and simultaneously owns the retail load that consumes it. That structure is not a diversification story — it is the hedge. It is examined in detail in Section 02. Layered on top since 2024 is the AI-power narrative: Vistra operates the second-largest competitive nuclear fleet in the United States, and has signed 20-year PPAs with Amazon Web Services (Comanche Peak, ERCOT) and Meta (output from Perry and Davis-Besse plus uprates there and at Beaver Valley, PJM). Investors bid the stock up hard on that theme in 2024–2025; at the November 9, 2026 close it was 36% below its July 2025 month-end high.

The East segment houses the PJM nuclear and gas fleet acquired largely through Energy Harbor (closed March 2024) — the transaction that converted Vistra from a Texas gas-and-coal story into a national nuclear owner. In Q3 2026 East EBITDA rose +36.2% YoY ($979M vs $719M), and +46.7% over the first nine months ($2,422M vs $1,651M). The Cogentrix acquisition (ten gas plants, about 5,500 MW across PJM, ISO-New England and one ERCOT cogeneration site; a $4B deal with funds of Quantum Capital Group) closed on October 30, 2026 and lands mostly in this segment; Vistra paid about $2.2B in cash and 5 million of its shares and took on about $1.7B of debt, which it repaid. PJM is where the capacity market matters most: capacity revenue is a fixed, auction-cleared payment that provides a floor beneath merchant energy margins.

This is the part of Vistra that most investors under-weight, and it is the core of the business model. Retail sells fixed-price electricity contracts to ~5 million customers; generation produces physical megawatt-hours. Standing alone, a retailer is short power (it owes fixed-price supply and must buy at market) and a generator is long power. Owned together, the two positions offset: the retail book is effectively a captive, high-margin, credit-worthy offtake for the fleet, and the fleet is a physical hedge for the retail book. That is why Retail delivered $1,622M in FY2025 — up 10.9% in a year when Texas generation EBITDA fell 9.7%. Retail margins expand when wholesale prices fall (supply cost drops faster than customer prices reset) and compress when they spike — precisely the inverse of generation. TXU Energy was the top-rated large retail energy provider in the Texas Public Utility Commission rankings in the FY2025 release. Retail is seasonal and smallest in the summer quarter, when it buys the most power: Q3 2026 Retail EBITDA was $98M (vs $37M), and over nine months it was $939M, down 3.9% from $977M while Texas generation rose 50% — the hedge cutting the other way.

Industry Size: Competitive retail electricity in Texas and the other states where customers choose their supplier; Vistra serves about 5 million retail customers "from California to Maine."

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
INTEGRATED RETAIL + GEN HEDGE #2 COMPETITIVE NUCLEAR FLEET ~49 GW DISPATCHABLE SCALE INTERCONNECTED BROWNFIELD SITES TXU BRAND EQUITY 20-YR HYPERSCALER PPAs MULTI-MARKET DIVERSIFICATION

Vistra's moat is not technological — it is positional and structural. Positionally, the company owns ~49 GW of already-interconnected, already-permitted generation at a moment when new interconnection in ERCOT and PJM takes years. Existing steel in the ground with a grid connection is the scarce asset; a hyperscaler cannot conjure a substitute. The nuclear fleet is the sharpest version of that: Perry, Davis-Besse, Beaver Valley and Comanche Peak produce firm, carbon-free, 24/7 baseload that no combination of solar and batteries currently replicates, which is exactly why Meta contracted 2,609 MW for 20 years and AWS contracted ~1,200 MW for 20 years with a 20-year extension option. Structurally, the retail-plus-generation hedge is a genuine and durable earnings-stabilizer that pure-play merchant generators lack: FY2025 is the cleanest proof, with Retail +$159M offsetting Texas −$198M. Layered on top, management hedges aggressively — roughly 100% of expected 2026 generation volumes are hedged, ~98% of 2027 and ~78% of 2028 (as of October 30, 2026) — which converts a commodity book into something closer to a contracted cash-flow stream over the guidance horizon. Finally, brand and switching inertia in Texas residential retail is real: TXU Energy topped the Texas PUC's large-provider rankings.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The moat does not set the price. Vistra is a price-taker in wholesale energy. Hedging defers the exposure, it does not eliminate it: the ~78% hedged position for 2028 means roughly 22% of that year's volume reprices at whatever future prices become, and on the Q3 2026 call Jim Burke said "we are much less hedged in ERCOT than the 78%." He also said "we have seen some depression in ERCOT forwards" (the prices for Texas power delivered in future years). Owning the plants is worth much less if power prices fall back.

2. Regulatory and market-design risk. Vistra's earnings depend on rules it does not write. FERC rejected PJM's IRAS market-rule proposal, which management welcomed on the Q3 2026 call, but further changes to PJM's capacity-market rules could still materially change capacity revenue. In Texas, a state audit of data-center interconnection requests may delay queue positions, and the November 2026 election added a new layer: Democrat Gina Hinojosa, the governor-elect, campaigned on tighter rules for data centers, including a moratorium on new construction until lawmakers set further requirements (Investing.com). KeyBanc named Vistra as the most exposed company it covers when it downgraded the stock on November 4. Co-location of data centers behind the meter at nuclear plants — the highest-value version of the AI-power trade — remains subject to FERC and regional-market approval processes that have already produced adverse rulings for peers in this industry.

3. Balance-sheet leverage and acquisition stacking. At September 30, 2026 total debt was $21.77B against $2.74B of cash, a net debt position of $19.02B and debt-to-equity of 3.37x. In October and November Vistra then paid about $2.2B in cash for Cogentrix, repaid about $1.7B of Cogentrix's debt, redeemed $1B of preferred stock and drew its receivables financing in full, taking available liquidity from about $8.9B at September 30 to about $5.4B pro forma (the release's figure). It is also funding Permian gas construction, nuclear uprates and a $2.5B remaining buyback authorization, to be used by the end of 2028. A current ratio of 1.16 and a quick ratio of 0.48 at September 30 leave modest short-term slack.

4. Customer concentration is being deliberately created. The bull case rests on a handful of counterparties — Meta and AWS — representing multi-decade commitments. That converts diversified merchant exposure into large single-name credit and performance exposure. It also means that PPA momentum, not just PPA existence, drives the multiple: if the next 12 months produce no new signed deals, the stock loses the story that has driven it, whatever EBITDA does. On the Q3 call management said Beaver Valley and the second part of Comanche Peak are still not contracted.

Notable One-Off Items

Vistra's GAAP earnings are materially distorted by non-cash items, and reading them at face value is a mistake. The largest is mark-to-market on hedges: FY2025 GAAP net income of $944M (vs $2,812M in FY2024) was reduced by an unrealized loss of $808M on hedges expected to settle in future years; Q2 2026 net income of $305M absorbed a further $472M unrealized hedge loss, and Q3 2026 net income of $1,140M (vs $652M) was lifted by a $559M unrealized hedge gain. These are accounting marks on positions that will settle physically against generation Vistra actually owns — economically, the loss on the derivative is offset by higher realized value on the underlying megawatt-hours. It is precisely why management guides on Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA, which rose to $5,912M in FY2025 from $5,643M in FY2024 even as GAAP net income fell 66%. Second, acquisition accounting: the March 2024 Energy Harbor close means FY2025 contained two extra months of those assets versus FY2024, inflating the East comparison; the November 2025 Lotus acquisition (2,600 MW) and the ~$4B Cogentrix purchase, closed October 30, 2026, will each create further non-comparable periods. Third, insurance, impairment and closure items: FY2025 Texas results included $120M of insurance income, including a gain on Martin Lake property-damage insurance, and the 100-MW Moss Landing facility was transferred into Asset Closure; Q3 2026 carried $269M of impairment charges, including a partial impairment of the Moss Landing 350 facility. Fourth, asset-closure drag narrowed from −$104M (FY2024) to −$74M (FY2025) as coal retirement obligations wound down.

Investors are paying less for each dollar of Vistra's earnings rather than reacting to weaker results, and that is the single most important fact in this analysis.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Vistra's EBITDA is growing fast — Q3 2026 Ongoing Operations Adjusted EBITDA +50% YoY, 2026 guidance narrowed to $7.1–$7.5B against $5.9B delivered in 2025, and 2027 opened at $7.7–$8.5B with Cogentrix and the Meta agreement included — while the stock trades at 18.1x trailing and 14.2x FY2027 consensus earnings and about 8.2x EV/EBITDA. The CFO says the company has "line of sight to approximately 70% growth in adjusted free cash flow before growth per share from 2027 to 2030." The $2.5B of buyback authorization left equals about 5.5% of the $45.45B market cap, about 98% of 2027 generation is hedged, and the retail book steadies cash flow. Beaver Valley and the second part of Comanche Peak are still uncontracted, so there is capacity left to sign with data centers. Twenty-three analysts average a $202.76 target.

Bear Case

The stock is 32% below its 52-week high while fundamentals improved: investors are paying less for each dollar of earnings, and that has no natural floor. Texas politics is now a live risk: the governor-elect campaigned on a moratorium on new data-center construction until lawmakers set further requirements, and KeyBanc called Vistra the most exposed company it covers. Jim Burke says "we have seen some depression in ERCOT forwards," and Vistra is less hedged in ERCOT for 2028 than its 78% fleet-wide figure. Leverage is heavy ($19.02B of net debt and 3.37x debt-to-equity at September 30, before about $2.2B of cash went out for Cogentrix; liquidity about $5.4B pro forma), GAAP earnings swing with hedge marks ($472M loss in Q2 2026, $559M gain in Q3), the seller of Cogentrix is free to sell its 5 million Vistra shares after January 30, 2027, and analysts keep trimming targets — five cuts and no raises in the nine calls since late October — even as 21 of 23 rate it a Buy.

Top 3 Risks
01 Power price normalization in ERCOT. Roughly 31% of FY2025 segment EBITDA came from Texas generation; that segment shrank 9.7% in 2025 as scarcity pricing faded, then rose 50% in the first nine months of 2026. Management says ERCOT forward prices have weakened; with ~22% of 2028 generation unhedged fleet-wide, and more than that in ERCOT, a lasting fall in Texas power prices flows almost directly to EBITDA and, through it, to the share price.
02 Texas policy and a stalling AI-power story. VST rose about 6.7x over the chart's five years, mainly on a story. That story needs a steady flow of signed data-center contracts and approvals, and Texas's incoming governor campaigned on a moratorium on new data-center construction until lawmakers set more rules. The January 2026 announcements failed to lift the stock, and the peers (CEG, NRG, TLN) have been falling together — a sign that investors are leaving the theme, whatever each company delivers.
03 Leverage plus regulatory dependence. $21.77B of total debt against $2.74B of cash at September 30, and about $5.4B of pro forma liquidity after the Cogentrix payments, leave limited cushion while the company funds Permian construction, nuclear uprates and buybacks at once. Revenue in the East segment depends on PJM capacity-auction outcomes and market rules Vistra does not control, and behind-the-meter co-location at nuclear sites still faces FERC and market-operator approval.
  Industry Commentary — What Actually Drives This Stock
AI Data-Center Power Demand
EMERGING TAILWIND

After twenty years of essentially flat US electricity demand, data centers have restarted load growth. On the Q3 2026 call Jim Burke said Vistra still sees load growth of 4% to 6% in ERCOT and 2% to 3% in PJM through 2030. The demand is real and the contracts are real: Meta signed for 2,609 MW across three PJM nuclear plants for 20 years (2,176 MW existing plus 433 MW of uprates, phased through 2034), and AWS signed for ~1,200 MW from Comanche Peak for 20 years with a 20-year extension option, delivering from late 2027 to full capacity by 2032. But the distinction between signed and speculated matters enormously here. Signed: Meta, AWS. Not yet signed: everything under the Helix Digital Infrastructure banner with KKR, KIA and NVIDIA, against an initial Vistra commitment of up to $1.0B. Investors who value VST on Helix are paying for possibilities, not contracted cash flow. On the Q3 2026 call management said Beaver Valley and the second part of Comanche Peak are still not contracted; in September Vistra signed a 207 MW, 20-year contract at its Odessa gas plant with New Era Energy & Digital. Demand also now depends on Texas policy: the governor-elect campaigned on a moratorium on new data-center construction until lawmakers set more requirements. The counterweight: requests to connect new large users overstate real demand — "overstated across the country and especially in ERCOT," in Burke's words on the Q3 call — and a state audit started by Governor Abbott is reviewing them.

Power Price & Capacity Market Dynamics — ERCOT vs PJM
ONGOING HEADWIND

The two markets are diverging, and Vistra sits in both. ERCOT is energy-only: there is no capacity payment, so generators earn on scarcity hours. FY2025 Texas EBITDA fell to $1,834M from $2,032M as scarcity faded; it then rose 56.5% in Q3 2026 on summer demand, but on the Q3 call Jim Burke said "we have seen some depression in ERCOT forwards." PJM is the opposite: it pays a separate capacity price, and East EBITDA rose to $2,282M in FY2025 and +36.2% YoY in Q3 2026. That divergence is why East has overtaken Texas as Vistra's largest segment (38% vs 31% of positive-segment EBITDA), and why the Cogentrix acquisition — mostly PJM and ISO-NE gas — is strategically coherent. The risk on the PJM side is mainly regulatory: FERC rejected PJM's IRAS proposal, as management noted on the Q3 call, but further capacity-market redesign could still reset the auction economics that are currently carrying the company.

Nuclear Revaluation — Bull vs Bear Implications
CRITICAL SWING FACTOR

The single question that determines whether VST is cheap at 14.2x FY2027 consensus earnings or fully priced at 18.1x trailing is whether existing US nuclear has been permanently revalued. The bull framing: a 20-year, investment-grade, inflation-linked contract on carbon-free baseload is a fundamentally different asset from a merchant megawatt-hour, and should carry a regulated-utility or infrastructure multiple rather than an IPP multiple. Vistra has 4,048 MW of PJM nuclear (Perry 1,268, Davis-Besse 908, Beaver Valley 1,872) plus Comanche Peak in ERCOT, plans to apply for further license renewals that would extend its PJM nuclear sites into the 2050s and 2060s, is adding 433 MW of uprates under the Meta agreements, and enjoys a nuclear production tax credit that floors the downside. On that framing, the new 2027 guidance of $7.7–$8.5B is a base for contracts still to come, not a ceiling. The bear framing: the revaluation already happened — that is what 2024's +257.9% year was — and what the market is now doing is taking it back. The stock is 32% below its 52-week high, the other power producers have fallen with it, sell-side targets are being cut while ratings are not, and the most consequential announcements in the company's history (Cogentrix, Meta, Helix/NVIDIA) produced negative price reactions; even the strong Q3 print only won back what the Texas election took. A thesis whose good news no longer moves the stock is a thesis the market has already priced. The resolution is empirical, not rhetorical: watch whether 2027 lands in the top half of $7.7–$8.5B, whether Helix, Beaver Valley or the rest of Comanche Peak turns into a signed contract, and what Texas does on data-center rules. If the contracts come, the bear case looks like a valuation air-pocket. If they don't, 14.2x forward earnings for a heavily indebted power producer is not obviously cheap.
