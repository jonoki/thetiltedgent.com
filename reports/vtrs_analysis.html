01 Company Overview

Viatris is what you get when you shuffle two old decks together. On November 16, 2020, generic-drug maker Mylan combined with Upjohn — Pfizer's off-patent medicines division — through a Reverse Morris Trust. The new company inherited Mylan's S&P 500 seat, its NASDAQ listing and a very large balance sheet. It sells brands (mostly established, off-patent medicines that keep their name and loyalty) and generics in markets around the world; the company says it supplies roughly 1 billion patients a year. FY2025 total revenue was $14.30B, down 3% reported, with brands at $9.18B and generics at $5.07B.

The hand Viatris has played since 2020 is a shrink-to-grow hand. It sold its biosimilars business to Biocon Biologics in a deal announced in February 2022 (closed November 2022), then in 2024 sold most of its OTC business, its women's healthcare business and its India API operations. The divestitures show in the numbers: revenue fell from $17.89B in FY2021 to $14.30B in FY2025 (−20%), and the stock drifted between roughly $8 and $15 for four years. Then two things went wrong at once. In December 2024 the FDA issued a Warning Letter and Import Alert for the oral-dose plant in Indore, India — 11 actively distributed products barred from the US, four exempted for shortage reasons — which the company later put at roughly $370M of lost 2025 revenue. And in Q1 2025 it booked a $2.9B non-cash goodwill impairment, which is why FY2025 GAAP EPS was −$3.00.

2026 is the year the story turned. Revenue is growing again (Q1 +8% reported, Q2 +5%), Greater China is growing 20%-plus, guidance has been raised, and the stock has run from an $8.42 month-end low in April 2025 to $17.10 — a double. The central debate: has Viatris finished shrinking and become a steady, cash-rich compounder with a real pipeline, or is it a generics-heavy business whose Indian plants keep dealing it bad cards? A second plant, Nashik, had a fire in February 2026 and received FDA Form 483 observations in May, with an expected $100–150M revenue hit in the second half of 2026.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Global manufacturing & supply network Established brand names (Upjohn legacy) Greater China brand franchise Complex-generics approvals Scale: $14.3B revenue ~$2B/yr free cash flow

Generics are the poker room's cash game: everyone can sit down, and the house (the pharmacy buyer or the government price-setter) takes a rake on every pot. Viatris' edge is not any single drug; it is breadth and reach — a very broad portfolio, registered in many countries, made in its own plants and shipped through its own channels. That breadth is what produced $1.94B of free cash flow in FY2025 even in a year of falling revenue and a $2.9B impairment. The weakness is the same breadth: when one plant fails an inspection, many products fail with it. Adjusted gross margin of 57.5% in Q2 2026 versus a GAAP gross margin of 38.8% also tells you how much of the reported cost base sits in items the company excludes from its adjusted measures.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Manufacturing quality in India. Indore: June 2024 inspection, December 2024 Warning Letter and Import Alert, ~$370M of 2025 revenue lost. Nashik: February 2026 fire, May 2026 Form 483, $100–150M expected H2 2026 hit. Two sites, two years. The FDA judges each site on its own, but a pattern shapes how investors price the next inspection.
02
Generics price erosion. FY2025 generics net sales fell 8% to $5,066.4M. The company cites competition in North America and government price regulation in Japan. New launches ($450–550M guided for 2026) are the counterweight; they need to keep arriving just to hold the line.
03
Leverage and GAAP losses. $13.62B of total debt, $12.11B net debt, debt/equity 0.96 and an Altman Z-score of 1.1 (StockAnalysis). TTM GAAP net loss of $410.7M, after the $177.8M Tyrvaya write-down in Q2 2026 and the $2.9B goodwill impairment in Q1 2025. The adjusted numbers look healthy; the GAAP numbers keep carrying write-downs from the 2020 deal and later decisions.
04
Concentration in Greater China. The one segment growing at 20%-plus is also the one most exposed to a single government's pricing policy. If China growth slowed to the low single digits, the company's operational growth — 3.5% in Q2 2026 — would shrink sharply.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort GAAP earnings. First, the $2.9B non-cash goodwill impairment in Q1 2025, which the company tied to higher discount rates on geopolitical, pricing, FDA and tariff uncertainty; it drives FY2025's −$3.00 EPS. Second, a $177.8M non-cash charge in Q2 2026 on the planned sale of Tyrvaya's global rights to Harrow ($30M upfront plus up to $70M in milestones) — the main reason for the $119M Q2 GAAP loss. Third, the enterprise-wide strategic review announced February 26, 2026: $700–850M of expected pre-tax restructuring charges over about three years, a workforce reduction of up to ~10%, and $600–700M of expected savings once fully in place. On the other side, sales of the Biocon Biologics stake brought in about $780M in total. Capital return: ~$550M in Q2 2026, including ~$270M of buybacks at a $16.42 average price, with ~$730M of authorization remaining.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Over five years, from the September 2021 month-end close of $13.55 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $17.10, VTRS gained 26.2% on price. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 6.8x this year's guided adjusted EPS ($2.52 midpoint) for a company that has stopped shrinking. Revenue is growing again (+8% Q1, +5% Q2 reported), guidance has been raised, free cash flow is about $2B a year — roughly a 10% yield on the $19.64B market cap — and the 2.81% dividend takes only 28% of free cash flow. The strategic review targets $600–700M of savings, management has set 2030 targets of 6–7% base-case adjusted EPS growth, and an Indore reinspection that goes well would bring back US products that cost ~$370M of revenue in 2025. Add two FDA decision dates before year-end and a Greater China franchise growing 20%, and the multiple has room to move.

Bear Case

The cheap multiple is on adjusted earnings; GAAP earnings are negative (TTM EPS −$0.35) and have been dragged down by write-downs year after year — $2.9B of goodwill in 2025, $177.8M on Tyrvaya in 2026. Net debt is $12.11B, 38% of enterprise value. Growth rests on one segment in one country, while generics sales fell 8% in 2025. And the manufacturing record is the real problem: Indore in 2024, Nashik in 2026. The stock has doubled from its low and RSI sits at 70; the good news is in the price, and the market sold the raised guidance 7.71% on the day.

Top 3 Risks

01
Another FDA action at an Indian plant. Indore cost ~$370M of 2025 revenue; Nashik already has a Form 483 and a $100–150M expected H2 2026 hit. If Nashik's response falls short, a Warning Letter would follow the same path as Indore.
02
Greater China pricing. The segment grew 21% in Q2 2026 and is doing most of the company's growing. A price-regulation round aimed at off-patent brands would take away the growth that supports the raised guidance.
03
Execution and capital allocation. A $700–850M restructuring, an interim CFO, and a plan to put about half of more than $11B of deployable cash into business development through 2030. Past deals and divestitures have produced large write-downs; the next ones need to earn more than the ~10% FCF yield that buybacks offer today.
Industry Commentary
Indian manufacturing and the FDA
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

A generics company is only as good as its worst plant. The FDA inspected Indore in June 2024, issued a Warning Letter and Import Alert in December 2024 and blocked 11 actively distributed products from the US, exempting four for shortage reasons. At the time the company said it did not expect an impact on 2024 guidance; the 2025 impact came to about $370M of revenue, plus knock-on effects in the ARV business and some European generics. In February 2026 a fire hit a service area at Nashik and suspended production; a May 2026 FDA inspection there ended with Form 483 observations. The company says it responded with a remediation plan using third-party experts. Both events are supply problems first and quality problems second — but investors price them the same way.

Generic price erosion and government pricing
ONGOING HEADWIND

Viatris' own releases name the two forces: competition on certain North American generics and government price regulation in Japan. FY2025 generics net sales fell 8% reported. The answer is a steady flow of launches — $324M of new-product revenue in 2025, $450–550M guided for 2026 — including complex products such as the generic Sandostatin LAR Depot (approved December 2025) and ferric carboxymaltose injection (approved 2026). New-product revenue has to outrun erosion every year; it is a treadmill, not a finish line.

From shrink-to-grow to capital deployment
EMERGING TAILWIND

Since 2022 Viatris has sold biosimilars, OTC, women's healthcare, India API, its Biocon Biologics stake (~$780M) and now Tyrvaya, while cutting gross leverage to 2.9x. The March 2026 Investor Event turned the story forward: more than $11B of cash available for deployment through 2030, about half for business development, alongside the dividend and buybacks. The bull framing: a company that no longer has to sell assets to cut debt can compound at mid-to-high single digits in EPS while paying ~2.8%. The bear framing: business development at this scale is exactly how the goodwill that was written down in 2025 got onto the balance sheet. The test is simple and public — each deal's price against the ~10% FCF yield that buying back its own stock already earns.
