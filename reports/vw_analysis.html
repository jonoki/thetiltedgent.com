01 Company Overview

Volkswagen is Europe's biggest carmaker: 9.0 million vehicles delivered in 2025 across Volkswagen, Škoda, SEAT/CUPRA, Audi, Bentley, Lamborghini, Porsche, Ducati motorcycles and the Scania, MAN and International truck brands, plus a captive bank and leasing arm. FY2025 sales revenue was €321.9B on an operating result of only €8.9B — a 2.8% operating margin. The stock on this page is the preferred share (VOW3), the liquid line: in 2025 the preferred shares turned over €26.6B on Xetra against €1.6B for the voting ordinary shares, per the company's share key figures. A preferred share has the same claim on profit as an ordinary share (plus €0.06 of dividend) but no vote.

Control is the first thing to understand. Porsche Automobil Holding SE (Porsche SE) holds 53.3% of the voting rights, the State of Lower Saxony 20.0% and Qatar Holding 17.0%, leaving 9.7% of the votes in free float (Volkswagen IR, as at Dec 31, 2025). Volkswagen's own H1 2026 report spells out the balance: although Porsche SE holds the majority of votes, it cannot appoint the majority of the Supervisory Board for as long as Lower Saxony holds at least 15% of the ordinary shares. A buyer of VOW3 is therefore a minority partner with no vote, in a company where a holding company, a German state government and a sovereign wealth fund decide — and where the state and the unions sit on the board.

2026 has been a year of resets. The first half delivered revenue of €158.1B (flat) and an operating result of €5.9B (−11.6%), and on July 24 the company cut its revenue outlook. On September 3 the Supervisory Board approved the Future Plan 2030: a 9% operating margin target for 2030, around 50,000 fewer positions, about half as many models by 2035, and an admission that European capacity exceeds demand by more than 500,000 units. Fifteen days later, on September 18, an ad hoc release cut the 2026 operating margin guide from 4.0–5.5% to up to 1%, on a ~€6B write-down of the goodwill on Porsche AG (the premium over net assets carried on Volkswagen's books) plus ~€2B of further restructuring and China charges. On October 29 the nine-month results booked those charges: a third-quarter operating loss of €5.9B, a nine-month operating result of €71M (€8.8B before special items) and every 2026 guide left unchanged. The preferred shares closed at €69.84 on November 6, and the whole group — both share classes — is valued at €34.9B, about the same as the €33.0B of net cash its car business held at September 30.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Scale: 9.0M deliveries (2025) Brand ladder from Škoda to Lamborghini Captive finance & leasing book Shared platforms across brands €33.0B automotive net liquidity Truck franchise (TRATON)

Volkswagen's moat is size and breadth: one set of platforms, factories and purchasing spread across more than a dozen brands, a lending arm that finances the cars it sells, and a balance sheet that still held €33.0B of automotive net liquidity at September 30, after June dividend payments of €2.7B to the shareholders of Volkswagen, Porsche AG and TRATON. The weakness is that size cuts both ways. A cost base built for a world of rising volume now faces what the June 18 AGM release called zero-growth markets, and the Future Plan's own diagnosis — European capacity more than 500,000 units above demand, a model range to be cut by about half — is a statement that much of the scale is now excess. Scale protects market share; it has not protected margin, which was 2.8% in 2025.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
China has stopped paying. Over the first nine months of 2026 Volkswagen's share of their result was a €113M loss against a €744M profit a year earlier, on JV vehicle sales down 35%. Chinese brands are winning their home market and, per the H1 investor presentation, took more than 8% of the European market (EU, UK and EFTA) in H1 2026 and 30% of its plug-in hybrid segment.
02
The cost base is German and political. CNBC reported that Blume told staff in July that the group's costs were 20% higher than comparable businesses. Cutting them runs through a Supervisory Board where the State of Lower Saxony and IG Metall sit; the Future Plan's ~50,000 positions and the open question over the Emden, Zwickau, Hanover and Neckarsulm plants from 2031 must all be negotiated with employee representatives. Antlitz to CNBC: "I'm not looking for job cuts per se and I'm not looking for plant closures per se."
03
Preferred holders have no vote. VOW3 holders cannot vote at the general meeting. Decisions on capital allocation, the Porsche AG stake, dividends and restructuring are made by a controlling group whose priorities — family wealth, regional employment, sovereign returns — need not match a minority investor's. The €0.06 dividend premium is the whole compensation.
04
The US is a tariff and EV problem at once. The H1 report cites higher US import tariffs since April 2025 and the expiry of US electric-vehicle subsidies on September 30, 2025; Volkswagen ended US production of the ID.4 in mid-April 2026 at a cost of around €0.5B. The Future Plan narrows North America to the most profitable segments.

Notable One-Off Items

The first nine months of 2026 carried €8.7B of special items, €7.8B of them in the third quarter: non-cash impairments of €6.4B on Porsche goodwill and about €1B on assets of fully consolidated companies in China, plus expenses tied to the planned sale of Volkswagen Osnabrück GmbH. Before them the nine-month operating result was €8.8B, a 3.8% margin, against the reported €71M. H1 had carried about €0.9B, including around €0.5B for ending US production of the ID.4. The September 18 ad hoc expects special effects of about €10B for the full year — the ~€6B goodwill impairment, about €2B for expanded early-retirement schemes, the Osnabrück sale and the China impairments — so about €1.3B is still to come (our arithmetic). That is why the reported 2026 margin guide is "up to 1%" while the underlying guide is about 4%. The Osnabrück plant agreement with Lower Saxony and Aurelius (Sep 7) is inside those charges; the Everllence sale is outside the guidance: the exclusive arrangement with Bain Capital (Jun 24) to sell 51% of the large-engine and turbomachinery maker for proceeds of about €7.4B.

06 Upcoming Catalysts & News
Nov 24, 2026
Labour talks with IG Metall open in Emden
On September 30 Volkswagen gave notice ending several collective agreements on December 31, 2026; the agreement that protects jobs is excluded, and the terminated ones stay in force until new arrangements are reached. On October 23 the company and IG Metall, Germany's metalworkers' union, agreed to open formal negotiations on November 24 in Emden, with a second session on December 15 in Zwickau. Volkswagen names a rapid cut in labour costs as its goal; IG Metall is demanding a 5% pay rise over twelve months and says strikes become possible from January 1, when the legal no-strike period ends.

Volkswagen gives its 2027 profit and cash-flow guidance with the full-year results, due in March (per the Quartr summary of the October 29 investor presentation). What matters: the underlying margin against that 4%, automotive net cash flow against the €3–6B guide, and the first numbers on Future Plan costs.
Key near-term event
By Jun 2027
European production concept due
The Future Plan commits to a concept for a competitive European production structure by the end of June 2027, with the Supervisory Board acknowledging that a competitive production allocation for Emden, Zwickau, Hanover and Neckarsulm cannot currently be secured on a staggered basis from 2031 to 2034. Alternative uses for those plants are being assessed. Every step requires agreement with employee representatives.

The Board of Management and Supervisory Board will decide the dividend proposal for 2026 at the beginning of 2027, for payment in June 2027 (ad hoc, Sep 18). The 7.5% trailing yield rests on a €5.26 payout set on 2025 earnings; 2026 reported earnings will carry about €10B of special effects.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 6, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of €193.82 to the November 6, 2026 close of €69.84, VOW3 lost 64.0% of its price. Dividends soften that only partly: €56.06 per preferred share went ex-dividend over the window (Yahoo's dividend record, May 2022 to June 2026), including the €19.06 Porsche IPO special dividend, so price plus cash dividends — before reinvestment and tax — still comes to −35.0%. The chart has no real rally in it: the October 2021 close is the high of the window, and every rebound since — early 2024, early 2025, late 2025 — stalled lower than the one before.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 30.6, just above the 30 line that marks oversold territory. That is not extreme for this stock: it touched 14.0 in November 2024 and 24.5 in June 2026. The 10-month moving average sits at about €79.80, 14.3% above the price. Momentum is weak, and oversold readings have been poor timing signals here: 8 of the 11 earlier oversold months in this series were followed by a lower month-end close within six months.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

At €69.84 the market values all of Volkswagen — both share classes — at €34.9B, about the €33.0B of net cash in the car business alone. Everything else comes free: Škoda, Audi, the majority of Porsche AG, the TRATON truck group, a finance arm that earned €2.7B in nine months, and the Chinese JVs. The stock trades at 0.19x book, the trailing yield is 7.5%, and automotive net cash flow over the last twelve months was €9.0B; the 31x trailing P/E reflects the third-quarter charges, not a rich price. The charges booked in Q3 are mostly non-cash — the cash guides did not move on October 29 — and the Future Plan for the first time puts numbers on what the company will cut: 50,000 positions, half the models, a third of the shareholdings, €7.4B from Everllence. If even part of the 9% margin target for 2030 is delivered, the market is pricing a business that never again earns a fair return on the money invested in it.

Bear Case

Volkswagen has been cheap on book value and earnings for five years, and the shares have fallen 64% across them. A business earning under 1% on equity over the last twelve months deserves a deep discount to book, and the first nine months of 2026 already show a €2.7B loss after tax. The net-cash comparison flatters: the group also carries €22.3B of pension provisions and €12.4B of hybrid capital (bond-like funding that ranks close to equity; both at June 30), and the money is needed for €135B of capital spending and R&D over 2027–2031. The 2030 plan depends on agreements with a works council, IG Metall and a state shareholder over four German plants and 50,000 jobs — not a process the preferred shareholder gets a vote in. China profit has turned into a loss, Chinese brands are taking share in Europe, and the 2026 dividend will be set on a year of heavy charges.

Top 3 Risks

01
The underlying margin keeps slipping. The guide before special items is now about 4% for 2026; in July the reported guide was still 4.0–5.5%. If underlying margin slips below the nine-month 3.8% in the fourth quarter, the 9% 2030 target loses credibility and so does the case that 2026 is a one-off.
02
Restructuring stalls in Germany. The Future Plan's job and plant measures need employee-representative agreement, and the board of a company partly owned by the State of Lower Saxony. Volkswagen has given notice on several collective agreements from December 31, and formal talks with IG Metall open on November 24. Antlitz has said he is not looking for job cuts or plant closures "per se". A slow, expensive compromise is the base case for a company with this governance.
03
Dividend cut. The FY2025 payout of €5.26 was already 17% below FY2024's €6.36. The FY2026 proposal comes at the start of 2027 on a year with about €10B of special effects, and the trailing 7.5% yield is computed on the old payout.
Industry Commentary
China: from profit pool to competitor
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

The Chinese joint ventures' contribution is shrinking fast. Over the first nine months of 2026 Volkswagen's share of their result was a €113M loss, against a €744M profit a year earlier, as JV vehicle sales fell 35%. Antlitz's diagnosis in the July 24 release: "In an environment where the Chinese total market is down by 20 percent and Chinese competitors are increasing exports and thereby competitive pressure in Europe, the currently planned initiatives are not sufficient." The September 18 ad hoc adds non-cash impairments on fully consolidated companies in China. Volkswagen's answer is "In China, for China" — locally developed models such as the ID.UNYX 08, built with XPENG — plus exports to the "Global South". The H1 investor presentation counts Chinese brands at more than 8% of the European market and 30% of its plug-in hybrid segment.

Europe: too many plants for the market
ONGOING HEADWIND

The investor presentation puts the European car market (EU, UK and EFTA) 16% below 2019 in 2025. Volkswagen's Supervisory Board has formally acknowledged European capacity more than 500,000 units above demand. Under a September 7 agreement the Osnabrück plant may be sold to Aurelius together with the State of Lower Saxony, and the Future Plan leaves Emden, Zwickau, Hanover and Neckarsulm without a secured production allocation from 2031 to 2034. The demand mix is also shifting faster than planned: the battery-electric share of Volkswagen's European order bank passed 30% at mid-year, and the September warning cites an accelerated shift toward battery-electric cars as a reason Audi and the VW brand are falling short — electric cars that, per the presentation, saw about 15% price deflation in 2024–25.

Self-help with a number on it
EMERGING TAILWIND

For the first time, Volkswagen's restructuring has a dated financial target attached: a 9% operating margin and about €31B of operating result by 2030, €37B of overhead costs, and €135B of capital spending and R&D over 2027–2031, approved unanimously by a Supervisory Board that includes Lower Saxony and IG Metall. Early evidence exists: CARIAD's nine-month loss narrowed to €1.32B from €1.50B, Škoda's operating result rose to €1.92B, Porsche's operating result improved to €1.49B from a €0.23B loss, and automotive net cash flow for nine months rose to €4.4B from €1.8B. The Supervisory Board also said it will resolve to limit its reserved approval rights to measures of material significance, in line with standard DAX practice — a governance change that, once made, speeds decisions in a company where every plant decision has been a negotiation. The market's verdict on November 6: not yet.
