01 Company Overview

Waters Corporation builds the analytical instruments that regulated industries use to prove what is actually in a sample — liquid chromatography (LC, which separates a mixture into its components) and mass spectrometry (MS, which identifies and weighs those components), plus the columns, vials and reagents those machines consume, plus the service contracts that keep them running. As of 9 February 2026 it is also a flow cytometry and clinical microbiology company: on that date Becton, Dickinson's Biosciences & Diagnostic Solutions businesses were spun off from BD and combined with Waters in a Reverse Morris Trust (a tax-efficient structure in which a company spins off a business and that business merges with another listed company). Standalone FY2025 revenue was $3.165B, up 6.99% on FY2024's $2.958B. On 3 November 2026 management raised its FY2026 reported revenue guidance to $6.479–6.497B (from $6.415–6.476B). Waters ranks #247 in the S&P 500 by market capitalisation at $46.87B.

Read every year-over-year number on this page twice. The company roughly doubled overnight, and it did so without restating prior periods: Waters' pre-closing shareholders retained 60.8% of the combined company (BD holders took 39.2% on a fully diluted basis), which makes Waters the accounting acquirer (the company treated as the buyer in the accounts), so FY2025 and everything before it is legacy Waters alone. The headline TTM revenue growth of +80.6% and the Q3 2026 reported growth of +121% ($1.767B against $800M) are not organic growth in any sense — they are the arithmetic of bolting on a business that did not exist inside Waters before February. The honest comparable numbers are the company's own: $871M of organic revenue in Q3 2026 (sales from the businesses Waters already owned), +9% reported and +10% in constant currency (with exchange-rate swings removed), and $896M from the acquired businesses against $885M in the prior-year comparable period, +1%. The central question of this report is whether the recurring, consumable-and-service half of this franchise — which is what has always justified Waters' premium valuation — survives the dilution of adding lower-margin businesses that management has put on a plan to "reinvigorate growth", and whether a TTM P/E of 411 is an accounting artefact or a warning.

For the acquired businesses the company publishes a "prior-year comparable revenue" convention that adds estimated BD revenue to the prior-year period: on that basis Biosciences earned $396M against $387M (about +2%), Diagnostic Solutions $500M against $498M (about +0.4%), and the two together $896M against $885M, +1%. That is below the mid-single-digit growth management reported for Q2. On the Q3 call the CEO said Diagnostic Solutions was flat as expected, and that excluding two effects — China's DRG hospital-payment reform (fixed payments per diagnosis), which cut growth by about two percentage points, and distributors' inventory being managed to end-customer demand after a build in the prior-year third quarter — that business's underlying growth was approximately 5%.

Instruments versus recurring — and why the disclosed split is misleading. The reported Q3 2026 revenue lines are Product $1,334M (75.5%) and Service $433M (24.5%). Do not read that as "75% instruments." Waters books its chemistry consumables — columns, sample-prep kits, reagents — inside Product, alongside the instruments themselves. True recurring revenue is service plus consumables: in Q3 2025, before the deal, service ($300M) plus Analytical Sciences chemistry consumables ($143M) were 55% of Waters' $800M revenue. What the company did disclose is directionally the key datapoint: within Analytical Sciences, instrument revenue was $282M (+9%) and chemistry consumables $157M (+10%) in Q3 — consumables growing at least as fast as the box they attach to. That is the quality signal. Instruments are lumpy capital equipment sold against customers' capital spending (capex) budgets; consumables and service are annuity-like (steady, repeating income), ride the instruments already in the field, and carry higher margin. The acquired BD businesses are also consumables-heavy — flow cytometry reagents and blood-culture media bottles are classic razor-blade revenue (sell the machine, then the refills) — which argues the recurring share of the combined company is higher, not lower, than legacy Waters. Waters' July 14, 2025 deal announcement said over 70% of the combined company's revenue was expected to be recurring.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

EMPOWER SOFTWARE STANDARD

INSTALLED-BASE SERVICE ANNUITY
VALIDATED-PROTOCOL SWITCHING COST
BLOOD-CULTURE MEDIA RAZOR-BLADE
FOUR-DIVISION CROSS-SELL

The moat is regulatory, not technological. When a pharmaceutical company files a drug, the analytical method used to test it — the specific instrument, the specific column chemistry, the specific software that generated the audit trail — becomes part of the filing. Changing vendor means revalidating the method and, in many cases, amending the filing. That is why Waters' installed base throws off consumables and service revenue for many years and why the company's overall gross margin could sit near 59% (legacy FY2025) without inviting the price war that would destroy a normal capital-equipment business. Empower, the chromatography data system, is the same lock in software form: it is the compliance record, and labs do not rip out their compliance record. The acquired BD businesses import the same structure into a different customer: a hospital microbiology lab that has standardised on BACTEC buys BACTEC bottles, and flow cytometry protocols are validated instrument-by-instrument the same way LC methods are. The strategic logic of the combination is that Waters is buying more of the same kind of annuity, not diversifying away from it.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Integration of businesses that need fixing. Management's own language concedes the point: on the Q1 2026 call the CFO spoke of a "180-day growth revitalization plan", and the CEO listed pricing discipline and a goal to "regain share in Flow research" among its priorities. Doing that while merging two sales organisations, two back-office software systems and two service networks is a multi-year job, and Q3 growth of 1% against the prior-year comparable base is not yet evidence it has been solved.
▸
Debt taken on to do the deal. Notes payable and debt went from $1.407B at 31 Dec 2025 to $5.133B at 3 Oct 2026. Total debt is now $5.13B against $593M of cash and EV of $51.41B. At 36.4× EV/EBITDA the share price leaves little room for a demand shock, and there is no dividend to cut as a buffer.
▸
Instrument demand is customer capex, and customer capex is cyclical. Waters spent 2023 and 2024 in a pharma slump, with customers running down stockpiles (destocking) and freezing capital spending — FY2023 revenue actually fell 0.52% and FY2024 grew 0.07%. Two consecutive flat years in a supposedly defensive franchise is the honest historical answer to "how defensive is this?" Biotech funding and academic budgets remain the weak spots, and the Biosciences division sits directly on them.
▸
Concentration in pharmaceutical end markets and China. Pharma and biopharma are the dominant customer base across Analytical Sciences and Biosciences. Chinese stimulus-driven instrument purchases and the broader US–China trade posture both swing Asia revenue, which was $495M in Q3 2026.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen a lot very quickly relative to its own recent history — "overbought"; below 30 means the reverse — "oversold". It is a measure of momentum stretch, not of value, and a stock can stay overbought for months in a company whose story is genuinely changing.

Five years of going nowhere, then a violent repricing. WAT closed December 2021 at $372.60 and closed 9 November 2026 at $477.09 — a price return of +28.0% over four years and ten months (about 5.2% a year), before any dividend, of which there is none. Over the same window the S&P 500 fund SPY rose from $474.96 to $752.65, a price return of +58.5% (Yahoo Finance and StockAnalysis closes, dividends excluded). A franchise with high gross margins and regulatory lock-in delivered less than half the market's return over the window, and most of that gain arrived in the last twelve months. What happened in between is more interesting than the endpoints.

The 2022–2023 slide was the rate shock plus a pharma capex freeze arriving together. The stock bottomed at $238.53 in October 2023, down 36.0% from the start of the window, and the fundamentals agreed: FY2023 revenue fell 0.52% and FY2024 grew 0.07%. Two flat years. The recovery through 2024 into a $415.48 peak in January 2025 was investors paying more for each dollar of earnings on the expectation that destocking was over; by June 2025 the stock had given back more than a third of that gain, closing at $349.04. Then July 2025: the stock closed the month at $288.76, down 17.3%, in the month of the announcement of a transformational, equity-issuing combination with BD's Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions businesses. Investors tend to dislike being told that a clean, high-margin, focused business is about to issue 39.2% of itself to buy lower-margin businesses. The shares recovered to $403.42 by November 2025, then fell through the February 2026 close to a $297.80 month-end low in March 2026 (intraday low of $282.77 on 13 March).

What has happened since is the interesting part. From the March month-end close the stock has run +60.2% to $477.09, through the 4 August Q2 print (acquired businesses growing mid-single digits, guidance raised) and the 3 November Q3 print (beat, guidance raised again, though the acquired businesses grew 1% against their prior-year comparable). The 6-period monthly RSI is 79.9, above the 70 line that marks a stretched run. The bet in the current price is that the last eight months, not the last five years, is the relevant sample.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Waters bought a second annuity. Flow cytometry reagents and blood-culture media are the same razor-blade economics as chromatography columns, sold to customers with the same validated-protocol switching costs — so the combined company should carry a higher recurring share than legacy Waters, not a lower one. Two quarters of evidence: Q3 organic growth of +10% in constant currency, consumables and instruments both growing in Analytical Sciences, adjusted operating margin held at 30.1%, cost savings set to reach a $200M run-rate more than two years ahead of schedule, and full-year adjusted EPS guidance raised at all three results since the deal closed. At 32.6× guided FY2026 adjusted EPS the stock is not expensive for what it is, and the US pharma reshoring build-out (76 sites tracked, roughly half under construction, about $100B of spending on the CEO's figures) could bring several years of instrument orders with consumables behind them.

Bear Case

Management issued 39.2% of the company and added $3.7B of debt to buy BD's diagnostics and biosciences businesses — and is now telling investors about a "revitalization plan" to fix them, including regaining share in flow cytometry research. In Q3 the acquired businesses grew 1% against their prior-year comparable ($896M vs $885M), and Molecular Diagnostics and Point of Care fell 6%. ROIC is 2.46%. Meanwhile the five-year record of the supposedly defensive core is two consecutive flat revenue years (FY2023 −0.52%, FY2024 +0.07%) and a price return of +28.0% over the window, against +58.5% for the S&P 500 fund. The stock has run 60.2% off its March month-end close, the RSI is 79.9, and a single analyst has a sell rating.

01
Integration execution on businesses that need fixing. The whole thesis rests on Waters running BD's Biosciences and Diagnostics businesses better than BD did. Q2 growth in the mid-single digits and Q3 growth of 1% against the prior-year comparable base, with Diagnostic Solutions flat (about 5% underlying, by the CEO's account), are encouraging and not proof. If Q4 does not deliver the guided 7–9% organic constant-currency growth, the rally since March could reverse quickly.
02
Heavy debt in a cyclical end market. $5.13B of total debt against $593M of cash and $290.81M of TTM free cash flow, in a business whose instrument revenue is customer capital expenditure. The 2023–24 pharma capex freeze showed exactly how flat this company can go; repeating that episode with this balance sheet would be materially worse than it was with the old one.
03
Nobody can actually see the earnings yet. A 411× trailing P/E, a 1.76% net margin, a 1.12% ROE and $0.82 of quarterly GAAP EPS sitting alongside $4.10 of adjusted EPS means investors are relying on a number management defines. Purchase accounting will keep GAAP and adjusted results far apart for years. Any restatement, guidance revision or disclosure change in that gap is a sharp, one-way risk.
Industry Commentary
Critical Constraint
Instrument capex is still the swing factor
Life-science tools spent 2023 and 2024 absorbing a pharmaceutical destocking and capex freeze, and Waters' own results show how complete that was: FY2023 revenue −0.52%, FY2024 +0.07%. Consumables and service held the line; instruments did not. The current cycle looks better — Analytical Sciences instrument revenue +9% in Q3 2026 — but this is the variable that has determined the stock's five-year return and it is set by customers' budget committees, not by Waters. Biotech funding and academic budgets, which drive the newly acquired Biosciences division, remain the softest part of the demand picture.
Emerging Tailwind
US pharmaceutical reshoring
On the Q2 call the CEO said Waters was tracking 76 expansion sites linked to US pharma investment announcements, roughly half under active construction and representing about $100B of capital spending. Every new regulated manufacturing or QC line in the US requires chromatography and mass spectrometry capacity and validated analytical methods — and once a method is validated on a Waters instrument, it stays there. This is the rare tailwind that turns a one-time capital project into years of repeat consumables sales. The timing risk is that announced capex and ordered equipment are not the same thing, and the conversion lag can run years.
Ongoing Headwind → The Framing
Consolidation is the sector's answer, and it has a cost
Waters is the latest of the tools majors to buy scale rather than grow into it, and the Reverse Morris Trust structure was a tax-efficient way for BD to separate these businesses. Bull framing: the recurring-revenue architecture is identical across chromatography, cytometry and microbiology, so scale builds on itself — more instruments in the field, more consumables, more service, more cross-selling, and a cost base set to reach $200M of run-rate savings more than two years ahead of schedule. Bear framing: the combined company has a lower gross margin (49.8% TTM vs 59.3% in FY2025), $5.13B of debt where there was $1.4B, and 65% more shares. Scale purchased that way has to earn its cost of capital before it counts as strategy, and at 2.46% ROIC that is not yet demonstrated.
