01 Company Overview

Waters Corporation builds the analytical instruments that regulated industries use to prove what is actually in a sample — liquid chromatography (LC, which separates a mixture into its components) and mass spectrometry (MS, which identifies and weighs those components), plus the columns, vials and reagents those machines consume, plus the service contracts that keep them running. As of 9 February 2026 it is also a flow cytometry and clinical microbiology company: on that date Becton, Dickinson's Biosciences & Diagnostic Solutions businesses were spun off from BD and combined with Waters in a Reverse Morris Trust. Standalone FY2025 revenue was $3.165B, up 6.99% on FY2024's $2.958B. Management now guides FY2026 reported revenue to $6.415–6.476B. Waters ranks roughly #654 globally by market capitalisation at $39.46B.

Read every year-over-year number on this page twice. The company roughly doubled overnight, and it did so without restating prior periods: Waters' pre-closing shareholders retained 60.8% of the combined company (BD holders took 39.2% on a fully diluted basis), which makes Waters the accounting acquirer, so FY2025 and everything before it is legacy Waters alone. The headline TTM revenue growth of +52.5% and the Q2 2026 reported growth of +113% are not organic growth in any sense — they are the arithmetic of bolting on a business that did not exist inside Waters before February. The honest comparable number is the company's own organic figure: $828M of organic revenue in Q2 2026, +7% reported and +9% in constant currency. The central question of this report is whether the recurring, consumable-and-service half of this franchise — which is what has always justified Waters' premium multiple — survives the dilution of adding a lower-margin, share-losing diagnostics business, and whether a TTM P/E of 181 is an accounting artefact or a warning.

Instruments versus recurring — and why the disclosed split is misleading. The reported Q2 2026 revenue lines are Product $1,220M (74.2%) and Service $425M (25.8%). Do not read that as "74% instruments." Waters books its chemistry consumables — columns, sample-prep kits, reagents — inside Product, alongside the instruments themselves. True recurring revenue is service plus consumables, and legacy Waters has historically run around half of revenue on that basis. A company-wide recurring-revenue percentage was not disclosed in any source fetched for this report, so it is not stated here as a number. What the company did disclose is directionally the key datapoint: within Analytical Sciences, instrument revenue grew 8% in constant currency while chemistry consumables grew double digits — consumables outgrowing the box they attach to. That is the quality signal. Instruments are lumpy capital equipment sold against customer capex budgets; consumables and service are annuity-like, ride the installed base, and carry structurally higher margin. The acquired BD businesses are also consumables-heavy — flow cytometry reagents and blood-culture media bottles are archetypal razor-blade revenue — which argues the recurring share of the combined company is higher, not lower, than legacy Waters. That thesis is currently an inference, not a disclosure.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

The moat is regulatory, not technological. When a pharmaceutical company files a drug, the analytical method used to test it — the specific instrument, the specific column chemistry, the specific software that generated the audit trail — becomes part of the filing. Changing vendor means revalidating the method and, in many cases, amending the filing. That is why Waters' installed base throws off consumables and service revenue for fifteen or twenty years and why instrument gross margins can sit near 59% (legacy FY2025) without inviting the price war that would destroy a normal capital-equipment business. Empower, the chromatography data system, is the same lock in software form: it is the compliance record, and labs do not rip out their compliance record. The acquired BD businesses import the same structure into a different customer: a hospital microbiology lab that has standardised on BACTEC buys BACTEC bottles, and flow cytometry protocols are validated instrument-by-instrument the same way LC methods are. The strategic logic of the combination is that Waters is buying more of the same kind of annuity, not diversifying away from it.

Key Vulnerabilities

▸
Integration of a business that was losing share. Management's own language — a "180-day growth revitalization plan" focused on pricing and "market share recovery" — concedes that the acquired BD assets were underperforming inside BD. Fixing a share-losing diagnostics franchise while simultaneously merging two sales organisations, two ERP estates and two service networks is a multi-year execution problem, and the first full quarter of mid-single-digit growth is not yet evidence it has been solved.
▸
Leverage taken on to do the deal. Notes payable went from $1.407B at 31 Dec 2025 to $5.086B at 4 July 2026. Total debt is now $5.48B against $539M of cash and EV of $44.99B. At 31% EV/EBITDA the balance sheet has no slack for a demand shock, and there is no dividend to cut as a buffer.
▸
Instrument demand is customer capex, and customer capex is cyclical. Waters spent 2023 and 2024 in a pharma destocking and capex freeze — FY2023 revenue actually fell 0.52% and FY2024 grew 0.07%. Two consecutive flat years in a supposedly defensive franchise is the honest historical answer to "how defensive is this?" Biotech funding and academic budgets remain the weak spots, and the Biosciences division sits directly on them.
▸
Concentration in pharmaceutical end markets and China. Pharma and biopharma are the dominant customer base across Analytical Sciences and Biosciences. Chinese stimulus-driven instrument purchases and the broader US–China trade posture both swing Asia revenue, which was $450M in Q2 2026. Tariff exposure on instruments manufactured across borders was not quantified in any source fetched.

RSI compares the size of recent gains to the size of recent losses on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen a lot very quickly relative to its own recent history — "overbought"; below 30 means the reverse — "oversold". It is a measure of momentum stretch, not of value, and a stock can stay overbought for months in a genuine re-rating.

Five years of going nowhere, then a violent repricing. WAT closed October 2021 at $367.55 and closed 10 September 2026 at $401.64 — a total return of roughly +9.3% over five years, before any dividend, of which there is none. That is the whole story in one number, and it is a bad one: a franchise with 59% gross margins and regulatory lock-in delivered less than 2% a year while the S&P 500 compounded at a double-digit clip over the same window. The underperformance is severe and unambiguous. What happened in between is more interesting than the endpoints.

The 2022–2023 de-rating was the rate shock plus a pharma capex freeze arriving together. The stock bottomed at $238.53 in October 2023, down 35.1% from the start of the window, and the fundamentals agreed: FY2023 revenue fell 0.52% and FY2024 grew 0.07%. Two flat years. The recovery through 2024 into a $415.48 peak in January 2025 was a multiple re-rating on the expectation that destocking was over, and it half-reversed when that took longer than hoped. Then July 2025: the stock closed the month at $288.76, down 17.3%, on the announcement of a transformational, equity-issuing combination with BD's Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions businesses. Markets do not like being told that a clean, high-margin, focused compounder is about to issue 39.2% of itself to buy a lower-margin business that was losing share inside its previous owner. That scepticism persisted through the February 2026 close and to a $297.80 low in March 2026 — the deal-doubt trough.

What has happened since is the interesting part. From the March low the stock has run +34.9% to $401.64, with the decisive leg after the 4 August Q2 print showed the acquired businesses growing mid-single digits, cost savings at a $200M run-rate against a $75M target, and every component of guidance raised. The 6-period monthly RSI has been pinned in the upper band through that move — the momentum is real and it is also stretched. Against the S&P 500, the five-year record is dismal but the six-month record is strong; against the NASDAQ-100, which has been the harder benchmark over this window, the five-year gap is wider still. The bet in the current price is that the last six months, not the last five years, is the relevant sample.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Waters bought a second annuity. Flow cytometry reagents and blood-culture media are the same razor-blade economics as chromatography columns, sold to customers with the same validated-protocol switching costs — so the combined company should carry a higher recurring share than legacy Waters, not a lower one. The first full combined quarter beat: organic +9% constant currency, acquired businesses growing mid-single digits, cost savings at a $200M run-rate against a $75M target, and every line of guidance raised. At 27.5× guided FY2026 adjusted EPS the stock is not expensive for what it is, and the 76-site, $100B US pharma reshoring build-out is a multi-year instrument order book that pulls consumables behind it.

Bear Case

Management issued 39.2% of the company and added $3.7B of net new debt to buy a diagnostics business that was losing share inside BD — and is now telling investors about a "revitalization plan" to fix it. Adjusted operating margin fell from 29.1% to 25.0% year over year: the dilution to quality is measurable and already here. ROIC is 3.35%. Meanwhile the five-year record of the supposedly defensive core is two consecutive flat revenue years (FY2023 −0.52%, FY2024 +0.07%) and a total shareholder return of +9.3% over five years. The stock has run 34.9% off its March low on two quarters of evidence, RSI is stretched, and the consensus contains zero sell ratings.

01
Integration execution on a share-losing asset. The whole thesis rests on Waters running BD's Biosciences and Diagnostics businesses better than BD did. Two quarters of mid-single-digit growth is encouraging and is not proof. If Q3 and Q4 do not hold the 8–10% organic constant-currency guidance, the re-rating since March unwinds quickly.
02
Leverage into a cyclical end market. $5.48B of total debt against $539M of cash and $398.81M of TTM free cash flow, in a business whose instrument revenue is customer capital expenditure. The 2023–24 pharma capex freeze showed exactly how flat this company can go; repeating that episode with this balance sheet would be materially worse than it was with the old one.
03
Nobody can actually see the earnings yet. A 181× trailing P/E, a 3.59% net margin, a 1.92% ROE and a −$1.39 quarterly GAAP loss sitting alongside a $3.05 adjusted EPS means investors are underwriting a management-defined number. Purchase accounting will keep GAAP and adjusted results far apart for years. Any restatement, guidance revision or disclosure change in that gap is a sharp, one-way risk.
Industry Commentary
Critical Constraint
Instrument capex is still the swing factor
Life-science tools spent 2023 and 2024 absorbing a pharmaceutical destocking and capex freeze, and Waters' own results show how complete that was: FY2023 revenue −0.52%, FY2024 +0.07%. Consumables and service held the line; instruments did not. The current cycle looks better — Analytical Sciences instrument revenue +8% constant currency in Q2 2026 — but this is the variable that has determined the stock's five-year return and it is set by customers' budget committees, not by Waters. Biotech funding and academic budgets, which drive the newly acquired Biosciences division, remain the softest part of the demand picture.
Emerging Tailwind
US pharmaceutical reshoring
Management says it is tracking 76 announced US expansion sites representing roughly $100B of capital expenditure. Every new regulated manufacturing or QC line in the US requires chromatography and mass spectrometry capacity and validated analytical methods — and once a method is validated on a Waters instrument, it stays there. This is the rare tailwind that converts a one-time capex event into a permanent consumables annuity. The timing risk is that announced capex and ordered equipment are not the same thing, and the conversion lag can run years.
Ongoing Headwind → The Framing
Consolidation is the sector's answer, and it has a cost
Waters is the latest of the tools majors to buy scale rather than grow into it, and the Reverse Morris Trust structure made it tax-efficient for BD to shed a business it could not fix. Bull framing: the recurring-revenue architecture is identical across chromatography, cytometry and microbiology, so scale genuinely compounds — more installed base, more consumables, more service, more cross-sell, and a cost base that has already delivered $200M of run-rate savings against a $75M target. Bear framing: the combined company has a lower gross margin (56.3% TTM vs 59.3% in FY2025), a lower adjusted operating margin (25.0% vs 29.1%), $5.48B of debt where there was $1.4B, and 64% more shares. Scale purchased that way has to earn its cost of capital before it counts as strategy, and at 3.35% ROIC that is not yet demonstrated.
