01 Company Overview

WEC Energy Group is a Milwaukee-based regulated electric and natural gas holding company serving customers across Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan and Minnesota, with roughly 35,200 miles of overhead electric distribution lines and 47,200 miles of gas distribution mains as of December 31, 2025. FY2025 revenue was $9.80B, up 13.96% from $8.60B in FY2024, with net income of $1.56B and diluted EPS of $4.81. The TTM revenue figure through June 2026 is $10.14B, up 8.9%. At $34.3B of market capitalisation the company ranks roughly #738 globally — mid-pack among US regulated electrics, larger than Alliant ($17.4B) but a third the size of Duke ($93.7B).

The central debate here is not whether WEC is a good utility — by the usual tests it plainly is, with a 23rd consecutive annual dividend increase in January 2026 and a beta of 0.46. It is whether the hyperscale data-centre buildout in southeastern Wisconsin turns WEC from a 6.5–7% grower into the 7–8% grower management now guides to through 2030, and whether the $37.5B five-year capital plan that funds it can be financed without an equity issuance that eats the growth it buys. The stock has fallen 10.7% from its April 2026 high of $117.94 even as guidance was reaffirmed — the market is pricing the financing risk, not the demand. Sections 04 and 07 take that apart.

The Wisconsin utilities — principally We Energies (Wisconsin Electric Power) and Wisconsin Public Service — are the business. WEC holds minority interests in transmission companies — chiefly American Transmission Company — which are accounted for on the equity method, so they show up in earnings rather than in revenue. That is why this segment can move the profit line without moving the top line. WEC's Q2 2026 operating income of $432.8M was reported alongside higher equity earnings from transmission affiliates. Transmission is the quietly attractive part of the utility complex right now: FERC-regulated returns, a national interconnection queue backlog, and load growth that cannot be served without new wires. If data-centre demand in the Midwest is real, transmission spending is downstream of it by construction.

Why it matters: equity-method earnings flatter ROE without consolidating the associated debt — worth remembering when reading the 11.99% ROE in Section 04.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
LEGAL MONOPOLY FRANCHISE RATE-BASE COMPOUNDING FORWARD TEST YEARS 23-YEAR DIVIDEND STREAK CONSTRUCTIVE WISCONSIN REGULATOR ANCHOR HYPERSCALE LOAD TRANSMISSION EQUITY STAKES

The moat is statutory, not commercial. WEC's utilities hold exclusive franchises: no competitor can string a second set of wires down the same street, and the barrier to entry is not capital or technology but a legislature. What varies between good and bad utilities is therefore regulatory quality — whether the commission grants forward-looking test years (so the utility earns on capital as it is spent rather than years later), whether it allows riders for specific programmes, and whether it authorises an ROE and equity layer that let the company finance growth without dilution. On both counts WEC currently reads well: Wisconsin is running forward test years for 2027 and 2028, and Illinois just resolved twelve dockets by unanimous settlement. Layered on top is a genuine demand shock — management expects roughly 15% of the asset base to serve very large customers by 2030 — which is the rarest thing in this sector: organic load growth after two decades of flat-to-declining kilowatt-hour demand.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Financing the $37.5B plan without dilution. H1 2026 shows $2.21B of operating cash flow against $2.08B of capex — the plan is running at roughly the level internal cash can cover today, and the plan is scheduled to grow. TTM free cash flow is −$1.37B, total debt is $23.02B against $50M of cash, and debt-to-equity stands at 1.58. Utilities fund shortfalls with debt and equity. Every share issued below the market's implied growth valuation dilutes the 7–8% EPS target this stock is priced on.
02
Customer concentration where there previously was none. If 15% of the asset base serves "very large customers" by 2030, WEC has swapped a diversified residential and commercial base for meaningful counterparty exposure to a handful of hyperscalers. The protection is contractual — minimum take obligations and exit provisions negotiated with those customers — and the specific contract terms, megawatt commitments and counterparty names beyond those disclosed on the earnings call were not verified for this report. A cancelled or deferred campus would leave approved capital without the load that justified it.
03
Interest-rate sensitivity in both directions. A 3.61% dividend yield and a 0.46 beta make WEC a bond proxy. With $23.02B of debt and a capital plan that must be continuously refinanced, higher long-term rates raise the cost of capital faster than rate cases can pass it through, and simultaneously make the dividend less competitive against Treasuries. The stock's 52-week change of −3.25%, against reaffirmed guidance, is largely this.
04
Rate-shock and political risk from the buildout. Large new generation and transmission built for data centres must be allocated between hyperscalers and retail ratepayers. If Wisconsin residential bills rise visibly to serve AI infrastructure, the politics of the 2027–28 rate orders change quickly. Regulatory goodwill is the asset most easily spent and hardest to rebuild, and the gas distribution segments carry the parallel long-run risk of decarbonisation policy stranding assets.

Three distortions are worth naming anyway, none of them one-offs in the accounting sense. First, revenue is not a clean growth signal for this company: FY2023 (−7.34%) and FY2024 (−3.30%) fell while earnings rose, because fuel and purchased-power costs are passed through to customers and therefore inflate and deflate the top line without touching margin. Second, quarterly results are violently seasonal — Q1 2026 earned $2.45 of the year's EPS while Q2 2026 earned $0.91 — so any single quarter read in isolation misleads. Third, WEC guides and is judged on an adjusted GAAP-to-guidance basis that assumes normal weather; the reaffirmed $5.51–$5.61 range is explicitly weather-normalised, so a hot summer or mild winter moves reported EPS away from guidance without anything operational changing.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen on most of the recent periods and is conventionally called "overbought"; below 30 means the opposite. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal — an overbought stock can keep rising and an oversold one can keep falling.

Over the five years from the October 2021 close of $90.06 to the September 10, 2026 close of $105.35, WEC returned +17.0% on price alone — roughly 3.2% compounded. Add back a dividend that ran from $2.71 per share in 2021 to $3.81 annualised today and the total return is materially better, in the region of 35–40%, though an exact total-return figure was not sourced and is deliberately not stated as precise. That shape — modest price appreciation, most of the return arriving as cash — is the honest profile of a regulated utility, and it is what someone buying WEC should expect to own.

The path was not a straight line. The stock spent 2022 as a defensive hiding place while the rest of the market de-rated, peaking at $105.07 in May 2022. It then spent two years being repriced by Treasury yields rather than by earnings, grinding to a five-year low of $78.46 in June 2024 — a 25.4% drawdown from the 2022 high with nothing wrong operationally. The recovery from that trough to $117.94 in April 2026 was +50.3%, and it maps almost exactly onto the emergence of the data-centre load narrative. The most recent five months have given back 10.7% of that, on financing and rate concerns rather than on any guidance change.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

WEC has what almost no US utility has had in twenty years: real organic load growth. A $37.5B five-year capital plan, a Microsoft campus forecast at 2.6 GW by 2030 and a Vantage/Oracle project forecast at 1.3 GW rising toward 3.5 GW convert into rate base, and rate base converts mechanically into earnings. Management guides to 7–8% compound EPS growth through 2030 with acceleration from 2028, and it has just cleared twelve Illinois dockets by unanimous settlement. Buy that at 20.4× with a 3.61% yield backed by 23 consecutive annual increases, and the average street target of $121.62 implies 15.4% upside — with even the lowest published target above today's price.

Bear Case

The growth has to be paid for before it pays. Trailing free cash flow is −$1.37B, total debt is $23.02B against $50M of cash, debt-to-equity is 1.58 and ROIC is 4.27% — thin returns on the total capital base that only become an attractive equity return through leverage. If financing requires meaningful equity issuance, or if the Wisconsin 2027–28 orders authorise a lower ROE or equity layer than assumed, the 7–8% target degrades toward the old 6.5–7%. Meanwhile 5.74% of the float is short, five of the last six analyst actions were cuts, and the stock has fallen 10.7% from its April high on reaffirmed guidance. A 20.4× multiple on a 0.46-beta utility leaves little room for a plan that slips.

01
Financing and dilution risk. A $37.5B plan against −$1.37B TTM free cash flow and $23.02B of existing debt has to be funded from somewhere. Equity issued at today's depressed price would dilute the very EPS growth rate the valuation rests on.
02
Adverse regulatory outcomes. The Wisconsin 2027–28 orders and the Illinois 2027 decision both land by year-end 2026. A below-expectation authorised ROE or equity layer, or disallowance of capital, would reset earnings power for the whole plan period — and the authorised figures were not disclosed on the sources fetched for this report.
03
Hyperscaler load failing to arrive on schedule. The gigawatt figures above are company demand forecasts, not verified contracted commitments. Data-centre plans are revised frequently. Capital approved and built against load that slips leaves WEC with rate base it must justify to a commission and to ratepayers who are already absorbing higher bills.
Industry Commentary
Emerging Tailwind
Midwest hyperscale load
US electricity demand was essentially flat for two decades as efficiency gains offset growth. AI data centres have ended that, and southeastern Wisconsin has become one of the country's more concentrated build zones. WEC's own figures: retail sales +4.2% in Q2 2026, roughly 15% of the asset base serving very large customers by 2030, a Microsoft site forecast at 2.6 GW by 2030 and a Vantage/Oracle project at Port Washington forecast at 1.3 GW with long-term potential to 3.5 GW on about $15B of initial-phase investment through 2028. For a sector whose growth algorithm has been "spend on replacement and hope," this is a different equation.
Critical Constraint
Cost of capital
Utilities are the most rate-sensitive equities in the market from both ends. They carry enormous refinanced debt — WEC has $23.02B, with $19.22B of it long-term as of mid-2026 — and they compete with Treasuries for income buyers at a 3.61% yield. Regulators allow recovery of a cost of debt, but with a lag measured in rate cases, not in weeks. The −3.25% 52-week return on reaffirmed guidance, the 5.74% short float on a 0.46-beta name, and a stock trading $2.40 above its 52-week low all point at the same thing: the market is pricing the funding gap, not the demand.
Bull vs Bear — the frame
Growth you must pre-pay
Both cases accept the same facts. The bull reads $37.5B of approved-and-approvable capital as a contracted earnings stream: rate base times allowed ROE, compounding at 7–8% with acceleration from 2028, bought today at 20.4× and 3.61% while the stock sits near its 52-week low. The bear reads the identical plan as an obligation that must be financed at −$1.37B free cash flow, 1.58 debt-to-equity and 4.27% ROIC, with the terms set by two commissions before year-end. The resolution is not analytical — it is two rate orders and one financing decision, all due within the next four months.
