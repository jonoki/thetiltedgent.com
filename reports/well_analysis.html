01 Company Overview

Welltower is the world's largest owner of senior housing real estate — a portfolio of more than 2,500 seniors and wellness housing communities across the United States, United Kingdom and Canada — and the only healthcare REIT in the S&P 500 with a market capitalization above $100B. Management describes the business as "an operating company in a real estate wrapper," and the description is literal: roughly 78% of FY2025 revenue came from resident fees and services collected at communities Welltower operates through its RIDEA operating structure, not from fixed rent.

FY2025 revenue was $10.838B, up 35.6% YoY from $7.991B in FY2024, and the trailing-twelve-month figure has since reached $12.76B (+37.6%). Normalized FFO per diluted share — the metric that actually matters for a REIT — grew 22.5% to $5.29 in FY2025 from $4.32 in FY2024, and management now guides FY2026 to $6.36–$6.44, a range raised twice this year. At $169.90B, Welltower ranks #74 among US companies and #129 globally by market capitalization.

Welltower's moat is not the buildings — anyone can buy a building. It is the cost of capital advantage compounding against an operating platform. The company carries net debt to adjusted EBITDA of 2.99x against roughly $9.5B of available liquidity, leverage that is low in absolute terms and dramatically low relative to healthcare-REIT peers (Ventas and Healthpeak carry debt-to-equity of 0.85 and 1.09 against Welltower's 0.41). That balance sheet, combined with an equity that trades at a substantial premium to the replacement cost and estimated NAV of its assets, lets Welltower do something competitors structurally cannot: issue equity accretively and buy assets at yields above its blended cost of capital. Share count rose from 665.1M to 718.9M in the year to June 30, 2026 (+8.1%, a −11.09% buyback yield) — and normalized FFO per share still grew 25.0%. That is the arithmetic proof that the issuance is accretive. It funded $15.5B of investments closed or under contract year-to-date 2026, on top of $13.9B of pro-rata gross investments in FY2025.

The second layer is the operating platform itself. By taking property-level P&L through RIDEA rather than collecting rent, Welltower converts a real estate spread business into an operating business with real operating leverage — 9.2% same-store revenue growth becoming 20.5% same-store NOI growth in Q2 2026. Replicating that requires operator relationships, regional density, and a data capability for submarket and rate-setting decisions that a smaller REIT cannot fund. The moat is self-reinforcing: better operating results → higher multiple → cheaper equity → more accretive acquisitions → more scale.

Key Vulnerabilities

1. The moat is the share price. The accretive-issuance flywheel only works while WELL trades at a premium to NAV. If the multiple compresses — because SHOP growth decelerates, because rates back up, or simply because the market re-rates the sector — the acquisition machine stalls, and with it the external growth that supports the multiple. This is a genuinely reflexive vulnerability and it distinguishes Welltower from a business with a product moat. At a trailing GAAP P/E of 106.7 and 36.8x forward P/FFO, very little cushion is priced in.

2. Labour cost reacceleration in senior housing. The entire SHOP margin story is the spread between RevPOR (+5.2% in Q2 2026) and ExpPOR. Wages and agency staffing dominate the expense line. If senior-housing wage growth reaccelerates toward or above rate growth, the 270bps of same-store margin expansion booked in Q4 2025 reverses, and 20% NOI growth becomes single-digit growth without occupancy doing anything at all. Welltower discloses RevPOR consistently; ExpPOR is not separately disclosed in the quarterly release, which makes the spread harder to monitor from the outside than it should be.

3. Occupancy runway is finite by construction. Same-store NOI growth of 20.5% is an occupancy-recovery number, not a steady-state number. NIC MAP puts industry occupancy at 89.5% in Q1 2026 — the 19th consecutive quarterly increase — with independent living above 91%. Physically, occupancy cannot exceed roughly the mid-90s. Every 100bps of recovery captured is 100bps that cannot be captured again, and when the occupancy tailwind exhausts, SHOP growth reverts to rate growth minus expense growth, plausibly a mid-single-digit number. The market is currently capitalising the 20% number.

4. Operator and reimbursement concentration. Long-Term/Post-Acute Care is 13.3% of same-store NOI and rides on operator tenants whose revenue is set by Medicare and Medicaid. Welltower does not control those rates. A reimbursement squeeze degrades rent coverage, forces operator transitions, and generates transition costs and NOI gaps while assets are re-tenanted — the mechanism that has repeatedly produced one-off charges across the healthcare REIT sector.

Notable One-Off Items

Welltower's GAAP earnings contain materially distorting non-recurring items, which is precisely why normalized FFO is the right lens.

Gains on sales of properties — $1.449B in FY2025 (of which $881M landed in Q4 2025 alone). This single line is larger than FY2025 GAAP net income of $936.85M. Put differently: Welltower's reported GAAP profit in 2025 was more than fully explained by selling buildings, not by operating them. It also explains the counterintuitive FY2025 EPS decline to $1.39 from $1.57 — the prior year's gain mix differed — even as normalized FFO per share rose 22.5%. Normalized FFO excludes these gains by construction.

Impairments of $121.3M in FY2025. Modest against a $187.57B enterprise value, but a recurring feature of a portfolio being actively pruned: assets marked down ahead of disposition or operator transition.

Operator transition costs. When Welltower moves a property from one operator to another — or converts a triple-net asset into the SHOP portfolio — there is a period of elevated cost and depressed NOI while the new operator ramps. These costs are one of the standard add-backs in the normalized FFO bridge and are a structural, ongoing consequence of a strategy built on RIDEA conversions.

Guidance geometry worth noting. At Q2 2026 management raised normalized FFO guidance to $6.36–$6.44 from $6.21–$6.35 while simultaneously lowering GAAP net income per share guidance to $3.11–$3.19 from $3.24–$3.38. The divergence is entirely a gains-and-depreciation artifact and is a clean illustration of why the two measures should not be conflated. Capital structure note: the −11.09% buyback yield is net share issuance, not a buyback — Welltower funds acquisitions by issuing equity.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

An occupancy recovery with years left to run, layered on a rate-growth engine, funded by the cheapest cost of capital in the sector. SHOP same-store NOI grew 20.5% in Q2 2026 on 330bps of occupancy gain and 5.2% RevPOR — and NIC MAP shows industry inventory growth at a record-low 0.4% with units under construction at their lowest since 2012, meaning the supply response that would normally cap this cycle simply is not coming. Nearly 60% of NIC's 140 tracked markets have no development underway at all. Welltower converts that into per-share value through $15.5B of YTD investments funded at 2.99x leverage with $9.5B of liquidity, and normalized FFO per share has compounded from $4.32 to a guided $6.40 midpoint in two years — with the dividend covered at 53% of FFO and raised 15% in the last quarter.

Bear Case

You are paying 36.8x forward FFO and 106.7x GAAP earnings for a growth rate that is arithmetically self-limiting. Occupancy recovery cannot continue past physical capacity — industry occupancy is already 89.5% and independent living is above 91% — and when it stops, SHOP growth reverts from 20% toward mid-single digits. The implied cap rate of ~3.2% (est.) leaves almost no spread over cost of capital, so a rise in long rates hits both the acquisition math and the multiple simultaneously; the 2022 drawdown of 35.6% is the live precedent. The whole flywheel depends on issuing equity above NAV — an 8.1% share-count increase in one year is not a bug but it is a dependency — and if the multiple compresses, external growth stops with it. Meanwhile GAAP net income of $936.85M in FY2025 was more than fully accounted for by $1.449B of property sale gains.

  Industry Commentary
The 80+ Demographic Wave Meets a Construction Pipeline That Isn't There
EMERGING TAILWIND
This is the single most important fact about Welltower's next five years, and it is a supply story more than a demand story. NIC MAP reports that units under construction in Primary Markets fell to roughly 17,000 as of 3Q25 — the lowest level since 2012. Fewer than 1,500 new units were added in that quarter, 0.7% year-over-year growth, the lowest on record since NIC began tracking supply in 2006. By Q1 2026 year-over-year inventory growth had fallen further to 0.4%, and nearly 60% of the 140 markets NIC tracks have no new senior housing development underway at all. Meanwhile occupancy has risen for 19 consecutive quarters to 89.5%, with ten markets now above 90% versus seven a quarter earlier, and same-store asking rents grew 4.3% annually in 3Q25. NIC's framing is blunt: the industry would need to build at nearly twice its maximum historical pace every year for two decades just to hold 90% occupancy, implying an estimated $800B supply gap through 2041. Occupancy is expected to approach 90% in 2026 and potentially stabilise near 93% by 2028. The eventual supply response is the thing to watch — high construction costs and low valuations relative to replacement cost are what suppress starts, and both can change — but new senior housing takes years from start to stabilisation, so the near-term shortage is effectively locked in.
Senior Housing Labour Costs — Both the Constraint on Supply and the Threat to Margin
ONGOING HEADWIND
Senior housing is a staffing business wearing a real estate costume. NIC explicitly attributes the development slowdown to "elevated costs for labor and materials, and property valuation dynamics" — noting that the constraint is on the supply side, not from any shortage of demand. That same labour inflation cuts the other way at the property level. Welltower's SHOP margin expansion — roughly 270bps of same-store margin gain in Q4 2025 — exists only because expense per occupied room grew more slowly than the 4.7%–5.2% RevPOR increases. Because 73.1% of same-store NOI sits in operating (RIDEA) assets rather than triple-net leases, Welltower absorbs 100% of any wage reacceleration; a triple-net landlord like National Health Investors does not. The asymmetry that makes SHOP so powerful on the way up is precisely what makes it exposed on the way down, and Welltower does not separately disclose ExpPOR in its quarterly release — meaning the key spread must be inferred from the gap between the 9.2% same-store revenue growth and the 20.5% same-store NOI growth.
Interest Rates, Cost of Capital, and the Reflexive Flywheel — Bull vs Bear
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Everything above resolves into one question: does the cost of capital advantage hold? The bull framing: Welltower's 2.99x net debt to adjusted EBITDA, ~$9.5B of liquidity, and premium-to-NAV equity let it buy assets no leveraged private buyer can compete for. It raised over $23B of capital in FY2025 and has $15.5B of investments closed or under contract in 2026 YTD. Share count rose 8.1% year-over-year and normalized FFO per share still grew 25.0% — accretion, demonstrated. Falling long rates would widen the acquisition spread and lift the multiple at the same time. The bear framing: an implied cap rate of roughly 3.2% (est.) against a $187.57B enterprise value means the spread over cost of capital is historically thin, and the flywheel is reflexive — it depends on a share price that depends on the flywheel. Rising long rates would compress the acquisition spread, halt equity issuance at accretive levels, and compress a 36.8x forward P/FFO multiple simultaneously. In 2022 that combination took the stock from $86.84 to $55.95 in seven months while the underlying occupancy recovery was improving throughout. The operating business and the stock are not the same instrument, and the last five years have demonstrated both directions.
