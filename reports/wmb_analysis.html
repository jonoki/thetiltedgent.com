01 Company Overview

Williams is not an energy producer. It is a toll road for natural gas — roughly 33,000 miles of pipe, gathering systems, processing plants and storage, anchored by the Transcontinental Gas Pipe Line ("Transco"), the artery that runs from South Texas up the Eastern Seaboard to New York. It gets paid a fee for moving and treating molecules, largely under long-term, take-or-pay and reservation-charge contracts, so its economics are driven by contracted capacity and volume, not by the price of the commodity flowing through. That is why the company guides on adjusted EBITDA and AFFO, not on earnings per share, and why the metrics table below marks two of the standard ratios as not meaningful. FY2025 revenue was $11.83B, up 10.0% on FY2024's $10.75B; TTM revenue through Q2 2026 is $12.32B, up 8.4%. Market cap of $90.70B makes it roughly the 269th most valuable listed company in the world.

The debate on this page is power. For a decade Williams was valued as a low-growth, high-yield, gas-levered utility proxy. Since 2024 it has been re-rated on a different story: gas-fired generation built behind the meter for data centres. In Q2 2026 the first of those projects, Socrates Phase 1, delivered 200 megawatts of utility-scale power to a single hyperscaler customer, on time and on budget; management raised full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $8.3–$8.5B and lifted the long-term growth target to "11-plus percent compound annual growth through 2030." The stock is up 28.4% over twelve months and trades at 29.5x trailing earnings — a premium to every midstream peer in this report. The question is whether the power business is contracted cash flow worth a utility-plus multiple, or a pipeline of announcements that has been capitalised in advance. Section 04 and the industry commentary take that apart, with a clear line drawn between what Williams has actually signed and what it has merely queued.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
TRANSCO CORRIDOR FERC RATE BASE UNREPLICABLE RIGHTS-OF-WAY TAKE-OR-PAY CONTRACTS 53 YEARS OF DIVIDENDS INTERCONNECT DENSITY BROWNFIELD EXPANSION

The moat is a legal and physical one, and it is close to absolute. Nobody is going to build a second pipeline from Louisiana to New York City. Transco's route was assembled over decades of easements, condemnation and FERC certificates through the most litigious, most densely populated corridor in the country, and the permitting record of the last fifteen years — projects cancelled in New York and New Jersey after years of process — is the best evidence that the barrier is real. That barrier cuts both ways for the incumbent: it makes Williams' existing pipe scarce, and it makes Williams' own expansions slow. The company's answer is brownfield: compression, looping and laterals off an existing right-of-way, which is why the expansion backlog reads as a list of incremental Bcf/d added to a line that already exists rather than new greenfield routes. Layer on contracts under which shippers pay for capacity whether or not they use it, and you get a business whose adjusted EBITDA has compounded through a period that included a 12.3% revenue decline in FY2023 — revenue fell, cash flow did not break. The behind-the-meter power business is an attempt to extend that moat sideways: Williams already owns the gas, the pipe and 300+ turbines of operating experience, so it can deliver power to a data centre faster than the grid can offer an interconnection. Whether that becomes a durable franchise or a merchant-power business with a pipeline attached is the open question.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Capital intensity has outrun free cash flow. Growth capex guidance for 2026 is $7.3–$7.9B against $8.3–$8.5B of adjusted EBITDA. Trailing free cash flow is negative $174M. Every dollar of the power build is funded from operating cash flow, debt, the Blackstone JV, or equity — and the Momentum deal already issues about $2.0B of stock. A business spending nearly its entire EBITDA on growth is not the low-risk toll road its multiple implies.
02
Counterparty concentration in the new business. Socrates serves a single hyperscaler. Management said on the call that contract terms are "expected to extend as the business model matures" — which is a candid way of saying the tenor is shorter than a pipeline contract. A power asset built for one customer, on terms shorter than its depreciable life, carries re-contracting risk that a Transco reservation charge does not.
03
Permitting and state-level veto. Northeast Supply Enhancement and Power Express run through jurisdictions with a documented history of blocking gas infrastructure. In-service dates of Q4 2027 and Q3 2030 are targets, not certainties, and the growth algorithm to 2030 assumes they land.
04
Leverage is at the ceiling of the plan. Debt-to-adjusted EBITDA is 3.67x, guided to roughly 3.9x at year-end 2026 against a self-imposed 4.0x ceiling. Management describes "in excess of another $2 billion" of incremental capacity. That is a real constraint on how much of the 6+ GW backlog can be converted before the balance sheet, the JV structure or the share count has to do more work.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

An irreplaceable pipeline corridor whose fee-based cash flows survived a 12.3% revenue decline without breaking, now attached to the fastest-growing source of electricity demand in a generation. Guidance is $8.3–$8.5B of adjusted EBITDA for 2026 with an 11%+ CAGR target through 2030, dividend coverage of 2.26x, leverage falling to 3.67x, and $6.75B of contracted transmission spend behind 16 projects with in-service dates between 2027 and 2030. Socrates Phase 1 proved Williams can deliver 200 MW behind the meter in about 14 months — faster than a grid interconnection — and Blackstone funded $5.34B of the build at a 6.35% cost of equity while Williams kept 51% and control. If even a fraction of the 6+ GW backlog converts, the 2030 algorithm is conservative.

Bear Case

You are paying 29.5x trailing earnings and 17.25x EV/EBITDA — a large premium to every peer — for a company with negative free cash flow, growth capex of $7.3–$7.9B against $8.3–$8.5B of EBITDA, and leverage guided to 3.9x against its own 4.0x ceiling. The power business that justifies the premium has one project in service, one customer, and no disclosed EBITDA contribution; the 6+ GW is a backlog, not a contract book. Two of the biggest transmission projects run through jurisdictions with a history of blocking gas infrastructure, and the longest-dated one does not come online until 2030. Strip the power option and you have a well-run pipeline growing mid-single digits, on which a 5% peer yield would imply a materially lower price.

Permitting and interest rates. Northeast Supply Enhancement (Q4 2027) and Power Express (Q3 2030) carry real state-level veto risk, and a 3.9x-levered, $30.8B-debt balance sheet re-funding into a higher-rate curve compresses the spread between project returns and cost of capital — the exact spread on which a 7.95% ROIC business creates or destroys value.
Industry Commentary
Data-centre power demand is the whole re-rating
EMERGING TAILWIND
Grid interconnection queues in PJM and the Southeast are measured in years, and hyperscalers cannot wait. That is the arbitrage Williams is monetising: it already owns gas supply, pipe and 300+ operating turbines, so it can build generation on the customer's site and skip the queue entirely. Company slides put data-centre electricity demand growth at 68% through 2035 and Williams' own queued gas demand across power, LNG and industrial at 14.3 Bcf/d across 30+ potential projects worth roughly $15.5B between 2027 and 2033. Against that opportunity set, what is actually in service today is 200 MW. The distance between those two numbers is the investment case and the investment risk at once.
The permitting bottleneck cuts both ways
ONGOING HEADWIND
The reason Transco is worth what it is worth is that nobody can build a competing route — and the reason Williams' growth is slow, capital-hungry and back-loaded is the same fact. The near-term backlog is deliberately brownfield: Southeast Supply Enhancement adds 1,597 MMcf/d in Q3 2027 and Northeast Supply Enhancement 400 MMcf/d in Q4 2027 by compressing and looping an existing corridor rather than opening a new one. Power Express, at 800 MMcf/d, does not arrive until Q3 2030. Investors capitalising an 11%+ CAGR to 2030 today are underwriting a permitting outcome they will not observe for four years.
The capital cycle: what bulls and bears are really arguing about
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Both sides agree on the facts. Williams will spend roughly $7.3–$7.9B of growth capital in 2026 against $8.3–$8.5B of adjusted EBITDA, has spent $9.6B on power projects in eighteen months, runs negative free cash flow, and has brought in Blackstone for $5.34B and issued ~$2.0B of equity for Momentum — all inside a 4.0x leverage ceiling with about $2B of headroom left. The bull reads that as a company with more high-return projects than it can fund, deploying third-party capital at a 6.35% cost of equity while keeping 51% and the $900M promote. The bear reads it as a 7.95%-ROIC business levering into a demand forecast, where a single re-contracting failure or permitting denial turns a growth premium into a yield-support argument at a 2.8% yield that offers none. The tiebreaker is not opinion: it is whether the next four quarters produce contracted, quantified power EBITDA. Until they do, the honest label for the premium in this stock is "option value."
