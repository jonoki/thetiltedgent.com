01 Company Overview

Williams is not an energy producer. It is a toll road for natural gas — roughly 33,000 miles of pipe, gathering systems, processing plants and storage, anchored by the Transcontinental Gas Pipe Line ("Transco"), the artery that runs from South Texas up the Eastern Seaboard to New York. It gets paid a fee for moving and treating molecules, largely under long-term contracts that charge customers for reserved capacity whether or not they use it (take-or-pay and reservation charges), so its economics are driven by contracted capacity and volume, not by the price of the commodity flowing through. That is why the company guides on adjusted EBITDA and AFFO, not on earnings per share, and why the metrics table below marks two of the standard ratios as not meaningful. FY2025 revenue was $11.83B, up 10.0% on FY2024's $10.75B; TTM revenue through Q3 2026 is $12.68B, up 9.5%. Market cap of $83.08B ranks it #157 in the S&P 500.

The debate on this page is power. For a decade Williams was priced as a slow-growing, high-yield, gas-sensitive utility look-alike. Since 2024 the market has priced it on a different story: gas-fired power plants built next to data centres, on the customer's side of the meter. The first of those projects, Socrates, is finishing ahead of schedule: phase one entered service in Q2 2026, and on the Nov 3 call CEO Chad Zamarin said the second and final phase would go into service later that week and that Williams expects to announce one to two more power projects by year's end. Management kept 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance at $8.3–$8.5B, now “tracking toward the upper half” of that range, and kept its goal of 11% or more adjusted EBITDA growth a year through 2030. Momentum Midstream, the gas-gathering purchase valued at up to $5.5B when announced in August, closed on Sep 3, 2026. The shares are up 11.5% over twelve months, but fell 11.3% between the Nov 3 and Nov 5 closes ($72.47 to $64.25) before recovering to $66.45, and trade at 26.2x trailing earnings — still above every midstream peer in this report. The question is whether the power business is contracted cash flow worth a utility-plus price, or a pipeline of announcements that has been paid for in advance. Section 04 and the industry commentary take that apart, with a clear line drawn between what Williams has actually signed and what it has merely queued.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
TRANSCO CORRIDOR FERC RATE BASE UNREPLICABLE RIGHTS-OF-WAY TAKE-OR-PAY CONTRACTS DIVIDENDS SINCE 1974 INTERCONNECT DENSITY BROWNFIELD EXPANSION

The moat is a legal and physical one, and it is close to absolute. Nobody is going to build a second pipeline from Louisiana to New York City. Transco's route was assembled over decades of easements, condemnation and FERC certificates through the most litigious, most densely populated corridor in the country, and the permitting record of the last fifteen years — projects cancelled in New York and New Jersey after years of process — is the best evidence that the barrier is real. That barrier cuts both ways for the incumbent: it makes Williams' existing pipe scarce, and it makes Williams' own expansions slow. The company's answer is brownfield: compression, looping and laterals off an existing right-of-way, which is why the expansion backlog reads as a list of incremental Bcf/d added to a line that already exists rather than new greenfield routes. Layer on contracts under which shippers pay for capacity whether or not they use it, and you get a business whose adjusted EBITDA kept growing through a period that included a 12.3% revenue decline in FY2023 — revenue fell, cash flow did not break. The behind-the-meter power business is an attempt to extend that moat sideways: Williams already owns the gas, the pipe and 300+ turbines of operating experience, so it can deliver power to a data centre faster than the grid can offer an interconnection. Whether that becomes a durable franchise or a merchant-power business with a pipeline attached is the open question.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Capital intensity has outrun free cash flow. Growth capex guidance for 2026 is $7.3–$7.9B against $8.3–$8.5B of adjusted EBITDA. Trailing free cash flow is negative $1.60B. Every dollar of the power build is funded from operating cash flow, debt, the Blackstone JV, or equity — and the Momentum deal, now closed, included about $2.0B of Williams stock as announced. A business spending nearly its entire EBITDA on growth is not the low-risk toll road its multiple implies.
02
Counterparty concentration in the new business. Socrates serves a single hyperscaler. CEO Chad Zamarin said on the Q2 call that “you will continue to see contract terms extend in duration” and on the Nov 3 call that “we also expect contracts to significantly extend in term length”, which implies today's terms are shorter than they will be. A power asset built for one customer carries the risk that the customer does not renew, a risk a Transco reservation charge does not.
03
Permitting and state-level veto. Northeast Supply Enhancement and Power Express run through jurisdictions with a documented history of blocking gas infrastructure. In-service dates of Q4 2027 and Q3 2030 are targets, not certainties, and the growth plan to 2030 assumes they land.
04
Leverage is close to the plan's ceiling. Debt-to-adjusted EBITDA was 3.80x at the end of Q3 (3.67x at Q2), and CFO John Porter said on the Nov 3 call he would expect year-end leverage “to be around 3.75x, reflecting the Momentum acquisition on a full year run rate basis” — against a self-imposed 4.0x ceiling. On the Q2 call management described “in excess of another $2 billion” of incremental capacity. That is a real constraint on how much of the 6+ GW backlog can be converted before the balance sheet, the JV structure or the share count has to do more work.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

An irreplaceable pipeline corridor whose fee-based cash flows survived a 12.3% revenue decline without breaking, now attached to the fastest-growing source of electricity demand in a generation. Guidance is $8.3–$8.5B of adjusted EBITDA for 2026 with a target of 11%+ growth a year through 2030, dividend coverage of 2.42x, leverage of 3.80x after Momentum closed and about 3.75x expected by year-end counting a full year of Momentum, and $6.75B of contracted transmission spend behind 16 projects with in-service dates between 2027 and 2030. Socrates Phase 1 proved Williams can deliver 200 MW behind the meter in about 14 months — faster than a grid interconnection — and Blackstone-led investors committed $5.34B at a capped 6.35% cost of equity while Williams kept 51% and control. If even a fraction of the 6+ GW backlog converts, the 2030 target is conservative.

Bear Case

You are paying 26.2x trailing earnings and 16.4x EV/EBITDA — above every peer here, though Enbridge comes close at 23.2x and 15.7x — for a company with negative free cash flow, growth capex of $7.3–$7.9B against $8.3–$8.5B of EBITDA, and leverage of 3.80x against its own 4.0x ceiling. The power business that justifies the premium has Socrates in service for a single customer, and no disclosed EBITDA contribution; the 6+ GW is a backlog, not a contract book. Two of the biggest transmission projects run through jurisdictions with a history of blocking gas infrastructure, and the longest-dated one does not come online until 2030. Strip the power option and you have a well-run pipeline growing mid-single digits, on which a 5% peer yield would imply a materially lower price.

01
The power backlog does not convert on the disclosed economics. Neither the Q2 nor the Q3 release and call put a figure on contracted EBITDA from the Power Innovation projects. The market is paying for a growth rate, and if the one to two additional projects management expects to announce by year-end slip or price worse than assumed, the multiple has no support beneath it.
02
Funding and dilution. $7.3–$7.9B of growth capex, negative FCF, an $8.4B EBITDA base and a 4.0x leverage ceiling leave roughly $2B of stated headroom. Momentum's ~$2.0B of stock is already issued, and those 26.87M shares are registered for resale by the former owners. Further acceleration means more JV structures, more stock, or a slower build.
03
Permitting and interest rates. Northeast Supply Enhancement (Q4 2027) and Power Express (Q3 2030) carry real state-level veto risk, and refinancing $33.9B of debt, at 3.8x adjusted EBITDA, into higher rates would narrow the gap between project returns and the cost of capital — the gap that decides whether a business earning a 7.22% return on capital creates or destroys value.
Industry Commentary
Data-centre power demand is the whole repricing
EMERGING TAILWIND
Grid interconnection queues in PJM and the Southeast are measured in years, and hyperscalers cannot wait. That is the gap Williams is charging for: it already owns gas supply, pipe and 300+ operating turbines, so it can build generation on the customer's site and skip the queue entirely. Company slides put data-centre electricity demand growth at 68% through 2035 and Williams' own queued gas demand across power, LNG and industrial at 14.3 Bcf/d across 30+ potential projects worth roughly $15.5B between 2027 and 2033. Against that opportunity set, what Williams reports in service today is Socrates, whose first phase delivered 200 MW. The distance between those two numbers is the investment case and the investment risk at once.
The permitting bottleneck cuts both ways
ONGOING HEADWIND
The reason Transco is worth what it is worth is that nobody can build a competing route — and the reason Williams' growth is slow, capital-hungry and back-loaded is the same fact. The near-term backlog is deliberately brownfield: Southeast Supply Enhancement adds 1,597 MMcf/d in Q3 2027 and Northeast Supply Enhancement 400 MMcf/d in Q4 2027 by compressing and looping an existing corridor rather than opening a new one. Power Express, at 800 MMcf/d, does not arrive until Q3 2030. Investors paying today for 11%+ yearly growth to 2030 are betting on a permitting outcome they will not see for four years.
The capital cycle: what bulls and bears are really arguing about
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Both sides agree on the facts. Williams will spend roughly $7.3–$7.9B of growth capital in 2026 against $8.3–$8.5B of adjusted EBITDA, has announced about $9.6B of power projects in eighteen months, runs negative free cash flow, and has brought in Blackstone for $5.34B and included ~$2.0B of stock in the price of Momentum — all inside a 4.0x leverage ceiling with leverage at 3.80x. The bull reads that as a company with more high-return projects than it can fund, using outside investors' money at a capped 6.35% cost of equity while keeping 51% and about $0.9B of additional payment. The bear reads it as a business earning 7.22% on its capital borrowing against a demand forecast, where one customer failing to renew or one permitting denial leaves the stock resting on a 3.2% dividend yield that offers little support. The tiebreaker is not opinion: it is whether the next four quarters produce contracted, quantified power EBITDA. Until they do, the honest label for the premium in this stock is "option value."
