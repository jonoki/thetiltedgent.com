01 Company Overview

Walmart is the world's largest company by revenue — $713.2B in total revenue for fiscal 2026 (ended January 31, 2026), up +4.7% YoY — operating roughly 10,750 stores and clubs across 19 countries plus a rapidly scaling e-commerce and marketplace operation. Once a pure brick-and-mortar discounter, Walmart now generates about 24% of net sales from e-commerce (Q3 FY27; global e-commerce grew +24% in FY26) and is deliberately shifting its profit mix toward higher-margin advertising, membership, and marketplace businesses — a transformation investors have rewarded with a share price, relative to earnings, more typical of a tech company. In December 2025 the company moved its listing from the NYSE to Nasdaq and joined the Nasdaq-100 index on January 20, 2026. The July quarter (Q2 FY27) leaned on roughly $2.9B of tariff refunds that management handed back to shoppers as lower prices; the October quarter (Q3 FY27, reported Nov 19, 2026) beat the company’s own guidance anyway: revenue of $188.8B (+5.2%), adjusted EPS of $0.65 against a $0.62–$0.64 guide, and adjusted operating income +5.5% in constant currency (without exchange-rate swings) against 2–4% guided. U.S. comparable sales (growth at stores and websites open at least a year) picked up to +2.9% from +2.6%, U.S. e-commerce grew +23% and U.S. advertising +37%, and management raised its full-year adjusted EPS outlook to $2.84–$2.89. GAAP EPS, which includes swings in the value of Walmart’s stakes in other companies, fell to $0.62 from $0.77. The shares had already run up before the report — +18.9% in October as investors moved money out of tech and into consumer staples (MarketBeat; 24/7 Wall St.) — and they barely moved on it: −1.0% on the day, +1.5% the next, to $127.95.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
Sources of Moat
Scale Purchasing Power
Store + Supply Chain Density
EDLP Price Brand
Omnichannel Flywheel
Walmart+ / Membership Ecosystem
Retail Media Data (Walmart Connect)
Automation & AI Logistics

Walmart's moat starts with scale nobody else in physical retail can match: ~$713B of revenue buys the lowest cost of goods in the industry, funding the "Everyday Low Prices" brand promise that keeps 90%+ of Americans shopping there annually. The genuinely new development is that its ~4,600 US stores — once viewed as a liability versus Amazon — now double as local delivery hubs: same-day delivery now reaches 95% of US households, US delivery grew +45% in the April 2026 quarter, and automated distribution centers (via its Symbotic partnership) are cutting the cost of each order. Each layer feeds the next: traffic generates first-party purchase data, data powers Walmart Connect advertising (+41% US in Q4 FY26 and +37% in Q3 FY27, at very high incremental margins), and advertising profits fund further price investment — a flywheel that is structurally hard for pure-play grocers or pure-play e-commerce to replicate.

▸
Amazon and the e-commerce ceiling. Amazon still commands ~40% of US e-commerce versus Walmart's ~7-9%, wins decisively with higher-income households, and its logistics + Prime + AWS-funded flexibility means Walmart is fighting an entrenched leader in the channel where all incremental retail growth occurs.
▸
Structurally thin margins. A ~2.8% net margin leaves little room for error: tariff absorption, wage inflation (average US hourly rates now well above $18), or a grocery price war with Aldi and Costco can each swing earnings materially. Q2 FY27 showed the flip side too: about 7.5 percentage points of operating-income growth came from tariff refunds, and management is spending them on price rather than banking them. The premium valuation assumes margin expansion continues uninterrupted.
▸
Regulatory & policy exposure. Roughly one-third of US cost of goods is imported (China, Mexico, Vietnam), making Walmart a headline tariff target; pharmacy reimbursement legislation is already costing about 1 percentage point of comparable-sales growth; and its size invites antitrust, labor, and drug-pricing scrutiny.
▸
Disruption of the shopping interface. If agentic AI shopping assistants (including third-party agents) become the default purchase interface, the store visit and app habit that anchor Walmart's flywheel could be bypassed. Walmart is investing aggressively (Sparky, "super agents") but the interface shift is a genuine wildcard.

From a split-adjusted $46.88 at the end of November 2021 to $127.95 at the Nov 20, 2026 close, WMT has returned +173% in price terms (dividends not included) over five years — an extraordinary run for a mega-cap staples retailer, and the character of the move changed completely mid-stream. From 2021 through 2023 the stock chopped sideways in a $40–$55 band, including a brutal two-day collapse in May 2022 when inventory gluts and freight costs crushed margins. Starting in early 2024 — catalyzed by the 3-for-1 split, the e-commerce profitability inflection, and the advertising narrative — the stock climbed from a $55.08 month-end close in January 2024 to a spring 2026 peak ($135.16 intraday; April closed at $131.93). Then two earnings prints did the damage: the May Q1 report (−7.3% on the day) and the August Q2 report (−9.15% on the day), each a beat the market read as fragile or borrowed, taking September to a $103.92 close, the lowest month-end since October 2025. 08 Summary & Key Risks

▲ Bull Case

Q3 answered the question the August print left open. With the tariff-refund money spent on 11,000+ price rollbacks, the business still beat its own guide: adjusted operating income +5.5% in constant currency against 2–4% guided, U.S. comparable sales back up to +2.9%, U.S. e-commerce +23%, U.S. advertising +37% and Sam’s Club comparable sales +4.9%. Management reported strong share gains in grocery and raised the full year again, to $2.84–$2.89 of adjusted EPS. Price cuts that bring in shoppers are the scale advantage that takes share from weaker retailers. Flipkart’s sale moving into Q4 should add back what it took off Q3, every post-results target listed in Section 05 sits above the share price ($130–$149), and the stock is still 5.3% below its May high.

▼ Bear Case

You are now paying ~49x trailing GAAP earnings and ~44x this year’s (FY27) consensus for a business with a 2.8% net margin. GAAP EPS fell to $0.62 from $0.77, global advertising growth slowed to 25% from 38% in Q2 (Flipkart’s timing explains part of it), Sam’s Club operating income fell 15.8%, and management expects the new Walmart+ and Sam’s Club Plus bundle to weigh on operating-income growth until Q3 FY28. A beat and a raise moved the stock less than 1% over two sessions ($127.36 → $127.95): at this price the market already expects good quarters, the $135.90 average analyst target is only about 6% above the price, and money that came in because investors were leaving tech can leave the same way.

01
Valuation. At ~49x trailing earnings, the biggest single risk is the price paid, not the business: if investors paid 30x today’s $2.62 TTM EPS instead, the stock would be about $79, some 39% below $127.95.
02
Tariff and policy margin squeeze. With ~1/3 of US cost of goods imported and pharmacy legislation already costing about 1 percentage point of comparable-sales growth, an escalation in trade policy or drug-pricing rules could stall the margin-expansion story the valuation depends on. The Q2 tariff refunds cut the other way — a one-off tailwind that has already been spent on price.
03
Amazon and agentic-commerce disruption. Amazon's e-commerce lead remains ~5x Walmart's share, and a shift to AI-agent-mediated shopping could neutralize Walmart's store-proximity and app-habit advantages faster than its own AI stack compensates.
  Industry Commentary: Retail Macro in November 2026
The Tariff Tax on Retail
ONGOING HEADWIND
The 2025–26 tariff regime remains the sector's dominant cost variable: general-merchandise import costs are up meaningfully, and retailers face a choice between eating margin and raising shelf prices into a value-sensitive consumer. Walmart imports roughly a third of its US cost of goods (heavily China, Mexico, Vietnam) but its grocery weighting (~60% of US sales, mostly domestic) and supplier leverage make it structurally the best-insulated large retailer — Target and mid-tier apparel/home players are far more exposed. The risk is political as much as financial: Walmart's 2025 comments about tariff-driven price increases drew direct White House pushback, and pricing decisions are now front-page news. In Q2 FY27 the regime produced a rare windfall: Walmart received roughly $2.9B of tariff refunds and chose to reinvest most of it in lower prices rather than keep it as profit.
The Value-Seeking Consumer & Trade-Down
EMERGING TAILWIND
The US consumer is bifurcated: lower-income households are stretched (elevated grocery prices, resumed loan payments, softening job market) while upper-income households keep spending — and both patterns favor Walmart. Households earning $100K+ have been the company's fastest-growing cohort for over two years, drawn by delivery convenience and Walmart+, while budget pressure pushes everyone else toward EDLP. Q1 FY27 transaction growth was the strongest in six quarters, and in Q3 U.S. transactions rose 1.5% with the average ticket up 1.4%. Add basket effects from GLP-1 drugs (Walmart is a top dispenser — a pharmacy revenue tailwind, with early evidence of smaller food baskets among users) and the demand backdrop skews net-positive for value formats even in a slowdown. In October that defensive appeal showed up in share prices: as inflation worries rose and investors sold tech, money moved into consumer staples (MarketBeat; 24/7 Wall St.).
Retail Media & AI: The New Margin Battleground
EMERGING TAILWIND
US retail media is a ~$60B+ ad market growing ~20% annually, and it is rewriting retailers' profit math: 70%+ of each extra ad dollar can be profit, against retail's 3-4% net margins. Walmart Connect (+41% US in Q4 FY26; U.S. advertising +38% in Q2 FY27 and +37% in Q3) is the clear #2 behind Amazon, super-charged by VIZIO's ad-supported TV operating system, while agentic AI (Sparky) threatens to reshuffle who owns the customer relationship. Bull framing: if ads, membership, and automation keep growing, Walmart's operating income can grow double digits for years and earnings can catch up with today's share price. Bear framing: if retail media growth decelerates or AI agents commoditize the storefront, WMT is a 3%-margin grocer trading at ~49x — the spring-to-September slide showed the market testing exactly that question, and October’s rally, with no new results, shows how much of today’s price depends on money moving between sectors.
