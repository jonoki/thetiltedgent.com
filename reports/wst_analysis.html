01 Company Overview

Every injectable drug has to sit in something, and something has to touch it. West makes that something: the rubber stoppers, plungers, seals and syringe and cartridge components that sit in direct contact with the medicine, plus self-injection devices and contract-manufactured delivery systems. By the company's own count it ships over 41 billion components and devices a year from 50 sites, 26 of them manufacturing plants. FY2025 net sales were $3.07B, up 6.25%, with GAAP EPS of $6.79. Founded in Philadelphia in 1923 by Herman O. West and J.R. Wike, in its early years it made rubber parts for packaging injectable drugs, used by makers of penicillin and insulin. The job has not changed much. The drugs have.

The last two years are a round-trip. On February 13, 2025 West guided 2025 EPS to $6.00–$6.20 against a Street estimate of $7.44, blamed on biotech customers still working down pandemic-era inventory and on a strong dollar. The stock fell 32% that month and bottomed at a $210.85 month-end close in May 2025, the lowest in five years. Then demand came back where West earns the most: High-Value Product components for biologics and GLP-1 obesity and diabetes drugs, plus upgrades pushed by new EU sterile-manufacturing rules. Organic sales growth (growth excluding currency moves and the business West sold) ran 15.3% in Q1 2026, 12.7% in Q2 and 11.9% in Q3. Adjusted-EPS guidance has been raised at all three 2026 reports, to $9.38–$9.48, with sales guided to $3.41–$3.42B. That happened even though a ransomware-style cyberattack in May 2026 took systems offline around the world. On October 29, 2026, in the first results under new CEO Michel Lagarde, Q3 adjusted EPS came in at $2.66 against a Nasdaq consensus of $2.18. The stock closed at $412.62 on October 30, 2026, up 95.7% from the May 2025 low and at its highest point on the five-year chart since December 2021. It trades at 46 times trailing earnings. The question now is whether a business guiding to 11–12% organic growth this year, with a drag of about 2.9 percentage points on 2027 reported growth already flagged, deserves a mid-40s multiple.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Drug-contact components specified into approved products >90% win rate on new biologics (mgmt) Annex 1 upgrade pipeline (~800 projects at Q2) Scale: 41B+ units/yr, 26 plants Daikyo Crystal Zenith licence Net cash balance sheet (D/E 0.11)

The moat is qualification. A stopper or plunger touches the drug. Once a biologic is approved with a particular component, changing that component means new stability and compatibility work and a regulatory change process. Few drugmakers will take that on to save a few cents on a part that costs little next to the drug it protects. That is why management can cite win rates above 90% on new biologic molecules. Every win is a stream of volume that lasts as long as the drug sells. It is also why 2023–2024 hurt so much without breaking anything. The customers did not leave. They stopped ordering while they used up inventory they had stockpiled during the pandemic.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customer inventory cycles. In February 2025 the destocking hangover produced a 2025 EPS guide of $6.00–$6.20 against $7.44 expected, and a 32% one-month fall. West sells into its customers' inventory, not directly into patient demand. Order swings are therefore larger than end-market swings in both directions. The 2026 upswing is partly the same effect running the other way.
02
GLP-1 concentration and the oral question. GLP-1 elastomers and obesity and diabetes self-injection devices were named drivers in the first two 2026 quarters, and on the Q3 call the CFO put HVP GLP-1 sales at just under 12% of revenue. Oral GLP-1s are the obvious threat to an injectable-packaging story. Trefis attributed the January 2026 slide (−16.0% in the month) to concerns about the SmartDose divestiture and the impact of oral GLP-1s on injectables. On the Q2 call management said orals appeared to be expanding the overall market rather than taking injectable volume. That is a claim to test, not a fact.
03
Operational fragility shown by the May 2026 cyberattack. An intrusion was detected on May 4. On May 7 the company determined it had been a material attack in which data was exfiltrated and systems were encrypted. West took systems offline globally. Shipping, receiving and manufacturing restarted site by site. The 8-K/A said the incident was not reasonably likely to have a material impact on guidance, and guidance was in fact raised. On the Q3 call the CFO said the West Vantage revenue it delayed had been fully recovered. The episode still showed that a single-source component supplier can stop shipping without any change in demand.
04
Contract manufacturing dilutes the mix. West Vantage earned a 16.5% gross margin in Q3, down from 19.3%, against 44.4% in Proprietary Products. It is 17% of sales at a little over a third of the Proprietary margin, and its sales fell 6.6% organically in Q3 as a glucose-monitoring program rolled off.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the SmartDose 3.5mL sale to AbbVie, which closed July 1, 2026. Its sales fell from $29.8M in Q3 2025 to $11.8M in Q3 2026 and none are expected for the rest of 2026, so reported growth runs below organic growth (Q3: +8.7% reported, +11.9% organic), and the CFO put the 2027 drag on reported growth at about 2.9 percentage points. The sale also produced a gain: the release takes $0.45 a share of M&A items out of GAAP EPS of $3.02 on the way to adjusted EPS of $2.66, which is why GAAP earnings ran above adjusted in Q3. Full-year GAAP EPS guidance is $9.31–$9.41 against $9.38–$9.48 adjusted. Second, the May 2026 cyberattack: its fees sit in "other nonrecurring professional fees" (2 cents a share in Q3, together with legal matters), and the West Vantage revenue it delayed was recovered in Q3, per the CFO. Third, tax: 1H 2026 adjusted figures exclude a one-time $12.0M tax cost tied to an internal legal-entity restructuring in Q1 2026. Buybacks also matter for per-share figures. The Q3 diluted share count fell from 72.6M to 70.8M year over year, so EPS growth runs ahead of net income growth.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been, on a 0–100 scale. Above 70 means the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"). Below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It measures momentum, not value: an expensive stock can stay overbought for a long time.

From the October 2021 close of $429.88 to the October 30, 2026 settled close of $412.62, WST lost 4.0% of its price. Over the same dates SPY, a fund that tracks the S&P 500, gained 64.6% on price and QQQ, which tracks the Nasdaq-100, 78.8%. Dividends were small (about 0.25% of the price a year at today's rate), so adding them back changes little. The path explains the endpoint. The stock peaked at $469.01 in December 2021 on pandemic-era demand. It halved to $230.10 by October 2022, recovered to $406.90 in August 2023, then fell back through the destocking years. It broke in February 2025 and bottomed at $210.85 in May 2025. The recovery since then is +95.7%, and the October 30 close is the highest point on the chart since December 2021. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

This is a business whose products are specified into approved drugs, with a management-cited 90%+ win rate on new biologics. It has a regulatory tailwind in Annex 1 that management calls early in a multi-year shift. Its premium line grew 20.3% organically in Q3, and quarterly gross margin has climbed from the FY2024 trough of 34.5% to 39.7%. Adjusted-EPS guidance has been raised at all three 2026 reports, to $9.38–$9.48, up 29–30% on 2025's $7.29. The balance sheet has net cash, ROIC is 21.6%, and $685M of 2026 buybacks were done at an average of $283. The cyberattack did not dent guidance, and the revenue it delayed is back. If the HVP mix keeps shifting and earnings grow near the 16.9% a year consensus expects for the next three years, about 40 times FY2027 consensus earnings is not extreme for a part that is hard to replace once a drug is approved with it.

Bear Case

Since the May 2025 low the price has nearly doubled while the earnings guide rose about 55%. At 46x trailing and 44x FY2026 earnings, with a 2.51 PEG and a 1.58% FCF yield, the stock is priced for the good news to continue. The last time the market paid for a West recovery, in August 2023, destocking returned and the stock fell 48% to its 2025 low. 2027 starts with known drags: the SmartDose exit takes about 2.9 points off reported growth, a glucose-monitoring program is rolling off in West Vantage, and consensus FY2027 EPS growth is only 8.3% (StockAnalysis). GLP-1s, a named driver, face oral versions. A material cyberattack showed the supply chain can stop. The stock reversed hard on a beat-and-raise in July, and the monthly RSI is now the highest in five years. Morgan Stanley's $400 target and Bank of America's $410, both below the close, show that even some of the buyers see little room from here.

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation versus a decelerating growth rate. Organic growth ran 15.3% in Q1, 12.7% in Q2 and 11.9% in Q3, and consensus has EPS growth slowing to 8.3% in FY2027. A 46x trailing multiple leaves little room for a slowdown. The February 2025 episode showed how far this stock falls when guidance disappoints: −32% in a month.
02
Customer inventory and GLP-1 concentration. West sells into customers' inventories, so demand swings are amplified. The 2023–2025 destocking cycle roughly halved the share price twice (−51% from December 2021 to October 2022, −48% from August 2023 to May 2025). The current upswing depends heavily on GLP-1 (HVP GLP-1 sales were just under 12% of revenue in Q3, per the CFO) and biologics. Any sign that oral GLP-1s are taking injectable volume, or that customers restocked too much in 2026, hits the highest-margin line first.
03
Execution through a leadership change and a cyber recovery. A new CEO from August 31, 2026, a new Board Chair, a divestiture closed July 1, new bank financing in October, and a West Vantage segment losing a glucose-monitoring program. Q3 was clean. But the first 2027 guide, due in February, is also the first under the new CEO, and it gives him an easy opening to set expectations lower.
Industry Commentary
Biologics and GLP-1s: more drugs that need a needle
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

West's demand comes from the number of injectable doses, weighted by how demanding the drug is. Biologics (proteins and antibodies) are sensitive to what they touch, which is why they use the premium components. That market group grew 23.2% organically in Q3 2026, after 29% in Q2. GLP-1 obesity and diabetes drugs added volume on top: on the Q3 call the CFO said HVP GLP-1 sales were growing well into the 30s organically, at just under 12% of revenue, and West Vantage's Q1 growth came from self-injection devices for obesity and diabetes. The open question is orals. On the Q2 call management said oral GLP-1s appeared to be expanding the overall market rather than taking injectable volume. The market took the opposite view in January 2026, when the stock dropped 15.3% in five trading days. The honest position: the two effects cannot be told apart in one or two quarters. The HVP GLP-1 growth rate over the next four quarters will show which one wins.

EU Annex 1: regulation as a mix-shift engine
EMERGING TAILWIND

Annex 1 is the EU's revised guidance on manufacturing sterile medicinal products. It raises the bar for contamination control, and for component suppliers that means demand moves from standard stoppers toward pre-washed, pre-sterilised, ready-to-use high-value parts. West quantified it on the Q2 call: about 800 projects in hand, up 50% YoY, and about 2 percentage points of expected 2026 revenue growth. The economics are what make it valuable. An upgrade does not need a new drug, a new patient or a new customer. It re-prices volume West already ships. It also explains why Standard Products grows slowly (+3.1% organic in Q3) while HVP Components grows 20%. Part of the second is coming out of the first.

The destocking cycle: the industry's memory
CYCLICAL RISK

In 2020–2021 pharma and biotech customers over-ordered components to protect vaccine and drug supply. West's FY2021 revenue grew 31.89%. For the next three years they worked that inventory down. Revenue went +1.95%, +2.18% and −1.92% from FY2022 to FY2024, gross margin fell from 41.59% to 34.51%, and in February 2025 management was still describing destocking as a fading but continuing headwind. Now the cycle has turned: 2026 organic growth is guided at 11.3–11.6%, and the sales guide implies about 11% reported growth, the fastest since FY2021. The bull reading: this is the true demand rate showing through now that inventories are normal. The bear reading: some of 2026 is customers rebuilding safety stock after a period of supply scares (including West's own May outage), and that will fade. Q3's 11.9% organic growth, against the 7–9% the company had guided, leans toward the bull reading for now. The 2027 guide in February is the next test.
