01 Company Overview

Every injectable drug has to sit in something, and something has to touch it. West makes that something: the rubber stoppers, plungers, seals and syringe and cartridge components that sit in direct contact with the medicine, plus self-injection devices and contract-manufactured delivery systems. By the company's own count it ships over 41 billion components and devices a year from 50 sites, 26 of them manufacturing plants. FY2025 net sales were $3.07B, up 6.25%, with GAAP EPS of $6.79. Founded in Philadelphia in 1923 by Herman O. West and J.R. Wike, it began by making rubber parts that kept penicillin and insulin sterile. The job has not changed much. The drugs have.

The last eighteen months are a round-trip. On February 13, 2025 West guided 2025 EPS to $6.00–$6.20 against a Street estimate of $7.44, blamed on biotech customers still working down pandemic-era inventory and on a strong dollar. The stock fell 32% that month and bottomed at a $210.85 month-end close in May 2025, the lowest in five years. Then demand came back where West earns the most: High-Value Product components for biologics and GLP-1 obesity and diabetes drugs, plus upgrades pushed by new EU sterile-manufacturing rules. Q1 2026 sales rose 21.0%, Q2 rose 13.8%, and full-year guidance has been raised twice, to $3.345–$3.380B of sales and $8.85–$9.05 of adjusted EPS. That happened even though a ransomware-style cyberattack in May 2026 took systems offline around the world. The stock closed at $361.78 on September 21, 2026, up 71.6% from the May 2025 low. It trades at 46 times trailing earnings. The collapse and the recovery are both done. The question now is whether a business growing sales at 10–11% organically deserves a mid-40s multiple, with a new CEO arriving just as the easy comparisons run out.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Drug-contact components specified into approved products >90% win rate on new biologics (mgmt) Annex 1 upgrade pipeline (~800 projects) Scale: 41B+ units/yr, 26 plants Daikyo Crystal Zenith licence Net cash balance sheet (D/E 0.11)

The moat is qualification. A stopper or plunger touches the drug. Once a biologic is approved with a particular component, changing that component means new stability and compatibility work and a regulatory change process. Few drugmakers will take that on to save a few cents on a part that costs little next to the drug it protects. That is why management can cite win rates above 90% on new biologic molecules. Every win is a stream of volume that lasts as long as the drug sells. It is also why 2023–2024 hurt so much without breaking anything. The customers did not leave. They stopped ordering while they used up inventory they had stockpiled during the pandemic.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Customer inventory cycles. In February 2025 the destocking hangover produced a 2025 EPS guide of $6.00–$6.20 against $7.44 expected, and a 32% one-month fall. West sells into its customers' inventory, not directly into patient demand. Order swings are therefore larger than end-market swings in both directions. The 2026 upswing is partly the same effect running the other way.
02
GLP-1 concentration and the oral question. GLP-1 elastomers and obesity and diabetes self-injection devices are named drivers in both 2026 quarters. Oral GLP-1s are the obvious threat to an injectable-packaging story. The January 2026 slide (−16.0% in the month) was attributed to concerns about the SmartDose divestiture and oral GLP-1 impact on injectables. Management's current view is that orals appear to be "expanding the overall market rather than cannibalizing injectables." That is a claim to test, not a fact.
03
Operational fragility shown by the May 2026 cyberattack. An intrusion was detected on May 4. On May 7 the company determined it had been a material attack in which data was exfiltrated and systems were encrypted. West took systems offline globally. Shipping, receiving and manufacturing restarted site by site. The 8-K/A said the incident was not reasonably likely to have a material impact on guidance, and guidance was in fact raised. The episode still showed that a single-source component supplier can stop shipping without any change in demand.
04
Contract manufacturing dilutes the mix. West Vantage earned a 14.2% gross margin in Q2, down from 17.5%, against 42.6% in Proprietary Products. It is 17% of sales at about a third of the Proprietary margin, and it is where the cyber headwind landed.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, the SmartDose 3.5mL sale to AbbVie, which closed July 1, 2026, removes a business that produced $55M of sales in 2H 2025. Reported growth in 2H 2026 will run well below organic growth. Q3 guidance, for example, is 1.9–3.8% reported against 7.0–8.9% organic. Full-year guidance also carries an estimated $0.54–$0.59 per-share GAAP charge for M&A activity including the sale, which is excluded from adjusted EPS. Second, the May 2026 cyberattack: its professional fees sit in the "Other" unallocated items ($6.9M pre-tax in Q2, together with certain legal matters), and it shifted West Vantage revenue from Q2 into the second half. Third, tax: 1H 2026 adjusted figures exclude a one-time $12.0M tax cost tied to an internal legal-entity restructuring in Q1 2026. Buybacks also matter for per-share figures. Diluted share count fell from 72.5M to 71.3M year over year, so EPS growth runs ahead of net income growth.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

This is a business whose products are specified into approved drugs, reporting a management-cited 90%+ win rate on new biologics. It has a regulatory tailwind (Annex 1, about 800 projects, about 200 bps of 2026 growth) that management calls early in a multi-year shift. Its premium line is growing at 18–19% organically and its gross margin has recovered from 34.5% to 37.7%. Guidance has been raised twice in 2026 to 10–11% organic growth and $8.85–$9.05 of adjusted EPS, up 21–24%. The balance sheet has net cash, ROIC is 20.6%, and 1H 2026 buybacks were done at an average of $258. The cyberattack did not dent guidance. SmartDose leaves with its margin drag. If the HVP mix keeps shifting, earnings compound in the high teens, and a 40x forward multiple on a 15–20% grower is not extreme for a quasi-monopoly component inside biologic drugs.

Bear Case

The multiple has recovered before the cycle has been proven. At 46x trailing and 40x forward earnings with a 2.58 PEG and a 1.72% FCF yield, the stock is priced for the good news to continue. The last time the market paid for a West recovery, in August 2023, destocking returned and the stock fell 48% to its 2025 low. Q3 guidance already has organic growth slowing to 7–9%. Reported growth is guided at 2–4%. GLP-1 exposure is a named driver just as oral versions arrive. A material cyberattack showed the supply chain can stop. The stock reversed hard on a beat-and-raise in July. And a CEO who has been in the job for three weeks gets to set 2027 expectations. Morgan Stanley's $365 Hold, about 1% above the close, sums up the view: the business is fine, and the price already reflects it.

Top 3 Risks

01
Valuation versus a decelerating growth rate. Organic growth runs from 15.3% (Q1) to 12.7% (Q2) to a guided 7.0–8.9% (Q3). A 46x trailing multiple leaves little room for a slowdown that is already in the company's own guidance. The February 2025 episode showed how far this stock falls when guidance disappoints: −32% in a month.
02
Customer inventory and GLP-1 concentration. West sells into customers' inventories, so demand swings are amplified. The 2023–2025 destocking cycle roughly halved the share price twice (−51% from December 2021 to October 2022, −48% from August 2023 to May 2025). The current upswing depends heavily on GLP-1 and biologics. Any sign that oral GLP-1s are taking injectable volume, or that customers restocked too much in 2026, hits the highest-margin line first.
03
Execution through a leadership change and a cyber recovery. A new CEO from August 31, 2026, a new Board Chair, a divestiture closed July 1, and a West Vantage segment whose Q2 revenue slipped into the second half because of the May attack. Any one of these is manageable. Together they make the Q3 and Q4 2026 prints unusually hard to read, and they create an easy opening for a guidance reset.
Industry Commentary
Biologics and GLP-1s: more drugs that need a needle
STRUCTURAL TAILWIND

West's demand comes from the number of injectable doses, weighted by how demanding the drug is. Biologics (proteins and antibodies) are sensitive to what they touch, which is why they use the premium components. That market group grew 29% organically in Q2 2026. GLP-1 obesity and diabetes drugs added volume on top: HVP GLP-1 component revenue grew in the high teens in Q2, and West Vantage's Q1 growth came from self-injection devices for obesity and diabetes. The open question is orals. Management's view is that oral GLP-1s appear to be "expanding the overall market rather than cannibalizing injectables." The market took the opposite view in January 2026, when the stock dropped 15.3% in five trading days. The honest position: the two effects cannot be told apart in one or two quarters. The HVP GLP-1 growth rate over the next four quarters will show which one wins.

EU Annex 1: regulation as a mix-shift engine
EMERGING TAILWIND

Annex 1 is the EU's revised guidance on manufacturing sterile medicinal products. It raises the bar for contamination control, and for component suppliers that means demand moves from standard stoppers toward pre-washed, pre-sterilised, ready-to-use high-value parts. West quantified it: about 800 projects in hand, up 50% YoY, and about 200 bps of expected 2026 revenue growth. The economics are what make it valuable. An upgrade does not need a new drug, a new patient or a new customer. It re-prices volume West already ships. It also explains why Standard Products is flat (+0.7% organic in Q2) while HVP Components grows 18%. Part of the second is coming out of the first.

The destocking cycle: the industry's memory
CYCLICAL RISK

In 2020–2021 pharma and biotech customers over-ordered components to protect vaccine and drug supply. West's FY2021 revenue grew 31.89%. For the next three years they worked that inventory down. Revenue went +1.95%, +2.18% and −1.92% from FY2022 to FY2024, gross margin fell from 41.59% to 34.51%, and in February 2025 management was still calling destocking a headwind that "continues to moderate." Now the cycle has turned: 2026 organic growth is guided at 10–11%, the strongest since the pandemic. The bull reading: this is the true demand rate showing through now that inventories are normal. The bear reading: some of 2026 is customers rebuilding safety stock after a period of supply scares (including West's own May outage), and that will fade. The Q3 guide to 7–9% organic suggests the answer is somewhere in between.
