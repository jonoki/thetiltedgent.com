01 Company Overview

WTW does not insure anything. It is a broker and adviser: it finds insurance for companies, and it tells employers how to run their pension plans, health benefits and pay structures. The name is a stack of history — Willis, a London broker dating from the Lloyd's market, merged with the actuarial consultancy Towers Watson (itself Towers Perrin plus Watson Wyatt) in January 2016, traded as WLTW, and switched its Nasdaq ticker to WTW on January 10, 2022 when it rebranded. FY2025 revenue was $9.71B, down 2.2% reported but up 5% organically — the gap is the sale of TRANZACT, a direct-to-consumer Medicare distribution business, which closed at the start of 2025. EPS was $16.26 GAAP and $17.08 adjusted.

This is a company that has spent five years trying to become boring on purpose. In 2020 it agreed to be bought by Aon for roughly $30 billion; the US Department of Justice sued, and on July 26, 2021 the deal was called off, with Aon paying WTW a $1 billion break fee. To make that merger palatable, WTW had agreed to sell its treaty reinsurance arm, Willis Re, to Gallagher for $3.25 billion; it completed that sale anyway on December 1, 2021. Then it sold TRANZACT for $632.4 million — a business it had bought in 2019 for $1.3 billion — taking a $1.6–2.1 billion non-cash write-down that pushed FY2024 to a $98M net loss. What is left is two segments, a steady organic growth rate, and a management team whose headline promise is margin: ~30% adjusted operating margin in 2028, up from 25.2% in 2025. The stock sat at $309.98 on September 18, 2026 — 12% below its 52-week high — after a 2026 in which the market first sold brokers as AI victims and then bought WTW as an AI beneficiary.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Big-four global broker scale Actuarial & pension advisory heritage Recurring annual advice Insurer software (Radar) Specialty & large-account placement No underwriting risk on balance sheet

The moat is relationship plus judgment. A multinational's property programme or a pension scheme's actuarial valuation is not something a treasurer shops around every year: the adviser holds the data, knows the history and carries some of the professional liability if it goes wrong. That shows in the numbers: enterprise organic growth has run between 3% and 6% in every period reported here (FY2025 5%, Q4 2025 6%, Q1 2026 3%, Q2 2026 5%). WTW's specific edge is the actuarial depth inherited from Towers Watson — the same skill set sells pension advice to employers and pricing software to insurers. Its specific weakness is that it is the smallest of the four large listed brokers shown here, at roughly a third of Marsh's market value, in an industry where scale buys data, technology spend and bargaining power with insurers.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
AI as a threat to the fee pool. In February 2026 an Insurify app that compares auto insurance rates inside ChatGPT, and new AI tools from Anthropic, set off a broker selloff: WTW fell 12% in a day, against 9.9% for Gallagher and 9.3% for Aon (Insurance Journal). Bloomberg Intelligence's Matthew Palazola called the tools a "force multiplier rather than an existential threat". WTW's commercial and consulting book is far from personal auto, but the market has shown it will price the fear first.
02
Organic growth is lumpy and small. Q1 2026 enterprise organic growth was 3%, with R&B at 2%; the stock fell 11.9% in April. Q2 was 5%. A company guided to mid-single-digit organic growth in both segments has little room for a soft quarter — one miss moved the stock more than a year of margin gains.
03
Execution load. Propel (~$650M of total cost through 2028), the Newfront integration ($61M of transaction and integration expense in Q2 2026 alone) and the start-up Willis Re joint venture all run at once. The 2028 margin target depends on all three going to plan.
04
Scale gap versus consolidating peers. Aon has agreed to buy USI Insurance Services from KKR for $17 billion (per StockAnalysis's Aon page), adding a large US middle-market platform. WTW's answer, Newfront, cost $1.3B including earn-outs. The largest peers are getting bigger faster.

Notable One-Off Items

WTW's GAAP history is a sequence of one-offs, and anyone reading a five-year EPS chart needs the key. FY2021 net income of $4.22B (EPS $32.75) included the $1B Aon break fee and the gain on selling Willis Re. FY2024's $98M net loss (EPS −$0.96) reflects the non-cash TRANZACT write-down, which the company estimated in advance at $1.6–2.1B. 2026 carries its own: $61M of mainly Newfront transaction and integration expense in Q2 alone (versus $2M a year earlier), Propel costs of ~$625M cash and ~$25M non-cash spread to 2028, a ~$0.30 adjusted-EPS drag from the Willis Re joint venture, ~$0.10 dilution from Newfront, and a currency tailwind of about $0.35 for the full year. This is why Q2 2026 GAAP EPS fell 27% to $2.43 while adjusted EPS rose 17% to $3.35. Interest income on client funds is also drifting down — $33M in Q2 2026 versus $40M a year earlier.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 15.6x consensus 2026 earnings for a broker growing 5% organically, expanding margin 100–130bps a year, retiring roughly 4% of its shares annually, and carrying no underwriting risk. Management has put a date and a number on the next leg — ~30% adjusted operating margin in 2028, from 25.2% — and funded it. If Propel delivers even most of that, earnings compound in the mid-teens without needing faster growth, and the post-divestiture business finally gets valued on clean numbers. The AI story may even run the other way: an actuarial firm that sells pricing software to Zurich is better placed than most to use the tools rather than be replaced by them.

Bear Case

WTW is the smallest of the big brokers, its organic growth swung from 2% to 7% in R&B inside two quarters, and its 2026 is loaded with execution: a $1.3B acquisition to integrate, a ~$650M restructuring programme and a reinsurance start-up costing $0.30 of EPS. The market has already shown twice this year — in February and again in September — how quickly it will sell brokers on AI headlines. Peers are consolidating at a larger scale (Aon–USI at $17B). And a 30% margin target set during a rally is exactly the kind of promise that is priced in on the way up and punished on the first miss.

Top 3 Risks

01
Organic growth slippage. Guidance is mid-single digits in both segments. Q1 2026 at 3% (R&B 2%) cost the stock 11.9% in a month; a repeat at Q3 on October 29 would undo the July re-rating.
02
Propel execution. ~$650M of total cost for ~$350M of net savings by 2028. Restructuring programmes of this kind tend to spend on schedule and save late; the ~30% target has no margin for that.
03
AI disruption sentiment. Whatever the fundamentals, the February 2026 selloff showed the stock can lose 12% in a session on a product launch it had nothing to do with. That risk is priced by the market's mood, not by WTW's results.
Industry Commentary
AI: disintermediation fear vs. productivity tool
CRITICAL DEBATE

The February 2026 selloff was triggered in part by a personal-auto comparison app, a product line far from WTW's corporate risk and benefits-consulting book, where placing a multinational's property programme needs human judgment and insurer relationships. Bloomberg Intelligence's Matthew Palazola saw the new tools as a "force multiplier rather than an existential threat" for brokers' consulting businesses. WTW's own answer is to be the user of AI rather than its victim — Propel is explicitly an "AI Acceleration Plan", and the Q2 release frames AI as the margin engine. The two readings predict different things: if AI is a threat, organic growth should slow; if it is a tool, margins should rise with growth intact. Q2 2026 showed the second. One quarter is not a trend.

Consolidation into the US middle market
ONGOING

The large brokers are all buying US middle-market businesses. WTW paid up to $1.3B for Newfront (closed January 27, 2026); Aon has agreed to buy USI Insurance Services from KKR for $17B, expected to close in Q4 2026 with ~$395M of identified synergies (per StockAnalysis's Aon page); Gallagher's 2025 revenue grew 19.04%. WTW is buying smaller and more selectively than its peers — cheaper to integrate, but it leaves WTW's relative scale shrinking.

The margin story — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

WTW's adjusted operating margin rose from 23.9% in 2024 (implied by the 130bps gain to 25.2% in 2025) and is targeted at ~30% in 2028. The bull framing: a broker with steady mid-single-digit organic growth and ~500bps of margin to come is a mid-teens EPS compounder — consensus already has 2026 EPS up 16.25% and 2027 up 15.10% — trading at a forward multiple below Marsh and Gallagher. The bear framing: the margin target was announced into a rally, cost savings are easier to announce than to bank, and falling interest income on client funds ($33M versus $40M in Q2) is a quiet headwind to the same margin. The test is simple: year-on-year adjusted operating margin expansion every quarter through 2028, with organic growth holding at 4–6%.
