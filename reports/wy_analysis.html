01 Company Overview

Weyerhaeuser owns trees. More than 10 million acres of US timberland, plus additional Canadian public forest it manages under long-term licences, making it one of the world's largest private timberland owners. It began in 1900, when Frederick Weyerhäuser's new timber company and its fifteen partners bought 900,000 acres of Washington timberland from James J. Hill's Great Northern Railway. It became a REIT with its 2010 tax return, and took its present shape in February 2016 when it closed the $8.4B merger with Plum Creek Timber. Weyerhaeuser also runs one of North America's biggest wood-products businesses: lumber, OSB, engineered wood and distribution. FY2025 net sales were $6.91B, down 3.07%, and net earnings were $324M ($0.45 per diluted share).

The business is simple to describe and hard to value. A tree is a call option on the housing market that pays you to wait: if lumber is cheap, you leave the timber standing and it keeps growing. That flexibility is why timberland trades as a real asset. The catch is that Weyerhaeuser also owns the sawmills, so a weak lumber market hits it twice, once in log prices and once in mill margins. That is where it has been. Revenue has fallen every year from $10.20B in FY2021 to $6.91B in FY2025, Adjusted EBITDA fell from $1,292M in 2024 to $1,021M in 2025, and the stock has gone from a $41.22 month-end close in April 2022 to $21.01 on September 21, 2026, the lowest point in the five-year chart below. The CEO's own summary of housing on the Q2 call: activity "remains largely stuck in second gear." The debate is whether $21 is a cyclical low in a long-duration real asset, or a fair price for land whose cash flow no longer covers its dividend.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

10M+ owned US acres Irreplaceable, slow-to-grow asset Harvest-timing optionality Log-to-mill vertical integration Higher-and-better-use land sales Solar, carbon & mineral rights REIT tax structure

You cannot build a Douglas-fir forest in less than a working lifetime, and nobody is making more land in the Pacific Northwest. That is the moat. Weyerhaeuser can defer harvest when prices are poor, sell parcels to solar developers or conservation buyers at more than their timber value, and route its own logs into its own mills. Pruning the portfolio (around $700M of timberland sales announced across 2025 and 2026 in the releases fetched) is how it turns that optionality into cash. The vulnerability is that none of this protects the price of lumber, and the mills and the log markets both take it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Housing is stuck. Stockfish described housing as "stuck in second gear," influenced by weak consumer confidence and affordability, with mortgage rates back in the mid-6% range. Repair and remodel activity is "relatively steady" but "at somewhat muted levels." Log and lumber demand both follow starts.
02
The dividend outruns the cash. A ~$608M annual base dividend against $397M of FY2025 Adjusted FAD. Land sales and easements fund the difference. They are real assets, but selling acres to pay a dividend shrinks the asset base that earns it.
03
Leverage in a trough. About 5x net debt to Adjusted EBITDA, and interest coverage of 0.65x on StockAnalysis's EBIT-based measure. That leaves limited room if lumber weakens again.
04
OSB is loss-making. −$6M Q2 Adjusted EBITDA with supply outpacing demand, and Q3 maintenance and resin costs guided higher. Commodity panels have no pricing power when the cycle is against them.

Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying the largest private US timberland estate at a five-year low. Timberlands and land sales earned $252M of Adjusted EBITDA in Q2 alone, and the land segment keeps selling acres above timber value, including $114M for 29,000 Oregon acres in June. Wood Products is at a cyclical trough: lumber realisations rose 15% in Q2, each $10/MBF adds about $50M of annual EBITDA, and the Canadian duty wall remains around 35%. Adjusted FAD is running at $265M for H1 2026 versus $397M for all of 2025. All 12 analysts rate it Buy or Hold, and even the lowest target ($28) is 33% above the price. With no 2026 debt maturities, a 4.0% yield while you wait, and what management calls "a significant housing shortage", you are paid to hold a real asset until the cycle turns.

Bear Case

This has been "a cyclical low" for four years. Revenue has fallen every year since 2021 and TTM Adjusted EBITDA is $975M against $1,292M in 2024. The ~$608M base dividend exceeds 2025's $397M of Adjusted FAD by half, and the gap is being filled by selling the forest. GAAP EPS of $0.65 is mostly land gains; adjusted consensus for 2026 is $0.33, a 64x forward multiple. Net debt is about 5x EBITDA, OSB is losing money, housing is "stuck in second gear" with mortgages in the mid-6% range, and the stock has closed below its 10-month average for most of two years. A REIT that has to shrink its asset base to fund its payout is not compounding; it is liquidating slowly.

Top 3 Risks

01
Housing stays stuck. Log and lumber demand follow US housing starts and repair-and-remodel spending. Management describes both as muted, with mortgage rates in the mid-6% range. Another year of this keeps Wood Products near FY2025's $250M of Adjusted EBITDA, less than a quarter of the group.
02
Dividend coverage. The base dividend (~$608M annualised) exceeds FY2025 Adjusted FAD ($397M). The 75–80% payout framework is not being met on trough cash flow. If land sales slow, the company has to borrow, cut, or sell more acres.
03
Leverage and execution on the 2030 plan. About 5x net debt to Adjusted EBITDA while spending about $300M on Monticello in 2026. The $1.5B incremental-EBITDA target requires the cycle to cooperate; a lumber-price relapse would push leverage up just as growth capex peaks.
Industry Commentary
Housing affordability and the lumber cycle
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Wood demand is housing demand. On the Q2 2026 call Stockfish said housing "remains largely stuck in second gear and continues to be influenced by weak consumer confidence and ongoing affordability challenges," that mortgage rates had "moved back up into the mid-6% range," and that turnover of existing homes "remains well below historical levels." He also called the passage of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act "a positive step forward" for housing supply over time. The medium-term argument is a US housing shortage. The near-term reality is that neither starts nor remodelling has turned.

Supply discipline: duties, closures and imports
MIXED

Lumber prices rose in Q2 on the supply side, not demand. Management cited previously enacted mill curtailments and closures, lower European imports, and Southern trucking constraints. After the seventh administrative review, the combined Canadian duty-and-tariff rate falls to "around 35% versus 45%." Stockfish's read is that this gives "a little bit more breathing room on the downside" for prices, but that "35% is still a healthy number" and closed Canadian capacity is not expected to return. Supply-driven price rises tend to be fragile: they hold only while the curtailments do.

Land as more than timber
EMERGING TAILWIND

The structural positive for timberland owners is alternative uses of the ground. On the call, management cited healthy demand for large-scale solar development, conservation easements (one worth $94M in Q1 2026 alone), industrial land demand from data-centre and solar developers, and biocarbon. Weyerhaeuser's land segment is guided to about $450M of Adjusted EBITDA in 2026, more than FY2025's entire Wood Products segment. The bull framing: the market prices WY as a lumber company when a growing share of its earnings comes from land. The bear framing: land sales are finite and lumpy, and a company that sells its best acres to fund its dividend is converting capital into income. Both readings fit the same numbers, and the difference comes down to whether Adjusted FAD recovers to cover the dividend without the land sales.
