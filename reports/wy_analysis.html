01 Company Overview

Weyerhaeuser owns trees. More than 10 million acres of US timberland, plus additional Canadian public forest it manages under long-term licences, making it one of the world's largest private timberland owners. It began in 1900, when Frederick Weyerhäuser's new timber company and its fifteen partners bought 900,000 acres of Washington timberland from James J. Hill's Great Northern Railway. It became a REIT with its 2010 tax return, and took its present shape in February 2016 when it closed the $8.4B merger with Plum Creek Timber. Weyerhaeuser also runs one of North America's biggest wood-products businesses: lumber, OSB, engineered wood and distribution. FY2025 net sales were $6.91B, down 3.07%, and net earnings were $324M ($0.45 per diluted share).

The business is simple to describe and hard to value. A tree is a call option on the housing market that pays you to wait: if lumber is cheap, you leave the timber standing and it keeps growing. That flexibility is why timberland trades as a real asset. The catch is that Weyerhaeuser also owns the sawmills, so a weak lumber market hits it twice, once in log prices and once in mill margins. That is where it has been. Revenue has fallen every year from $10.20B in FY2021 to $6.91B in FY2025, Adjusted EBITDA fell from $1,292M in 2024 to $1,021M in 2025, and the stock went from a $41.22 month-end close in April 2022 to $19.05 at the end of September 2026, the lowest month-end in the five-year chart below. It closed at $21.29 on November 2, 2026, after the third-quarter results. That quarter (to September 30) earned $61M, or $0.08 a share, with no special items, on net sales of $1,862M; Adjusted EBITDA was $248M, down from $310M in Q2. On the call, CEO Devin Stockfish said "mortgage rates now back over 7%" were "further impacting affordability." The debate is whether $21 is a cyclical low in a long-duration real asset, or a fair price for land whose cash flow no longer covers its dividend.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

10M+ owned US acres Irreplaceable, slow-to-grow asset Harvest-timing optionality Log-to-mill vertical integration Higher-and-better-use land sales Solar, carbon & mineral rights REIT tax structure

You cannot build a Douglas-fir forest in less than a working lifetime, and nobody is making more land in the Pacific Northwest. That is the moat. Weyerhaeuser can defer harvest when prices are poor, sell parcels to solar developers or conservation buyers at more than their timber value, and route its own logs into its own mills. Pruning the portfolio (about $860M of timberland sales announced since the start of 2025, our sum of the company's figures, including October's $146M Arkansas deal) is how it turns that optionality into cash. The vulnerability is that none of this protects the price of lumber, and the mills and the log markets both take it.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Housing is stuck. In July Stockfish described housing as "stuck in second gear," with mortgage rates in the mid-6% range. By the October 30 call he said mortgage rates were "now back over 7%" and that he expects housing activity "to remain subdued through year-end." Log and lumber demand both follow starts.
02
The dividend outruns the cash. A ~$604M annual base dividend against $397M of FY2025 Adjusted FAD; in the first nine months of 2026, $454M paid against $375M generated. Land sales and easements fund the difference. They are real assets, but selling acres to pay a dividend shrinks the asset base that earns it.
03
Leverage in a trough. About 5x net debt to Adjusted EBITDA, and interest coverage of 0.84x on StockAnalysis's measure (operating profit ÷ interest expense). That leaves limited room if lumber weakens again.
04
OSB is loss-making. A $18M Adjusted EBITDA loss in Q3, after $6M in Q2, with prices 2% lower on the quarter. Commodity panels have no pricing power when the cycle is against them.

Notable One-Off Items

GAAP earnings here are mostly a record of land sales. Q4 2025 included $266M of pretax gains on Georgia/Alabama and Oregon timberland sales, offset by a $111M pension settlement charge and a $14M environmental remediation charge (both after tax). FY2025 net earnings of $324M ($0.45) became $143M ($0.20) before special items. Q1 2026 net earnings of $156M included about $79M of special items (GAAP $156M vs $77M before special items). Q2 2026 included a $71M gain on the Oregon sale. Q3 2026 had no special items: its $61M ($0.08) is a clean quarter. Strip the gains out and the first nine months of 2026 earned $229M before special items ($77M + $91M + $61M), not $379M. Separately, Adjusted FAD excludes Monticello capex (about $300M planned for 2026; $63M in Q2). That is a legitimate carve-out for spending on growth, but a reader should know the headline FAD is after it. StockAnalysis's free-cash-flow line uses a different capex definition and does not reconcile to the company's FAD. It is not used here.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 2, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the November 2021 month-end close of $37.61 to the November 2, 2026 settled close of $21.29, WY lost 43.4% of its price; the SPY S&P 500 fund gained 65.1% on price over the same dates. With dividends reinvested, on Yahoo's dividend-adjusted closes over the same dates, the loss is about 31%. For a REIT sold as an inflation hedge, that is five lost years. The shape is a spike and a long leak. The lumber-boom peak came at a $41.22 month-end in April 2022. Rate hikes then took it to $28.56 by September 2022. It spent 2023–2024 in a roughly $28–36 range, and has fallen gradually ever since: $28.15 at the end of 2024, $22.21 in November 2025, $19.05 at the end of September 2026, and $21.29 now, after the Q3 print.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 39.1, up from 19.9 at the September month-end, which was the lowest reading of the five-year series (below November 2025's 21.8). The 10-month moving average sits at about $23.10, roughly 8% above the price. The trend is down, and it has been for longer than most cyclical downturns run. Oversold readings in this stock have tended to bring bounces rather than reversals: the January 2026 rally to $25.78 gave way within weeks. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are buying one of the largest private US timberland estates near a five-year low. Timberlands and land sales earned $219M of Adjusted EBITDA in Q3 alone, against $91M from the mills, and the land segment keeps selling acres above timber value: $114M for 29,000 Oregon acres in June, $146M agreed for 81,000 Arkansas acres in October, with part of that going to buybacks. Wood Products is at a cyclical trough, and each $10/MBF on lumber adds about $50M of annual EBITDA. Q3 earnings had no one-off gains in them and beat the Nasdaq consensus ($0.08 vs $0.05). All 12 analysts rate it Buy or Hold, ten of them Buy, and even the lowest target ($23) is above the price. With no debt due within a year, a 3.95% yield while you wait, and Monticello starting up in mid-2027, you are paid to hold a real asset until the cycle turns.

Bear Case

This has been "a cyclical low" for four years. Revenue fell every year from 2021 to 2025 and TTM Adjusted EBITDA is $1,006M against $1,292M in 2024. The ~$604M base dividend exceeds 2025's $397M of Adjusted FAD by half; Q3 alone generated $110M of FAD against $151M of dividends, and the gap is being filled by selling the forest. GAAP EPS of $0.63 is mostly land gains; adjusted consensus for 2026 is $0.24, an 89x forward multiple. Net debt is about 5x EBITDA, OSB lost $18M in Q3, Q4 is guided lower in both the mills and land sales, management says mortgage rates are back over 7%, and the stock has closed below its 10-month average at 19 of the last 24 month-ends. A REIT that has to shrink its asset base to fund its payout is not growing its value; it is selling it down slowly.

Top 3 Risks

01
Housing stays stuck. Log and lumber demand follow US housing starts and repair-and-remodel spending. On the Q3 call management said mortgage rates were back over 7% and expected housing to stay subdued through year-end. Another year of this keeps Wood Products near FY2025's $250M of Adjusted EBITDA, less than a quarter of the group.
02
Dividend coverage. The base dividend (~$604M annualised) exceeds FY2025 Adjusted FAD ($397M), and nine-month 2026 dividends ($454M) exceed nine-month FAD ($375M). The 75–80% payout framework is not being met on trough cash flow. If land sales slow, the company has to borrow, cut, or sell more acres.
03
Leverage and execution on the 2030 plan. About 5x net debt to Adjusted EBITDA while spending about $300M on Monticello in 2026, on a project now expected to cost moderately more than its $500M initial estimate. The $1.5B incremental-EBITDA target requires the cycle to cooperate; a lumber-price relapse would push leverage up just as growth capex peaks.
Industry Commentary
Housing affordability and the lumber cycle
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Wood demand is housing demand. On the Q2 2026 call in July Stockfish said housing "remains largely stuck in second gear and continues to be influenced by weak consumer confidence and ongoing affordability challenges," with mortgage rates "back up into the mid-6% range." Three months later it was worse. On the October 30 call he said: "More recently, interest rates have moved higher, with mortgage rates now back over 7%, further impacting affordability." He added that builder confidence remains low and that he expects housing activity "to remain subdued through year-end." The medium-term argument is a US housing shortage. The near-term reality is that neither starts nor remodelling has turned.

Supply discipline: duties, closures and imports
MIXED

Lumber prices rose in Q2 on a seasonal pick-up in demand against tight supply. Management cited previously enacted mill curtailments and closures, lower European imports, and transport constraints and higher fuel costs. By Q3 the lift had faded: the average lumber price was only slightly above Q2's, OSB was 2% lower, and the September presentation put lumber about $20/MBF below the Q2 average on September 18. On the Q2 call, management said that after the seventh administrative review, the combined Canadian duty-and-tariff rate falls to "around 35% versus 45%." Stockfish's read is that this gives "a little bit more breathing room on the downside" for prices, but that "35% is still a healthy number" and closed Canadian capacity is not expected to return. Price gains that rest on tight supply tend to be fragile: they hold only while the curtailments do.

Land as more than timber
EMERGING TAILWIND

The lasting positive for timberland owners is alternative uses of the ground. On the Q2 call, management cited healthy demand for large-scale solar development, conservation easements (one worth $94M in Q1 2026 alone), industrial land demand from data-centre and solar developers, and biocarbon. Weyerhaeuser's land segment is guided to about $450M of Adjusted EBITDA in 2026, more than FY2025's entire Wood Products segment. The bull framing: the market prices WY as a lumber company when a growing share of its earnings comes from land. The bear framing: land sales are finite and lumpy, and a company that sells its best acres to fund its dividend is converting capital into income. Both readings fit the same numbers, and the difference comes down to whether Adjusted FAD recovers to cover the dividend without the land sales.
