01 Company Overview

Wynn Resorts builds and runs luxury integrated resorts in three places: the Las Vegas Strip (Wynn Las Vegas and Encore), Macau (Wynn Palace and Wynn Macau) and Everett, Massachusetts (Encore Boston Harbor). A fourth, Wynn Al Marjan Island in Ras Al Khaimah, UAE, is under construction in a 40%-owned joint venture and is scheduled by the company to open in September 2027. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to June 30, 2026 was $7.41B, up 6.4%; FY2025 revenue was $7.14B, up just 0.14% on FY2024, and trailing GAAP EPS is $4.17. About 63% of TTM revenue is casino revenue; the rest is rooms (15.6%), food and beverage (14.2%) and entertainment and retail (7.2%).

Here is the thing a card player notices first about the latest quarter. In Q2 2026, Wynn Palace's mass-market tables won 29.7% of the chips dropped at them, against 22.3% a year earlier. That percentage — the hold — is the house's short-run luck, and it swings. On roughly $1.9B of mass drop, seven extra points of hold is on the order of $140M of extra win (our estimate: $1.9B × 7.4pp) — more than Wynn Palace's entire $113.8M revenue increase. Meanwhile the US side ran cold: Encore Boston Harbor held 18.1% versus 21.3%, and Las Vegas Adjusted Property EBITDAR fell 8.3% despite a record month in May. The report card that the market saw — revenue up $119.1M, adjusted EPS $1.24 versus $1.09 — was one hot table offsetting two cold ones.

The stock has not been rewarded for the beat. From a $128.68 month-end close in November 2025 it has fallen to $83.32 — down 35.3%, and 30.8% since December 31, 2025 — with the Street unanimously bullish (21 analysts, zero Holds or Sells) while 11.25% of the float is sold short. The debate is simple to state: is WYNN a luxury-resort franchise temporarily priced for Macau softness and a war near its UAE project, or a heavily indebted company (net debt $10.24B, negative book equity) whose growth leg is a UAE resort whose budget has just risen by about $600M, to roughly $5.7B, because of a war in the region?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Macau concession (agreed Dec 16, 2022) Licensed, limited-entry markets Luxury brand & service reputation Non-gaming mix in Las Vegas First-mover position in the UAE 68.3% gross margin

The moat is permission plus product. Casino licences are scarce by law — Wynn reached agreement with the Macau government on an extension of its concession on December 16, 2022 (per Wikipedia) — and within the licensed markets Wynn competes on being the most expensive room in town: a Las Vegas ADR of $575 and a Wynn Palace occupancy of 98.9%. In early 2026 the company said it received 18 Forbes Travel Guide Five Star Awards (company press release, as listed on StockAnalysis). The vulnerability is that a licence is also a leash: a regulator sets the terms, and the concession, the Massachusetts licence and the UAE licence each sit with a government, not with shareholders.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage with negative equity. Total debt of $12.34B against $2.10B of cash (StockAnalysis; the company's release reports $10.72B of current and long-term debt at June 30, 2026 ; StockAnalysis's figure adds about $1.62B of long-term lease liabilities to the $1.43B current and $9.30B long-term debt that make up the company's figure). Book equity is −$906.67M, debt/EBITDA is 6.62x and interest coverage is 1.92x. A business with lumpy, luck-driven quarterly earnings carries a fixed interest bill; the refinancing calendar matters more here than at most S&P 500 names.
02
Macau is half the business and growing slowly. 54.1% of Q2 revenue. Macau's August 2026 gross gaming revenue fell 1.2% year over year, per the government bureau's figures. VIP turnover fell 32% at Palace and 56.4% at Wynn Macau in Q2.
03
The UAE project sits near a war. In May 2026 CEO Craig Billings said the war in Iran would cause a "modest delay" at Wynn Al Marjan Island (Zawya); the company now states a September 2027 opening, pushed back from early 2027. On the Q2 call the budget rose by about $600M to roughly $5.7B, with Billings saying "these disruptions have impacted both the timing and cost of the project" (Skift, Aug 5, 2026).
04
Reported results include luck. Hold swings of several points move quarterly EBITDAR by tens of millions of dollars in either direction. Q2 2026 was flattered at Wynn Palace and penalised at Encore Boston Harbor; investors extrapolating one quarter are extrapolating variance.

Notable One-Off Items

Three items distort the reported figures. First, hold: Q2 2026 benefited from above-normal mass hold at Wynn Palace and was hurt by below-prior-year hold at Encore Boston Harbor; the release discloses the percentages but not a hold-adjusted EBITDAR, so no adjusted figure is shown here. RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Five years, almost no net movement: the September 2021 month-end close was $84.75 and the September 21, 2026 close is $83.32, a 1.7% price decline (dividends, paid again from 2023, are not included). The path in between was anything but flat. The stock fell 37% from its October 2021 month-end to a $56.98 low in June 2022, doubled to $114.28 by April 2023, drifted down to $76.88 by August 2024, ran to a five-year high month-end of $128.68 in November 2025, and has since given back 35.3%. For a stock whose earnings contain a luck term, the chart looks like it: long swings, little drift.

The 6-period monthly RSI is 25.3, in oversold territory and the lowest reading since the 17.8 of June 2022. The 10-month moving average is about $101.68, some 22% above the price: the trend is down and has not turned. Oversold describes the speed of the fall, not where it stops — in 2022 the RSI sat below 40 for seven straight months (April–October) before the reversal.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 17.7x this year's consensus earnings and about 10x trailing EBITDA for the premium assets in two of the world's largest resort markets, with a 9.4% free-cash-flow yield, a shrinking share count and a 1.2% dividend. Underlying Macau volume is growing — mass drop +8.3% at Wynn Macau — and Las Vegas is holding price at a $575 ADR with group pace ahead of 2025. The UAE resort opens in September 2027 per the company; the bull argues today's price gives it little credit, so an on-time opening and ramp is upside. All 21 covering analysts rate it a Buy, with the lowest target 39% above the price.

Bear Case

The best number in the Q2 print was luck. Strip out Wynn Palace, where mass hold ran 7.4 points above last year, and property EBITDAR fell $28.4M; Las Vegas margin fell 3.3 points despite a record May. Macau, more than half the business, is growing low single digits with VIP collapsing. Net debt is $10.24B against negative equity, interest coverage is under 2x, and the refinancing just done cost 1.625 points more in coupon. The growth leg is a roughly $5.7B project beside a war, already delayed and $600M over its earlier budget. A unanimous sell side and an 11.25% short float cannot both be right; the tape has been siding with the shorts since November.

Top 3 Risks

01
Hold reversion at Wynn Palace. Q2's 29.7% mass hold against 22.3% a year ago is worth on the order of $140M of win (our estimate). If it reverts on flat drop, Q3 and Q4 comparisons deteriorate without any change in demand — and consensus FY2026 EPS of $4.70 may embed some of Q2's good fortune.
02
Al Marjan timing and cost. $1.06B of Wynn cash is already in; the company has acknowledged a war-related delay and a budget increase of about $600M (Skift, Aug 5, 2026). Any further slip extends the period in which that capital earns nothing while debt costs rise.
03
Leverage in a soft Macau. Debt/EBITDA 6.62x, interest coverage 1.92x, current ratio 0.91 and negative book equity leave little room for a demand shock in the market that supplies over half of revenue. August Macau GGR was down 1.2% year over year.
Industry Commentary
Macau: slow growth, shrinking VIP
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Macau's August 2026 gross gaming revenue was MOP 21.89B, down 1.2% year over year, according to the government's Gaming Inspection and Coordination Bureau (reported by Macau Business, Sep 1, 2026). Wynn's own Q2 split tells the same story at property level: premium mass is growing, VIP is shrinking fast, and the headline was lifted by hold.

The UAE bet and the Iran war
ONGOING HEADWIND

Wynn Al Marjan Island is the company's diversification away from Macau: a resort in Ras Al Khaimah, built by a joint venture in which Wynn holds 40% and to which it has contributed $1.06B of cash. The project was costed at $5.1B (Zawya, May 2026); in May the CEO said the war in Iran would cause a "modest delay", and on the Q2 call the budget rose by about $600M to roughly $5.7B, with the opening now set for September 2027 (Skift, Aug 5, 2026). Whether the share price gives the project much value is the swing question: if it gives little, that is both the bear case (it may be right) and the bull case (it leaves room to be wrong in the other direction).

Luck, skill and the valuation — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

Every casino quarter is a sample, not the population. Wynn discloses hold by property against its own expected ranges, which lets a reader separate volume from variance — and in Q2 2026 variance helped in Macau and hurt in Boston.
