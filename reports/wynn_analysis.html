01 Company Overview

Wynn Resorts builds and runs luxury integrated resorts in three places: the Las Vegas Strip (Wynn Las Vegas and Encore), Macau (Wynn Palace and Wynn Macau) and Everett, Massachusetts (Encore Boston Harbor). A fourth, Wynn Al Marjan Island in Ras Al Khaimah, UAE, is under construction in a 40%-owned joint venture and is scheduled by the company to open in September 2027. Trailing-twelve-month revenue to September 30, 2026 was $7.27B, up 2.2%; FY2025 revenue was $7.14B, up just 0.14% on FY2024, and trailing GAAP EPS is $3.54. In Q3 2026, 62.2% of revenue was casino revenue; the rest was rooms (15.5%), food and beverage (15.3%) and entertainment, retail and other (7.1%).

The last edition asked whether Wynn Palace's lucky second quarter would last. In Q3 the luck mostly did: Palace's mass-market tables kept 25.8% of the chips dropped at them, against 22.6% a year earlier. That percentage — the hold — is the house's short-run luck. What failed was volume: Palace mass drop (the cash and chips bought at the tables) fell 12.9% to $1.79B and VIP turnover 14.3%, so Palace revenue fell 8.1% even with the dice on its side. In Las Vegas the cards ran the other way: table hold was 18.9%, below the 22–26% range the company expects, which CFO Craig Fullalove put at "just under $24 million" of lost Adjusted Property EBITDAR. At Encore Boston Harbor, more than 1,300 workers were on strike from September 4. Revenue and EBITDAR fell at every property; group EBITDAR dropped 18.6% to $464.2M.

The stock reached a $74.66 October month-end, 42.0% below the $128.68 November 2025 month-end and its lowest month-end since October 2022, then rose 16.1% in the first six sessions of November to $86.70, including gains of 4.78% and 4.32% in the two sessions after the miss. It is still down 32.6% from that November 2025 month-end and 27.9% since December 31, 2025. The Street remains unanimously bullish (21 analysts, zero Holds or Sells, per StockAnalysis) while 12.46% of the float was sold short at the October 15 settlement. The debate is simple to state: is WYNN a luxury-resort franchise priced for a bad quarter that luck and a strike made worse, or a heavily indebted company (net debt $10.56B, negative book equity) whose Macau volumes are now shrinking and whose growth leg is a roughly $5.7B UAE resort that has yet to open?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Macau concession (agreed Dec 16, 2022) Licensed, limited-entry markets Luxury brand & service reputation Non-gaming mix in Las Vegas First-mover position in the UAE 67.6% gross margin

The moat is permission plus product. Casino licences are scarce by law — Wynn reached agreement with the Macau government on an extension of its concession on December 16, 2022 (per Wikipedia) — and within the licensed markets Wynn competes on being the most expensive room in town: a Las Vegas average room rate of $522 in Q3 2026, up 3.4% in a quarter when its casino revenue fell 23.6%, and a Wynn Palace occupancy of 99.0%. In early 2026 the company said it received 18 Forbes Travel Guide Five Star Awards (company press release, as listed on StockAnalysis). The vulnerability is that a licence is also a leash: a regulator sets the terms, and the concession, the Massachusetts licence and the UAE licence each sit with a government, not with shareholders.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Leverage with negative equity. The company reports $10.73B of current and long-term debt at September 30, 2026 against $1.64B of cash plus $150.2M of short-term investments at Wynn Macau; StockAnalysis, which also counts lease liabilities, shows $12.36B of total debt. Book equity is −$985.04M, debt/EBITDA is 7.02x and interest coverage (operating profit divided by interest expense) is 1.74x, both weaker than in June (StockAnalysis). Q3 interest expense of $151.7M was close to Q3 operating income of $178.2M. A business with lumpy, luck-driven quarterly earnings carries a fixed interest bill; the refinancing calendar matters more here than at most S&P 500 names.
02
Macau is half the business, and its volumes fell. 54.4% of Q3 revenue. Mass drop fell 12.9% at Wynn Palace and was flat at Wynn Macau; VIP turnover fell 14.3% and 53.9%. Macau's October 2026 gross gaming revenue fell 10% year over year despite Golden Week (government figures, via Focus Gaming News), although Billings said on the call that at Wynn's Macau properties "In October, Golden Week drop, handle, and retail sales were in line with last year," adding that volumes declined toward the end of the month.
03
The UAE project sits near a war. In May 2026 CEO Craig Billings said the war in Iran would cause a "modest delay" at Wynn Al Marjan Island (Zawya); the company now states a September 2027 opening, pushed back from early 2027 and repeated in the Q3 release. On the Q2 call the budget rose by about $600M to roughly $5.7B, with Billings saying "these disruptions have impacted both the timing and cost of the project" (Skift, Aug 5, 2026). On the Q3 call he said "the costs are substantially locked in."
04
Reported results include luck. Hold swings of several points move quarterly EBITDAR by tens of millions of dollars in either direction. In Q3 2026 Las Vegas hold cost just under $24M and Macau VIP hold added just under $12.3M, by the CFO's numbers, while Palace mass hold stayed well above last year; investors extrapolating one quarter are extrapolating variance.
05
A new labour contract in Boston. The strike is over, but its terms are not public: a workers' spokesperson told WBUR the contract includes "significant raises that meet the Boston standard." Higher wages at a property earning a 25.8% EBITDAR margin come straight out of that margin unless revenue grows.

Notable One-Off Items

Four items distort the reported figures. First, hold: by the CFO's numbers, Las Vegas table hold cost just under $24M of Q3 EBITDAR and above-normal VIP hold in Macau added just under $12.3M; Wynn Palace's mass hold (25.8% against 22.6%) also helped, with no dollar figure given. Second, GAAP vs adjusted EPS: Q3 GAAP diluted EPS of $0.17 against adjusted $0.33. The adjustments add back $22.0M of property charges and other, a $5.4M loss on debt financing transactions, $2.6M of pre-opening costs and $1.9M of currency and other losses, less a $10.5M gain on derivatives and the tax and minority-holder effects. Third, the Boston strike ran from September 4 to November 5, so it touched Q3 and will touch Q4; the company did not put a dollar figure on it. Fourth, the share count is shrinking (−2.10% YoY), which lifts per-share figures without any change in profit. The 2021–2022 per-share series is dominated by pandemic-era losses (FY2021 net loss $755.8M, FY2022 $423.9M); no stock splits are on record.

⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF NOV 9, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

Five years, a small net gain: the November 2021 month-end close was $81.01 and the November 9, 2026 close is $86.70, a 7.0% price gain (dividends, paid again from 2023, are not included), against 65.2% for SPY over the same dates. The path in between was anything but flat. The stock fell 34.1% from its February 2022 month-end ($86.52) to a $56.98 low in June 2022, doubled to $114.28 by April 2023, drifted down to $76.88 by August 2024, ran to a five-year high month-end of $128.68 in November 2025, then fell 42.0% to $74.66 at the end of October 2026 before rising 16.1% in early November. For a stock whose earnings contain a luck term, the chart looks like it: long swings, little drift.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying 22.9x this year's consensus earnings and about 11x trailing EBITDA for the premium assets in two of the world's largest resort markets, with a 5.9% free-cash-flow yield, a shrinking share count and a 1.2% dividend. Part of Q3's damage should not repeat: by the CFO's numbers Las Vegas hold cost just under $24M, the Boston strike is settled, Las Vegas room rates still rose 3.4%, and Billings said the rest of the fourth quarter "looks quite healthy." The UAE resort opens in September 2027 per the company, with costs, in Billings's words, "substantially locked in"; the bull argues today's price gives it little credit. All 21 covering analysts rate it a Buy.

Bear Case

The Q3 miss was not only bad luck. Wynn Palace kept above-normal hold and still lost 8.1% of its revenue because mass drop fell 12.9%, and Macau's October gaming revenue fell 10% even with Golden Week. Net debt is $10.56B against negative equity, debt is 7.0x EBITDA, interest coverage is 1.74x, and Q3 interest expense of $151.7M nearly matched operating income of $178.2M. Boston returns with a costlier labour contract. The growth leg is a roughly $5.7B project beside a war that has already slipped and gone over budget. Analysts kept their Buys but cut their targets: the average fell from $132.01 to $116.75 between the two editions, and the lowest now sits 6% above the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Macau volume, not just hold. In Q3, Wynn Palace mass drop fell 12.9% and VIP turnover 14.3%, Wynn Macau's mass drop was flat and its VIP turnover halved. Palace mass hold (25.8% against 22.6%) is still flattering the result; if it returns to last year's level on Q3's drop, Palace win falls by about $58M a quarter (our estimate). Consensus FY2026 adjusted EPS is now $3.79, down from $4.70 at the last edition (StockAnalysis).
02
Al Marjan timing and cost. $1.12B of Wynn cash is already in; the company has acknowledged a war-related delay and a budget increase of about $600M (Skift, Aug 5, 2026). Any further slip extends the period in which that capital earns nothing while debt costs rise.
03
Leverage in a soft Macau. Debt/EBITDA 7.02x, interest coverage 1.74x, current ratio 0.91 and negative book equity leave little room for a demand shock in the market that supplies over half of revenue. Macau's October gaming revenue was down 10% year over year.
Industry Commentary
Macau: revenue turning down, VIP shrinking
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Macau's October 2026 gross gaming revenue was MOP21.68B, down 10% year over year despite the Golden Week holiday; January–October was MOP208.79B, still up 1.6% on 2025 (Gaming Inspection and Coordination Bureau figures, reported by Focus Gaming News, Nov 2, 2026). Wynn's own Q3 split tells a similar story at property level: mass drop down 12.9% at Wynn Palace and flat at Wynn Macau, VIP turnover down 14.3% and 53.9%, and the Palace headline still lifted by hold.

The UAE bet and the Iran war
ONGOING HEADWIND

Wynn Al Marjan Island is the company's diversification away from Macau: a resort in Ras Al Khaimah, built by a joint venture in which Wynn holds 40% and to which it has contributed $1.12B of cash. The project was costed at $5.1B (Zawya, May 2026); in May the CEO said the war in Iran would cause a "modest delay", and on the Q2 call the budget rose by about $600M to roughly $5.7B, with the opening now set for September 2027 (Skift, Aug 5, 2026). The Q3 release kept that date, and on the call the CFO said $1.76B of the construction loan had been drawn. Whether the share price gives the project much value is the swing question: if it gives little, that is both the bear case (it may be right) and the bull case (it leaves room to be wrong in the other direction).

Luck, skill and the valuation — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

Every casino quarter is a sample, not the population. Wynn discloses hold by property against its own expected ranges, which lets a reader separate volume from variance — and in Q3 2026 variance hurt in Las Vegas and helped in Macau, while volume fell almost everywhere. The bull framing: strip the noise and the strike and Wynn is a luxury operator with firm Las Vegas pricing and an unpriced UAE option, trading at about 11x trailing EBITDA with a 5.9% free-cash-flow yield after a 42% fall from the November 2025 month-end to October 2026. The bear framing: the Q3 volume declines are real, and a balance sheet carrying debt of 7x EBITDA turns small misses into large moves in the share price. The data that settles it: Wynn Palace drop (not win) in Q4, Las Vegas margin at normal hold, Boston's margin under the new contract, and any change to the Al Marjan schedule.
