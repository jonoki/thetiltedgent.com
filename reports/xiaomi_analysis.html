01 Company Overview

Xiaomi sells the phone in your pocket, the robot vacuum on your floor, the TV on your wall, the advertising inside the software that runs all of them — and, since 2024, the car in your driveway. It is the world's No. 3 smartphone maker by shipments (Omdia, Q2 2026, the 24th straight quarter in the top three), runs an IoT platform with 1,160.8 million connected devices, counts 766.5 million monthly active users on its software, and delivered 411,082 cars in 2025. FY2025 revenue was a record RMB457.3B, up 25.0%, with adjusted net profit of RMB39.2B, up 43.8%.

Then the hand turned. Memory chips — inside every phone, TV and car Xiaomi sells — got sharply more expensive, and Xiaomi's filings for both 2026 quarters list that cost among the headwinds. Xiaomi's revenue fell 10.9% in Q1 2026 and 6.1% in Q2; adjusted net profit fell 42.6% in Q2 to RMB6.2B. Both Q2 figures missed the analyst average of RMB112.2B in revenue and RMB6.6B in adjusted profit (LSEG data, via Reuters, Aug 18, 2026). Smartphone shipments dropped 26.5% year-on-year to 31.2 million as the company cut shipments of mid-range and low-end models and global demand weakened, and the smartphone gross margin — what is left of each sale after the cost of building the phone — fell to 8.5%. The stock, which closed June 2025 at HK$59.95, fell for nine straight months from October 2025 to June 2026 and closed at HK$24.24 on October 2, 2026: down 59.6% from that peak, valuing the whole company at about US$79.7B.

The debate is a two-handed one. The car business is growing — Q2 deliveries +28.2% while China's passenger-car retail market fell 22% — but it lost RMB2.6B at the operating line in Q2, and the 550,000-car 2026 target needs a steep fourth-quarter ramp. The phone-and-gadget business is the cash engine, and its margins are hostage to a memory cycle Xiaomi does not control. Management is buying back stock hard (about HK$11.7B spent from January 1 to August 13, 2026) and raising research spending (up 18.9% in Q2 to RMB9.2B, with AI a stated focus). With the Q2 results Xiaomi said the pace of memory-price increases had started to slow and should keep slowing in the second half, and President William Lu said the most difficult period for the phone business had passed (Reuters); on the call he also said the cars go on sale outside China in the second half of 2027. Whether the memory squeeze is a cyclical toll or a permanent tax on a thin-margin hardware model is the whole question.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

1.16B-device IoT ecosystem 766.5M monthly users Top-3 phone scale in 53 markets High-margin internet layer Own retail: 270+ big China stores, 640+ abroad RMB219.3B cash resources In-house OS (HyperOS) & AI models

Xiaomi's edge is the ecosystem, not any single device. One app — Xiaomi Home, with 124.1M monthly users in June 2026 — controls the vacuum, the air conditioner, the TV and the lights. Each device is sold close to cost, and the money is made on scale, on the internet services layered on top, and on the next device the household buys. That is why IoT connections kept rising 17.4% even in a quarter when IoT revenue fell 19.2%: the installed base is the asset. The balance sheet backs it — RMB219.3B of cash resources against RMB39.3B of borrowings at June 30, 2026. Xiaomi is also designing more of its own chips: on August 24, 2026 it unveiled the XRING O3, its newest in-house flagship phone processor, made by TSMC on a 3-nanometre process, for the Xiaomi 18 Fold foldable due in September (Huawei Central). The weakness is that a thin-margin hardware model has little cushion when a key input cost jumps, and memory is exactly that input.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Memory costs it cannot control. Xiaomi's Q2 filing opens on "significant increases in key component costs, including memory"; the Q1 filing cited "significant increases in key component prices, including memory and commodities". On the Q2 call, President William Lu said the company could not pass the whole increase to consumers. Phones, TVs and cars all carry memory, so the squeeze hits every segment at once.
02
A car business still losing money. The EV, AI and new-initiatives segment lost RMB5.7B in H1 2026 (RMB3.1B in Q1, RMB2.6B in Q2), in a mainland car market where retail volume fell 22% in Q2. Xiaomi's average selling price per vehicle dropped 9.6% YoY, which the company attributes to fewer deliveries of the pricey SU7 Ultra.
03
Safety headlines move the stock. On October 13, 2025, after reports that the doors of a burning SU7 failed to open in a fatal Chengdu crash (CNBC), the shares fell as much as 8.7% intraday and closed 5.71% lower. In August 2026 Xiaomi filed with China's market regulator (SAMR) to recall 390,435 SU7 cars of the 2024 series, part of a recall of more than 7 million vehicles across several carmakers over interior emergency door releases that are hard to find; the fix is warning labels and an over-the-air software update (CnEVPost, Aug 21, 2026). A car brand carries a different kind of headline risk than a phone brand.
04
One founder controls the vote. Lei Jun's Class A shares carry about 60.8% of shareholder votes. Outside investors cannot outvote him on ordinary resolutions, and he holds both the chairman and CEO roles.
05
India tax dispute. Indian authorities allege Xiaomi India improperly deducted costs including royalty payments; INR49.17B (about RMB3.54B) of its bank balances were treated as restricted at June 30, 2026. Xiaomi has made no material provision and says it has valid grounds to respond.

Notable One-Off Items

Two items blur the reported profit. First, investment gains: Xiaomi holds stakes in about 410 companies (book value RMB92.0B), and Q2 2026 IFRS profit of RMB9.5B included a RMB6.5B fair-value gain on financial instruments, driven by listed-equity and preferred-share investments. That is why IFRS profit nearly doubled from Q1 while adjusted profit, which strips such gains out, rose only 2.4% to RMB6.2B. Read the adjusted line for the operating trend. Second, buybacks: Xiaomi cancelled 477.5M repurchased Class B shares in five batches between March 19 and August 11, 2026 (2026 Interim Report), so per-share figures now benefit from a shrinking count — modestly, since that is under 2% of the total.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of HK$21.35 to the October 2, 2026 close of HK$24.24, Xiaomi rose 13.5% — a flat-looking number that hides a round trip of almost sevenfold. The stock fell to HK$8.81 in October 2022, climbed to HK$59.95 by June 2025 (an intraday high of HK$61.45 on June 27, 2025), then gave back nearly 60% in fifteen months. Xiaomi has paid no dividends in the window, so Yahoo's dividend-adjusted series is identical to these closes and the price return is the total return.

The 6-period monthly RSI shows both extremes. It hit 97.7 in February 2025, the month of a 35.9% gain, and fell to 14.0 in June 2026 at the end of a nine-month losing streak. After July's rebound it has slid back to 31.0, on the edge of oversold, and the price sits below its 10-month moving average of about HK$28.67. The trend is still down; the selling is no longer as one-sided as in June.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 18x trailing adjusted profit at the bottom of a memory cycle, for a company with RMB180B of net cash — about a third of its market value — a 76.8%-margin internet business riding on 766.5M users, a 1.16B-device ecosystem still growing 17% a year, and a car business that grew deliveries 28% in a market that shrank 22%. It has already spent more on buybacks in 2026 than in all of 2025. If memory prices peak, the margin loss reverses across phones, TVs and cars at once, and consensus has adjusted EPS rising 39.6% in 2027. Management already says memory-cost increases are slowing and the hardest stretch for phones is behind it. Twenty-three of 31 analysts rate it a Buy, with a median target 41% above the price.

Bear Case

Xiaomi's model is to sell hardware near cost, so it has a thin cushion against a component shock: smartphone gross margin is already 8.5%, and Q2 missed the analyst average on both revenue and adjusted profit. The car business lost RMB5.7B in H1 2026 and needs to more than double its September delivery pace to reach its own 550,000 target. IFRS profit leans on investment gains, operating cash flow collapsed to RMB2.1B in H1, and consensus has FY2026 adjusted EPS falling to RMB0.94 from RMB1.47 — 22x forward. One founder controls the vote. A stock that fell nine months in a row is not obviously done falling.

Top 3 Risks

01
The memory squeeze lasts. Xiaomi listed rising memory costs among its headwinds in both 2026 quarters, and its president said on the Q2 call it cannot pass all of the increase on. Every quarter it lasts, the phone and TV margins stay compressed and the car margin with them.
02
EV losses and the delivery target. Q4 needs roughly 87,800 deliveries a month to reach 550,000, against September's 40,000-plus. A miss plus continued segment losses would undercut the growth story that justified the 2025 valuation.
03
Mainland demand and policy. IoT revenue fell 19.2% as China cut national trade-in subsidies, and mainland car retail fell 22% in Q2. Mainland China supplied 64.0% of Xiaomi's Q2 revenue.
Industry Commentary
Memory costs land on the device makers
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Memory makers are in a boom — CXMT swung to a RMB35.43B first-quarter operating profit from a loss a year earlier (CNBC) — and device makers are paying for it. Xiaomi's Q2 2026 filing leads with "significant increases in key component costs, including memory," and Omdia counted global smartphone shipments down 6% in Q2. When Chinese DRAM maker CXMT listed in Shanghai on July 27, 2026, its shares rose nearly 466% on the first day; Theodore Shou, CEO of Yiyi Capital, told CNBC that day that sentiment around the memory cycle was nearing a short-term peak and that today's memory margins "have to normalize over a cycle." For Xiaomi that normalisation is the bull case: it is a price-taker on chips, so its margins recover when memory makers' margins fall. Xiaomi's answer meanwhile is mix — fewer cheap phones, a record RMB1,351 ASP — which protects revenue per phone but costs volume, as the 26.5% fall in shipments shows.

China's car market: growth inside a shrinking pond
ONGOING HEADWIND

Mainland passenger-car retail volume fell 22% year-on-year in Q2 2026 (CPCA, cited by Xiaomi), and retail sales of extended-range cars — the category Sky Nomad enters — fell 15.85% in the first eight months (CPCA via CnEVPost). Xiaomi still grew deliveries, taking share in a falling market, while its average selling price fell 9.6% YoY on fewer SU7 Ultra deliveries. The FY2025 model showed what scale does: the segment reached its first operating profit (RMB0.9B) in the year it delivered 411,082 cars. In 2026, with quarterly deliveries of 80,856 and 104,199, it is back to losses. The car business needs volume to cover its factories; the market is not supplying it.

The valuation gap — bull and bear framing
EMERGING TAILWIND

At HK$24.24 Xiaomi is valued at RMB534.3B: about 1.2x trailing sales, 17.8x trailing adjusted profit and 22x the FY2026 consensus — against a Hang Seng Index P/E of 14.16 at August 31. Strip out the RMB180B of net cash and the operating business is valued at roughly RMB354B. The bull framing: this is a cyclical trough priced as permanent, with an ecosystem, a cash pile and an aggressive buyback underneath. The bear framing: earnings are still falling (consensus adjusted EPS of RMB0.94 for 2026 against RMB1.47 for 2025), the car division is consuming the hardware profit, and a premium to the index is hard to defend while both are true. The tell will be the smartphone gross margin and the Q4 delivery count: both are reported, and the next prints come with Q3 results and the year-end delivery tally.
