01 Company Overview

Xylem is the plumbing behind the plumbing. It makes and services the pumps, treatment systems, meters and sensors that move, clean and measure water for utilities, factories and buildings in more than 150 countries. It was born on October 31, 2011, when ITT Corporation spun off its water businesses, and it has grown mostly by buying: Sensus, the smart-meter maker, for $1.7B in 2016, then Evoqua Water Technologies, a water and wastewater treatment and services provider, in an all-stock deal valued at $7.5B that closed in May 2023. FY2025 revenue was $9.04B, up 5.52%, with net income of $957M and diluted EPS of $3.92. At $88.23, on the 229.33M shares outstanding at October 23, 2026 (10-Q cover), the market values it at $20.23B (our arithmetic).

Here is the puzzle this report is about. The business keeps getting better on paper — adjusted EBITDA margin up 70 bps to 23.9% in Q3 2026, GAAP EPS up 34%, orders up 10% organically and backlog at $5.5B, up 13%, per the CFO on the call. But the top line went backwards. On October 27 Xylem cut its 2026 outlook to revenue of about $8.95B, down 1%, with organic growth now flat (it was 2–3% in July), and trimmed adjusted EPS guidance to $5.40–$5.45. The stock fell 14.2% that day and closed October 28 at $88.23, after touching a 52-week low of $87.40 during that session, 41.5% below the $150.85 month-end close of October 2025. The causes management gave: utilities deferring meter purchases under affordability pressure (now spreading from electric to water meters), large industrial projects ramping more slowly than planned, and an 80/20 programme that is deliberately walking away from low-margin sales. The response was structural: Xylem intends to sell two businesses that the CFO put at about 9% of revenue and under 5% of EBITDA, and from January 1, 2027 will report three segments instead of four. The question for an owner is unchanged but sharper: is this a slow-growth pump maker with better margins, or a water-technology compounder in the middle of a messy clean-up?

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Installed base in utilities Multi-decade outsourced water contracts Meter + software lock-in Breadth: pump to meter to treatment Service & aftermarket network Largest pure-play water tech (post-Evoqua) Moderate leverage (D/E 0.29)

The moat is trust in the unglamorous. A water utility does not change pump or meter vendor lightly: the equipment runs for decades, failure is a public-health event, and every meter in the field ties the utility to the software that reads it. Evoqua added the service side — running treatment for industrial customers under long contracts, the kind of deal Dow signed in Q2. The weakness is that none of this protects the pace of spending. Utilities fund capex through regulated rates and municipal budgets, and in 2026 that pace is being set by affordability, not by need. Q3 showed the moat and the weakness side by side: orders up 10% organically, revenue flat, because customers are committing but taking longer to spend.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
Growth has stalled. 2026 guidance is now about $8.95B of revenue, down 1% reported and flat organically — the second cut this year. StockAnalysis's consensus now has FY2026 revenue at $8.95B and FY2027 at $9.35B (+4.5%, our arithmetic), so the Street is already counting on a rebound next year.
02
Metering waits on utility budgets. MCS revenue fell 5% organically in Q3, and the CFO said the affordability pressure on electric meters is now reaching water meters. Utilities deferring purchases under affordability pressure is not something Xylem can fix with execution.
03
80/20 shrinks the top line on purpose. Walking away from low-margin sales raises margins but makes revenue look worse. On the Q3 call the CEO put the 2027 headwind at 50 to 100 basis points of growth, smaller than this year's. Selling two businesses worth about 9% of revenue will shrink the reported top line further.
04
A lot is moving at once. A $1.46B pump acquisition due to close in Q4, two divestitures, a new three-segment structure from January 1, a CFO who started on September 1, and $1.58B of buybacks in nine months, which the CFO said lifted net debt to 1.1x adjusted EBITDA (Section 04).

Notable One-Off Items

Three things bend the reported numbers. First, buybacks: Xylem repurchased $1.58B of stock in the first nine months of 2026 (cash flow statement), $337M of it in Q3 (our arithmetic against $1.243B in the first half) — about 7.8% of today's $20.23B market cap. Shares outstanding were 229.33M on October 23, 2026 against 244.1M diluted shares a year earlier in Q3 2025, so per-share growth is running well ahead of profit growth: Q3 net income rose 28% ($291M from $227M) while GAAP EPS rose 34%. Second, the GAAP-to-adjusted gap: Q3 GAAP EPS was $1.25 against adjusted $1.44. This quarter the special items cut both ways — $33M of refunds of tariffs collected under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) were counted as a special gain and left out of adjusted earnings, alongside acquisition, divestiture and restructuring costs ($50M of restructuring and impairment charges in nine months). Third, portfolio pruning: the international metering business was sold on May 26, 2026, divestitures brought in $95M in nine months, 80/20 walkaway keeps removing low-margin sales, and two more businesses (about 9% of revenue, per the CFO) are now slated for sale.

RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX (RSI) — 6-PERIOD, MONTHLY
⚠ STATIC DATA AS OF OCT 28, 2026
RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

From the October 2021 month-end close of $130.59 to the October 28, 2026 settled close of $88.23, XYL lost 32.4% of its price over five years. Over the same dates SPY, the S&P 500 fund, rose 68.2% on price. Dividends of roughly $1.12–$1.72 a year add back about $7 a share over the period (our estimate from the annual dividend-per-share series), which still leaves the total return deeply negative. The endpoints hide a round trip. The stock lost 40% into a $78.18 close in June 2022, nearly doubled to a $150.85 month-end in October 2025, and has since given back 41.5% in a year — most of the 2022–2025 gain.

The 6-period monthly RSI reads 16.6 — deeply oversold, and the lowest reading on the chart since June 2022 (7.1). It was 22.1 in May 2026. The 10-month moving average sits at about $114.94, 30% above the price. On daily data the 14-day RSI is 23.5 (StockAnalysis). That is the shape of a stock in a downtrend that has become stretched, not one that has turned. Oversold readings in 2022 were followed by a sharp recovery; they were also accompanied by a market-wide selloff, which is not what is happening now: this fall is about Xylem's own guidance.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Margins keep doing what 80/20 promised: adjusted EBITDA margin up 70bps to 23.9% in Q3 even as revenue fell, and guided to about 23.2% for the year, 100bps above 2025. Demand has not gone away; it is waiting. Orders rose 10% organically, metering orders 30%, backlog is $5.5B (+13%), and the CFO calls the metering problem one of pace, not demand. The walkaway drag should shrink to 50–100bps in 2027, and selling two businesses worth ~9% of revenue but under 5% of EBITDA leaves a higher-margin company. The pump acquisition buys 30%-plus EBITDA margins at ~11.6x, about the multiple the whole company now trades at. At 19.6x trailing GAAP earnings and about 16x this year's adjusted EPS guidance, the premium that made the stock hard to own has mostly gone, $1.58B of stock was retired in nine months, and the average analyst target is still 48% above the price.

Bear Case

Organic growth guidance for 2026 has gone from 2–4% to flat, cut in July and again in October. The latest reason is new: affordability pressure has spread from electric to water meters, so the metering slowdown is widening, not ending. Water Infrastructure orders fell organically again. The company is doing about $1.9B of acquisitions, two divestitures and a segment overhaul in the same months it changed CFO, while cash fell from $1.48B to $708M and borrowings rose to $2.90B. Adjusted EPS guidance was cut too, so the "earnings up, growth down" defence no longer holds. The stock sold off after the February, July and October results, which says investors want proof of growth, and the next real test is the February guide.

Top 3 Risks

01
Growth does not come back in 2027. Guidance has been cut twice this year, to flat organic. Consensus already assumes FY2027 revenue of $9.35B (+4.5%) and EPS of $5.98 (StockAnalysis). If the February guide falls short of that, even a 19.6x trailing multiple has room to fall further.
02
Metering delays spread and last. MCS revenue fell 5% organically in Q3, and the slowdown has spread from electric to water meters. Management's explanation is affordability pressure on utilities, and utility budgets do not follow Xylem's guidance. Strong Q3 orders only help if utilities take delivery.
03
Execution load. A $1.46B acquisition closing in Q4, two bolt-ons to integrate, two businesses to sell, a three-segment reorganisation on January 1 and a CFO in her second quarter. Any one is manageable. All of them at once raise the odds of another slip in guidance or cash conversion.
Industry Commentary
Utility spending meets affordability
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Water and electric utilities pay for new meters and pipes through the rates they charge customers. On the Q3 call Xylem's CFO put it this way: "The affordability pressures that have weighed on electric metering are now extending into water metering purchases." The need does not go away — ageing networks still leak and old meters still fail — but spending can be pushed back. For Xylem that shows up first in Measurement & Control Solutions: organic orders rose 15% in Q1, 2% in Q2 and 30% in Q3, while Q3 revenue fell 5% organically. The key question is whether this is a delay or a cancellation. Q3's orders point to delay — utilities are signing but taking delivery more slowly — and a delay builds pent-up demand; a cancellation would reset the growth rate.

Simplification vs. growth
ONGOING HEADWIND

Xylem's 80/20 programme shows in margins — operating margin from 9.8% in FY2023 to 15.5% TTM — and in revenue, where walking away from low-margin customers and China business takes about 2 points off growth this year, per the CEO. That trade is sensible for owners if the walkaway ends and the remaining business grows. It is expensive if the market decides the slower growth is the real Xylem. Management has put a date on it: on the Q3 call the CEO said "we plan to be at our peak walk away this year at 2%", and put the 2027 headwind at 50 to 100 basis points of growth. The planned sale of Applied Water International and Specialty Flow Control is the same trade on a larger scale: about 9% of revenue for under 5% of EBITDA, per the CFO. That is a falsifiable promise, and the February 2027 guide is its first test.

Water as an industrial input: data centres, chips, mining
EMERGING TAILWIND

On the Q3 call Pine pointed to AI-related infrastructure and data centres, microelectronics and power generation as growth markets — all industries that need a lot of clean water and have to deal with what they discharge. The evidence so far: data-centre orders up more than 200% again in Q3, Applied Water the only segment growing in Q3 (+9% organic), the Dow outsourcing contract, and a pump acquisition aimed at mining and construction. The bull framing: Xylem is moving from selling to cash-strapped utilities to selling to industrial customers with money to spend. The bear framing: data centres will leave 2026 at about 2% of revenue on a run-rate basis, and one large contract does not make a trend. Both are true today. The one to watch is orders outside the Dow contract: in Q3 they grew in three of four segments.
