01 Company Overview

Block runs two consumer-and-merchant ecosystems: Square, the point-of-sale, payments and business-software stack used by more than 4.5 million sellers, and Cash App, a 59-million-user consumer money app that has quietly turned into a bank-substitute and a lender. FY2025 revenue was $24.19B, up just 0.3% from $24.12B — a number that tells you almost nothing, because roughly a third of Block's reported revenue is the gross proceeds of bitcoin it sells to customers at near-zero margin. The metric management actually guides on is gross profit: $10.36B in FY2025, up 17%, and on track for $12.51B in FY2026, up 21% on the August guidance raise. The company also changed its ticker from SQ to XYZ in January 2025 — the price series below is continuous through that change.

The central debate is not whether Block grows. It is what the earnings power actually is, and who paid for it. In February 2026 the company cut more than 40% of its workforce — headcount now stands at 10,205 — and in the two quarters since, adjusted operating income margin has hit successive records (25% in Q1 2026, 27% in Q2). That is either a business that had years of accumulated slack and has now found operating leverage, or a business that has bought two quarters of margin with a one-time cost reset. The stock trades at 134× trailing GAAP earnings and roughly 17–20× forward adjusted earnings. Reconciling those two numbers is the whole job of this page.

This is the single most important accounting fact about Block, and most screens get it wrong. When a Cash App customer buys bitcoin, Block books the entire sale amount as revenue and the cost of the bitcoin in cost of revenue. In Q2 2026 that was $1.89B of revenue producing $72M of gross profit — a 3.8% margin. Across the first half of 2026 bitcoin revenue was $3.69B ($1.80B in Q1, $1.89B in Q2) against total H1 revenue of $12.68B, i.e. 29% of reported revenue generating about 2.4% of reported gross profit (derived from the segment figures, not a company-stated ratio). Three consequences follow. First, headline revenue growth is meaningless for this company — FY2025 revenue grew 0.3% while gross profit grew 17%. Second, the gross margin "improvement" from 25.15% (FY2021) to 46.57% (TTM) is substantially a mix effect: bitcoin revenue is shrinking (−13% YoY in Q2) faster than the rest is growing, so the low-margin denominator keeps falling. Third, this is exactly why management guides on gross profit and adjusted operating income and why any peer P/S comparison against PayPal or Toast is structurally invalid.

Adjacent bitcoin assets: the FY2025 10-K classifies holdings as "investing purposes" and "operating purposes"; Q1 2026 GAAP results included a $173M bitcoin loss, so the balance-sheet position moves earnings independently of the trading business. Specific holdings quantities were not verified from sources fetched. Hardware/protocol projects (Bitkey, Proto, Spiral) sit here too.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
59M Cash App actives 4.5M+ Square sellers $316B annual inflows Proprietary underwriting data Two-sided commerce loop Bank charters & licences Consumer brand

The durable advantage is data plus distribution in the same box. Cash App sees the inflow — paychecks, transfers, card spend — for 59 million people and 9.4 million primary banking customers, and Square sees the receipts for 4.5 million merchants and 5.9 billion transactions a year. That gives Block a credit-underwriting dataset that a standalone lender has to buy and a bank has to wait for. The September 2026 decision to open Cash App Score to third-party lenders via Nova Credit is the tell: management believes the underwriting data is good enough to sell. Lending originations up 59% (Q2 2026) and 69% (Q4 2025) without a corresponding rise in user count is the mechanism working. The moat is not the payment rails — those are commoditised and Stripe, Fiserv and Toast compete hard on price — it is the ability to originate credit cheaply against behaviour Block already observes.

Key Vulnerabilities

—
Growth now depends on credit, not users. Monthly transacting actives were 59 million in Q4 2025 and 59 million in Q2 2026. Every point of Cash App's 31% gross profit growth therefore came from monetising the existing base, and the fastest-growing lever is lending. Credit businesses look like high-margin software until the cycle turns, at which point provisions arrive all at once. Block does not disclose a loss rate or reserve ratio in the sources fetched.
—
The 40% headcount cut is unrepeatable. Margins hit records in the two quarters immediately following a February 2026 reduction of more than 40% of staff. Some of that is genuine operating leverage; some is simply a smaller cost base against a revenue line that was already booked. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of −$309M included $852M of restructuring charges. The test is FY2027, when the cut has annualised and there is nothing left to remove.
—
Bitcoin cuts both ways on the income statement. Bitcoin gross profit fell 31% YoY in Q2 and 26% in Q1, and a $173M mark-to-market bitcoin loss hit Q1 GAAP earnings. The economics are tiny (2.3% of gross profit) but the volatility is not, and the brand is entangled with an asset class Block does not control.
—
Regulatory surface area is enormous and widening. Block operates as a payments company, a lender, a broker-dealer, a virtual-currency business and — pending the September 2026 Builders Bank trust-charter application — a national trust bank. The FY2025 10-K lists all of these as risk factors. Each new licence adds capital, compliance and examination exposure that a pure software company never carries.

Five-year trajectory. From the October 2021 monthly close of $254.50 to the September 2026 close of $79.07, the stock has lost 68.9% — a compound annual return of roughly −20.9%, with no dividend to cushion it. Set that against the operating record over the same window: gross profit went from $4.44B (FY2021) to $10.42B (FY2025), up 134%, and is guided to $12.51B this year. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Read the income statement in the right order, or you will get this company wrong. Start at the top and Block looks stagnant: FY2025 revenue $24.19B, up 0.3%. Start one line lower and it looks like a compounder: FY2025 gross profit $10.36B, up 17%, guided to $12.51B and 21% growth this year. The difference is bitcoin. Block books the full sale price of every bitcoin a Cash App customer buys as revenue — $1.89B in Q2 2026 alone, 28.5% of all revenue, generating $72M of gross profit at a 3.8% margin. That line is also shrinking (−13% YoY), which mechanically flatters gross margin (25.15% in FY2021 → 46.57% TTM) without anything improving. Any screen that ranks this company on revenue growth, price-to-sales or gross margin is measuring an accounting artefact.

What actually happened in 2026. In February, Block cut more than 40% of its workforce — headcount is now 10,205 — and took $852M of restructuring charges in Q1, producing a GAAP net loss of −$309M in the same quarter that adjusted operating income was +$728M at a then-record 25% margin. Q2 took the margin to 27% on $864M of adjusted operating income, with adjusted EBITDA of $1.17B and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.02, up 65%. Guidance has been raised twice, from $12.20B of gross profit in February to $12.33B in May to $12.51B in August. The honest summary is that Block traded a very large one-time charge for a permanently smaller cost base, and the market has paid for two quarters of it — the stock is +51% from its March 2025 low but still fell roughly 5% on the Q2 beat itself.

Where the growth now comes from — and why that changes the risk. Cash App's gross profit grew 31% in Q2 on a user base that did not grow at all: 59 million monthly transacting actives in Q4 2025, 59 million in Q2 2026. The growth came from depth — primary banking actives +17% to 9.4 million, inflows +13% to $87B, and lending originations +59% (after +69% in Q4 2025). Square, meanwhile, has genuinely re-accelerated: GPV of $72.8B, +13%, with U.S. GPV growth at 9.8% and international at 28%, and gross profit growth improving from +7% (Q4'25) to +9% (Q1'26) to +13% (Q2'26). So the two halves are working, but the faster half is increasingly a lender. Block discloses origination growth and does not disclose loss rates or reserve coverage in anything fetched for this report. Credit revenue is recognised up front and credit losses arrive later; that asymmetry is the single largest unmodelled risk on this page.

Valuation — and which earnings base. The answer moves by a factor of seven depending on the denominator, so be explicit. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $0.59, XYZ trades at 134× — arithmetically correct and analytically useless, since the base includes the $852M restructuring charge and a $173M bitcoin loss that will not recur. (ii) On FY2026 consensus EPS of $4.05 it trades at 19.5×, and on the company's own adjusted EPS guide of $4.02, 19.7×; the reported forward P/E of 17.28× implies a slightly later blended base. (iii) On TTM free cash flow of $3.88B the stock trades at about 12.3× FCF, an 8.1% free cash flow yield — and this is the number that does the most work, because FCF is not distorted by either the bitcoin denominator or the restructuring charge. (iv) On the FY2026 adjusted operating income guide of $3.47B against a $48.23B enterprise value, roughly 13.9×. The defensible band is (ii) through (iv): 12–20× depending on how much credit you give adjusted measures. That is not an expensive stock for 21% gross profit growth — which is precisely why 38 of 45 analysts rate it Buy with a $98.35 average target. The catch is that (ii) and (iv) both add back stock-based compensation and both assume the post-restructuring cost base holds through FY2027, when there is nothing left to cut and the comparison gets hard.

Bull Case

Both engines are working simultaneously for the first time in three years: Cash App gross profit +31% on deepening monetisation of 59M users, and Square GPV +13% with U.S. growth at a three-year high. The February restructuring converted a bloated cost base into a 27% adjusted operating margin, and guidance has been raised twice since. At roughly 12× trailing free cash flow and 19–20× forward adjusted earnings for 21% gross profit growth — a "Rule of 40" score of 49 — the valuation does not demand heroics. Net debt is effectively nil ($7.17B debt, $6.74B cash), $2.3B of stock was repurchased in FY2025, and the Builders Bank charter application plus the Nova Credit deal show the underwriting data being turned into new revenue lines.

Bear Case

The margin records follow a 40%+ headcount cut, not a demand inflection, and that lever can only be pulled once — FY2027 laps it with nothing left to remove. Cash App's user base has been flat at 59M for three quarters, so all growth now comes from lending into a consumer credit cycle whose loss rates Block does not disclose. Reported revenue growth of 5% and a 1.43% net margin are what a screen sees; a beta of 2.52 and an 84% peak-to-trough drawdown are what shareholders have lived. The stock fell on the Q2 beat, 38 of 45 analysts are already positive, and the only recent initiation was a Hold at $82 — the good news is in the price and the sell side has no upgrades left to give.

Top 3 Risks

01
Undisclosed consumer credit quality. Lending originations grew 69% and 59% in the last two reported growth prints and are the primary driver of Cash App's 31% gross profit growth, but no charge-off rate, reserve ratio or vintage performance appears in any source fetched for this report. If the consumer credit cycle turns, provisions hit at exactly the moment the growth narrative depends on originations accelerating.
02
The cost cut annualises. Record margins in Q1 and Q2 2026 both sit inside twelve months of a 40%+ workforce reduction. From roughly Q1 2027 the comparison includes the smaller base on both sides, and margin expansion has to come from revenue leverage alone. A guide that implies continued expansion into FY2027 without a new lever is the thing to stress-test.
03
Regulatory expansion with a 2.52 beta. Block is simultaneously a payments processor, lender, broker-dealer, virtual-currency business and — pending the Builders Bank application filed September 8, 2026 — an aspiring national trust bank, each with its own capital and examination regime. Add a bitcoin balance-sheet position that produced a $173M loss in a single quarter, and the equity carries operating leverage, credit leverage and asset-price leverage at the same time. Position size, not conviction, is the control variable here.
  Industry Commentary
Fintech Is Being Repriced as Credit, Not Software
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Every consumer fintech that ran out of user growth has turned to lending, and the market is starting to price them accordingly. The evidence is in the same-day multiples: PayPal at 10.05× earnings and Fiserv at 9.38× with its market cap down 63.6% over a year, against Toast — still a pure software-and-payments story — at 40.50×. Block sits between the two identities: 62% of its gross profit now comes from a segment whose fastest-growing line is consumer credit (originations +59% in Q2 2026, +69% in Q4 2025), while it is valued on software-style forward multiples. Whichever identity the market settles on determines the multiple more than any operating result will.
Merchant Acquiring Is a Price War Fought With Software
ONGOING HEADWIND
Square's $250B of FY2025 GPV is real scale, but payment processing itself is a commodity: the differentiation is the software wrapped around it, and everyone knows it. Toast grew revenue 23.1% to $6.80B targeting restaurants specifically; Fiserv's Clover attacks the same small-merchant base from a $20.87B revenue install base; Stripe and Shopify Payments compete online. Block's answer — selling hardware at a loss to acquire sellers, then monetising through software and Square Loans — is why management quotes Square gross profit "excluding hardware losses." It works (U.S. GPV growth just hit a three-year high), but it means the segment's incremental margin depends on attach rates, not on processing spreads, which are only going one way.
Charters and Stablecoins Are the Next Land Grab
EMERGING TAILWIND
Block's September 8, 2026 application for Builders Bank — an uninsured national trust bank for bitcoin and stablecoin custody — puts it in the queue alongside the rest of the industry for direct federal banking status, and its September 1 deal to license Cash App Score to outside lenders through Nova Credit monetises the underwriting data without originating the loan. Bull framing: a charter removes the bank-partner dependency the 10-K flags as a risk, cuts funding costs on a lending book growing 59% a year, and turns custody into a fee stream — the pieces that would justify re-rating Block toward a financial-infrastructure multiple rather than a payments one. Bear framing: charters take time, invite examination, consume capital, and none of the above is yet in a revenue line. The verifiable position today is $79.07 against a $98.35 consensus target, 134× trailing GAAP earnings, and a business whose last two quarters of record margin followed a one-time 40% cost reduction.
