01 Company Overview

Block runs two consumer-and-merchant ecosystems: Square, the point-of-sale, payments and business-software stack used by more than 4.5 million sellers, and Cash App, a 60-million-user consumer money app that has quietly turned into a bank substitute and a lender. FY2025 revenue was $24.19B, up just 0.3% from $24.12B — a number that tells you almost nothing, because more than a quarter of Block's reported revenue (26.8% in Q3 2026) is the gross proceeds of bitcoin it sells to customers at near-zero margin. The metric management actually guides on is gross profit: $10.36B in FY2025, up 17%, and on track for $12.63B in FY2026, up 22% on the November guidance raise. The company also changed its ticker from SQ to XYZ in January 2025 — the price series below is continuous through that change.

The central debate is not whether Block grows. It is what the earnings power actually is, and who paid for it. In February 2026 the company announced it would cut more than 40% of its workforce, from over 10,000 people to just under 6,000, and in the three quarters since, adjusted operating income, which leaves out restructuring and legal charges, has hit successive record margins measured against gross profit (25% in Q1 2026, 27% in Q2, 29% in Q3). That is either a business that carried years of slack and has now learned to grow without adding cost, or a business that bought three quarters of margin with a one-time reset. Block's first outlook for 2027, published with Q3, calls for at least 15% gross profit growth and a 32% margin, so management is arguing for the first reading. Against that sits a $764M accrual for a Justice Department settlement over Cash App's anti-money-laundering controls. The stock trades at 115.5× trailing GAAP earnings and about 16× FY2027 consensus adjusted earnings. Reconciling those two numbers is the whole job of this page.

This is the single most important accounting fact about Block, and most screens get it wrong. When a Cash App customer buys bitcoin, Block books the entire sale amount as revenue and the cost of the bitcoin in cost of revenue. In Q3 2026 that was $1.80B of revenue producing $52M of gross profit — a 2.9% margin, or 26.8% of reported revenue generating 1.6% of reported gross profit (our arithmetic on the shareholder letter's figures). Gross profit on this line halved from a year earlier, which Block's letter attributes mainly to a deliberate fee cut. Three consequences follow. First, headline revenue growth is meaningless for this company — FY2025 revenue grew 0.3% while gross profit grew 17%. Second, the gross margin "improvement" from 25.0% (FY2021) to 47.50% (TTM) substantially reflects a change in what the revenue is made of: bitcoin revenue is shrinking (−11% YoY in Q3) while the rest grows, so the low-margin part of revenue keeps getting smaller. Third, this is exactly why management guides on gross profit and adjusted operating income and why any peer P/S comparison against PayPal or Toast does not compare like with like.

Adjacent bitcoin assets: Block's filings split its bitcoin into holdings for investment and holdings used in operations; GAAP results included a $173M bitcoin loss in Q1 2026 and a $230M bitcoin gain in Q3, so the balance-sheet position moves earnings independently of the trading business. Hardware/protocol projects (Bitkey, Proto, Spiral) sit here too.
03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities
60M Cash App actives 4.5M+ Square sellers $316B annual inflows Proprietary underwriting data Two-sided commerce loop Bank charters & licences Consumer brand

The durable advantage is data plus distribution in the same box. Cash App sees the inflow — paychecks, transfers, card spend — for 60 million people and 9.4 million primary banking customers, and Square sees the receipts for 4.5 million merchants and 5.9 billion transactions a year. That gives Block a credit-underwriting dataset that a standalone lender has to buy and a bank has to wait for. The September 2026 decision to open Cash App Score to third-party lenders via Nova Credit is the tell: management believes the underwriting data is good enough for other lenders to use. Lending originations up 40% (Q3 2026), 59% (Q2 2026), 82% (Q1 2026) and 69% (Q4 2025) on a user count that grew 4% is the mechanism working. The moat is not the payment rails — those are commoditised and Stripe, Fiserv and Toast compete hard on price — it is the ability to originate credit cheaply against behaviour Block already observes.

Key Vulnerabilities

—
Growth depends more on credit than on users. Monthly transacting actives grew 4% in the year to September, to 60 million, while consumer lending originations grew 40% to $19.0B and transaction, loan and consumer receivable losses grew 66% to $602M. Credit businesses look like high-margin software until the economy turns, when the money set aside for bad loans jumps all at once.
—
The 40% headcount cut is unrepeatable. Margins hit records in the three quarters immediately following the February 2026 reduction. Some of that is the business genuinely growing faster than its costs; some is simply a smaller cost base against revenue that was already coming in. The Q1 2026 GAAP loss of −$309M included $852M of restructuring and other charges, a figure that also covers accrued legal contingencies. The test is FY2027, when the cut is fully in the comparison and Block's own outlook asks for a 32% margin.
—
Bitcoin cuts both ways on the income statement. Bitcoin gross profit fell 50% YoY in Q3, 31% in Q2 and 26% in Q1. Marking its bitcoin to market took $173M off Q1 GAAP earnings and added $230M in Q3. The economics are tiny (1.6% of gross profit) but the volatility is not, and the brand is entangled with an asset class Block does not control.
—
Regulatory exposure is large and now has a price. In October 2026 Block and the Justice Department reached an agreement in principle on a deferred prosecution agreement covering a charge that it failed to maintain an effective anti-money-laundering program. The expected net payment is about $764M, accrued in full at September 30, over a three-year term. The 10-Q says it still needs a final agreement and court approval and that losses above the accrual are reasonably possible. Block also operates as a payments company, a lender, a broker-dealer and a virtual-currency business, and the September 2026 Builders Bank trust-charter application would add a national trust bank. Each licence adds capital, compliance and examination exposure that a pure software company never carries.

In plain English: RSI measures how much of the recent move has been up versus down. Above 70 the stock has run hard and may be stretched; below 30 it has been beaten up and may be washed out. It is a momentum gauge, not a valuation signal.

Five-year trajectory. From the November 2021 month-end close of $208.33 to the November 6, 2026 close of $85.48, the stock has lost 59.0% — a compound annual return of roughly −16.5%, with no dividend to cushion it. Over the same dates SPY rose 61.4% and QQQ 66.6% on price alone. Set that against the operating record: gross profit went from $4.42B (FY2021) to $10.36B (FY2025), up 134%, and is guided to $12.63B this year. An investor who owned this business for five years was right about the business and wrong about the price, which is what happens when investors stop paying zero-interest-rate prices for growth. The path inside that number is violent even by growth-stock standards: −80.7% from November 2021 to the October 2023 close of $40.25, then +120.0% to $88.55 in November 2024, then −38.6% to $54.33 in March 2025, then +57.3% to $85.48 now. A beta of 2.56 is not a statistical curiosity here; it is a description.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Read the income statement in the right order, or you will get this company wrong. Start at the top and Block looks stagnant: FY2025 revenue $24.19B, up 0.3%. Start one line lower and it looks like a steady grower: FY2025 gross profit $10.36B, up 17%, guided to $12.63B and 22% growth this year. The difference is bitcoin. Block books the full sale price of every bitcoin a Cash App customer buys as revenue — $1.80B in Q3 2026 alone, 26.8% of all revenue, generating $52M of gross profit at a 2.9% margin. That line is also shrinking (−11% YoY), which mechanically flatters gross margin (25.0% in FY2021 → 47.50% TTM) without anything improving. Any screen that ranks this company on revenue growth, price-to-sales or gross margin is measuring an accounting artefact.

What actually happened in 2026. In February, Block announced it would cut more than 40% of its workforce, from over 10,000 people to just under 6,000. Q1 carried $852M of restructuring and other charges, including accrued legal contingencies, producing a GAAP net loss of −$309M in the same quarter that adjusted operating income was +$728M at a then-record 25% margin. Q2 took the margin to 27% on $864M and Q3 to 29% on $923M, with adjusted EBITDA of $1.23B and adjusted diluted EPS of $1.13, up 109%. Guidance has been raised three times, from $12.20B of gross profit in February to $12.33B in May, $12.51B in August and $12.63B in November. The honest summary is that Block traded a very large one-time charge for a smaller cost base, and the market's verdict has swung: the stock rose 16.8% the day after the cut was announced, fell 6.2% the day after the Q2 beat and rose 15.3% the day after Q3. The other event of 2026 is legal: an agreement in principle with the Justice Department, with about $764M set aside for it.

Where the growth now comes from — and why that changes the risk. Cash App's gross profit grew 27% in Q3 on a user base up 4%: 59 million monthly transacting actives in June, 60 million in September. Much of the rest is depth — primary banking actives +13% to 9.4 million, inflows +10% to $86B, and lending originations +40% to $19.0B (after +82% in Q1, +59% in Q2 and +69% in Q4 2025). Square, meanwhile, has genuinely re-accelerated: GPV of $76.4B, +14%, with U.S. GPV growth at 10.4% and international at 26%, and gross profit growth improving from +7.5% (Q4'25) to +9% (Q1'26), +13% (Q2'26) and +14% (Q3'26). So the two halves are working, but the faster half is increasingly a lender. Transaction, loan and consumer receivable losses rose 66% to $602M in Q3, faster than originations. Block's letter says Borrow loss rates remained healthy; a consumer downturn is the test of that, and the largest risk on this page.

Valuation — and which earnings base. The answer moves by a factor of seven depending on the denominator, so be explicit. (i) On trailing GAAP EPS of $0.74, XYZ trades at 115.5× — arithmetically correct and analytically useless, since the base includes 2026's restructuring and legal charges. (ii) On FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS of $4.14 it trades at 20.6×, also 20.6× on the company's own guide of $4.15, and about 16× on FY2027 consensus of $5.36. (iii) On TTM free cash flow of $3.45B the stock trades at about 15.0× FCF, a 6.7% free cash flow yield — and this is the number that does the most work, because FCF is not distorted by the bitcoin revenue, though the Justice Department payment is still to come out of it. (iv) On the FY2026 adjusted operating income guide of $3.52B against an enterprise value of about $51.0B (market cap plus debt less cash, our calculation), roughly 14.5×. The defensible band is (ii) through (iv): about 14–21× depending on how much credit you give adjusted measures. That is not an expensive stock for 22% gross profit growth — which is why 38 of 47 analysts rate it Buy with a $100.46 average target. The catch is that (ii) and (iv) both leave out the restructuring and legal charges, though Block's letter calls adjusted operating income "fully burdened by share-based compensation", so ordinary stock pay is counted as a cost, and both assume the post-restructuring cost base holds through FY2027, when there is nothing left to cut and the comparison gets hard.

Bull Case

Both engines are working at once: Cash App gross profit +27% with users growing again (60M, +4%), and Square GPV +14%, its strongest growth since Q1 2023. Adjusted operating margin reached a record 29%, guidance has been raised three times this year, and Block's first 2027 outlook calls for at least 15% gross profit growth and a 32% margin. At roughly 15× trailing free cash flow and about 16× FY2027 consensus adjusted earnings, for a business guiding to a "Rule of 40" score of 50, the valuation does not demand heroics. Cash and short-term investments ($8.24B) exceed total debt ($7.54B), the Justice Department matter now has a number on it and that number is accrued, and the Builders Bank charter application and the Nova Credit deal are two attempts at new lines of business.

Bear Case

The margin records follow a 40%+ headcount cut, and that lever can only be pulled once — FY2027 laps it with nothing left to remove. Lending growth slowed to 40% from 59% in a quarter when losses rose 66%, so the credit book is getting more expensive as it gets slower. The Justice Department deal is not final, losses above the accrual are reasonably possible, and a breach in its three-year term could revive the charge. Reported revenue growth of 7% and a 1.80% net margin are what a screen sees; a beta of 2.56 and an 81% fall from November 2021 to October 2023 are what shareholders have lived. After a 15.3% one-day jump the stock is 3.4% from its 52-week high, 38 of 47 analysts are already positive, and the good news may be in the price.

Top 3 Risks

01
Consumer credit quality. Lending originations grew 69%, 82%, 59% and 40% over the last four quarters, and losses grew 66% in Q3. If consumers start falling behind on their loans, the money set aside for bad loans rises at exactly the moment the growth story depends on originations.
02
The cost cut drops out of the comparison. Record margins in Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026 all sit inside twelve months of a 40%+ workforce reduction. From roughly Q1 2027 the comparison includes the smaller base on both sides, and margin expansion has to come from sales growing faster than costs alone. Block's 2027 outlook of a 32% margin is the thing to stress-test.
03
Regulation, with a 2.56 beta. Block has an agreement in principle on a three-year deferred prosecution agreement with the Justice Department, about $764M accrued and court approval still ahead. It is simultaneously a payments processor, lender, broker-dealer, virtual-currency business and — pending the Builders Bank application filed September 8, 2026 — an aspiring national trust bank, each with its own capital and examination regime. Add a bitcoin holding that swung GAAP earnings by $173M one way in Q1 and $230M the other in Q3, and the equity carries business risk, credit risk and asset-price risk at the same time. Position size is the control here.
  Industry Commentary
Fintech Is Being Repriced as Credit, Not Software
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT
Consumer fintechs that run out of user growth tend to turn to lending, and that changes what investors will pay for them. The same-day multiples show the split: PayPal at 11.3× trailing earnings, with its share price down 10.6% over a year, against Toast — still a pure software-and-payments story — at 38.1×. Block sits between the two identities: 64% of its gross profit now comes from a segment whose lending originations grew 40% in Q3 2026 and 69% in Q4 2025, while investors value it on forward adjusted earnings like a software company. Whichever identity the market settles on will shape the price more than any single quarter.
Card Processing for Merchants Is a Price War Fought With Software
ONGOING HEADWIND
Square's $250B of FY2025 GPV is real scale, but payment processing itself is a commodity: the differentiation is the software wrapped around it, and everyone knows it. Toast grew revenue 22.1% to $7.15B targeting restaurants specifically; Fiserv, with $20.80B of company-wide revenue, goes after the same small merchants with Clover; Stripe and Shopify Payments compete online. Block's answer — selling hardware at a loss to acquire sellers, then earning money through software and Square Loans — is why management quotes Square gross profit "excluding hardware." It works (U.S. GPV growth reached 10.4% in Q3), but it means the segment's extra profit depends on how many sellers add paid software, not on the fee earned on each payment.
Charters and Stablecoins Are the Next Land Grab
EMERGING TAILWIND
Block's September 8, 2026 application for Builders Bank — an uninsured national trust bank for bitcoin and stablecoin custody — puts it in the queue alongside other crypto and payments firms seeking federal trust charters, and its September 1 deal to open Cash App Score to outside lenders through Nova Credit puts the underwriting data to work without originating the loan. Bull framing: a charter would turn custody into a fee stream, one of the pieces that would justify valuing Block more like financial infrastructure than a payments company. Bear framing: charters take time, invite examination, consume capital, and none of the above is yet in a revenue line; the Justice Department's anti-money-laundering charge is also an awkward backdrop for an application to a federal bank regulator. The verifiable position today is $85.48 against a $100.46 consensus target, 115.5× trailing GAAP earnings, and a business whose last three quarters of record margin followed a one-time 40% cost reduction.
