01 Company Overview

In 1927 Justin O. Zimmer started making aluminium splints in Warsaw, Indiana. Almost a century later the company he founded is still headquartered in Warsaw, now as Zimmer Biomet: a maker of artificial knees and hips, sports-medicine, extremities, trauma and craniomaxillofacial implants, and the ROSA surgical robot that helps put them in. Zimmer was spun out of Bristol-Myers Squibb and began trading on the NYSE on August 7, 2001 — the same day it entered the S&P 500 — and became Zimmer Biomet when it closed the Biomet acquisition on June 24, 2015, a deal valued at $13.4B when announced. FY2025 revenue was $8.23B, up 7.20%, with GAAP EPS of $3.55.

The story of the last five years is a company that kept selling more implants while its share price kept going the other way. Revenue rose from $6.83B in FY2021 to $8.51B on a trailing basis; the stock went from a $146.36 month-end close in September 2021 to $94.32 today. Part of that gap is structural — in March 2022 Zimmer Biomet handed its dental and spine businesses to shareholders as ZimVie — and part is the market's verdict on a knee franchise that has stopped growing. The live question is whether the newer engines (robotics, the Paragon 28 foot-and-ankle business, a switch to a direct US sales force) can grow fast enough to carry a mature, flat core. The market is paying ~10.8x forward earnings for the answer, which says it is not yet convinced.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Surgeon training & instrument familiarity ROSA robot installed base Regulatory clearances Hospital purchasing contracts Long clinical track records 69.5% gross margin

An orthopedic implant is not bought by the patient; it is chosen by the surgeon, who learned on one system's instruments and would rather not relearn in the middle of a career. That habit — reinforced by hospital purchasing contracts and, increasingly, by a robot on the floor that is designed around one company's implants — is the moat. It shows in a 69.52% gross margin. The weakness is that the same stickiness protects the incumbent's share but not its growth: once the installed base is saturated, the business grows with procedure volume and price, and Zimmer Biomet's own guide says that is low single digits.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The biggest franchise is flat. Knees — 38% of sales — grew 0.4% reported in Q2 2026, with international knees down 1.5% per management, citing China and emerging markets. Every other category has to outrun that drag for the group to grow at all.
02
A sales-force rebuild mid-flight. The company is moving to a predominantly direct, specialised US sales organisation, which it said tempers 2026 sales guidance. Changing who carries the bag in front of surgeons is exactly the kind of transition that leaks share if it goes wrong — and it is happening with an interim CFO.
03
Leverage and acquired growth. Total debt is $7.60B against $464.5M of cash (Debt/EBITDA 2.94x). Part of the growth story is bought — Paragon 28 adds ~110bps to 2026 reported growth — and bought growth flatters the top line for exactly four quarters.
04
Earnings quality gap. Guided adjusted EPS of $8.47–$8.59 against TTM GAAP EPS of $4.12. The reconciling items were not itemised in the sources fetched; a gap that size is the first place to look before accepting a ~10.8x forward multiple as cheap.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). It is a measure of momentum, not of value — a cheap stock can stay oversold for a long time.

How the series treats the ZimVie spin-off. On March 1, 2022 Zimmer Biomet distributed 80.3% of ZimVie — its dental and spine businesses — at one ZimVie share for every ten ZBH shares held on the February 15, 2022 record date, keeping 19.7% to divest later. The chart plots actual traded closes (digrin's unadjusted "Real price" column), so the six points from September 2021 to February 2022 include the value of that ZimVie entitlement and everything from March 2022 on does not. The series is not back-adjusted: a shareholder who held through the spin also received ZimVie shares that this chart does not show. For scale, digrin's own adjusted column steps by about 3.2% across the spin month (ratio of adjusted-to-real prices, February versus March 2022 — my arithmetic), which is the approximate size of the discontinuity.

On that basis, from the September 2021 close of $146.36 to the September 21, 2026 settled close of $94.32, ZBH lost 35.6% of its price — overstated by the spin by roughly three points, and before $0.96 a year of dividends (about $4.80 per share over five years at an unchanged rate). Even generously adjusted, it is a lost half-decade in which revenue rose about a quarter. The shape: a sharp late-2021 drop, a 2022–2023 recovery that peaked at $145.60 in June 2023 — nearly back to the pre-spin level — then a three-year grind lower to $82.33 in May 2026, and a four-month rebound since.

The 6-period monthly RSI bottomed at 15.7 in June 2022, the most oversold reading in the window, and touched the high 20s again through May–July 2025. It now reads 52.0, squarely neutral. The price sits above its 10-month moving average of roughly $90.50 for the first time in a sustained way since 2023 — the long downtrend has at least paused. Momentum has turned; the fundamentals that would justify a turn in trend are still the knee line and the organic guide.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 10.8x forward adjusted earnings for a company with a 69.5% gross margin, $1.20B of trailing free cash flow (~6.7% yield), a guide that was just raised on both revenue and EPS, a technology line growing 21% with record ROSA placements, and up to $1B of buybacks authorised for the year. The stock sits near the bottom of its five-year range with a 0.47 beta and no Sell ratings on a 27-analyst panel. If the direct US sales force lands and robots pull through implant volume, knees stop being a drag, organic growth moves toward the top of the guide, and a double-digit forward multiple on a steady compounder looks too low.

Bear Case

The cheap multiple is on adjusted earnings roughly twice GAAP; on GAAP the stock is at 22.9x trailing. The largest category, knees, grew 0.4%, and part of the headline growth is Paragon 28, which laps this year. The company is re-wiring its US sales force with an interim CFO and $7.60B of debt. Organic guidance of 2.25%–3.25% is what a mature franchise looks like, and five years of shrinking share price while revenue grew says the market has consistently refused to pay for this growth. Eighteen Holds out of 27 is the Street's polite version of the same view.

Top 3 Risks

01
Knee stagnation. 38% of sales grew 0.4% in Q2 2026, with international knees down 1.5% on management's figures. Without a knee recovery, group organic growth is capped near the low end of guidance.
02
Execution of the commercial transformation. A move to a predominantly direct, specialised US sales force, which management said tempers 2026 sales, run by a leadership team with an interim CFO. Disruption to surgeon relationships shows up in share, and share is slow to recover.
03
Earnings quality and leverage. A ~2x gap between adjusted and GAAP EPS whose components were not itemised in the sources fetched, on top of $7.14B of net debt at 2.94x EBITDA. If the adjustments prove recurring, the stock is not at 10.8x — it is at 22.9x.
Industry Commentary
A mature core in a procedure-driven market
STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINT

Joint replacement demand follows ageing populations and surgeon capacity, not the economic cycle — which is why ZBH has a 0.47 beta. The flip side is that growth is bounded: the company's own organic guide for 2026 is 2.25%–3.25%. Within that envelope, share moves slowly and is decided by surgeon preference and hospital contracts. In Q2 2026 Zimmer Biomet's knees lagged its hips by almost five points, and management tied the international knee weakness to China and emerging markets.

The sales-model reset
ONGOING TRANSITION

The company is shifting to a predominantly direct and specialised US sales organisation, which it said tempers 2026 sales guidance but is meant to drive more durable long-term growth. The first read was encouraging — Q2 US organic constant-currency sales rose 4.6%, and management promoted leaders to drive what it calls the commercial transformation — but one quarter is not a trend, and the transition is running without a permanent CFO.

Robots as the lock-in
EMERGING TAILWIND

US technology sales rose more than 50% in Q2 2026 on record ROSA capital sales, and a next-generation ROSA Shoulder launched. The bull framing: every robot placed is a hospital that will keep buying Zimmer Biomet implants for years, which should eventually show up in knee share. The bear framing: robots are a cost of staying in the game rather than a source of excess return — the capital sales are lumpy, and the knee line has not yet responded. The way to adjudicate is the knee growth rate over the next four quarters, not the robot count.
