01 Company Overview

In 1927 Justin O. Zimmer started making aluminium splints in Warsaw, Indiana. Almost a century later the company he founded is still headquartered in Warsaw, now as Zimmer Biomet: a maker of artificial knees and hips, sports-medicine, extremities, trauma and craniomaxillofacial implants, and the ROSA surgical robot that helps put them in. Zimmer was spun out of Bristol-Myers Squibb and began trading on the NYSE on August 7, 2001 — the same day it entered the S&P 500 — and became Zimmer Biomet when it closed the Biomet acquisition on June 24, 2015, a deal valued at $13.4B when announced. FY2025 revenue was $8.23B, up 7.20%, with GAAP EPS of $3.55.

The story of the last five years is a company that kept selling more implants while its share price went the other way. Revenue rose from $6.83B in FY2021 to $8.57B on a trailing basis; the stock went from a $119.60 month-end close in November 2021 to $105.59 at this edition's close. Part of that gap is structural — in March 2022 Zimmer Biomet handed its dental and spine businesses to shareholders as ZimVie — and part is the market's verdict on a knee franchise that is now shrinking: knee sales fell 2.6% in the third quarter of 2026 at constant currency, which ignores exchange-rate swings. The rest of the company moved the other way, with hips, the S.E.T. group and technology each growing 7% or better on that basis, and the stock rose 13.49% on the day of the report. The live question is whether the newer engines (robotics, the Paragon 28 foot-and-ankle business, a switch to a direct US sales force) can carry a core that is going backwards. The market is paying ~12.3x this year's expected adjusted earnings for the answer, more than the ~11x of the last edition but still a price that says it is not yet convinced.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Surgeon training & instrument familiarity ROSA robot installed base Regulatory clearances Hospital purchasing contracts Long clinical track records 69.3% gross margin

An orthopedic implant is not bought by the patient; it is chosen by the surgeon, who learned on one system's instruments and would rather not relearn in the middle of a career. That habit — reinforced by hospital purchasing contracts and, increasingly, by a robot on the floor that is designed around one company's implants — is the moat. It shows in a 69.33% gross margin. The weakness is that the same stickiness protects the incumbent's share but not its growth: once the installed base is saturated, the business grows with procedure volume and price, and Zimmer Biomet's own guide says that is low single digits. Q3's knee decline shows the moat can leak when the people selling to surgeons change.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The biggest franchise is shrinking. Knees — 37% of sales — fell 3.1% reported in Q3 2026, and 2.6% at constant currency, with the US down 2.0% and international down 3.4%. Management pointed to the sales-force change, a competitive knee market and China. Every other category has to outrun that drag for the group to grow at all.
02
A sales-force rebuild mid-flight. The company is moving to a predominantly direct, specialised US sales organisation, which it said tempers 2026 sales guidance. Management says over 75% of the US sales force is now fully dedicated and that the change has been less disruptive than first expected, but Tornos also said "we got one final stage to go through, which is the higher risk." It is happening with an interim CFO and a new head of the Americas, Gary Campbell, in the job since October 1.
03
Leverage. Debt was $7.50B against $399.9M of cash at September 30, 2026 (StockAnalysis puts debt at 2.94 times EBITDA, a rough measure of a year's operating cash earnings), and $1.29B of that debt falls due within a year. Part of 2026's growth was bought: the CFO expects Paragon 28 to contribute 1.1 percentage points to the year's reported sales growth, and that help stopped at its April anniversary.
04
Earnings quality gap. Guided adjusted EPS of $8.50–$8.60 against TTM GAAP EPS of $3.96. A gap that size is the first place to look before accepting ~12.3x expected earnings as cheap.

Notable One-Off Items

Four things distort the reported figures. First, the GAAP/adjusted gap: Q3 2026 GAAP diluted EPS was $1.00 (−13.8%) against adjusted $1.90 (flat YoY); net earnings $191.6M GAAP versus $362.8M adjusted. Second, Paragon 28, acquired April 2025, is expected by the CFO to contribute 1.1 percentage points to 2026 reported sales growth and passed its anniversary in April 2026. Third, the China distributor consolidation, which management called a one-time headwind in fiscal 2026: it cost about one point of Q3 growth and is expected to cost about one point in Q4 and roughly half a point for the year. Fourth, the ZimVie spin-off (March 1, 2022) removed the dental and spine businesses; FY2021 revenue of $6.83B and the pre-March 2022 share price are not like-for-like with what followed.

RSI measures how one-sided recent price moves have been on a 0–100 scale. Readings above 70 mean the stock has risen hard and fast relative to its own recent history ("overbought"); below 30 means it has fallen hard and fast ("oversold"). How the series treats the ZimVie spin-off. On March 1, 2022 Zimmer Biomet distributed 80.3% of ZimVie — its dental and spine businesses — at one ZimVie share for every ten ZBH shares held on the February 15, 2022 record date, keeping 19.7% to divest later. 08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

You are paying about 12.3x this year's expected adjusted earnings for a company with a 69.3% gross margin, $1.29B of trailing free cash flow (~6.5% yield), a guide raised on both revenue and EPS in August and again in November, a technology line growing 12% with US technology sales up more than 30%, a Monogram robot planned for launch in the first half of 2027, and $579.3M of stock already bought back this year. The stock has a 0.47 beta and no Sell ratings on a 27-analyst panel, and it has risen 28.3% from May's month-end close. If the direct US sales force lands and robots pull through implant volume, knees stop being a drag, organic growth holds at the top of the guide or better, and a price of about 12 times expected earnings on a steady business looks too low.

Bear Case

The cheap-looking price rests on adjusted earnings roughly twice GAAP; on GAAP the stock is at 26.7x trailing. The largest category, knees, is shrinking, and management would not say which quarter that turns. The stock rose 13.49% in a day on EPS one cent above the Nasdaq consensus, in a session when Stryker rose 9.37% too. The company is re-wiring its US sales force with an interim CFO, a new head of the Americas and $7.50B of debt, and what Tornos called the higher-risk final stage of that change is still ahead. Organic guidance of 3.0%–3.5% is what a mature franchise looks like, and a share price still lower than five years ago while revenue grew says the market has consistently refused to pay for this growth. Twenty Holds out of 27 is the analysts' polite version of the same view.

01
Knee decline. 37% of sales fell 3.1% reported in Q3 2026, with the US down 2.0% and international down 3.4% at constant currency. Management expects improvement but gives no quarter. Without a knee recovery, group organic growth stays in the low single digits.

Execution of the commercial transformation. A move to a predominantly direct, specialised US sales force, which management said tempers 2026 sales, run by a leadership team with an interim CFO and a new head of the Americas, and with what Tornos called the higher-risk final stage still to come. 03
Earnings quality and leverage. A ~2x gap between adjusted and GAAP EPS, on top of $7.10B of net debt and 2.94x Debt/EBITDA. If the adjustments prove recurring, the stock is not at 12.3x — it is at 26.7x.
Industry Commentary
A mature core in a procedure-driven market
STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINT

Joint replacement demand follows ageing populations and surgeon capacity, not the economic cycle — which is why ZBH has a 0.47 beta. The flip side is that growth is bounded: the company's own organic guide for 2026 is 3.0%–3.5%. Within that envelope, share moves slowly and is decided by surgeon preference and hospital contracts. In Q3 2026 Zimmer Biomet's hips outgrew its knees by about nine percentage points, and management pointed to China and its own sales-force change for the knee weakness.

The sales-model reset
ONGOING TRANSITION

The company is shifting to a predominantly direct and specialised US sales organisation, which it said tempers 2026 sales guidance but is meant to drive more durable long-term growth. On the Q3 call management said over 75% of the US sales force is now fully dedicated, that the change has been less disruptive than first expected and that sales-rep turnover remains in healthy single digits. The cost is showing up where it matters most: Tornos put most of the knee shortfall on commercial execution, and the transition is running without a permanent CFO.

Robots as the lock-in
EMERGING TAILWIND

US technology and data sales rose more than 30% in Q3 2026, after more than 50% in Q2, on demand for ROSA and TMINI systems, and the Monogram robot is targeted for launch in the first half of 2027. The bull framing: every robot placed is a hospital that will keep buying Zimmer Biomet implants for years, which should eventually show up in knee share. The bear framing: robots are a cost of staying in the game rather than a source of excess return — the capital sales are lumpy, and the knee line has so far gone the wrong way. The way to settle it is the knee growth rate over the next four quarters, not the robot count.
