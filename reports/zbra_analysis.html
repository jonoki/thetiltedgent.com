01 Company Overview

Every time a warehouse worker scans a pallet, a nurse scans a wristband or a store clerk checks stock on a handheld, there is a decent chance the device came from Lincolnshire, Illinois. Zebra builds the hardware and software of automatic identification and data capture: rugged mobile computers, barcode scanners, label, wristband and card printers, RFID readers, machine vision and workflow software, sold into retail, manufacturing, healthcare, transportation & logistics and the public sector. FY2025 revenue was $5.40B, up 8.33%, with GAAP diluted EPS of $8.18; revenue over the last four quarters, through Q3 2026, has since climbed to $6.14B (+16.9%).

The story of the last five years is a round trip that has not finished. The stock closed December 2021 at $595.20, at the top of a pandemic-era buying spree for scanners and handhelds, then lost almost two-thirds of its value to a $209.08 month-end in March 2026 as that spending boom unwound — revenue actually fell 20.71% in FY2023. Then came two things at once: a $1.3B cash acquisition of Elo Touch Solutions (self-service kiosks and touchscreens, closed October 1, 2025) and a memory-chip squeeze that should have hurt a device maker. Instead, on August 4, 2026, Zebra reported record Q2 sales of $1,557M (+20.4%, +9.2% organic), non-GAAP EPS of $6.35 against a $4.36 consensus, and raised its full-year outlook; the stock rose 26.47% that day. On November 3, 2026 came another record: Q3 sales of $1,614M (+22.3%, +11.3% organic) and non-GAAP EPS of $4.95, above the $4.70–$4.90 the company had guided, and a higher full-year outlook. The stock still fell 8.76% that day, to $329.50. Gross margin slipped to 45.7% from 48.0% as memory chips cost more. Tradingpedia tied the drop to fourth-quarter EPS guidance whose $5.20 midpoint sat just under the $5.21 analyst consensus it cited; Investing.com pointed to investors' focus on margin pressure and pricing. The central debate now: can price increases keep up with memory costs that management expects to keep rising through the first half of 2027, and how much of 2026's growth was customers buying ahead of those increases? On the Q3 call management said it had seen no effect on revenue from customers buying early.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

Enterprise installed base Breadth: scan, print, compute, RFID, vision Printer consumables tail Workflow software on the device Supplier relationships in a shortage Elo self-service footprint

Zebra's advantage is less any single gadget than the fact that a large retailer or distributor standardises on one family of devices, one management console and one set of apps, then refreshes that fleet every few years. Changing vendor means retraining a floor full of workers and rewriting the apps they use. The competitor set shows how broad the footprint is: Honeywell; Datalogic in barcode readers, mobile computers and vision systems; Cognex in machine vision; Impinj in RFID; SATO in label printers — most of them strong in a slice rather than across the whole span. In a memory shortage, scale matters in a new way: management said in Q2 that its suppliers were delivering on their commitments, which let sales keep growing through the shortage.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
It is a hardware business in a component squeeze. A $145M gross memory headwind in FY2026 is about 2.4% of trailing revenue. In Q3 pricing recovered only about half of the quarter's memory cost increase, and gross margin fell to 45.7% from 48.0%. Management expects memory prices to keep rising through the first half of 2027; if they rise faster than customers accept price increases, the gap comes straight out of gross margin.
02
The best quarter had one-offs in it. Q2's 27.7% adjusted EBITDA margin included $73M of tariff recoveries. Without the refund, Q3 came back to 21.6%, and Q4 is guided at about 22%.
03
The pandemic hangover is the template. FY2021 revenue rose 26.51%; FY2023 fell 20.71% as customers digested what they had bought. Enterprise device demand is lumpy, and a pull-forward cycle can reverse the same way.
04
Thinner balance-sheet cushion after Elo. Borrowings are $2.58B against $182M of cash, and $2.08B of them fall due within a year as the credit facility matures in May 2027, so the refinancing management plans for November matters. The current ratio is 0.57 (quick ratio 0.32), and buybacks took $643M in the first nine months. Leverage of 1.6x is manageable, but there is less room if a downturn arrives.

Notable One-Off Items

Three things distort the recent figures. First, IEEPA tariff recoveries: $73M booked in Q2 cost of sales ($46M in CF, $27M in AVA), of which $46M had arrived in cash by October 3 and $27M was still a receivable (10-Q). They lifted Q2 and did not repeat in Q3. Second, the Elo acquisition: acquisitions added 8.9 points to Q3 reported growth (17.2 points in CF), so reported growth overstates underlying demand. Third, the GAAP/non-GAAP gap is wide: Q3 GAAP diluted EPS was $3.43 against non-GAAP $4.95, mostly $54M of share-based pay and $37M of amortisation of acquired intangibles, before tax (release reconciliation); FY2025 GAAP EPS of $8.18 compares with $15.84 on the adjusted basis analysts use. That gap is why the trailing P/E (27) and forward P/E (15) tell such different stories.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

Organic growth of 11.3% in Q3 and 8.3% over nine months, double-digit growth in healthcare, transportation & logistics the fastest-growing market with a device refresh starting in 2027, and management expecting 2027 growth at the high end of its long-term range. An October price increase of about 15% on mobile computers is meant to cover memory costs dollar for dollar into 2027. Elo, a $1.3B self-service business with about $400M of revenue, grew at a high-single-digit rate. Management guides to at least $1B of free cash flow; buybacks have cut the share count 4.98% in a year (StockAnalysis); the stock trades at about 14.9x FY2027 consensus earnings, and even the lowest analyst target ($350) sits above today's price.

Bear Case

Memory costs are still rising, and management expects increases through the first half of 2027: Q3 gross margin fell to 45.7% from 48.0%, and pricing recovered only about half of the quarter's memory cost increase. With Q2's tariff refund gone, the adjusted EBITDA margin is back at 21.6%. Some of 2026's growth may be customers buying ahead of price increases (management says it has seen no sign of it), and this company has been here before: revenue rose 26.51% in FY2021 and fell 20.71% in FY2023 once customers had what they needed. $2.08B of borrowings fall due by May 2027 and must be refinanced, the current ratio is 0.57, and on GAAP earnings the stock trades at 27x.

01
Memory costs outrun pricing. A $145M gross headwind in FY2026, with management expecting prices to rise through the first half of 2027; if customers resist the October price increase or memory climbs faster than expected, gross margin keeps falling from the 45.7% of Q3.
02
Pull-forward demand reverses. If customers are ordering extra devices now to avoid later shortages, that means fewer orders later; management said on the Q3 call it had seen no such effect on revenue. The FY2021–FY2023 cycle shows how sharply Zebra's revenue can swing when a buying wave ends.
03
One-offs flatter the run-rate. Industry Commentary
The memory squeeze hits the device makers
CRITICAL CONSTRAINT

Every mobile computer, tablet and kiosk needs DRAM and flash, and in 2026 those chips got scarce and expensive. Zebra flagged industry-wide memory price increases in February; by August it was describing a $120M gross headwind for the year, and by November about $145M, with memory costs up about $50M on a year earlier in Q3 alone. Its response: quote deals at higher prices from the start of the year (before the formal price increase in late March), a second increase of about 15% on mobile computers in October, qualifying 10 new memory suppliers, and leaning on long-standing supplier relationships to keep shipping. The Motley Fool's framing — memory as "the best problem in tech" — captures the bull reading: when customers will pay more for scarce devices, the constraint is supply, not demand. The bear reading is that shortages make customers buy early, and early buying is borrowed from later.

Tariffs — a cost, then a refund
ONGOING HEADWIND

Zebra's Q2 2026 results include $73M of IEEPA tariff recoveries — money back on tariffs previously paid — which lifted gross margin to 53.0% and adjusted EBITDA margin to 27.7%. Without it, Q3 gross margin was 45.7% and adjusted EBITDA margin 21.6%. For a hardware maker with global supply chains, trade policy has become a line item that can swing a quarter by several margin points in either direction, and it is not one management controls.

Automation and AI on the front line
EMERGING TAILWIND

The long-term case for Zebra is that warehouses, stores and hospitals keep adding sensors, readers and smarter handhelds. CEO Bill Burns said on the Q2 call that nearly three quarters of warehouses worldwide are early in automating, and he describes Zebra as a business with 5–7% organic sales growth over a cycle. The evidence so far is mixed but improving: in Q2, RFID was flat on project timing and transportation & logistics was flat against a tough comparison; in Q3, machine vision, printing and RFID led AVA's 7.0% organic growth and transportation & logistics was the fastest-growing end market (Q3 call). The bull framing: AI-capable devices start a refresh cycle across a huge installed base. The bear framing: that refresh is exactly what 2021 looked like, and 2023 followed it.
