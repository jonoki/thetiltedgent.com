01 Company Overview

Zurich is one of Europe's largest multi-line insurers: it insures factories, construction projects, cars and homes, sells life protection and pensions, and runs the US Farmers business for a fee. It serves more than 82 million customers in more than 200 countries and territories. It reports in US dollars, though the shares trade in Swiss francs. For the six months to June 30, 2026 it earned a record business operating profit (BOP) of USD 4.77 billion, up 13%. Net income attributable to shareholders was USD 3.49 billion, up 14%, and the Swiss Solvency Test ratio stood at 266%.

That is the operating story. The share price tells a different one. ZURN closed the banner date at CHF 572.40, down 4.9% for 2026 and 8.3% below its July 17 close of CHF 624.40, while the SMI is up 3.0% for the year. There are three reasons, all in this report. First, currency: the US dollar fell against the franc, so H1 diluted EPS rose 10% in dollars (USD 23.52 from 21.34) but only 0.7% in francs (CHF 18.50 from 18.37). Second, Beazley: Zurich bought the London specialty insurer for £8.1 billion in cash. It sold 7.09 million new shares at CHF 550 in March to help pay for it, and the deal became effective on October 1, 2026, the day before the banner date. Third, the Swiss regulator: in July it became public that FINMA had opened enforcement proceedings against the Swiss life unit over its occupational-pension business. The question for an owner is whether a business guiding to more than 23% core return on equity, now with the world's largest specialty book by Zurich's own pro-forma count, deserves more than 14 times trailing earnings and 3.4 times book value. Underneath that is whether the commercial pricing cycle, which management says is now flat to negative, eats into the margin first.

03 Competitive Moat & Vulnerabilities

Sources of moat:

P&C / Life / Farmers diversification Global commercial underwriting network Construction & data-center lead lines Farmers fee stream (no capital at risk) SST 266% balance sheet Specialty scale with Beazley Global Life underwriting platform

Zurich's edge is being able to choose. A group writing commercial property in the US, motor in Spain, protection in Australia and specialty construction across continents can grow in lines that are pricing well and shrink the rest. Greco put it this way on the call: the breadth of the portfolio across geographies, products and customer segments gives "the flexibility to dynamically manage our portfolio through different market cycles." Farmers adds a stream of fees that needs no underwriting capital. Beazley adds Lloyd's access and specialty lines such as cyber. Zurich's pro-forma table puts the combined Commercial Specialty premiums at USD 15.7 billion (Zurich 9.6, Beazley 6.1, FY2025). On the business Zurich says the deal creates, Moneycab (Oct 2) reported that Zurich's completion release calls it the world's largest specialty insurer, headquartered in London and led by Kristof Terryn. The weakness is that choosing protects margins only while some lines are still pricing well. When rates soften across the board, as commercial property is now, there are fewer good places to put the capital.

Key Vulnerabilities

01
The commercial pricing cycle has turned. Commercial rates fell 1% in H1 2026. Greco told analysts that US property rates remain about 10% negative, while specialty, liability and motor are still rising. Kepler Cheuvreux's analyst noted on the call that North American rates had moved from flat in Q1 to −1%, and BNP Paribas' analyst that the commercial accident-year combined ratio excluding catastrophes was 120 bps higher year on year. Growth at a 91% commercial combined ratio is the bull case; holding that ratio while rates fall is the test.
02
A Swiss regulatory case at the Life unit. In July 2026 it became public that FINMA had opened enforcement proceedings against Zurich's Swiss life company. dpa-AFX reported that the case concerns alleged breaches of regulatory requirements over several years in the occupational-pension business, centred on its cooperation with the Vita collective foundation, which dpa-AFX says is not directly affected by the proceedings. Greco told Bloomberg that more than a dozen staff had been dismissed and that FINMA had imposed a partial sales ban on certain policies, as reported by Moneycab on July 28. Fines or sales limits remain open until FINMA concludes.
03
Integration risk on an £8.1 billion deal. Beazley became a subsidiary on October 1, 2026. On August 5, Jefferies' Philip Kett described Beazley's own half-year results as disappointing: prices down, premiums cut back on purpose, costs up, claims experience worse and investment income lower (dpa-AFX). Zurich promises mid-single-digit core EPS accretion from the first full year and a double-digit return on investment in the medium term. Specialty pricing, cyber in particular, will decide whether it gets there.
04
Dollar earnings, franc share price. Zurich earns and reports in US dollars and pays its dividend in francs. In H1 2026 Zurich's average rate was 1.2711 USD per CHF, against 1.1613 a year earlier. That shrank 10% dollar EPS growth to 0.7% in francs. A weaker dollar is a direct headwind to CHF earnings per share and to dividend growth.

08 Summary & Key Risks

Bull Case

For 14.4 times trailing earnings and a 5.2% dividend yield you get a group that has just posted record BOP (+13%), core ROE of 27.1% against a 23% target, and an SST ratio of 266%. Life is growing faster than planned, with guidance raised to at least +10%. Greco says the Farmers Exchanges are gaining market share organically for the first time in more than a decade. Commercial growth is concentrated in short-tail specialty and middle-market lines at a 91% combined ratio, and Zurich leads about 70% of the data-center projects it underwrites. Beazley now adds USD 6.1 billion of specialty premiums with mid-single-digit EPS accretion promised from the first full year, and the placement that diluted 2026 per-share numbers has already happened. If the dollar stabilises, CHF EPS growth catches up with USD growth.

Bear Case

The bear case rests on the cycle. Commercial rates are now falling (−1%), US property is about 10% negative, the commercial accident-year combined ratio excluding catastrophes rose 120 bps, and 2.4 points of the combined ratio came from reserve releases management calls above normal. Beazley arrives at the top of the specialty market, having just reported softer prices, higher costs and worse claims in its own half year. FINMA's case against the Swiss life unit is open, with a sales ban already on some policies. LBBW calls the investment portfolio above-average in risk, and Jefferies' CHF 520 target sits 9% below the price. At 3.4 times book, the stock is priced for a high ROE that the cycle may not allow.

Top 3 Risks

01
Soft commercial pricing compresses P&C margins. P&C supplies over half of segment BOP. Rates fell 1% in commercial in H1 and US property is about 10% negative. If loss trends, especially US casualty social inflation, keep rising while rates fall, the 92.7% combined ratio drifts up, and reserve releases cannot fill that gap for ever.
02
Beazley integration and specialty pricing. £8.1 billion, financed with CHF 3.9 billion of new shares and a bank term loan, bought as specialty pricing (cyber included) has been softening. The accretion promise starts with the first full year after completion, which is 2027.
03
Regulatory and currency drag on the franc shareholder. The FINMA case can end in sanctions or business limits on the Swiss life unit. Meanwhile every 10% fall in the dollar against the franc removes about the same share of CHF earnings per share, as H1 2026 showed: +10% in dollars, +0.7% in francs.
Industry Commentary
The commercial P&C cycle is turning
ONGOING HEADWIND

After several years of rate increases, commercial insurance pricing has gone flat to negative. Zurich's commercial rates fell 1% in H1 2026, and Greco told analysts that US property is "in the rounding of 10%" negative while specialty is double-digit positive and liability and motor high single digits. Zurich's own answer is not to chase volume in lines that are getting cheaper. "We're not targeting low combined ratios," Greco said. The aim is BOP and EPS growth, which means steering growth toward short-tail specialty, construction and middle-market business. The release notes that cyber rate cuts are moderating, "with early signs of stabilization as AI-related developments increase risk awareness."

AI infrastructure as an insurance market
EMERGING TAILWIND

Data-center and energy construction is now a line item in an insurer's growth. Zurich's Construction premiums grew 18% in H1 2026. It took out a new data-center construction quota-share reinsurance providing up to USD 1 billion of capacity and extended its "Data Center Project Guard" product to Europe and Latin America. When Mediobanca asked whether local opposition to data centers was a risk, Greco said Zurich insures only authorised projects. What Zurich carries is construction risk, the builder's risk of a project failing to deliver, not the risk that a project is blocked.

Specialty consolidation
STRUCTURAL SHIFT

With Beazley, Zurich bought scale in Lloyd's and specialty lines rather than building it. Its pro-forma chart puts the combined Commercial Specialty premiums at USD 15.7 billion on FY2025 figures, against a peer set it lists as AIG, Allianz, AXA, Berkshire Hathaway, Chubb, Liberty Mutual, Munich Re and QBE (numbers partially estimated). AM Best said on September 4 that the deal "further enhances Zurich's profile as a leading global commercial and specialty franchise." The open question for the industry is whether buying at this point in the specialty cycle pays off.
