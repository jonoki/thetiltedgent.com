Where the numbers come from. House edges are the standard published figures for each game's rules and paytable (the Wizard of Odds compilations, which are the industry reference), quoted as the edge on the amount initially bet unless stated. Casinos vary their rules and paytables constantly; the edge on the table in front of you is whatever its rules say, and this page teaches you how to read them. Nothing here is advice to gamble — it's the price list.

How the grade works

Five things I weigh before I sit.

A grade isn't just the house edge. A 1% game that drains $2,000 an hour is worse for you than a 3% game that drains $60. Here's the scorecard behind every letter.

01

House edge

What the best available bet costs, and how badly the typical player overpays it. Weighted heaviest — it's the rent.

02

Decision leverage

Do your choices move the number? Blackjack: enormously. Baccarat: not at all. Skill you can learn is edge you can keep.

03

Pace & bleed

Edge × bet × decisions per hour = what the night costs. Slots resolve 600 times an hour; roulette, 40. Speed is a hidden multiplier.

04

Trap density

How many bets on the layout exist purely to punish people who don't read? Craps and blackjack side bets are minefields around a good core.

05

Fun per dollar

Entertainment is a legitimate purchase. A game that's social, slow and cheap earns credit — you're paying for the evening, not the outcome.

Advantage play is noted separately for each game: what it is, whether it's legal, and how the house treats you if it notices. It never changes the grade — the grade is for the player the casino expects, not the one it fears.

The takeaway

The house always wins. Learn to be the house.

Every game above has the same shape: a fair-ish core bet, surrounded by expensive bets that look more exciting, on a layout designed to make the expensive ones feel like the main event. Read the price before you play — and notice that the one game on this list that doesn't have a house edge is the one this whole site is built around.

The exception: poker

Poker isn't on this list because the casino isn't your opponent — it takes a rake and the other players take everything else. There's no house edge to grade, only a skill edge to earn. That's a different curriculum, and the one twenty years at the table paid for. Coming to Pillar 02 next.

Sizing the bet is the other half

A 0.5% edge and a 5% edge both go to zero if you bet enough of your bankroll on one hand. Kelly, fractional Kelly, drawdown math and tilt control — the part nobody wants to study — live in Pillar 03: The Bankroll.
