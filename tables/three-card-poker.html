Game 06

Three Card Poker: one decision, one trap, one pleasant hour.

Invented in 1994 by a British poker player who wanted a table game simple enough for anyone and fast enough for a casino. He succeeded. There is exactly one strategic decision in the game and it fits on a matchbook: play Queen-Six-Four or better, fold everything else. The rest is a side bet designed to look like the main event.

How it plays

Post an Ante. You and the dealer each get three cards; yours face up, theirs down. Look at your hand and either fold (lose the ante) or Play by putting up a second bet equal to the ante. The dealer needs Queen-high to qualify. If the dealer doesn't qualify, your Ante pays even money and Play pushes. If the dealer qualifies, better hand wins both bets, worse hand loses both. Hand rankings are three-card rankings: a straight beats a flush here, because with three cards a straight is rarer. An Ante Bonus pays extra on a straight or better, win or lose.

Pair Plus is the separate bet on the felt: it pays according to your own three cards alone, regardless of the dealer — a pair pays 1:1, a straight flush 40:1 — and it requires no decisions. Because it looks like the "fun" bet and pays out big now and then, it's where most of the money at the table goes. Which is the point.

The quirks

  • Q-6-4 is the entire strategy. Play any hand of queen-six-four or higher, fold anything worse. Playing every hand costs about 2 percentage points more; folding "correctly" by feel costs about 1.
  • Pair Plus paytables moved. The original 40-30-6-4-1 (straight flush / three of a kind / straight / flush / pair) runs 2.32%. The now-common version pays 3:1 on a flush instead of 4:1 — a one-number change that takes it to 7.28%. Same felt, three times the price. Look at the flush line.
  • The Ante Bonus paytable matters too. 5-4-1 (straight flush / trips / straight) gives the 3.37% figure; 4-3-1 is 3.83%; 3-2-1 is 4.28%.
  • Six Card Bonus and the "Progressive" bets that have sprouted on the layout run 6–15% on the common paytables. They're fun the way a scratch card is fun.
  • You'll win the Ante and push the Play a lot when the dealer fails to qualify with a hand better than yours. That's correct and it feels like a bug.

Best bets
  • Ante & Play with the Q-6-4 rule at a table paying 5-4-1 on the ante bonus. About 2% of what you put on the felt.
  • Pair Plus only if the flush pays 4:1. At 2.32% it's an acceptable bit of fun. At 3:1 it isn't.
Worst bets
  • Pair Plus on the 6-3-1 paytable. 7.28% — and it's the bet the table is designed to sell you.
  • Six Card Bonus / progressives. Double digits.
  • Playing every hand. The 3.37% is for people who fold; the always-play crowd pays over 5.

Hole-carding, again

How it works. The dealer picks up three cards to check for a qualifying hand, and a careless dealer lifts them where a player at first or last seat can see one. Knowing a single dealer card flips the Play decision from a 3.4% loss to a player edge of a few percent — you play more hands when the dealer's card is low, fold more when it's high. It's the same skill as in Ultimate Texas Hold'em and the same small community of professionals who scout dealers across a city.

Legality and acceptance. As above: in Nevada, seeing what's shown to you without a device isn't cheating; elsewhere it depends. Casinos treat it as an emergency — new dealer, table closed, player flagged. There is no version of this for a recreational player beyond knowing that it's why the dealer now uses a card-reading shoe.

The Gent's verdict. A civilised, low-stress table game where the main bet is fairly priced and the flashy bet beside it isn't. Learn one rule, ignore one bet, and you're paying about 2% for a relaxed hour. Do the reverse and you're paying 7% for the same chairs.
